# Name lookup for the unread names (Wotton to Queen Mary, Soissons, June 1554)

Short pass. Printed sources checked: CSP Foreign Mary, June and July 1554 (BHO).

## Printed context
- CSP For. Mary no. 221, 14 June 1554, Soissons: Wotton to Petre. So the letter is from mid-June, at Soissons.
  https://www.british-history.ac.uk/cal-state-papers/foreign/mary/pp89-101
- No. 222, 14 July, Rheims: Wotton to the Queen, with a deciphered part on Carew ("determined to leave France and go
  to Italy, relying upon her Majesty's mercy"). No. 223: Throckmorton is asking for mercy, and Edward Randal is
  expected. These are later letters with the same themes, not a crib.
- The calendar summary on BHO has no June letter from Wotton to the Queen, so this letter does not seem to be
  calendared there. Not checked: Tytler, *England under Edward VI and Mary* ii; CSP Spain XII; APC v.

## (a) p2.25 "the lord p-r-(i)-m-s-t-a-n-e-a-s-c-o-t-h-n"
Proposed reading: **"the lord Brymstane, a Scot, [to] him"**. This would be Alexander Crichton, laird of Brunstane
(Brunston), a Scottish Protestant who worked with the English. English letters of the time spell the name
"Brimstone"/"Brymstane" and call him "lord" or "laird of Brunstane". He was in exile in France and Italy after 1548.
- p-r-i-m-s-t-a-n-e matches BRYMSTANE except for the first letter. P for B is either a phonetic spelling or a
  one-homophone slip.
- "a Scot" follows it directly. "hn" is probably "hm" (him), from the n/o slip.
- Other readings: Kirkcaldy of Grange, or the Lord of St John. Neither fits the letters.
- Confidence: medium (about 0.5). Next check: find Brunstane in France in 1554 (CSP Scot. i; ODNB "Crichton,
  Alexander, of Brunstane").

## (b) p6.27 "the Duke of Gwyse p-e-n-s-?-t-e, Monsieur de ..., and g-a-w..."
- "pens?te" is probably an English verb, not a name: "pensith" or "is passid", i.e. Guise "passeth"/"is gone", followed by
  a list of commanders.
- For "gaw...", the best candidate is **Gaspard** (de Coligny, the Admiral, Montmorency's nephew). He served with
  Guise and the Constable on the June 1554 campaign (Mariembourg, Dinant, Renty).
- "Monsieur de ..." is open. D'Andelot, Termes and Vieilleville are all possible.
- Confidence: low (about 0.2).

## (c) p6.09 "Greneberye"
- No match found in the printed sources checked. It could be an English surname, such as Greenbury.
- Confidence: low. Leave it tentative.
