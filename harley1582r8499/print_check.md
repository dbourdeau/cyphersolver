# Print check: Wotton to Queen Mary, June 1554 (Harley 1582 ff. 8-10)

Checked 22 Sept 2026. The OCR texts are in `lit/`.

## 1. Main finding: the manuscript holds a contemporary deciphered copy

The Harleian catalogue (1808, vol. 2, p. 138-139, "Num. 1582") describes the volume as follows (`lit/harlcat2.txt`,
lines 32880-32900; https://archive.org/details/gri_33125008529436):

> 1. Original Letter of Mr. William Pykerynge, to the Lords of the Privy Council. Paris 25 February 1552. [fol.] 1.
>    Part of this Letter is in Cyfer; but being interlined in plain Hand, 'tis legible enough.
> 2. Copie in Common-hand, of the following Letter.
> 3. Original Letter of Dr. Nicholas Wotton, to Queen Mary I. Soissons, 13 June 1554, almost all in Cyfer,
>    which is explained by the preceding Copy.
> 4. Original Letter of Mr. Thomas Wilks, to Mr. Francis Walsingham. Poissy, 12 February 157[3/4]. [fol.] 11.

This means:
- **Art. 2 is a plain-text copy of our letter.** It is bound just before it, somewhere between f. 1 and f. 8
  (probably ff. 3-7). It is the decipherment, or at least a clear text of the letter. None of the DECODE R8499
  images (ff. 8r, 8v, 9r, 10v) shows it. Next step: check the neighbouring DECODE records (for example R8498) and the
  BL digitised Harley 1582 for ff. 1-7, and look for the copy before doing more key work.
- **The date is 13 June 1554, at Soissons**, according to the catalogue. The folder currently dates the letter
  8 June. The difference may be the catalogue reading the date line differently. It fits CSP For. Mary no. 221
  (Wotton to Petre, Soissons, 14 June).

## 2. Is the letter printed anywhere?

- **Tytler, *England under Edward VI and Mary*, vol. 2 (1839)**, `lit/tytler2.txt`
  (https://archive.org/details/englandunderreig02tytluoft): not printed. The 1554 Wotton letters Tytler prints are
  31 March (to the Queen, p. 352 ff.) and 17 April (to Petre, "Mostly in cipher", pp. 370-371, on the rebels in
  France). There is no June letter and no Soissons or Compiègne letter. The April letter is useful background:
  "these rebels here will not be delivered, but be employed here in service ...".
- **CSP Foreign, Mary** (`lit/cspf_june1554.htm`, `lit/cspf_july1554.htm`): not calendared, because the calendar
  covers SP 69 and not Harleian manuscripts. Nos. 221-223 give the context (Carew going to Italy; Throckmorton
  "will not serve with the rebels", wants to serve with the Gascons; Edward Randal).
- **CSP Scotland i (Bain 1898)**, `lit/cspscot1.txt`
  (https://archive.org/details/calendarstatepa00baingoog): nothing on Wotton in 1554.
- Not reached: CSP Spain XII, APC v, Strype and the Guise *Mémoires-journaux*. British History Online search now
  requires a CAPTCHA, and these are lower priority now that the manuscript has its own clear copy.

## 3. The names

- **Brunstane / "Brymstane"**: in CSP Scot. i the spellings "Brymstone", "Brymston" and "Brimstone" are all attested
  for Crichton of Brunstane (lines 5830, 9156, 21923 and elsewhere), so the reading of the spelling is sound.
  However, the calendar index (p. 1-ff., `lit/cspscot1.txt` l. 50013 ff.) has him only in 1547-48 and then from
  1559-60. The entries for 1559-60 are probably his son John, since Alexander was dead before December 1558
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Crichton_of_Brunstane). Nothing I found puts Brunstane in France in 1554.
  The identification stays unconfirmed. The art. 2 copy should settle it.
- **Cornwel, Greneberye, the Scottish lord, and the French commanders "Gaw..." and "pens..."**: no match in the
  sources checked. Tytler and CSP For. give the exiles as Carew, Pickering, Killigrew, Throckmorton, Randal and
  Staunton. I did not find a "Cornwall" or a "Greenbury" among them. The art. 2 copy is the best route for all of these.
