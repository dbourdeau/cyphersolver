## Proposals for the open labels in key_v6.md

- **B.**: the sheet has "of" = B followed by a dot. "of [B.]reet" p1.05/p1.07 is probably not a letter group. Read B. as "of" and check "of Reet" / "of [..]reet" as a name.
- **T.**: "me" (crossed double-stem T with a dot after). This is the closest shape.
- **9..**: "it" (ꝗ with a dot after).
- **.I / .i / i**: Thérouanne (tick-before I). Plain ī is Parma and i̲ is Mirandola. In a 1554 letter these are all plausible place names in the p1-2 place runs.
- **.Z**: "that" (crossed Z with a bar, the same sign as 4 / -4).
- **.D**: "to" (D) is the only D form. It has no dotted variant, so the dot is probably a separator.
- **.M**: Trent (M4) is the only M-like code. Otherwise use ɱ forms: we, Peter Carew, the Rebels.
- **.p / .p.**: "there" (plain p). The alternatives are "whom" (circled p) and the English nation (crossed P with an overbar).
- **Y_SCRIPT_UL / Y_BOLD.**: "either" (script y with a tail). For Y_SCRIPT_UL, also consider ʒ̂ = Luxembourg.
- **L2**: Calais (⅃), tentative.
- **-P**: "robbery on the seas" (Pe) or the English nation. Weak.
- **Ie on p1-2**: the closest shape is the Emperor's ambassador (looped ℓ with a t-bar). "hath [the Emperor's ambassador] sent me" p2.09 and "Carew, [the Emperor's ambassador] he have had" fit in sense. Tentative.
- **S (ticked)**: no S-shaped code on the nomenclator. It stays an alphabet homophone (a).
- **CURL_D**: the sheet has two looped-D codes, "the lords of the Council / your lordships" and "the Earl of Devonshire" (ꝺ). Before keeping CURL_D as a null, check whether some of its roughly 20 places are these codes.
