# Unknown sender to unknown recipient, 1664: HHStA Verschwörerakten Fasc. 327 Konv. D ff. 30-31 (DECODE R688)

Catalogue item 270 (class A, scored by rule). DECODE R688: "Non-decrypted", 3 pages, simple substitution, graphic
signs, no language, no sender or receiver; dated 1664-1668, which is the span of the bundle ("Chiffres 1664-1668"),
not of the letter. Access by login; the image is not in the public domain.

Status: explained, 22 Sept 2026. **Not a cipher.** The "graphic signs" are Cyrillic letters writing vernacular Serbian.

## What the pages are

Two DECODE images (`img/`, git-ignored): image 1 is f. 30r, image 2 an opening with f. 30v on the left and, on the
right, a small slip mounted on f. 31 (foliated 31, docketed on its edge "Epistola ..."). The hand is an untrained,
upright Cyrillic uncial-like script with Church Slavonic spellings (ѿ, ѥ, ї, final ъ/ь, titla).

* **Letter A (f. 30r-v)** opens *Сїи листь писа Стоянь ... ѿ града З[?]вачь* ("This letter was written by Stojan from
  the town of Z?vač"). He sends greeting, "life and health" to the aghas, knezes, odabaše, "small and great", and to
  "my Vlachs" by name: Vuk, Gavrilo, Radonja(?), Vučeta(?), kum Prodan, Mirko, Pavle, Dimitrije, Marko, "with all
  the Christians". The plea: *братїа да ми се смилуете, можите мене извадить из немачкий тамнице а ваше душе из
  пакле* ("brothers, have mercy on me: you can get me out of the German prison, and your souls out of hell"). He
  mentions groschen, money he lacks, pledges and what is needed; on f. 30v, bring the money to the town of Bor(?),
  "I know horses and men, we can get [me] out, and all be glad, amen". Signed as written for him: *Писа намъ Мла-
  дь Бакиі[?] у градъ Бор[у]*. A postscript greets his wife and asks kum Prodan to go himself to the Magyar(?).
* **Slip B (f. 31)**: four damaged lines, *Сїи лисъ писа Х[?]саань[?] у Пет...*, greetings to the writer's mother
  and brothers.

`transcription.txt` is line for line with doubtful words marked; `reading.txt` has the English gist. About a
quarter of the words are doubtful or unread (blots, deletions, a crossed-out last line on f. 30r, the torn slip).
That is a limit of the transcription, not of a cipher: nothing on the pages is enciphered.

## Why it is filed as a cipher

Fasc. 327 Konv. D of the Hungarian conspiracy files (Wesselényi period) is headed *Chiffres 1664-1668*. A Cyrillic
letter would have been unreadable to the Vienna clerks, and was bound with the ciphers for that reason; DECODE
catalogued it from the bundle heading as a substitution in graphic signs. The neighbouring key record R707 (ff.
60-61) is a Latin numeric nomenclator and has nothing to do with it.

## Open

* The place names: *З[?]вачь* (Stojan's town) and *Бор[у]* (where the money is to go) are uncertain readings.
  A Serbian or Hungarian specialist could settle them and date the letter.
* Why a Serb prisoner's plea sits among the conspiracy papers (an intercepted letter; a prisoner of the
  Habsburg border garrisons) is not known.

R688: not a cipher (clear Serbian in Cyrillic); cleartext language Serbian; symbol set "graphic signs" and cipher
type "simple substitution" wrong; 1664-1668 is the bundle span. Queued in `decode_updates/queue.json` with the
transcription.
