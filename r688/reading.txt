A. Letter of Stojan, a prisoner (f. 30r-v)
"This letter was written by Stojan from the town of Z[?]vač(?): greeting, and life and health, to the aghas,
the knezes, the [odabaše = company commanders], to small and great. And again I send life and health to my
Vlachs: to Vuk, Gavrilo, Radonja(?), Vučeta(?), my kum Prodan, Mirko, Pavle, Dimitrije, Marko, with all the
Christians. And again, Vuk and Vučić(?) ... Brothers, have mercy on me: you can get me out of the German prison,
and your souls out of hell. [My good wife and kum ...] ... clothes ... groschen ... what is needed ... money,
which I have not ... pledged ... until he comes ... [Đurko?] ... what is needed goes there.
From over here, where there is no ... you can [send] money ... bring it to the town of Bor(?) ... measures of
land ... I know horses and men; we can get [me] out, and all be glad, [God willing], amen.
Written for us by Mla[den?] Baki[ć?], in the town of Bor(?)."

Postscript (f. 30v): greetings, life and health to my wife; ask kum Prodan to go himself to the Magyar [?] ...
groschen ... [largely uncertain].

B. Slip on f. 31: a second short note in the same kind of hand: "This letter was written by H[?]asan(?) at
Pe[?]... greeting, life and health to my ... my mother and brothers ..." (four lines, damaged).

Content in one line: Serb Christians (Vlachs) under Ottoman-side aghas and knezes are asked to ransom or
free Stojan, held in a "German" (Habsburg) prison.
