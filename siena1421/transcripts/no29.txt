WHAT IT IS: a torn scrap (irregular, c. 1/4 of a leaf, torn on two sides) from a KEY / nomenclator list, not a
  letter: each entry is "name — code", with a long dash between the clear word and its equivalent, and the
  entries are spaced far apart (one entry per ruled space).  Both sides written, in two different orientations,
  one hand, brown ink, 15th-century mercantesca/humanist letter forms.  Pencil archive number "29" on P2 (twice,
  once upside down).  No date, sender or address.  Words at the torn edges are cut.

SIGN LEGEND: letters as written (lower case); '—' = the long dash separating entry and value; '&' = the
  looped sign like a figure 8 / 'g' with a closed lower bowl ("ȣ"/"ɡ" with loop) that begins two entries on P1;
  '²' = a small superscript sign like a 2/z above the line; '[..]' = lost at the tear.

P2 side (turned 90 deg clockwise to read; large decorated initials, i.e. the "clear" column of the key)
LP2.1  [R?/D?]oma(?) — te ſſ[..]        second line under it: rona ll[..]
       (initial = a large looped capital like "D"/"R" with a long lead-in stroke; the word reads "omma"/"oma", so
        "Roma"?  The value "te ſſ.. / rona .." is cut off at the right tear: maybe one long value word
        "teſſ...rona" or two values)
LP2.2  [S?]ndistia(?) / [A]nditia(?) — [..]      under it: tavo(?) / taro(?) with a long descending tail (value?)
       (large looped initial like a reversed S or a G; the word after it "nditia"/"ndistia"; value lost at tear)
LP2.3  [G]iovan[..]                    under it: "v.." (cut)   = Giovan(ni) ...
       (large capital G with a flourish bar on top)

P1 side (turned 90 deg anticlockwise to read; smaller hand, no decorated initials: probably the other column of
  the key, i.e. code words, or a second list)
LP1.1  & la gT —— [co/ca?]                    ("& la gT", then a long dash, then a cut word "ca"/"co")
LP1.2  & la — Lodyo²                         ("Lodyo" or "Lodijo"; the final superscript mark is a small 2/z)
LP1.3  (written upside down relative to LP1.1-2, at the foot of the same side:)
       [U]ngaria —— 1&7 / 187(?)             ("Ungaria" = Hungary; the value is three signs: an upright "1",
                                               a looped &/8-like sign, a 7-like hook; could be the number 187
                                               written with an old-style 8)

COUNTS: 6 entries (3 per side), all fragmentary; about 40 letters.  Plain words: Roma(?), Giovan(ni), Ungaria.
  If "Ungaria — 187" is a number code, the scrap belongs to a numeric/word nomenclator of the 15th c. (Hungary
  appears in the 1450s keys, e.g. R4753 "Imperator ... Rex Portugalie", and in the c. 1455-58 Calixtus-era
  material).  The "& la" entries resemble "& la ..." = "e la ..." i.e. plain text rather than code.
