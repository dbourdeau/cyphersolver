WHAT IT IS
- Salutation (P1 l.1): "Magnifici et excelsi d(omi)ni Officiales Balie. post humilem commendationem etc."
  A long Italian despatch (c. 45 lines on P1) from a Sienese orator to the Officials of the Balia.
- Line 4 of P1 names "m(esser) Lionardo Benvoglienti" (in the third person, "... d(e) m(esser) Lionardo
  Benvoglienti v(ost)ro ...") and line 3 ends "... Milano(?)". Near the end (P1, ~y 3300) "... de lo
  Ill(ustrissi)mo S. duca d(i) Melano(?) ..." and just above "... in Napoli(?) ...". P2 mentions the
  "conte Iacomo" (Jacopo Piccinino?), "la materia de Genova(?)", "Bologna", "Castel...". So a despatch of
  the 1450s from the Milan embassy. Note for the solvers: key R4750 in fasc. 1 is "a messer Leonardo e
  messer Antonio, ambasciatori a Milano, 1454" (Latin-word nomenclator); this cipher (mixed digits,
  letters and signs, homophonic-looking) should be tried against it and against R4746/R4749/R4753.
- Signature (P1 foot, right, torn edge): "Servitor v(este)r Orator  A?n?t?(onius)? / Le?nar?(dus)?
  N?ic(olai)?? ... ??"  -- the name is not legible with confidence (first name could be Antonius or
  Leonardus; the second word looks like "Nicolai"/"Nicho..."; last word illegible).
- Date (P1 foot, left, on the torn/stained edge): "D?ie?g? ... xi?? ... ur? . e? . a . . n? ... 8?" --
  place/day/month/year NOT legible.
- P2 (verso, upper third): clear postscript headed "Genova"(?) ("Ge nova" / "Genua"), then "el polizuolo"
  (the slip), beginning "Tenuta a di 27, e non ancora mandata(?) ... che poch'altro nono(?) ... la risposta
  ... vedra ... Francesco(?) Sforza(?) ... conte Iacomo ... la materia de Genova(?) ... Bologna ...".
  NO cipher on P2. A capital "R(esponsum)?" "R°" mark at left below the text.
- Address (P2 centre): "[Mag]ni(fi)cis et ex(cel)l(enti)bus d(omi)nis / [Offi]cialibus Balie civitatis /
  Sen(arum) d(omi)nis meis sing(ularissi)mis"; paraph below.
- The cipher occupies P1 lines L02-L12 below (11 lines, y 2575-3105 on P1), with clear words
  interleaved in almost every line. Interlinear plaintext glosses (single letters) are written over some
  signs in L04 and L08 (partial decipherment by a reader).

SIGN LEGEND (letters/digits as themselves; clear words in [ ])
  o      small closed circle (single)
  oo     two circles joined horizontally (like an infinity sign / "oo" ligature); written as one token
  c      open circle / c; often immediately before "oo" ("c oo" = what looks like three joined rings, the
         first open on the right. Where the first ring looks closed it is still written "c oo")
  1      upright stroke (i / 1), sometimes with a heavy top
  2      z-shaped 2 with a long flat foot stroke to the right
  3      3 with descender (z-shaped)
  B3     3 preceded by a tall vertical bar joined to it ("[3", like a capital B/ß) (L08 only)
  4      4 with closed triangular head and cross-bar (ꝝ-like); also looks like a crossed "y"
  5      old-style 5 (hooked top, s-like)
  6      round 6 (G-like, bowl with rising stem)
  6~     6 with a tilde/wave over it (distinct from 6; L04, L05, L06, L07)
  7      7 (hooked top, straight descender)
  7#     7-hook joined to a double upright crossed by a bar (ɥ + #), always written as one unit
  #      double slanted upright crossed by a bar, without the 7-hook (L02 only)
  8      8 (sometimes with a looped top, or a filled lower bowl)
  8o     a circle on a stem with a second circle below ("o-stem-o", like 8 with a stroke through) (L08)
  9      9 with closed bowl and tail curving down-left
  DEL    round bowl with a curled stroke rising from the top to the left (∂ / ϑ shape); an 8 open at the
         top-left. Distinguished from 8 where the shape is clear; ? where not.
  THETA  circle with a horizontal bar inside (θ) (L02)
  G      large circle with a stroke inside (G/Θ-like) (L03, once)
  x      plain slanted cross (x)
  V      checkmark/γ-shaped sign: short left stroke meeting a long rising right stroke, often with a dot
         at the right (ɤ / √)
  PCT    x with a small ring on the upper-left and lower-right arms (like %); in L04 with a tilde above
  +      plus / cross
  =      two parallel horizontal bars
  HEART  solid filled triangle/heart (▼) (L09, once)
  sl     long stroke descending to the lower left, attached after o or a (ʃ / ρ-like); "o sl" and "a sl"
  q      closed circle with a straight descending stem (φ / q)
  QP     q and p written joined (ꝗp)
  p      p;  P_ = p with a hook/bar at the foot (ꝑ)
  a b f h i k m r t S H  as the letters (h = ʒ-like h with hooked top; S = capital S; H = two uprights and
         bar)
  LAM4   Λ joined to a 4-like cross (Λ4), one sign (L05 end "f . LAM4", L11)
  TU     u-shaped bowl with a flat bar on top (ʊ with a lid) (L08, L09)
  VO     v-shape with a small ring on top (L11 end)
  .      a point set between signs by the scribe (kept as ".")
  /      a gap or stroke left by the scribe between groups

NOTES
- Clear/cipher boundary: the cipher signs are written separately and upright; clear words are cursive
  and ligatured. Some short cursive items inside the cipher ("mezo", "omo" in L02, "a f a" in L06,
  "gra(n)d(e)" at L08 end, "sim" at L09 end) are uncertain: they may be clear words or cipher letters.
  "a f a H o" (L06) is taken as cipher.
- Glosses: L04 "o" over 2 and "e" over QP; L08 a run of small letters "l a n o t? e r o i? o" written
  between L07 and L08, over the first 10 signs of L08 (7 q 6 + B3 2 x + 8o c): the alignment is not
  one-to-one (the "l a" sits over 7/q). Taken literally the glosses give o for several different
  signs (2, x, +, c), i.e. a homophonic cipher with several signs per vowel. L06 small "er"(?) above 3,
  L12 "+"(?) above x, L03 a few tiny marks under "o sl =". All glosses are faint and uncertain.
- Repeated groups: "x 3 5 m" three times (L08 "S x 3 5 m", L12 "4 q x 3 5 m" and "4? q x 3 5 m");
  "c oo" very frequent; "7#" 13x, typically after 4 or x; "o sl" and "a sl" pairs; "p c oo t" twice
  (L03, L04).
- In L04 "3 PCT 4" is underlined by a long horizontal stroke (possibly a correction mark).
- P1 line 4 names Leonardo Benvoglienti in the third person, so the writer is probably his colleague
  in Milan (cf. key R4750, "messer Leonardo e messer Antonio, ambasciatori a Milano, 1454"); the
  signature's first name could then be "Antonius". Not verified.
