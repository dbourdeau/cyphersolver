SIGN CLASSES (task 1)
---------------------
Method: every line was re-viewed on 2.6x contrast-enhanced crops that follow the line's slope
(crops/agentB/Q5_<line>_<k>.jpg), with 4-5x zooms (v*.jpg, w*.jpg, s*.jpg) for doubtful words.

TH = theta, n.  Tall 8-like sign: an open loop at ascender height that runs down in a slanting stroke
     into a small bowl on the line.  All former D (except those listed as DL) and the tall "8"s are TH.
     In SIGNS every former D is now TH or DL; "TH?" = shape not clear.
8  = compact 8, v/u.  Two closed loops of about equal size, upright, within or barely above x-height.
     "trovandosi" (L06 w6) has both: IB VB P [8] EQ [TH] OR 6 RR Q4.
DL = delta, c.  Solid round bowl on the line with a straight stem rising from it, usually with a knob
     at the top.  It is probably the same sign as B (c): B's "b" and DL's "delta" differ only in how
     upright the stem is.  Found in: co(n)servacione L14 (x2), da Toscana L07, cose L17, e a ca(m)po L18,
     via d'acordo L19, docati L20, dispac(c)iati L21, acresca and conserv[i] L22, (n)gnifica and ci
     racoma(n)diamo L23, cioe L24, [pia]cierà L16.
PHI = phi, i.  A loop on a stem that crosses the loop and runs above and below it.  It is the sign I
     had called OSTEM?, some "P" and some "Q".  It must be i in Domenico L06, ragionando L07,
     dispiacie L08, gratisime L04, Signoria L15, (pia)cierà and tornati L16, presencia L17, di nuovo L18,
     sperasi and via L19, speriamo L20, di scrivarlo L21, (n)gnifica L23, cioe L24.
P  = rho, o.  Loop with a stem below only.  Answer to the P question: the "P = i" cases are PHI,
     a separate glyph; true P is always o.
F  = o-o, f.  Two o's joined by one stroke (Mangnifici L01, significhiamo L05, e fati L07, efeto L08,
     [a]fferma L10, [ef]feto L15-16, a F[?]rci L19, (n)gnifica L23).
M  = the m-shaped sign (compa(n)gnia L02).  N? = u/v (uno, unita, tuti, altrove, vi sarano, di
     nuovo).  N = n.
X  = a cross-shaped sign, previously read IB, in l'Altisimo L22 (= a?).
RR^bar = RR with a bar over it, in "se aveste" L21 (previously read as the code sign Z7).
Arcs over groups = nasal or abbreviation marks: co(n), qu(e)sta, discopertame(n)te, ma(n)dare,
     se(n)tiremo, vi sara(n)o, ca(m)po, me(n)te, racoma(n)diamo, Sig(n)oria.
Spelling habits: gn written ng/ngn; single consonants (tuti, efeto, deto, docati); Q also serves as i
     in a few words (tenut[i], dispac(c)iati, l'Altisimo), so Q/Q4 may not be kept apart.

CODE SIGNS AND NUMBERS (task 3)
-------------------------------
<7H>  = the sign 7 followed by H.  It occurs 4 times: "servidor[e] di <7H>" L06, "che a <7H> è
        dispiaciuto" L08, "lo deto <7H> e Carlo" L11, "<7H> è a ca(m)po" L18.  As letters it gives "ph",
        not a word, so it is a code name for a person: a lord who "is in the field" (in campaign), whose
        servant talked with Domenico, and who is allied or in talks with "Carlo [M]alatesti".
<Z7>  = a large s-like sign with a long top bar.  It stands alone between points in L09 ("per <Z7>
        di (420)") and L13 ("guera co(n) <Z7>").  L11 "Z7?" was only RR (it gives "medesima").  The
        L21 sign that looks like it reads as an ordinary s ("se aveste").  So <Z7> is a one-sign code for
        the party the war is waged against or for.
(420) = LP Q4 2 O PAREN, twice: "per <Z7> di (420)" L09, "per co(n)servacione da (420)" L13.  As
        letters the three signs would give i-e-u, which is no word.  The brackets and the fact that
        Q4/2/O are the digit shapes 4/2/0 mark it as a number used as a code, probably a person or
        state ("the enterprise given by Z7 to/of 420"; "for the preservation of 420").
<13>  = a circled sign enclosing a stroke and a 3/b (L21 "dispac(c)iati da <13>").  Another code name.
10    = "1" and "0" between points in L20, followed by "docati": "10 ducati"?
Numbers in clear: none other.  I found no sign sequence that spells a name for 7H, Z7 or 420.

"[stroke] | a-v-i-s-o-n-o | roma' | [erased] | [luogo?] | d-i-l-[blot]-n | io | o-f-a-t-o-n |
cioè | per | n | [8-like]".
- "roma'" (r-o-m-a with a tick) is written in ordinary letters, not in the cipher's r/o/m/a signs.  It
  is most likely the place of writing (Rome) or a subject.
- "cioè per" is clear; the rest is not.
- No month name, no year number and no day (other than the uncertain "10 docati" in L20) were found.
L25-L26 (right half, signature block): "[stroke] [LAMBDA] li servidori vostr[i] [7X?] | [q with
overline] | e-o-c-o-g..." and "d | [ETA] | Marco | a e".  One legible name: "Marco"; "d" + ETA before it
may be an abbreviated title or first name.  Not a date.
