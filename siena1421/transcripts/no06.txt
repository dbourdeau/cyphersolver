WHAT IT IS
- Clear salutation on m.01: "Magnifici et potentes .d(omini). .d(omini). mei singularissimi doppo humile
  recomandatione" (Italian, 15th-century mercantesca-like hand), then cipher to the end of the letter.
- 14.5 lines of continuous cipher, no word division, no clear words inside, no date, no signature on the text side.
  The text ends on m.15 at about 3/4 of the line (last sign "6"); the rest of the page is blank.
- One scribal deletion (m.03): 4-5 signs struck through with a heavy stroke, then the text continues.
- No interlinear glosses anywhere.

OBSERVATIONS
- Continuous writing, no word separators; signs are Latin minuscules, a few capitals (B E H T), digit shapes
  (0 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9) and invented signs (BOX, CIRC, THETA, PHI, VENUS, YCROSS, SS, SP, A, RX, PAREN ...).
- Crosses are the main diacritic: a cross above m and above the d-bowl occurs 80 times ("m+", "d+"), and a
  cross through the descender of y (YCROSS, 24) or o (VENUS, 4). Plain m (3) and plain d (26) also occur, so the
  cross looks meaningful (a different sign, or a doubling/null marker). A few other signs carry a cross once.
- Frequent repeats: "p p" (~20x), "a PAREN" (5x, PAREN only ever after a), "SS 3" (6x), "W BOX", "BOX W".
- With ~54 base signs and W/p/THETA/t each at 4.5-6%, the system looks homophonic with nomenclator-type signs
  rather than simple substitution.
- Uncertainties to recheck first: W vs "c o"/"a u"; THETA vs o + connecting hairline; 8 vs 6/e; q vs 9 vs PHI;
  the A sign (merged from F?/A variants); the blotted sign in m.05; RX? in m.14.
