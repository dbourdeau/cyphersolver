# acciaiuoli1513: Roberto Acciaiuoli's dispatch from Amiens, 23 October 1513 (catalogue 2351)

Status: **read in part.** Both faces of ASF Mediceo avanti il Principato LXXII, doc. 192 are transcribed (1,887 signs) and build a continuous Italian text. Of the 757 cipher words of `reading-it.md`, 660 (87.2%) are the key's output with nothing supplied or changed. The 97 holes are 41 words with a bracketed letter or a second key value (19 of them the declared departures of `192-repairs.tsv` and the unread last sign), 26 proposed words marked `[?word]`, 25 unread words and 5 numbers with no value. That is under the 95% read bar. ## What the letter says

Acciaiuoli writes to the Signoria. Louis XII has ordered Monsieur de Bourbon into Burgundy with 8 hundred lances and 10 thousand Landsknechte. A plan involving a passage of the mountains has been overturned by a copy of a Martelli letter, sent from Florence on the 11th, reporting the rout of the party written 15. In a long conversation the king is torn between fear of the Swiss and anger at people who excuse themselves by fear of the English army: he being their sovereign lord, they had no regard for putting his kingdom in danger. The English king has put his bed and baggage aboard and lacks only his own person; [an unread word] went to Calais and [?embarked] there with part of the troops, the crossing split in three parts. The French have 2,500 lances and 14,000 Landsknechte to meet a Swiss attack, to be placed in Champagne and Burgundy. The king tells Acciaiuoli to write that he is secure and will fight if the Swiss move. The prelates leave for Rome after All Saints, and the king is disposed to please the pope in everything. Texts: [Italian](reading-it.md) · [English](translation-en.md).

Historical context. Sanudo's *Diarii*, vol. XVII, cols. 321-324, has a separate report of Bourbon assigned to Burgundy with 900 men-at-arms and 10,000 infantry, and prints a Florentine letter of 28 August 1513 citing reports from "nostro Piero Martelli" at Vicenza, where the Venetians were routed on 7 October. That is the ground for taking 15 as the Venetians; it is a proposal, and 15 is printed as `[15]`. Desjardins and Canestrini, *Négociations*, vol. II, print Acciaiuoli's letter of 26 September 1513 and then one of 27 January 1514; this letter is not there.

## The system and the key

The cipher is the Florentine syllabary of pandolfini1514: a consonant sign with the vowel as a superscript figure (1-5 = u, o, i, e, a), bare vowel and consonant signs, doubled consonants, word signs, and numbers as code words. Three outside sources and this letter itself give the values, and `key.tsv` marks each row by source:

- **G120**: Pietro Domenico Gabbrielli's reconstruction of this cipher, no. 120 "Acciaioli (de) Roberto, 1512-13-14" in his nineteenth-century *Alfabeti ... del carteggio dei Dieci di Balìa*, vol. I (ASF; DECODE record R5807, catalogued one leaf off). Transcribed in `gabbrielli-120.md`. It gives the alphabet, the syllabary, the doubles, 35 word signs (36 with a pencilled *bene*), the nulls and thirteen numbers (2 Papa, 4 Re di Francia, 5 Re di Spagna, 6 Inghilterra, 8 Re di Portogallo, 20 Svizzeri, 22 Lanzichinecchi, 34, 64, 65, 66, 68, 78). The letter of 23 October 1513 lies outside the filze Gabbrielli worked from, so the key is independent of it. It is an archivist's reconstruction from letters deciphered at the time, not a chancery key sheet.
- **v6**: Dudout's pandolfini1514 key, which first opened the letter. Almost all of its values used here are also in G120.
- **190/191**: docs. 190 and 191 of the same filza are Acciaiuoli's cipher letter of the same day to Giuliano de' Medici and its contemporary copy in clear. Aligned sign by sign, they fix the beta-like e, the closed-loop o, the pi-like p, the barred long s, the barred circle x and several word signs. On held-out signs whose clear word was read before decoding, the corrected key gives the clear's letters at 84% to 94% (93 signs). The calibration is `190-calibration.md`, with `190-transcription.txt`, `191-clear.txt` and the alignment `190-alignment.tsv`; the crops and the scorer it names are in the linked edition, not here.
- **192**: values that rest on sense within this letter: a plain cross for n, an E-like sign for e, and *ora* for a G with a raised tick (plain G is *ora* in G120).

Gabbrielli's two other keys for Acciaioli, no. 89 (1505-12) and no. 111 (1511-12), are different ciphers and give nothing for the open numbers.

`192-literal.txt` is the unedited output of the key on the transcription (`python3 decode.py --raw`). The reading departs from it at 20 declared places (`192-repairs.tsv`), each marked in brackets in `reading-it.md` and counted as a hole: the sign 2 read b (14 places), the triangle read as the word sign *bene* (twice), and four single signs (*vostr[e]*, *trov[a]ndosi*, *f[o]rza*, *[f]ran[z]ese*). The second values are in `key.tsv` as rows of kind `second`, which the decoder does not apply. The figures of *8 cento* and *2 mila 5 cento* are written `=8`, `=2`, `=5` in the transcription and come out of the key as numerals. `192-reading-rows.txt` gives the reading row by row against the manuscript lines, with every unread group shown sign by sign.

The sign 2. G120 has a plain 2 for b and a z-like 2 for c, and both forms are on the page: a squat 2 with a hooked head and a long base, and a tall z. `sign2_forms.tsv` lists what four readers saw at each of the 67 places, from crops with no reading and no key. On the first criterion (top of the sign only, tight crops) the forms did not follow the readings: both readers saw a curved head at 7 of the 14 places read b and at 10 of the 53 read c. On a second criterion, written with that result known (whole sign against its neighbours), both readers saw the squat form at 12 of the 14 b places and at 4 of the 53 c places. The bar set before the second survey allowed 3, so no token is renamed and the 14 stay holes. Two of the four are *pericolo* (R4) and *ricept[o]* (R21), which are c beyond doubt. The other two are in words not read: *deli[bera]ti* at V14, where b is what the sense wants, and the unread group *c‹o x›* at V2. Of the b places, *du[b]itare* (V5) was seen tall by both and *distri[b]uire* (R2) doubtful by both.

Two superscripts. The mark over the y of *f[o]rza* (R6) and over the 9 of *trov[a]ndosi* (R3) was read by two further instances from the mark alone, among twelve other superscripts of y and 9 as controls (all twelve read as transcribed, one doubtful by one reader). R3 is a 1 to both, so the key gives *trovundosi*. R6 is not the flat tilde of a 2 to either (both lean to 5), so *forza* is not the key's output. Both stay marked.

## How it was checked

- Two blind transcriptions of all 41 lines by fresh model instances given shape names and no key: decoded with the same key, their signs give the printed letters at 85% of the signs counted as read. A second pair re-read the thirteen hardest lines from tighter crops; where both agreed against the key-aware pass their sign was usually adopted, which opened V8-10.
- Internal recurrence: *distribuire le gvernigione* (R2) and *distribuito el passaggio* (V4); *gvernigione* again at R22; *passaggi-* with the double-g sign at R4 and V4.
- Codex's review against the manuscript and key images confirmed Bourbon and Burgundy, the troop figures, Martelli and the rout, the king's complaint, Calais and the three-part crossing, the core of the king's message, All Saints and the closing prayer, and corrected *nientedimeno* (R19), *perché* (R11), *facto* and *passaggio* (R4, V4).
- The first key-aware transcription was not hashed before decoding.

## Write-up escalation, 24 Nov 2026

Made for the maintainers by an AI agent (Claude) while writing the page, from the archive's public images (1272 px) and the sibling folders. Nothing here changes the reading, the count or the key files: each result is a proposal, and the words stay holes.

- **26 = Arciduca.** The nomenclator of Dudout's key (`targets/pandolfini1514/key_v6.json`; the value is already in his frozen key v3, learnt from the glossed and paired letters of 1514-15) has 26 = *arciduca*. At R12 the signs are two bare figures, 2 6, then *et*, then 4 7, with no raised figure on any of them, as numbers are written elsewhere in the letter. With that value the king's anger is "contro a [Arciduca] et [47]", and his words "sendo io loro soprano signore" fit. Not counted: the transcription keeps 2 6 as two signs, which the key reads as letters, and the value comes from the later letters. 47 is not in G120, in v6 or in pandolfini1515.
- **15.** Not in v6. `targets/pandolfini1515` has one .15., beside the Swiss as the King's ally in September 1515 ("havendo il Cristianissimo seco li [15] et li Svizeri"), where the contributor of that folder also thought of the Venetians. On doc. 191 (image 79572) the word struck out under *Rubertet* at line 16, where the cipher of doc. 190 has 15, begins with what looks like a v and is about nine letters long; the strike hides the rest at 1272 px. *Venitiani* would fit both, and neither is a key source. 15 stays `[15]`.
- **F with a raised mark = essere?** At R13 and V05 the F sign carries a small raised c-like mark, the same on both crops. In doc. 190 (verso, line 3) an F with a small raised circle stands where the clear copy 191 has *essere* ("doverrete essere avisati", `190-alignment.tsv`). *Essere* reads at both places: "col dire d'essere suti forzati" (R13) and "le forze di Sua Maestà in tale essere che non ha da dubitare" (V05). G120 gives *effetto* for F with a raised 0. The mark cannot be told apart at 1272 px, so both stay holes (`[?d'essere]`, `[?effetto]`).
- **B with a raised 1.** Not in G120, v6 or the 190/191 table. Nothing found.
- **Print.** The OCR text of Desjardins and Canestrini, vol. II (archive.org `gri_33125010469852`) has Acciaiuoli's letter from Amiens of 26 September 1513 (pp. 588-589), then a letter from Malines of 20 November 1513; no letter of 23 October 1513.
- **The leaf and the key sources, recounted.** The recto opens with a short address in clear (the catalogue vetting of 13 Nov 2026 read "Mag.ce Vir"; seen again on image 79574), then 22 lines of cipher; the verso has 19 lines of cipher and the clear closing. By the source column of `key.tsv` the 1,887 signs split as G120 1,774, 190/191 50, v6 33, 192 21 (the plain cross 6, the E-like sign 3, *ora* 3, the number 15 twice, seven plain numerals) and no value 9.
- **Key replay tried, not kept.** An adapter for `docs/_replay.py` (the raw key on the transcription against `reading-it.md`, numerals spelled alike on both sides) was run once. It gave 653 of 752 words of the reading as the key's output, 64 that differ with a mark and 24 unread, close to the count of the reading file, which is stricter (it counts *desidera[+]* and the numbers as holes). It also flagged 11 places as unmarked. Each was looked at in the alignment: eight sit beside an unread group whose one mark covers several signs (R05, V01, V02, V13, V14-15), one is the number 15, which `key.tsv` values and the reading leaves open, and two are a supplied word or letter that repeats its neighbour (*pare [bene]*, *via [a]*), where the aligner takes the wrong one of two equal letters. None is a change the reading fails to mark. The adapter is not in the folder, because the tool would report a failure that is the aligner's.
- **corsi1513.** Giovanni Corsi's letters of the same weeks are in another key (vowel figures 1, 3, 5, 7, 9); nothing applies here.

## Relation to pandolfini1514 (one key, one entry)

This folder holds one letter in one cipher. As far as the two key files can be compared, it is the cipher of `targets/pandolfini1514` and `targets/pandolfini1515`: Francesco Pandolfini's letters from the French court of 1514-15 use the same syllabary, word signs and numbers (2 papa, 4 Cristianissimo, 6 Inghilterra, 20 Svizzeri in both). By the sign names of the two files, v6 gives the same value as `key.tsv` for 1,690 of the 1,887 signs of this letter, another value for 31 (23 of them the barred long s, f in v6 and ss here) and none for 166 (signs v6 does not name, 67 of them this hand's tall a). The two transcribers' shape names were matched by description, so the count is indicative. They differ at 64 (*Santità* by context in v6, S. Severino in G120 and in docs. 190/191) and at V with a raised 0 (nothing in v6, *tempo* in G120 and 190/191). The targets stay apart because the basis differs: pandolfini1514 is Dudout's key rebuilt from pairs and tested on held-out pairs; this letter, of another writer and an earlier year, takes its values from Gabbrielli's key for Acciaioli.
