# Poupet de La Chaulx to Charles V, Vitoria, 28 May 1522 (DECODE R1187)

Status: read (contemporary decipherment bound in; checked against the cipher)

Catalogue entry: "Charles Poupet de Lachaulx to Charles I", BNE MSS/20212/39/1-2, DECODE R1187, rule-scored
class B, "what the cipher hides is not known". Worked 21 Sept 2026.

## What the record is

- 11 DECODE images (double openings), fetched with the bordeaux cookie into `decode/img/` (git-ignored; BNE
  images, not public domain). Record page saved as `decode/rec1187.htm`.
- **39/1** (images 1-8): the original letter, in **French**, not Spanish, signed *Lachaulx*, dated at the end
  "Escript en vostre ville de Victoire le xxviij{me} de may 1522" (image 8). The DECODE date 1 Jan 1522 is a
  placeholder (1522-01-01 to 1522-12-31). Pencil note on f.1: "Vitoria 28 de Mayo 1522". Endorsed (image 9
  left) "A l'empereur ... 1522 de Victoria ... de la Chaulx". Gayangos stamp.
- Cipher: homophonic substitution in graphic signs and digits (`o+`, `ꝑ`, `m4`, `Ꝛc`, `ɗo`...), French
  plaintext, no word division. Five cipher passages, each marked in the margin by a letter:
  **A** (img 2 right, foot, running to img 3 left head), **b** (img 3 left foot, running over the whole of img 3
  right), **C** (img 4 left), **D** (img 5 right, continuing on img 6 left), **E** (img 7 left). A few signs carry
  interlinear glosses by the decipherer (img 4 left: "que sont quatre mille hommes"; img 7 left).
- **39/2** (images 9 right to 11 left): the **contemporary decipherment**, in a different French hand, keyed by
  the same margin letters A, b, C, D, E. This is DECODE's "Inline Plaintext: Yes".

So the cipher was read in 1522. Nothing is unread in the sense of the catalogue.

## Check

The decipherment was aligned against the first cipher line of A (img 2 right):
`E o+ o φ 2 ꝑ 3 | m4 7 o | Ꝛc ꝭ | o+ ▷ c ɗo ʆ ɗ ꝑ ...` against *Combien | que | je | vouldroie ...*: seven
signs for *combien*, three for *que*, two for *je*, e written with three different signs (homophones; caveat: as typed here the glyph o stands for both m and e and o+ for both o and v, probably look-alike shapes typed alike, not rechecked on the image). The
sign count and the letter repeats fit. The key was **not** rebuilt in full: that would need a careful
transcription of ~1,500 signs from 1024-px scans, and the letter is read already.

## Content (from the decipherment, graded M)

- **A**: however much he would stake his soul that the Emperor is the person in the world the Pope loves most,
  once at Rome Adrian will be solicited and worked on from so many sides that they may turn him from something
  he cannot then easily withdraw from.
- **b**: the Pope's departure. Plague at Barcelona, so the fleet must gather at Tarragona or Cartagena; the
  Pope waits for the galleys of Naples; the ships from here are ready by the end of the month; he will not
  leave before mid-June or July; he means to take 4,000 men (rumour says 20,000); "unless you see the Pope
  before he leaves, you will not see him for a long time".
- **C**: money and the Cortes; Madame Catherine (the Emperor's sister) and the King of Portugal; the Queen.
- **D**, **E**: the news from France and the Swiss, the King of England's part, the Landsknechts and the
  Italian army; Bayonne. Read in the decipherment, summarised here only.

Printed? Gachard, *Correspondance de Charles-Quint et d'Adrien VI* (1859), prints letters of this mission, but
from Brussels/Vienna copies; not checked whether this letter is among them. Not found online.
