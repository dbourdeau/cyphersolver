# Robert R. Livingston to John Jay, Philadelphia, 28 Nov 1781: "Make this addition to your cypher"

Status: read (existing decipherment: the draft's glosses and Jay's own key sheet; the edition had missed both)

Catalogue 636 (class A). Worked 27 Oct 2026.

## The item

Livingston's first official letter to Jay as Secretary for Foreign Affairs, carried by Lafayette. It is printed
in *Selected Papers of John Jay* 2: 644-46 (Founders Online Jay/01-02-02-0272). The coded passages of the body
are given there decoded from the draft. The last line is printed only as figures:

> Make this addition to your cypher: 69. 226–600; 538. 226–601; 346. 226–602; 216. 226–603; 209. 226–604; 259. 226–605.

Note 7 there says the line is "an attempt to simplify the Thomson code ... adding six symbols for syllables
ending in r" and that it "is undecipherable, as explained in JJUP, 1: 664". JJUP is R. B. Morris, *John Jay: The
Making of a Revolutionary* (1975). It is not online and was not consulted.

Witnesses (Founders source note): ALS NHi Jay (EJ 598), docketed "Recd. 24 Feb. 1782 from marqs. de la Fayette
— not decd."; triplicate LS NHi, received 15 Mar.; duplicate and quadruplicate LSs NNC (EJ 7763, 7925); draft
NHi R. R. Livingston Papers (EJ 1537); letterbook copies PCC item 79, 1: 332-34 and item 118, 48-50.

## The codes

- **Thomson's code** (Weber WE007): a printed form of about 600 words and syllables, with the numbers written
  in by hand. Franks carried Jay's copy, which survives at Columbia (Jay Papers, ldpd:50144, "Thomson cypher,
  11 July 81, as modified by Livingston 6 July 1782"), two pages.
- **The lost copy**: Thomson gave Livingston a second copy of the same printed form, numbered differently, by
  mistake. It was meant for Jay but went by William Palfrey, who was lost at sea. Livingston wrote the 20 Oct
  private letter and the ALS of 28 Nov in it. Jay read neither. Livingston explains this in his letter of 9 May
  1782 (Founders Jay/01-02-02-0344): "my second by the Marquis De la Fayette, is in a cypher delivered to me by
  mistake, by Mr. Thompson, and lost with Colonel Palfrey". In the same letter he says his letters "only notes a
  slight alteration in Mr. Thompson's cypher". That slight alteration is this line. The later LSs and the
  letterbook copies were re-encoded in WE007.

## Reading

`transcription.txt` (17 cipher tokens, measured), `reading.md`.

> Make this addition to your cypher: be, 600; ce, 601; de, 602; fe, 603; ge, 604; he, 605.

Three witnesses agree:

1. **The draft** writes the pairs one to a line, with a gloss under each: *Be, Ce, De, Fe, Ge, he*. Be and Ce
   are clear. The lower four are smudged in the photostat but can be read in that order. On their own they would
   be grade C; Jay's sheet, which is clear, raises all six to H.
2. **Jay's own copy of the Thomson code** has the same six syllables entered by hand against 600-605. A first
   "he — 604" is overwritten before "he — 605". The WE007 duplicate (ldpd:50588) ends at the signature without the
   addition, so Jay did not take it from there. The sheet is headed "as modified by Livingston 6 July 1782", and
   Livingston's letter of 6 July 1782 (NNC, EJ 90354; not seen here) is the likely source. The entries
   are independent of the draft either way: they give the same six syllables in the same order.
3. **The ALS** has "to your cypher" interlined over 398, 264, 23.473.370.

Each pair spells the syllable letter by letter: b, c, d, f, g, h followed by e = 226. The printed Thomson form
has no entry for bare *be, ce, de, fe, ge, he*, so the addition fills a real gap. The editors' "syllables ending
in r" is a misreading of what 226 stands for. The r-syllables (ar 76, er 86, ir 150, or 382, ur 331 in WE007)
are already in the form.

17 of 17 cipher tokens read. Nothing is open.

## Values of the lost code

The line gives b 69, c 538, d 346, e 226, f 216, g 209, h 259, to 398, your 264, and cypher 23.473.370 (three
groups). The 19th-c. copy of the 20 Oct passage (ldpd:32488) adds more by alignment: not 203 (twice), will 175,
for 235, his 139 (twice), public 151, when 172, against 477. The copyist's figures drift against the words, so
the rest of that copy is not aligned here. One conflict to note: 473 also stands under *thing* there. Either that
alignment is off, or *cypher* does not divide as cy·ph·er. The editors have this code in part already ("Decoding
based on Dft, with additional decoding by the editors"), so it is not a new reconstruction.

## Prior work

Not in print as a reading: the edition prints the figures and calls them undecipherable (n. 7). The decipherment
was in the archive all along: Livingston's draft and Jay's key sheet. Neither is mentioned in the edition's note.
Searched: Founders Online (letter, editorial note "John Jay's Use of Codes and Ciphers", 9 May 1782 letter),
Columbia's Jay Papers catalogue, web search. Tomokiyo's cryptiana has no page on this line (web search).

## Method

Existing decipherment: the draft's interlinear glosses and Jay's key-sheet entry give the whole line. The work was
finding them, transcribing the ALS line, and checking the three witnesses against each other and against the
printed Thomson form.

## Brice Dudout: independent reading of the addition line (credit added 4 Nov 2026)

On 27 Oct 2026, the same day and without this folder, Brice Dudout's session read the line by another route. It
aligned the ALS (the lost Palfrey numbering) word by word on the triplicate (Thomson code, whose key survives). The
letter's own text gives 226 = e (re-ception, met, finesse), 69 = b ("can be"), 346 = d (pride) and 398 = to. Hence
be 600, de 602 and the e of all six, with c, f, g, h from the order and from Livingston's entries on Jay's key sheet
(ldpd:50144 p. 2). It also found that the syllables end in e, not r. The result is the same as this folder's reading
from the draft's glosses, which has priority. Credited `dudout`, role "verification". A second fragment on ALS p. 3
("500 need 203, 2?4 = 90.149, 193.51.221") was not read; only 149 = er is sure.
