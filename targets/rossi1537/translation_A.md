# Letter A, 4 Nov 1537: English translation

## Translation

[c. 31r] I am writing this after supper, so Your Excellency will [?hold] me excused if I am [...] brief, for besides having drunk very well of good muscatel and [?vernaccia], [?fra] l'Angiolino is waiting for me, so that the wine does not let me think of a subject, nor, if I thought of one, could I write it. Already [...] failed, for I had forgotten to say "subject". Think [?how things go]; but in spite of the wine I will say how the valiant French [?datem] [...] in this world and [...] Astolfo, who even to you had written, all through Italy, that he would fight, with the consent of our Zopo; such is the [?report], that he has withdrawn, whether [?captains] or camp-[?master], to preserve an army. The first retreat was of 20 miles; this next one will be of 40, unless your successor, the most unconquered stradiot with his beard, holds them. The good botiglioni come on shouting Tedam, Tedam, with so much noise that they [?would] frighten [?Vicencio] the Venetian if he were alive. Consider that Astolfo did not stand firm, and the first news I expect to hear is that he has ridden post to ask for money, fleeing on this [?occasion] the fearsome and [...] frightful Gallic cries, though to his [...] which to others would be a music in part-song.

[c. 31v] The great Cesare Pallavicino went by the post, quick quick, to the Marquis, to be present at this glorious retreat, one to set beside that [...]. You understand me: can it be that you have forgotten the one of [...]? I have said it after all. I am talking comedy this evening, and if one more glass were added I would go on to add the departure from Genoa in the month of December: it was not a flight, or we would not have gone to the mole to embark, and Monfalconeto had already advised how the business should be handled. I do not know whether you were at the council; still, you ought to remember it. What the devil am I saying? I have got into the cases of "fuit" and had not noticed. Let the excess of wine excuse me with you, and my not having to do business with Covos. I have spoken as a free person. I make an end, and from day to day, as long as these French swine do not fail in their duty, I will keep you informed of the cowardices of Astolfo, which I believe must be greater [?than] the wretchednesses of Covos.

> [clear, c. 31v] Having thought that by now Your Excellency will have had the cipher, I have written this, in which you will find something. And kissing your hands humbly I commend myself to you. From San Secondo, the 4th of November 1537. Your Excellency's
