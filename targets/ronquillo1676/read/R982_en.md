# R982, Ronquillo to Fuenmayor, Nijmegen, 19 Mar 1678

⟨... with which the favours Your Lordship does me continue.⟩ [...] I have read with particular pleasure Your
Lordship's reflections and what [...] moving towards the steps, which are very much in keeping with Your Lordship's
prudence, zeal and energy. And would to God our misfortunes were not so overwhelming, or that we found ourselves in a
position to attend to them all; but as they are of the bad kind Your Lordship [?will have] understood, we must resign
ourselves to the will of God and ascribe to His just judgements that Flanders should run [...] to the last despair,
which, I assure Your Lordship, leaves us all so astonished and taken by surprise that even the pleasure of discussing
with the [...] fails us, [...] the grief and the thought of our misfortune.

England still remains in the same ambiguity and irresolution we experienced two months ago, as Your Lordship will have
been informed by the [...]. So, seeing the critical point [...] the present state of affairs, and the danger and
evident risk in which the Low Countries, and in consequence the rest of the North, stand after the loss of Ghent, we
may hope, unless God in His mercy lets us suffer the final punishment, that our allies, setting aside their private
quarrels, will apply themselves wholly to defending us and all of them to defending themselves against the advances of
France. To that end, and so that the Brunswick princes should not persist in [...] of their claims, the news we
[?have] of the incursion that Count Königsmarck has made into Mecklenburg will also weigh heavily; and to this bad
state of affairs is added the talk that, as Your Lordship will have learnt from other correspondents, the [al] are
already making, who cannot help foreseeing the worst consequences. So in every way we are in a very bad position. I
pass these melancholy reflections on to Your Lordship not only because they belong to [...] but [...], because, the
courts of [...], The Hague and Brussels having taken over the [...] of these hands, there is nothing I can offer to
tell Your Lordship about the congress.
