# R984, Ronquillo to Fuenmayor, Nijmegen, 26 Feb 1678

⟨... with which I beg Your Lordship to favour me.⟩ I have read with particular attention all that Your Lordship tells
me in your said letters about the matters treated in that assembly of the northern allies and the steps taken in
it. And since everything hangs on the resolutions of England, of which Your Lordship will have been informed in
advance by the letters from The Hague, we must expect things to change their aspect everywhere, if those awaited
from that court are for a break with France. But I, from the knowledge I have gained of that kingdom and parliament,
and knowing that the King is absolute master of it and of everything else, [?shall not] yet venture any firm judgement
either of peace or of war until we have surer and more certain signs of the deliberations of His Britannic Majesty,
although [135] that the whole disposition is for war, and that we see Parliament minded to engage the King in it.

Your Lordship acted very wisely, and very much in keeping with your [138], in trying to prevent the ministers of the
allies who reside at that [113] from making the threat they had resolved on to the States General. It might be that, had they
carried it out, the Dutch would have seconded it in such a way that it would have stung them well, as happened with
the other envoy of Denmark, of which Your Lordship will be informed. And I promise Your Lordship that, if it comes to the
King of England breaking, they may very well tame their anger and their eagerness to threaten, for if they find
themselves alone, with the King of Sweden in Pomerania as he will be, they will do well enough to defend themselves
and look to their own safety, without putting forward [dun] novelties every day. Here we too suffer much
mortification from them and bear much impertinence, as they do not declare themselves satisfied with the remittances
that have come from Spain for their masters, particularly the Danish [460], who goes so far as to say that they can do
nothing with them because they have come in monthly instalments. So everywhere these [cus] give us trouble, and while
we [?finish] with [...] as regards the main point of the negotiation of the congress, the labour of having to
listen to them, maintain them and [90] them is very considerable.

Your Lordship has done me a particular favour in pointing out to me the intrigue of the Duke of Hanover and the
marriage of the Count of [...] with the [?daughter] of the Duchess of Celle, for this person has been [?here] more than two months,
excusing his stay by saying that he could not take service in the war on account of religion. With what Your Lordship
tells me I can cast my nets, for, as I told Your Lordship, I had understood some days ago that Marshal d'Estrades
[?was dealing] with the Duke of Hanover by way of the kinship he has with Madame de [?Arvor]; and [...] that a week
from today I can tell Your Lordship something firm about this plot.
