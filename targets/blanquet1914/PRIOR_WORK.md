# Witnesses and prior work

*Update, 22 Nov 2026: Narváez's 2019 book, described below as unavailable or unchecked, has since been checked (chapters VII-XI, pp. 261-410, and the index, pp. 487-495). It prints neither the three telegrams nor Clave núm. 109. See NOTES.md.*

Brice Dudout deciphered three Rubio Navarrete drafts to the Minister of War with the card named “Clave núm. 109. Secretaría de Guerra y Marina, con el General Guillermo Rubio Navarrete,” CEHM DLXXIII.1.28.14-23. The published positive-control witnesses were dated 18 July 1913, 13 March 1914 and 18 March 1914. A separate interlined draft and contemporary typed copy supplied his test. These existing readings take precedence over any historical claim. Our comparison uses the unaltered file targets/rubio1913/key.tsv from the previously preserved checkout, SHA-256 77cb440bedac8e21df5109cae12528867ea9f60d72d6aee358c2bf66fdb9302d. Dudout's prose mentions a different frozen-source hash prefix, 9adb1190. The precise representation used for that older hash was not located. All 160 official numbered rows in the supplied TSV are retained. [Repository target](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/tree/main/targets/rubio1913)

The Mexican archive survey, catalogue 1468, already lists both held-out shelfmarks. The CEHM calendar describes the 19 May 1914 manuscript from [México] to Guillermo Rubio Navarrete as “en clave” and attributes it to [Aureliano] Blanquet. Its second page ends “Blanquet.” A military departmental key naming the addressee merits prospective transfer to this reverse-direction telegram. The archive record supplies no decipherment. [CEHM DLXXIII.2.41.22-41, record 138008](https://www.cehm.org.mx/Buscador/FichaBasica/138008?Tipo=A)

The CEHM dates the 25 March 1914 typescript at Zacatecas, addressed to Victoriano Huerta and signed [Guillermo Rubio Navarrete], and describes it as incomplete. One digital sheet was recovered; a 404 for the next image does not establish the fate of a physical continuation. Dudout previously classified it as presidential-code traffic different from the War Ministry table, without printing a reading. Comparing the actual table is a check of that classification. The clear railway correspondence resembles an earlier War Ministry telegram, but does not establish an identical key. [CEHM DLXXIII.2.41.17-41, record 138003](https://www.cehm.org.mx/Buscador/FichaBasica/138003?Tipo=A)

The 2019 Narváez monograph, *Criptografía diplomática, política y militar en México 1813–1926*, and other unindexed editions or calendars were not consulted here. Princeton's machine-readable catalogue records a first edition in Spanish, Ciudad de México, Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores, 2019, 495 pages, ISBN 9786074461527, OCLC 1280354387. Its bibliographical references occupy pp. 475–485 and the volume has an index. Call number F1231.5 .R15 2019 is in the Firestone stacks. The catalogue supplies no text or shelfmark-level entry. The locally retained response from the publisher URL is a Radware loader. For comparison with the volume, the 19 May incoming telegram is CEHM DLXXIII.2.41.22-41 (138008), dated 19 May 1914, signed Blanquet, with the phrase “abstenerse de hacer indicaciones”. The neighboring 25 March comparison is DLXXIII.2.41.17-41 (138003), addressed to Huerta. The index terms Rubio Navarrete, Blanquet, Téllez and Secretaría de Guerra y Marina would distinguish the people, archive and key from a publication of a particular text. Priority remains open. [Princeton catalogue](https://catalog.princeton.edu/catalog/99120513173506421)

Roberto Narváez’s “Sobre algunos criptosistemas mexicanos de principios del siglo XX” (2015) uses CEHM material. Its 26 pages and seven figures cover Magonista alphabets, Argumedo’s October 1921 letters to Félix Díaz, Wellington’s alphabetic shift cipher, and an English letter with alphabets sent to Carranza. The identified CEHM witnesses belong to the Félix Díaz and Carranza papers. In CEHM 138008, War Ministry Clave 109 renders *abstenerse* from 50 · 70 · 88 · 159 · 60 · 86 · 145. The article identifies neither this telegram nor its key. [Narváez, 2015, pp. 118–132](https://doi.org/10.5209/rev_DOCU.2015.v13.49742)

A subsequent check covered the text and all 20 figures of Roberto R. Narváez’s 2025 article on Constitutionalist ciphers (pp. 841–876). Its numerical tables in figs. 10 and 12–16 name other correspondents. Figs. 19–20 reproduce the two Washington drafts by Urquidi and Pesqueira, deciphered by transposition. The article never identifies CEHM 138008 or prints the Blanquet–Rubio passage *abstenerse de hacer indicaciones*. None of its five citations of the author’s 2019 book identifies this telegram. The book’s chapter IX, footnotes, illustrations and name index still require inspection. [Narváez, full 2025 article and figures](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/600/60082677009/60082677009.pdf)
