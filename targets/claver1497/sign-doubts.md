1. Individual verification of the five provisional (V) and three unresolved (U) signs

The form numbers below count from left to right within each row of Parisi's alphabet no. 2 (2004, p. 73). The nomenclator references are to p. 72.

G11 → i (V, 26 occurrences)
Closest published form: i/j, form 1.

The manuscript shows a rho-like loop with a long descender, without a consistently visible lower cross-stroke. This limits the graphical identification, since uncrossed forms in the a row also show some resemblance. Nevertheless, the value i works consistently across all 26 occurrences, including both archiduque and both alli groups. The graphical comparison and decoder output support retaining the provisional reading.

G13 → s (V, 12 occurrences)
Closest published form: s, form 1.

The baseline and two uprights correspond to the printed form, although the manuscript's uprights are less compact. I also compared the potentially confusable h2 and r4 forms. The value s remains consistent in all 12 occurrences, including senior, cosas and quales.

G23 → h (V, 4 occurrences)
Closest published form: h, form 2.

The connected arches over a baseline resemble the printed underlined, m-like form, although compressed in the manuscript. This distinguishes G23 from G13's separate uprights. All four readings are consistent: dicha, echar, archiduque and honrra. The other occurrence of archiduque uses G16, corresponding to h1.

G25 → n (V, 1 occurrence, L03)
Working identification: n, form 2, also represented by G37.

The manuscript shows a compact, closed double loop, more 8-like than the printed 6-like form. It also resembles some forms in the o row, so the graphical identification remains qualified. I compared G37 and the second occurrence of gente in L07, which uses the different n-homophone G05. The contextual and decoder evidence supports n, but I would retain the provisional classification.

G39 → y (V, 1 occurrence, L09)
Closest published form: y, form 1.

The manuscript shows a compact crossed and hooked form, with its components more closely joined than in G01. Comparison with the three G01 occurrences supports the reading. Its position as the conjunction between duque and le provides additional contextual support. G39 and G01 remain separate analytical identifiers.

G31 (U, 1 occurrence, L03)
Proposed identification: probable a, form 3 (G09), joined to the preceding G12 (d3).

I find your interpretation persuasive. The apparent upper part of the previously unidentified 8-like sign may belong to the preceding d stroke, leaving the oval a. I compared the separate G12–G09 sequence in aiuda on the same line.

A temporary decoder substitution produces ademandar. I would therefore annotate G31 as probable a, completing demandar, while retaining the graphical uncertainty.

G40 (U, 1 occurrence, L10)
Proposed identification: probable ba = rey de los Romanos, Parisi's nomenclator no. 2 (p. 72).

The visible a, preceding ascender and damaged bowl are consistent with the intact ba/G34 in L06. The tear prevents a complete graphical comparison, but the surviving features and context support your interpretation.

A temporary substitution produces el rey de los Romanos procure estas cosas. I agree that ba is probable, while retaining the manuscript-damage qualification.

G42 (U, 1 occurrence, L11)
Possible identification: n, form 1 (G05).

The surviving s-like contour resembles G05, but its left edge meets the tear. Testing n produces only […]nale, without establishing the complete word or its beginning.

I would therefore retain n? as a possible value and leave the word unresolved.

2. Extent of the lacuna in L11

Comparison with the neighbouring left margins supports a loss of approximately two sign-widths, assuming normal alignment.

This is consistent with your estimate of at most one short word. However, the physical extent does not establish a word boundary: the missing material may include the beginning of the surviving word.

I recommend retaining […] in the transcription and documenting the approximate physical extent without supplying conjectural letters.

3. The historical form sopiendo

I agree that sopiendo should count as read.

The existing key mechanically decodes:

"G20 G06 G07 G11 G04 G05 G12 G06 → sopiendo"

The manuscript and the key therefore support this exact spelling.

There is also independent historical-linguistic support. Pato and O'Neill, Los gerundios «analógicos» en la historia del español (e iberorromance) (2013), p. 4, explicitly discuss medieval sopiendo, citing Penny (2002), p. 215.

I therefore recommend preserving sopiendo without emendation. The earlier suggestion sabiendo was only a contextual conjecture and should not replace the documented reading.
