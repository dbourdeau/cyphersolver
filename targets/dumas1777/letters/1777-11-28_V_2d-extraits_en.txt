"Second extracts of my correspondence with the Courier du Bas-Rhin. From the first letters he wrote me I had reason to suspect that he passed mine on, whole or in extracts, directly or indirectly, to a certain Court. ... He answered me last month as follows: 'Your project, on Emden, pleased me very much ... I will make a memorial of all this, which I will have delivered into the King's own hands through persons who approach him every day and with whom I am in touch. I know that he has the strongest wish to found a navy; that is what all his aims tend to today.'"
"Meanwhile I had learned from our gentlemen that Mr. Lee had gone to Berlin." ... "He is quite right to want to found a navy; that alone is lacking to complete his power ... Let American privateers be free to enter Emden, for example, to bring in and sell their prizes there ..." ... "Besides, if they [the jealous powers] do it [blockade the port], it will be up to you to seize Bremen, Stade, etc., which you would do well to keep."

Whose correspondence: the deciphered heading names the "Courier du Bas-Rhin", the French gazette printed at Cleves, in Prussian territory; its editor (Jean Manzon, by outside knowledge, not named on the page) is Dumas's correspondent. The "certain Court" and "le Roi" are Prussian: Frederick II, Emden (Prussian East Frisia) as a port for American privateers, a Prussian navy, and Arthur Lee's journey to Berlin (June-July 1777). Bremen and Stade belonged to George III as Elector of Hanover.
