## Normalized text

"I am to set out after to-morrow for [— Texel,] with Letters & secret Instructions to [csmmodore jones e his squadron,] whose arrival there I expect every day."

As written, the numbers spell "csmmodore jones e his squadron". The contemporary decipherer of item 93 image 260 wrote S and E over 12 and 24. "Commodore" (12 read as o) is certain by sense and agrees with the print. The "&" (24 taken as 241 with a figure dropped) is a guess; "Jones' (e) his squadron" with a stray e is as possible. Sense either way: "[Commodore Jones (&?) his squadron]".

## Emendations

- 12 (s) in "C[o]mmodore": `{emended: 12 s -> o; no neighbour gives o (11 s, 13 e); 2 = o if the 1 is an extra figure}`. In both copies; the item 93 decipherer wrote S over it.
- 24 (e) after "Jones": `{emended: 24 e -> &, from 241 = & (final figure dropped)}`. Read as written: "Jones e his squadron". In both copies.
Run 1 begins with 7 = the hyphen, used as a dash before "Texel".

## Print check

**Printed with the cipher read** (the letter is in English): Wharton 3:333 (`print/wharton3_22269.txt`): "I am to set out after to-morrow for Texel with letters and secret instructions to Commodore Jones' squadron, whose arrival there I expect every day"; Sparks 9 (`print/sparks9_15952.txt`): "... secret instructions to Commodore Jones's squadron". Differences: our text has "Commodore Jones & his squadron" (after emending 24 to 241); the print has "Commodore Jones' squadron" (Wharton) / "Jones's squadron" (Sparks), which drops "&" and "his" or reads the letters "e his" as a possessive. "Commodore" in print agrees with our emendation 12 -> o. Otherwise agreement.

## Correction after the independent check (14 Nov 2026)

A second AI agent of ours checked image 260: the numbers are clearly 28 **12** 76 ... and 11 **24** 424, and the decipherer wrote S over
