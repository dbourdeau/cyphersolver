## Print check

Wharton vol. 4 is not in `web/` (only vols. 2 and 3), so Wharton could not be checked.
Sparks vol. 9 **prints the P.S. with the cipher passage omitted**: under the letter "To the President of Congress, The Hague, August 23d, 1781" (with continuations of Aug 24 and 30), lines 19808-19990 of `web/diplomaticcorres09sparuoft.txt`: "September 2d. A very interesting resolution of August 28th, of one of the principal cities of this Province, was received the day before yesterday by her Deputies here, of which the substance is as follows." The bracketed cipher sentence naming Dort is left out. Difference: Sparks dates the letter 23 Aug (its first part), where the manuscript copy is F6 of 30 Aug with this P.S.

## Additions after the independent check (14 Nov 2026)

- Wharton vol. 4 (checked by the second agent of ours in the Internet Archive OCR): Wharton 4:655-658, Dumas to the President of
  Congress, 23 Aug 1781, P.S. "September 2" on p. 657: "A very interesting resolution of August 28th, of one of the principal cities of
  this province, was received the day before yesterday by her deputies here, of which the substance is as follows". The cipher
  sentence is omitted, as in Sparks 9:464.
- The plain copy (item 93 vol. 1 images 488-489) was looked at on the image (14 Nov 2026, by the packaging agent): its P.S. "Sept. 2d"
  reads "A very interesting Resolution of Aug. 28th of one of the principal Cities of this province has been received the day before
  yesterday by her Deputies here, of which the substance is as following", with no cipher and no name of the city; the resolution
  itself speaks only of "this City". So the name Dort is given only by the 13 numbers on image 493, which carry no gloss.
