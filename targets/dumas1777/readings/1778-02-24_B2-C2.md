## Normalized text

"... Je vous dirai seulement, que j'ai trouvé moyen de [me procurer les dépêches des ministres de la Rép(ublique) dans les cours de l'Europe, & que j'en envoie les extraits régulièrement, —] à votre honorable Comission Plénipotentiaire en Europe. Dans les circonstances présentes cela ne peut pas leur être indifférent. J'ai tant prêché des amis dans certaine ville, qu'enfin je leur ai vu reprendre la hardiesse de leurs ancêtres. [Une maison hollandoise à Amst(erdam) a ouvert une souscription de dix mille pièces, pour envoyer quatre vaisseaux, chargés de premières nécessités, négocier directement avec votre continent. Il y a déjà trois mille pièces de souscrites.] J'ai écrit à leur réquisition à l'hon. Comission, pour demander des [listes pour les cargaisons, & autres directions. —] Les affaires en Allemagne sont toujours indécises ..."

## Translation

"I will only tell you that I have found means to procure the despatches of the ministers of the Republic at the courts of Europe, and that I send extracts of them regularly to your honourable Commission Plenipotentiary in Europe. ... I have preached so much to friends in a certain town that at last I have seen them take up again the boldness of their ancestors. A Dutch house at Amsterdam has opened a subscription of ten thousand pieces to send four ships, loaded with first necessities, to trade directly with your continent. Three thousand pieces are already subscribed. At their request I have written to the honourable Commission to ask for lists for the cargoes, and other directions."

## Print check

**Not found in print.** Wharton vol. 2 has no Dumas letter between 16 Dec 1777 and 14 April 1778; Sparks vol. 9 likewise. No Franklin Papers extract in `print/`.

Contemporary gloss compared with our reading. Counting each distinct number that carries a gloss (transcribers' readings of the glosses, both passes pooled): C2 image 9, 190 of 200 agree; image 10, 136 of 141; B2 image 143, 157 of 185; image 144, 118 of 135. On the image, the disagreements I checked are almost all the transcribers' alignment (a gloss set over the neighbouring number) or letter shape (the round d read as o, j as f, i as l), not the decipherer's. Real differences found:
- C2 image 9 L25: gloss d over 243, which is a in the key ("à Amst."): the decipherer erred (perhaps read "d'Amst").
- 298 in B2: decipherer wrote r, correcting the copyist.
- 410: decipherer of C2 wrote u, following Dumas's slip.
