## Normalized text

242: "... Il y a une cabale, [— banquiers genevois & suisses,] tant en France même qu'à Amst[erdam], amie de vos ennemis, qui décrie & nuit tant qu'elle peut sous le masque de l'amitié. Il étoit de mon devoir d'en démasquer quelques-uns à Mr. Franklin, & de lui faire connoître une maison non suspecte, anti-angloise, patriotique, ayant la confiance de la Magistrature d'Amst[erdam]. Le Ministère en France le sait. Ce sont vos amis, là & ici, qui m'ont fait agir; leur voix unanime est, [, — que J. de Neufville & fils mérit(ent)] seul [— votre confiance] à Amsterdam, que les précédents [ont été] de [faux amis.] On [trompe l'ambassadeur de F=]. Mais nous espérons de le détromper aussi ..."

239 / 253: "What I have [had] the honour to tell you in my Letters Q3, R3, May 17th, of [J. de Neufville & fils] must be so understood, that it not exceeds the mercantile line ..."

## Translation

242: "There is a cabal — Genevan and Swiss bankers — in France itself as well as at Amsterdam, friendly to your enemies ... It was my duty to unmask some of them to Mr Franklin and to make known to him a house above suspicion, anti-English, patriotic, having the confidence of the magistracy of Amsterdam. The ministry in France knows it. It is your friends, there and here, who made me act; their unanimous voice is that J. de Neufville & Son alone deserve your confidence at Amsterdam, that the previous ones were false friends. Someone is deceiving the ambassador of F[rance]. But we hope to undeceive him too ..." (The preference for de Neufville is given as the friends' unanimous voice, not as Dumas's own statement. "F=" is written with the key's hyphen sign, 223, as an abbreviation mark.)

## Print check

242 (May 1779): **printed with the cipher read in part, the rest omitted** (in translation). Wharton 3 (`print/wharton3_10623.txt`) and Sparks 9 (`print/sparks9_15484.txt`), both dated "The Hague, May 15, 1779". Printed: "There is a cabal of Genevan and Swiss bankers, as well in France as at Amsterdam, friendly to your enemies, which does as much injury as it can under the mask of friendship. It was my duty to unmask some of them to Dr. Franklin, and to make known to him a safe anti-English patriotic house, having the confidence of the magistracy of Amsterdam. The ministry in France know it." Ours: "une cabale, [banquiers genevois & suisses,] tant en France même qu'à Amst-". Agreement on the first run. Differences: (1) the print omits everything from "Ce sont vos amis ..." to "... de le détromper aussi" (runs 2-6: the friends' unanimous view that de Neufville alone deserves confidence, the previous houses false friends, "On trompe l'ambassadeur de F=") and goes on to "Upon the last petitions of the merchants of Dort ..."; (2) date: the manuscript reads "May 1779" with no day, and Dumas's English letters 239/253 call it "Q3, R3, May 17th"; the editors date it 15 May.

239 (S3/T3) and 253 (21 June 1779): **not found in print** (no Dumas letter of 21 June 1779 in Wharton 3 or Sparks 9; "mercantile line" occurs in Wharton only in the 20 Sept 1779 letter, where Neufville is called "a house very proper to deal with in the mercantile line").

## Remarks

Checked on the image: 84/89 and the struck numbers on 242; the 41 on 253. Numbers: 242 has 108, 239 and 253 have 17 each. ## Corrections after the independent check (14 Nov 2026)

A second AI agent of ours transcribed image 242 on its own (`transcriptions/second_agent/trans_1779-05.txt`) with the same 108 numbers
and the same reading. Two points of wording are applied above: (1) the preference for "J. de Neufville & fils" is reported as "leur voix
unanime", the view of "vos amis, là & ici", not as Dumas's own statement; (2) the cipher says "On trompe l'ambassadeur de F=", which is
quoted as it stands (the earlier summary "l'Ambassadeur de France est trompé" was a paraphrase). "seul" is a plain word of the letter
between runs 2 and 3. The blotted figure in "trompe" (read 89; the second agent 84 corrected to 82 or 89) gives e either way.
