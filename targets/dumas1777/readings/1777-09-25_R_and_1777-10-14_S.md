## Normalized text

R (after the signature): "[Le paquet, L, est dans le vaisseau pris.]"

S: "... Nos États ont envoyé une Instruction à ce sujet à leur Envoyé à Londres ... On est impatient ici d'apprendre la réponse de l'Angleterre. [Le paquet, L, est dans le vaisseau pris.] La seconde besogne ..."

## Translation

"Packet L is in the captured ship." In S the sentence follows the account of the Dutch ship bound for St. Eustatius that an English warship took in the Channel (the vessel the States-General were reclaiming): Dumas is telling the Committee that his packet L was on board that prize.

## Print check

R, 25 Sept 1777: **not found in print.** Wharton vol. 2 has no Dumas letter between 22 Aug and 14 Oct 1777 and says in a note (`web/revolutionarydip021889unit.txt` line 21851) that other letters are omitted "as without public importance ... (in this respect following Sparks)". Sparks vol. 9 has none either.

S, 14 Oct 1777: **printed with the cipher passage omitted** (in translation): Wharton 2:407-408 (`web/revolutionarydip021889unit.txt` lines 23491-23540) and Sparks 9:329-330 (`web/diplomaticcorres09sparuoft.txt` lines 14241-14299). Both print "...and to its conventions with Great Britain. We are impatient here to learn the answer of England." and go straight on to "Their second debate was on a petition..."; the sentence in cipher is left out silently. Our reading has nothing to compare with in print. Wharton's note to this letter refers to A. Lee's Prussian correspondence (Schulenburg).

## Remarks

Checked on the image: the two blotted figures. The cipher is in Dumas's own hand (same hand as the prose). No glosses, so no contemporary decipherment on these pages.
