## Summary

The Duke reminds Sforza that on the 15th of last month he received a letter (or messenger) from him and sent
Giovanni (Zohane) del Maino to him the very next day with the widest mandate and power to act as the Duke himself
could; but so far he has seen no result, except that all Sforza's men-at-arms who were in Cremona and the Cremonese
have gone over to join the enemy camp (the Venetians). He urges Sforza to consider that it is far better and more
honourable to win as one should than to sit in winter quarters, and closes with remarks on provisions and on
not taking revenge. The second half has more gaps than the first; the date and intitulatio are clean.

## Notes

- Zohane del Maino: spelled out (4 β ‡ ʒ̶ ♀ ·‖· ꝯ ƀ c -o ∂÷ y), as on f. 36/37. A Maino, relative of the Duke's
  mistress Agnese del Maino (mother of Bianca Maria, Sforza's wife), was a natural envoy.
- "Cremona ... Cremonese": Sforza held Cremona (Bianca Maria's dowry); in autumn 1446 the Venetians had overrun the
  Cremonese and part of Sforza's troops there went over to them. The slip says his men "sono andate ... unite col
  campo de li inimici nostri".
- 4 = z in Zohane (as the key); ꝗ (q with a stroke through the descender) = z in "posanza", as on f. 64.
- "co[n]" is written o## xo (c o) with no n sign.
