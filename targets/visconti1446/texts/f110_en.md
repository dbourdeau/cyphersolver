# BnF ital. 1583 f. 110r-v (DECODE R7902): English translation. Filippo Maria Visconti to Francesco Sforza, 1 November [1446]

Translated from `f110_text.md` (the cipher reading). Gaps in the cipher are kept as [...]; words in square brackets
are supplied from the context. The recto reads almost in full; the verso is cramped and about a third of its signs
are unread, so its sentences are broken. [name] is the name sign left blank in the duke's key.

Illustrious son-in-law and our dearest son. Many and many times you have told us that you would never bring yourself
to be with us out of necessity, in such a way that we could say we had had [you] out of necessity [...] and not by
your own good will.

That was never our view: for the stronger and more powerful you had been, being one and the same thing with us, so
much the better it would have [...] for our state and for us.

And it seems to us that fortune has been very favourable and kind to you and has provided well for this, because no
danger that [has] arisen in the past has made you willing to change your mind; and now you can say that in doing so,
that is, in coming to be one and the same thing with us, you will do it of your own will and not out of necessity.

We assure you that since the rout that befell our troops, we have always persisted in the view that we would rather
suffer the Venetians to have our whole state than that you should have even one crumb of it; and in this view we
persisted until we received this brief of yours, [the duplicate?] of the one you wrote dated the fourth of this
month.

Having understood it well, and considering how kindly you have been moved to write to us, so that it does not seem to
us you could have done it with more kindness if we were your father or your son or the dearest thing you have in this
world, it has made us change our mind entirely; so that we have resolved not to be as stubborn and obstinate as you
are, for we do not wish to say at all that we have no need of you. On the contrary, we say that we have as much need
and necessity of it as could be said, and we believe we could not have a greater one than we have.

So that if you come to our aid [now], we shall be all the more obliged to you for doing it, since we are in the
greatest need that could come about in this world.

We therefore tell you that we are content to come to a good understanding with you, and we have resolved never to act
against you, in your state or in your affairs; and as regards your own particular interests, that is, your person,
your company and your state, to do all that you will know how and wish to ask. We say the same of the [name].

In the matter of the Venetians we are also content to do as you wish and as you please, provided that they keep
nothing of ours beyond what they held before the rout of our troops.

We remind you that we mean to be a good father to you and to have you as a good son, and we wish and intend that from
this hour on you may always deceive us in your own way; for whatever you do, we shall always believe you do it for
good and to a good end, and in good faith and not in bad.

We earnestly urge and beg you that from now on, in all the matters you will have to treat with us, you treat them
either through the hands of your own trusted men who are from beyond the Panaro and have no dependence on this side,
or through those of our men whom we trust.

We remind you that the chief cause of all the quarrels there have been until now between you and us has been our
having had your affairs handled through the hands of our men who were suspect to us, and who have been the cause of
making us suspicious of you as well.

We further tell you that if, because of this understanding of yours with us or for any other cause whatever, the
Venetians should take away from you the pay they give you, we are content [to give] you the same pay that the [name]
give you, so that you may maintain your state [and your men].

We further tell you that beyond what is said [...] we are content to help and support you with three [...] horse
and [tanti] [a thousand?] at our own [...]

[f. 110v] which will remain with you [...] [en]terprise you may wish to undertake [...] against Holy Church [...]
against those lands that [the Church] holds directly [...], against [...] which we would never do on any condition in
the world; not meaning at all those that [...] its vicars, feudatories or protected lords, but only those that the
Church holds [as its own] and directly, as said.

We tell you further that we do not [...] to wish to be in defence of the Church, nor to your harm on behalf of the
[Ch]urch; rather [...] that we shall be neither in defence of the said Church nor to your harm on behalf of the
[Ch]urch.

Reminding [you] further that from now [...] we shall make [...] against the majesty of the King of Aragon for what he
holds in the kingdom. [...] reasons(?): the one for our honour, the other so that, not having [...] it should never
come to the ears of [...] that we had consented [...] the kingdom. [...] that of this [...] we shall always do what
[...]; and take it for certain and firm.

We tell you plainly that [...] we can stand in [...], and [...] come quickly to a conclusion, because if [...]
things might(?) [...] take such a form that afterwards, even if you wished [...], provide [...].

[...] on the first day of November [...].

[...] of [Milan?] [...] [C]ount [...] of Genoa, lord [...].
