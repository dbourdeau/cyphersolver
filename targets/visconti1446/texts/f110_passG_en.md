# BnF italien 1583 f. 110 -- Filippo Maria Visconti to Francesco Sforza, Milan 1 Nov 1446 (cipher slip): English

A long, personal letter of the Duke to his son-in-law, opening with the Latin salutation, in the warm and insistent
register of ff. 130 and 136. What reads: the Duke has told Sforza "molte et molte volte"; something is a matter of
"necessitate" (twice); he speaks of Sforza's goodwill ("volontà vostra", "favorevele[?]") and of "stato nostro ...
integramente"; he has received Sforza's brief ("havemo recevuto quest[o] ... breve", L12); Sforza writes that
"not even we could have done more" ("humanamente ... non nui el havesti possuto fare con più", L14); the Duke has
decided not to change his mind ("mutare de opinione ... havemo deliberato", L16); "anzi dicemo che ne havemo tanto
... bisogno" (L18: the Duke's need of Sforza); obligations ("tanto più ... obligati", L20); "per respecto al ...
vostra" and Sforza's state ("al stato vostr[o]"), "whatever you wish to ask" ("quelo che ... voriti domandare",
L24); "recordandove" twice (L27, L33) about men of the Duke's "che havemo fedati[?]" (L32-33); "et pregamo che da
qui inance in tute le cose ..." (L31); people who "have been the cause of making us suspect you too" ("stati
casone de farne havese[?] suspecto ancora vui", L36); and "la provisione vostra" (L38-39), Sforza's provision or pay.

No name sign is read with certainty: the looped-δ signs in L15 and L25 (ꝺ, ꝺ+, ꝺı) are unidentified. Cosimo (Á),
Cremona, Alfonso of Aragon and Giovanni del Maino are not found in what is read of the recto. The verso closes
with the date "Data [Mediol]ani die primo Novembris" and the title ending "Dux Mediolani ... Anglerieque comes ac Ianue
Dominus", which confirms 1 Nov (no year enciphered, as on f. 158). The letter fits the Duke's courting of Sforza in early
November 1446 (Sforza in the Marche, the Venetian war, the question of his pay and of mutual suspicion), but its
specific matter cannot be stated from this reading.

The verso body is read only in scattered words, but "sancta chiesa" (L43) and "chiesa" (L45, L47) recur, with
"contra" twice, "honore", "acio che non" and "venir[e] ve con ... vui": the end of the letter seems to turn to the
Church (the Pope, Eugenius IV, whose condottiere Sforza had been and against whom he was fighting in the Marche) and
to Sforza coming to the Duke. That is a lead, not a reading.
