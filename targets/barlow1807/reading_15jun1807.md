# Barlow to Tierney, Fort William, 15 June 1807: reading

The letter is in clear English. Running text from the transcription, line-end hyphens joined; the catchword at the foot of the first page is left out.

Fort William the 15th June 1807. Sir, The Dispatches of this Government to the Honorable the Court of Directors & to the Secret Committee forwarded by the Extra Ship Indus, contain ample Details of Communication upon every Subject of Importance connected with the Political Interests of the British Government in India, which has engaged our attention since the Departure of the homeward bound fleet in the Month of February. Your access to those Dispatches precludes the necessity of describing to you in a separate Address the Course of our proceedings in the conduct of Political Affairs. You will have the Satisfaction to observe that the same Prospect of durable Tranquility which we announced in our last Dispatches to England continues to prevail, and that the Sources of internal and external Security and Prosperity are in a Condition of progressive improvement. The system of our external Relations supported by the power and ascendancy of the British Government in India, and maintained in its original purity by a strict adherence on our part to the principles on which those Relations were originally founded, continues to preserve the primary States of India, in a Condition of peacefull inactivity and secure Repose, whilst the Minor States and those with which we are unconnected by the Obligations of Alliance engaged in mutual dissentions, or in the prosecution of the objects of their respective views and conflicting Interests have neither leisure to meditate and Combine, nor power to carry into effect any feasible projects of Hostility or Ambition against the British Government & its Allies. You will observe from the general Course of our Proceedings & especially from our reports of recent transactions at Hyderabad, at Poona &c and at Delhi, that without departing from the funda -mental principles of our established Policy, we guard with unremitted vigilance against every deviation from the existing System of Political Relation & that the mainte -nance of that System has practically demonstrated its efficiency in the preservation of our requisite ascendancy in India & in the Consolidation of the basis of durable Tranquillity and Peace. You will be apprized of the improved Condition of the Company's finances & of public Credit by our address to the Court of Directors upon those subjects now forwarded by the Indus, & you will have the satisfaction to learn from that Address that we have every reason to expect a considerable Surplus Revenue in the ensuing year 1808/9 applicable to the discharge of the Public Debt & to the provision of the Investment. I have the honor to be with the highest respect Sir Your faithful and obedient Servant G H Barlow

Addressed at the foot of the first page: The Right Honorable George Tierney &c. &c. &c.
