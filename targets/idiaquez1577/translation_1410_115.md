# Translation of AGS Estado 1410,115, cipher of images 1-2 (Juan de Idiáquez and Pedro de Mendoza to Philip II, Genoa, 24 November 1577)

English translation of `reading_1410_115.md`, with the same holes. Square brackets: a guess or a hole in the reading.
The cipher continues on images 3-6 (not read yet).

As for the French sending an ordinary ambassador to this Republic, perhaps some one of those who are in exile over the
conspiracy, or their relatives, may have persuaded them to it through the Count of [Fiesco], as they say. But that this
has been a general request of those who are [called here] the "new" [nobles], Your Majesty should not believe, nor can
it be, because the best of them are not of that mind; and these leading men make one body with the "old" [nobles], and
they are the ones who, in their company, now have power and weight, and not the [lesser] ones, of whom one might have
doubts. Rather, if at any time one may hope that a French ambassador, on his coming, would be excluded, it is today;
although things here are so changeable [...] that nothing can be promised, and so it is well to live with caution, as
will be done.

As for the business of the conspiracy, we gave this Signoria Your Majesty's letter, urging on it [strongly] what we
were ordered, in its credence, concerning the investigation of the truth and the exemplary punishment of the guilty;
and on the first point [continues on image 3].

Context (from the clear text of image 1, not ciphered): the ambassadors acknowledge the King's letters of 16 and 21
(October), report that Spanish troops leaving Flanders have passed, that Lope de Figueroa's tercio is in Lombardy, and
that the troops from Naples were to embark on the 15th. The "new" and "old" nobles are the two orders of the Genoese
nobility whose quarrel led to the civil war of 1575 and the settlement of 1576.
