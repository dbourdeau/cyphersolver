# Sign conventions (shape names only)

Hand: the Genoa embassy cipher clerk of 1577 (AGS Estado 1410,115). The cipher is written in Arabic numerals with small
marks attached to the right of a numeral and small marks written above it. This file names shapes only; it gives no
values.

## Base signs

- One- and two-digit numerals, transcribed as written: `1` ... `36`. A leading `1` is often written as a short upright
  stroke; it is read as the digit 1 when it stands close to the next digit, and as a separate sign when there is a clear
  gap. Two-digit numerals are joined; a visible gap between digits splits them.
- `10` is often written as a small stroke followed by a small `o`; it is transcribed `10`.

## Marks written after a numeral (on the line)

| transcription | shape |
|---|---|
| `+` | small cross after the numeral |
| `.` | dot after the numeral, at mid-height or on the line |
| `@` | small loop shaped like a 6 (a 6 written smaller and joined to the numeral) |
| `e` | small hook shaped like a lower-case e |
| `\|` | short upright or slanting stroke after the numeral, like a dotless i |

A numeral with no mark after it is transcribed bare.

## Marks written above a numeral

Transcribed directly after the numeral and its on-line mark, in this order.

| transcription | shape |
|---|---|
| `^` | caret (opening downward) |
| `v` | check or breve (opening upward) |
| `_` | horizontal bar |
| `'` | small tick or apostrophe |
| `%` | small tilde |
| `²` | small figure 2 written above the numeral |
| `†` | horizontal bar with a short upright stroke through or on it (a small cross above) |

## Marks written below a numeral

| transcription | shape |
|---|---|
| `=` | a horizontal stroke under the numeral (underline) |

## Layout

- Signs are separated by a single space. A visible word gap is written ` / `.
- Line ends are kept as on the page; `#` lines are comments (image and line number).
- `?` after a sign: the shape is uncertain. `[...]`: an illegible stretch.
- A long flourish at line end (filler) is written `~`.
