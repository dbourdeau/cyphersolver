# 10 February 1865 — Taylor to Beauregard (provisional, decoded not read)

NARA RG109 chapter II vol196 frame0018 manuscript p31, Meridian10 February 1865,
General G.T.Beauregard, Augusta, Georgia.
[Record and images](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/45485022).

Working sense; this is **not** a clean decipherment. Brackets enclose proposals
whose raw cipher groups have unresolved letter differences:

> You telegraphed me that I could keep [?BRANTLEYS] [?AND] SHARPS [?BRIGADES].
> Unaware of your present position and necessities I ask further instructions
> about them. Last OF [?THEIR] FURLOUGHS [?EXPIRE] on twelfth.
> [?THESE] [?MEN] ARE [?RETURNING] [?VERY] SLOWLY owing probably in some
> degree to high water and difficulty of transportation.
> [?TOTAL] [?OF] BOTH BRIGADES is now but [?LITTLE] [?OVER] [?TWO] HUNDRED.
> They can do good [?SERVICE] IN [?MOBILE] and there collect absentees
> [?QUICKER] [?THAN] IF [?SENT] [?EAST]. [?BUT] IF YOU NEED THEM I will send
> them TO YOU AS SOON AS LAST OF [?FURLOUGHS] EXPIRE. Please answer by telegraph.
>
> R.Taylor, LieutGenl.

203 cipher letters; 174 agree positionally with proposed text. Most of the
remaining differences are plausible serif/crossbar glyph ambiguities, but
this report does not convert those plausible corrections into verified letters.
The key phase is continuous through cipher letters only, including words
split at line ends: BRI/GADES, EX/PIRE, RETUR/NING, BRIG/ADES, TWOH/UNDRED,
QUICKE/R, B/UT. Raw candidates and reconstructed stream remain separate.

Known COME RETRIBUTION strongly fits the long stream; this is further evidence
of its use in the Beauregard channel during February. The provisional reading
cannot meet a complete-reading claim as currently marked. No exact prior
plaintext found in bounded searches of OR I/49/1 and I/47/2 OCR; not proof of
nonpublication. The other two telegrams on the left page are clear text;
the bottom right telegram is also clear. No other cipher on this frame was
identified by visual inspection.
