# 11 April 1865: Surget to Maury, how long Mobile will be held (new key RALLY TO THE FRONT)

Meridian, April 11th 1865. Majr. Genl. D. H. Maury, Mobile.

Let me know EXACTLY HOW LONG YOU WI[L]L HOLD THE CITY as the RUNNING OF THE TRAINS DEPENDS on that. If you can HOLD ON
UNTIL NINE O CLOCK TOMORROW Can have four or five ADDITIONAL TRAINS THERE.

E. Surget, A.A. Genl.

Notes. Capitals are cipher words decrypted with RALLYTOTHEFRONT, a key not otherwise known in this folder or in any
source we could open (`v11-0046b-maury_decryption.txt`, `blind_2026-11-15/rally_key/README.md`); the rest is in clear
in the letter book (clear words as read by AI readers, not checked by a person). WI[L]L needs J where every reader reads
the clerk's T: a hole by the counting rules. Ten further letters are the readers' alternatives or shape readings the
image supports (this clerk's F is his E without the lower-right tick; a blind test sorted 12 of 14 such letters as the
key needs); two (the gutter M of EXACTLY and the C under a blot in CLOCK) are possible but not settled. On two new blind
transcriptions the key alone gave 11 of 22 groups as words, a miss against the frozen mark of 15, with every control
at 5 or below. Pencil numerals stand over every fifteenth cipher letter from the 30th. No reading review is recorded
here (that grade is the reviewers').

Context: the same day Gibson withdrew the infantry and artillery from Mobile "during the night" and told the cavalry
to cover the troops and trains (OR I/49/2 p. 1225), and Bullock asked Maury in clear "Why remove all your troops if
enemy have made no demonstration?" (p. 1226). Taylor reported on 20 April that "Mobile was evacuated by General Maury on
night of 11th" (p. 1255). This telegram is not printed there.
