# Capitals this clerk writes alike (reader A1)

The cipher capitals on all six frames are in the same engrossing-style hand: separate printed-looking capitals, most with a short baseline lead-in stroke, joined by dashes. Pairs that are easy to confuse:

| Pair | How they differ (when they differ) | Example group, frame |
|---|---|---|
| E / F | E has a lower bar that ends in a comma-like curl or dot; F has only a top and middle bar. The baseline lead-in stroke under many letters can pass for E's lower bar, and the curl is sometimes just a dot. | `TSYSM[E/F]E`, v196_0064; `Z[F/E]U[F/E]LR`, v196_0056; `GKM[F/E]Y`, v196_0053 |
| M / N | M is written like an N with an extra hook or tail on the right ("Nι"); N is plain. At speed the hook shrinks. | `QDUMM` beside `TSNIQ[F/E]L`, v196_0061; `HIEAWECTM`, v196_0053 |
| Y / V | Y normally has a long descending loop; some Ys are short, with only a flick at the top right, and look like V. | `MOAB[Y/V]S[F/E]` and `B[Y/V]U`, v196_0056 |
| P / F | P has a closed bowl at the top. A P with a crossing stroke or dot looks like F. | `QG[P/F]QR` and `JKRGOYQZ[P/F]MW`, v196_0053 |
| L / I | L has a looped or 4-like foot; I has a short top bar and a base. A small L can pass for I, and a 4-shaped L can look like Z. | `T[L/I]GAIIOKGS` and `YLMIO`, v196_0053 |
| U / C | U is two strokes; a single open curve with a hook at the end of a group can look like C. | `EPQIM[U/C]`, v196_0053 |
| J / I | J's first stroke drops below the line; I sits on the line. | `[J/I]H`, v196_0061 |
| R / B | R's leg can close up against the bowl and look like B. | `Q[R/B]NVIDT`, v196_0053 |
| V / N | When a group starts with a lead-in stroke, a V can look like N. | `[V/N]ASD`, v196_0053 |
| G / A (round forms) | Both are written as open round forms with a cross-stroke. Here they were told apart by the inner loop (A) or the flat cross-bar (G), but a checker should watch for this. | `QAQDEGUMS`, v196_0061 |
| H / A | H has a flat crossbar between two uprights; the clerk's A is a round looped form, but a cramped H at the end of a group comes close. | `JKMHRZH`, v196_0056 |

Other features of the hand that are not letters:
- Commas and dots often follow E and L inside a group (`E,`, `L,`), and dots appear between doubled V's (`K V.V.V`). I read these as pen habits, not separators, and did not split groups at them.
- Groups often run to the end of a line with no closing dash, and the next line starts with a short group (`QJVVZCDC` / `TM`, `YD?I` / `W`, `HHEYQ` / `V`). Each case is flagged in the transcription as a possible run-on.
- Some telegrams have pencil interlinear words above the cipher (v196_0053 throughout; one or two pencil letters on v196_0064). Pencil was ignored.
