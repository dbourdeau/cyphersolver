# Confusable capitals (reader B1)

Blind palaeographic reading only; no key applied. Pairs below are the letter forms this clerk (or these clerks) write alike. The print-style capitals on v11_0039, v11_0044, v11_0045, v11_0046 (2nd telegram) and v10_0056 share most habits; the flowing script capitals on v11_0046 (1st telegram) are listed separately.

## Print-style capitals

| Pair | How they differ (when they do) | Example group, frame |
|---|---|---|
| E / F | E's bottom bar runs to the right under the middle bar; F has only a short foot under the stem. The clerk often reduces E's bottom bar to a comma-like flick ("E,"), which then looks like F with a dot. | `UG[E/F]Q` v11_0044; `O[F/E]U` v11_0039; `M[E/F][F/E]M` v10_0056 |
| I / L | I has a top bar and a flat foot; L has no top bar and its foot curls up into a comma-like hook. | `LIM` v11_0039 (L then I); `NILKEBLW` v11_0044 |
| M / N | The clerk's M is an N with one more short stroke at the right, which can be faint, so M can look like N. | `WMMF` v11_0039; `JMMH` v11_0045 |
| C / G | G is a C with a short inner stroke at the base ("Ci"); some Cs carry a raised r-like flourish at top right that can be taken for that stroke. | `YXK[C/G]` and `K[C/G]KCGGPO` v10_0056 |
| O / Q | Q has a tail at lower right; a quickly closed O with a trailing flick looks the same. | `YSQ[O/Q]` v11_0044 |
| P / D | P is written with a loop and crossed foot at the base, which can resemble the open D. | `[P/D]P` v11_0044 (compare `PQZY` same frame) |
| H / U | A small H with a short crossbar sits close to U. | `Q[H/U]WM` v11_0044 |
| A / R | The script-form A (a looped stroke with no bowl) appears among print capitals and can be taken for R. | `VEASGW` v11_0044 and v11_0045 |
| Y / V | Y differs only by its descender. | `IY` v11_0044; `TYQ` v11_0046 |
| I / J | J has a curled foot below the line; a short J can look like I with a hook. | `KPJLW` v11_0039 |

## Flowing script capitals (v11_0046, telegram 1)

| Pair | How they differ (when they do) | Example group |
|---|---|---|
| Q / Z | Both are 2-shaped; Z drops a loop below the line (as in `NVXZCIN`), Q sits on the line. | `[Q/Z]B[Q/Z]` |
| H / A | A looped-stem form (oval at left plus a slanting stem rising to a peak) is used repeatedly; read as H after the H in `BSHBVG`, but it is close to the copperplate A. A second A form is like a small "a" (`XAV`, `GAVEFDE`). | `BN[H/A]`, `G[H/A]DXB[H/A]R` |
| N / H | N is a looped 9-shape with a separate second stroke; it can be taken for H when the strokes touch. | `S[N/H]` |
| D / CL | D is written like a "c" with a tall ascender; it can be read as two letters C L. | `GAVEFDE`, `DY` |
| G / C | G is a 6-shape closed at the base; C is open. | `M[G/C]` |
| Y / Z | Both have a descender loop; Y has a rounded u-shaped top, Z a flat top. | `[Y/Z]C[V/?]N` |
| I / J | J has a descender loop; I stays on the line. | `UASUQ[I/J]E` |
| F / T | F has a small crossbar tick on the stem; T has none. Read F throughout. | `ECFXF[R/A]` |
| R / A | | `ECFXF[R/A]` |
