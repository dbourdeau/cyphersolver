# 9 March 1865: Taylor to Levy, the Mobile and Montgomery cars

Meridian, March 9th 1865. Lieut. Col. Wm. M. Levy, A.A. Genl., care Post Comdt., Montgomery, Ala., who will find Col.
Levy and deliver this.

The Lieut. Genl. Comdg. understands there are one hundred & fifty five CARS or [on] MOBILE and MONTGOMERY R. R., and
thinks such of them as are not needed for daily use should immediately be TRANSFERRED ON TRUCKS to WIDE [?GUAGE] AT
COLUMBUS, GA. He wishes you to confer confidentially with SUPERINTENDENT OF R. R. and suggest propriety of doing this.
It might be done UNDER PRETENSE OF ASSISTING ROADS in Georgia.

W. F. Bullock Jr., A.A. Genl.

Notes. Capitals are the cipher words, decrypted with COMPLETE VICTORY (`v196-0056-levy_decryption.txt`); the rest is
in clear in the letter book. The book has "or" before MOBILE (all four readers); "on" is the sense. [?GUAGE] is a hole
by the counting rules: the cipher JJMVP gives HVAGE, and GUAGE (the period spelling of gauge) needs I for J twice,
which the image does not show. 21 cipher words, 20 read, 1 hole. Reading by AI readers, not checked by a person; no
reading review is recorded here (that grade is the reviewers').

The same day Bullock wrote to D. W. Adams (`v196-0056-dw_reading.md`, upper telegram on the same page) that Levy, "in
Montgomery", would confer with the railroad superintendent about moving cars "on pretext of helping Georgia Rail
Roads"; this is that order to Levy. Columbus was where the Georgia lines (five-foot gauge) met the Montgomery and West
Point's branch from Opelika, which was not of that gauge, so cars from the Alabama side had to be carried on other
trucks there.
