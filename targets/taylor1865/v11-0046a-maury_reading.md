# 11 April 1865: Surget to Maury, the trains and the railroad company's cotton (read in part)

Meridian, April 11th 1865. Maj. Genl. Maury, Mobile, Ala.

ONE [?OF] THE [?TRAINS] from Mobile last night brought [... 9] and quite a number of BALES OF COTTON FOR THE RAIL
ROAD COMPANY. Have you seen Mr. [name not settled]? Order him to run the trains according to your requirements.

E. Surget, A.A.G.

Notes. Capitals are cipher words decrypted with COME RETRIBUTION (`v11-0046a-maury_decryption.txt`); the rest is in
clear in the letter book (clear words as read by AI readers, not checked by a person). [?OF] and [?TRAINS] each need one
letter that no reader saw on the image, so they are holes by the counting rules; [... 9] is nine unread cipher words.
21 cipher words: 10 read, 2 doubtful, 9 unread. The hand is a flowing copperplate in which the blind readers agreed on
only about two letters in three; the unread stretch needs a better reading of the image before any key work. No
reading review is recorded here (that grade is the reviewers').

Context: the same day Bullock told Maury in clear to "see that no mistake occurs in orders about cotton" (OR I/49/2
p. 1226); Gibson ordered the infantry and artillery out of Mobile during the night of 11 April (p. 1225).
