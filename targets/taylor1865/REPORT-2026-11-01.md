## 8 March: Brent telegram now follows one continuous key phase

NARA RG109, chapter II vol.196, frame0052, manuscript p99, Taylor to Col.
George Wm. Brent, Augusta, dated Meridian, 8 March 1865.
[Record and images](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/45485022).

Provisional continuous reading (cipher-derived text in capitals):

> Genl Maury reports ENEMY AT LEAST TWENTY THOUSAND STRONG AT PENSACOLA
> ready to move. If he MOVES AGAINST [MONTGOMERY?] that place will necessarily
> [BE EVACUATED?] as I have NO [FORCE?] [ADEQUATE?] to its DEFENCE any
> communication with East will be CUT OFF. ENEMY had commenced MOVING FROM NORTH.

The former apparent phase break was chiefly **STR / ONG**, divided across a
line. The final MOVING FROM NORTH now follows from the beginning without a
key restart or arbitrary offset. Cipher-only letters consume COME RETRIBUTION;
clear words do not. All 124 proposed cipher letters share that phase.

The current working candidate gives 116/124 direct position agreements against
the contextual reconstruction. This is transcription agreement, not a measured
95% readable-token claim. Several glyphs remain uncertain, especially the
Montgomery/evacuated/force/adequate groups; the reading stays provisional.
No complete prior plaintext was located in the bounded OR OCR search, which
is not proof of nonpublication. See `nov1_alignment.json`, `.tsv`, and controls.
The older `v196-0052_partial.txt` remains preserved as the earlier state.

## 4 February: earlier COME RETRIBUTION use in the Davis channel

NARA vol.196, frame0010, manuscript p15, Taylor to President Davis, Richmond,
Meridian **4 February 1865** (date checked on manuscript).
A complete prior plaintext is printed in **OR I/49/1 p955**:
[volume scan](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion014901rootrich/page/955/mode/1up).
The OCR misreads the date, so it cannot by itself establish the day.

Sense, from the existing printed decipherment: Taylor calls the Mobile navy
a farce, says the vessels stay at the wharf rather than cooperate with the
army, and calls its expenses a waste of money. A letter explaining his reasons
is going by mail. This is an **existing decipherment checked with a known key**,
not a newly recovered plaintext.

The image's later groups follow COME RETRIBUTION continuously and strongly:
TIED UP AT THE WHARF NEVER CO OPERATING WITH THE ARMY; after clear "The payment,"
OF ITS EXPENSES IS A WASTE OF MONEY. MONEY is split YS / EIR across lines.
The raw candidate V/B in WHARF needs correction. Importantly, FTXIIUCGO
deciphers OPERATING exactly: the manuscript says "never co-operating with the
army," while the printed edition gives "never in co-operation with the army."
This is a coherent wording variant, not two bad cipher letters or a missing IN.

A fresh enlarged crop resolves the apparent five-letter UYFPG as six-letter
UYIFPG, giving A FARCE exactly without an insertion. The next eight-letter group
MILJTMEL corresponds approximately to printed VESSELS, but its length and
spelling are unresolved. VESSELLS is a provisional alignment hypothesis, not
an established manuscript spelling. Do not copy this hypothesis into a clean
reading without qualification. `nov1_analysis.py` excludes unequal-length
pairs from its position count and prints the uncorrected uninterrupted raw
output as well as the proposed alignment. No silent correction of the raw
stream is made.

Despite the garbled VESSELS group, the long exact downstream correspondence
supports the key's use in this Taylor–Davis telegram on 4 February. This
extends our image-checked selected examples earlier than the 8 March Brent
telegram and the 9 March local instructions to the two Adamses. It does not
establish the first use of COME RETRIBUTION in general, a universal change date,
or the end of COMPLETE VICTORY use in other channels.

### Wirt Adams, 21 February

The dispatch gives instructions for responding to an enemy advance from Baton
Rouge or below Vicksburg: concentrate in front, FALL BACK SLOWLY, use the M&O
railroad north of the point where the enemy will strike it, and keep contact
with Taylor and Forrest. Three groups remain marked uncertain in the reading.
No exact printed counterpart found in bounded OR I/49/1 searches.

This provides a direct old-key witness for a recipient of the9 March change
order. Alongside4 February Taylor–Davis COME RETRIBUTION, it supports concurrent
channel-specific use of different keys; the chronology is not a universal
sequence of keys used exclusively one after another.

### Beauregard, 10 February

The provisional plaintext concerns Brantley's and Sharp's brigades, expiring
furloughs, very slow returns due to high water and transportation difficulties,
a combined strength of about two hundred, and whether to retain them at Mobile
or send them east. Many words still have unresolved glyph differences and are
explicitly bracketed. The exact long stream has not been verified in a prior
edition. Bounded OR I/49/1 and I/47/2 searches produced no exact counterpart.
This is a research lead, not a clean or newly published reading.

### Equipment telegram, 20 April

"Have completed equipment of Mobile garrison for the field. No indication of
any decided movement from Mobile nor from Mississippi or Tennessee Rivers.
Former very high, all its lowlands being under water."

The **of any** after INDICATION is clear text. Treating OF as cipher had caused
the old alignment problem. Excluding it restores the whole message's phase;
only final D/F in FROM remains ambiguous. The complete printed decipherment
is on OR p1255. This updates the earlier prior-print pointer; it does not
claim a new plaintext.

### Readiness/Richmond telegram, 8 April

Surget asks whether Col.H.Maury's regiment can move by rail immediately to
reinforce Wirt Adams. Its cipher reports Vicksburg newspapers' account of
Richmond's capture on the3rd and Lee moving towards Danville. The existing
printed version on OR p1216 has TOWARD; the manuscript has TOWARDS. The final
Danville cipher group needs glyph segmentation review and remains marked.
