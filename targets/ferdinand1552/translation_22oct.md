# Ferdinand I to Castaldo, Vienna, 22 October 1552: English translation of the cipher passages

Translated here from `latin_22oct.txt`. `[...]` marks a clear passage between cipher passages. Codes: [Hamet], [the Pasha
of Buda], [Transylvania], [Lippa], [the army], [the Moldavian].

[...] the difficulties which arise there daily, and especially [...] we cannot do without their service at this time
[...] [should they] leave [Transylvania] [...] and [that you] keep them with you, [so that] they do not leave until
their service there is necessary, as we do not believe they will leave. [...] to stay there [...] so that they are paid.

[As to your view that Duke Maurice should] advance against the enemy besieging Agria, and that you too should move
against them from the other side, so that, placed in the middle and surrounded, they could be pressed the harder from
two sides [...] to free Agria, [it is] not unsuitable [...] [but it is uncertain whether His Highness could join you]
without danger or hindrance from the enemy; for His Highness's [army] is not so large that he could freely and outside
a safe place give the enemy the chance to fight a battle. For although very many of the enemy die daily and they are
said to be weak, yet in so large an [army] a few thousand are hardly missed or felt to be lacking; indeed you too, alone
with the troops of ours who are with you, would not be enough to withstand their whole onslaught if it were turned
against you, since earlier, when you had the troops of the kingdom with you and [Hamet] had not yet joined with [the
Pasha of Buda], you would not be their match. Moreover the aforesaid Duke is not well enough provided to give a pitched
battle, for lack of light cavalry; he has fewer of them than he needs, and to you, a man most experienced in war, it is
not obscure how necessary that kind of soldier is for waging war properly against the Turks. And there are many other
obstacles that prevent him from bringing help to Agria in that way and in open battle, which it is superfluous to
recount to you, who know them. There is this too: if Duke Maurice, with this [army], went against the enemy following
your advice, setting aside what has been said, and the plan did not succeed and he suffered even some slight defeat,
which God mercifully forbid, how much disturbance and confusion it would bring into that kingdom of Hungary, since that
[army], by its fame and the hopes placed in it, now sustains almost the whole state of affairs.

We therefore think it safer that the said Duke Maurice remain in these parts and harass the enemy from here with
continual raids, and draw them off from the siege of Agria by other stratagems, than undertake so long, tangled and
dangerous a march there. For His Highness has already sent raids towards Veszprém, Székesfehérvár and the places near
them, and does not cease to try and enter on every way and means by which he trusts the enemy can be drawn off from
there. That place, however, is still defended stoutly, with God's kind and strong help, and its men, as we hear, are
good and very brave and still in good heart; winter too is coming on, by which the enemy's obstinacy will at last be
broken, and in the end, as we hope, they will be driven off against their will, if other means do not succeed.

Moreover we think that you should by no means leave that kingdom of ours [Transylvania] now with our soldiers or any
part of them; we want you to persevere there in its defence still, both for the reason set out in our last letters, and
because you yourself show that the Turks at [Lippa], who are growing daily, would invade the kingdom at once if our
soldiers left, and that the nobles and most of the subjects have gone home again from the camp. So if you too crossed
the borders of the kingdom with them and moved towards Hungary, the passes of Deva and others would be left without a
garrison and open to the enemy to take without effort, to the plain ruin of the kingdom; for the subjects cannot be used
to guard them, since they have stayed long enough in the field. Nor do we doubt that for the present you are enough
alone, without danger, with the strength of our soldiers, for the defence of the kingdom, the holding of the passes and
the checking of Turkish raids, because their number cannot be so great that it cannot be held back by those troops of
ours, since [Hamet] stays with the [army] before Agria. Add to this the cold, which grows sharper every day, and with it
the enemy will soon be driven off. [...] [that you might seek out a place] outside the borders of our kingdom of
[Transylvania] [...] [that you] stay [...] since the common penny decreed us by the estates of the Holy Roman Empire is
now being collected, and is being counted out to our commissaries in many places. [...]

[It seems necessary to do the same to] the Wallachian [...] as once befell Stephen [of Moldavia]; but when that could
conveniently be done, because of the peace negotiation that [the Moldavian] has in hand, you will be able to foresee
better than we.
