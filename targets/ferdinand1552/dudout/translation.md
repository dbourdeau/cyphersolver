# English translation

[… we say nothing else, except that it would have pleased us greatly] if you had been able, or were still able, to make at least a demonstration or show of an expedition towards Lippa, so that the enemy might more easily be drawn away from the siege or assault of Eger, and if you had been able, or were able, to support there with your skill and diligence the effort that the illustrious Duke Maurice, Elector of Saxony, is making in this matter on this side with the army under him. And we believe that the Turks left at <C7> and at Lippa would have greatly feared such a movement on your part, and might have been forced to ask <C3> for help. For although it is easy to believe that Lippa and the other places bordering on our kingdom <C6> are held by a sufficient garrison, it cannot be that you were not superior to all of them in numbers or strength, so that there would have been no danger to fear from the enemy on that side. But it is not hidden from us that you have been prevented from undertaking anything of the kind by other difficulties, which you have often set out to us and repeat in your latest letters, and that you have left nothing undone that could be done, as a prudent man, most expert in military matters, and present on the spot.

Now since we earlier helped those faithful soldiers of ours with a certain sum of money, and are now helping them again with another, larger one and a great quantity of cloth, and will moreover help them, as soon as it can be done, with a further subsidy in money, we are fully persuaded that, seeing this fatherly and merciful care of ours, if they have formed any wish to leave, they will give it up and stay faithfully and steadfastly ready in our service under you, all the more since they can easily see that they could not leave safely. For their departure could not fail to cause us much damage and confusion. You will therefore take care in future too, with your singular industry, by good words and by all other suitable means and ways, to keep both the soldiers and the people of the country to their duty.

For the enemy, too, will not be able to stay much longer in the field because of the coming winter, and at Eger, as we hope in God, they will achieve nothing; in the end they will be drawn away, either by the advance of our army on this side, with your skill and cleverness helping, or driven off by the winter.
