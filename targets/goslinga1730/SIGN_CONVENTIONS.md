# Sign conventions (shapes only, no values)

Source: Gelders Archief 0613 inv. 3889, letter of Sicco van Goslinga, Compiègne 23 July 1730 (scans 23, 24, 27;
decipherment slip on scan 25). Screenshots of the archieven.nl viewer at device scale 2 (about 2490 x 3400 px).

- The cipher is written in Arabic numerals in the secretary's hand of the letter, set inside clear Dutch (and on scan 24
  inside clear French). Each code group is followed by a full stop. A group is written as one token: `292`.
- Some groups carry a small caret (^) above the last digit. Written as `292^`. A group with a caret is kept as a
  different sign from the same digits without one (e.g. `697` and `697^`).
- Clear words are written with a leading `=`: `=aan`, `=geweest`. They are copied as spelled, without expanding
  abbreviations.
- Line breaks follow the manuscript: one line of the file = one written line. Line ids: `23.1` = scan 23, first code
  line used.
- Digit shapes in this hand: the 4 is closed with a short tail and can look like a 9; the 9 has a round bowl and a long
  descender; the 7 has a flat top with a crossing stroke. Where two readings remain, the group is written `[4/9]1`
  style in the pass files and settled in the reconciled file, with a note.
- A group written over an erasure or blot, with the replacement digits written above it by the same hand, is
  transcribed with the replacement: `49` (note `over erasure`).
- `[?]` = group present but not legible; `158` in the left margin of scan 23 line 2 is transcribed as a group in its
  place at the start of the line (`158`), because it stands where an insertion mark would be.
