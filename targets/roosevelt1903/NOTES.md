# roosevelt1903: Theodore Roosevelt to John Hay, coded telegram of 27 May 1903 (Library of Congress, Roosevelt Papers reel 413 frame 0026)

Status: read (9 Nov 2026). ## The document

Library of Congress, Manuscript Division, Theodore Roosevelt Papers, reel 413, frame 0026 (Theodore Roosevelt Center record o266250), a typed telegram of 27 May 1903 from Roosevelt, to Secretary of State John Hay. Catalogue 762 listed it with "no decoding noted" and suggested a State Department or commercial code of 1903 (a copy at Hay's end might carry the decode; the project did not look).

`transcription.txt` is the contributor's transcription, `key_excerpt.tsv` the 22 codebook entries he used, `decryption.txt` his literal decipherment, `reading.md` the reading, `contributor_issue.md` his submission as filed (shortened).

## What it says

Roosevelt asks what the object of the British government's request is, says he does not think it desirable that the trip (which the contributor reads as the American squadron's, not checked here) should be made a junketing one, and says that if the squadron touches at an English port it should be after the trip to Kiel, in his judgment. The contributor's gloss is that Roosevelt asked what Britain wanted from a visit by the American squadron; the history around it is his and was not checked here.

## The cipher

A codebook: each five-digit group stands for a phrase, the first three digits giving the printed page and the last two the entry, in the State Department Red Cipher of 1876 compiled by John H. Haswell (NARA catalog id 417267710, microfilm T1171; a repository note (research/nara_state) says Weber 1979 prints it; not consulted here). The column "Message or true reading" is the plain side. Names the book lacks are spelled with a spelling table, keyed by the first number group of the message (here 56169): the coded letters U A W C read K I E L. A clear word may stand between groups ("junketing"), and two groups are cancelled. Florentijn van Kampen's study of the State Department codes supplied the clue for the group 10000 and pointed to the digitized codebook. This is a code, not a cipher recovered from the ciphertext: the book is an existing key found to fit.
