# Translation, letter 1 (AGI Indiferente 737 N.171, images 1-2)

This is an English translation of the letter as the clear copy in AGI Panama 29 R.6 N.23 gives it. The cipher itself is
decoded, not read (see reading_ind737_letter1.md and NOTES.md): about 75-80% of its letters decrypt to the copy's text,
and the copy supplies the rest. The heading appears only in the cipher; it decrypts to the title the copy carries.
Square brackets are editorial.

**Heading (cipher):** Copy of the letter that the governor of Tierra Firme wrote to the officials of Seville.

**1.** All who travel in this fleet can give the news I could give from here, so I will not spend much effort telling it
to Your Worships, since it is well known to all. As for the risings in Peru caused by Francisco Hernández [presumably
Hernández Girón; the copy gives only "fran[cis]co hernandez"], I have no news that they are over, because no ships have
come for many days. Still, I trust in Our Lord that they will end as all the other traitors have ended. From that kingdom
there is more to fear than this one rising, since so many people are up in arms and have nothing to live on but that; it
is their trade for earning their bread. May Our Lord remedy it all as best serves Him. With those kingdoms in this state,
all of us who are their neighbours have our share of it. We must live on guard against those over there, and also
against the men we have around us without knowing them, as happened to me here a few days ago with some rogues who were
plotting to kill me and to seize the land; I had them executed.

**2.** The goods that came in this fleet have fetched a high price. Everything here was so short of all necessities that
the merchants rushed to buy, thinking that each of them would have bought and sold two or three times before the fleet
left. Having bought so dear, they are left with the goods, and they bought more than they can pay for. This has led to
much fraud. We have had news here of Frenchmen in great numbers; they say there are sixteen ships. God knows how afraid I
am, both for this land and for our fleet. What I can tell Your Worships is that if things here were safe I would not send
His Majesty's money, but I see nothing safe.

**3.** Cosme Rodríguez Farfán, general of this armada, has assured me that the whole good of this fleet lies in leaving
here soon, both because of the winter season and because of the French, and this is why it has been dispatched so
quickly. I do not know whether this has harmed the merchants, but his haste has been so great that I do not know whether
it will have harmed the merchants there and here.

**4.** The money they carry for His Majesty is [275,000] pesos [the copy's "ccLxxvU" as I read it; see NOTES.md]. I well
believe that but for the risings it would have been a great sum. At present nothing else occurs to me to tell Your
Worships. May Our Lord [keep you]. Nombre de Dios, 4 July 1554. Álvaro de Sosa.
