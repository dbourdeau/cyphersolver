## Weaknesses first
1. **Still a weak reading.** Setting the signs side by side settled some classes and unsettled others. I would give a blind solve on it about one chance in four.
2. **One language assumption went into the classes.** I kept `3` and `K` apart partly because together they would be 24 of 125 signs, more than any one letter of Spanish takes. That is knowledge of the expected language used in a reading, and it is disclosed here for that reason.
3. **The three-like signs are the worst.** `3` and `K` differ by one crossing stroke, and the photostat's double image can fake it. Some of each have a rounded lower bowl and some a straight tail; I have treated that as handwriting and not as a difference of sign.
4. **A plain slash may or may not be a sign of its own.** See change 1 below.
5. **One reader, no person has looked,** and my count of 125 signs is still far below the ledger's "about 170 to 200".

## What changed from v1, and why
1. **`V` is gone: it was two signs.** In v1, `V` was a slash with a small angle and a dot at its top left. Side by side, the angle and dot are the sign `R`, which also stands alone before dividers and before other signs. So each `V` is now `R` followed by a plain slash, `/`. That adds four signs.
2. **`/`, a plain slash, is new.** Its members are the slashes that follow `R` in lines 6 and 8, and the three bold tapering slashes of lines 2 and 6 that v1 called `7`. Every one is marked doubtful with `7` as the alternative: I cannot rule out that `/` and `7` are one sign.
3. **`7` now means a slash with a clear bar at its top left.** Only 8:539 is sure.
4. **`G` is gone.** 3:705 is `R?`, 4:587 is `H?` (its arm comes back down, as the caret of `H` does), and 6:724 is unread.
5. **`t` is split.** 3:586 and 5:285 are alike, a stem with a flag and a crossbar, and are now `F?`. 4:670 stays `t?`: it sits on the crease.
6. **2:303 and 3:356 are `T?`, not `R?`.** Both have a top stroke that crosses the stem, as a T's bar does.
7. **4:390 is `3?`, not `S?`.** The three sure `S` (1:447, 1:597, 7:425) are a plain stem with a top bar and a foot; this one has a waist.
8. **4:732 is `P?`, not `7?`.** It has a dot at its upper left and a small loop at the top, as 7:388 has.
9. **The opening of line 8** was `R 7?` and is now `R /?`, by change 1.
10. **Marks above signs** are noted, not read: two marks over 7:456; marks over 2:554 and 6:364; a bar over 3:332; a dot over 1:405.
