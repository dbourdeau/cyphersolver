# Catalogue 93 and 92 (part): Coligny to Elizabeth I, BL Add MS 4136 ff. 141-148 (DECODE R9237, R9238, R9239, R9240) — NOTES

Status: no write-up

**Verdict: closed. All three letters were read at the time and are in print.** Session 2026-09-21.

"Amiral to D. Angle" is DECODE's reading of the margin headings "L'Amiral de France à la Reine d'Angleterre". These
are three letters from Gaspard de Coligny, Admiral of France, to Elizabeth I, written after the battle of Dreux. They
are copied into Patrick Forbes's deciphering file (Add MS 4136, see `targets/smith1562/NOTES.md` and `targets/foix1563/NOTES.md`).
Forbes copied only the ciphered text, in numbered passages. The copyist wrote a few glosses in the margin, and each
letter is headed by a line of signs (the address, with '&' and '14' as nulls and 'ω' = *La Royne d'Angleterre*).

All nine images were viewed (fetched with the DECODE cookie; git-ignored, not in the repository). R9239 image 4 and
R9240 image 1 are the same file (f. 146). The sign set is the graphic cipher of R9238 (the 12 Jan letter, catalogue 92).
Its key is DECODE R335, TNA SP 106/1 f. 69, "A Cipher of the P. of Condé", endorsed 2 Dec 1562.

The English decipherments are on the originals in TNA SP 70, and Forbes (1741) printed the French clear texts in
full. DECODE's date range "2 Jan 1562 – 29 Jan 1562" is Old Style: the letters are of January 1563 New Style.
Recipient: Elizabeth I, not "D. Angle". Language: French.

## What was not done

The cipher was not deciphered here. The key (R335) and the printed clear texts are both available, so a sign-by-sign
check against the key would be routine. Nothing is unread. The catalogue entry is removed.

## DECODE corrections

R9237, R9239, R9240: author Gaspard de Coligny, Admiral of France; recipient Elizabeth I ("Reine d'Angleterre", not
"D. Angle"); French; dates 2, 24 and 29 Jan 1563 (NS; 1562 OS); deciphered at the time (CSP Foreign vi nos. 7, 145 and
the 29 Jan entry), printed in Forbes, *Full View* ii 247-249, 300-303 and 319-321. Key: R335 (Condé cipher). R9239 image 4
and R9240 image 1 are the same image of f. 146, which holds the end of the 24 Jan letter and the start of the 29 Jan one.

R9238: author Gaspard de Coligny, recipient Elizabeth I, camp at Villefranche, 12 Jan 1563 (not 2 Jan), French;
deciphered at the time (CSP Foreign vi no. 76; Forbes, Full View ii 272-274); f.143 lower half begins the 24 Jan letter (R9239).

## R9238 joins this entry (14 Oct 2026)

Under the one-key rule (George Lasry, 14 Oct 2026) R9238, first worked in `../foix1563/` with the Queen Mother's
letter to de Foix (R9241, de Foix's own cipher, a different key), moved here: it is the same Condé cipher R335 and the

### R9238 (ff. 142-143): Coligny to Elizabeth, Villefranche, 12 Jan 1563: read at the time, in print

- Margin: "L'Amiral de France à la Reine d'Angleterre, [1]2 Jan. 1562/3". DECODE read the day as "2". Passages (1)-(35)
  in graphic signs (about 60 types), ending with the signature in cipher.
- The copyist's margin glosses fix the letter. "sins" and "desracine" at passage (9) are *il rallie les siens …
  de desraciner du tout l'evangile de ce royaume*, which is in the Villefranche letter of 12 Jan (Forbes, *Full View*
  ii 272-274, "Du camp a Villefranche, 12 Jan. 1563"), not in the 2 Jan letter from Meur (Forbes ii 247-249).
- *CSP Foreign* vi no. 76: "Admiral Coligni to the Queen … Camp at Villefranche, 12 Jan. Signed. Orig. almost entirely
  in cipher, deciphered." So the English decipherment is on the original (TNA SP 70), and Forbes printed the clear text.
- Closed as already solved. Key: DECODE R335 (TNA SP 106/1 f.69, 'A Cipher of the P. of Condé', endorsed 2 Dec 1562) has the same graphic signs; on it '&' and '14' are nulls and 'ω' = 'La Royne d'Angleterre', which is the heading line of f.142 ('… & 14 ω …'). Not applied beyond that check.
- f.143 lower half starts another "L'Amiral de France à la Reine d'Angleterre" letter ("Si …", passages (1)-(7)).
  That letter, and the passages (15)-(24) at the top of f.148, belong to catalogue 93 (R9237/R9239/R9240, "Amiral to
  D. Angle" = Coligny to the Queen of England). The glosses there ("delivr", "surprendre", "escrire") match Forbes's
  printed Coligny letters of late January (for example "surprendre le dict Lyon par escalade", Forbes ii c. 319).
  Catalogue 93 is very likely in print too. That was not checked passage by passage here.
