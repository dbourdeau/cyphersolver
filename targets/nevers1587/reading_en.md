## f. 28r-v (DECODE R4285): 27 March 1587, from Randan

Monseigneur, I have received by this lackey the letter it pleased you to write me, receiving too much honour from the
continuation of your good graces, of which you will never make worthy a more obedient servant than I am to you. I
praise God for your happy return, and doubly for the hope it gives me that you will come for some days to Nevers,
where I shall have the honour of going to kiss your hands very humbly. And to answer what you are pleased to send me,
I will tell you that, if I had thought that the journey I made to Lyon deserved to be reported to you, I would have
done so; and I beg you very humbly to believe that I went there on my private affairs. Indeed Messieurs de Tournon and
St Vidal were there, with Monsieur the Bishop of Le Puy, to settle with Monsieur de Mandelot and the people of his
charge on the occasion of the war in Vivarais, the way in which the deputies went to ask the King's permission; and
at that very time **94** [the marquis] arrived there, who sold an estate to **6** [M. d'Entragues], for the bargain of
which they had referred themselves to Monsieur of Lyon: that is what was done there, which did not seem to me worthy
of troubling you with. As for what concerns **23** [the Queen of Navarre], I have never wished to meddle with it, and
beg you very humbly to believe so; I know how much respect I owe in those quarters, and therefore I honour them
without involving myself further. As for that, Monseigneur, about which it pleased you to want to take too much
trouble for me some days ago [?], it is not from then nor from this hour that I recognise the very great obligations I
have to you, but from all time, you doing me too much honour in loving me, as I know; therefore I will tell you freely
that I can make no resolution in this matter without having had the good fortune to talk with you for an hour, and
meanwhile I beg you in all humility to leave things as they are. God, if it please Him, will keep me such as I ought
to be, and make me more fortunate than I have been until this hour; and whatever I am, I shall say with truth [only]
that I am your very faithful servant, begging you very humbly always to love me as such and keep me in your good
graces, which I salute with all humility, praying God, Monseigneur, to give you in very perfect health a very happy
and very long life.

## f. 30r (DECODE R4286): 4 April 1587

Monseigneur, I have sent back to you the lackey you were pleased to despatch to me, who I think will not have reached
you so soon, having gone to look for you at Chenonceau, where you sent me word you would stay until after Easter; and
I hope henceforth to amend the fault I have committed in not letting you know the news of these parts; but it was
almost impossible for me, given the distance of the places and the uncertainty I was in about your stay. I praise God
for your happy return to Paris, and beg Him to grant me the grace of being able to show you by worthy effect the very
faithful service I owe and have vowed to you, as much as I have obligation and undying will to do so. **45** [the Duc
d'Épernon] has been in very great alarm passing near **π ^ ꭓ é ∞ ƥ F ƨ h Ŧ ◊ 7 o** [Lyon · null null · and · this ·
country], and without any cause, for I can assure you no one ever thought of doing him displeasure; these are the
charities people lend one another in these times, which, I am sure, will fall back on their inventors, for the truth,
if it is not already, will very soon be known. That is all I know that deserves to be written to you, Monseigneur;
and therefore, begging you very humbly always to honour me with your good graces as your very obedient servant, I
will kiss your hands in all humility, with prayer to God, Monseigneur, that He give you in very perfect health a very
happy life.

This 4th of April 1587.

Your very humble and very obedient servant [monogram].
