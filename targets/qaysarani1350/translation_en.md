# Translation: Penn LJS 51, f. 106v (Ibn al-Qabāqibī's cipher verses)

Many a girl with drowsy eyelids: love has made my heart [her] dwelling, and my cheek the ground beneath her.
I lay awake, though my eyelids are sound, and did not sleep; she slept and did not lie awake, though her eyelids are sick.

("[her]" stands for a damaged word of line 2 that is not read; لها is what the metre allows.)

("Sick" eyelids are the languid eyes of the beloved, a standard figure of Arabic love poetry; the second verse plays
the sound-eyed sleepless lover against the sick-eyed sleeper.)
