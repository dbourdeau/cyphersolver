# Catalogue 1565: Camillo Capilupi to Ferrante Gonzaga, Worms, 8 May 1545 (cc. 15-16), English

{QUAN} called me this morning and told me to write to Your Excellency these things, which I shall write in cipher, charging me to keep them as secret as I can, and he would wish them to be deciphered by the person you trust most.

About the matter of {SED}, which {IMA} [?knows]: his vanity and instability, who now wants and now does not, loves and unloves; and so you cannot go wrong in answering him as you did, showing that you have never been separated from [?him], rather that you have [...] [?always] given with [...] as the service of {ULIR} required [?and] his merits, and that it would grieve you if His Holiness had thought otherwise. {QUAN}

About Prince Doria, he says he stands well [?with] {SED} because of the affairs [of] Genoa, [?in which] the m. {SED} can be of use, and because of the business he has with messer Adam, and that he too is a person who wants to be visited often with letters, which, he not having done, since it is not his custom nor has he leisure to write empty letters, has brought him complaints at times made through the ambassador of Genoa.

About the marriage with Signor Ottavio, he says that Farnese comes to court with words alone, not that he tried this himself, but he has found a way with [...] of painting for him the ruin of the Pope and [?of] the Church if the Pope did not come to terms with the Emperor, it seeming to him to matter greatly {QUAN} to the affairs of {ULIR}, both toward the [?fin] and toward Germany and France, that it should be believed {QUAN} [...] that the Pope and the Emperor were well agreed together, but that for all that they are not yet [?in accord], and if Farnese does not [?come] well resolved, he [?would have] done better to stay away.

This preamble he gave me to show me that whatever will be treated will be started with him, and according to the [?proceeding] of Farnese, whether fine or sincere, one will be able to judge whether [?that] marriage of his too moves purely or else maliciously, of which {QUAN} {IMA} will be informed. And about the matter of "mi" (a clear word, not resolved) he has thought that, since {LUS} is here, who is a person who seeks to stand well with everyone, he would of himself complain to him about it, showing that he has heard of it from Naples and greatly wonders at it, and would have him of himself write a good letter to "mi", warning him lovingly to {QUAN} [...] [?have] account of whom he [?should]; and he does not approve that these things should reach the ears of ulir, so as not to give him cause to think [?without] reason of these bad understandings [?between] the one and the other.

Then about what is to become of the life of {IMA}, he says that whether or not an enterprise of war is undertaken this year, he thinks that {IMA} [?is] not to stay at [?Mantua], and therefore that you should consider a little well, in case [...] {QUAN} war should not be made, whether it will suit {IMA} better, or else to be summoned here too, and to [?come] and then [?to have] standing in your own affairs, because perhaps the affairs of Monsieur d'Orleans will be treated here, where [?] or to go [?elsewhere], and that he then reserves his opinion.

When he spoke to me of Farnese, taking the occasion, I reminded him of the priory. He answered that you should not doubt, because, he being here, {IMA} is more, more present than if you were here, since he could say to him things that Your Excellency would not say. [...] {QUAN} [?of] Augsburg wishes {IMA} here, that you might help him to urge {ULIR} to subdue the Lutherans, that is some of them, because it seems to him that he will never again have {QUAN} [...] so fine an occasion as now.

From Worms, 8 May 1545.

About that abbey of which Your Excellency writes, I spoke with {QUAN}. At the end of the talk, when I asked him whether he had anything else to write, he answered me: [?Idiaquez] has come; they write of [?Spain] in a bad way, but of this I shall write afterwards. I asked nothing more.
