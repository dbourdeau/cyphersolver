# Edmondes, camp before Laon, 9 July 1594: modernized reading

Modern spelling and punctuation of the passages that carry cipher, with the clear text around them. What came out
of the cipher is in *italics*; [... n] marks printed cipher letters not read, sized by words (Holes are counted by
words, 14 Nov 2026): the letters carry no word division, so n is the least number of words they can make, an estimate
(the letters are counted in `reading/reading_words.tsv`); extent unknown: the two bare [...] in the governor's sentence
(after "some of the" and after "assurance in"), where no cipher letter is missing and the sentence lacks a word the
print does not show; [55], 4s, 300 and 26 are the calendar's person signs and
figures, not deciphered. Words chosen by sense or resting on an assumed misprint are marked in `reading.md`, not here.
The rest of the letter (the war in Artois and Hainault, the "dog in the manger") is in clear and is not repeated.

---

Since the decision taken here, after Sir Roger Williams's negotiation, that the Duke of Montpensier should return to
Brittany, the Duke has pressed [55] hard to go with him and help him in that service. He gives as his reason that,
because of the many matters *needing his presence in Normandy*, he will not also be able *to hold [... 1] the
government [... 1]*, so that he would *only make a journey there to install him in it*.

Thereupon [55], speaking with [the m-sign], told [it] that he wished to have [the ho-sign's] advice and opinion on
this, and that if [the oo-sign] encouraged him he would accept the offer more willingly. First, as to its
usefulness, he *argued how convenient that country is for keeping correspondence with us*; and the like advantage
*of neighbourhood* that it has from 4s on one side and on the other *from those of the religion [the Protestants] of
Poitou*. He was sure that, *once established there, he could get some port towns into his hands to give us a
footing* in case of need, and he had the same hope *of places on the river*.

Second, as to how easy it would be: *he is wanted by the country, and the governor of Rennes* has promised him that
as soon as he arrives there *he will declare himself for the religion, which he has long professed in secret; that
some of the* [...] have given him assurance in [...] *[... 1] given of [... 1] (v)ehement to the cause of religion; that
he can count on help from Poitou in any need*; and he hopes that "I" (so printed) will, for the love they bear
him, *also give him some assistance*. But since 300 alone can keep that country, and without its help it will be lost, he
asks me to advise him how 300 is disposed to take up its relief, so that, if the province is otherwise doomed, he may
avoid the dishonour of its being lost in his hands.

[the clear text on the war in Artois and Hainault is left out here] I asked him whether, if that war went forward, he meant to serve in it, in which case the proposal for
Brittany would come to nothing. He answered that *the Marshal of Biron* has taken such a hold *[... 1] in governing the
army under* 26, since in his ambition he wants to do everything, that 26 does not care to cross him; so that 26,
finding he cannot use him here as his reputation requires, is willing to employ him elsewhere, to enter Luxembourg
with some forces, where he would try to make himself safe, unless he is drawn off to Brittany instead. From this and
my earlier talk with him, made by your Lordship's command, to keep him here at the Court, I find he has no wish for
it, both as long as the other has the credit of *commanding the army*, and because he says he can use himself to
better profit elsewhere.

---

"hopeth that I will for the love they bear him": the calendar's clear text has "I", but the verb that follows in
the cipher is "extend themselfes", so "I" may stand for a sign or a plural the editor did not show. The hope of
assistance is [55]'s (he "hopeth"), not the governor of Rennes's. Whether "they" are the English or the Protestants
of Poitou is not settled by the text.
