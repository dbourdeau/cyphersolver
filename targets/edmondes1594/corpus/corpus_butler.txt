Since my last by S Roger Williams, there hath fallen out noe other matter of action here, the tyme having ben hitherto spent in travelling in Certeine mynes, wch they are preparing to plaie, at such tyme as they will make their battrys: neither also vntill yesterdaie came not the rest of the Canon, and muytions from Compeigne, wch they nowe shape wthin fewe daies to put both into profe of effect. Manie unexpected difficulties haue risen, both in the hardnes of the enterprize, and our sundrie great wantes, as doth render the successe of the action doubtfull: and that also dyuers of the foote companies of french doe daylie disband, and runne to the enemie, whereof some haue cast themselfes into the Towne. The Counte Charles is retyred beyond Han, and drawing towardes the lowe Countries: They saie to goe to receiue a renforcement of 4 or 5000 men, and then to retourne to raise this seege in the doubt whereof, the k: doth still retaine wth him his nobyllitie, saving such as he must necessarelie lycence: but some rather thincke they haue a meaning to attempt some other place, to seeke to dyuerte the k: from this, if in the meane tyme he doe not Carrie it by dilligence Monsr du Mayne vnderstanding that Amyens was in tearmes to render to the k: notwthstanding anie thing that the duke of Aumale could labor to the contrarie by his contynuall residence there, is gonne thither in dilligence to see if he can rassure them to him; whereof the k: hath yett noe other newes then that he is entred the Towne wth a fewe wth him, but denyed to be receiued wth anie troupes. The k: hath there a stronge formed partye who haue promised to declare for him if Monsr. du Mayne, doe not discouuer the intention, and break the pourpose thereof. The said duke hath wth great persecution contynued his instance of offer to treate of a peace to the wch the k: sheweth hitherto to geiue noe belief, and resolued as he hath made him answeare, not to treate wth him in other degree then concerning his perticuler; for that to treate wth him as head of a partye, wch is broken, and goeth extinguished, were to geiue him restablishement in the power and Creditt thereof.
It hath been aduertised the k: wthin these fewe daies that the armye levyed in Savoye and Mylan, is arryued at Thurin, said to consist of 12000 men, wch they pretend shall descend for lyon, wch sollicitacion the Marquis of St Surlyn, Brother to the duke of Nemors, hath ben to make towards the duke of Savoy geiuing assurance of great probabyllitie by the passages wch he holdeth on the ryuer, and otherwise, vppon lyon for the recouuerie of the said Towne to them, wch being knowne here to be true and that the combustion is thereby great in the said countrie as doth threaten great perill to the same, it hath ben represented to the k: that he must necessarelie make a Journey thither in his owne person to assure the said countrie; wherein that it will both spetiallie serue to pourpose in that kind, as also to make diuers places to declare for him at his arryuall there, and in that regard also is perswaded to take his waie throughe Bourgoundie. These considerations and necessities haue nowe againe wthin these fewe daies newelie resolued the said Journey of lyons to beginne shortlie after this seege ended. And that the armye here shall in the meane tyme be left vnder the conduction of the duke of Neuers, to empeache the enemies proceadinges in these partes, And to the duke Memorancy the k: hath written that if on his waie he find anie thinge attempted against lyons, that then he make staie wth his forces in that countrie to make hedd to the enemies desseignes, in attending the ks coming but if not that then he doe wth speede aduance hither, who being arryued the k: would comitt this armye to him, and then might wth more safetie, for the feare of perill to befall him here in his absence, perfourme his Journey to lyons. Monsr. Ballagny arryued here 6 or 7 daies since and hath brought wth him 1000 foote and 500 horse. He is made an other Mareshall, and hath geiuen him the Counte of Marle. He hath not yett broken the truce wch he hath made for the Countrey of Cambresy wth the Archd. Ernest wch he alleageth to be to preserue the same from Spoyle, vntill the k shall resolue to a Certein warre in those partes of Artoys and Hennault to the wch he is much sollicited as well by him; as the States of the lowe Countries to emploie therein the succors wch they send him.
The duke of Bouillon is arryued also this daie who hath brought with him 500 horse and 300 harquebuse on horsebacke. Chasteau Portian is declared for the k: according as he attended. The Marquis of Coeuures, Monsr d Estres sonne dyed this daie of the hurte wch he receaued in a skirmishe, when the enemies army was here. And so I most humbly take my leaue ffrom the Campe before laons the 20th of June 1594. The king hath an Intelligence in this Towne for a Porte to be delyuered him; I praie god it proue not a fraude. A reporte is presentlie come hither that the enemie is gonne to beseege dourleans to diuert this seege but it is not yett here belieued. It is also instantlie aduertised the k. that they are in a great combustion in Amyens, whereof the k: knoweth not yett what interpretacion to make, whether good or badd for him. If that people will, they haue a good opportunitie to lay hould on Monsr. du Mayne.

The hope of Assurance wch the k. did promise to himself in Amyens, Monsr. du Mayne hath by his presence there frustrated, and reduced that people to their former yoake, yett without receiving anie garrison so as what hath not ben nowe effected, tyme maie hereafter wourke. The enterprise held with a Capten of this Towne for the delyuerie of a Porte to the k: as by my last I aduertised yor l; having had contynuance vntill wthin these fewe daies to whom was promised a reward of 2000 Crownes, and thereof covenanted that the one half should be before hand aduanced vnto him, the wch as soone as he had receiued, he vsed dirision in steede of the expected perfourmance. The next daie Monsr Gyury going to the Trenches, was in his retourne vnhappelie slayne, wth a smale shott in the head, wch disaster hath bredd so great a sorrowe both in the king and the whole armye as the like hath not ben here expressed for anie man, as he well deserued being one of the most accomplished gentellmen of this realme. These twoe daies past haue ben spent in treaties wth the President Janin and others of the Towne by whom was demanded a suspension of armes for eight daies, and to treate of a peace generall, or otherwise that they could not vnderstand to anie composition. The k: accepted to treate of a peace but not to the forbearing or slacking of his seege wch brake of the disputes and thereuppon yesterdaie the battry played contynuing from the morning vntill eight of the Clocke in the evening, when having made twoe reasonable breaches, in twoe Towers wch were beaten in fflancke, it was comanded to goe to the assaulte. The breache of the battrye was appointed to be entred by the kinges light horsemen and frenche regimentes of foote accompanied wth some Swisse, wherein finding a warme resistance they turned hedd, wthout the losse of anie man of sorte saving the Barron of Montoisier a gentellman of quallitie of the relligion of Poictou and an other called Beauregard. At the said breache the Counte Soissons and the duke of Bouillon had prepared themselfes to haue geiuen, but the k: would not permitt them. If those there had ben better seconded wth some nomber of gent. who are onlie of resolution and the ffrenche foote of noe wourthe, they had vndoubtedlie Carried the Towne.
There is a practise in hand to procure St disier to revolte from the duke of Guise in the example of Vitry wch succeeding, then hath he no other places at his deuotion but rheims, and Roucroy, and the latter not well assured to him. It is also treated to drawe the Marquis of Bellisle to the kinges seruice, and for that he pretendeth that if he quyte the partye of Monsr de Mercure that he shall loose Bellisle, for not being able to keepe the same against him, it is in speache that the k: shall buye it of him; and geiue him in exchange the Countie of Clermont. The k: is aduertised that in those partes of Brittaigne the nobyllitie doe dailie fall awaie from the duke Mercure, and growe to declare for him.
