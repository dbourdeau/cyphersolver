# Bernardo d’Alamanno de’ Medici to Averardo de’ Medici, 17 April 1432

**The enciphered passages are read in full.** Writing from Lorenzana, Bernardo reports Florence’s order to strip the fortifications from five castles. He questions the wisdom of moving against Chianni and asks whether Florence really means his soldiers to plunder the inhabitants. He calls that prospect *una gran crudeltà*. [April letter, recto](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6242) · [Address and document number](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6243) · [Cyphersolver 2954](https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/catalogue.html#2954)

The edition covers the letter on c. 208r. The address on the verso and the separate clear postscript on c. 209r are linked below. Read the [Italian text](reading-it.md) alongside the [English translation](translation-en.md). The [line transcription](transcription.tsv) encloses each stretch of cipher signs in braces. It preserves the manuscript line breaks, expands unambiguous abbreviations and distinguishes uncertain clear words with brackets. Double brackets enclose cancelled writing, which is left out of the continuous reading. The number written as 25 is certain, though the precise relation between the mules and the goods is unclear. ## The letter in English

Bernardo received a letter that morning from the Ten. He has acknowledged their instructions and writes to Averardo about them.

> I am to dismantle and remove every fortification from all these castles, namely Parlascio, Castelnuovo, Santa Luce, San Regolo and Lorenzana. And I am to try to get my hands on Chianni.

He needs supplies from the man through whom he expects to obtain the necessities. Once supplied, he will make every effort. He cannot see how to take Chianni or keep it safe. It could scarcely be relieved afterwards. The wood lies on one side and Lajatico, Orciatico and Pietracassa intervene on the other. If nothing is left on his side, Chianni will be left “in the pincers” (*nelle forbici*). He will nevertheless do what the authorities want.

> I should also like to know whether it is their intention that these soldiers should plunder the property of these poor people. It seems to me a great cruelty.

He turns to property of the commune that is of little use. He has written to Giovanni Cirioni (reading of surname provisional) to see if he can sell it or send it to him, and mentions twenty-five mules. He holds the inventory and fears that he would bear the loss if the property went missing.

Bernardo asks Averardo to intercede with the Ten for either this place or Antonio Mattaione’s. He brings up leaving Chianni, and says it would not be the worst course to leave one of these places and make a choice among the others. He commends himself to Averardo, then asks him to remind Giovanni del Vinaio of a letter about his private affairs. The adjacent slip, c. 209r, adds a postscript in ordinary writing. The register groups both leaves under document 196. ## How the reading was checked

The letter of **8 December 1431**, filza V, doc. 140, has a contemporary decipherment above numerous stretches of cipher. Twenty sign values were taken from that page. Examples include the two signs for *a* in *cassa*, the repeated *t* in *tutti*, and the distinction between *r* and *c* in *ribandire* and *cassa*. Five more values were entered from the April page. The same substitutions then apply to the names, verbs and ordinary prose. The transcribed April text has 339 sign positions. The December table supplies 294 and the five additions supply 45. Those counts measure key coverage. Word identification and transcription uncertainty are recorded separately. [8 December letter](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6122) · [Archive inventory, filza V](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/asfi/fileadmin/risorse/allegati_inventari_on_line/s13_inventario.pdf)

The **9 December 1431** check, doc. 143, uses the twenty December values alone. Its two lines give *u?rra sec?* and *mandarci bu?ha?no con la sua conp?na*. I read the interlinear gloss as *verrà seco*, *mandarci Bughagno con la sua conpagna*. With u/v treated alike, the difference is six letter edits against 41 gloss letters, versus at least 23 edits for 1,000 shuffled keys. The omission of the *a* in the cipher spelling of *conpagna* contributes one edit. The check uses a separate letter, but I had seen its image and parts of its gloss during selection. Transcription and scoring were done by the same reader. This is a cross-letter consistency check with no predeclared threshold. [9 December letter](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6130)

**Antonio’s identity has independent manuscript support.** In a letter filed in the same volume, he signs himself Antonio Mattaione de’ Ghatani and dates it at San Reghulo. The inventory assigns it to 13 November 1431. Other correspondents write from Parlascio and Castelnuovo in the same file. Antonio’s signature and the archive’s entries for the other two correspondents corroborate names recovered with the key. The reference to “Antonio’s place” in April therefore probably means San Regolo. That last identification is an inference. [Antonio’s own letter, doc. 41](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=5912) · [Parlascio, doc. 40](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=5910) · [Castelnuovo, doc. 42](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=5914)

## Notes on the readings

- **The inventory clause.** The last four signs, `onDE`, give *refe*. The *Grande dizionario della lingua italiana* records *a suo refe* as an expression meaning “a proprio danno.” Bernardo uses the first person, *a mio refe*. In context, *non vorrei perdendosi andasono a mio refe* means “I would not want to bear the loss if they went missing.” [GDLI, vol. XV, p. 675, *refe*, sense 3](https://www.gdli.it/contesti/refe/1158006)
- **The request to the Ten.** Bernardo writes *se vi paressi di* in ordinary script. The cipher begins with *ra-* at the end of line 18 and continues on line 19. Compare the shape of the *d* and the angular finishing stroke of *i* in *la roba di* (line 13) and *di pocho utile* (line 15). Reading these two letters as cipher would produce *fi*. [Manuscript recto, lines 13, 15 and 18](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6242) The clear *a X* after Mattaoni matches the Roman numeral in the opening reference to the Ten. In December the glossed surname has the sequence *Mataioni*, and Antonio’s own signature supplies the identification.
- **Three spellings.** The April key yields *Lorezano*, *Mattaoni* and *lascire*. Lorenzana appears in the dateline and the archive inventory, and Mattaione signs his own letter. The unciphered *lasciando* just before *lascire* supports *lasci[a]re*. The named people and places are identifiable even where the literal spelling differs.
- **Prior art.** Cyphersolver’s 15 November 2026 vetting records checks of DECODE, Tomokiyo, Lasry and the inventory. Searches by sender, date, place and distinctive wording on 17 November returned the inventory and related sources. I found no published decipherment of doc. 196. The claim here is a reproducible reading of the manuscript, with priority open to further evidence.

The project’s explicit-hole scorer counts 381 words in the continuous Italian reading, with a hole rate of 0.021 (read share 0.979). That figure includes the surrounding clear Italian, its doubtful words, and bracketed editorial supplies. The 339-sign count above is confined to cipher.

## Reproduction

Run `python3 verify.py --controls` in this directory. The script checks the saved literal reading, applies the December-only key to doc. 143, and reruns the fixed-seed key-permutation comparison. The control preserves the distribution of homophones, the unknown signs, the sign transcription and the proposed word divisions.

- `december-key.tsv` gives the twenty values and their source words.
- `december-check.tsv` gives the check-letter transcription and comparison gloss.
- `april-key.tsv` gives the five added values and the April words that support them.
- `april.tsv` gives the transcription and edited text by physical line. Spaces in the sign column indicate proposed word boundaries. The single-character labels stand for distinct manuscript shapes. In particular `o` is a plain oval, `C` is the oval with a long right stroke, `B` has an upright on the long stroke, `X` is the tailed cross, `x` the simple cross, `+` the division sign, and `G` the crossed descender. The 9-like signs can be difficult to separate by their tails. Capital and lower-case labels are distinct.
- `literal.tsv` gives the exact result of applying the two tables, before editorial repairs. The transcriptions were made from the Florence archive images at native resolution. All manuscript links refer to the archive’s copies.
