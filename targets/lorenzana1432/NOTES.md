# Bernardo d’Alamanno de’ Medici to Averardo de’ Medici, 17 April 1432

**The enciphered passages are read in full.** Writing from Lorenzana, Bernardo reports Florence’s order to strip the fortifications from five castles. He questions the wisdom of moving against Chianni and asks whether Florence really means his soldiers to plunder the inhabitants. He calls that prospect *una gran crudeltà*. [April letter, recto](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6242) · [Address and document number](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6243) · [Cyphersolver 2954](https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/catalogue.html#2954)

The edition covers the letter on c. 208r. The address on the verso and the separate clear postscript on c. 209r are linked below. Read the [Italian text](reading-it.md) alongside the [English translation](translation-en.md). The [line transcription](transcription.tsv) encloses each stretch of cipher signs in braces. It preserves the manuscript line breaks, expands unambiguous abbreviations and distinguishes uncertain clear words with brackets. Double brackets enclose cancelled writing, which is left out of the continuous reading. The number written as 25 is certain, though the precise relation between the mules and the goods is unclear. Codex transcribed and deciphered the images, compared the glosses, and prepared the translations and Python verification for Andrew Aymeloglu. Cyphersolver’s November MAP survey identified the letter and pointed to the December glosses.

## The letter in English

Bernardo received a letter that morning from the Ten. He has acknowledged their instructions and writes to Averardo about them.

> I am to dismantle and remove every fortification from all these castles, namely Parlascio, Castelnuovo, Santa Luce, San Regolo and Lorenzana. And I am to try to get my hands on Chianni.

He needs supplies from the man through whom he expects to obtain the necessities. Once supplied, he will make every effort. He cannot see how to take Chianni or keep it safe. It could scarcely be relieved afterwards. The wood lies on one side and Lajatico, Orciatico and Pietracassa intervene on the other. If nothing is left on his side, Chianni will be left “in the pincers” (*nelle forbici*). He will nevertheless do what the authorities want.

> I should also like to know whether it is their intention that these soldiers should plunder the property of these poor people. It seems to me a great cruelty.

He turns to property of the commune that is of little use. He has written to Giovanni Cirioni (reading of surname provisional) to see if he can sell it or send it to him, and mentions twenty-five mules. He holds the inventory and fears that he would bear the loss if the property went missing.

Bernardo asks Averardo to intercede with the Ten for either this place or Antonio Mattaione’s. He brings up leaving Chianni, and says it would not be the worst course to leave one of these places and make a choice among the others. He commends himself to Averardo, then asks him to remind Giovanni del Vinaio of a letter about his private affairs. The adjacent slip, c. 209r, adds a postscript in ordinary writing. The register groups both leaves under document 196. [Slip](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6244) · [Filza V image register](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/unita-archivistica/?id=799)

The term *mettere a saccomanno* means to plunder. The sign string runs continuously across the compound. The words *questi poveri* close one manuscript line and *huomini* opens the next. ## How the reading was checked

The letter of **8 December 1431**, filza V, doc. 140, has a contemporary decipherment above numerous stretches of cipher. Twenty sign values were taken from that page. Examples include the two signs for *a* in *cassa*, the repeated *t* in *tutti*, and the distinction between *r* and *c* in *ribandire* and *cassa*. Five more values were entered from the April page. The same substitutions then apply to the names, verbs and ordinary prose. The transcribed April text has 339 sign positions. The December table supplies 294 and the five additions supply 45. Those five values (2, E, G, D, f) are not in the table from doc. 140 and were assigned from context in this letter. Two of them, E and G, also stand under the gloss of the 9 December letter, so three (2, D, f) rest on this letter alone (see Write-up checks, 24 Nov 2026). Those counts measure key coverage. Word identification and transcription uncertainty are recorded separately. [8 December letter](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6122) · [Archive inventory, filza V](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/asfi/fileadmin/risorse/allegati_inventari_on_line/s13_inventario.pdf)

The **9 December 1431** check, doc. 143, uses the twenty December values alone. Its two lines give *u?rra sec?* and *mandarci bu?ha?no con la sua conp?na*. I read the interlinear gloss as *verrà seco*, *mandarci Bughagno con la sua conpagna*. With u/v treated alike, the difference is six letter edits against 41 gloss letters, versus at least 23 edits for 1,000 shuffled keys. The omission of the *a* in the cipher spelling of *conpagna* contributes one edit. The check uses a separate letter, but I had seen its image and parts of its gloss during selection. Transcription and scoring were done by the same reader. This is a cross-letter consistency check with no predeclared threshold. [9 December letter](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6130)

**Antonio’s identity has independent manuscript support.** In a letter filed in the same volume, he signs himself Antonio Mattaione de’ Ghatani and dates it at San Reghulo. The inventory assigns it to 13 November 1431. Other correspondents write from Parlascio and Castelnuovo in the same file. Antonio’s signature and the archive’s entries for the other two correspondents corroborate names recovered with the key. The reference to “Antonio’s place” in April therefore probably means San Regolo. That last identification is an inference. [Antonio’s own letter, doc. 41](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=5912) · [Parlascio, doc. 40](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=5910) · [Castelnuovo, doc. 42](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=5914)

## Notes on the readings

- **The inventory clause.** The last four signs, `onDE`, give *refe*. The *Grande dizionario della lingua italiana* records *a suo refe* as an expression meaning “a proprio danno.” Bernardo uses the first person, *a mio refe*. In context, *non vorrei perdendosi andasono a mio refe* means “I would not want to bear the loss if they went missing.” [GDLI, vol. XV, p. 675, *refe*, sense 3](https://www.gdli.it/contesti/refe/1158006)
- **The request to the Ten.** Bernardo writes *se vi paressi di* in ordinary script. The cipher begins with *ra-* at the end of line 18 and continues on line 19. Compare the shape of the *d* and the angular finishing stroke of *i* in *la roba di* (line 13) and *di pocho utile* (line 15). Reading these two letters as cipher would produce *fi*. [Manuscript recto, lines 13, 15 and 18](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=6242) The clear *a X* after Mattaoni matches the Roman numeral in the opening reference to the Ten. In December the glossed surname has the sequence *Mataioni*, and Antonio’s own signature supplies the identification.
- **Three spellings.** The April key yields *Lorezano*, *Mattaoni* and *lascire*. Lorenzana appears in the dateline and the archive inventory, and Mattaione signs his own letter. The unciphered *lasciando* just before *lascire* supports *lasci[a]re*. The named people and places are identifiable even where the literal spelling differs.
- **Prior art.** Cyphersolver’s 15 November 2026 vetting records checks of DECODE, Tomokiyo, Lasry and the inventory. Searches by sender, date, place and distinctive wording on 17 November returned the inventory and related sources. I found no published decipherment of doc. 196. The claim here is a reproducible reading of the manuscript, with priority open to further evidence.

Counted in cipher words, the letter has 58 (a word split across two lines counts once: *isfa-sciare*, *pietra-chassa*, *ra-chomandare*). All 58 are decoded. Fifty-six give the word sign for sign; two carry a one-letter slip, marked in place as *Lore[n]zano* and *lasci[a]re*. The profile records 56 of 58 (0.966) as both the read share and the fraction read. The 339-sign count above measures key coverage, not words.

A second Codex reader checked a sample of the handwriting, including the December *Antono* and *cassa* anchors and the April inventory clause *perdendosi andasono a mio refe*. On line 5 the reader questioned the clear-text verb *tento*. The transcription, Italian reading and English translation mark it as uncertain. [Side-by-side edition](https://aaymeloglu.github.io/unsolved-ciphers/lorenzana-reading.html) · [Evidence grades and review in the merged edition](https://github.com/aaymeloglu/unsolved-ciphers/tree/f0e4a6601037806c8a755f0771be15e934bccff8/lorenzana-1432)

## Remaining gaps

None of the holes is in cipher.

- Doubtful clear words: *[?credo]* (line 1), *[?tento]* (line 5), *[?iscorio]* (lines 15-16) and *[?Cirioni]* (line 16). The readings fit the sense, but the letter forms do not settle them.
- Two passages shown as [...] in the reading, where the transcription has *[?scotta]* (line 16) and *[?madasse]* (lines 23-24). Neither gives sense.
- The *di* on line 18 is read as ordinary script. Read as cipher, its two signs would give *fi*.

## Transcription doubts

Added at the write-up, 24 Nov 2026, by the integration (an AI agent for the maintainers, not a person). The doubts are the ones these notes and the key files name. Each was looked at on the archive's image of c. 208r (1,272 px wide, the largest the server gives, enlarged three to five times with no overlay) and against the decode. No second transcription of the letter exists.

- last sign of Chianni (lines 5, 8, 20) - read: 2 on lines 5 and 20, q on line 8, both i; checked: image, decode; the angular 2 and the tailed q are different shapes on the image, and both spell Chianni.
- E, the short squat 9 (lines 3, 4, 9, 18, 20) - read: e, kept apart from 9 = p; checked: image, decode; the E of e levare, queste, refe and lascire has a short tail and the 9 of Parlascio, pare, poveri and peggio a long one, and each gives a word.
- =8onf3+8 (line 4) - read: lorezano, eight signs, edited Lore[n]zano; checked: image, decode; the image has eight signs and the transcription leaves none out, so the missing n is in the letter as written. It counts as a hole.
- =7xC2oE (line 20) - read: lascire, seven signs, edited lasci[a]re; checked: image, decode; the image has seven signs and the transcription leaves none out. It counts as a hole.
- H3yy38+2 (line 19) - read: mattaoni, eight signs; checked: image, decode; eight signs on the image. The name is Mattaione in his own signature (doc. 41); the reading keeps the letter's spelling and supplies nothing.
- di (line 18, before the cipher resumes) - read: clear di; checked: image, decode; the first letter is the rounded d of the clear hand (lines 13 and 15) and the second stroke is close to the cipher 2. As cipher the pair gives fi, which makes no word. Kept as clear text; it stays under Remaining gaps.

## Reproduction

Run `python3 verify.py --controls` in this directory. The script checks the saved literal reading, applies the December-only key to doc. 143, and reruns the fixed-seed key-permutation comparison. The control preserves the distribution of homophones, the unknown signs, the sign transcription and the proposed word divisions.

- `december-key.tsv` gives the twenty values and their source words.
- `december-check.tsv` gives the check-letter transcription and comparison gloss.
- `april-key.tsv` gives the five added values and the April words that support them.
- `april.tsv` gives the transcription and edited text by physical line. Spaces in the sign column indicate proposed word boundaries. The single-character labels stand for distinct manuscript shapes. In particular `o` is a plain oval, `C` is the oval with a long right stroke, `B` has an upright on the long stroke, `X` is the tailed cross, `x` the simple cross, `+` the division sign, and `G` the crossed descender. The 9-like signs can be difficult to separate by their tails. Capital and lower-case labels are distinct.
- `literal.tsv` gives the exact result of applying the two tables, before editorial repairs. The transcriptions were made from the Florence archive images at native resolution. All manuscript links refer to the archive’s copies.

## Write-up checks, 24 Nov 2026

Added by the integration at the write-up (an AI agent for the maintainers, not a person). Nothing above was changed except the new Transcription doubts section, one spelling in the table of cipher passages (*rachomandare*, as the signs give it, where the table had *racchomandare*) and, after the claims review of 24 Nov 2026, the sentence on the five April values under "How the reading was checked" (see "The 9 December gloss and the April values" below).

- **Recount.** `april.tsv` has 61 groups of signs divided by spaces; three words run over a line end, so 58 cipher words. The two tables give 56 of them as the edition has them and two with a letter supplied: 56 of 58, 0.966. The transcription has 20 stretches of cipher in braces and 339 signs.
- **Key replay.** `python docs/_replay.py lorenzana1432` (adapter `replay.py`): 58 cipher words, 56 exact (96.6%), 2 marked, none unmarked; 339 of 339 signs exact. The comparison is with the literal spellings in `april.tsv`; `reading-it.md` regularises the spelling and says so.
- **Statistical validation.** `python docs/_validate.py lorenzana1432` on `validation.tsv` (the 339 signs in order, model `it-cinquecento`, order 4): the reading scores −2.268 per letter, random keys −4.803 (z 6.5), the best key annealed onto shuffled signs −3.025 (mean −3.088, z 15.1). Verdict: pass. The model is of 1490 to 1620, later than the letter.
- **Image comparison.** The 20 stretches of cipher in `transcription.tsv` were compared with the archive's image of c. 208r (1,272 px wide, enlarged three times). No sign was found to differ. At this size a difference between two similar shapes could be missed, and no person has made the comparison.
- **The December page.** On the image of doc. 140 (c. 148), line 8, the word glossed above the signs `D7o=8` looks like *farlo*. If so, D = f has a gloss in December and is not one of the values that rest on April alone. This is one look at a 1,272 px image by an AI agent. `december-key.tsv` and the counts above are left as the contributor gave them (20 values from December, 5 from April).
- **Signature and address.** The signature on c. 208r reads *Bernardo dalamanno de medici*. The address on the verso reads *[Spec]tabili et egregio viro Averar[do] de Medicis flor. patri et domino*; the abbreviation after Medicis may be *Florentie* or *florentino*, so where Averardo was that day is not taken from it.
- **Prior art, checked again.** F. C. Pellegrini, *Sulla repubblica fiorentina a tempo di Cosimo il Vecchio* (Pisa, 1889), has an appendix of 125 numbered documents, among them many letters to and from Averardo of 1431 and 1432 from the Medici archive, some with cipher. Its list of documents has none of April 1432 (no. 101 is of 28 March, no. 102 of 6 May), and a search of the full text on the Internet Archive (OCR) for Lorenzan-, Parlasci-, Santa Luce and Regolo finds only two places in the appendix. Averardo's letter of 11 October 1431 (no. 78) names the ten castles taken in the Pisan hills, San Regolo, Castelnuovo, Santa Luce, Lorenzano and Parlascio among them, and says he left "Bernardo" there as commissioner. In his letter of 2 November 1431 (no. 82) Averardo writes that the castles are useful for now and that once the war is won they will all have to be dismantled (*tutte quand'aremo vinto si vorranno disfare*). A note to that letter says that many letters of this Bernardo dated from Lorenzano are in the Medici archive (filza III, as the OCR gives it) and tell of the revolt of Chianni; it takes him for Bernardo di Antonio (Bernardetto), where this letter is signed Bernardo d'Alamanno. Also searched with no result: the OCR texts of C. Guasti, *Commissioni di Rinaldo degli Albizzi*, vol. 3 (1873), A. Fabroni, *Magni Cosmi Medicei vita* (1789), and J. Ross, *Lives of the early Medici* (1910), and web searches by sender, place, date and wording. D. Kent, *The Rise of the Medici* (1978), was not seen. No printed text or decipherment of doc. 196 was found.

The Averardo de' Medici with an English article and a print is another man (1320 to 1363 in Wikipedia's description). Commons has a photograph of the arms of a Bernardo di Alamanno de' Medici at Certaldo, which is not a portrait.
