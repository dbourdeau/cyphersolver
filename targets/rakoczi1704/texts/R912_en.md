# R912, an agent with the Swedish army to Ferenc Rákóczi II, 5 November 1707

## Translation

5 November 1707, at the King of Sweden's quarters at [?Slupce], 5 November. My Lord, having had the honour of informing
Your Highness on the 29th of last month that King Stanisław has taken from the Palatine of Bełz the office of Grand
General of the Crown and has given it to the Palatine of Kiev, [...] since he took this step only out of these
considerations and his own interests, because the King of Sweden wished it; the latter not wishing to expose himself to
the embarrassment he would have been in, as his minister says, of taking from a man who had come back to his duty an
office of which he had of necessity to be deprived in order to punish him, and to have nothing more to fear from his
[?commitments] with the Muscovites. The Poles believe that the King of Sweden, by putting so great an obstacle to the
pacification of the troubles of Poland as the proscription of the Palatine of Bełz, has designs on this kingdom quite
contrary to the interests [...] of which he has so often boasted.

It is, in my opinion, more likely that this Prince wished to keep a pretext for staying in Poland this winter so as not
to move away from Germany, nor lose sight of the general affairs of Europe. So it is given out as an almost certain
thing that he will not enter Muscovy until the coming campaign.

It is now said in the Swedish Chancery that Your Highness has in fact not accepted the Crown, but [...] to put it on your
head, but in the hope that the embarrassment of a new election in Poland and the need for a powerful diversion in
Germany against the Emperor would bring the King of Sweden to satisfy you on the things you had so often asked of him;
so that I already see a change on that side. I have also been told that, as this Prince, in the present state of his
affairs, was in no condition to do Your Highness a service, we [?could] be assured that he was no more disposed to [?do
you] harm, seeing that since the letter you had written to Marshal Rehnskiöld he had not [...] once happened to speak of
you; I did not despair that you might make him think of you, My Lord, [?in a way] that could have some good [?outcome],
if, as I had the honour to [?tell] you in my last, you wrote to him, or else to his minister, to explain the motives
of your dealings with the Tsar, in the [...] of which [93] I have spoken.

The Polish court [?is] more withdrawn. King Stanisław claims that you [...] not yet, My Lord, [...] of the return of your
friendship able to efface the impressions that a step like [...] has made on his mind, and he believes he need not be
satisfied with the sending back of the [?children] of the Palatine of Ruthenia, because Your Highness, in writing on this
subject to their father, at the same time reminded him of the loss [?of] his regiment of [?Bielck]. I shall not detain you,
My Lord, with an account of all I represented on this subject, nor point out to you what appears of itself, namely that
King Stanisław [...] the Germans [...] obliged not to satisfy Your Highness over the regiment. I shall only have the
honour of telling you that when I asked what proofs they wished you to give, My Lord, to persuade the King of Poland of
the sincerity of your feelings, [...] expect you on the compensation for the regiment, I was told on the first of these [?two] [?points]
that Your Highness should give yourself up [...] the Palatine and [...] the Palatine of Bełz [...].
On the second, that they were [?not] in a position to satisfy at present the Palatine of Ruthenia, who speaks to me constantly of the obligations he
owes Your Highness, has again asked me, as a means of arranging matters for the restoration of a good understanding,
[...] that the King his master asked for a [?letter] on Your Highness's part to this Prince. I cannot [?nevertheless] refrain from telling you, My Lord, [...]
the obligations he owes you [...] of the refuge you gave Thököly [...] that he owes you none [...].

Could one not say that the Polish court follows in its conduct, [?towards] Your Highness as much as towards the Palatine of Bełz, its
attachment to the Emperor's interests? I did not even know whether it might not [...] the King of Sweden to vex Your Highness by reason
of your ties with the Palatine of Bełz. I may be mistaken, but I believe I must not hide my thought from Your Highness,
