… of which I very much doubt, the term having passed without my hearing anything from there. I have nothing to report to
Your Lordship, except that this kingdom goes on in its usual disorders, and at the last diet, which lasted so long, nothing
good or stable was concluded. Eight lords were appointed to govern the King of Hungary and the kingdom during His Majesty's
youth; four of them, that is two spiritual and two secular, were always to stay at court, dividing the time so that each
should have his turn, to dispatch the business arising from day to day without waiting for the diet, as has been done until
now; and I was one of the first named. But I believe this order will not be kept, because it would be a very great expense
to each of the said eight, and each gladly avoids it, as did the Cardinal of Esztergom (Strigonio) and the lord palatine, who
govern everything so as to do as they please. They do not care that the matter goes forward, because if the others stayed at
court they would want to do as they please too; and the officials and ministers of the King, who have all been placed by
means of the said two lords and are at their pleasure, could no longer obey the said two as they do now, and so little by
little the great authority of the said [Cardinal] of Esztergom and of the palatine would be taken away.

It had been ordered that the lords and the whole kingdom should impose on their subjects a levy of one ducat per hearth for
the benefit of the King and kingdom, because the lords now … their life, and of it they are very sparing (?), as is known,
and the poor King is dying of hunger. The counties, that is the nobility, will not consent to this payment by their
subjects; they have gathered and decided that every county send four nobles, in the midst of another of theirs (?), for the
next feast of St James, where they say they will summon these lords to make them understand their mind. This could easily
stir up a new and dangerous fire in this kingdom, which God forbid.

… we must go back to Buda, and each must bring with him all his men-at-arms, who are unanimously to be sent against the
Turks, which will be a very great expense for all without any fruit. I have strongly dissuaded this, in general to all at
the diet and in particular to each one individually, as it seems to me a very dangerous thing, with nothing to be gained,
since these troops are sent only to make raids, which they will do when they find the enemy unprepared, otherwise not; and
