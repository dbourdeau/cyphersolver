# Philip to Ferrante Gonzaga, Monzón, 6 October 1552

Philip, then prince, wrote to Ferrante Gonzaga, governor of Milan. On c. 32v the dispatch mentions Ambassador Figueroa’s letters of 6 August and the copies they enclosed, then switches to cipher. The last paragraph on c. 33r repeats this distinctive opening and continues in ordinary Spanish across c. 33v. The separate sheet is a contemporary clear witness. It has no heading naming a decipherer or explaining whether it was made from ciphertext, a draft or another copy. The date and subscription follow the cipher on 34r. The address is on 34v. Modena, Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera F, Felipe II, sfasc. I, cc. 32–34. [Manifest](https://edl-jarvis.cultura.gov.it/meta/iiif/897e61b7-4957-4f07-b4d2-debae54659a1/manifest) · [Change of script](https://edl-jarvis.cultura.gov.it/images/iiif/db/ec37f387-91fb-4f0f-b50d-dc5bd3d120a6/full/1000,/0/default.jpg)

**Extent.** C. 33r begins mid-thought. It covers the distribution of men on royal orders (lines 1–2), the Turkish fleet and provisions on the coasts and islands (3–6), Don Diego de Mendoza’s report on Siena and an order for 20,000 escudos for the castle works (7–13), and the Florence–Doria correspondence (14–22, continuing on 33v, lines 1–7). The first three subjects have no established sign boundaries. Other matters in the dispatch, including the castle of Nice and the Cortes of Aragon, are absent. Brice Dudout had identified this October dispatch in issue 615. The maintainer reported seeing cipher on 32r and 34r. WAINUT recorded that 32v–33v were unopened. The added identification is the text of c. 33 and its correspondence with 32v. **English rendering of 33r lines 1–13, by AI agents.** “And since the troops have been distributed by order of His Majesty, that must be for the best. You will inform us of whatever else arises there. The coming of the Turkish fleet has also caused us great concern. Here I have ordered the preparations and provisions that have seemed necessary, both in the frontier and coastal lands and on the islands. Ambassador Don Diego de Mendoza has informed us in detail of the affairs of Siena and where they stood. So that work on the castle may continue, I have ordered twenty thousand escudos sent to him, which he will have received. We earnestly ask you to continue to attend to these affairs with the same care as before and to tell us what else is done in our favour and service.” The two witnesses differ in spelling and certainty at several points, especially lines 2, 4–5 and 10–11. The English gives their common sense. [Independent first reading](33r_one.txt) · [Independent second reading](33r_two.txt) · [Collation](collation.tsv)

**English rendering of the final paragraph, by AI agents.** After writing the foregoing, we received letters from the ambassador dated 6 August, with copies of letters the Duke of Florence and Prince Doria, who was at Livorno, had written him concerning […] in Siena while Don Diego de Mendoza was in Rome. […] The ambassador tells us the duke was negotiating certain agreements with that city. […] Let us know what has happened since, what has been done, whether any relief was provided with the Germans whom the prince was bringing at Livorno, what you think of these matters and what His Majesty has ordered. […] Until we know in detail the state of this business we shall remain as concerned as is fitting.

**Documentary readings.** After freezing their isolated cipher-row readings, two readers independently transcribed both faces of c. 33. They agreed on 224/237 recto word positions and 71/71 verso positions. Four recto positions were doubtful, eight differed in spelling or verb, and one was cancelled. Hyphenated words crossing a line count twice. The collation disregards capitalization, punctuation and abbreviation notation. It retains cedillas and accents, and counts only visible letters. Angle brackets supply letters editorially. Barred vi and xx with a raised m give six and 20,000. One reader doubted *Figueroa* on 33r. Two further independent readers saw only 32v and both read *figueroa*, *vj de agosto* and *con ciertas copias de*. The clear sheet supplies *cartas* for the final abbreviation on 32v. Run `python3 collate.py` for the 308-position collation.

- On 32v, first cipher row, both readers favored *saa* then *lim*. Their alternatives included *sua*, *sim* and *lin*. Neither occurrence met the certainty rule. Of 53 fixed code labels the two leading choices would give *duque* and *principe*. - On 32v, cipher rows two and three, two readers independently selected numbered specimens from keyless shape plates. Their 46 combined positions yielded 20 identical published labels and 19 unambiguous non-null values (41.3%, threshold 55%). The unchanged key emitted 29 known letters. Two uninterrupted strings, `tosob` and `demendoca`, match in manuscript order, spanning *escrito sobre* and the name *de Mendoça*. The score of 14 met the length threshold. - On 34r, final cipher row, two readers found nine ink groups. Five shared a first-choice shape, three were certain in both and one yielded a unique table value, *ra*. ## Three places raised in review, 20 November 2026

Daniel Bourdeau pointed out the following correspondences after reading the images. An AI agent then examined the same passages with his readings in view. These observations have no blind score.

- **34r, line 5.** The rightmost group has a tall crossed *t*, joined *a*, and an open final curve with a long exit stroke. The native image favors *tac*. The two earlier blind readers both preferred *tae*, one allowing *c*. The Simancas reconstruction gives *[prí]ncipe* for *tac*. In sequence the accepted *quod*, possible *saa*, proposed *tac*, and accepted *dide* correspond to the ambassador, duke, prince and His Majesty in the companion text, from 33r line 21 to 33v line 4. The prince occurs in “el Prinçipe lleuaua” on 33v line 2. This supports alignment across the closing cipher block. Its last sign remains unresolved. [Native close-up](https://edl-jarvis.cultura.gov.it/images/iiif/db/46489049-e682-405e-8b8a-bc3fed01d60a/4590,1390,600,240/full/0/default.jpg) · [Earlier title readings](../beling_codewords/NOTES.md)
- **32v, fourth cipher line after the change of script.** The line opens with an omega-shaped sign, the crossed *ɑ+* sign, and a connected group that reads *vela*. The first two forms fit the table’s *e* and *n*. In the frozen comparison the readers agreed on the second sign. The first was unresolved and the connected group was scored as individual bodies. After the preceding line’s `demendoca`, this fits the companion’s “Don Diego de Mendoça en Roma” on 33r line 18 and supports WAINUT’s earlier Rome proposal at this occurrence. Other uses of *vela/bela* require their own checks. [Original, lower block](https://edl-jarvis.cultura.gov.it/images/iiif/db/ec37f387-91fb-4f0f-b50d-dc5bd3d120a6/1030,5480,4200,690/1200,/0/default.jpg)
- **32v, lines 1 and 3 at the top of the page.** The upper block opens with the omega-shaped and horizontal loop-ended signs (table *e*, *l*) followed by the alphabetic word *quod*. On the third line two *x* forms stand together near the right edge. On 33r lines 7–10 the companion names “el Embaxor. don Diego de Mendoça” and “xx” with a raised *m*, followed by *escudos*. These two points support a match for the earlier Siena paragraph. The rest of the cipher between them and the multiplier after *xx* still require transcription. **Documentary checks.** Charles V’s 1 July 1552 letter to Ferrante has an explicitly headed *Descifrado de Su Magestad* on 82r–v. Its subjects include the Prince of Salerno and Duke of Urbino. For the October events, Sozzini describes Florence’s envoy to Siena and troops leaving the citadel on 5 August. Neither source supplies a copy of the letters Figueroa forwarded.
