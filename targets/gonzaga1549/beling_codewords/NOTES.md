# Titles in Philip’s dispatch of 6 October 1552

Two readers independently transcribed the same five shuffled crops from Philip’s letter to Ferrante Gonzaga. On c. 34r both confidently read *quod* on line 1 and *dide* on line 7. WAINUT’s key, derived from contemporary decipherments of other letters, gives **embaxador** and **Su Magestad** respectively. Neither group identifies a particular person. The other interspersed cipher signs remain unread. Issue 615 had already located the October letter and dated it to Monzón. The Simancas reconstruction of 1866 lists all three title codes. A contemporary gloss also supports *saa* for *duque*. In the Brussels dispatch of 23 December 1549, c. 76r, *Saa* begins a cipher run annotated *Duq octauio*. The October spelling remains uncertain. The repository’s earlier collation of the March 1549 cipher on c. 15r with its clear slip on c. 17r also reports *dide* and *quod*. ## The eight code positions on c. 31r, 27 May 1549

McBratney lists seven distinct code spellings as seven holes. His transcript records **eight positions** because *ne* occurs on both lines 4 and 15. The first-pass ledger already includes both. Its 382 units and 29 holes need no numerical revision. This audit uses his single-reader sign record and his diplomatic transcript of the contemporary decipherment. He had seen the block openings before copying the signs. Across 1,000 permutations of the 49 eligible entries, 876 gave zero matches, 118 gave one and six tied the observed two. The test missed its three-type mark. The original read share is retained. This supports the sense of the abbreviated ambassador word, while the sense of *ne* remains open.
