# Parma in the letters of November and December 1549

When Pope Paul III died, who would control Parma while the cardinals chose his successor? Ottavio Farnese wanted the city back. Camillo Orsini held it. Charles V had to tell Ferrante Gonzaga, his governor in Milan, how far he could go for Ottavio. Three sources preserve different stages of their exchange.

**14 and 20 November.** On 14 November Charles gave Gonzaga discretion to act in two circumstances. On c. 61v the first turns on Orsini’s conduct and Parma passing outside papal obedience. The second, continuing on c. 62r, concerns Orazio Farnese or another person taking possession. WAINUT read these clauses in cipher. Daniel Bourdeau and Brice Dudout compared the November readings. Giuliano Goselini, Gonzaga’s secretary, later tells the story in Italian. He introduces the same two cases in the same order with “in due casi” and makes the danger from France explicit in the first. He also records Gonzaga’s question about the cardinals defending Parma for the Church, a subject of the 20 November letter. Goselini summarizes the decisions in his own words and gives neither November date in this passage. **29 November, Milan.** Gonzaga told Charles that Ottavio had sent the Count of Lodron with two proposals. Cardinal Farnese and his party would support Charles in the papal election. Ottavio also asked for Siena, offering Parma, the district of Piacenza, Novara and his possessions in Naples. Gonzaga welcomed the offer of support but declined to promise Siena. He enclosed copies of his letters to Diego de Mendoza and the Cardinal of Mantua. The English calendar prints an extract and cites the Archivo General de Simancas, Estado 1196. [*Calendar of State Papers, Spain*, IX, pp. 479–480, 29 November](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/cal-state-papers/spain/vol9/pp465-481#mt1-55)

**9 December.** Charles’s answer survives at the Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Modena, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera K, Karl V, sfasc. IV, cc. 71–75. Folio 72r names the count, the election and the proposed exchange. Charles approves Gonzaga’s answer because it avoided committing him further. The next paragraph takes up his letter to Mendoza. The exterior, c. 75v, addresses Gonzaga as governor of Milan and bears a December endorsement and the year “49”. Goselini also reproduces Gonzaga’s later defence of his conduct. Here Gonzaga gives the date explicitly, “VIIII. di Dicembre … MDXLVIIII”, and quotes Charles in Spanish. The first quotation follows the lower paragraph of c. 74v. Both say that Camillo might “entregar Parma” to the French and compare that prospect to their occupying Milan. The distinctive sequence identifies the same passage across the manuscript and the early printed book. The folio is in ordinary Spanish writing and includes corrections and a marginal summary. This comparison supplies historical context for the 14 November cipher. It settles none of the disputed November spellings. WAINUT’s published reading stands at 817 of 910 words.
