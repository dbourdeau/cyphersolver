# Parma in the correspondence of October and December 1549

Charles V’s letter to Ferrante Gonzaga of 14 November distinguishes Camillo Orsini’s custody of Parma under Paul III from what might follow the pope’s death. Two dispatches from Rome describe Orsini’s conduct on either side of the vacancy. A third gives a parallel for the disputed verb on c. 61v. The comparisons below use August von Druffel, ed., *Briefe und Akten zur Geschichte des sechzehnten Jahrhunderts*, I (Munich, 1873). [Modena manuscript](https://edl-jarvis.cultura.gov.it/meta/iiif/bf3cf88e-72bf-4608-8e75-a0390e17284b/manifest)

**27 October, Diego Lasso to King Ferdinand.** Lasso reports that Ottavio left Rome on 20 October with three servants to enter Parma. The pope sent orders and news to Camillo, who refused what the duke wanted. This places Camillo in control of admission while Paul III lived. [No. 343, pp. 293–294](https://archive.org/download/briefeundaktenz00kommgoog/briefeundaktenz00kommgoog.pdf#page=319)

**8 December, Diego Hurtado de Mendoza to Charles V.** Mendoza summarizes Camillo’s letter and instruction to the College. Camillo said he had received Parma from a living pope and Ottavio. He would hand it over to a new living pope and to Ottavio if the pope wished. Until then he would guard Parma and account to Ottavio for its revenues and justice. Druffel identifies his Vienna source as a copy of Mendoza’s cipher dispatch. The terms reach us through the ambassador’s report. [No. 355, heading on p. 313](https://archive.org/download/briefeundaktenz00kommgoog/briefeundaktenz00kommgoog.pdf#page=338) · [Terms on p. 316](https://archive.org/download/briefeundaktenz00kommgoog/briefeundaktenz00kommgoog.pdf#page=341)

**24 December, Charles V to Mendoza.** Charles recalls instructing Ferrante to correspond with Duke *Octavio* about recovering Parma. In the next paragraph, on Cardinal Verallo, he writes “y assi os remittimos que si” and leaves the decision to Mendoza. This supports the sense of the delegation on c. 61v. Druffel prints *Octavio* and *remittimos* on the same page. In the November cipher a single cross occurs nine times in the duke’s name and twice in the verb. The December spellings would give that cross two different expansions. Druffel also describes adjusting doubled letters, particularly Spanish *ss*. The November value **tt/ct** remains open at all eleven positions.
