# Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga, Brussels, 14 November 1549

Native archive images support ten proposed local adjudications among the 23 contested spellings and words identified by the project. The existing sign records support one additional spelling. Four favor the project reading on the five de/no signs, and one favors WAINUT. The two other results concern the vowel in apuntar and the extra i in pretiende. The first sign of the line after “hallar” is visible but its value remains disputed.

The document is Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Modena, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera K, Karl V, sfasc. IV, cc. 60–63; the cipher occupies cc. 60r–62r. The initial check used native tiles for 60r and 60v. A later pass used native regions on 61v and a comparison on 61r. A saved GitHub issue snapshot lists the 23 differences as open on 17 November 2026, after WAINUT confirmed three previously incorporated code words. The maintainer retained the strict count of 817/910. These proposals have no effect on that published count. The saved comments for #220, #615 and #618 were checked locally. There was no fresh request for current issue status. ## The medial letter in nonbre

One more of the 23 differences concerns nombre in the project’s continuous reading against nonbre in WAINUT’s. On 60v at B27.17–20 the project’s own sign ledger gives no·n·b·re, including ap = n at position 18. WAINUT’s primary reading also gives no·n·b·re, and the pre-existing second pass agrees on the contested medial sign as = n. The latter differs on two other shapes here. Under the repository’s rule allowing a reading’s own sign file to decide a discrepancy, **nonbre** is supported. This eighth proposal is a collation of existing records. spelling_ledger.tsv reproduces the labels and values.

## Method and provenance

The coordinator knew the alternatives, located the signs and prepared the references. Two visual readers received opaque filenames. One was the session coordinator with prior project context and one was a separate reader. Neither was given candidate text, location names or the other's answer in the comparison prompts. The session coordinator had learned the f08 family value before the four follow-up crops n31, d46, s52 and m63. That pass is position-blind for these four. The independently tasked reader received only the specimen images and card. A third reader in a fresh context examined those four follow-ups with no values or document information and independently returned A, B, C and B respectively. All four classifications therefore have two value-blind readings in addition to the coordinator’s pass. The initial k17 description from the separately tasked reader was relayed by the coordinator. The other observations are transcribed in reader_messages.txt. Their transmitted descriptions are summarized in readers.tsv. They are independent visual judgments on the same pixels. Brice’s atlas was generated from the project’s sign labels and locations, and shares that provenance. WAINUT’s first pass on A00–A07 had no corresponding P2 pass, including the disputed sign in apuntar. In several other places both WAINUT passes used the same label. The table shows where that agreement held up on the image.

Reference card group_x contains the atlas specimens 60v L12.3 (A), L1.1 (B), L8.12 (C) and L12.13 (D). A/D show a fine crossing line and belong to the x-stroke-o family, X̶, de in K4. B/C have a c-shaped curl with a lower-left terminal and belong to the x-curl family, ɔ°, no. Reference card group_l uses 60v L15.3 and L20.1 for po (A/D). The pu pair (B/C) uses 60r WAINUT A15.12 and 60v B24.15. Both pu examples have the same sign assignment in the project and both WAINUT passes. The three-way agreement establishes provenance for the reference labels; it does not prove that every graphic variant has a unique sound. Reference selection was retrospective. The native images are not bundled for public distribution. archive_regions.tsv holds direct archive URLs for all eight locations.

## Separate test of tt / ct

The physical plus sign is already concordant at eleven other disputed positions, including the two spellings Ottavio / Octauio and remittimos / remictimos. K4 gives tt from K1. WAINUT’s working table gives ct from this November letter. The pre-existing, blind 1547 Figueroa transcript has two plus signs and a contemporary Spanish copy. We withheld the letters at the two locations and searched only for their surrounding sign-derived letters on the copy. img06 L07.8 gave six exact letters on the left and two on the right; L09.6 gave two and three. Neither met the frozen four-letter minimum on each side. The number of anchored loci was zero. All 1,000 shuffled keys tied or beat zero. This check leaves the eleven spellings open. The diagnostic best gaps are in cross_loci.tsv and have no status as sign readings.

## Three comparisons on folio 61v

Two further readers saw four unidentified crops without the key or proposed alternatives. At D11.2 they independently reported two distinct marks above the body. That supports the zi sign and the spelling **dezis**. At D11.22 they saw a single detached dot in the ra position, favouring **segura**. At D23.17 both readers distinguished the closed right oval with a short lower-left arm from the adjacent de sign at D23.21. Both then independently matched the target to the same scribe’s pro example on 61r, C18.14. This supports **procurar**. WAINUT’s earlier second pass differed at D23.17. The frozen plan, comparisons and precise library image coordinates are in folio61.
