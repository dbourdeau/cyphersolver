# The November cross and the Simancas reconstruction

The dark cross on 14 November 1549, c. 60r, is a separate sign. Charles V used the same name in a letter to Ferrante Gonzaga on 23 December. On c. 76r the darker interlinear gloss reads *Duq octauio*. The cipher line below the name visibly includes a figure-eight shape followed by a separate wedge-shaped mark. The existing repository ledger assigns those two signs c and ta. We compared that transcription with the image after reading the ledger. [December original, c. 76r](https://edl-jarvis.cultura.gov.it/images/iiif/db/5b124d6e-ee4e-4050-87f3-169c77e647d6/full/1200,/0/default.jpg) The November writer used the disputed cross followed by a different sign for a. The later spelling supplies no value for the November cross. [November source and discussion](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/issues/618)

Nemesio Ruiz de Alday signed the Simancas reconstruction on 6 March 1866. He names his 1545 evidence as Estado, Flandes, legajo 501, folios 42, 111, 157, 178, 181, 183, 187 and 188. On the archived sheet the double-letter box has four columns, labelled ll, nn, rr and ss. The crossed stroke under nn sits below a loop. No column is labelled tt or ct. In the code-word portion the ambassador of England is given as the two-part expression *et ud*. That entry alone cannot identify an isolated ud in the November letter. The null note also includes et. [Ministry facsimile, AGS Biblioteca D/00203, no. 243](https://www.cultura.gob.es/dam/jcr:bfe499e7-ee87-4bdd-b172-d687f189a705/cifra.pdf) · [Repository correction to the date](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/issues/618#issuecomment-6879292418)

Francisco de Vargas’s 25 August 1552 dispatch, AGS, Estado, legajo 1320, ff. 35–38, carries a contemporary decipherment. The first search found the page-indexed transcription and a DECODE record with restricted images. A later check located a public illustration of part of archive image 0002, f. 35v (viewer P3). Its open words fix the passage at *Con esto ay* and *por lo dicho como*. The third cipher row from the bottom aligns with the end of *dissension*, then *a effecto que viniesse*. The five-figure reading of *effecto* is **e · ff · e · c · to** in the existing table. Two s-shaped figures surround a sideways T in the position of ff. The u-shaped c comes before the triangle touching an oval read as to. In the 1100 × 524 image the word occupies x=394, y=397, width=200, height=39 measured from the upper left. [Vargas illustration, f. 35v](https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/vargas1552_lead.jpg) · [Page-indexed contemporary decipherment](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/blob/main/targets/vargas1552/texts/R9967_decipherment.txt)

Two independent readers, blinded to the key and text, agreed on the four separated figures at the beginning of this crop and both saw a triangle touching an oval at its right end. The visual boundary between those last two components is uncertain. The reading as one composite to sign comes from the historical table and the guided alignment. The preceding cipher line has the distinct word *meterlas*. The reported *metter el pie* lies earlier than the pictured cipher. Vargas thus supplies a contemporary example of c followed by to. The November writer’s isolated cross and the eleven spellings depending on it remain unresolved. The published strict count stays at 817/910.
