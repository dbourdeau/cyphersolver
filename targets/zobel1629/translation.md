# Johan Zobel to Christian IV, Paris, 20 Sept 1629 (new style): English

By the contributor (Malcolm McBratney, issue #456). Capitals mark the translation of the 19 cipher words; (?) is his doubt about the clear text.
D., H., G., B., Bp., Bo. are Zobel's cover letters; "G. = Christian IV" is from context, the others are the contributor's guesses (D. France, H. Sweden, B. the Emperor), not proved from this sheet.

Most serene, most mighty King, most gracious Lord. For a good while I have noticed that D. has cast a particular eye on H. and holds that those ARMS are fortunate and well worth seconding. I could say neither yes nor no to this; but as long as G. was engaged in war I did not thwart that intention, and held that H. was rightly to be upheld, so that the burden might the sooner come off G.'s neck. For that reason too, concerning the PROTECTION of the TOWN OF STRALSUND, I handed over the letters and humbly let G. know that it was done. I would have continued this in the former(?) WAR. Now, however, I hesitate and will do nothing without G.'s express order. I would have liked G. to give me such order and leave, so that nothing passed between D. and H. of which G. had no knowledge, and G. had remained arbiter of the counsels and actions. These matters need a conference by word of mouth, and I think G. would do well if His Majesty called ZOBEL to him; he is willing not only to come but to set aside all his own affairs, as before, so that G. may have useful service of him. It is very necessary for many reasons that G. knows all this. ZOBEL would most cleanly(?) bring it about with D. that nothing is GRANTED or ENTRUSTED to H. by D. that has not first been communicated to G. and G.'s advice taken on it. During the WAR ZOBEL (three words unread) for G., urging help where he could; now he can rid himself of H. as of all public business. Yet it would be good to know G.'s mind on this before he leaves, so that His Majesty always stays in the FOREMOST consideration. I can readily judge that G. has not, with the PEACE, put off the care for public liberty, and that the INVESTITURE of WALLENSTEIN with MECKLENBURG cannot be pleasing to His Majesty. Nor can I think G.'s state secure while Bp. and Bo. remain with B.; and I know good people who would risk a black eye to INSTAL DUKE FREDERICK in his BISHOPRICS again. Commending His Majesty faithfully to God's protection. Paris, 20 Sept. new style 1629. J. Zobel.
