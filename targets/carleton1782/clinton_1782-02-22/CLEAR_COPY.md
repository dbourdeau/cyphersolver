# Clinton, 22 February 1782: clear witnesses

Source-assisted transcription of the nineteenth-century Canadian transcript, LAC B.148, BL Add MS 21808, reel H-1649. Original manuscript not inspected. Capitalization and punctuation lightly normalized; wording preserved. These are existing clear witnesses, not newly deciphered plaintext.

## Quadruplicate, p.8 / frame 1077

Quadruplicate of Letter sent in Cypher overland.
New York February 22d 1782

Sir,

I think it right to send by express to Your Excellency the following Intelligence, which has just been communicated to me by The Honourable William Smith Chief Justice of this Province, exactly as it was given to me by him.

About a fortnight since an Intelligent person from Connecticut suggested, that tho' the Rebels talked of an Attack in the Spring upon New York, he had strong suspicions of a preparation to invade Canada, and yesterday I learnt by a direct channel, that General Schuyler said to one of his Confidents, and yet our friend, that La Fayette went to France to propose that project, and that they were waiting to know the Result.

The following Intelligence has been received since the above by another channel —

Large Stores of Provisions at the falls of Ohio.

The Rebel General Clark a very enterprising man, a Virginian at Kain Tuck and is to have the Command of two State Regiments, besides other Troops, with about One thousand French and Spaniards, with these the ensuing Spring he set off on an Expedition against Detroit and its dependencies, his rout is down the Ohio,

## Quadruplicate continued, p.9 / frame 1078

[Repeated catchword Ohio,] to the Mouth of the Wabashe, thence up the same to the head thereof where we have a carrying place, from thence to Detroit. To induce the Rebel to undertake this Expedition they have received accounts that our Garrison is very weak at Detroit, the information to the Rebels is from Prisoners that have been carried to Detroit who have returned. Some of the Rebel Officers say that there will be an expedition by the Allied Forces against Canada the ensuing Summer, and its asserted that the Rebels have agreed to give the French Canada in case the Combined Forces of the French and Rebels should be successfull enough to reduce it. That this engagement by the Rebels to the French, is asserted by some who have seen the agreement in the proceedings of Congress this Winter.

I have the honour to be,
Your Excellency's
most obedient and
most humble Servant
H. Clinton

P.S. Be so good to inform Lieut. Col. Maclean, that I have not received any Official Information of his being appointed to Command the Artillery in this Quarter; but I understand that by the death of Genl Williamson he is appointed Lt Colonel to the 4th Battalion.—/

Quadrupte L.S.
[Endorsed]

## Unciphered continuation after cipher, p.12 / frame 1083

Be so good to acquaint Lt. Col. Maclean that I have not yet received any official information of his being appointed to command the Artillery in this Quarter—but I understand that by the death of General Williamson, he is appointed Lt. Colonel to the 4th Battalion.—

If the Baron should receive any money from Your Excellency on Account pray let me know the sum.—/ Cypher initialled.

[Endorsed:] From Sr. H. Clinton / 22 Febr — 1782. / rec'd 6th April / By Green / Triplicate / By Davis.

The money-account sentence is absent from the quadruplicate pp.8–9. The postscript itself has inform/acquaint and not/not yet variants. Neither is encoded on the three cipher leaves transcribed in this packet. The cipher ends H.C.; a bottom catchword “Be” points to this unciphered continuation.
