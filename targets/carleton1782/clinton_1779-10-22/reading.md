# Clinton to Haldimand, 22 Oct 1779: reading of the two transcribed cipher pages

Decryption on the frozen key `test/frozen/key2_armylist.tsv`, word by word in `decryption.tsv`. Conventions: `{x}` a
letter the sense wants where the key gives another (the decode follows in the note); `[...]` text on the untranscribed
p. 121; words in the clear in the cipher in *italics*. Spelling as the cipher gives it.

## p. 120 (opening)

I was favored with your Letters of the *19th July* by the Matross of the Artillery. There are now no hopes of
recovering the Army of the Convention by exchange, the Rebels having had nothing in view by the negociation they
proposed for that purpose but to still the clamours of their own {o}{f}ficers prisoners with us. I am disappointed in my
expectations of a reinforcement from the West Indies; however I sent you thre{e} Regiments, one of which was British,
which had they arrived [... p. 121, not transcribed]

Notes: *Matross* (an artillery private) is `1-6 1-27 1-21 1-5 1-10 2-44`, the last a doubled 2-4 (*ss*); the clear
copy was misread "Mohawk" in our 9 Nov notes. *favored* where the clear copy has "honored"; *I am disappointed in my*
where it has "disappointed as my". *th{e}* (by the negociation) decodes *tee*; *{o}{f}ficers* is seven groups decoding
*h?icers* (1-23 for o, 5-22 past the end of line 5), i.e. the cipher spells *oficers*; *thre{e}* decodes *thrr*.

## p. 122 (end of the letter and P.S.)

[... having therefore] no alternative left, I shall detach a considerable armament for that purpose. I hope your
Indians will be prevailed upon to threaten the {f}rontiers of Virginia in great force, which will operate in favor of
the southern move[ments], while a considerable {f}{l}eet may probably be employed to cooperate i{n} the Chesapeak.

P.S. I hav{e} received your dispatches by the Defianc{e}.

(Signed) H. Clinton

Notes: *move* is all the cipher has for "movements". The key misses: 6-9 gives n for f (twice) and 6-12 i for l
(cells not seen in training, taken from the Army List line); 1-1 b for n in *in*; 4-1 l for e in *have*; 2-13 a for e in
*Defiance*. All six are letters inside words whose other groups read; none changes a word.

## Translation

Not needed: the text is English.

## What the letter says

Clinton answers Haldimand's letters of 19 July: no exchange of Burgoyne's Convention Army is possible, Congress having
used the negotiation only to quiet its officers held prisoner at New York; the reinforcement expected from the West
Indies failed; three regiments sent to Quebec under convoy of the *Renown* were scattered by a storm (p. 121, from the
clear copy); with Georgia in danger he will send a considerable force against South Carolina, asks for Indian raids on
the Virginia frontier to support it, and expects a fleet to act in the Chesapeake. The P.S. acknowledges Haldimand's
dispatches by the *Defiance*. Received at Quebec by way of Halifax on 18 January 1780; a second copy came by Niagara in May 1780.
