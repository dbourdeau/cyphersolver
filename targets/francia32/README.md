# Francia 32: Filippo Sega, nuncio in Paris, to Rome, 1590 to 1591

Filippo Sega, bishop of Piacenza and nuncio in Paris, writes to Rome during the war of the League. Most of his letters here are mostly in clear, with cipher passages. The duplicate R5615 is almost all in cipher.

This folder gives a transcription of the DECODE images of nine records: R5612 to R5618, R5620 and R5621. It also gives the decode of 28 pages with A. Meister's printed key no. 65 (1906).

- The state of each decoded page is "from key".
- The folder holds no reading in sentences, so each decoded document is "decoded, not read".
- R5618 and R5620 are not decoded. Key no. 65 gives them .58 and .60 units with a value, against .80 to .99 on the main group.

## Who read these letters before me

- **Brice Dudout** read f. 17 (R5612) and f. 71 (R5616) with key no. 65, and R5615 in part (`targets/sega1590`, `targets/sega1591b`, 29 October 2026). He also tested the key on R5621, held out.
- **George Lasry** tied key no. 65 to Sega's letters. He read the Paris copy of the letter of 9 January 1591 (`targets/sega1591`), the letter that R5613 also holds.
- **The clerks of 1591** deciphered two of these letters: R5613 on f. 46r, and R5621 on f. 230r (dated 26 March 1591).
- **A. Meister** printed the key in 1906.

- **A unit** is one element of the decode: a pair, a lone vowel sign, a code letter, a symbol or a three-digit code. Nulls are not units. "With a value" means that the key gives it a value. `check.md`, section 2, gives the exact rule and its faults.
- **The main group:** my digit-pair counts, with no key, put 24 pages together, and R5618 with R5620 apart. Four pages hold fewer than 70 pairs, so the counts cannot place them: ff. 17v, 71v, 106v and 231v. The output of the counts is not in this folder.
- **The agreement** is the share of the decoded letters that match the decipherment of the time, in order. `check.md` gives its exact rule and its limits. It is a second measure, with no held-out protocol of its own.

## How to read the decode

Each record page ends with the decode, row by row. Each line names its record, its folio and its row in `transcription/`. The decode is the output of `scripts/decode.py` on the rows. Nothing in it is corrected.

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `abc` | letters of the key: pairs and lone vowel signs |
| `[word]` | a code letter or a symbol of the key |
| `[<con?>]` | the sign ∞, given the value con with a doubt |
| `[<sym>]` | the sign #, which has no value |
| `{name}`, `{449}` | a three-digit code: with its value, or with none |
| `<61>` | a pair that the key does not hold |
| `<P-shaped loo>` | a sign that the key does not hold; the text is the start of its description in the rows |
| `?` | a digit that opens a pair but is not followed by a plain digit, or a digit whose mark the key does not hold |
| `‖` | clear text stood here; this folder does not copy it |

- Nulls are dropped.
- Some signs leave no mark: signs given in brackets (for example `[two uprights on a baseline]`), symbols other than ω, ∞ and #, and a bare ω. An unresolved span between complete units leaves no mark either.
- On f. 62v each line begins with `[per]`, and on f. 71v two lines hold `[perche]` and `[che]`. These come from row labels, not from the cipher.
- `key/README.md` gives the rules by which the decode reads a row.

## What the letters say

Each record page gives one concrete doubt.

From the decode (my guess, in English):

- **R5614, 9 January 1591.** Words that seem to stand in the decode: confusion, the soldiery, discredit, the politiques, the death of a chevalier, a return, Saint-Denis.
- **R5616, ff. 61v to 63v.** Words that seem to stand in the decode: Nemours and his son, a wife, troops of a thousand, six hundred and two hundred, a garrison, help promised.
- **R5617, March 1591.** Words that seem to stand in the decode of f. 107r: governed, merchants.

From the readings of others:

- **R5612, 26 November 1590.** The imprisoned bishop leans to the opposite party (Navarre's). Sega will temporise. This agrees with Brice Dudout's reading; my decode shows parts of it.
- **R5615, early January 1591.** The duplicate of a long letter, read in part by Brice Dudout.
- **R5616, 16 January 1591 (f. 71).** Brice Dudout's reading gives: Umena (Mayenne) wrote something good for Sega to Rome.

From the decipherments of the time:

- **R5613, 9 January 1591.** Lignerac goes, on the order of Umena (Mayenne), to Poitou and Brittany. George Lasry's reading of the Paris copy gives the same.
- **R5621, 21 December 1590.** The agreement at Tours may fail. La Châtre withdraws, on the pretext that Umena (Mayenne) did not keep his promises. The Duchess of Guise is angry about her son's release.

Not read:

- **R5618 and R5620, July 1591.** Not decoded. I make no claim about their system.

## Files

- `NOTES.md` and `profile.json`: the notes and the profile of this folder.
- `check.md`: the two measures against the decipherments of the time, the units with a value, and the page groups.
- `r5612.md` to `r5621.md`: one page for each record, with the decode row by row.
- `key/`: the key table of the decode, with the source of each value, and a note.
- `scripts/decode.py`: makes the decode lines from the rows.
- `scripts/check_decifrato.py` and `decifrato/`: the two measures of `check.md`, section 1, and the clerk's lines of f. 46r and f. 230r that they use.
- `TRANSCRIPTION.md`: what the rows are, their marks and their counts.
- `transcription/`: the rows, one file for each of the 30 cipher pages.
