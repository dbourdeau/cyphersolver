# R5616: Filippo Sega, Paris: ff. 61v to 63v, and the letter of 16 January 1591 (ff. 71r, 71v)

My own transcription of the DECODE images. The decode uses A. Meister's printed key no. 65 (1906), as this ledger's `targets/sega1590/key.tsv` gives it. State: decoded, not read.

- **DECODE:** record R5616, https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/5616
- **Archive:** Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 32.

  Héder and Megyesi (2022), "The DECODE Database of Historical Ciphers and Keys: Version 2", HistoCrypt, https://doi.org/10.3384/ecp188397. No DECODE image is published.
- **Date:** ff. 61v to 63v: not dated in my record. Ff. 71r and 71v: 16 January 1591, as Brice Dudout gives it (`targets/sega1590`).
- **Before this folder:** Brice Dudout read the letter of 16 January 1591 on ff. 71r and 71v before me (`targets/sega1590`, with the same key). His count: 52 cipher words, all read in their sense. No reading of ff. 61v to 63v is in this ledger.
- **What this page adds:** The rows and the decode of ff. 61v to 63v. For ff. 71r and 71v, a second transcription, made apart from his, and its decode; it adds no reading to his.
- **Cipher pages:** f. 61v (11 rows, `transcription/R5616_f061v_digits.txt`); f. 62r (14 rows, `transcription/R5616_f062r_digits.txt`); f. 62v (7 rows, `transcription/R5616_f062v_digits.txt`); f. 63r (17 rows, `transcription/R5616_f063r_digits.txt`); f. 63v (21 rows, `transcription/R5616_f063v_digits.txt`); f. 71r (10 rows, `transcription/R5616_f071r_digits.txt`); f. 71v (4 rows, `transcription/R5616_f071v_digits.txt`).
- **Counts** (this folder): 2,965 digit signs, of which 131 doubtful.
- **Units with a value in the key** (as counted on 17 November; `check.md`, section 2, gives the rule and its faults): .85 (1,393 of 1,635); by page: f. 61v .84, f. 62r .90, f. 62v .85, f. 63r .81, f. 63v .86, f. 71r .98, f. 71v .67.
- **Counts by the word rule:** not counted. The decode has no word division, and no reading in sentences stands. `README.md`, "The counts", says why.

## Summary

- Ff. 61v to 63v. Words that seem to stand in the decode (my guess, in English): Nemours and his son, a wife, troops of a thousand, six hundred and two hundred, a garrison, help promised.
- F. 71. Brice Dudout's reading gives: Umena (Mayenne) wrote something good for Sega to Rome. My decode does not show the name.

**Doubt:** On f. 61v the transcription marks every cipher row as tentative (between ⟦ and ⟧). On f. 62v each decode line begins with `[per]`: the decoder read the letter C of the row label `{C01:` as a code letter. On f. 71v the span labels `a:` and `b:` give `[perche]` and `[che]` in the same way. F. 71v gives .67 units with a value and does not read.
