# Rubio to Joaquín Téllez, 14 October 1913

*Update, 22 Nov 2026: Narváez's 2019 book, described below as unavailable or unchecked, has since been checked (chapters VII-XI, pp. 261-410, and the index, pp. 487-495). It prints neither the three telegrams nor Clave núm. 109. See NOTES.md.*

General Guillermo Rubio Navarrete sent a telegram addressed to General Joaquín Téllez at Laredo on 14 October 1913. The typed Spanish discusses railway and telegraph interruptions and repairing the track. A numerical syllabary reconstructed from earlier documents gives three distinct held-out passages, “Laredo”, “de esa plaza” and “tren”, without changing any sign. The larger telegram remains partly unread. The CEHM dates the sending place to [Lampazos, Nuevo León] in brackets. [CEHM, DLXXIII.1.32.5-41, record 137927](https://www.cehm.org.mx/Buscador/FichaBasica/137927?Tipo=A)

The source of the key is Rubio's 19 July 1913 draft transcribing an order of the President to the head of the División del Bravo. Pencil syllables above the groups give a partial contemporary reading. The initial 26-entry extraction was made for an earlier test on a 10 July letter and missed there. Enlarging the dark-on-light contrast exposed additional pencil glosses. The fitter assigned 133 = **par** from the contexts *ocupar* and *desamparada* on page 1 and *reparación* on page 2. Two subsequent blind readers confirmed the beginning of the gloss but could not agree on a definite final r at any of its three occurrences. See JULY_GLOSSES.md for their spot check. The earlier extraction had *pa*. These July key readings are from a single reader in each pass and are uncertain in the faintest places. The new held-out witness tests the resulting table in use. [July draft, CEHM 137860](https://www.cehm.org.mx/Buscador/FichaBasica/137860?Tipo=A)

On the 8 October training telegram, the catalogue gives Joaquín Téllez to [Guillermo Rubio Navarrete], Laredo, October 1913. The first image types “LAREDO 8 ... OCTUBRE DE 1913.” The 8 is placed between the town and the destination, as a day in a telegram dateline, and the catalogue confirms the month. The newly retrieved second sheet ends “J TELLEZ.” It was retrieved after the October 14 scoring and was excluded from training and all tests. Its text mentions Camargo and an order to discipline the addressee’s telegraph operator. The first sheet explicitly names “GRAL TRUCY” in clear. The late telegram therefore independently attests the earlier Posada letter's rare string TRUCY in the same military correspondence. The October text itself also discusses a column's march and Torreón. Pencil glosses appear over some of its ciphertext. Two independent readings agreed on 94 of 97 training groups. Thirteen values were inferred from its ciphertext and surrounding clear text, including the spelling of the surnames and of repeated place names. They enter only the expanded table and are not blind confirmations. All 71 distinct entries were frozen before scoring October 14. [8 October telegram, CEHM 137925](https://www.cehm.org.mx/Buscador/FichaBasica/137925?Tipo=A)

## The independent test

A visual reader and the Apple Vision on-device recognizer each saw the October page without a key or proposed decode. The independent visual transcription and the raw optical text are included. The line-one last group is visually 143 versus OCR 123 in the watermark. It remains one unknown group throughout. All 53 positions are retained, in eight runs separated by clear Spanish. The two readings agreed on 52/53. Their independence is methodological. Both read the same sometimes-overtyped scan and neither is a human review.

The frozen score was the mean Spanish conditional log probability of trigrams crossing a known code boundary. Trigrams confined to one rearranged syllable, or crossing a missing or clear span, do not count. Controls permuted the values of the table 1,000 times. The plan required 40% group coverage, 10 known adjacent pairs, 12 scored trigrams, a score higher than at least 995 controls, and two unrepaired words of at least four letters across two cipher runs.

One full-key control scored higher than the expanded table. LAREDO (la-re-do), PLAZA (p-la-za) and TREN (t-r-en) each meet the word and distinct-run conditions. Laredo is also the telegram's clear destination and therefore is weaker evidence on its own. The named place was not used to fit this telegram. The exact PLAZA group sequence also occurs on the October 8 training sheet, after the clear article LA; it is a held-out occurrence of a previously seen word. TREN is a separate unrepaired complete word. The score evaluates all eligible held groups. The approach benefited from July and October 8 context before the freeze. Later conjectures from October 14 have no part in these figures.

## Diplomatic reading

Capitals give mechanical output. Lowercase phrases transcribe the clear passages with editorial accents, capitalization and punctuation. A question mark is exactly one unknown group, whose plaintext length may exceed one letter. All letters that follow directly from a table have been retained.

> … carecer de MATE??DORE??CION suficiente para llevar LARE?RACION hasta LAREDO. Yo trabajaré con toda actividad día y noche, pero ruego a usted que DEESAPLAZA parta otro TRENRE?RACION. He tenido el tráfico al sur interrum[?]do y la línea telegráfica CONP?TA?NTEDESTRU?DA. Por esta razón carecía de comunicaciones. Antier quedó la vía lista y ayer noche el telégrafo. Repararé la vía haciendo [typewritten phrase cancelled by Xs] DESVIASEN vez de ?ENTES, y espero que pronto restableceremos el tráfico.

The word interrum[?]do has one overstruck or ambiguous position. Its ordinary spelling is interrumpido. The X-overtyped passage immediately follows *haciendo* and precedes the final numerical passages. A subsequent source auditor found the roughly 25 typewriter positions consistent with [¿desvíos en vez de puentes?]. The extent and full wording are conjectural. Some underlying letter shapes at the right end are compatible with *puentes*. No complete reading of the cancellation was settled by independent blind transcriptions. It may preserve a contemporary clue to both DESVIASEN and ?ENTES. The key fitter saw the page and its OCR; the subsequent assignment 140 = *pu* was a nonblind contextual guess from ?ENTES after clear *vez de* in a railway-repair report. No claim depends on reading through the Xs. The proposed *puentes* and the unresolved sense of *desvias* contribute no sure word. The final *en* reproduces code 78, though the cancelled phrase may have suggested the division. October 15 contains no 140 and therefore provides no test of that assignment.

The initial 26 July assignments completely emit only *la* among the seven words credited to the expanded key. Adding the subsequent July pencil assignments also completes *Laredo, de, esa, plaza* and *en*. Adding the training values inferred from 8 October completes *tren*, through 38 = t. The middle table was never tested separately. The word-source ledger is a descriptive comparison of the frozen assignments. Run `python3 -I word_sources.py` to regenerate word_sources.tsv.

The cipher words credited under the full table in order are **la, Laredo, de, esa, plaza, tren, en**. With the tentative division in the per-word table the strict mechanical read share is **7 of 16**. Six of those words lie outside the possible cancelled parallel; *en* overlaps it. Seven is a count of mechanical readings, not of words first recovered here. The text probably refers to repair equipment or material, Laredo and a further repair train. It gives literal *do* in the first passage. Replacing it with *de* would be an editorial correction. The forms *reparación*, *destruida* and *puentes* contain unsupported slots. The sense of *desvias* is open. The split of the opening damaged run into three words and the final two runs into three words is an editorial choice. The original telegram has no interword spaces in its number sequences. The seven-word count includes neither *desvías* nor a repaired word. The adjacent clear article or noun is excluded everywhere.

Its Rubio target reads three War Ministry telegrams with Clave 109 and explicitly describes the Téllez/División del Bravo traffic as a different, previously untreated alphabetical syllabary. Its description already identifies the July pencil draft. The earlier possession of that draft and its annotations is credited. Only the October 14 transfer is the held-out finding here. The Tomokiyo index preserved during the earlier search contained no Mexican/Rubio entry. The public CEHM catalogue identifies the October piece as “en clave” without a transcription. The prior Mexican survey reviewed accessible articles by Roberto Narváez and the relevant Mexican cryptological survey.
