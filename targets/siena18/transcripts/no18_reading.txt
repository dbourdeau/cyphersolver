ENGLISH SUMMARY
  Three sets of articles negotiated between the Sienese envoys and the imperial side:
  (1) P1, end of a set signed by the King's secretary "Niccolaus Ziegler(?)": Siena to set its artillery,
      gunpowder, stones and balls ready; to put its troops in order; the King to send Siena some help; Siena to
      lend the King 5,000 ducats, which "Mx." will repay within two months.
  (2) P1-P3, "second requests of the Imperial Majesty": B. to take Siena, its present government and its whole
      jurisdiction under protection against all comers; Siena to break war on X. wherever B. sees fit, and at
      the request of the King and of the Emperor to open war on X. around Ponte a Valiano, without being given
      any help; for a war against the Florentines Siena to give passage, lodging, supplies and war material; and
      Siena to provide at its own cost 100 men-at-arms and 60 light horse.
  (3) P3, "requests of the Sienese envoys": all towns, fortresses and castles within five miles of the Sienese
      border taken in the war, by force or by agreement, with their appurtenances, to be handed over free to
      Siena; no peace or alliance of B. with X. without first handing Siena these places.
  (4) P4, "first requests of the Imperial Majesty to the Sienese envoys": a statement of Siena's claims against
      Florence and of the fortresses, castles and lands the Florentines hold that belong to Siena; the Emperor
      to judge the dispute as ordinary and supreme judge; if Siena enters the league/union with B., B. is to
      defend it, and Siena is to pay a quota of the league's costs fixed by the Emperor's judgement (the text
      compares it with what the Pope and "S." pay; the exact rule is unclear); B. to seize the castles so that at least half are restored to Siena, Siena
      agreeing to stand to law before the Emperor at the Florentines' request, if Florence does the same for the
      places it holds; the next article (Siena to send back ... for a bombard?) breaks off.
  B., X., S. and Mx. are left in clear as abbreviations; X. is the enemy (Florence or its protector), and B.
  the protecting power or its commander.  I did not resolve them.
  Date clues: the subscription "Niccolaus Ziegler(?) Regis Secretarius" (Niklas Ziegler, secretary to Maximilian I
  and to Charles V, d. 1526); "Serenissimus Rex & Cesarea Maiestas" as two parties; "Ponte a Valiano" on the
  Chiana, the Siena-Montepulciano/Florence border; Florentines holding Sienese castles (Montepulciano went over
  to Siena in 1495 and back to Florence in 1511); a "liga" with a papal quota.  Taken together this points to the 1510s-1520s,
  but these clues alone do not fix a year.
