# R9487, Lope de Soria to Charles V, Mirandola, 13 December 1527: English

Translation of `R9487_reading.md` (as revised 14 Nov 2026), same holes.

## Translation

A. I at once had the three thousand ducats paid to the lord of Monaco that Your Majesty ordered by your letter, which
had remained in the merchants' hands, [...] another thousand, notwithstanding that I have given them receipts for the
whole sum of the exchanges, which was done for good reasons. Your Majesty will order what I am to do about them, and
about the jewels that were Bourbon's, which were pawned at Genoa.

B. Yesterday I received a letter from Andrea de Burgo, written at Ferrara the day before [...], and he tells me that the
hostages who were held at Rome in [soc] of the [zu]s had got loose and fled to Narni; if that is true, it would be a
[?great] inconvenience. [...] it is no wonder at any disorder that [?there may be] in the said [?army], [pib] the troops
are without obedience and with some divisions [yod] [ync]. May Your Majesty [?provide] for it as befits your service.
There had arrived the prince [?of Orange] [pur] [?...] were the marquis del Guasto, and he says that the two were coming
with the [?army].

C. The said dukes and marquis say that [?servants] of Your Majesty; this [?could] be, [?but] they do not cease to show
disrespect in [tal] themselves to be against Your Majesty and making a marriage with France, and it [?may] well be
believed that, the [?army] [vi[+ud]endo], they will be with Your Majesty so as to come to no harm, for they are people
who know how to sail with every wind.

All the enemy have withdrawn from near Milan, so that Antonio de Leyva is now master of the field and rides where he
will; but inside Milan there are very few victuals, and the country outside is [?...] and [?...] and destroyed, so that
they need provision to be made to supply the city.
