# 4Q313 fragment 2

**Working result.** Line 1 most plausibly contains `שגעו`, the beginning of the noun `שגעו[ן…]` (“madness …”). Line 2 probably contains `שים`, with the word and neighboring traces unresolved. Pfann’s alignment with MMT B 62–64 has physical conflicts on both lines. The corpus comparison supports Ratzon’s leading lexical suggestion for line 1 and gives no textual basis for extending it into a whole sentence.

### Historical photographs

I subsequently located the piece in three PAM negatives and examined the corresponding full-resolution tiles. PAM 41.867 shows it at the far right of the second row. PAM 42.430 shows it on the lower right, just to the right of a larger pale piece and above the ruler. PAM 43.380 shows it above the left end of the ruler, immediately to the right of the smaller 4Q313 piece. [PAM 41.867](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-280307) · [PAM 42.430](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-283916) · [PAM 43.380](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-284413)

The two short descending marks at position 1.4 are discernible on both 41.867 and 42.430. The right member is thinner and more interrupted. Those photographs show more skin above the marks than the modern infrared photograph does. Thus the paired pattern predates the present shape of the tear. On line 2, the hook and rounded remnant at 2.2–3 are also visible, but the photographs supply no additional legible letter between the rounded remnant and the leftmost traces. This strengthens the photographic basis for the line 1 shin and leaves the lexical result on line 2 open. The IAA catalogue identifies PAM 40.599 as B-278403, taken by Najib Anton Albina in May 1953. The scan contains many pieces photographed together. I examined the plate and checked candidate areas against the later photographs, but could not securely locate fragment 2 on it. The additional stroke reported at the right end of line 2 remains dependent on Ratzon’s reading of that negative. [PAM 40.599](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-278403) · [Ratzon 2023, p. 12](https://www.academia.edu/108644780/Ratzon_E_A_New_Reading_of_4Q313_Revue_de_Qumran_121_2023_pp_3_15)

For an unsegmented reading, a question mark after a Hebrew letter marks an uncertain identification, `◦` marks unidentified ink, and ellipses leave the amount of missing text unspecified. The `מ` on line 2 is left in medial form here because the word division remains open.

- Line 1 in writing order: `א? · מ? · ◦ · ש · ג? · ע · ו/ת`.
- Line 2 in writing order: `ש? · י · מ? · … · ◦`.
- Preferred lexical supplement on line 1, conditional on `ג` and `ו`: `שגעו[ן…]`. The nun supplies the minimum completion of the attested noun. Any possessive suffix or clause following it remains open.

## The proposed MMT passage

| Witness | Passage | What the witness supplies at the proposed alignment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4Q394 | 8 iv 12–14 | Much of the opening including `על` falls within a restoration in Qumran-Digital. The last `ת` in `[מטע]ת` survives. `כראשית הוא` in the next line is restored. |
| 4Q396 | 1–2 iii 2–4 | `ואפ ע[ל מ]טעת` supports the opening with `על`. `כראשית הוא` is transcribed as preserved. |
| 4Q397 | 6–13, lines 4–5 in Qumran-Digital | `וא֯פ֯ על מטע֯[ת]` preserves `על`. `כראש֯י֯ת֯` survives with uncertainty. The following `הוא` is restored. |

Pfann’s alignment omits `על` and requires `מ` at 1.4 and `ת` at 2.3. The first clashes with the paired strokes and available width. The second runs against the shape of the surviving curve. Inserting the attested `על` into the proposed alignment leaves the line 1 signs out of sequence and does not address line 2. The tentative reading of the lower-left ink as `א` supplies a weaker, supplementary objection to Pfann’s `ה` at that point. The test addresses the passage used to identify the fragment as MMT. MMT as a whole has six standard witnesses, three of which transmit this passage. ## Corpus test and lexical limits

I searched 52,886 transcribed physical lines in the Abegg–Bowley–Cook corpus as encoded in ETCBC DSS 2.0.5. The search folds Hebrew final forms, allows matches across word boundaries within a line, and stops at punctuation or explicit gaps. It reports matches in editorial restorations separately from unbracketed matches. Unbracketed letters can still be uncertain in the edition. The scan excludes 4Q313 and related sigla to avoid circularity. [ETCBC corpus](https://github.com/ETCBC/dss/tree/master/tf/2.0.5) · [Sign feature documentation](https://github.com/ETCBC/dss/blob/master/docs/feature_documentation.md)

| Query | All matches | Unbracketed |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Preferred seven-sign sequence, assuming `ו` in the third position | 0 | 0 |
| Seven signs with the listed alternatives at positions 2, 3, 5 and 7 | 0 | 0 |
| `שגעו` or `שגעת` | 5 | 1 |
| `שגעון`, allowing word-internal nun | 5 | 1 |
| `שימ` followed by aleph, with zero intervening consonants | 94 | 39 |
| Same, with one intervening consonant | 98 | 49 |

- **Line 1.** All five `שגעו`/`שגעת` matches continue with nun and belong to the noun `שגעון`. The one unbracketed match is Ratzon’s comparandum, 4Q504 1–2 ii 14. Its preceding consonants before `שגעו` are `נומ`, which differ from the preferred `אמו` in 4Q313. The four restoration-dependent hits include 4Q385a and 4Q387, overlapping witnesses of a text that repeats the Deuteronomic triad of madness, blindness and bewilderment in a context of punishment. This shows why the noun by itself cannot determine the genre. 4Q425 1+3:8 tentatively reads `ו[ש]געונו` in a different context, providing an example of the same opening with a different possessive suffix. [4Q504](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q504/2026-10-06/index.html) · [4Q387 and 4Q385a](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q387/2026-10-06/index.html) · [4Q425](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q425/2026-10-06/index.html)
- **The preceding `אם`.** Ratzon’s `[רפ]אם` (“heal them”) is plausible if positions 1.1–2 end a word. Even within the root `רפא`, the surviving letters permit other verbal forms. `ורפאם` (“and he will heal them”) is attested in two Qumran Isaiah witnesses at Isaiah 19:22 (ETCBC 1Qisaa 15:31 and 4Q8 8a_b:4). The extra consonants and the verb form would come entirely from the lost text. [ETCBC DSS 2.0.5](https://github.com/ETCBC/dss/tree/master/tf/2.0.5)
- **Line 2.** `אשימים` (“guilty”) has `ש־י־מ` at positions 2–4. Its following yod and final mem would need to fit in or near the gap after 2.3. The shorter spelling `אשמים`, preserved at 4Q266 2 i 13 and restored at 4Q268 1:16, has mem after shin, so it cannot reproduce the contiguous `ש־י־מ` run in the same position. A simple finish such as `[אנ]שים` would fit the three signs without requiring an extension after the mem. The proposed lower-left aleph and a semantic link with line 1 do not distinguish these readings. [CD 1:9](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/CD/2025-11-11/index.html) · [4Q266](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q266/2026-10-06/index.html) · [4Q268](https://forum.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q268/2026-05-21/index.html)
- **Both lines.** Searching adjacent lines within the same catalogued fragment for `ש[גטר]ע[ות]` followed by `שימ` found one hit, 1QM IV 3–4. The upper string crosses the word boundary in `בשר עול`, while the lower string occurs in `החמשים`. The short strings have a different sense in that context and the wider sequence is absent. Different manuscript column widths can place equivalent text on different lines. [1QM IV 3–4](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1QM/2026-02-11/index.html)

The new comparison narrows the restoration to one defensible lexical proposal on line 1 and exposes a spelling constraint on the suggested restoration in line 2. A clause joining “heal them,” “their madness” and “the guilty” still depends on several independent, unresolved readings.
