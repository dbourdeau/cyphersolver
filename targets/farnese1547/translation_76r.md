# c. 76r / c. 79r in English (approximate translation of the c. 79r text, Brice Dudout with Claude, 9 Nov 2026)

Copy of a chapter of a letter from Rome of 10 August 1547.

The ambassador of France, by way of a short discourse, wrote some days ago to his King that the greatest glory he could win
at this beginning of his reign was simply to look to his own guard, to keep what his father had left him and to govern
himself well; and that the desire to gain by breaking into war went against him, since he saw in it many great dangers;
and that he should consider that he was young and had time to take up better occasions than these, which God would show
him at one time or another; and so he counselled and dissuaded him otherwise. I understand that the said ambassador was
given to judge that, with the ardour his King had to throw himself into some enterprise, which some great men of France
were hoping for, it was not possible to hold him back from this desire, and that it was for him to do the work he thought
fitting to his rank and to his knowledge of the humours of Italy, and of the end towards which [the King] was moving, that
urged him to it in these times; and that those over there who reminded the said King of this and tried to draw him back were
held to be of little spirit. He further persuaded the ambassador to make every effort to stay united with England and to
win it over entirely, since he saw no easier or more open way to have [the Emperor] troubled in his own house than to be
disunited from it.

Context (not checked against an edition): Henri II had succeeded Francis I on 31 March 1547; the ambassador is not named in
the text. The last sentence is the hardest of the text and its sense is uncertain.
