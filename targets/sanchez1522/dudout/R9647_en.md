the French party hold back their letters so that what they are doing is not known. Their news: French affairs are

[winter] to the enterprise of [Milan?]; the King of France [is coming?] and must pay his men the money promised them

ambassador went to the Signoria and told them that what they had to say was as persons wishing its good and repose,
and as an office of ambassadors, each having been advised by letters from outside; the French partisans here are
plotting with them.

**f. 257v.** If it were true [that they had made] an agreement with the King of France against His Majesty — as they
had often said they had not — it would break the peace. They answered that they now had no obligation nor capitulation
with the King of France, being released from it, and that anything done would be against the truce they have with His
Majesty; it was not true, they wished only to live in peace. They complained strongly of the answer given in England,
and of the detention of their [ships/goods] in His Majesty's lands, saying they did not know why England did so.
Sánchez answered that His Majesty's will towards them was very good, if they gave no cause for the contrary; the
English ambassador would satisfy them about England. They said they still [...] fifty thousand ducats of damage.

seeking money by every way. He has written to His Majesty, to don Juan Manuel and to the Duke of [Sessa]. The Republic
wrote to its nuncio [in Rome?] on the 16th of last month to work for the universal peace among Christian princes, and
