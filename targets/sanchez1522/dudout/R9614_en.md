weighed what His Majesty had replied they would see his good will; they should consider His Majesty's last [offer] and
ask the [Pope?] (`hod`) to be guarantor. On the Infante: His Majesty could not [bind] what was not in his hands. They
answered that His Majesty had not accepted those conditions and had not wholly [rejected] them; Sánchez told them not
to take amiss what was not known, and to set out the causes that could prevent it.

**f. 209r.** If they came to the conditions contained in His Majesty's writing, they would see he would not depart from
the first [proposal] of peace; if they asked [more], His Majesty asked more [too], as a fair satisfaction. If, being
matters of [importance], they needed more time, a truce could be made meanwhile, which shows His Majesty's good will;
but he asks it only as a step to peace. They say the Infante [rejects] the truce and that peace cannot be [made]; Sánchez
said they could come to such honest conditions that His Majesty would have the Infante enter the peace.

**f. 209v.** They will [discuss] the matter among themselves. He has been told that a few days ago in the [Pregadi?]
they were [divided] over what to do with [the men] at Cremona; one said God had [helped] the Republic. They will not
declare themselves free of the obligation to France except under necessity. The French claim letters with the usual
bravado; the French ambassador says the King of France told him France has [held out] against the [Emperor] and
England. From Turin, letters of the 7th: [lances] and [x] thousand Gascon adventurers have arrived. As to the Turk, he
is not arming by sea this year; the [ships] they held are released.

**f. 210r.** The [defenders] resist bravely, with damage to the Turks, who keep arming at sea; Venice arms [for
reputation], on its consul's letters from [Constantinople], but they will not spend their money in the air. They make
money by selling offices. Some say they cannot come to terms with His Majesty, others that they may. The Viceroy of
Naples wrote from Mantua of his dealings with the rector of Verona and with Prospero [Colonna]; the rector will have
reported.

**f. 210v.** The Viceroy stayed five [days] in Mantua and then left; the French still hope for [Swiss] help but cannot
leave [Lombardy] unless forced. He has written to the Infante as ordered.
