**f. 132r.** Yesterday afternoon Sánchez had letters from Banisio at Trento by a post: the Viceroy of Naples had sent
there a man with 24,000 ducats that Prospero [Colonna] had asked for, to be taken to the camp; once in Mantua [the
Marquis] would forward it, but to pass it through the Signoria's lands Banisio asked Sánchez to obtain a safe-conduct.
The Signoria answered that they could not give a safe-conduct, but would write to the rectors of Verona to send [an
escort] so that the money could pass safely through their lands, and asked that those carrying it warn the rectors of
Verona when they would be in the Signoria's lands, so that men would be there to accompany them. He writes so to
Banisio by this post. He thinks they decline the safe-conduct so that the French should not learn of it.

**f. 132v.** He has also obtained a full safe-conduct for the Viceroy of Naples and all his company, and sends it to
Banisio to keep until the Viceroy arrives, which will not be long: the Viceroy wrote on the 26th of last month that he
would leave [...] by easy stages, having no need to stop to raise infantry. This morning he was told they would let him
know who is to be sent to the Viceroy; before this letter is closed His Majesty will be told who it is.
A French gentleman of Theodoro [Trivulzio], whom the Venetians sent to the King of France after the loss [at the
Bicocca?], left Lyon on the 17th of last month with letters of the King of France for the Republic and for [Andrea
Gritti]: in substance, that they should hold for certain he would lose France and his life rather than abandon this
enterprise; excuses for what was done on his side; the bad government of Lautrec was the cause of all the damage; he
was sending the Admiral of France to the enterprise.

**f. 133r.** The men he is bound to [provide] would not be lacking, and they would do more; [...] the Admiral, who will
bring [...] and do what they advise. Sánchez knows for certain that Lautrec was much [...] with the Venetians and wanted
to put himself in their lands with the troops that went to Cremona; they did not allow it; Andrea Gritti even protested
that [his own men] should go to guard Cremona; Lautrec asked them to put their own troops in Cremona and they answered
they would guard their lands. Lautrec left on the 25th, they say, [from ...] on the way back to France; those of
Cremona went with him for he had [no ...]; [a hundred ...] went by the road the Bastard of Savoy took. Others say the
Admiral of France will come with 1,200 lances, [...] thousand Gascons and [...] thousand of his own; but from what is
heard, on the whole road to Lyon there is not a man of war.
The Turk's ambassador, here since the 25th, is thought to have come more as a spy than for anything else. He says the
Turk complains that [...] are made in the Venetians' lands against his subjects, and that Christians who flee take
refuge there; that they should provide otherwise, or hold the peace broken.

**f. 133v.** He also asks for [...] a Venetian, not now here, who formerly left with jewels of the Turk's treasure worth
up to [...] thousand ducats. The Venetians say the ambassador has come for nothing else, and have written so to their
ambassadors everywhere. Sánchez has had a [spy] at the door of the ambassador's house to see whether anyone of those
who keep the secrets of the Signoria goes in; so far no one has, public or [secret]. The ambassador says the Turk will
not arm more than [...] sails; the reports agree that he is arming a hundred sails; the Signoria has letters of [22?]
April saying that he was arming without it being known where. The Doge told Sánchez he does not believe [the Turk will
do] anything this year. Venice is arming with reasonable speed, but he thinks they will not complete the fifty galleys
[...] until they have surer news of the Turk's armament; then their captain will leave, and on his departure the
reports of the Turk's fleet will be known. Those of the [government] say the Turk will go against [Apulia?].
A procurator of San Marco has been made here for [...] thousand ducats paid, a new thing, for procurators used not to be
made except [...].

**f. 134r.** [...] went to take leave of the Signoria [...] has left for Ferrara. He understands that the French relief
will not come in time and that Cremona will surrender to His Majesty's men. While he was writing this, the Signoria
gave him a copy, which goes with this letter, of the news they have had that the Turks were before Knin; they told him
they have written to Verona to give the Viceroy of Naples every honour and to receive him very well.
Venice, 2 June 1522.
