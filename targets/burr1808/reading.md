## 1. Baltimore, 10 February [1808] (img1, above the first ruled line)

Below the black line is copy of my last, by the schooner Wolf, Capt. Jones, to sail about the eighteenth. You will
hear again. Keep up your spirits. My plans are not changed. Yet at Baltimore incog., the tenth February. Business now
detains me. We must submit, submit, submit to ten days more of suspense. The promise in[?] my last was rashly made.
Affairs begin to look up. J'y travaille. What would Gamp not give to know that you are all well.

## 2. Copy of the letter of 31 January [1808] (img1, between and below the ruled lines)

My last letter was written eight days ago. Poor Gamp is still at Baltimore on this blessed thirty-first day of
January, still hoping and still persisting in all the plans so often written to you. It distresses me to think that
the hope of seeing me keeps you at the Oaks at the hazard of your life. Indeed my be[s]t endeavors are used and shall
be used to get to you. If after ten days further delay it be found impracticable, a person shall be sent express to
you, and for you just four hours work, huzzey.

No person must suspect that I mean to visit you in S.C. Let it be believed, except by mari, that I am in
Philadelphia.

## 3. Baltimore, 18 February [1808] (img2)

Baltimore, eighteenth Feb. Nothing new. The same hopes and intentions continue. Have[?] patience. {2?,11?,1,23}
hourly expectation of money for the long voyage, and nothing else detains[?] me.

---
Notes: "Gamp" is Burr himself. "J'y travaille": French, "I am working at it". "Huzzey": as written, hussy (a pet
name). "Mari": as written, French for husband (Joseph Alston). The Oaks: the Alston plantation on the Waccamaw, S.C.
Measured: 973 cipher numbers, 969 read as sense (99.6%); the open group is rubbed on a fold.
