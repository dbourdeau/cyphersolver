# Stenbock to Charles XII, petition of [1 May] 1714: English working translation

Translated from our French working translation of the established text (`petition_reading_sv.md`). Only the
uncertainties are marked here: {probable}, ‹unresolved›; … = not read. Read in part: about 83% of the cipher groups
sure or probable (`petition_counts.txt`).

**Most mighty King, most gracious Lord,**

I do not doubt that my earlier most humble letters, sent through Major Silfversparre, the war fiscal Melander and
Commissary Helding, have reached Your Majesty and been graciously received. But I lament, most gracious King, that God
and my fate, through the faithlessness of the Danes and their unheard-of and unchristian conduct, have taken from me the
means of doing Your Majesty any service … All my remonstrances to {Sweden} … {were} in vain; the men … without any
{provision} for {their} keep from the Danes, with … hunger, prison and other unheard-of constraints, and tortures …;
I myself am kept guarded like the worst spy, … so that no one may come to me … nor talk with me; so that I resolved not
to cross …

… I asked leave to go elsewhere for my health; it was refused me … the fear {for my person} is greater than I described it
before; by a tyrannical animosity … my {wretched} … captivity, out of chagrin … to want to murder me, to take my life …
Their talk of the Swedes and the whole nation is so beyond description that my heart bleeds when I must hear their
shameless songs of victory over what happened last year and lately in Finland and ‹in Pomerania?›. May God bring down
their contemptuous pride!

… I have learnt, most gracious King, that Baron Görtz seeks to blacken me and Meyerfeldt with Your Majesty, and that he
has written that "Count Stenbock behaves like an unreasonable man"; that title rightly belongs to Mr Görtz … the troops in
Holstein … all the more as his falseness comes to light, he who has not {fulfilled} what he had promised … for the
preservation of the men … the march; the Danes would have had no turn of fortune, and Your Majesty's men would {already}
be in Sweden. … {him} … the fatherland …

… forgotten, God the Most High … my innocence … What Baron Görtz has cooked up for the interest of Holstein, Tönning now
shows: it is razed to the ground, and the whole country left to the discretion of the King of {Denmark}. … Tönning could
{have passed} into neutral hands, the principality of Holstein to the disposal of Duke Charles Frederick, and Schleswig …
until a later decision. I readily admit that I could well have been relieved at Tönning, had anyone wished it, and that if
I had obtained leave to capitulate before Baron Görtz over the surrender of the fortress, it could just as well have been
kept in statu quo … the conditions … {endure}.

… that the Holstein affair be let go, and moreover [he] has striven … Stettin … I have letter and order from the Senate
to sound out secretly about ‹…› … nor to want to make peace; but, most gracious King, to beg for peace is shameful, and
{this court} seems too {proud} {at present} … to withdraw … ‹the Russian army› …; so that, not to prostitute Your
Majesty's gloire, I would not open my mouth; had I at once at the {beginning} or {beaten} … a commission … when Stettin
was being treated, and the King of {Denmark} …

… satisfied … with his allies, I believe … the {French} minister here, who shows me every friendship and sincerity; he
showed me a letter of the Marquis de Torcy, in which he {shows} that the King of France … ‹…› … about a separate peace
… keeps quiet … France … that Sweden not be brought down … the enemies … God … that the English and the Dutch … and
the other allies … their peace … the English and the Danes … the Swedes …

[p. 705] … the Danes on Sweden … {the court} … {harmful} … this very moment I learn that the Danes have the {design} of
crossing ‹…› … on Wismar and {Stettin} [Erlund: Stralsund] … defensively … to cover Holstein … {money} … to relieve …
to suffer want … new chicanery … with us … ransom … my captivity {will be} more than long, unless God and Your Majesty
help me. [If I knew that it would not displease Your Majesty, I would slip away in one way or another; but as I am
uncertain of it and fear that] my flight might in the long run harm Your Majesty's right in some point, I will … ‹…› …,
although I see that this captivity is not only the hindrance of all my welfare and fortune, but perhaps soon my coffin.
What God the Most High wills shall become of me … Your Majesty's gracious orders … I, an infirm man and ‹…›, could have
slipped away … design … my last drop of blood … for Your Majesty's service. That has thrown me into this misery … a
mortal fall … in the campaign … the jealousy towards me …

… acting defensively … advised … the Most High God … lamented. I throw myself in deepest submission at Your Majesty's
feet, most humbly praying you not to withdraw your grace from me and not to let my enemies {rejoice} over me, my
late(?) wife and my children, … poor … in affliction. May God the Most High keep and bless Your Majesty eternally in body
and soul, and bring you soon in health back to your kingdom; I remain, in deepest devotion and veneration, until my last
breath, Your Majesty's, my most gracious King's, [most humble servant].
(Erlund's N.B.: no name nor anything else was signed, but it was taken from Count Stenbock's drafts.)
