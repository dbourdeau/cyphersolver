# Delacroix to Létombe, Paris, 25 messidor an V (13 July 1797): the ministry's draft, merged transcription

Source: Archives diplomatiques (French foreign ministry archive), Correspondance politique, États-Unis, vol. 48
(39CP/48), ff. 41r-42r (f. 42v blank), images 81-83 of the volume in the archive's own viewer (a scan of its
microfilm, about 950 x 1,440 pixels): [f. 41r](https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/bg45qmrw70n9/2b676193-3faa-4124-8969-36508dbd0385),
[f. 41v](https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/bg45qmrw70n9/b9c3c223-5dcd-4f49-9812-0a19cafc5310),
[f. 42r](https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/bg45qmrw70n9/20350b61-e4d4-4561-ae72-5403928abb96).

Who read it: two AI agents (Claude Opus), each working alone from the images on 21 Nov 2026, 01:07 to 01:13 EST
(`transcription_A/`, `transcription_B/`). A third AI agent (this file's writer) compared them and looked at the image
where they differed, 01:32 to 01:36 EST. No person has checked it.

Agreement: of about 510 words on the three pages the two readings differ in 24 places; 23 are accents, capitals,
line-end marks or one reader flagging doubt where the other did not. **One is a difference of wording:** f. 41r line 22,
"aurez tenu" (reader A, doubtful) against "avez tenu" (reader B). On the image I read "aurez"; the code groups later
gave au-rez (`350 39`), so "aurez" stands.

Conventions: line breaks as in the manuscript; spelling as written; capitals normalised (the clerk writes capital-shaped
C, S and V at the start of many words); `[x]` = letters lost in the binding gutter and supplied; `{x?}` = doubtful
reading; `<del>` struck out; `<ins>` added.

Lines 24-25 are in a smaller, quicker script squeezed in at the foot of the page. They were added before the letter
was put into code: the groups carry them (`818 1033 677 301 141 1035 494 ...` = d'-é-co-no-mi-e politique ...).

Line 11: the word is written heavily over an earlier one. Both readers gave "facilité" with "utilité" as the
alternative. The code has group `1126` here and again four words on for "utiles", so **the text that was enciphered
read "d'une grande utilité"**. Either "utilité" is the word underneath and "facilité" was written over it after the
enciphering (to avoid "utilité ... utiles"), or the heavy word is "utilité" itself. Not settled from this image.
