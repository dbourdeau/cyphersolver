# Bernardo Bresegno to Ferrante Gonzaga, Rome, 5 Oct 1548: English translation of the cipher on c. 15r

From the Spanish of `reading_c15r.md` (the text of the contemporary decipherment, c. 22r, which the signs carry). The
departures of the signs from that text are marked in `reading_c15r.md`; none changes the sense.

Clear opening: Most illustrious and most excellent lord. [...] I wrote to Your Excellency and advised you of what there
was; what there is now is that

Cipher: Cardinal Morone has told me that the day before yesterday they confronted Fierrauante with his brother-in-law
in the castle, and that there was great contradiction between them; and afterwards, at night, they took his
brother-in-law out of the castle and with a boat moved him to Tor di Nona; and there, as the Pope has told him, they
put him to the rope without hoisting him, or anything else at all. And he confessed that it was true that for many days
he had of his own accord thought of making this treaty, and that he had many times told this man [Fierrauante] that he
should serve Your Excellency, that he would have a better position than the one he had there, without telling him
anything of the treaty; and that afterwards he had gone to Your Excellency and told you this thought of his, and that
Your Excellency had answered him that you could give him no commission at all without first advising His Majesty and
seeing what he ordered; that he should keep it going and carry the talks forward, and that you would advise him what he
was to do as soon as you knew His Majesty's will. That he then came to Bologna, Morone not being there, and spoke to
Fierrauante plainly and made promises on Your Excellency's behalf; and asked how he meant to carry this out, he said he
meant to put three hundred men in the Palace, who would hold out for him until the lord Duke of Florence sent men to
relieve him. This is what he has confessed so far. Your Excellency will please keep it to yourself, because Morone
would suffer harm for having told it in confession to the Pope.

Notes: "who would hold out for him" renders "losquales le sostuuiessen" (an earlier version had "who would hold him
there"). Cardinal Giovanni Morone had been legate of Bologna until July 1548; in October he was the former legate, which
fits "Morone not being there". "the rope" is the strappado; "without hoisting him" renders "sin al calle" (alzalle). "This man" is a reading
of "a este"; the Spanish does not name him. "Fierrauante" is given as on c. 22r and is not identified here.
