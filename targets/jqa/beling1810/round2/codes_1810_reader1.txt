= And he in my opinion there is no real change in the policy of France.—The Ministers and People about the Emperor prevail upon him some times to sign Edicts and orders which they think and represent to be changes of his policy; but they find themselves mistaken. His intentions remain the same. He thinks the only means by which he can influence England is by distressing English commerce—and that is after all his real object. I said I believed this opinion correct but certainly the means to the end were continually changing and the experiment upon which he now seemed to rely was to levy upon imports the most excessive Duties which if really levied must ultimately fall upon his own People—the Consumers.
