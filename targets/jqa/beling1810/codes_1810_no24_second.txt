= I then told him that my countrymen felt an extraordinary anxiety at these unusual detentions, from remarking their coincidence with the ordinances of Prussia, Mecklenburg, and Denmark, excluding us from their ports, and their apprehension that the same influence under which it was known that these orders had been issued might be exerted even here. = But in the public opinion it is viewed as an event of the most portentous complexion to the interests of both those Powers. Besides the personal disappointment of the King of Denmark's hopes in a manner which must have been highly aggravating to his feelings, Norway, the most extensive province of his dominions and far from being well affected to his government, lies contiguous to Sweden and separated from the rest of the Danish territories by sea. = And he in my opinion there is no real change in the policy of France.—The Ministers and People about the Emperor prevail upon him some times to sign Edicts and orders which they think and represent to be changes of his policy; but they find themselves mistaken. His intentions remain the same. He thinks the only means by which he can influence England is by distressing English commerce—and that is after all his real object. I said I believed this opinion correct but certainly the means to the end were continually changing and the experiment upon which he now seemed to rely was to levy upon imports the most excessive Duties which if really levied must ultimately fall upon his own People—the Consumers.
