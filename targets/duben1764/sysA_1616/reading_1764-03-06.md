## Reading

Mon Cher Comte — Deux jours avant d'avoir reçu Votre lettre du 7 Fevr. Monsieur Panin m'a prevenû sur ce qui en faisoit
l'objet. Il m'avoit appellé exprès et commencat par me dire qu'on lui avoit marqué une nouvelle de Stockgolm qui lui
faisoit de la peine, et sur laquelle il vouloit m'entretenir d'avaince sachant qu'elle ne manqueroit pas de parvenir a
ma connoisance. Monsieur d'Ogier dit il doit avoir mandé a Breteuil que son Ami Moltke l'avoit averti d'un concert et
aliance entre la Cour de Dannemarc et la Russie pour empecher tout changement dans la forme de Votre gouvernement,
nouvelle fausse et qui ne se fonde que sur les circonstances, ce que je vais Vous detailler, il y a deia quelque tems
que le Ministere Danois alarmé des bruit qui couroit d'une Diette extraordinaire en Svede s'adressat a monsieur de Korff
a Copenhague pour lui faire connoitre qu'il seroit tems que la Russie fût attentive aux affaires de ce Royaume, ou l'on
étoit a la veille d'une revolution totale qu'il faudroit prevenir, n'etant pas moins de l'interet de la Russie que de
cellei du Dannemarc d'empecher l'introduction de la Souveraineté en Svede: Sur cette insinuation les ordres auroient été
donnés a Monsieur de Korff de repondre qu'on avoit ici de toutes autres notions sur les affaires de la Svede et que
comme il paroissoit qu'en Dannemarc on les envisageait sous un autre point de vue, sa Cour desireroit fort qu'on s'en
ouvrit confidemment a elle, lui Korff seroit en état de donner la dessus les eclaircissements necessaires. Voila une
demarche fort innocente ajoutat Monsieur de Panin a laquelle l'on pourra donner une interpretation desavantageuse a
moins que le Roy ne soit instruit au vraï comment la chose s'est passé. Il reitera a cet ocasion les assuranses les
plus fortes sur l'intention ou étoit l'Imperatrice de concourrir avec le Roi en tout ce qui pourrait rendre sa situation
plus favorable et mettre nos affaires sur un pied solide et durable. Je le remerciois beaucoup de la confidence qu'il
m'avoit faite dont je promis de faire usage ajoutant au reste que comme le Roi en avoit agi si rondement en
communiquant les veritables vues lesquelles l'Imperatrice meme avoit trouvées aussi juste, que moderées il esperoit de
son coté que cette princesse en agiroit avec la meme sincerité et sans aucun detour qui ne pourroit que faire le plus
mauvais effet. Monsieur Panin protesta que rien n'étoit plus réel que l'intention ou étoit l'Imperatrice de retablir
l'autorité du Roi et de remedier aux inconveniens de notre constitution, qu'il convient être vicieuse de tout facon au
reste au travers de ces assuranses je demelais pourtant quelque crainte d'une revolution totale chez nous.
Apprehension que je tachay de la(?) dissiper autant qu'il m'étoit possible disant meme que la Cour perd de plus en plus
l'esperance qu'Elle avoit conçû de ramener peu a peu quelques membres du Senat et du parti François afin d'entretenir
la Ministere d'ici dans l'idée que le Roi ne cherche et ne s'attend a d'autre soutien qu'a celui de la Russie; avec un
peu de patience l'on saura a quoi s'en tenir; et je Vous promets de ne pas me laisser amuser longtems par des belles
parolles. Il faut prendre un parti et celui ou le Roi trouvera sa plus grande Sureté doit être preferé meme a l'amitié
de l'Imperatrice qui devient inutile si cette Princesse tarde trop a realiser Ses promesses.

## English translation

My dear Count — Two days before I received your letter of 7 February, Monsieur Panin forestalled me on the matter it
concerned. He had called me in on purpose and began by telling me that he had been sent a piece of news from Stockholm
which troubled him, and about which he wished to speak to me in advance, knowing that it would not fail to reach me.
Monsieur d'Ogier, he said, is supposed to have written to Breteuil that his friend Moltke had told him of a concert and
alliance between the Court of Denmark and Russia to prevent any change in the form of your government: a false piece of
news, founded only on the circumstances, which I shall set out for you. Some time ago the Danish Ministry, alarmed by
the rumours going round of an extraordinary Diet in Sweden, applied to Monsieur de Korff at Copenhagen to let him know
that it was time Russia paid attention to the affairs of that kingdom, where things stood on the eve of a total
revolution which ought to be prevented, it being no less in the interest of Russia than in that of Denmark to prevent
the introduction of sovereignty in Sweden. On this insinuation orders are said to have been given to Monsieur de Korff
to answer that here one had quite different notions of the affairs of Sweden, and that, since in Denmark they seemed to
be seen from another point of view, his Court would much wish that the Danes open their minds to it in confidence; he,
Korff, would be in a position to give the necessary explanations on that point. "There," added Monsieur de Panin, "is
a quite innocent step, to which an unfavourable interpretation may be given unless the King is truly informed of how
the thing happened." On this occasion he repeated the strongest assurances of the Empress's intention to work with the
King in everything that might make his situation more favourable and put our affairs on a solid and lasting footing. I
thanked him warmly for the confidence he had shown me, of which I promised to make use, adding moreover that, as the
King had acted so openly in communicating his true aims, which the Empress herself had found as just as they were
moderate, he hoped for his part that this Princess would act with the same sincerity and without any evasion, which
could only have the worst effect. Monsieur Panin protested that nothing was more real than the Empress's intention to
restore the King's authority and to remedy the drawbacks of our constitution, which he agrees is faulty in every
respect. Through these assurances, however, I made out some fear of a total revolution in our country, an apprehension
that I tried to dispel as far as I could, saying even that the Court is losing more and more the hope it had conceived
of bringing round, little by little, some members of the Senate and of the French party, so as to keep the Ministry here
in the idea that the King seeks and expects no support other than that of Russia. With a little patience we shall know
where we stand; and I promise you not to let myself be beguiled for long by fine words. One must take a side, and the
one where the King will find his greatest security must be preferred even to the friendship of the Empress, which
becomes useless if this Princess is too slow to fulfil her promises.

## Summary and context

Düben reports a conversation with Nikita Panin, who heads Russian foreign affairs. Panin forestalls a report, passed
from Ogier (the French envoy at Copenhagen) to Breteuil (the French envoy at St Petersburg) on the word of the Danish
minister Moltke, of a Danish-Russian concert to block any change in the Swedish constitution. His version: Denmark,
alarmed by talk of an extraordinary Diet, had urged the Russian envoy Korff to act against a "total revolution", that
is, the introduction of sovereignty in Sweden, and Russia answered only by asking Denmark for its views. Panin repeats
the Empress's promise to restore the King's authority. Düben notes that the Russians still fear a royalist coup. He
advises that the King should take whichever side gives him most security, even at the cost of the Empress's friendship
if her promises are slow to come. The names (Panin, Breteuil, Moltke, Korff, Ogier) are given as the clear writes them;
the identifications in this paragraph are this reading's, not the document's.
