## English translation
I have received with very deep gratitude your two letters of 10 May and 22 April, as well as another in cipher without a date.
You know my feelings well enough not to doubt the pleasure with which I learned of the good prospects that are opening at home
for the success of your affairs. The court here does not seem to hold such favourable ideas. On the contrary, I have been given
to understand that Their Majesties let themselves be imposed upon more and more by the promises of certain people who, far from
ever meaning to keep them, only try to turn the Court away from its old alliances and then leave it in the lurch. I apply
myself as far as I can to destroying these impressions, and at least they sometimes pretend to believe me. Monsieur de Panin
also continues to give the most positive assurances of the Empress's firm intention to put the King in a more desirable
situation; but as far as I have been able to fathom this Princess's way of thinking, it would be useless to hope that she will
push this project to any real point, and I know beyond doubt that the King of Prussia strengthens her in these feelings. That
Prince has just bound himself closely to her and will probably persist for a long time yet in his old system. You see, then,
how important it is in such delicate circumstances to hide ("cacher ses predilections") one's preferences, to offend no one and to handle both parties so as
to profit skilfully from circumstances, all the more because there are people who, to pay court to the Empress, try to alarm
her about the dispositions of Her Majesty the Queen. I will not believe that Saldern and Stakejeff are among them, but what I
know for certain is that they report here all kinds of stories that they would do better to keep to themselves. The former,
far from showing me the least confidence, avoids speaking to me, which I would rather put down to pure cowardice, which always
makes him see the knout before his eyes, than to any other reason, since I have always tried to win him over with a thousand
courtesies. If the Diet is brought forward, I shall not fail to use the occasion to press Monsieur de Panin to state
categorically what help Her Majesty the Empress intends to offer.

## Summary
Spring 1764: Düben reports that St Petersburg is cooler than Stockholm hopes. Panin keeps promising the Empress's support for
the King, but Düben judges that Catherine will not go far, held back by Frederick II, with whom she has just allied. He asks
for discretion about the Queen's party preferences, distrusts the Russian diplomats Saldern and Stakejeff (Stachiev), who
carry stories to St Petersburg, and plans to press Panin for a clear answer on Russian help if the Diet is called early.
Names are as written; identifications are not checked against the literature.
