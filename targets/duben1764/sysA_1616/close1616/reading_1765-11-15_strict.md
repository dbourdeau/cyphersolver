## English translation (annotated)

The gloss is translated with the clear passages around it; passages in cipher are between « ». Notes in brackets
are ours, not the document's.

15 Nov. 1765. Sir, Do not be surprised, my dear Count, at the silence I have kept towards you for some time. The
delicacy of the circumstances made it necessary, as no doubt you felt yourself, since you for your part decided to
leave my last letter unanswered. «Besides [1], it would have been superfluous to add anything to what I had the honour
of writing to you in my previous letters, once I had seen that Their Majesties [2], far from following the advice I
took the liberty of giving them, saw fit to take other measures and to adopt a plan quite contrary to the one that had
been settled at my departure [3]. Its consequences have been most unfortunate and could only become so, and I lament
it with all my heart. What gives me no less keen distress is the step the King has lately been advised to take at
this Court [3] concerning the marriage of the Crown Prince [4]: a step that is not only useless but will only
compromise Their Majesties in a disagreeable way, since this Court, far from wishing to oppose an affair that it
regards as advantageous to Sweden and fit to strengthen the tranquillity of the North, will on the contrary try to
contribute to it and make a merit of it with the Court of Denmark, which, for its part, once informed of this attempt,
cannot but be much irritated.» Judge then, my dear Count, whether it would not have been better to spare ourselves
«this negotiation, which could only be fruitless. For my part I suppress every reflection on a project as essential
as the marriage in question,» while forming the most ardent wishes that it may soon be happily concluded, to the full
satisfaction of the parties concerned. «As the Empress [5] never wears the Order of the Seraphim, and the King, by a
just reciprocity, might therefore perhaps not wish to wear the Order of St Andrew on the 30th of this month [6],» I
would beg you, my dear Count, in that case to try to arrange the matter so that at least «no open displeasure is
shown [7]; the best would be for the King not to go out that day, on the pretext of being unwell.» God knows that in
this advice I have no other aim than to remove anything that might cause the least coolness between two Courts that
have so great an interest in remaining united. I know your zeal for the service of our dear Master [8], and so I
flatter myself that you will make common cause with me in the matter at hand. «The crisis we are in is terrible, and I
assure you as a man of honour that it is more important than ever to handle the Empress with care and to win her
friendship by strengthening a system whose only aim is to keep the peace of the North. If, on the contrary, one
continues to thwart this Princess's views, I cannot answer [9] for the fatal consequences that will follow.» I write
this to you in the greatest confidence, hoping, my dear Count, that you will make no other use of it than your
friendship for me and your known discretion will dictate. Allow me at the same time to ask you for a short word in
reply, which cannot but give very keen pleasure to one who is as attached to you as I am. [monogram "C. F.?"] A million
compliments to Madame Mörner.

[1] The cipher has "Aussi bien" ("besides", "in any case"); the gloss "Si bien" drops the first word. [2] *L: L: M:
M:*, Leurs Majestés: King Adolf Frederick and Queen Lovisa Ulrika. [3] "cette Cour" is the court where the writer is:
the step was taken "auprès de cette Cour", and this Court will "make a merit of it with the Court of Denmark", so it is
the Russian court, and the writer writes from St Petersburg; "a mon depart" is then his departure from Stockholm. This
answers the question raised in the review of PR #641: the 14 Nov summary ("the writer, who has left St Petersburg")
should read "the writer, at St Petersburg". [4] Crown Prince Gustav (later Gustav III), whose marriage to Sophia
Magdalena of Denmark was then in negotiation. [5] Catherine II. [6] St Andrew's day, 30 November, the feast of the
Russian Order of St Andrew; the Order of the Seraphim is the Swedish order. [7] The gloss runs "pourqu'au moins On ne
marque quelque mecontentement ouvert", without "pas"; between "On ne" and "marque" the cipher has seven groups that
neither the gloss nor the key reads. [8] Adolf Frederick. [9] The gloss "je ne me Reponde pas"; the cipher, as far as
the key reads it, has no "me" or "pas" here. The writer is not identified; the identifications are ours, from the
text, and none is in the document.
