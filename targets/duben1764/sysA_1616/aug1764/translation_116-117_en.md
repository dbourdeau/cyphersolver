## Translation

[116] I have never dared to guarantee the sincerity of Her Majesty the Empress's dispositions. I only said that they
seemed favourable, that Monsieur Panin had confirmed their reality to me with the strongest assurances, and that it was
therefore absolutely necessary to handle this Princess with more care than ever. I will add today that I am more and more
persuaded that she means to increase the King's authority, and that she will also spend money to that end. But one must
know at the same time that her intention is to go no further than restoring the form of government of seventeen hundred
and twenty-one (so written), and that she keeps to herself the right to have her Minister distribute the sums she means to
spend on it. All the representations I have made to obtain some change on this point have been fruitless, and I do not
even think it would be prudent to insist further, because that would only strengthen this Princess in the suspicions she
may perhaps already have that someone aims at sovereignty, a project which she will not only never approve but will rather
thwart as far as she can. For my own part I think that in such circumstances it is important to dissemble and to take
from a bad payer what one can, while always trying to gain ground. As for the attempts Denmark is making to bind itself
to Russia through Bernsdorff, they are very real, but I believe I can say with confidence that they will do no harm to the
King's personal interests. For the rest, my dear Count, I am very far from daring to blame the conduct of Her Majesty the
Queen; I rather applaud it with all possible submission, only reporting,

[117] as my duty requires, the opinion held of it here. Besides, I must warn you that from now on I think I should use
greater caution in what I write, since unfortunately my news comes back to this country, I do not know how. Stakejef[?]
came to complain to me that the Queen believes he sends from here reports harmful to her service; from this I see clearly
that there are people who betray the secret and abuse Her Majesty's confidence. So I hope she will agree that, for her own
safety more than for mine, I shall from now on be sparing with correspondence, while keeping myself ready to act on every
occasion with the same warmth and the same zeal that have always moved me when her service was in question. [Please]
express to her my respectful attachment and the eternal gratitude I owe for the kindnesses with which she deigns to honour
me; her gracious letter of the eighteenth of June is a proof of them that has touched me deeply, and I beg you, my dear
Count, to convey my most humble and most submissive thanks.
