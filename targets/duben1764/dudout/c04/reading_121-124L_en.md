# Düben to Gyldenstolpe ("mon cher Comte"), St Petersburg, 31 Dec 1764 (v.st.), cipher pages 121-124L — English translation

Translated from the contemporary clear text on images 127-128 ("Tillhör brev av den 31/12 1764"), which the cipher follows
group by group (decode_121-124L_key_c04.txt). Cipher page order = clear order: 121, 122L, 123R, 124L, then 122R.
Words in [ ] are added for sense.

You will see, before the arrival of this one, that not only have I anticipated the King's orders concerning Count Osterman,
but also that the steps I took to obtain for him marks of the favour of Her Majesty the Empress have not been fruitless.
It remains only to wish that this Count make himself worthy of the King's protection by representing the affairs of Sweden
in their true light, and without letting himself be blinded by interested persons who give false ideas of them.

To answer the other articles of your dear letter of 28 November, I could refer to what I had the honour of writing to you
on the 31st of August; but as I do not know whether that letter reached you or not, I will briefly recall its content.
Heaven is my witness, my dear Count, that since I have been here I have done everything to engage the Empress to support
the King's interests, and above all to grant him a good sum of money. If I have not been fortunate enough to succeed, it is
assuredly not my fault. Whether from want of generosity on the part of this Princess, or from distrust of the King's true
intentions, it has not been possible to obtain what I wanted. What would you say, my dear Count, if this distrust had been
inspired in her by a person in whom one has most confided, and who [has not ceased] to insinuate covertly to Mr Osterman
that the Court was aiming at sovereignty? Stachieff will name you the mask and will make known more particularly the
Empress's intentions regarding our affairs. As for me, I have contradicted in vain this rumour, as well as several others
that have been spread about the predilection their Majesties had for Fersen and for the French party; all this has produced
nothing but a very visible coolness towards me, and very [?] in truth, since people may believe I came here to impose upon
the Empress. I do not however cease making my representations, and they keep promising to support the King's interests
vigorously, but the little confidence shown me when it comes to opening up about the means does not give me a high idea of
the vigour with which this affair will be pushed.

As regards the money, I have already told you in my last, my dear Count, that no reliance at all is to be placed on it.
Some will be spent during the Diet, but it will be Count Osterman who makes the distribution. If the Empress wants to help
the King, she has a far more effective means for it than anything the Court, backed by a million roubles, could achieve.
Time will show whether one will want to use it. Meanwhile it is certain that false steps at home have spoiled matters here.
I am in despair at not having been able to render all the services I would have wished, but at least I warned several
months ago that people were confiding in rascals whose only intention was to deceive. The sequel has well proved it since,
and the grief this causes me, together with my uselessness here, makes me ardently wish to be able to return.

I expect from your friendship that you keep this letter, as well as all those I write to you in cipher, to be returned to
me on my return.
