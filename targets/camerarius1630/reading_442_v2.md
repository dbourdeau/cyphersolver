## English (gist)

At the very hour I was sealing the packet, Prince Frederick Henry came to me, offering at length his zeal and all he
could do at Your Majesty's wish. I took the chance to commend my negotiation with the States General to him, chiefly
that he bring the States of Holland to sounder counsels. I raised again the matter of Count Wilhelm's troops, lest,
if they were disbanded, the companies fall into the enemy's hands, to Your Majesty's prejudice: the Prince and the
States would do Your Majesty a most welcome thing if they kept them, either making some diversion of their own, or
maintaining them for some months at their cost under Your Majesty's name. He answered that he could promise nothing
certain, since all depends on the States, and nothing is to be hoped for the King of Sweden before the assembly of
Holland. But since the Prince of Neuburg has obtained from the Infanta that the Spanish soldiers be led out of
Jülich (except a few places), and has gone ... to obtain the same from the Emperor, there is no doubt that if the
Imperial soldiers are likewise removed from the borders of the United Provinces, the States General will also
disband Count Wilhelm's troops. Your Majesty would therefore do well to send orders in good time to Camerarius and
to send here someone versed in war, who in case of the disbandment would take those companies into Your Majesty's
service. The whole difficulty will be about ..., since the States have resolved to be neutral with the Empire and
the neighbouring bishoprics, and will not suffer any trouble or reproach to fall on them from it. I thanked the
Prince and asked him meanwhile to discuss it again with the most loyal of the college, whether a better way might
be found to satisfy Your Majesty's wishes and turn the danger away from the common cause. He promised, and
Camerarius will press the matter. He said again that orders must come from Your Majesty in good time and some
officer arrive for every event. Dated as in the letter.

End of the main letter: a friend warned me in secret that some persons ill-disposed to Your Majesty are scheming to
set the King of Denmark against Your Majesty over the Prussian tolls, so that jointly, by mutual intercession or even
by force, they may throw that burden off. ... Camerarius will strain every nerve to find the truth; the next
assembly of Holland will reveal everything. Your Majesty's ministers in Denmark and Hamburg should enquire into the
King of Denmark's counsels and actions and keep him friendly to Your Majesty. Camerarius knows that for this matter
of the tolls he incurs the hatred of some here, and that some may plot against him with Your Majesty by calumny;
but he trusts that his loyalty and constancy in defending Your Majesty's reputation will weigh more.
