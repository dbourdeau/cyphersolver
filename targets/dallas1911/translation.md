# dallas1911: English translation of the letter of 17 March 1911

Translation by SeldomPhooey (issue #520, 13 Nov 2026; AI agents), as published in the issue. CAPITALS = deciphered
words; `*` = emended; `[?...]` = rests on context alone; `[MAU...]` = a hole. Square brackets without `?` are the

17 March [1]911. Courier: Escudero. Key: the Dallas one.

Mr. President:

As I believe my previous couriers have not reached you, I inform you that I went to San Antonio, where I left fully drawn up the documents that had to be signed and sent to Washington; I know that with them direct steps have already been taken with the representatives of the friendly nations. I wrote to the Dr. asking whether he accepted the appointment you gave him, and he has not yet decided; but from his work we are convinced that he has taken up the matter with the same interest as we have. I send you several letters from your family in reply to the one you wrote to Sarita; by telegraph I asked Gustavo to write, but his letter has not arrived.

Of Don Venustiano nothing definite is known, except that he went to meet Limantour. I sent the Dr., in the form of a decree, the appointment of the Commission to issue bonds, and we believe we shall soon have a good result. As business here grows more numerous and important every day, we decided that I should come and settle here, and I took charge of affairs about ten days ago. I send you a letter from CARACRISTI about the appointment you made in his favour, as he asserts; he says he spoke with you at Guadalupe. We have doubted it, because if that gentleman is really authorised by you, he has overstepped his powers in a most dangerous way, as you can see from the last [?DECLARATION*] he made here on the eve of ROOSEVELT's arrival, which naturally caused a sensation. I found myself obliged to issue the attached warning until you tell me exactly what role you have given that gentleman. I had the good fortune to obtain the ORIGINAL of the LETTER* that he is SENDING you by MASON. It gives me to understand that in reality you have given him no authority at all and that he is after an authorisation. I see that it contains a [?DANGEROUS*] THREAT, about which you should not [sic: "no tener cuidado"; probably meant "should take care"] be CAREFUL. I suspect that CRISTI* has had a hand in producing enough agitation in Washington to move the troops towards our border, which I believe true from the explanations you will see in the enclosed editorial "The Reason for Movilization." CRISTI* told me that he spoke with KNOX, revealing to him that he knew that DÍAZ [?HAD ARRANGED] with JAPAN to bring in 15 THOUSAND JAPANESE* disguised as [?COLONISTS*], in separate parties, but with the purpose that the said EMPIRE should have IN [?MEXICO*], at the right moment, enough FORCES with which to [?ATTACK*] THE [?AMERICANS*]. This gentleman went to Washington and did not get in touch with the Dr., since he considers all of us envious of his position, whereas I alone know him. He is a man who, I am told, seeks to monopolise, if only in appearance, the whole management of [?OUR*] DIPLOMATIC AFFAIRS, and to obtain that representation he does not hesitate to make declarations as bold as the one I SEND YOU. Shortly before this he made others in a Dallas newspaper, setting out part of our programme, but saying that you would adopt towards the Asiatic races the same policy as the U.S., provided the latter helped us morally and materially. He also spoke of the 15 THOUSAND JAPANESE* and asserted, besides, that your programme contained the promise to return to the clergy the property confiscated from it. I have informed the Dr. of all this. Unfortunately I have received no letter, and I do not know whether he made in Washington, as I did, some similar correction concerning [the claim] that we would be willing to accept peace THROUGH INTERVENTION, to which CRISTI refers. It is a fact that MASON is supported here by that gentleman and even lives in his room. Now, since my correction has surely upset all his plans, he may manage to get into his hands and read all the letters I delivered to MASON, who is the courier you appointed to bring you news of us. For this reason, pay no attention to what he may tell you in the letter MASON will bring you, and rely only on the contents of this one. However, so that CRISTI does not realise that I have seen through his game, I will put into MASON's hands several other letters that I judge to be of no great importance. I MADE A COPY of that LETTER and sent it to the doctor, but NOBODY ELSE knows of the matter, of which I SHALL KEEP perfect SECRECY. Please do not let MASON know that you are aware of his ties with CRISTI; on that understanding you may go on employing him AS A COURIER.

We are much impressed by the battle of Casas Grandes; I sincerely regret that victory did not accompany our insurgent patriots on this occasion. The whole American public admires the courage of the insurgents in facing the machine guns. The high opinion held of you has now risen to an extraordinary height, and nobody doubts in the least that the insurrection has as its chief a man capable of every sacrifice and every heroism. In the strictest confidence I tell you that roughly this judgement of you has reached my ears: "Mr. Madero is a brave, intelligent man with all the qualities of a statesman; but on the BATTLEFIELD he is very INDULGENT* towards HIS SUBORDINATES." That is to say, he has not managed to shed HIS NATURAL BENEVOLENCE when MILITARY [?DISCIPLINE] [?REQUIRES] you to be MORE [?RIGOROUS] ----, and I fear there is some truth in it. The same person, explaining this INDULGENCE, says that YOU LET your STAFF [?OFFICERS] give their OPINION a great deal, and that they all but IMPOSE their WILL on you. I believe there is some partiality in this, but I remember having TAKEN THE LIBERTY OF WARNING YOU that this would be the only [?DANGER*] YOU would RUN in the CAMPAIGN, unless YOU were [?MORE*] FORCEFUL. Americans of standing here, who express public opinion, told me some days ago that there is a fear that "the wine may turn to vinegar". They gave me to understand that if you do not soon master the situation, in the sense that everyone in the FIELD* OF [?OPERATIONS*] RENDERS YOU complete [?OBEDIENCE], the Americans will believe that things will GO ON LIKE THIS. Concerned by this idea, I am striving to instil deeply in the minds of our fellow partisans the idea of COMPLETE SUBMISSION to YOU and TO the Plan you have proclaimed, making them solemnly issue the appropriate declarations in each EXPEDITION. You will see something of this in a little sheet of instructions that I send you, which I addressed to Lic. Guadalupe González, whom I appointed our Delegate in Douglas on account of Blanco's approach to Agua Prieta. So that you may be informed of the situation, I send you a copy of the letter I have just received from the Delegate. I had previously informed him that you had commissioned Mr. G. Valencia to have BLANCO [?JOIN HIM*]. Mr. Mason assures me that my brother Roque was always in your sight. But since you do not mention him in your letter to Sarita, I have been a little uneasy; yet if his conduct is as that gentleman has described it to me, I am proud. Please give him an affectionate embrace of congratulation and tell him that Alfonso is already attending to the matter he recommended to Julio. Notwithstanding what you say, there are many doubts as to who died in the battle. It would be very useful and advisable for you to order complete data sent to me, since on all sides I am asked who the prisoners, the wounded and the dead are. I send you the latest press cuttings, from which you will see that there is great movement in Washington. At the last moment Gustavo tells me that they spoke with Limantour, who offered a complete change of policy. But given the opinion the Dr. (V. Gómez) holds on how this matter should be conducted, which is also mine, according to cuttings I send you, I do not think much can be obtained, since first of all one has to reckon with you. I send you letters in which the Dr. sets out his views. It is on those bases that something could be done, for you should know that Pino Suárez has appeared on the scene and is in New Orleans ORGANISING an EXPEDITION, while in Yucatán the Revolution has already broken out in force. He says he wants no compromises, to the point that Yucatán, Tabasco and Campeche would oppose them. I know nothing of the quarrel between the Dr. and Gustavo that the press spoke of, but it cannot be important, since Gustavo denied the rumour. I enclose messages from Don E., and I confirm them by telling you that the Revolution is in full force in Yucatán, Guerrero, Morelos, Durango, Sinaloa, Sonora, Coahuila (Cepeda has risen, defeating federal troops), Nuevo León (Rayones region), Oaxaca, Veracruz, Puebla, Tlaxcala and California. As regards California, we believe that the danger of great difficulties with the so-called Liberal Party has been averted; Flores Magón [and his group] have been left alone. Antonio Villarreal came to see me and agreed that shortly he will take command of an expedition of liberals. On entering [the country] he will issue an excellent manifesto recognising you as President and Chief of the Insurrection, whose orders he will obey completely, with the reservation that after the triumph of the Revolution they will go on fighting on democratic ground. We are going to supply him with materiel, and we believe that this fine example will be more effective than the speeches and tours we had planned as a means of attracting the remaining liberal elements. I consider it of immense importance that you write to me immediately, telling me what policy we should follow on the question of expeditions: whether they are to aim at JOINING YOU or act otherwise. The second SHIPMENT [MAU...] has arrived; it was found to be in bad condition and the "confite" [code word, perhaps for ammunition] largely burst, of which I have already advised New York so that they remedy it. In accordance with the verbal instructions that reached me, Escudero is today taking 28,000 "canutillos" [code word, perhaps for cartridges] of the three kinds, and I will deliver [them] to you in the region of PALOMAS, for which we are already looking for an agent whose name and place I will communicate to you. Escudero is accompanied by about FIFTY* well-armed men. Some days ago an "agricultural" expedition was organised under the command of CANO; it failed through inexperience in taking precautions. Many people ask whether, in view of the failure at Casas Grandes, you will order those responsible to be tried. Please pay attention to the Dr.'s recommendations on the requirements Washington demands for granting belligerency. In view of them, if Agua Prieta or Ojinaga should fall, you would communicate it urgently, so that steps may be taken towards establishing the Provisional Government.

I enclose a letter from Sarita, but none from Alfonso or Gus., since it was impossible to hold the courier. May all the brave men receive, in the name of the Mexicans resident here, warm congratulations for their conduct at Casas Grandes.

Your most affectionate and attentive servant,
Olliphant.
(F. González Garza)
