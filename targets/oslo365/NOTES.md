# Five line endings on the Michigan–Oslo papyrus

Status: attempted, open

A name in the Isis spell on the front fits two tentative letters on the back. A third would have to be beta where the editor reads tau.


The external searches use the proposed letters as constraints. The searches
for neighboring lines leave line 5 out of selection. A Coptic comparison
maps only the Greek-derived characters to corresponding Greek letters.
Unmapped characters split the search. This tests graphical coincidence, not
translation. The data include later texts of different genres.

| Round | Five tests | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greek-script search, Greek-derived Coptic character search, ordered matches across lines, flexible gaps, reversed sequences. | The Kyprianos export supplies an external control. Across 341 records with eligible mapped strings, only line 4 yielded strict hits among lines 2–4. T1479 supplies an incidental line-5 fit crossing words in the Letter of Abgar. Reset. |
| 2 | Subject-selected tests for Isis, Osiris, attraction, compulsion and stars; independent sample of Greek editions checked as a control. | No multi-line parallel. The fragment of an ordinary word and following letters in GEMF 4 fits the line-4 mask. No exact matches to the line-2 or line-3 mask in the Greek edition sample. |
| 3 | Test whole Greek words, word endings, continuous Greek phrases, co-occurrence of all three selection masks in one sentence, and the physical distinction between beta and tau in the published key. | In 26,492 sentences across two treebanks, the line-5 suffix mask occurred 24 times in running text and zero times as a whole word. The cipher table distinguishes a shallow cup for beta from an upright with a lateral stroke for tau. No verso photograph established which is present. Reset. |
| 4 | Test equal line widths, ragged widths, short name-list widths, a lost continuation after line 4, and reverse arrangement within each recto passage. | Zero eligible chains for lines 2–4 under all five hypotheses, even allowing one discrepant tentative letter per line while keeping omega and tau on line 3 fixed. First negative round. |
| 5 | Test adjoining names, an inserted EI refrain, the great-named epithet, IAKŌ, and IŌTHĒ between each ordered pair of the selected recto names. | No individual strict match for lines 2, 3 or 4 in any family. These are candidate constructions; their order on the back is unknown. Second negative round. |

Additional controls included 40 deterministic permutations of letter
positions, and reflection, doubling, progressive deletion from either end,
and expansion with a seven-vowel series for individual recto names. None
produced a multi-line reading. The Greek comparison with GEMF 1, 3–7 and
9–10 used 245 uninterrupted machine-extracted strings at least seven letters
long, within printed lines and excluding bracketed restorations. This small
sample has incomplete coverage and OCR errors.

The comparisons use the Kyprianos texts export and the Perseus and PROIEL Ancient Greek training files linked below. The surviving traces leave all five line endings unresolved.
