# Biermann to Meyercron (Paris), Copenhagen 23 March 1675: cipher passage, English translation

Translated from the French reading in `reading_1675-03-23_meyercron.md` (cipher of pp. 104-105, Arkivalieronline
image 109; key: TKUA "Chiffer" 1670-1709, image 55). Words in [ ] are in clear in the copybook; { } is a name code
of the key sheet; [?] marks the one sign not on the sheet (the open square of p105.24).

[Notwithstanding which,] His Majesty wants you not to cease from continuing [always], at the court [where] you are,
the same arguments that you have held there until now, [and particularly that] you make it understood there how
little relation the demands of the {Swedish ambassador} bear to the advantageous fruits he had earlier let it be
rumoured would come of his commission, [and] the evident [and] solid reasons that His Majesty has to believe that the
ardour which the said {Swedish ambassador} shows for concluding some just [and] reasonable {treaty} here is
[hardly] sincere, [seeing the little likelihood there is that] His Majesty could condescend to the conditions which
he[?] proposes.

Notes

- "he[?] proposes": the French is `qui[?] propose`; the sign after `qui` is an open square that the key sheet does
  not carry. `new_values.tsv` gives it the value l from three places in the Høeg letter of 9 January 1675, which
  makes "qu'il propose" (he proposes). The translation follows that sense and keeps the mark.
- "you": the cipher writes `vos` for `vous` at p104.22 (no u sign), a slip of the copyist kept in the French.
- "condescend": the cipher has `condescondre` (343 where the sense needs 341), kept as written in the French.
- The {Swedish ambassador} is code 154; the sheet's first word is unclear ("Svenske"/"Spanske") and the value is
  taken from the context (Count Niels Brahe's negotiation in Copenhagen), as the French reading says.
