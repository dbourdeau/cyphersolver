# LJS 51, chains 73r.5 to 84r.12 (images 150-173): English translation

Source: `chains_0150_0173.jsonl` only (master-TIFF readings). The range has 24 chains, and every one has a plaintext
on the page. Four chains need a word before they are read:

- **73v.8 and 74r.3**: two pieces sent together to the same solver, whose answers stand on the next opening (74v).
  74v l.2-3 answers 74r.3 (the second piece) and 74v l.6-7 answers 73v.8 ("the first two verses"). Both are petitions
  about a chancery decree (tawqi'), and the chancery terms are explained under 73v.8.
- **76v.6 and 77r.3**: one verse enciphered twice, the second time "in another pen" with one word changed.
- **80r.9, 80v.9 and 81r.5**: verses that "allude to" the names of scripts (pens). 80r.9 names seven, and its record
  cites a book, the *Kitab al-Ajwiba al-Shamiyya*.
- **83r.1**: the dividers between words are a cipher letter, not the usual dotted stroke.

The Arabic is copied as it stands in the record. A slash (/) in the Arabic is the record's own line division. The
hemistich division in the English is mine, made from the metre and rhyme where they could be found. [?] marks a word
the record flags as doubtful; alternatives are only in the notes. "Translation confidence" is my confidence in the
English. It is not the record's plaintext certainty.

In the headings, "the compiler" is the writer of the book (Ibn al-Durayhim in the record) and "the master"
(al-makhdum) is the patron who solves most of the pieces; at 83r he is called "the master al-'Ala'i". On ff. 73r-84v
the circle is wider than before, and several pieces are solved by others or in company. Named in the record: Shaykh
Shihab al-Din the translator (al-mutarjim); Amin al-Din al-Hanafi, "qadi of Hama today" (al-Mizzi at 83v); the qadi
Nasir al-Din ibn al-Shati [?], muwaqqi' al-dast al-sharif (a secretary of the chancery bench); the chief qadi Jamal
al-Din al-Hanafi and his son the qadi Sadr al-Din; Shaykh Burhan al-Din al-Qudsi al-Hanafi, imam of the malik
al-umara' in Syria; Shaykh Ibrahim al-Wazfi [?] in Tripoli; a Shaykh Ahmad Siba'a [?]; Shaykh Shams al-Din, khatib of
al-Salihiyya in Damascus; and Shaykh Nasir al-Din [ibn?] Sallar in Damascus. Poets named: Sharaf al-Din Ibn al-Halawi
al-Mawsili (76r) and Ibn al-Rumi (81v).

## 73r.5 (cipher 73r l.5-10; plaintext 73r l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
في ماله عز بذله العين حاجب فباب نداه لا يرى قط مغلقا / حياض سماح مو رياض [?] فضايل فلله ما اندى واذكى واعبقا

**English:**
- In his wealth, a chamberlain (hajib) is rare ('azza) for his spending of coin (al-'ayn),
- so the door of his bounty is never seen shut.
- Pools of liberality, [مو?] gardens [?] of virtues:
- how moist (generous), how bright, how fragrant, by God!

**Metre:** tawil (the second verse and the second hemistich of the first scan; the opening does not cleanly as read).

**Note:** the compiler's own verse in praise of the master ("of my own composition", 73r l.4). The first verse turns on
a double sense: al-'ayn is both "the eye" and "cash, gold coin", and hajib is both "the eyebrow" (which sits over the
eye) and "the chamberlain" (who keeps the door). His gold has no doorkeeper, so the door of his bounty is never shut.
This is my reading of a compressed line; the record gives only the Arabic. The three exclamations of the last
hemistich answer the pools (moist, nada) and the gardens (bright and fragrant). The third word of the second verse is
cut by a paper crack: مو, a lost letter, then ياض. The cipher groups (2 + 4, with the alif sign in the place it has in
رياض) support مو رياض or a near form, not the draft's مورودة (record). The cipher has 3 signs for في, so its first word
may be وفي or ففي, and it writes ماله as two words (record). The piece was solved by Shaykh Shihab al-Din the
translator, and the compiler says the solution "did not reach me" (73r l.11). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 73r l.4: "by my life, he is indeed so. Then I wrote for him, may God make his rank great, (verse)
of my own composition"; 73r l.11: "the shaykh Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim solved it ... and (the solution) did not reach
me; and it is". 73r l.1 closes the previous chain (72v.9).

## 73v.8 (cipher 73v l.8-12; plaintext 74v l.6-7)

**Arabic (record):**
يقبل الارض ويهنى باني [?] جا توقيع في بهدله / شكرا لاحسانك فارسم له سياقة والبا والحمدله

**English:**
- (Your servant) kisses the ground and reports [ويهنى, see the note] that a tawqi' has come (to me) in a sorry state
  (bahdala);
- thanks for your kindness: so order for it the siyaqa, the ba and the hamdala.

**Metre:** not scanned. Colloquial verse; the two lines rhyme in -dala (بهدله / الحمدله). The compiler calls them "the
first two verses".

**Note:** a petition in colloquial verse, and a joke on the chancery's own forms. The story in front of it (73v l.1-7)
is that the compiler asked for the renewal of an office, and the decree came back with the address "al-majlis al-sami"
written without the ba, and without "al-hamdu lillah" and the siyaqa. He asks the secretary to have it redone with
them. The terms, as far as I can explain them without checking:

- **yuqabbilu al-arda** "kisses the ground" is the stock opening of a petition (qissa) to a superior in the Mamluk
  chancery, and in that formula it is followed by **wa-yunhi** "and reports (that ...)". The record reads ويهنى
  ("and congratulates"), with the same letters as وينهي in another order; the English follows the formula, and the
  written form is kept in the Arabic (see the suspected errors).
- **tawqi'** here is a decree of appointment issued from the chancery, the document that would renew his office. The
  verse plays on the word, since the tawqi' is also the name of a script (cf. 80r.9).
- **bahdala** is colloquial for "a humiliating mess": the decree came in a form below his rank.
- **al-hamdala** is the opening "al-hamdu lillah". In Mamluk practice, as set out in the chancery manuals (al-Qalqashandi;
  not checked for this case), decrees were graded by their opening, and those that began with the hamdala were of the
  highest rank. Its absence marked a lower grade.
- **siyaqa** is, I take it, the formal run of titles and preamble owed to the recipient's rank; the record gives the
  word and does not explain it. It also sets up the pun on siyaq "death-throes" in 74r.3.
- **al-ba** is one of the small marks of the honorific "al-majlis al-sami". The manuals grade that title by such marks
  (for example al-sami with and without its final ya were two ranks). Which mark "the ba" is, and whether it is a
  preposition or a letter of the title, the record does not say, and I have not checked it.

باني [?] is colloquial bi-anni "that I" (the draft had بان). The cipher has 6 signs for توقيع, the last the ya sign
(record), so it may read توقيعي "my tawqi'". The pair was sent "in haste" to the qadi Nasir al-Din ibn al-Shati [?],
muwaqqi' al-dast al-sharif, the kind of official who drafts such decrees, and he solved both. The compiler's comment
after the answer (74v l.8-11) discusses the words فارسم له and الشكر; the record does not give it in full. Poet: the
compiler.

**Technique note:** 73v l.1-7: the chancery story (above); 73v l.7: "so I wrote for the aforementioned, may God make
his rank great"; 73v l.13, 74r l.1-2: "then I composed something else on the road to the Citadel, enciphered both ...
the qadi Nasir al-Din ibn al-Shati [?], muwaqqi' al-dast al-sharif, in haste, and sent both to him"; 74v l.5: "then he
wrote the first two verses, and they are".

## 74r.3 (cipher 74r l.3-7; plaintext 74v l.2-3)

**Arabic (record):**
ايا بحرا لجود بلا مغيض ويا بدرا تضي بلا محاق / تصدق بالسياقة ان نفسي تناهت في السياق لها التراقي

**English:**
- O sea of generosity that never sinks (bila maghid),
- and O full moon that shines (tudi'u) without waning (bila mihaq)!
- Grant me the siyaqa as alms, for my soul
- has reached, in its death-throes (al-siyaq), the collarbones (al-taraqi).

**Metre:** wafir (the second hemistich and the second verse scan, reading تضي as tudi'u; the first hemistich does not
as read).

**Note:** the second piece of the pair, "composed on the road to the Citadel". It asks again for the siyaqa of 73v.8,
and the point is the pun siyaqa (the chancery item) / siyaq (the death agony). The last words echo Q 75:26 ("when it
reaches the collarbones"), of the soul leaving the body; the soul here is dying for want of the right form of decree.
The sea "without ebb" and the moon "without waning" are stock praise of a patron's constancy. The solver pointed out two
slips in the cipher (74v l.4-5): an alif dropped in al-siyaq, and بلا محاق written without a divider. The record finds
the second (one group of 7) but not the first; the one missing letter is in the group for تصدق (3 signs). The first word
has one dot below (ابا or ايا) and the cipher has the ya sign. Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 74r l.1-2 (with 73v l.13): the second piece, sent with the first; 74v l.1: "... to the house, and he
had written the second, and it is"; 74v l.4-5: "he pointed out that in al-siyaq an alif had dropped, and that bila mihaq
(was written) without a divider, and that it was a slip".

## 75r.1 (cipher 75r l.1-3; plaintext 75r l.5)

**Arabic (record):**
وما انا وحدي قلت ذا الشعر كله ولكن لشعري فيك من نفسه شعر

**English:**
- It was not I alone who spoke all this poetry (shi'r);
- rather, my poetry has, about you, a poetry of its own.

**The master's version (75v l.2, record):** وما انا وحدي قلت ذا الشكر كله ولكن لشكري فيك من نفسه شكر, "It was not I
alone who spoke all this thanks; rather, my thanks for you has a thanks of its own."

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** a well-known verse; I believe it is al-Mutanabbi's, but that is not checked. The praise is so natural that the
poem seems to make itself. Amin al-Din al-Hanafi, qadi of Hama, wrote it for the compiler, who solved it at once. The
compiler then sent it to the master, who solved it and wrote it back with shukr "thanks" in place of shi'r "poetry"
throughout, changing the 'ayn to kaf. The compiler "marvelled at the speed of his solution and at his replacing the
'ayn by kaf" (75v l.3-4). The cipher is the shi'r version: the same sign stands for 'ayn in all three words (record).
So the master's change was a deliberate turn of the verse into thanks to the sender, not a misreading.

**Technique note:** 74v l.12-13: "the qadi Amin al-Din al-Hanafi mentioned before, qadi of Hama today, ... wrote this
verse and I solved it at once"; 75r l.10-11: "Amin al-Din wrote it for the mamluk and I solved it at once, easily";
75v l.3-4 (above).

## 76r.4 (cipher 76r l.4-8; plaintext 76v l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
لنا من نشرها ومن المحيا وقامتها وناظرها السقيم / تارج عنبر وضيا بدر ولين اراكة ولحاظ ريم

**English:**
- We have, from her fragrance, and from her face,
- and her stature, and her languid (literally "sick") eye,
- the spreading scent (ta'arruj) of ambergris, the light of a full moon,
- the suppleness of an arak branch, and the glances of a gazelle.

**Metre:** wafir.

**Note:** a textbook laff wa-nashr ("folding and unfolding"): four things named in the first verse and their four
likenesses given in the same order in the second (fragrance / ambergris, face / moon, stature / arak branch, eye /
gazelle). The compiler says he chose the verses "for their delicacy and the craft in them, the laff wa-nashr and other
things". They are from a qasida by Sharaf al-Din Ibn al-Halawi al-Mawsili (record; the attribution is not checked), whose
opening he quotes on 76r l.11 (not the cipher text). تارج is ta'arruj, written without the hamza; the record's certainty
for it is medium. The compiler solved it quickly.

**Technique note:** 76r l.9-13: "these two verses are from a qasida by Sharaf al-Din Ibn al-Halawi al-Mawsili, which
begins ...; I chose them for their delicacy and the craft in them, the laff wa-nashr and other things; I solved them
quickly and wrote them ...".

## 76v.6 (cipher 76v l.6-8; plaintext 76v l.10)

**Arabic (record):**
ظفرت بخط من ظلوم تعاظمت ظواهره للناظر المتيقظ

**English:**
- I won a letter (khatt) from a cruel one (zalum),
- whose outward signs (zawahir) grew great to the wakeful observer.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** a verse built on the letter za' (ظ), which occurs six times in eight words (ظفرت, ظلوم, تعاظمت, ظواهره, الناظر,
المتيقظ). The za' is a rare letter and a hard one to find in a cipher, which is presumably the point. Zalum "unjust,
cruel" is the stock word for a hard-hearted beloved. Khatt is a piece of writing, a letter; "its outward signs" may also
glance at the visible strokes of the writing, but I am not sure of the point. Amin al-Din wrote it for the compiler in
the majlis of the chief qadi Jamal al-Din al-Hanafi, and he solved it at once. The waw of ظلوم and ظواهره is an asterisk
sign, which fixes the fourth word as ظلوم (record). Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 76v l.3-5: "Amin al-Din and I were in the majlis of our lord the chief qadi Jamal al-Din
al-Hanafi ... and he wrote me this verse"; 76v l.9: "I solved it at once, and it is".

## 77r.3 (cipher 77r l.3-5; plaintext 77r l.7)

**Arabic (record):**
ظفرت بخط من عظيم تعاظمت ظواهره للناظر المتيقظ

**English:**
- I won a letter from a great man ('azim),
- whose outward signs grew great to the wakeful observer.

**Metre:** tawil (as 76v.6).

**Note:** the 76v.6 verse, enciphered by the compiler "in another pen" (a second alphabet) for the master, with ظلوم
"a cruel one" changed to عظيم "a great man". He explains that the change "is no error but the replacement of a word by
a word for fear of misinterpretation" (ta'wil): sent to his patron, "a letter from a cruel one" could be taken as
aimed at him. The new word keeps a za' and the same length, so the counts cannot tell the two versions apart (record).
The master solved it. The middle word of the solver line (77r l.6), وكسه, may be وكتبه (record).

**Technique note:** 76v l.11-13, 77r l.1-2: "then I enciphered it in another pen and changed the word zalum to 'azim,
and wrote it for the aforementioned ... and (let him know) that Amin al-Din wrote that for me and that I solved it in
the presence of the Hanafi chief qadi at once, and that what I meant is no error but the replacement of a word by a word
for fear of misinterpretation; and this is its cipher"; 77r l.6: "he solved it ... and sent it".

## 77r.10 (cipher 77r l.10-13, 77v l.1; plaintext 77v l.5-6)

**Arabic (record):**
حبيبي على الدنيا اذا غبت وحشة فيا قمري قل لي متى انت طالع / سروري ان تبقى بخير وغبطة واني من الدنيا بذلك قانع

**English:**
- My beloved, when you are away the world is a desolation to me;
- so, O my moon, tell me: when will you rise?
- My joy is that you stay well and happy,
- and with that I am content with the world.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** a plain love couplet, used for an easy cipher. The absent friend is a moon that has set. It opens the series
of "ciphers I promised to mention": the qadi Sadr al-Din, son of the Hanafi chief qadi, wrote and sent it. The compiler
solved it at once and sent the solution by the hand of Shaykh Burhan al-Din al-Qudsi al-Hanafi, imam of the malik
al-umara' in Syria. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 77r l.8-9: "and of the ciphers I promised to mention: what the qadi Sadr al-Din, son of the Hanafi
chief qadi, wrote and sent in this cipher"; 77v l.2-4: "I solved it at once because it was easy, wrote it out and sent
it by the hand of ... the shaykh Burhan al-Din al-Qudsi al-Hanafi, imam of the malik al-umara' in Syria today; and it is".

## 77v.9 (cipher 77v l.9-11, 78r l.1-3; plaintext 78r l.5-6)

**Arabic (record):**
تتوق نفسي الى من بالحمى باتت تتلو حديث ليا لبنا التي فاتت / تتيه بالحسن مذ روض الحشى افنات [?] تتلف جناني [?] وكم تفتنها [?] ماتا [?]

**English:**
- My soul longs for her who spent the night at al-Hima,
- reciting the tale of [ليا لبنا: Layla and Lubna?], who are gone (fatat).
- She is proud of her beauty since [روض الحشى افنات?, "the garden of my insides ... "];
- she destroys my heart [?], and how many [تفتنها ماتا?] ...

**Metre:** not scanned. The four half-lines end in -at (باتت, فاتت, افنات, ماتا), the pattern of the mawaliya.

**Note:** written by the qadi Sadr al-Din in the house of the chief qadi's father, before Amin al-Din and Burhan al-Din;
the compiler solved it at once "because it was easy", and the company marvelled at the speed. The verse is built on
words that begin with ta, mostly a doubled ta (تتوق, تتلو, تتيه, تتلف, تفتنها), which makes the commonest sign pair of
the cipher stand out at once (record: the pair 66 = تت). Al-Hima, "the protected pasture", is the stock place of the
beloved's tribe. ليا لبنا is written as one cipher group (record); it may stand for two famous beloveds, Layla and
Lubna, but that is a guess. The second line is written small and crowded on the page, and its last four words are
doubtful. The record notes that the last cipher group ends in the ta-ta pair, so the last word is likely ماتت "(she)
died" rather than ماتا, and that the cipher's word for افنات has one more letter (see the suspected errors). Only the
first line is translated with any confidence. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 77v l.7-8: "when we met at the house of the chief qadi's father, this mutarjim wrote (this) in his
presence and in the presence of Amin al-Din and Burhan al-Din too"; 78r l.4: "I solved it at once because it was easy,
and it is"; 78r l.7: "they marvelled at the speed of the solution".

## 78r.12 (cipher 78r l.12-13, 78v l.1-5; plaintext 78v l.7-10)

**Arabic (record):**
اي بدر تم ثقه جودر حوى خدري دع ذكر ريا زهي سولي / شفا صدري / ضيفي طلع ظبي علي غلو في قدري كم لي معه نوم هم / والضد لا يدري

**English** (the sense is strained by design):
- What a full moon (badr tamm), a trusty one, a fawn (jawdhar), has come into my chamber (khidri)!
- Leave the mention of Rayya; my wish is in bloom,
- the cure of my breast:
- my guest has appeared, a gazelle; my standing has risen high;
- how many a sleep I have had with him, [هم: "they"? "cares"?],
- and the rival does not know.

**Metre:** not scanned. The short lines (شفا صدري, والضد لا يدري) and the rhyme in -dri (خدري, صدري, قدري, يدري) suggest
a strophic, zajal-like form.

**Note:** an alphabet acrostic, "in the order of the letters" ('ala nasaq al-huruf): each word begins with the next
letter of the Arabic alphabet, from alif to ya, with lam-alif in its traditional place before ya:

| letter | word | letter | word | letter | word |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ا | اي | ز | زهي | ق | قدري |
| ب | بدر | س | سولي | ك | كم |
| ت | تم | ش | شفا | ل | لي |
| ث | ثقه | ص | صدري | م | معه |
| ج | جودر | ض | ضيفي | ن | نوم |
| ح | حوى | ط | طلع | ه | هم |
| خ | خدري | ظ | ظبي | و | والضد |
| د | دع | ع | علي | لا | لا |
| ذ | ذكر | غ | غلو | ي | يدري |
| ر | ريا | ف | في | | |

That is 29 words, one for each of the 28 letters and lam-alif. The constraint is why the words are odd: جودر is
jawdhar, the young of the wild cow (a stock image for a beauty's eyes), written without the hamza and with dal; سولي is
su'li "my wish"; Rayya is a stock name for a beloved. The cipher was the master's, sent to Shaykh Ibrahim al-Wazfi [?]
in Tripoli, and Shaykh Shihab al-Din the translator said that he solved it. The cipher has only ر ي for ريا, with no
divider before زهي (record). After the plaintext the compiler adds (78v l.6): "this I wrote in an old pen, with which I
found things in a book", which refers to the alphabet. Poet: the master (as sender; authorship not stated).

**Technique note:** 78r l.9-11: "then the aforementioned master wrote to the shaykh Ibrahim al-Wazfi [?], resident in
Tripoli, ... in the order of the letters, and the shaykh Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim said that he solved it and sent it
...; and it is"; 78v l.6 (above).

## 78v.13 (cipher 78v l.13, 79r l.1-2; plaintext 79r l.3)

**Arabic (record):**
فبت الثم عينيها ومن عجب اني اقبل اسيافا فاسفكن دمي

**English:**
- So I spent the night kissing her eyes, and it is a wonder
- that I kiss swords that [فاسفكن as written; "that shed"] my blood.

**Metre:** basit (the first hemistich scans; the second scans with سفكن, see the suspected errors).

**Note:** the eyes of the beloved as swords that have killed the lover, and he kisses them. فاسفكن is as written; the
sense wants "that shed (safakna)", and the cipher's word has only 4 signs (record), the length of سفكن. The compiler
sent it to the master. The heading (78v l.11-12) is partly doubtful: a Shaykh Ahmad Siba'a [?] "claimed (it) after
solving it", and "he enciphered it for him a second time after a long while, and he solved it a second time". The
words after the block, وهو فديم [?], may be "and it is old" (قديم). Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 78v l.11-12: "and of what I wrote for him, may God extend his shade, ... the shaykh Ahmad Siba'a [?]
after solving it; he enciphered it for him a second time after a long while and he solved it a second time"; 79r l.2:
"and it is ... [?]".

## 79r.5 (cipher 79r l.5-10; plaintext 79r l.11-12)

**Arabic (record):**
وكنت متى ارسلت طرفك رايدا لقلبك يوما اتعبتك المناظر / رايت الذي لا كله انت قادر عليه ولا عن بعضه انت صابر

**English:**
- Whenever you sent out your glance as a scout (ra'id)
- for your heart one day, the sights wearied you:
- you saw that over which, in whole, you have no power,
- and without which, in part, you cannot endure.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** a famous couplet on the eye as the heart's scout, which brings back a beauty the lover can neither win nor do
without. The poet is not named in the record and is not checked. It is introduced as "the one he solved twice",
continuing the story of 78v.13. There is no solver line. The cipher's word for اتعبتك has one letter fewer (record).

**Technique note:** 79r l.4: "and of that, the one he solved twice".

## 79v.1 (cipher 79v l.1-5; plaintext 79v l.6-8)

**Arabic (record):**
اري دهري على ضعفي تعدا وجاوز حد تهذيبي وعدا / وصيرني له غرضا معدا / وايام تعد علي عدا وحظي من رغايبها يفوت

**English:**
- I see my fate has wronged me in my weakness,
- passed the bounds of my good breeding, and run wild;
- it has made me a target set up for it;
- and days are counted against me, count by count,
- while my share of their good things slips away (yafut).

**Metre:** wafir.

**Note:** a stanza of a takhmis, a poem in which the poet adds three hemistichs to each verse of another poet's poem,
rhyming them with its first hemistich, and keeps the original verse as the fourth and fifth. Here the added lines rhyme
in -da (تعدا, عدا, معدا, then عدا of the fourth) and the fifth keeps the original rhyme in -ut (يفوت). The heading
says the takhmis is the compiler's, and names Ishaq al-Sabi, but the words between are doubtful (record). It may mean
that the poem made into a takhmis is by (Abu) Ishaq al-Sabi, or that the takhmis was sent to someone of that name; I
cannot tell from the record. The cipher writes غرضا معدا as one group (record). No solver line.

**Technique note:** 79r l.13: "and of that, from a takhmis ... (of) my verses [?] to Ishaq al-Sabi" (words before الى
doubtful).

كمالي بما ينقص يشين [?] وعلمي كله در ثمين / وعندي سراتها [?] ودي مبين / مكاني من خصيصه مكين وحالي من خصاصتها تفوت [?]

**English:**

## 80r.9 (cipher 80r l.9-13; plaintext 80v l.3-4)

**Arabic (record):**
غبار ذنوبي في الرقاع محقق بنسخ كرام كاتبين عدول / وتوقيع ريحاني رجاي لعفو من ناديك ثلث الليل عند نزول

**English:**
- The dust (ghubar) of my sins is in the scrolls (riqa'), made certain (muhaqqaq)
- by the copying (naskh) of noble scribes (kiram katibin), just witnesses;
- and the signature (tawqi') of my sweet-scented (rayhani) hope is for pardon
- from Him who calls to you in the third (thuluth) of the night, when He descends.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** the "seven pens" verse. It names seven scripts of Arabic calligraphy in order, each used in a second sense:
**ghubar** (the tiny "dust" script; here the dust of sins), **riqa'** (the script for notes; here the record scrolls),
**muhaqqaq** ("verified"), **naskh** ("copying"), **tawqi'** (the script of signatures and decrees; "signature"),
**rayhani** ("of sweet basil"), and **thuluth** ("a third"). The record lists the same seven. The religious sense runs
on two references: the "noble scribes" are the recording angels of Q 82:11 (kiraman katibin), who write down a man's
deeds, and the last hemistich alludes to the hadith of God's descent to the nearest heaven in the last third of the
night, when He calls "who asks My forgiveness?". The sinner's record is written and witnessed, but his hope is signed
for pardon. The heading says the verse is by Shaykh Shams al-Din, khatib of al-Salihiyya in Damascus, who had
enciphered it for the compiler "in another pen, alluding in it to seven pens". The compiler sent it on to the master,
who solved it quickly. The solver line cites a book: "its rendering is recorded in the *Kitab al-Ajwiba al-Shamiyya*"
(كتاب الاجوبة الشامية, "the Book of Syrian Answers"), cited again on 80v l.7. The record does not identify the book; the
name suggests a collection of answers exchanged with Damascus, possibly the compiler's own, but that is not checked.
محقق is written without clear dots (record). In the stretch ريحاني ... ثلث the cipher has two letters and three
dividers fewer than the page (record), so the cipher's wording there may differ. Earlier waves read this as "reference
only"; the verse is in fact written out after the reference.

**Technique note:** 80r l.6-8: "and of what I wrote for him ...: what the shaykh Shams al-Din, khatib of al-Salihiyya in
Damascus the well-guarded, had enciphered for me of his own verse, in another pen, alluding in it to seven pens"; 80v
l.1-2: "he solved it quickly and sent it ... and its rendering is recorded in the Kitab al-Ajwiba al-Shamiyya; and it is".

## 80v.9 (cipher 80v l.9-13; plaintext 81r l.2-3)

**Arabic (record):**
نسخ ريحان عارضيك نسيب بحواشي رقاع حسنك ملحق / ثلث عمر العذول فيك يقضى في غبار فليت وصلي محقق

**English:**
- The copy (naskh) of the sweet basil (rayhan) of your cheeks is a love-prelude (nasib),

answer". The master solved it "in the quickest time". The cipher has one sign more than the page in ريحان, نسيب and في
(record).

**Technique note:** 80v l.5-8: "then I wrote for him what the shaykh Nasir al-Din [ibn?] Sallar in Damascus enciphered for
me, when he saw the solution of the above and its answer, with a qasida recorded in the Ajwiba al-Shamiyya and its
answer; he alluded in it to fifteen kinds of writing in four verses, of which"; 81r l.1: "he solved it in the quickest
time and sent it, and it is".

## 81r.5 (cipher 81r l.5-10; plaintext 81r l.12-13)

ان تكن قاتلي بطومار هجر وبشعر العذار قلبي معلق / فلقد باح سلوتي عنك توقيع غدا مزوجا وذلك اوثق

**English:**
- If you are killing me with a scroll (tumar) of desertion,

- then a signature (tawqi') has disclosed my consolation for you,

**Metre:** khafif (the first verse scans).

**Note:** the second half of Nasir al-Din Sallar's four verses, which the compiler enciphered with a changed alphabet
("so I wrote its completion and changed the letters"). Script names: **tumar** (the large chancery script; here a long
scroll), **mu'allaq** ("hanging", also a script name), **tawqi'**, and perhaps **mazuj / muzawwaj** and others I cannot
identify; the fifteen are not all found in my reading of the four verses. بطومار is read from بطوما + ر in line with the

consolation for you" suggests the decree of his release from love, and "more binding" fits a legal document, but the

**Technique note:** 81r l.4: "so I wrote its completion and changed the letters"; 81r l.11: "he solved it quickly [?] and

## 81v.6 (cipher 81v l.6-10; plaintext 82r l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
ضرب في الضريب ام اغريض وابتسام ام در ثغر وميض / ضعفت منتي وامرض جسمي ودواي طرف ضعيف مريض

**English:**
- Is it white honey (darab) in frost (darib), or a palm-spathe (ighrid),
- and a smile, or the pearls of a mouth, flashing?
- My strength has failed and my body is made ill,
- and my cure is a weak and sickly glance.

**Metre:** khafif (the second verse scans).

**Note:** two verses by Ibn al-Rumi (record; not checked), chosen by the compiler as "strange in their words, with many

frost, the white spathe, or pearls. The second turns to the lover's sickness, cured only by the beloved's languid

authors of the treatises he named earlier would have failed at it. The cipher's word for وامرض has one letter fewer

**Technique note:** 81v l.1-5: "when I saw he had become practised in this matter, and nothing hard was hard for him,
I chose for him two verses of Ibn al-Rumi, strange in their words, with many dads [?] and few alifs [?], abstruse, and I
thought they would be hard to solve and might take him days ...; and their cipher"; 81v l.11-13: "he solved it quickly.
O wonder! I swear by God that had this been written for a group [?] of the authors of treatises mentioned before, they
would have failed at it ...".

## 82r.9 (cipher 82r l.9-13, 82v l.1; plaintext 82v l.3-4)

**Arabic (record):**
يا سيدي فدا حالون [?] على غزة وما حصل من وصولي لا ولا رزه / نحتاج من جاهك العالي الى همة عسى يجي ما يوصلني الى غزة

**English:**
- My lord, [فدا حالون?] on Gaza, and nothing has come of my receipt (wusul), no, not [رزه?] a scrap;
- we need from your high standing some effort, so that perhaps there may come what gets me (paid) at Gaza.

## 83r.1 (cipher 83r l.1-4; plaintext 83r l.6-7)

**Arabic (record):**
فكم كم وكم كم [كم] وكم كم وكم وكم وكم كم وكم يا لليل هذا التناسا

**English:**
- And how many, how many, and how many, how many, [how many,] and how many, how many,
- and how many, and how many, and how many, how many, and how many:
- O night! this feigned forgetting (al-tanasi)!

**Metre:** not scanned.

**Note:** a verse made almost entirely of kam "how many, how often", the question of the count: how many nights, how
many promises, how many times, and still the other "pretends to forget" (tanasi, the sixth form, is feigning to forget,
not forgetting). The cipher form has التناسيا with the ya (record). The point for the cipherer is the repetition: the
same three-letter unit (k, m, and the divider letter) comes back eleven times, with a waw before it where the text has
وكم. The other device is that the dividers between words are not the usual stroke with three dots. Amin al-Din "made
its dividers a letter of the same kind as the signs" (82v l.13), and in the block a round ha-like sign with a tail
stands after every word (record), so the dividers look like one more letter of the text. The cipher has eleven kam-words
(the page's sequence without the small كم written above the fourth word) and runs يا لليل together (record). The
heading (82v l.12) says the verse was written "in praise of al-Kamal [?]" and has the doubtful word اضمارا (record).
The compiler solved it "after great difficulty". Poet: Amin al-Din (as sender; the record does not say whose the verse is).

**Technique note:** 82v l.12-13: "and of what he also wrote for me, in praise of [?] al-Kamal, the qadi Amin al-Din
mentioned, ... and he made its dividers a letter of the same kind as the signs"; 83r l.5: "I solved it after great
difficulty, and it is".

## 83r.13 (cipher 83r l.13, 83v l.1-7; plaintext 83v l.8-10)

**Arabic (record):**
يا ليتني اشبه المزي امين الدين لما لحظته بعينك بالرضا واللين / قد نال ما ساله في غاية التمكين الى اليمن فلك قوي عزمي واما الصين

**English:**
"Would that I resembled al-Mizzi Amin al-Din, when you looked on him with your eye with favour and gentleness: he
obtained what he asked in the fullest measure. To Yemen [?] then, for your sake my resolve is strong, or else to China!"

**Metre:** not scanned. Rhymed prose (saj') in -in (الدين, اللين, التمكين, الصين), as the record describes it; it may
be colloquial verse.

**Note:** a petition in cipher, in a familiar, teasing tone (inbisat). Amin al-Din had written a petition and the
master al-'Ala'i granted what he asked. The compiler then asked the like and received only part of it. The master
"was excused", but the compiler did not accept the excuse until later, and wrote this "presuming on (his) servitude,
on his kindness and noble character". The end I do not understand well. "To Yemen ... or China" reads like a threat to go
to the ends of the earth; al-yaman (Yemen) may also play on al-yumn "good fortune", and فلك may be fa-laka "then for
you" or falak "the sphere". The marginal واللين is needed for the count (record). No solver line.

**Technique note:** 83r l.8-12: "it happened that Amin al-Din wrote a petition about some matter, and the master
al-'Ala'i ... granted what he asked; after that the mamluk asked the like and was granted part of the request, and he
was excused, and the mamluk did not accept the excuse until later; so I wrote to him in a familiar tone, presuming on
servitude, on his kindness and noble character".

## 83v.12 (cipher 83v l.12-13, 84r l.1-4; plaintext 84r l.6-7)

**Arabic (record):**
وكنت اظن اني بانتماي اليك انال ملا الراحتين / وما ذنبي سوى حبي رضاكم فقلنا [?] الياس احدى [?] الراحتين

**English:**
- I used to think that by belonging to you

- What is my sin, except that I love your favour?

## Suspected Arabic errors

None of these readings has been changed above; each is a suspicion for the next look at the master.

1. **78v.13** فاسفكن: the cipher's word has 4 signs (record), the second hemistich of the basit scans with سفكن
   (safakna, "which shed") and not with فاسفكن, and the sense wants سفكن. Strong.
2. **73v.8** ويهنى: the petition formula is يقبل الارض وينهي ("kisses the ground and reports"). ويهنى has the same
   letters with ha and nun in the other order; the record reads a hard-to-see ya tooth and counts 5 signs. Moderate.
3. **77v.9** ماتا: the last cipher group ends in the ta-ta pair (record), so ماتت. افنات: the cipher's word has 6 signs
   against 5 (record). The whole second line is written small and crowded. Moderate.
4. **82v.7 / 83r.1** اضمارا: the record reads it once as the last word of the verse of 82v.7 (written above the start of
   l.12) and once in the heading of 83r.1 (82v l.12). Check whether there are two words or one word assigned twice; the
   count of 82v.7 needs a 6-letter word. Moderate (to check).
5. **74r.3** first hemistich (ايا بحرا لجود بلا مغيض): the rest of the couplet scans as wafir and this hemistich does
   not as read. The solver's note that an alif dropped in al-siyaq is not seen in the counts; the short group is تصدق
   (3 signs). Weak to moderate.
6. **83r.1** التناسا: the cipher has التناسيا (record); the word is al-tanasi. Orthography only. Weak.
7. **79v.10** يشين [?], سراتها [?], تفوت [?]: doubtful words; the first hemistich does not scan as read and its cipher
   groups fall differently (record). The takhmis rhyme supports تفوت. Weak.
8. **73r.5** في: 3 cipher signs (وفي or ففي?), and the opening of the tawil does not scan cleanly as read; مو رياض is
   under a paper crack. Weak.
9. **82r.9** فدا حالون [?] (perhaps فذا حالنا) and رزه [?]. Weak.
10. **83v.12** فقلنا [?]: the cipher has فقلت (record); both scan. احدى is written with a joined first stroke. Weak.
11. **84r.12** ويمنك [?] / وبينك. Open.
12. **81r.5** مزوجا: as written; unexplained. بطومار is the record's reading against the draft's بطول ما. Open.
13. **Divergences in the cipher, not in the written text** (record): 73v.8 توقيع 6 signs (توقيعي?); 77v.9 ليا لبنا one
    group; 78r.12 ريا only ر ي; 79r.5 اتعبتك 5 signs; 79v.1 ضعفي 3, وحظي 5; 80r.9 الرقاع محقق one group and two letters
    fewer in ريحاني ... ثلث; 80v.9 ريحان, نسيب, في one sign longer; 81v.6 وامرض 4 signs; 82v.7 رضاكم 7 signs; 83r.13
    فلك 2 signs; 83v.12 مل, رضاكم 4, حدى; 84r.12 وجودك 4, قلبي 5, جميل 3.
