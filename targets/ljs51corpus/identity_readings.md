# LJS 51: the identity passages re-read on the master TIFFs

Re-reading, 28 Nov 2026, of the passages that `identity_ibn_durayhim.md` builds on. Every passage was read on the Penn
master TIFF (`output/ljs51/master/0084_NNNN.tif`, image = 6 + 2*(folio-1), +1 for a verso) at 3x, with 4x-6x crops of
the doubtful words. The crops are in `output/ljs51/crops/idn/` (named `f<folio>_l<lines>.png`; page overviews `p<image>.png`).

Transcription conventions: letters and dots as written, no vowels added; `[?]` marks a doubtful reading; `/` is a line
end; `(margin)` marks a word written in the margin. Dots are often missing in this hand: where I give a dotted form
the dots are on the page unless noted.

Image numbers in the brief were off by one or two in places. The formula gives: 38v = 81, 39r = 82, 39v = 83,
45v = 95, 46r = 96, 46v = 97, 47r = 98, 47v = 99, 49r = 102, 49v = 103, 50r = 104, 62r = 128, 80r = 164, 80v = 165,
102v = 209. Each of these was checked against the page: the text on it is the text the chain records cite.

## 1. f. 102v l.6-10 (image 209): "my book Miftah al-kunuz"

    l.6  وكتب لي تاج الدين تاج الدين ناظر قطيا كان مترجما على
    l.7  غير هذه القاعدة بل نوع من انواع المترجم المذكور
    l.8  في كتابي مفتاح الكنوز في حل المرموز من باب
    l.9  المقلوب كتبه بحضوري ظانا ان هذا النوع لا اعرفه
    l.10 ولا يجي ذهني اليه بل اتوهم انه من باب الابدال وهو

"Taj al-Din Taj al-Din, the nazir of Qatya, wrote me a cipher not on this rule but in one of the kinds of cipher
set out in my book *Miftah al-kunuz fi hall al-marmuz*, from the chapter of al-maqlub. He wrote it in my presence,
thinking that I did not know this kind and that my mind would not reach it, and that I would take it for the
chapter of al-ibdal. It is:"

- **كتابي**: certain. The ya of the suffix has its two dots below (4x crop `f102v_l8_R.png`).
- **حل**, not ايضاح: certain. حل is written plainly between في and المرموز. The words are مفتاح الكنوز (كنوز dotted, with
  a damma) في حل المرموز.
- The name: تاج الدين is written twice with no ابن between. The second الدين is cramped and runs into ناظر. It could
  be a slip for "Taj al-Din [son of] Taj al-Din".
- **Voice**: the compiler's own narrative, in the first person throughout (لي, كتابي, بحضوري, اعرفه, ذهني, اتوهم).
  It is not a quoted letter. l.13 continues in the same voice: فحللتها في الحال فتعجب وهو سهل ("I solved it at once,
  and he was astonished; it is easy").

**Bearing on the identification: strong support.** The compiler calls the *Miftah al-kunuz fi hall al-marmuz* his
own book, and it has a chapter of al-maqlub and a chapter of al-ibdal.

## 2. ff. 49v l.9-13 and 50r l.1-13 (images 103, 104): Ibn Dunaynir or Ibn Durayhim

49v l.1 is فكتبت ("so I wrote"): the compiler's own cipher (49v l.2-8). Then:

    49v l.9  فحل ذلك سريعا وكتبه وهو
    49v l.10 تحققت اني عند [حبرك؟] قطن[ة؟] وكنت اراني واحدا في
    49v l.11 المترجم                                   (written small under l.10)
    49v l.12 اذا عرض المرموز فالحل جوهر[ا؟] من [?] ابن دنينير وان ذكرهم [?]
    49v l.13 ثم كتب هذه لفظة او لو كانت فكيف كان اصلح
    49v l.14 وهنا طريق العرض لا الاعتراض فاجابه المملوك

    50r l.1  [ب]شكره على احسانه وتفضله وادبه وعرفه ان
    50r l.2  الذي رسم به من ان يكون من ابن دنينير وكيف
    50r l.3  ابن دريهم احسن من وجهين احدهما التنازل
    50r l.4  وهو غاية في البلاغة والثاني لنخلص [ليخلص؟] من
    50r l.5  التاسيس فانه عيب في الشعر ان يكون في بيت
    50r l.6  دون بيت ولكني قصدت ثلثة اشيا الاول ان لقب
    50r l.7  الجد الاعلى الدريهم مصغرا وانما حذفت منه الالف
    50r l.8  واللام لمعنيين لئلا تكثر في الوضع فيسهل حله
    50r l.9  ولمناسبة دنينير فانه بغير الف ولام والثاني لمناسبة
    50r l.10 دنينير فانه مصغر ايضا وهو حلو [سمعه؟] والثالث
    50r l.11 انه لو كان [يكبر؟] الحسن [بصغر؟] من [نديه؟] فكيف وهو مصغر
    50r l.12 صورة ومعنى ثم انقطعت المكاتبة بيننا مدة فقصد[?]
    50r l.13 و[جدد؟] وكتب هذه

English: "He solved it quickly and wrote it out, and it is: 'I have found that beside your [ink?] I am cotton
[or: a mere wit?], though I used to think myself unique in cipher. / When the enciphered is presented, the solution
is a jewel [from?] Ibn Dunaynir, even if they mention them [?].' Then he wrote: 'This is a word; or if it were [the
other], how much better it would be' (and this by way of suggestion, not objection). The mamluk answered him,
thanking him for his kindness, favour and courtesy, and told him that what he had prescribed, that it should be
'min Ibn Dunaynir', [was good], and how 'Ibn Durayhim' is better on two counts: the first, the self-abasement
(*tanazul*), which is the height of eloquence; the second, to be free of the *ta'sis*, for it is a fault in verse to
have it in one verse and not in another. **But I intended three things. The first: the laqab of the remote ancestor
(*al-jadd al-a'la*) is al-Durayhim, in the diminutive. I dropped the alif and lam from it for two reasons: so that
it would not add to the enciphered text and make it easier to solve, and to match Dunaynir, which has no alif and
lam.** The second: to match Dunaynir, since it is also a diminutive, and it is sweet to the ear. The third: that if
beauty [grows less?] when it is small, how much more when it is diminutive in form and meaning. Then the
correspondence between us broke off for a time; then he [wrote again?] and wrote this:"

- **First person**: yes. فكتبت (49v l.1), "the mamluk answered him" (the compiler's usual self-reference to the
  patron, 49v l.14), then ولكني قصدت ("but I intended", 50r l.6) and حذفت ("I dropped", 50r l.7).
- **"The verse is mine"**: no phrase such as من نظمي. The verse is the compiler's own cipher (فكتبت), and he explains
  his intentions in composing it (قصدت), so it is his verse in substance.
- **New reading, not in the chain records**: 50r l.6-7 «ان لقب الجد الاعلى الدريهم مصغرا». The compiler says that
  al-Durayhim is the laqab of "the remote ancestor" and that he dropped the article to fit the cipher and to match
  Dunaynir. That is, the name in his verse is a family laqab, and he is speaking of himself. No other passage in the
  book comes this close to the compiler naming himself.
- 49v l.12: the end of the line reads وان ذكرهم [?], as in the chain record, not وابن دريهم. On the 6x crop there is
  one tooth (with the nun's dot) between the alif and the final bowl, not the two that ابن needs. The word before
  ابن دنينير may be من, which would make the line the correspondent's proposed wording «من ابن دنينير» (50r l.2).
  That would mean the verse as solved and written out on 49v is his amended form. The original final words of the
  cipher (49v l.2-8) were not re-checked here.

**Bearing on the identification: strong support, stronger than the record suggested.** The compiler defends "Ibn
Durayhim" in his own verse and gives al-Durayhim as the laqab of the remote ancestor.

## 3. f. 62r l.1-9 (image 128): the patron's cipher after his brother's death

    l.1 الفضيلة فسبحان الوهاب وانا معترف والله اني لو كتب لي
    l.2 مثل هذا لعجزت عنه ودليله انه كتب لي هذا البيت الموعود به
    l.3-5 [cipher]
    l.6 وذلك بعد وفاة اخيه الامير صلاح الدين تغمده الله برحمته
    l.7 فبقي عندي عدة ايام لم ينحل ثم ذكر ان فيه كلمة من كلمتين
    l.8 كتبها على اصلاح مرسوم خط المصحف [الساو؟] فاكد ذلك
    l.9 للشيخ شهاب الدين المترجم وهو كان لي فقلت يكون مثل يا ايها

"[...] the merit. Glory to the Giver! And I confess, by God, that had such a thing been written to me I would have
failed at it. The proof: he wrote me this promised verse [cipher]. That was after the death of his brother the
emir Salah al-Din, God cover him with His mercy. It stayed with me several days unsolved. Then he said that it
contained one word made of two, written after the convention of the orthography of the Mushaf [...]; he confirmed
that to Shaykh Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim, who was with me [?]. I said: then it is like يا ايها [...]"

- l.6 «بعد وفاة اخيه الامير صلاح الدين تغمده الله برحمته»: certain.
- اخيه refers to the patron ("he wrote me this verse"). The compiler is the recipient, and the cipher stays "with me"
  (عندي).
- l.9 places Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim at the scene. The words وهو كان لي are uncertain.

**Bearing: indirect support.** The passage dates the piece after the death of the patron's brother, the emir Salah
al-Din. With Safadi's Musa b. Yahya Ibn Fadl Allah (d. Safar 760), that makes the patron 'Ala' al-Din Ibn Fadl
Allah and puts the passage in Ibn al-Durayhim's last Cairo years. The page itself does not name the patron.

## 4. ff. 39r-39v (images 82, 83): "Ibn Fadl Allah"

    39r l.9  وكتب بدر الدين المذكور لغيره
    39r l.10-13, 39v l.1-5 [cipher]
    39v l.6  فحللتها من راس القلم لسهولتها وهي
    39v l.7  جذبت دواعي النفس عن طلب المنى وقلت لها كفي
    39v l.8  عن الطلب المر
    39v l.9  فان ابن فضل الله صار بكفه مدار حيا[ط above] بالرزق
    39v l.10 دايبه تجري
    39v l.11 وكتب لي انسان شريف من اهل البلاد

"The aforementioned Badr al-Din [al-Jawlani] wrote for someone else [cipher]. I solved it straight from the pen,
for it was easy, and it is: 'I drew the soul's urges back from seeking its wishes, and said to it: enough of bitter
seeking, / for Ibn Fadl Allah has become, in his hand, the pivot of rain [bounty] with livelihood, flowing ever.' And
a sharif, one of the people of the country, wrote to me: [...]"

- فان ابن فضل الله: certain (ابن with its dot, فضل dotted). It is a name, not بفضل الله.
- Context: al-Jawlani wrote this piece for another person (لغيره), not for the compiler. The compiler solved it.
  The verse praises Ibn Fadl Allah as the source of livelihood.

**Bearing: weak, circumstantial.** It shows the Ibn Fadl Allah house as the patron figure in this circle of
correspondents. It does not say which Ibn Fadl Allah, and it does not concern the compiler's identity directly.

## 5. f. 80r l.6-8 and f. 80v l.1-2, 5-8 (images 164, 165): the *Kitab al-Ajwiba al-Shamiyya*

    80r l.6 ومما كتبته له اسبغ الله ظلاله ما كان ترجم لي من نظمه الشيخ
    80r l.7 شمس الدين خطيب الصالحية بدمشق المحروسة بقلم اخر
    80r l.8 يوري فيها بسبعة اقلام
    80v l.1 فحلها سريعا وارسلها ولها ولما [ابدها/بعدها؟] اجوبة وترجمته
    80v l.2 مذكورة في كتاب الاجوبة الشامية وهي
    80v l.5 ثم كتبت له ما ترجم لي الشيخ ناصر الدين [بن؟] سلار بدمشق ايضا
    80v l.6 لما راى حل هذا المذكور وجوابه مع قصيدة مذكورة في
    80v l.7 الاجوبة الشامية وجوابها ووري فيه بخمسة عشر
    80v l.8 نوعا من الكتابة في اربعة ابيات منها

"Among what I wrote for him (God make his shade ample): what Shaykh Shams al-Din, khatib of al-Salihiyya in Damascus
the well-guarded, had enciphered for me of his own verse in another pen, alluding in it to seven scripts. He [the
patron] solved it quickly and sent it back. It, and what [follows?] it, have answers, and its rendering is recorded in
the *Kitab al-Ajwiba al-Shamiyya*. It is: [...] Then I wrote for him what Shaykh Nasir al-Din [ibn?] Sallar had
enciphered for me, also in Damascus, when he saw the solution of the piece above and its answer, with a qasida that
is recorded in the *Ajwiba al-Shamiyya*, and its answer. In it he alludes to fifteen kinds of writing in four
verses, of which: [...]"

- **Whose book**: the text says كتاب الاجوبة الشامية. There is no كتابي and no possessive. The pieces in it are
  ciphers and qasidas sent "to me" in Damascus, together with "its answer". The answers are presumably the
  compiler's, but the text does not say so.

**Bearing: moderate.** A book called *al-Ajwiba al-Shamiyya* that records Damascus exchanges with the compiler matches
the *al-Ajwiba al-Shamiyya* on Safadi's list of Ibn al-Durayhim's works. The compiler does not call it his own here.

## 6. ff. 45v l.10-12 and 46r l.7-10 (images 95, 96): the vizierate of al-Sham

    45v l.10 وكتبت له بعد ان طلبت لاباشر وزارة الشام فابيت
    45v l.11 الا ان تبطل المكوس واستعفيت ودخلت على احسانه
    45v l.12 الى ان سد الامر بين يدي المواقف الشريفة خلد الله سلطانها
    45v l.13-46r l.6 [cipher]
    46r l.7  فحل ذلك وارسله وهو
    46r l.8  كم حاز مولاي باعفاي من وزارة الشام من الاجر   (final letter written like د)
    46r l.9  اعظم بها لكن مشقاتها مشتقة حقا من الوزر
    46r l.10 وكتبت له ايضا مضمنا البيت الثاني

"And I wrote to him after I had been called on (*tulibtu*) to take up the vizierate of al-Sham. I refused, except
on condition that the *mukus* be abolished, and I asked to be excused and threw myself on his kindness, until the
matter was closed before the noble presence (God make its sultanate eternal). [cipher] He solved it and sent it
back, and it is: 'How much reward my lord has earned by having me excused from the vizierate of al-Sham! / How great
a thing it is; but its hardships are truly derived from *wizr* (sin).' And I also wrote to him, working in
(*tadmin*) the second verse: [...]"

- **Who asks whom**: the compiler was called on (passive طلبت, as in the 46v verse لما طلبت لانال وزارة شام). He
  refused and asked to be excused. He appealed to the patron's kindness (ودخلت على احسانه), and the release was
  settled "before the noble presence", that is, the sultan. The verse thanks "my lord" (the patron) for obtaining
  his release.
- **Correction to the record**: l.10-11 read فابيت / الا ان تبطل المكوس, "I refused unless the mukus were
  abolished". The record and the identity note have "now the mukus are abolished" (الان). There is no "now". The
  abolition of the mukus was his condition for accepting.

**Bearing: neutral to weakly for.** The compiler is a man the court thought fit for the finance vizierate of Damascus,
and he refused it on a fiscal-reform condition. Safadi does not report this. It is compatible with Ibn
al-Durayhim's financial posts in Damascus and with Safadi's remark that he never rose in office (he declined).

## 7. f. 38v l.8-9 and f. 39r l.6-8 (images 81, 82): the verse naming Taj al-Din Ibn al-Durayhim

    38v l.8 بفهمه وكتب لي الشيخ بدر الدين الجولاني المذكور
    38v l.9 من نظمه
    38v l.10-13, 39r l.1-5 [cipher]
    39r l.6 فحللته في الحال من راس القلم لسهولته وهي
    39r l.7 ابا سايلي عن سيد وابن سيد ومن قد حباه الله حل المترجم
    39r l.8 ومن قد كساه الله حسنا وبهجة هو الحبر تاج الدين نجل الد / ريهم (margin, ريهم undotted)

"[...] by his understanding. And the aforementioned Shaykh Badr al-Din al-Jawlani wrote me [a cipher] of his own
verse [cipher]. I solved it at once, straight from the pen, for its ease, and it is: '[O] you who ask me about a
lord and son of a lord, and one whom God has granted the solving of the enciphered, / and whom God has clothed in
beauty and splendour: he is the learned Taj al-Din, scion of al-Durayhim.'"

- **Sender and recipient**: al-Jawlani, of his own verse (من نظمه), to the compiler (وكتب لي). The compiler solved
  it.
- The last word is split: الد at the line end, ريهم in the left margin (4x crop `f39r_l8_margin.png`).

**Bearing: moderate support.** A cipher sent to the compiler that praises "Taj al-Din, scion of al-Durayhim" as the
man God gave the solving of ciphers is most naturally a compliment to its recipient. It matches 50r, where the
compiler gives al-Durayhim as his ancestor's laqab.

    l.4  [cipher] فلم تنحل الا بعد تعب كثير
    l.5  لصعوبتها وفيها [شبح/شيخ؟] على تلك [اللغة/اللغز؟] ومما

"When I looked at my state compared with his in solving, though I think myself skilled in it **and have written

Ibn al-Durayhim.

## 9. Other places where the compiler might name himself

Searched in `chains_all.json` (technique notes, plaintexts and notes) for كمال, تاج, الدريهم, دريهم, علي بن محمد,
and also المملوك, مفتاح, كتابي, تصانيف, مصنف, الاجوبة:

- **No self-naming** by kunya, ism or nasab (علي بن محمد, تاج الدين as the compiler) anywhere in the records. The
  كمال hits (12r.8, 79v.10, 103v.2) are the word kamal in verses. Kamal al-Din b. al-qadi Jamal al-Din (60r.5) and
  al-Kamal al-qadi Amin al-Din (83r.1) are other people. The other تاج الدين hits are other people: Taj al-Din Ibn
  al-Malih (100r, 100v, 101r), the nazir of Qatya (102v), Taj al-Din Ibn 'Ata' (86r).
- The only places that carry the name al-Durayhim are 39r l.8 (al-Jawlani's verse to the compiler, item 7) and
  49v-50r (the compiler's own verse and argument, item 2).
- **المفتاح** (65v l.11-12, image 135, checked on the master): «فكتبتها وكتبت تحتها ببيتين قديمين مشهورين من ابيات /
  المفتاح وارسلت ذلك» ("I wrote it out and wrote under it two old, well-known verses from the verses of al-Miftah,
  and sent that"). It is ambiguous: this could be his own *Miftah al-kunuz*, if that book quotes verse examples, or
  another *Miftah*. The same pair is cited as في المفتاح on 87r-87v (not re-checked on the image here). It is
  neutral, but worth checking against the edited *Miftah al-kunuz* (Mrayati et al. vol. 3) for the pair
  كم عاقل عاقل اعيت مذاهبه.
- المملوك: the compiler's self-reference to the patron (49v l.14, 75r, 83r, 90v, 93r): it is chancery humility, not
  a name.

## 10. Codicology: why 46v.11 continues on 13r

### What the pages show

- **46v → 13r.** 46v l.10 has وكتبت في المعنى, then three cipher lines (l.11-13) at the foot of the page, with no
  solution. 13r opens with no heading: four cipher lines in the same alphabet (K, triangle, knot, box-hooks, 8), then
  the plaintext «قلت لنفسي حين مالت الى وزارة الشام وفعل / الاثام / اين الحجى اين التقى تقنعني بقولهم هذا اوزير الشام»
  and the compiler's grammatical note (l.8-9). The text is split inside a word (ا|لاثام). The vizierate theme matches
  45v-46v. On 13r l.9 the next item (وكتب للمخدوم المشار اليه ...) starts in mid-line, so 13r was written straight on.
- **13v → 14r** is continuous: 13v.10 runs on to 14r l.1-3, with its solution and plaintext on 14r l.4-6.
- **12v → 13r is broken.** 12v ends at the foot with the second of two cipher verses, "each in a different pen"
  (12v.8, 12v.11). They were brought by Shihab al-Din, and the text says فحله ولم يكتب عليه. 13r does not continue
  them: different alphabet, and no heading.
- **46v → 47r is broken.** 47r opens with no heading, with a cipher (47r l.1-11) whose plaintext is only verses 6-8 of
  an eight-verse riddle. The compiler writes the whole riddle out after فحللته سريعا لسهولته فوجدته (47r l.12-47v
  l.7). The cipher of verses 1-5 is missing from 46v-47r.
- **Catchwords**: there are none at the foot of 12v, 13v, 46v or 47v (crops `bot29.png`, `bot31.png`, `bot97.png`,
  `bot99.png`). The scribe does not use catchwords on these leaves, so they cannot help. 46r has العاشر ("the tenth")
  at the top outer corner in another hand (`f46r_top.png`). This is presumably a quire signature, but it does not fit
  Penn's collation (see below). Small numerals at the lower inner corners of some versos (12v, 102v) were not
  interpreted.

### The missing riddle cipher is on ff. 20r-20v

20r l.7 ends with the heading وثالث المترجمات ("and the third of the enciphered pieces"). It is followed by 19 cipher
lines (20r l.8-13, 20v l.1-13) in the alphabet of 47r: X-on-legs, the 5-loop, the 3, the 4-ayn, the triangle and the
hooked 7. At the foot of 20v, below l.13, stands a lone divider, and 47r's lines open with a divider. I read 20r l.8
(crop `f20r_l8.png`) with the 47r key (`keys_eye/47r.1.json`: 4-ayn و, 3 ل, X م, 5-loop ا, triangle ر, hooked-7 ب):

    20r l.8 groups:  [?]-X-5  |  4-3-[?]-I  |  triangle-7-[loop]  |  [?] ...
    with the 47r key:  ف م ا  |  و ل ي د   |  ر ب ا             |  ف[ي]

The riddle begins «فما وليد ربا في شهر مولده». The first and fourth groups open with the same sign, so that sign is
ف. Only one line was read sign by sign, so this is a strong pointer, not a full decipherment. Lengths agree: 20r
l.8-20v l.13 hold about 228 signs (the record's per-line counts), against about 208 letters in riddle verses 1-5.

This makes chain 20r.8 the first part of the 47r riddle cipher, not an encipherment of the rhymed prose on 21r. Its
record ran 20r-20v-21r l.1-5 on length alone. The `keys_fork` test of it against the 21r prose came out at chance
(letter fit 0.27). Also, 21r l.1-5 are in a different, numeral-like sign set, as that record noted.

### Conclusion: quire 3 is displaced

Penn's collation, 1² 2¹⁰ 3⁸ 4⁸ 5¹⁰ 6⁴ 7⁶ 8-12¹⁰ 13⁸ (106 leaves, matching images 6-217), gives:
q1 ff.1-2, q2 ff.3-12, **q3 ff.13-20**, q4 ff.21-28, q5 ff.29-38, q6 ff.39-42, q7 ff.43-48, q8 ff.49-58, and so on.

All the joins are explained if **the whole of quire 3 (ff.13-20), a regular quaternion, originally stood between
f.46 and f.47**:

- 46v → 13r: the vizierate cipher split inside ا|لاثام (proven by the text).
- 13r-20v read in order within the quire (13v → 14r continuous).
- 20v → 47r: the riddle cipher, verses 1-5 on 20r-20v and verses 6-8 on 47r, with the solution and the full riddle on
  47r-47v. This explains why 47r opens with no heading.
- The series then reads in order: "the third of the enciphered pieces" (20r l.7) is followed two leaves later by
  "and the fourth of them" (48r l.7, the block that enciphers the 21r rhymed prose; `solve_48r/REPORT.md`). In the
  present binding these two headings are 28 leaves apart.
- At its present place the move leaves 12v (last page of q2) against 21r (first page of q4), so both ends of the
  displaced quire fall on quire boundaries. 21r also opens with no heading: five cipher lines (l.1-5), then
  فحللته سريعا لسهولته وهو and the rhymed-prose riddle. Whether 21r l.1-5 is the end of the 12v two-pen piece was not
  tested. The 12v.11 second pen and 21r l.1-5 are both letter- and numeral-like, but the lengths do not fit that
  prose (12v.8 + 12v.11 + 21r l.1-5 is about 141 signs, the prose 293 letters). A further loss at the 12v/21r join, or
  at the 48/49 join (where 49r begins mid-verse, per chain 49r.1), is possible.

So the explanation is not one displaced leaf, and no lost leaf is needed for the 46v/13r break. A whole quire has
been bound in the wrong place, 26 leaves too early. A swap of ff.13 and 47 alone is ruled out, because 13v → 14r and
47v → 48r both read on.

What does not fit: Penn's q7 (ff.43-48, six leaves) straddles the proposed insertion point between 46 and 47, and
العاشر stands on 46r. Either the description of q6-q7 (4 and 6 leaves, both irregular) needs a physical check, or
q6-q7 were reshaped when the book was resewn. This, and the 12v/21r join, are the two points to settle on the
volume itself or with Penn.

Consequences for the records: 20r.8 should be re-keyed with the 47r key against riddle verses 1-5 (47r l.13, 47v
l.1-4). The 21r prose is the clear text of 48r.8 ("the fourth"), and 21r l.1-5 is a separate block. The candidate
plaintexts for 12v.8 and 12v.11 on 13r are taken (they belong to 46v.11 + 13r.1). 12v's pieces probably carry no
plaintext at all: the text says the solver "solved it and wrote nothing on it" (فحله ولم يكتب عليه), unless 21r
l.1-5 belongs to them.
