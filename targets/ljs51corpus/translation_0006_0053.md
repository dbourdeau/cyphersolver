## 1v.3 (f. 1v l.3-8; plaintext f. 2r l.1-2)

**Arabic plaintext:**

يقولون لي ان كان سمعك عاشقا فمالي دمع العين في الخد جاريا / فقلت لهم قد لمتم طرفي فقال لي استغفر من ذا ان غدا جاريا

**Translation:**

> They say to me: "If it is your hearing that is in love,
> then what is [the matter] with me, the tear of the eye running on the cheek?"
> So I said to them: "You have blamed my eye";
> and it said to me: "Ask forgiveness for this, if it has come to be running."

**Metre:** probable ṭawīl (the first hemistich scans as ṭawīl; the rest does not scan cleanly as recorded).

**Note:** love poetry on the motif of falling in love by hearing (the ear loving before the eye). The rhyme word جاريا
is repeated in both verses; whether the second is a jinās (jāriyan "running" against jārī-yā / jāriya "neighbour",
"slave girl") cannot be decided from this text. فمالي sits oddly in the mouth of "they"; the variant on 1v l.12 reads
فما بال دمع للعين ("then what about the tear of the eye"). Source and poet: not checked.

**Technique note (f. 1v l.1-2):** "[He] wrote ... al-Makhzūmī al-ʿAlāʾī, mentioned above, may God extend his shade; he
solved it and thought that he knew the solution, so [I] wrote him this." 1v l.10-11 (... له ولخذ و هو) is not read.

## 2r.4 (f. 2r l.4-9)

**Arabic plaintext:**

رهنت يدي بالجزء عن شكر بره وما بدلت شكري للمشكور / ولو كان ما يستطاع استطعته ولكن ما للاستطاع شديد

**Translation:**

> I pledged my hand, by the portion (bi-l-juzʾ), [and held it] from thanking his kindness,
> yet I have not changed my thanks to the one thanked.
> Had it been what can be done, I would have done it;
> but what [belongs] to the possible (mā li-l-istiṭāʿ) is hard.

**Metre:** ṭawīl for the first hemistich and the opening of the second verse; as recorded, the second hemistich of each
verse does not scan (see Suspected Arabic errors).

**Note:** a verse of thanks expressing the poet's inability to repay a benefactor. As written, بالجزء ("by the
portion") and ما للاستطاع make little sense; the verse appears to be a known one with بالعجز ("through incapacity")
and ما لا يستطاع ("what cannot be done"); not checked. The record notes that it could be the compiler's own thank-you
added after the solution. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note (2r l.10-12):** "[I] solved them (فحللتهما, reading not secure) quickly despite their difficulty and
the repetition of words and their predominance in them, and wrote them in the hand of the fortunate one, unique in his
age, like the lofty pearl." The first word of l.10 is unclear (an earlier pass read فخمسها). Heading l.3: "and from him
also, for al-Makhzūmī".

## 2v.3 (f. 2v l.3-7; plaintext f. 3r l.4-5)

**Arabic plaintext:**

لبي بجواك شاهد صلف تخبرني عنك فوق ما نصف / فلا تكلف ايضاح معذرة اني بما تدعيه معترف

**Translation:**

> My heart (lubbī), in its ardour for you (bi-jawāka), is a vainglorious (ṣalif) witness
> that tells me about you more than we describe.
> So do not take the trouble (lā tukallif, for lā tatakallaf) of making an excuse plain:
> I admit what you claim.

**Metre:** munsariḥ (the second verse scans cleanly; the first hemistich of the first verse does not scan as recorded).

**Note:** a love epigram (the lover needs no excuse from the beloved). The compiler's remark on 3r (dropping one tāʾ
"in al-tamannī", with Qurʾānic parallels) fits تكلف here, written for تتكلف. صلف ("vainglorious, harsh") is odd beside
"witness"; صدق ("a true witness") would be the expected sense; not filled. Source: not checked; the compiler calls it
verse "of the eloquent ...".

**Technique note:** 2v l.1-2: "And he wrote the like of them as the answer, something from the verse of the eloquent
... in them the utmost truth." 2v l.8-13: "I solved them quickly because of their ease. The first to come apart was
yāʾ, then nūn, then alif, then mīm, then thāʾ, then kāf, then ʿayn, then lām, then fāʾ, then bāʾ, then qāf, then ṣād,
wāw and jīm, then khāʾ and rāʾ, then hāʾ and dhāl, then dāl and shīn, then ḍād and ḥāʾ." 3r l.1-3: "I dropped the
single tāʾ in al-tamannī, which is among the most eloquent usages, as in His word (most high) nāran talaẓẓā ('a fire
that blazes', Q 92:14) and tanazzalu l-malāʾika ('the angels descend', Q 97:4) and the like; so I wrote them."

## 3r.7 (f. 3r l.7-11; plaintext f. 3v l.5-8)

**Arabic plaintext:**

سرت نسمة من جلق منذ لذة سحيرا انها جت لوعني وبلابل / تذكرنا عهد الحمام بدوحها وتغريد شحرور وسجع بلابل

**Translation:**

> A breeze travelled from Jilliq (Damascus), since a delight (mundhu ladhdha),
> at early dawn; it (innahā) came (jat, for jāʾat) [to?] my anguish (lawʿanī) and cares (balābil).
> It reminds us of the time of the doves in its tall trees,
> and the warbling of the blackbird and the cooing of nightingales (balābil).

**Metre:** ṭawīl (the second verse scans cleanly; the first as recorded does not).

**Note:** nostalgic verse on Damascus (Jilliq). The rhyme word بلابل is a jinās tāmm: "cares, agitation" in the first
verse, "nightingales" (pl. of bulbul) in the second; the compiler names jinās among the difficulties. In the first
verse منذ لذة and لوعني are not construed with confidence (the compiler says he dropped the first-person yāʾ in the
first verse, which may bear on لوعني). Poet: the compiler's own verse ("I wrote the like of it, two verses");
not checked further.

**Technique note:** 3r l.6: "And I wrote the like of it, two verses, ... Damascus, in this form." 3r l.11-12: "I dropped
the speaker's yāʾ from the first verse so that it should not be substituted [for it]; he solved that quickly." 3v l.1-4:
"His quoting this verse occurred to the mind and hints that he solved and understood them ... solving it is proof of
solving the two verses; then I wrote them in his noble hand lest ... and sent them." 3v l.9-11: "I marvelled at how fast
they were solved, given what we relied on in them: few alifs, rare words, paronomasia (jinās) and the dropped yāʾ."

سكران سكر هوى وسكر مدامة انى يفيق فتى به سكران

**Translation:**

## 5r.12 (f. 5r l.12-13 and 5v l.1-2; plaintext f. 5v l.10-11)

**Arabic plaintext:**

رايت كلبا اسدا احطوما فصدمه ظهر مجنى المرصع وسطها النه بدت تبهره

**Translation:**

> I saw a dog, a lion, [aḥṭūm: sense not established] (aḥṭūmā), and it struck him; the back of
> a shield (mijann-ā?) inlaid [with gems] - its middle [an-nh: sense not established] - appeared, dazzling him.

**Metre:** not applicable (prose; a composed riddle text, not verse).

**Note:** a multi-layer riddle (the compiler: one form, four wordings). The surface wording is a "statement in the form
of a command"; the hidden layers are a prayer from the initial letters (رب احفظ ما وهبت, "Lord, preserve what You
have given") and two names (Mūsā, Ḥāmid) built from genera (city, wild beast, weapon, vessel). احطوما and النه are
not construed; مجنى is taken as mijann "shield" because the compiler names mijann as the weapon. As recorded, the
initial letters do not all give the prayer (see Suspected Arabic errors). The surface sense is necessarily strained.

**Technique note:** 5r l.3-10: "[He] kisses the ground ... the following cipher contains four kinds of cipher (tarājim)
... the first is what we are used to, separated letters and varied shapes. When it is solved by the master, may God
extend his shade, he will find its wording a statement in the form of composition [a command]; within it another cipher
in the sense of prayer; within that the name of a prophet mentioned in the Qurʾān; and within that within, the name of
a grateful man. The form is one and the wordings differ." 5v l.9-13 repeats: four kinds; first the wording [above],
second the prayer رب احفظ ما وهبت from the initial letters, third from names of masculine genera. 6r l.1-10 gives the
hidden names: Mūsā (m from Ḥalab, the city; w from asad, the wild beast; s from mijann, the weapon; y from a vessel)
and Ḥāmid (from asad, mijann, durra), and: "I swear I know no one in this age who could solve this."

## 6r.11 (f. 6r l.11-13 and 6v l.1-4; plaintext f. 6v l.7-8)

**Arabic plaintext:**

نفسي فدا من يهدي مني وصلا لا شعر اضلها في قربه جبلا وردا واسا وغصنا والنقى ودجى بدر وردا ومسكا والطلا و

**Translation:**

> My soul is ransom for one who grants (yuhdī) union from me (minnī) [... sense of the next words not established:
> lā shiʿr, "no hair" or "I do not know" (lā ashʿur)]; he led it astray (aḍallahā) in his nearness, a mountain (jabalā);
> rose and myrtle and a bough, and the sand-dune and darkness,

**Metre:** basīṭ (the second half, وردا واسا وغصنا والنقى ودجى بدر وردا ومسكا والطلا, scans as basīṭ; the opening
also starts as basīṭ, نفسي فدا, but the middle does not scan as recorded).

**Note:** a love poem by the patron in the "folding and unfolding" manner (laff wa-nashr): the second half is a catalogue
of the beloved's charms in their stock images (rose = cheek, myrtle = down, bough = stature, dune = hips, darkness =
hair, full moon = face, musk = scent, wine = saliva), presumably answering a list of features in the part that is not
construed. The compiler praises "the craft in them". The middle of the first line is not construed and the text ends
in an unfinished و. Source: the patron's own verse; not checked.

**Technique note:** 6r l.9-10: "I found among my papers a cipher in the hand of the master al-ʿAlāʾī mentioned above,
which he had sent long ago; it was lost among the papers; then I solved it at last. And from his fortunate verse:"
6v l.5-6 (after the block): "Let one look at the beauty of meaning of these two verses and the craft in them; glory to
the Bestower."

## 7r.12 (f. 7r l.12-13 and 7v l.1-6; plaintext f. 7v l.8-11)

**Arabic plaintext:**

يا قلب مو بنقري لي من حر وفك خط بقلو ومن قرص / بقه في الهوى تسقط / اخضع وذل لمن تهوى ودع يشترط عاشق ولو نقس دا شي ما / سمح بوفا

**Translation (tentative; colloquial mawāliyā, dialect and spelling not secure):**

> O heart, isn't it (mū, colloquial "not") [bi-naqrī lī: not construed] from the heat of [wafk? khaṭṭ? biqlū?: not construed], and from the bite (qarṣ)
> of a gnat (baqqa) in love you fall?

> he did not grant (samaḥ) fidelity (bi-wafā).

**Metre:** not identified (colloquial mawāliyā; the record's line breaks do not show the usual four-line monorhyme

> 8. And he has brought me (ballaghanī) [to?] those who seek him (qāṣidīhi) with a beaming face
>    and manners gentler than the breeze.
> 9. When the enraged one is minded to come [at him],
>    [he meets him] with cheer, the nature of the dutiful, forbearing man.
> 10. By his grace (bi-mannihi, as written) he never uttered, in all time, to one who drew near,

**Metre:** wāfir, both poems (e.g. سلكنا مهمه القفر الصميم = mufāʿaltun mufāʿaltun faʿūlun); one rhyme in -īm throughout.

**Note:** the compiler's answer, in cipher, to a riddle (14r l.7-14v l.1) whose solution is mahmah ("waterless waste").
Poem 1 is a desert journey ending at the addressee's generosity; poem 2 is a panegyric to a patron named Ibn Faḍl Allāh
(identification with the Banū Faḍl Allāh al-ʿUmarī, heads of the Mamluk chancery: not checked). The answer word and
the riddle's clue words are planted in the verse, as the compiler's commentary (17v l.5-18r l.8) explains: poem 1 opens
with mahmah; the "verb that commands silence" is answered by mah (poem 2 l.10, where the recorded بمنه "by his grace"

poem 2 l.4 echoes the proverb al-mulk ʿaqīm ("kingship is barren", i.e. it knows no kinship); poem 2 l.5 echoes the
prophetic description of Joseph, "the noble son of the noble son of the noble". In l.8 the construal of وبلغني قاصديه is
uncertain ("he brought me to his seekers" or "he made his seekers reach [their aim]"). Source: the compiler's own poems.

**Technique note:** 14v l.2-6: "I wrote the answer in cipher, of the kind called tashifi: that is, one makes a single
form for a letter shape and the letters like it in Arabic; so bāʾ, tāʾ, thāʾ, nūn and yāʾ are made one form, jīm, ḥāʾ

## 20r.8 (f. 20r l.8-13, 20v l.1-13, 21r l.1-5; plaintext after 21r l.6)

> are permitted, though the people of righteousness shun it (tajāfāhā). It visits
> when night falls and departs at morning. [People] imagine it
> [al-ʿasā [?]: not translated], and the spearheads (al-asinna) pierce it, yet it is counted among
> the bliss of the people of Paradise. Its gathering is covered by the backs (or: by appearing, bi-l-ẓuhūr), and spreading
> it out (iftirāshuhā)

> the schools of law. Its back is the most trodden [? awṭaʾ] back [? ẓahr] for the two riders; its beauty [? jamāluhā]:

## Suspected Arabic errors (not changed in the records)

These are places where the recorded Arabic seems to need another reading, by metre, grammar, sense or the compiler's own
comments. They are suggestions for a re-check on the master, not corrections; none has been applied above.

1. **2r.4** بالجزء probably بالعجز ("through incapacity"); ما يستطاع probably مما يستطاع (metre: ṭawīl needs the extra
   syllable); ما للاستطاع probably ما لا يستطاع. وما بدلت شكري للمشكور does not scan; the familiar form of this verse
   has a different second hemistich (not checked).
2. **2v.3** صلف ("vainglorious") against the expected صدق ("a true witness"); لبي بجواك does not scan as munsariḥ.
3. **3r.7** first verse: منذ لذة and لوعني do not construe or scan; worth re-reading.
4. **4v.8** والمرق breaks the ṭawīl; سادر and ها also need a re-check (already doubtful in the record notes).
5. **5r.12** the initial letters of the twelve recorded words are ر ك ا ا ف ظ م ا و ا ب ت, against the prayer the
   compiler says they spell, رب احفظ ما وهبت (ر ب ا ح ف ظ م ا و ه ب ت). Nine of twelve agree; words 2 (كلبا, expected a
   word in ب-), 4 (احطوما, expected ح-) and 10 (النه, expected ه-) disagree, so these three words are the ones to re-read.
6. **6r.11** the middle of the first line (مني وصلا لا شعر اضلها في قربه جبلا) does not scan or construe; the line ends
   in a bare و.
7. **6v.11** مصرا for مصر (grammar only).
8. **7r.5** the second hemistich (يغلب صفوا ويكدر) is short for ṭawīl; a word may be missing.
9. **8r.11** يا ادم probably أنادم ("are you regretful"), with the interlinear ام ناسي completing the first verse;
   بمنعه probably يمنعه.
10. **9v.3** صفو الكدر probably صفوه الكدر ("its clearness [is followed by] turbidity").
11. **10r.2** a word is missing at the end of the second verse (the rhyme in -aj set by فيروزج).
12. **10v.1** the second and fourth hemistichs stop short (يحسن, فاضل); معاز needs a re-check.
13. **13r.11** بكوه is not a standard word.
14. **13v.10** نخيل البريد probably بخيل البريد / خيل البريد ("[with] the post horses").
15. **14v.7** poem 2 l.10 بمنه may be بمه ("with 'mah!'"), which is the answer word the commentary says is planted
    there; poem 2 l.12 ونم may be وهم (wahm), the word the commentary dismantles (but tashifi nūn and hāʾ are different
    sign classes, so the cipher should decide); poem 2 l.8 وبلغني قاصديه construes poorly (a verb such as يلقى, "he
    meets", would fit, but the letter count differs; not proposed as a reading).
16. **19r.7** قمرا for قمر (grammar only); third verse ومدد ... وحذف لرابع is rough.
17. **22r.1** اخيا probably حياء ("modesty", the pair of عفه); التناثر, as the rhyme of a poem in -ir, may be التهاتر
    ("mutual abuse"); the fourth hemistich lacks its rhyme word, and the interlinear الجوادر may belong there (as a
    variant of a word such as الخوادر, "lions in their lairs"; not checked); انتقام perhaps اتقاء ("guarding against");
    المعابر perhaps المعاير ("reproaches"); خافون for يخافون and همو / عارهو as written.
18. **22v.9** لبني / بنيك need a re-check; the record already marks the sense unclear.
19. **24v.6** the second verse is too long for basīṭ; المخادم uncertain (record).
