# LJS 51, chains 85r.5 to 96r.2 (images 174-197): English translation

Source: `chains_0174_0197.jsonl` only (master-TIFF readings). The range has 26 chains, and every one has a plaintext
on the page. Six things need a word before they are read:

- **Red glosses.** Eight chains (87r.3, 87v.8, 89v.4, 90r.4, 90v.1, 90v.12, 93v.7, 95v.10) carry one red Arabic letter
  over every cipher sign. Where the red reading differs from the written plaintext, the difference is given under the
  chain, as the record states it. In 90v.12 the red reading is the better text (see the note and the suspected errors).
- **Exchanges.** 93v.1 and 93v.7 are a question and its answer: the compiler sends a verse of al-Mutanabbi, and the
  master answers in cipher with a verse on the same rhyme. 95r.10, 95v.10 and 96r.2 are a second run of the same kind.
- **94v.5** is a cipher with optional words: the compiler marked two pairs of groups "if he wishes, he leaves it out",
  and the solver gave the short form and changed one word for a better one.
- **95r.10** enciphers two verses in the reverse of their order in the poem; the page labels them "the second, which is
  the first" and "the first, which is the second".
- **88v.10**: the second line of the plaintext is taken by a hole in the paper. Only the first line is translated.
- **The dated line on 86r** (Shaykh Dawud at Alexandria, in the 730s AH) and the **note on 85v** (Taqi al-Din Muhammad
  b. Ja'far al-Husayni) are translated in a separate section at the end.

The Arabic is copied as it stands in the record. A slash (/) in the Arabic is the record's own line division. The
hemistich division in the English is mine, made from the metre and rhyme where they could be found. [?] marks a word
the record flags as doubtful; alternatives are only in the notes. "Translation confidence" is my confidence in the
English. It is not the record's plaintext certainty.

In the headings, "the compiler" is the writer of the book (Ibn al-Durayhim in the record) and "the master"
(al-makhdum) is the patron who solves most of the pieces, here called al-maqarr al-'Ala'i or al-makhdumi al-'Ala'i.
Named in the record on ff. 85r-96v: al-Sharif Shihab al-Din, muwaqqi' al-dast al-sharif (a secretary of the chancery
bench), whose verse the compiler enciphers on 85r; Taqi al-Din Muhammad b. Ja'far al-Husayni, "God have mercy on him",
and 'Ulayya bint al-Mahdi (85v); Shaykh Dawud, pupil of Shaykh Taj al-Din ibn 'Ata (86r); Sharaf al-Din Musa ibn al-Ajall
al-Baridi (87r); Shaykh Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim, "the translator" (88v, 95r); Ibrahim b. al-Mahdi and al-Ma'mun
(91v); the vizier al-Muhallabi (91r); al-Mutanabbi (93r); Sibawayh and the Qur'an reader Hamza (96v). Places:
Alexandria (86r) and Siryaqus (95r).

## 85r.5 (cipher 85r l.5-7; plaintext 85r l.8)

**Arabic (record):**
ما ركب الذل في طباعي وليس للذل قط داعي

**English:**
- Abasement was never set (rukkiba) in my nature,
- and there is never any call for abasement.

**Metre:** mukhalla' al-basit (both hemistichs scan, reading ركب as rukkiba).

**Note:** a proud single verse by al-Sharif Shihab al-Din, muwaqqi' al-dast al-sharif, enciphered by the compiler for the
master. The rhyme is the same word-final -a'i in both hemistichs (طباعي / داعي). The record finds no solver line; the
plaintext stands under the block. In the cipher, داعي is split by a divider after the dal (record). Poet: al-Sharif
Shihab al-Din (named by the compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 85r l.3-4: "... I wrote from the verse of al-Sharif Shihab al-Din, muwaqqi' of the noble dast"; the
block is followed by وهو ("and it is").

## 85r.10 (cipher 85r l.10-13, 85v l.1-3; plaintext 85v l.4-5)

**Arabic (record):**
قد راينا من الزمان عيونا كم قلوب انكت وابكت عيونا / اذ عرضنا بمولمات ملمات تمنينا اذ عرضنا المنونا

**English:**
- We have seen from Time ('uyun: "eyes", or choice sorts of things);
- how many hearts have they wounded (ankat), and made eyes ('uyun) weep!
- When we were exposed to painful (mu'limat) visitations (mulimmat),
- we wished, when we were exposed, for death (al-manun).

**Metre:** khafif (the first hemistich scans; the rest was not scanned closely).

**Note:** a second piece by the same al-Sharif Shihab al-Din. It is built on repetition: 'uyun closes both hemistichs of
the first verse (a full pun, jinas tamm, if the first means something other than "eyes"; I cannot fix its sense, and
"springs" or "notables" are also possible), اذ عرضنا is said twice in the second verse, and mu'limat / mulimmat
("painful" / "calamities") is a pun on near-identical letters. بمولمات is mu'limat with the hamza unwritten (record,
read at 3x and confirmed sign by sign). انكت is doubtful in sense to me (from nakā, "to harm", or naka'a, "to reopen a
wound"). Poet: al-Sharif Shihab al-Din (named by the compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 85r l.9: "and I wrote to him from the verse of al-Sharif also". No solver line; the plaintext follows
the block.

## 85v.8 (cipher 85v l.8-13; plaintext 86r l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
مجال الكلام مجال وسيع وصمت لسان حصن منيع / ونفس الشريف اذا ما تهان تعز ولا يعتز بها خضوع

**English:**
- The field of speech is a wide field,
- and the silence of a tongue is an impregnable fortress.
- The noble soul, when it is humiliated,
- holds itself dear, and [يعتز بها as written; read يعتريها: "no submission comes over it"].

**Metre:** mutaqarib (the first verse and the first hemistich of the second scan; the last hemistich scans with يعتريها
and not with يعتز بها).

**Note:** a verse of Taqi al-Din Muhammad b. Ja'far al-Husayni (see the last section for the heading). The first verse
praises the safety of silence against the open field of talk; the second, the pride of the noble soul. The written
words يعتز بها leave the last hemistich without a clear sense ("submission is not made mighty by it"). The cipher has
no divider between them (one group of 7 signs), and its sign for the fourth letter is the one that also stands for ra
in الشريف (record): ي ع ت ر ي ه ا, يعتريها "comes over it", is 7 letters and fits the cipher, the metre and the sense
(see the suspected errors). The cipher also has a fifth sign in لسان, a ya (record): لساني "my tongue". Poet: Taqi al-Din
Muhammad b. Ja'far al-Husayni (named by the compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 85v l.6-7: the heading translated in the last section. No solver line; the plaintext stands at the
top of 86r.

## 86r.4 (cipher 86r l.4-5; plaintext 86r l.6)

**Arabic (record):**
ان عينا لن تراكم دمعها فيه تراكم

**English:**
- An eye that will not see you (lan tarakum):
- its tears pile up (tarakama) in it.

**Metre:** majzu' al-ramal (both halves scan).

**Note:** a pun across a word boundary (jinas murakkab): ترا كم "sees you" and تراكم "heaped up" have the same letters,
and the cipher writes the two alike (record). The heading names Taqi al-Din again ("from the verse of the aforementioned")
and calls the piece تخميسها "its takhmis", but the record does not read that word with certainty, and a single couplet
of two short halves is not a takhmis as it stands. The masculine فيه after the feminine 'ayn is as written. Poet: Taqi
al-Din (named by the compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 86r l.3: "and I wrote [its takhmis?] from the verse of the aforementioned". No solver line.

## 86r.9 (cipher 86r l.9-13, 86v l.1-3; plaintext 86v l.4-7)

**Arabic (record):**
هذي جراحي طرية بالدما تصيح وقاتلي نصب عيني / قط ما يبرح / فقال خذ لك مداوي فقلت ذا يقبح الى جرحي يداويني / وذا الاصلح

**English:**
- "These are my wounds, fresh, crying out with blood,
- and my killer is before my eyes; he never leaves."
- He said: "Take yourself a healer." I said: "That would be ugly.
- The one who [wounded me, see the note] should heal me; that is the better (way)."

**Metre:** not scanned. Colloquial verse in dialogue, with run-over endings written as short lines (قط ما يبرح, وذا
الاصلح) and the rhyme in -ah (تصيح, يبرح, يقبح, الاصلح).

**Note:** the piece Shaykh Dawud recited at Alexandria (heading in the last section). It is colloquial: الى is the
dialect relative illi "the one who", not the preposition ila. As written, الى جرحي يداويني reads "the one who ... my
wound heals me"; the cipher has a five-sign word with a lam- or nun-like sign before the final ya (record), that is
جرحني, "the one who wounded me heals me", which completes the sense: the lover will take no physician but the beloved
who wounded him. The record also notes that the cipher has قلت for فقلت; the two differences cancel in the count.
Whether the verse is Dawud's own or one he recited, the record does not say. In a Sufi circle (his master Ibn 'Ata, see
the last section) the beloved who wounds and heals may be read of God, but that is my gloss.

**Technique note:** 86r l.7-8: the dated heading (last section). No solver line; the plaintext follows on 86v.

## 86v.9 (cipher 86v l.9-12; plaintext 86v l.13-14)

**Arabic (record):**
عقد تسعين ضيق صدري ورزقي واتساع الدنيا همومي / وفكري

**English:**
- The knot of ninety ('aqd tis'in) is the tightness of my breast and of my livelihood,
- and the breadth of the world is (the breadth of) my cares and my thoughts.

**Metre:** not scanned.

**Note:** a conceit from finger-reckoning (hisab al-'aqd). The compiler explains it on 87r l.1-2: "the knot of ninety in
the Coptic reckoning is joining the index finger to the root of the thumb, tightly". Ninety is the tightest figure of
the hand, so it stands for a narrow breast and a narrow living, set against the wide world that is all care. He
enciphered it "in the Coptic pen, to suit the wording" (86v l.8): a cipher alphabet of Coptic-looking letters for a
verse on the Coptic reckoning. The cipher may write الدنا for الدنيا (one sign short; record). Poet: not stated (the
heading says only "I wrote to him also").

**Technique note:** 86v l.8: "and I wrote to him also in the Coptic pen, to suit the wording [?]"; 87r l.1-2 (above).

## 87r.3 (cipher 87r l.3-11; plaintext 87r l.13-14, 87v l.1-3)

**Arabic (record):**
كم من قوي قوي في تقلبه مسدد الراي عنه الرزق / منحرف / وكم ضعيف ضعيف بات في سعة كانه من خليج البحر / يغترف / هذا دليل على ان الاله له في الخلق سر خفي ليس ينكشف

**English:**
- How many a strong man, strong in his ways of getting on (taqallub),
- sound of judgment, from whom provision turns aside;
- and how many a weak man, weak indeed, spends the night (bata) in plenty,
- as though he scooped from a gulf of the sea!
- This is a proof that God has,
- in His creation, a hidden secret that is not disclosed.

**Metre:** basit (the first hemistich scans with قوي twice).

**Red gloss:** the red letters agree with the plaintext, and give ت in بات (the dot over the written word is unclear;
بان is possible on the page). The cipher has قوي only once (record: 117 letters, 114 signs, exactly the three letters
of the doubled word).

**Note:** the parallel "strong, strong" / "weak, weak" is the figure of the piece, and the metre wants both doublings.
Sharaf al-Din Musa ibn al-Ajall al-Baridi had sent it to the compiler, who solved it at once, in his presence [?],
because it was easy. The compiler adds (87v l.4-6) that "this sayer took this idea from the two verses mentioned in
al-Miftah" and quotes them (كم عاقل عاقل اعيت مذاهبه ...); they are not part of the chain. The record does not identify
al-Miftah or the poets; not checked. Poet: not stated.

**Technique note:** 87r l.2: "Sharaf al-Din Musa ibn al-Ajall al-Baridi had written to me"; 87r l.12: "I solved it at
once, in his presence [?], because of its easiness, and it is"; 87v l.4-6 (above).

## 87v.8 (cipher 87v l.8-13, 88r l.1-4; plaintext 88r l.5-10)

**Arabic (record):**
كم فاضل فاضل في العلم قد قعدت به الليالي فلا جاه / ولا ذهبا / وناقص ناقص ذي ثروة كثرت يثنى عليه وان لم / يكتسب ادبا / هذا دليل على عجز الانام فلا سعي يفيد بل المقدور / ما كتبا

**English:**
- How many a man of merit, of real merit in learning, have the nights
- left sitting idle, with neither standing nor gold;
- and how many a deficient man, deficient indeed, with wealth grown great,
- is praised, though he has gained no cultivation (adab)!
- This is a proof of the helplessness of mankind: no striving
- avails, but only what is decreed, what is written.

**Metre:** basit.

**Red gloss:** equal to the plaintext except كثرت, which the red gloss and the cipher both give as كثت, without the ra
(record).

**Note:** the compiler's own versification of the same idea ("so I put it in verse"), sent to al-maqarr al-'Ala'i [?].
It copies the frame of 87r.3 closely: the doubled word, "how many ... and how many ...", and "this is a proof", but
draws the opposite moral: not a hidden divine secret, but human helplessness before what is written. No solver line.
Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 87v l.7: "so I put it in verse and wrote it to al-maqarr al-'Ala'i [?], may God extend his shade".

## 88r.12 (cipher 88r l.12-13, 88v l.1-4; plaintext 88v l.5-6)

**Arabic (record):**
لا سعيد من ليس يسعده جد سعيد وطاير مامون / ولسان مثل الحسام وقلب صادق عزمه وراي رصين

**English:**

Poet: not stated.

## 89r.4 (cipher 89r l.4-9; plaintext 89r l.13, 89v l.1)

**Arabic (record):**
مستهاما خالي الوفاض من الهم غريب الديار ناي العرين / وقطوب الخطوب قد ساورتني وحروب الكروب قد تعريني

**English:**
- Distraught with love (mustahaman), empty-handed (khali al-wifad), from care,
- a stranger far from home, distant from my den (al-'arin);
- the frowns of misfortune (qutub al-khutub) have assailed me,
- and the wars of affliction (hurub al-kurub) strip me bare.

**Metre:** khafif.

**Note:** two verses from a qasida of the compiler's own, whose opening verse he quotes first (89r l.11, not
enciphered). The second verse is built on two rhyming pairs inside each hemistich: qutub / khutub and hurub / kurub.
خالي الوفاض is "with an empty bag", that is with nothing; من الهم I take as "from (the weight of) care", but it could
also be read "empty of care", against the sense. Al-'arin is the lion's den, the warrior's home. The cipher's sign that
the draft took for a null is ya, the rhyme letter (record). In the black cipher الكروب lacks its ra, added later in grey
ink (record). No solver line.

**Technique note:** 89r l.3: "and I wrote to al-maqarr al-makhdumi, the aforementioned, may God extend his shade";
89r l.10-12: "these are of my own verse, from a qasida whose beginning is (verse), and the two enciphered verses (are)".

## 89v.4 (cipher 89v l.4-11; plaintext 89v l.12-13, 90r l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
عندي حدايق جود من نوالكم قد مسها عطش فليسق / من غرسا / تداركوها وفي اغصانها ورق فلن يعود اخضرار / العود ان يبسا

**English:**
- I have gardens of bounty from your gifts
- that thirst has touched: so let him who planted them water them.
- Tend them while there are still leaves on their branches,
- for the green of the bough (al-'ud) will not return once it has dried.

**Metre:** basit.

**Red gloss:** equal to the plaintext letter for letter; 90r l.1 reads وفي and اخضرار as in the gloss (record).

**Note:** a plea to a patron not to let his favour lapse: the favours he planted are the garden, and neglect is the
drought. The compiler wrote it "to one, may God grant him the enjoyment of his recovery", "to win back the noble heart"
after a liberty (inbisat) that had not been well taken, perhaps the one told on 84r. Poet: the compiler, as I read the
heading.

**Technique note:** 89v l.2-3: "and I wrote to one, may God grant him the enjoyment of his recovery, seeking to soften the
noble heart after the familiarity that had passed, which I had made bold with towards him, and which was not borne
[?]"; 89v l.11: "[he solved it?], and it is".

## 90r.4 (cipher 90r l.4-10; plaintext 90r l.11-12)

**Arabic (record):**
انتم لنا غيث ونحن كروضة تنمي سحابكم علينا نورق / ان تمنعوا عنا فواضل جودكم ونوالكم فيذبل [?] من [?] شغلوف [?]

**English:**
- You are rain to us, and we are like a meadow:
- your clouds make it grow, and we put out leaves (nuriq).
- If you withhold from us the overflow of your generosity
- and your gifts, then [فيذبل من شغلوف?: "... withers"].

**Metre:** kamil (the first hemistich scans).

**Red gloss:** the red letters give سحايبكم for written سحابكم, which makes the count exact (79 = 79); they agree with
the black signs throughout (record). The record does not give the red reading of the last three words.

**Note:** the same image as 89v.4, rain and garden, sent to the master. The last words are not read: فيذبل "it withers"
is likely, but من شغلوف is not a word I know, and it does not keep the rhyme in -q (نورق). The red gloss over the end
of the block should settle it (see the suspected errors). The solver line after the block reads "فحللته وهو"
("I solved it, and it is"; written compressed, record), which would make the compiler the solver, while the heading "and
I wrote to him" makes him the sender. I leave the question open.

**Technique note:** 90r l.3: "and I wrote to him, may God extend his shade"; 90r l.10: فحللته وهو, written compressed.

## 90v.12 (cipher 90v l.12-13, 91r l.1-5; plaintext 91r l.6-7)

**Arabic (record):**
وكيف يكون حال غريب دار كئيب من خطوب تعتريه / يكررها [?] في النهار وبالدياجي الا موت يباع فاشتريه

**English:**
- And how is it with a stranger far from home,
- downcast by the misfortunes that come over him,
- who repeats [يكررها as written; the red gloss and the cipher have يكرر], by day and in the dark nights:
- "Is there no death for sale, that I may buy it?"

**Metre:** wafir (the second verse scans with yukarriru, not with يكررها).

**Red gloss:** the red letters and the cipher both have يكرر, four signs, for written يكررها (record). Read so, the line
scans and the sense is whole: the stranger repeats, day and night, the cry of the last hemistich.

**Note:** the master had asked after the compiler, and this is his answer. It ends on a tadmin (a borrowed half-verse):
"is there no death for sale, that I may buy it?" from the verses of the vizier al-Muhallabi, "whose story is famous"
(91r l.8), which the compiler then quotes in full (91r l.9-10, not enciphered). Poet: the compiler, with al-Muhallabi's
hemistich (named by the compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 90v l.10-11: "it reached the mamluk that he had graciously asked how the mamluk was; so I wrote to
him, may God spread his shade"; 91r l.8: "I worked in this half(-verse) from the verses of the vizier al-Muhallabi,
whose story is famous, and its completion is".

## 92v.9 (cipher 92v l.9-13, 93r l.1; plaintext 93r l.6-8)

**Arabic (record):**
بسامي القرن ان قرنثمه [?] فيعتضده / فياخذه فيرفعه فيخفضه فيقتصك [?] / فيدمغه فيحطمه فيخضمه فيزدرده

**English:**
- With a high-horned [القرن?] (one), if [قرنثمه?] ..., he grips him by the upper arm,
- then seizes him, then lifts him, then lowers him, then [فيقتصك?] ...,
- then strikes him on the skull, then smashes him, then chews him, then swallows him down.

**Metre:** not scanned.

**Note:** one of "the two knotty verses" (al-'awisan) that the master enciphered and sent to the compiler. Its device
is a chain of eleven verbs of the same shape, fa-yaf'aluhu "then he does it to him", which makes the cipher both long

taking and devouring its prey; who or what is "high-horned" depends on القرن, which is doubtful, and the fourth word
and the last word of the second line are not read (record; the cipher's groups are longer than both written words).
When the compiler was slow, the master sent him half of the first verse as a hint; the compiler's description of it
("all of it is in five letters [?], of which only ba, waw and mim repeat ...") I cannot square with the verse as read.
He solved it "after difficulty". Poet: not stated.

enciphered and sent are among ..."; 93r l.2-5: "when I was slow to solve it he sent the half of the first verse ...;
so I solved it after difficulty".

وفي تقصير انياب الافاعي دليل ان تعيش [م]دا [?] طويلا

**English:**
- And in the shortening of the vipers' fangs

## 94r.3 (cipher 94r l.3-10; plaintext 94r l.12-14)

**Arabic (record):**
كيف السبيل الى مرضاة من عظمت علي حقوقه حتى يعود كما / قد ضاقت الارض بي حتى لقد سيمت نفسي الحياة وقد طال الصدود / اما

**English:**
- How shall I find the way to the good pleasure of one whose rights over me
- are great, so that he may be again as [he was]?
- The earth has grown narrow for me, so that my soul has wearied (su'imat)
- of life; and the estrangement has lasted long: is it not (enough)?

**Metre:** basit.

**Note:** written impromptu "seeking to soften the noble heart again". The point of craft is the first verse's end,
يعود كما "may return as", with كان "(he) was" left out. The master solved it and thought the compiler had made a mistake,
and the compiler explains (94v l.1-4) that he dropped the word because the run of the speech shows it. The lone اما on
94r l.14 is the last word of the cipher, not a catchword (record). The last verse echoes Q 9:118, "the earth, wide as it
is, grew narrow for them"; that is my note. Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 94r l.1-2: "so I wrote to him, seeking to soften the noble heart again, impromptu verse of my own";
94r l.11: "he solved both and sent both"; 94v l.1-4 (above).

## 95r.10 (cipher 95r l.10-12, 95v l.1-3; plaintext 95v l.8, then 95v l.6)

**English (in the cipher's order):**
- Sometimes a miser (danin) is generous with what is precious,

- The eye was lavish with tear after tear,

**Metre:** khafif (the first verse scans).

## The dated line on f. 86r and the note on f. 85v

**86r l.7-8 (heading of 86r.9), Arabic (record):**
وكتبت له ما انشد به الشيخ داود تلميذ الشيخ تاج الدين / بن عطا بثغر الاسكندرية في سنة بضع وثلثين وسبع[مية]

**English:** "And I wrote to him what Shaykh Dawud, pupil of Shaykh Taj al-Din ibn 'Ata, recited at the port (thaghr) of
Alexandria in the year thirty-odd and seven hundred."

**Note:** "Thirty-odd and seven hundred" is AH 731-739, about AD 1330-1339. The hundreds are abbreviated on the page
(وسبع with a small superscript sign for مية), and تلميذ is written undotted (record). No formula of blessing follows
either name. The verb is anshada bihi, "recited": the line does not say that Dawud composed the verse. Taj al-Din ibn
'Ata is presumably the Shadhili master Taj al-Din Ibn 'Ata' Allah al-Iskandari, who died in Alexandria in 709/1309, and
the pupil could be Dawud al-Bakhili, a Shadhili of Alexandria; neither identification is in the record, and both are
not checked. The line dates the recitation, not the copying of the manuscript: it shows that the compiler wrote this
part after the 730s.

**85v l.6-7 (heading of 85v.8), Arabic (record):**
وكتبت له اسبغ الله ظلاله من نظم تقي الدين محمد بن جعفر الحسيني رحمه الله عملا بقول القايل [?] لعلية بنت المهدي

**English:** "And I wrote to him, may God extend his shade, from the verse of Taqi al-Din Muhammad b. Ja'far al-Husayni,

**Marginal verse (85v right margin, written upwards; read only roughly, record):**

## Suspected Arabic errors

None of these readings has been changed above; each is a suspicion for the next look at the master.

1. **85v.8** يعتز بها: read يعتريها. The cipher has one group of 7 signs with no divider, its fourth sign is the one
   for ra in الشريف (record), يعتريها has exactly 7 letters, the mutaqarib scans with it and not with يعتز بها, and the
   sense ("no submission comes over it") is whole. Strong.
2. **90v.12** يكررها: the red gloss and the cipher both have يكرر (4 signs; record), and the wafir scans with yukarriru
   and not with yukarriruha. Strong.
3. **86r.9** جرحي: the cipher has a 5-sign word with a lam- or nun-like sign before the ya (record), جرحني; with الى =
   colloquial illi, "the one who wounded me heals me". Strong for the sense; also فقلت against the cipher's قلت. Moderate.
4. **94v.5** بعدهم: the cipher's group has 6 signs and the short form on 95r has بعهدهم; the cipher also has لهم after
   عبيدهم (record). Moderate to strong.
5. **90r.4** فيذبل [?] من [?] شغلوف [?]: the rhyme wants a word in -q (cf. نورق); the red gloss over the end of the block
   should be read. Moderate.
6. **92v.9** قرنثمه [?] and فيقتصك [?]: the cipher groups are 3+5 and 8 signs against written 6 and 6 (record); القرن is
   doubtful. Moderate.
7. **88v.10** العذير [?] (العذيب or الغدير) and the holed second line. Open (physical damage).
8. **96r.2** درنحوا [?] not read; القاكم with an extra tooth; الرب وابقاكم unclear. Open.
9. **87r.3** بات / بان: the red gloss has ت. Weak.
10. **93v.7** [م]دا [?]: blotted; the red gloss has دا. Weak.
11. **95r.10** فهذين: grammatically odd (فلهذين would scan in khafif), but the cipher's count agrees with the written
    word. Weak.
12. **86r.4** تخميسها [?] in the heading: not certain, and the piece is not a takhmis as it stands. Weak.
13. **Divergences in the cipher, not in the written text** (record): 85r.5 داعي split 1 | 3; 85v.8 لساني (5 signs);
    86v.9 الدنيا 5 signs (الدنا?); 87r.3 قوي once; 87v.8 كثت (also in the red gloss); 88r.12 من as two groups; 89r.4
    الكروب without ra (added in grey); 89v.4 none; 90r.4 سحايبكم (also in the red gloss); 91r.12 final hamzas dropped,
    بعد كم one group, تحشمك 6 signs; 91v.10 hamza of ماء dropped; 93v.7 انيب, الفاعي (also in the red gloss).
