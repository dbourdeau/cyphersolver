# LJS 51, chains 49v.2 to 60v.2 (images 102-125): English translation

Source: `chains_0102_0125.jsonl` only (master-TIFF readings). Seven chains have a red gloss over the signs (51v.9,
52v.4, 53r.3, 54v.1, 54v.9, 57v.4, 59v.7); in each the record's red reading equals the plaintext letter for letter,
so the plaintext and the red reading are one text here. The one exception is 57v.4: the cipher and the red letters
have رنقا (4 signs) where the written plaintext has رونقا. The translation follows the written text. 55v.5 has no red
letters (record). Every chain in this range has a plaintext, and all 23 are translated.

The Arabic is copied as it stands in the record. A slash (/) in the Arabic is the record's own line division. The
hemistich division in the English is mine, made from the metre and rhyme. [?] marks a word the record flags as
doubtful; alternatives are only in the notes. "Translation confidence" is my confidence in the English. It is not
the record's plaintext certainty.

In the headings, "the compiler" is the writer of the book (the record identifies him as Ibn al-Durayhim at 49v.2) and
"the master" (al-makhdum) is the patron who solves most of the pieces. The 49r-60v pieces are an exchange of short
cipher verses between the two, with a few brought by others.

## 49v.2 (cipher 49v l.2-8; plaintext 49v l.10-12)

**Arabic (record):**
تحققت اني عند خدك قطنة وكنت اراني واحدا في المترجم / اذا عرض المرموز فالحل جوهرا من ابن دنينير وان ذكرهم [?]

**English:**
- I have come to know for certain that beside your cheek (khadd) I am a tuft of cotton (qutna),
- though I used to think myself unique in the enciphered (art) (al-mutarjam).
- When the cryptogram (al-marmuz) is put forward, the solution (comes as) a jewel
- from Ibn Dunaynir, even if they mention [?].

**Metre:** tawil (the first verse scans; the last hemistich is a syllable short as read).

**Note:** the compiler's own piece, a modest boast to the master: he thought himself the one expert in ciphers and
now finds himself "cotton" beside him. The image is not clear to me as written. The record gives فطنة "acumen" as a
possible reading of قطنة. خدك "your cheek" makes little sense; حدك "your edge" (cotton against a blade) would make
more, but that is my guess (see the suspected errors). The second verse is the one the record says was disputed:
Ibn Dunaynir (the cryptologist whose book is cited at 49r) is named here, and on 50r the compiler argues that ابن
دريهم, his own name, would be better. Dunaynir is the diminutive of dinar and Durayhim the diminutive of dirham, so
the two names pair as coins. The record says the correspondent proposed من ابن دنينير. That l.12 belongs to the
plaintext at all is inferred from the count (record), and the last word ذكرهم [?] (or ذكرتم) is not secure, so the
fourth line is rendered only as far as it goes. Poet: the compiler (heading فكتبت).

**Technique note:** 49v l.1: "and I wrote" (the compiler's own cipher); 49v l.9: "he solved that quickly and wrote it,
and it is"; 50r l.1-12: the wording is discussed. The correspondent proposed من ابن دنينير, and the compiler argues
that ابن دريهم is better in two ways (tanazul, and avoiding a ta'sis in one verse only). 49v l.13-14 and 50r are prose:
"then he wrote: [?] a word, or if it had been, how would it have been better / and here by way of offering, not of
objection, on his part towards kings" (record's text; rendering tentative).

## 50v.1 (cipher 50v l.1-7; plaintext 51r l.3-4)

**Arabic (record):**
رسايلكم الله لنا فتصدقوا وعودوا بها ان الكريم يعود / ولا تقطعوا وصلا سوى من جزازة تمنوا بارسال لها وتجودوا

**English:**
- Your letters, for God's sake, (send them) to us: be charitable,
- and come back with them, for the generous man comes back (again).
- Do not cut off contact, even if (you send) only a slip of paper (jazaza):
- grant us the favour of sending one, and be generous.

**Metre:** tawil, with the short final foot (fa'ulun) in both verses (the first hemistich scans only with a rare
shortened foot, lu-kul-la-ha).

**Note:** a plea for letters, addressed to the compiler. رسايلكم الله لنا is rendered loosely ("your letters, for God's
sake, to us"); the phrase is elliptical as written and the record keeps رسايلكم as written. تمنوا is read tamunnu
"grant as a favour" (tamannaw "wish" is also possible as written). The play is on ya'udu "he comes back" (also "he
pays a return visit") and 'udu "come back". The compiler's remark on the solution matches the text: verse 2 ends
وتجودوا with waw and alif beyond verse 1's يعود, and jim occurs twice (جزازة, تجودوا). Poet: not checked (the writer
of the cipher is not named in the record's extract).

**Technique note:** 50v l.8-10: "I solved it at once; the reason it was quick to solve was the appearance of waw and
alif at the end of the second verse beyond the first; and the jim has two shapes, a slip occurred in them"; 51r l.1-2:
"and I was struck [?] by the beauty of the meaning of the two verses, and I wrote the answer after them, and they are".
The prose on 50v l.11-13 is courtesy (the evil eye, joy).

## 51r.5 (cipher 51r l.5-7; plaintext 51v l.7)

**Arabic (record):**
لست اشكو الزمان اذ جاد منك بدر من بحر علم تدفق

**English:**
- I do not complain of Time, since it has granted me, from you,
- a pearl (durr), from a sea of knowledge that has overflowed.

**Metre:** khafif (the first hemistich scans; the second does not scan cleanly as read).

**Note:** the compiler's answer in one verse ("the single verse") to 50v.1: Time has been generous, since it gave him
the master's letter. بدر is read bi-durrin "a pearl", because the reply (51v.9) takes up "pearls" and "sea"; badr "full
moon" is also possible as written. The master answered with 51v.9, which turns this verse round (لست اشكو becomes
كيف لا اشكر). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 51r l.1-2: the compiler "wrote the answer after them" (in cipher); 51v l.6: "he solved the single
verse and wrote it, and it is". Its answer stands after the answer to the next chain (record).

## 51r.10 (cipher 51r l.10-13, 51v l.1-2; plaintext 51v l.4-5)

**Arabic (record):**
احين علمت انك نور عيني واني لا ارى حتى اراك / جعلت مغيب شخصك عن عياني يغيب كل مخلوق سواك

**English:**
- Is it now, when I have learned that you are the light of my eye,
- and that I do not see until I see you,
- that you have made the absence of your person from my sight
- hide from me every creature but you?

**Metre:** wafir; each verse ends with an alif of itlaq (اراكا, سواكا), which the master wrote apart and the compiler
says he dropped from the cipher.

**Note:** a love verse on absence: without the beloved the poet sees nothing at all. يغيب is read yughayyibu "makes
vanish" (the record notes the dots give بغيب). احين may also be a statement rather than a question ("Now that I have
learned ..., you have made ..."). The compiler sent it for its closeness to the theme of absence; he calls it the
riddle (lughz) of the author of al-Jami' fi l-lugha. That author is not named in the record and is not checked, and
nor is the attribution of the verses.

**Technique note:** 51r l.8-9: "then after this single verse I wrote [?] the riddle of the author of al-Jami' fi
l-lugha, because they are close in meaning to absence and because they are hard, and they are"; 51v l.3: "I dropped
the two alifs of itlaq; he solved it, and it is".

## 51v.9 (cipher 51v l.9-12, red gloss; plaintext 52r l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
كيف لا اشكر دهرا جاد لي منك بحبر / يقذف الدر كبحر زاخر من لج حبر

**English:**
- How should I not thank a Time that granted me,
- in you, a learned man (habr)
- who flings out pearls like a swelling sea
- from a deep of ink (hibr)?

**Metre:** majzu' al-ramal (each line of the record is one verse; both verses rhyme in -br).

**Note:** the master's answer to 51r.5, turning "I do not complain of Time" into "how should I not thank Time". The
rhyme words are the same letters, حبر, read habr/hibr "learned man" and hibr "ink", a full pun (jinas tamm). Which is
which is my choice; "granted me ink from you" for the first is also possible. Poet: the master (the compiler
admires the turn; not checked further).

**Technique note:** 51v l.8: "then he wrote under it and sent it"; 51v l.13: "I solved it quickly for its ease, and
may God reward you, and the good of the [?], and it is"; 52r l.3-6: the compiler admires that the master turned the
meaning of his words لست اشكو into the answer كيف لا اشكر.

## 52v.4 (cipher 52v l.4-7, red gloss; plaintext 52v l.9-10)

**Arabic (record):**
يا ربيعا فوق وصف جامعا من غير حصر / نسبتي صححت منه قابل الكسر بجبر

**English:**
- O spring (rabi') beyond description,
- gathering (jami') all (good) without count (hasr):
- you have made whole (sahhahta) my standing (nisba) with him,
- which was open to breaking (kasr), by setting it (jabr).

**Metre:** majzu' al-ramal, in the rhyme -br of the master's 51v.9, which this answers.

**Note:** the compiler's short answer, with "five words punning on the craft of arithmetic" (his heading). The
candidates are jami' (jam', addition), hasr (enumeration), nisba (ratio), sahhahta (sahih, a whole number; taṣḥīḥ,
reducing to whole numbers), kasr (fraction) and jabr (restoration, algebra). That is six candidates, and the compiler
does not say which five he meant. Kasr/jabr is also "fracture / bone-setting". منه "with him (or: from it)" is
rendered loosely. Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 52v l.1-3: "I wrote the answer briefly, since it could not be avoided, admitting my evident
inability, and I put in one verse five words punning on the craft of arithmetic, and it is this"; 52v l.8: "he solved
it quickly despite the strangeness of its words and sent it, and it is".

## 53r.3 (cipher 53r l.3-5, red gloss; plaintext 53r l.7)

**Arabic (record):**
قيد ودي لديك مطلق ما اوليتني يا ولي كل على

**English:**
- The fetter (qayd) of my love for you
- is the unbound (mutlaq) bounty you have bestowed on me, O patron of every high deed ('ula).

**Metre:** khafif (the first hemistich scans; the second does not as read).

**Note:** a compliment built on qayd / mutlaq, "fettered / unbound" (also "restricted / absolute" in grammar and law):
his love is held fast by the master's unrestricted gifts. The compiler says he meant كل على as "every high deed", but
the solver read على as the name 'Ali, "O patron of all, 'Ali" (53r l.8-9). The verse uses only twelve different
letters, which is why the compiler expected it to be hard. Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 53r l.1-2: "then I wrote for him, may God seal (his life) with good"; 53r l.6: "he solved it quickly
and sent it, although there are only twelve letters in it, and it is"; 53r l.8-9: the compiler meant it in single
meanings, but the solver took it as a proper name (علي).

## 53v.6 (cipher 53v l.6-8; plaintext 53v l.13)

**Arabic (record):**
حمدك والمدح داب ذي ادب يقوم منه ببعض ما يجب

**English:**
- Praising you (and praise is the habit of the cultivated man)
- discharges some part of what is owed.

**Metre:** munsarih (the second hemistich with a rare doubly shortened middle foot).

**Note:** a compliment with two plays on sound: hamd / madh (praise, the same three letters in another order) and
da'b / adab ("habit" / "culture"). The compiler's "jinas in it" refers to this. Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 53v l.5: "then I wrote for him, may God make his shade ample"; 53v l.9: "he solved it and sent it,
in spite of its few letters and the jinas in it"; 53v l.12-13: "... and the verse" introduces the plaintext.

## 54r.2 (cipher 54r l.2-5; plaintext 54r l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
كم ساحة شرفت غداة حللتها وقضية قد اشكلت فحللتها

**English:**
- How many a courtyard has been honoured on the morning you alighted in it (halaltaha),
- and how many a case had grown obscure, and you solved it (halaltaha)!

**Metre:** kamil.

**Note:** praise of the master as the solver of hard cases, on the double sense of halla, "to alight / settle" and "to
solve" (a full jinas), apt in a book of decipherments. The compiler takes the verse up in his note ("by God, I spoke
the truth"). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 54r l.1: "then I wrote for him, may God satisfy Islam and the Muslims with his long life"; 54r
l.6-7: "by God, I spoke the truth in this: this is one of the obscure cases that he solved as he solved others, and he
sent it quickly, and it is".

## 54v.1 (cipher 54v l.1-4, red gloss; plaintext 54v l.6-7)

**Arabic (record):**
جزيل علم وحجى ذو منطق محجاج / جعلت نهجي مدحه وما له من هاج

**English:**
- (A man) abundant in knowledge and understanding,
- with a power of speech that wins every argument (mihjaj):
- I have made praising him my road,
- and he has no one to lampoon him (hajin).

**Metre:** rajaz (each line of the record one verse, rhyming in -aj).

**Note:** two verses from the compiler's own poem, in which he bound himself to put jims at the start, middle and end
of each verse (جزيل, حجى, محجاج; جعلت, نهجي, هاج). There are only three alifs (محجاج, وما, هاج), and he counts two of
them as alifs of ta'sis (his term). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 54r l.10-13: "then I wrote for him two verses from a poem of mine in which I bound myself to jims at
the beginning, middle and end ... and in the two verses there are only three alifs, two of them alifs of ta'sis, so in
reality there is only one alif in them"; 54v l.5: "he solved it and said that nothing harder than these two had come
to him, and they are".

## 54v.9 (cipher 54v l.9-13, red gloss; plaintext 55r l.2-3)

**Arabic (record):**
نديمي قم فاسقني بكاس كبير هني / ودع كاسنا اطلسا ولا تسقني مع دني

**English:**
- My boon companion, rise and give me to drink
- from a large and wholesome cup;
- and leave our cup unadorned (atlas, tentative),
- and do not give me drink in company with a base man.

**Metre:** majzu' al-mutaqarib (fa'ulun fa'ulun fa'al per half-line; three half-lines scan, the first, نديمي قم
فاسقني, does not as read).

**Note:** a wine piece by the master. اطلسا is the least clear word: atlas can mean "smooth, plain (unengraved)",
"dark, dusky" or "worn", and none is certain here. هني is hani' "wholesome" without its hamza, and دني is daniyy
"base". Poet: the master (the correspondent's cipher); whether the verses are his own is not stated.

**Technique note:** 54v l.8: "then he wrote, may God make his standing great" (the master's cipher); 55r l.1: "I solved
it quickly and sent it, and it is".

## 55r.7 (cipher 55r l.7-12; plaintext 55v l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
فعلام تعتمدين قتل متيم افما نهاك يا سعاد نهاك / حللت قتلي وهو غير محلل فبطرفك الفتاك من افتاك

**English:**
- Why, then, do you set out to kill a man enslaved by love?
- Did your reason (nuhaki) not forbid you (nahaki), O Su'ad?
- You have made my killing lawful, though it is not lawful:
- by your murderous (fattak) glance, who gave you that ruling (aftaki)?

**Metre:** kamil (the second hemistich does not scan with يا سعاد as read; see the suspected errors).

**Note:** a love complaint in legal terms: the beloved has declared the lover's blood lawful, and he asks who gave her
the fatwa (aftaki). The plays are nahaki / nuhaki and fattak / aftaki. The compiler says the original wording was
حللت قتل الصب وهو محرم ("you made lawful the killing of the lover while he is in ihram", i.e. in pilgrim sanctity,
when killing is forbidden). He changed it to قتلي وهو غير محلل to avoid the article and make the solving harder, and
the cipher has the changed wording. Poet: the compiler presents the verses as his (not checked).

**Technique note:** 55r l.4-6: "then I wrote him two verses [?] with jinas in them; of them I changed «you made lawful
the killing of the lover while he is in ihram» and wrote «you made lawful my killing though it is not lawful», because
of the alif-lam in it, to make it hard to solve, and it is"; 55r l.13: "he solved them quickly and sent them, and they
are".

## 55v.5 (cipher 55v l.5-10; plaintext 55v l.12-13, 56r l.1)

**Arabic (record):**
شهود ودي تودي وهي صادقة وحاكم القلب يدري كمابه [?] حكما / هب انني مدع قد غاب شاهده اما ضميرك يقضي في سما علما

**English:**
- The witnesses to my love give their testimony (tu'addi), and it is true,
- and the judge of the heart knows [?] (as) arbiter.
- Suppose I am a claimant whose witness is absent:
- does your own conscience not judge [في سما علما, not understood]?

**Metre:** basit (the second hemistich scans only if كمابه is read without its ك; see the suspected errors).

**Note:** a love plea in the language of the law court: witnesses, testimony, judge, claimant, absent witness,
conscience as judge. تودي is read tu'addi "give (testimony)" (ada' al-shahada), which also echoes wuddi "my love".
كمابه [?] is as written and its word division is unsure (record), so the half-line is left open. في سما علما is not
understood as written ("in a height, a sign"?); see the suspected errors. The compiler changed words so that the
definite article occurs only once (القلب), which the record confirms. Poet: the compiler presents the verses as his
(not checked).

**Technique note:** 55v l.3-4: "then I wrote him two verses [?] in which I changed words so that the definite article is
not repeated, and knotted (enciphered) them for him, to make them hard to solve, and they are"; 55v l.11: "he solved
that quickly and sent it, and it is".

## 56r.4 (cipher 56r l.4-8; plaintext 56r l.10-11)

**Arabic (record):**
ولاحسن الاسماع حديثكم مشافهة يملى علي فانقل / وامري موقوف عليك وليس لي على احد الا عليك معول

**English:**
- There is nothing fair (hasan) but hearing (sama') your discourse (hadith)
- face to face (mushafaha), dictated to me (yumla) so that I transmit it (anqul).
- My case is suspended (mawquf) on you, and I have
- no one to rely on (mu'awwal) but you.

**Metre:** tawil (scanning ولا حسن as wa-la hasanun and وامري as wa-amriya).

**Note:** two verses by Ibn Farh al-Ishbili (named by the compiler) from his love poem in the terms of hadith
science: hasan (a grade of hadith), sama' (hearing from the teacher), hadith, mushafaha (face-to-face transmission),
imla' (dictation), naql (transmission), mawquf (a report stopped at a Companion), mu'awwal. I read the record's
ولاحسن الاسماع as ولا حسن الا سماع (word division only). Which poem these verses come from is not checked (record).

**Technique note:** 56r l.2-3: "then I wrote him two verses from verses by Ibn Farh al-Ishbili, [?] in them the science
of hadith; I selected [?] them, and they are"; 56r l.9: "he solved that quickly and sent it, and it is".

## 56r.13 (cipher 56r l.13, 56v l.1-2; plaintext 56v l.5)

ارض لمن غاب عنك غيبته فذاك ذنب عقابه فيه

**English:**
- Be content, for one who stays away from you, with his staying away:
- that is a sin whose punishment lies within it.

**Metre:** munsarih.

**Note:** the master's reproach to the compiler, who had kept away for a while, in a well-known verse (record): staying

me for it"; 56v l.3-4: "I solved it quickly, praise God, at once; God Most High made it easy, despite its brevity; but

## 56v.8 (cipher 56v l.8-10; plaintext 56v l.12)

**Arabic (record):**
نديمك في كل وقت جذل ومستنصر بك ما ان خذل

**English:**
- Your boon companion is glad (jadhil) at every time,
- and whoever seeks your help is never forsaken (khudhil).

**Metre:** mutaqarib.

**Note:** the compiler's own answer to 56r.13, a compliment instead of an excuse. The rhyme words jadhil / khudhil
differ only in the dot of their first letter (ج / خ, the same shape). 56v l.13 glosses the rare word: "al-jadhal
is joy, and jadhlan is like farhan (glad)". Only two alifs (ما, ان), as the compiler says. Poet: the compiler ("of my
own composition").

**Technique note:** 56v l.6-7: "I wrote it (the decipherment) out, and wrote under it this cipher, and sent it into his
noble presence; it is its answer, but of my own composition, and I had begun [?] with it"; 56v l.11: "he solved it
despite its brevity and strangeness, there being only two alifs in it, and sent it, and it is".

## 57r.2 (cipher 57r l.2-4; plaintext 57r l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
حاشاك ان يثنيك عني كاشح فبصدق وعدك يطمين ضميري

**English:**
- God forbid that a hidden enemy (kashih) should turn you away from me:
- in the truth of your promise my heart finds rest.

**Metre:** kamil (scanning يثنيك as yathnika and يطمين as yatma'innu).

**Note:** the compiler asks the master not to listen to slanderers. يطمين is yatma'innu written without its hamza
(record). The master pointed out two things in his solution. The compiler had written shin in two forms (in حاشاك and
كاشح). And فبصدق showed he had understood the compiler's intent, though "the fa ought to be a waw" (وبصدق). Poet:
the compiler.

**Technique note:** 56v l.13-57r l.1: "and I wrote to him ... may God [make great] his standing, and I had intended [?] a
thing that did not come about"; 57r l.5-8: "he solved it and pointed out that I had erred and made the shin two forms,
and pointed out that he understood my intent [?] by his (my) words fa-bi-sidq, even though there is some lapse of
manners on my part in it, but not everyone understands it; and he wrote it"; 57r l.10-11: "and the fa ought to be a
waw".

## 57v.4 (cipher 57v l.4-10, red gloss; plaintext 57v l.12-13, 58r l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
على وجهه ما الجمال مرقرق يزيد اذا رددت طرفك رونقا / تبدي فاخفى صبر عشاقه وكم عيون بدمع حين اشرق اشرقا

**English:**
- On his face the water of beauty (ma' al-jamal) shimmers,
- growing in lustre each time you turn your gaze back to it.
- He appeared (tabadda), and the patience of his lovers vanished;
- and how many eyes, when he rose (ashraqa), "rose" (ashraqa) with tears!

**Metre:** tawil (with the written رونقا; the cipher's رنقا is a syllable short).

**Note:** two verses from the compiler's own poem on a beautiful youth. ما is ma' "water" without the hamza (record);
"the water of beauty" is the bloom of the face. The last words make a jinas: ashraqa "he rose (like the sun)" and a
second اشرقا, which I take as a play on shariqa "to choke (with tears)". My rendering keeps the echo. The compiler says
(58r l.3-8) that he changed غرب to اشرقا to make the jinas hard to solve, so the original probably had a rising /
setting contrast (ashraqa / aghraba). The master preferred حققت to رددت ("each time you fix your gaze") in his solution.
The cipher and the red gloss have رنقا where the written text has رونقا (see the suspected errors). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 57v l.2-3: "then I wrote for him, may God make his standing great, two verses from a poem of mine,
which I selected"; 57v l.11: "he solved it quickly and sent it, and chose that it be حققت instead of رددت"; 58r l.3-8:
remarks on iham and jinas (the change from غرب to اشرقا).

## 58r.11 (cipher 58r l.11-13, 58v l.1-3; plaintext 58v l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
مولى سمي جدوده ونجوده كالليث في وثباته وثباته / ذو راحة وكفت ندى وكفت ردى فقضت بهلك عداته وعداته

**English:**
- A lord lofty (samiyy) in his forebears (judud) and his valour (nujud, tentative),
- like the lion in his leaps (wathabatihi) and his steadfastness (wa-thabatihi);
- with a palm that rained down (wakafat) bounty and warded off (wa-kafat) ruin,
- and so carried out the ruin of his enemies ('udatihi) and (the fulfilment of) his promises (wa-'idatihi).

**Metre:** kamil.

**Note:** a panegyric built on paired near-homonyms: wathabatihi / wa-thabatihi, wakafat / wa-kafat (both written
وكفت; the metre wants wa-kafat, not wa-kaffat, for the second), 'udatihi / wa-'idatihi. The last line uses qada for
both "decree (the destruction of)" and "fulfil (promises)". سمي may also be "namesake (of his forebears)". نجوده
(nujud, plural of najd "upland", or "help, valour") is rendered tentatively. The compiler explains that he made these
two verses by joining the first hemistich of the first to the second hemistich of the second. He quotes the displaced
hemistichs (58v l.10-11): فالمجد من وصفاته وصفاته ("glory is among his qualities ..."), كالبحر في ارواءه ورواية
("like the sea in giving drink (irwa') and in narration (riwaya)"), and a waw in place of the kaf of كالليث. The cipher
has the recombined verses (record). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 58r l.8-10: "then I wrote him two verses; I set the first hemistich of the first on the second
hemistich of the second, and followed it with the third [?], and there was (little?) of the definite article in them;
here is their cipher"; 58v l.4-7: "he solved them and sent them ... and he wrote سمي with the ba [?], as I had erred,
and its cipher ... and I understood that, and they are"; 58v l.10-11: the original hemistichs (above).

## 59r.2 (cipher 59r l.2-6; plaintext 59r l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
كم حرقة لي في الحشا من ولد لي قد نشا / كانشاء رشده فما انشا كما نشا

**English:**
- How much burning I have in my entrails
- from a son of mine who has grown up (nasha'),
- [كانشاء, as written] his right conduct (rushd), yet he did not develop (ansha')
- as he grew (nasha').

**Metre:** majzu' al-rajaz in the first verse; the second does not scan as read.

**Note:** two verses by Najm al-Din al-Qazwini, author of al-Hawi (named by the compiler), a father's grief over a son
who grew up without growing in sense. The play is on nasha'a / ansha'a (to grow up / to bring forth, also to compose).
The written كانشاء is joined, but the cipher's groups suggest كان plus a three-letter word (perhaps نشا). The first
half of the second verse is therefore left untranslated as written. فما انشا could also be "he did not compose (well)",
a jab at a son in the chancery craft; that is my guess. The solver's remark that the first verse is "two lines and the
second one line" refers to how the verses are laid out (record). Poet: Najm al-Din al-Qazwini (compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 58v l.12-59r l.1: "then I wrote for our master, the excellent shaykh Shihab al-Din Ahmad the
mutarjim, two verses by Najm al-Din al-Qazwini, author of al-Hawi, may God have mercy on him, and he showed them to
the master referred to, and they are"; 59r l.7: "he solved them and sent them back, and they are"; 59r l.10-12: the
solver wrote under them that it hardly occurs to anyone that the first verse is two lines and the second one line.

## 59v.7 (cipher 59v l.7-8, red gloss; plaintext 60r l.1)

**Arabic (record):**
مودته تدوم لكل هول وهل كل مودته تدوم

**English:**
- His affection endures through every terror;
- and is everyone one whose affection endures?

**Metre:** wafir (scanning كل as kullun).

**Note:** a palindrome. The verse reads the same letter by letter from either end, and it is built of seven letters
(م و د ت ه ل ك) and, in effect, two words (مودته / تدوم reversed, لكل هول / وهل كل), as the master says. Poet: not
checked.

**Technique note:** 59v l.5-6: "then I wrote for him, may God make his standing great, a verse [?] read forwards and
backwards, and it is"; 59v l.9-13: "he was slow over it for days because of its difficulty, then solved it, wrote it
and sent it, and with it an apology for the delay: this verse is four words, each two of them with their letters
reversed in the other, so how can it be solved when in truth they are two words? By God he spoke the truth, for it is
built of seven letters, and it is".

## 60r.5 (cipher 60r l.5-10; plaintext 60r l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
اه واشوقا لمن قد بدلوا صفو عيشي بعدهم بالكدر / كلما اشتقت تمنيتهم ضاع عمري في المنى واعمري

**English:**
- Ah, how I long (wa-shawqa) for those who, once they were gone,
- changed the clearness of my life into murk!
- Whenever I long for them I wish them back;
- my life has been wasted in wishes. Alas for my life!

**Metre:** ramal (scanning واعمري as wa-'umuri).

**Note:** a lament for absent friends. The piece was enciphered by Kamal al-Din with the master's own signs and
solved by the compiler. واشوقا is read wa-shawqa ("oh, my longing"), as the cipher's groups divide it (وا شوقا). The
last words echo: 'umri "my life" and wa-'umuri "alas for my life". Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 60r l.2-4: "and al-Mawla Kamal al-Din, son of the qadi Jamal al-Din, son of al-Shihab Mahmud,
brought me a cipher that he had enciphered himself with the signs of the (master) referred to, may God make his
standing great; its form:"; 60r l.11: "I solved it and sent it to al-Mawla Kamal al-Din, and it is".

## 60v.2 (cipher 60v l.2-8; plaintext 60v l.11-12)

**Arabic (record):**
ان كنت تريدنا فدع لذاتك لا تخطر غير ذكرنا في ذاتك / ضيعت بطول هجرنا اوقاتك كم فاتك من وصالنا كم فاتك

**English:**
- If you want us, give up your pleasures (ladhdhatik);
- let nothing but the thought of us cross your inmost self (dhatik).
- You have wasted your hours (awqatik) by staying away from us so long:
- how much of union with us has passed you by (fatak), how much has passed you by!

**Metre:** dubayt (the rubai metre; all four hemistichs scan, rhyming -atak).

**Note:** a dubayt in the voice of the beloved (or of God, in a mystical reading; not checked). The compiler calls it
"two old, well-known dubayt verses". The plays are ladhdhatik / dhatik, and the repeated kam fatak. لا تخطر is
rendered as a wish; the metre reads it la takhturu. Kamal al-Din could not solve it, and the master did. Poet: not
checked.

**Technique note:** 60v l.1: "and I wrote under it two old, well-known dubayt verses"; 60v l.9-10: "it reached him and he
could not solve it; I put it before the master referred to, may God make his shade ample, and he solved them, and they
are"; 60v l.13: "then the master asked me to write something knotted (enciphered) that would be hard to solve, to test
himself" (this introduces the chain on 61r, outside this range).

## Suspected Arabic errors

None of these readings has been changed above; each is a suspicion for the next look at the master.

1. **57v.4** رونقا / رنقا: the tawil rhyme foot needs raw-na-qa (u-u-), so the written رونقا is right, and the cipher
   and red gloss (رنقا, 4 signs) have dropped the waw. This error is in the cipher, not in the written plaintext.
2. **55r.7** افما نهاك يا سعاد نهاك: the kamil hemistich does not scan with يا سعاد (ki-ya-su-'a-du is no kamil
   foot), though every other hemistich of the piece scans. A word may be misread, or the compiler's rewording
   ("I changed ...") may have broken the metre. Moderate.
3. **55v.5** كمابه [?]: the basit hemistich scans exactly if read يدري ما به حكما (without the ك). The count fits
   كمابه as written (record), so if this is an error it is in the compiler's text, not the transcription. Also في سما
   علما: not understood. فيما علما "according to what it knows" (the judge ruling by his own knowledge) would fit the
   legal imagery, but it has one letter fewer, against the exact count. Moderate.
4. **59r.2** كانشاء: the cipher's 3+3 groups and the second group's signs (looking like نشا, record) suggest كان نشا
   or similar where the written text has كانشاء. The second verse does not scan in majzu' rajaz as read. Strong for
   "something is wrong", weak for what.
5. **49v.2** خدك: "beside your cheek I am cotton" barely makes sense, and حدك "your edge (blade)" would (my guess). قطنة
   / فطنة is open (record). The last hemistich (وان ذكرهم [?]) is a syllable short in tawil, and جوهرا (accusative)
   has no clear construction. The whole of l.12 rests on the count (record).
6. **54v.9** نديمي قم فاسقني: a syllable too many for the majzu' mutaqarib half-line; the other three half-lines scan.
   One of قم / ف may be intrusive. Weak.
7. **53r.3** ما اوليتني يا ولي كل على: the second hemistich does not scan in khafif as read. The verse is built under
   a 12-letter constraint, which may excuse it. Weak.
8. **51r.5** بدر من بحر علم تدفق: the second hemistich does not scan in khafif as read. Weak.
9. **50v.1** رسايلكم الله لنا: the first hemistich scans only with a rare shortened foot, and the phrase is
   elliptical. يعود is written يعو with د above (record). Weak.
10. **57r.2** يثنيك scans only as yathnika (licence for yathniyaka), and يطمين stands for يطمئن (record). The solver
    himself says فبصدق should be وبصدق. Weak.
11. **51r.10** يغيب is dotted بغيب on the master (record); the sense and metre want yughayyibu. علمت may be علمنا
    (record); علمت scans and fits.
12. **60v.2** ذكرنا is dotted ذكريا (record); ذكرنا scans and fits the sense.
