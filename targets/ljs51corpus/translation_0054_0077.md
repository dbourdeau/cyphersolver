## 25r.4 (cipher 25r l.4-11; plaintext 25v l.1-3)

**Arabic (record):**
والله ما ندري اذا ما فاتنا طلب لك من الذي تطلب [?] / ولقد ضربنا في البلاد فلم نجد احدا سواك الى المكارم ينسب

**English:**
- By God, we do not know, when a request to you [lit. "for you"] escapes us,
- who it is that you [?] seek.
- We have travelled through the lands, and found
- no one but you to whom noble deeds are ascribed.

**Metre:** kamil (mutafa'ilun); see the suspected errors for two words that do not scan as read.

**Note:** praise of a patron as the only source of generosity. Poet: not checked. On تطلب [?]: نتطلب ("we should seek") is the alternative the record raises; "you seek" is the reading as written.

**Technique note:** 25r l.3: "and a man from the people of al-Ramla (or al-Rahba [?]), called [name left blank], wrote (in cipher) ..."; 25r l.12: "I solved it quickly because of its ease, and it is".

## 25v.4 (cipher 25v l.4-10; plaintext 25v l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
قصدتك حين ضاق الامر لما رايت الدهر انشب مخلبيه / علمت ان غيرك ليس يرجى وانك كهف من ياوي اليه

**English:**
- I came to you when things grew hard, when
- I saw that Fate had sunk in its two talons.
- I knew that no one but you is to be hoped for,
- and that you are the cave (shelter) of everyone who takes refuge there.

**Metre:** wafir.

**Note:** a plea for help addressed to a patron, with Fate shown as a bird of prey. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 25v l.4 opens with "and of that kind"; 25v l.11: "I solved that because of its ease, and it is".

## 26r.1 (cipher 26r l.1-7; plaintext 26r l.9-10)

لعبدك وعد قد تقدم ذكره فاوله اجر وآخره شكر / وقد جمعت فيك المكارم كلها فما لك عن تاخير مكرمة عذر

**English:**
- Your servant holds a promise that was mentioned before:
- its beginning is a reward, and its end is thanks.
- All the noble qualities are gathered in you,
- so you have no excuse for putting off a noble deed.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** a polite reminder to a patron to keep his promise. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 26r l.1 opens "and of that kind likewise"; 26r l.8: "I solved that quickly, and it is".

## 26r.12 (cipher 26r l.12-13, 26v l.1-5; plaintext 26v l.7-9)

**English:**
- Short of your door I have no door to take refuge in,

## 26v.11 (cipher 26v l.11-13, 27r l.1-5; plaintext 27r l.7-8)

**Arabic (record):**
ولقد نذرت لين رايتك سالما لاصوم لله الكريم شهورا / حذرا عليك من الزمان وصرفه حتى تعود مويدا منصورا

**English:**

## 27v.3 (cipher 27v l.3-10; plaintext 27v l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
يا نفس ذا الكبر يا من اين الست من مخرج السبيلين / ابوك بالامس كان من حماء وجيفة انت بعد يومين

**English:**
- O soul, (why) this pride? O, from where (do you come)?
- Are you not from the outlet of the two passages?
- Your father, yesterday, was (made) of black mud,
- and a carcass is what you will be after two days.

**Metre:** not identified (the second verse scans as munsarih; the first hemistich does not scan as read).

**Note:** a moral verse against pride: man comes from base matter (the "two passages", i.e. the body's lower openings, and Adam's clay, hama') and ends as carrion. Poet: not checked. يا من اين is rendered literally; the phrase is doubtful.

**Technique note:** 27v l.2: "he (Nur al-Din al-Baghdadi) had written me two ciphered pieces before that; one of them ..."; 27v l.11: "I solved it quickly, and it is".

## 28r.2 (cipher 28r l.2-9; plaintext 28r l.11-12)

دع الاعتراض فما الامر لك وليس بحكمك جرى الفلك / ولا تشرك بالله في حكمه فمن خاض لجة بحر هلك

**Metre:** not identified (the opening is mutaqarib-like, but the lines do not scan regularly as read).

## 28v.1 (cipher 28v l.1-6; plaintext 29r l.1-2)

**Arabic (record; the two verses on 29r l.1-2):**
اذا دكت جبال الصبر دكا ترى مني فوادا مستقرا / سوآ عندي يوماك [?] دهري فكن خلا واما شيت خمرا

**English:**
- When the mountains of endurance are crushed flat,
- you will see in me a heart that stays steady.
- The two days [?] of my fortune are the same to me,
- so be vinegar, or, if you like, be wine.

**Metre:** wafir.

**Note:** steadiness in good and bad fortune (the "two days": a good day and a bad day, vinegar and wine). The compiler places it in a qasida of the vizier Ala al-Din Ata-Malik al-Juwayni. The record reads يوماك with a doubtful ك; "the two days of my fortune" reads it without the ك.

**Technique note:** 28r l.13: "and his (Nur al-Din's) companion wrote to me"; 28v l.7-8: "I solved it quickly; it is from a qasida of the vizier Ala al-Din Ata-Malik al-Juwayni, which begins" (four opening verses follow on 28v l.9-12, quoted for context); 28v l.13: "and the two ciphered verses are among what (follows)".

## 29r.4 (cipher 29r l.4-8; plaintext 29r l.11-12)

**Arabic (record):**
ما ثم الا ما يريد فدع همومك واطرح / واترك خواطرك التي [?] شغلت فوادك تنشرح

**English:**
- There is nothing there but what He wills:
- so leave your cares, and cast (them) off;
- and abandon the [?] thoughts of yours
- that have occupied your heart: you will be at ease.

**Metre:** majzu' al-kamil (two mutafa'ilun to the hemistich).

**Note:** resignation to God's will as a remedy for worry. The compiler names the author as shaykh Abd al-Aziz al-Dabrini (the nisba is doubtful). The wordplay is on the first word: read ثم it gives "there is nothing there but what He wills"; read تم it gives "nothing comes to pass but what He wills". The compiler says both readings work.

**Technique note:** 29r l.3: "and (someone, unnamed) wrote to me"; 29r l.9-10: "I solved it quickly; the two verses are by shaykh Abd al-Aziz al-Dabrini [?], and they are"; 29r l.13-29v l.1: "I think he wrote it with the three-dotted tha', following the speech of the Egyptians, and it also has a meaning with the two-dotted ta'".

## 29v.2 (cipher 29v l.2-8; plaintext 29v l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
ارى سرج الجزيره من بعيد كلحاظ تغازل من تغازل / كان كواكب الجوزا حطت وافرغت المنازل في المنازل

**English:**
- I see the lamps of the Island from afar,
- like glances flirting with whoever flirts (with them),
- as if the stars of Gemini had come down
- and the (lunar) mansions had emptied themselves into the dwellings.

**Metre:** wafir (كلحاظ scans as كألحاظ, "like glances").

**Note:** a description of the lit-up Island at night (al-Jazira, perhaps the Nile island of Cairo; not checked). The wordplay is on manazil, "lunar mansions" and "houses". Poet: not checked. The record notes that وافرغت could be read otherwise.

**Technique note:** 29v l.1: "and shaykh Shihab al-Din the cipherer wrote to me"; 29v l.9-11: "I solved it quickly in his presence; his excellency al-Ala'i copied it, solved it quickly too and brought (the solution) in his own noble hand, and it is".

## 30r.4 (cipher 30r l.4-11; plaintext 30r l.13, 30v l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
وجاريه حسبناها غلاما بفتره مقله ونشاط قد / راها الشيخ فانبعث قواه وعاد الى الشباب المستجد / واتبع ردفها نظرا فقالت ان الشيخ من اكناف نجد

**English:**
- A slave-girl whom we took for a boy,
- for the languor of her eye and the briskness of her figure:
- the old man saw her, and his powers were roused,
- and he returned to youth made new;
- he followed her buttocks with his gaze, and she said:
- "The old man is from the outskirts (aknaf) of Najd."

**Metre:** wafir (the last hemistich does not scan as read).

**Note:** a bawdy epigram. It turns on aknaf Najd ("the sides / outskirts of the highland"), with Najd (high ground) as a figure for the rump (the exact pun is not checked). Al-Ala'i answered with the pun rather than the solution: "our lord the shaykh is from the middle of Najd, not from its outskirts". Poet: not checked. The record's line division splits qadd ("figure") as قد / راها; I have read it as the end of the first verse.

**Technique note:** 30r l.1-3: "shaykh Shihab al-Din the cipherer also wrote two rhymed pieces, one for me and the other for his excellency al-Ala'i"; 30r l.12: "I solved that in his presence, quickly because of its ease, and it is"; 30v l.3-6: "as for my lord al-Ala'i, he did not write out the verses; the cipherer wrote for us in his hand 'our lord the shaykh is from the middle of Najd, not from its outskirts', and so it was known that he had solved it".

## 31r.11 (cipher 31r l.11-14, 31v l.1-3; plaintext 31v l.5-6)

**Arabic (record):**
مرضت لله قوم ما فيهم من جفاني / عادوا وعادوا وعادوا على اختلاف المعاني

**English:**
- I fell ill: God bless a people, not one of whom shunned me;
- they came back (ʿadu), and came back, and came back, each in a different sense.

**Metre:** mujtathth (majzu').

**Note:** a pun (jinas) on ʿadu, used three times in three senses that the compiler explains (31v l.7-8): first "they visited (the sick)", second "they kept up ties (sila)", third "they returned". The compiler wrote it himself to test al-Ala'i.

**Technique note:** 31r l.9-10: "I wrote my lord al-Ala'i two verses with a pun in them, to test him with in solving"; 31v l.4: "he solved them and sent them back, and they are"; 31v l.7-8: "the first from visiting the sick, the second from keeping ties, the third from returning".

## 31v.11 (cipher 31v l.11-13, 32r l.1-4 and margin; plaintext 32r l.10-13)

**Arabic (record):**
قد قد قد حبيبي حبل صبري كسر وسلسل الريق / سلسل اذ اسرني اسر / ولولو الثغر بسامه حصر بي حصر فهل سبيل الورده / وضم الخصر

**English (tentative; colloquial/strophic):**
- My beloved's figure (qadd) has indeed (qad) cut (qadd) the rope of my patience: it broke (it);
- and the cool sweet water (salsal) of his saliva / chained (salsala) me, when he took me captive, a captivity;
- and the pearls of his mouth, its smiling one, held me tight (or: struck me dumb), a tightness (ḥaṣar);
- so is there a way to the rose / and to embracing the waist?

**Metre:** not identified (it looks like strophic or colloquial verse, perhaps mawaliya-like; not checked).

**Note:** a love piece full of badiʿ: qad/qadd/qadd, salsal/salsala, asar/asr, ḥaṣar/ḥaṣar. The rose is presumably the cheek. ولولو = لؤلؤ "pearls"; حصر can be "confinement" or "tongue-tied speechlessness". The record says the word الى may be understood before الورده. Author: the compiler reports that Ibn al-Q... the reckoner had ciphered it (name doubtful); the poet is not checked.

**Technique note:** 31v l.8-10: "Hasan ibn al-Qalanisi of Mosul, naqib of ... [?], brought me a cipher without separators; he said that Ibn al-Qayyani [?] the reckoner had ciphered it, and it is"; 32r l.5-9: "I solved it, marvelled at it and rejoiced at solving it; I mentioned it to the master, who told me that Hana'allah [?] and the said Hasan had written him a cipher in its metre, claiming that he knew how to solve ciphers, so he solved it and wrote out Hana'allah's copy in his own hand; I understood that the man meant me to solve it for him, together with its wordplay, and it is".

## 32v.3 (cipher 32v l.3-9; plaintext 32v l.11-12)

**Arabic (record):**
جز بوادي عقيق دمعي وسايل فهو جار على الخد وسايل / انا في بابهم فقير وسايل فهم للاله خير وسايل

**English:**
- Pass by the valley of the carnelian (ʿAqiq) of my tears and ask (wa-sa'il),
- for it runs down the cheek, flowing (sa'il);
- at their door I am a poor man, a beggar (sa'il),
- for they are, toward God, the best of means (wasa'il).

**Metre:** khafif.

**Note:** the compiler's own verse, built on a four-way pun on وسايل (and ask / flowing / beggar / means). Wadi al-ʿAqiq (a valley near Medina in poetry) is played against ʿaqiq "carnelian", i.e. red tears. Who "they" are is not stated (presumably holy figures; not checked).

**Technique note:** 32v l.1-2: "I wrote to my lord al-Ala'i, of my own verse"; 32v l.10: "he solved it quickly despite its difficulty and its pun, and I marvelled at that; it is"; 32v l.13-33r l.1: "and the two letters in place of the letter: if so, the better reading is wasa'il ma'rifa, so that their love for God is the best of means" (a note on reading the last rhyme word).

## 33r.4 (cipher 33r l.4-11; plaintext 33r l.13-14 and 33v l.1-2)

**Arabic (record; both verses, as corrected):**
منشور فضلك بين الناس مشهور ولفظك الدر منظوم / ومنثور / ان كنت وافيت مصرا والعراق فجي بمثل قول الحاجري / النوم منكور

**English:**
- The patent (manshur) of your merit is famous among people,
- and your speech is pearls, strung (as verse) and scattered (as prose).
- If you have come to Egypt and to Iraq, then bring
- the like of al-Hajiri's saying: "Sleep is denied (mankur)."

**Metre:** basit (the first verse scans; the second hemistich of the second verse does not scan as read).

**Note:** praise, then a riddle (uhjiyya) that quotes al-Hajiri (named in the text). Al-Hajiri is presumably the Irbil poet; that identification is not checked. Puns: manshur / manthur and manzum / manthur. The compiler's explanation of the riddle (33v l.3-5) turns on al-kara "sleep" and mankur taken as "reversed", with the Tigris of Baghdad and the Nile of Egypt; the record reads it only in part, so the answer is not established here.

**Technique note:** 33r l.2-3: "I wrote (it) to shaykh Shihab al-Din the cipherer; he showed it to the master (al-Ala'i), who solved it, and it is"; 33r l.12: "he solved it and solved the riddle, and it is"; 33v l.3-5: "sleep is al-kara, and the reversed (mankur) ... in the Tigris of Baghdad ... in the Nile of Egypt ..." (read only in part).

## 33v.6 (cipher 33v l.6-8; plaintext 33v l.10-11)

**Arabic (record):**
حلي عملك حقا لكل سمع تشنف / فخد ونظر مقابلي في عاشق حال عنف

**English (tentative):**
- The ornament of your work is truly, for every ear, an earring (tushannif);
- so take, and look at my counterpart in a lover whose state is violence.

**Metre:** not identified.

**Note:** al-Ala'i's riddle (uhjiyya). Its answer is the next cipher (33v.12), and the riddle is not solved here. The words are unpointed: حلي can be ḥaly/ḥuliyy ("ornament") or ḥulla; فخد can be fa-khudh ("so take") or fakhdh ("thigh"); ونظر is taken as wa-nzur ("and look"). The record calls the sense not secure. Source: not checked.

**Technique note:** 33v l.4-5: "then my lord al-Ala'i wrote me after that a riddle, also in cipher"; 33v l.9: "I solved it quickly, solved the riddle, and wrote it and its answer".

## 33v.12 (cipher 33v l.12-13, 34r l.1; plaintext 34r l.4-5)

**Arabic (record):**
من ذا يلوم كيما اذا سلا او تخنف / ارقى السلالم اهوى فما عساي اصنف

**English (tentative):**
- Who is it that blames, so that, if he forgets (his love) or takhannuf [sense unclear] ...?
- I climb the ladders, I fall (or: I love), so what might I compose?

**Metre:** not identified.

**Note:** the compiler's answer to the riddle 33v.6, which he says he made up and which "had no reality". A likely pun is ahwa, "I fall" or "I love". The compiler's remark on 34r l.6 (bi-ʿunf chosen instead of takhannuf) shows that the two pieces share their rhyme words. كيما and تخنف are not understood in context. Source: the compiler's own.

**Technique note:** 33v l.9: "and I wrote it (the riddle) and its answer"; 34r l.2-3: "I wrote this answer matching what I had imagined ..., with some change, and it had no reality; he solved it, and it is"; 34r l.6: "and he chose bi-ʿunf instead of takhannuf ..." (read only in part).

## 34r.8 (cipher 34r l.8-13, 34v l.1; plaintext 34v l.3-4)

**Arabic (record):**
هيات كميتنا لكوب ولجام ليست بكميت لركوب ولجام / كم سام سيأها بما عملك سام هذا ولكم حام على ذلك حام

**English (second verse tentative):**
- Far from it! Our kumayt is for the cup (kub) and the goblet (wa-l-jam?);
- it is not a bay horse (kumayt) for riding and bridle (wa-lijam).
- How many a one has bargained for (sama) its ... [سيأها, not understood] by what your work ... lofty (sam);
- this one, and how many a one has hovered (ḥama) around that, hovering (ḥam).

**Metre:** not identified.

**Note:** al-Ala'i's pun verse. Kumayt means both "red wine" and "bay horse", and ولجام is taken first as wa-l-jam ("and the goblet", my reading) and then as wa-lijam ("and bridle"). The second verse puns on sam/ham (perhaps also Shem and Ham, not checked). هيات = هيهات; لكوب stands against لركوب, as the record notes. سيأها is not understood, and the syntax of بما عملك سام is unclear. Source: not checked.

**Technique note:** 34r l.7: "then he (al-Ala'i) wrote to me after that"; 34v l.2: "it was solved only with difficulty, and it is"; 34v l.5: "then I wrote its solution and sent it into his presence".

## 34v.6 (cipher 34v l.6-12 and margin; plaintext 35r l.1-4)

**Arabic (record):**
اجبت باحسانك من كان ضعيف يا من ينيف [?] بابه الناس / تطيف [?] / من لطفك قد شرفني كل ظريف مولاي مني يكتب توقيع / شريف

**English (tentative; colloquial grammar):**
- By your kindness you have answered one who was weak,
- O you who rise high [?], around whose door people circle [?];
- out of your graciousness every man of refinement has honoured me:
- my lord, a noble decree (tawqiʿ sharif) is being written from me.

**Metre:** not identified (the rhyme is -if).

**Note:** the compiler's reply to al-Ala'i, sent with his solution, in a courtly register. A tawqiʿ is a chancery decree or endorsement; the sense of the last hemistich ("from me", or "who will write ... for me") is not secure. ضعيف (for ضعيفا) and يكتب توقيع (for توقيعا) are colloquial or pausal forms. The doubtful words: ينيف could be يضيف, and تطيف could be نطيف, as the record notes. Source: the compiler's own (per the technique note).

**Technique note:** 34v l.5: "then I wrote its solution and sent it into his presence" (this block follows); 34v l.13: "he solved it quickly, and it is".

## 35r.6 (cipher 35r l.6-11; plaintext 35v l.2-3)

**Arabic (record):**
سل قلبك فهو بالنبا خبير يخبرك فكم بان ضمير لضمير / اني لك مثلما انشا مجتهد في قصدك والنجح على الرب يسير

**English:**
- Ask your heart, for it is well informed of the news;
- it will tell you: how often one inner heart has shown itself to another!
- I am, for you, just as I have composed [?], striving
- in your purpose, and success is easy for the Lord.

**Metre:** dubayt (as the compiler says on 35v l.4-7).

**Note:** a reply in kind, about sincerity shared between correspondents (the author is not named on the page; the record thinks al-Ala'i most likely). مثلما انشا is ambiguous: "as I composed (this)", or "as (He) created". The compiler says the writer used al-Rabb rather than Allah, because the name Allah is quick to solve, and avoided an easy rhyme letter.

**Technique note:** 35r l.5: "I wrote it and sent it"; 35r l.12-35v l.1: "the first letters that came out were ba and alif, then lam and nun, then sin and ra, then kaf, then qaf and ya, then ha [or kha], then mim, then dad; then its solution was complete, and it is"; 35v l.4-7: "I marvelled at this fine verse and apt answer; he used the dubayt metre and avoided the rhyme letter so that it would not be called easy to solve, and for that reason he said al-Rabb and not Allah, because the name Allah is quick to solve".

## 35v.8 (cipher 35v l.8-13 and margin; plaintext 36r l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
يا من لطراز ملك مصر حلا كم [?] من مترجم اتاه حلا / تشريفك لي لمر عيشي حلا ما حل بازدكرن [?] ملحلا [?]

**English (tentative):**
- O you who have adorned (ḥalla) the embroidered band (tiraz) of the realm of Egypt,
- how many [?] a cipherer has come to him, (and he) solved (it) (ḥalla)!
- Your honouring me has made the bitterness of my life sweet (ḥala);
- ... [ما حل بازدكرن [?] ملحلا [?]: not understood, not translated].

**Metre:** not identified (the record's notes call it a dubayt-like pair; not checked).

**Note:** the compiler's piece to al-Ala'i, with four rhyme words in ḥ-l (jinas), and no article al- in it, as he says. The first ḥalla is taken as "adorned" and the second as "solved"; كم is doubtful (a ر-like stroke follows it). The second half of the last line is clearly written but not understood. The compiler's "superfluous ay" is not found in the reading. Source: the compiler's own.

**Technique note:** 35v l.7: "then I wrote to him after that"; 36r l.1-7: "he solved that quickly and sent it back, though I had made it strange in several ways: I put in a superfluous ay, which is for the vocative and is rarely used, indeed rare except in the mawaliya; there is no alif-lam in it, because that helps the solver; the four rhyme words are a pun; I wrote ḥila without alif in both lines; but this is a gift from God, and it is".

## 36r.11 (cipher 36v l.1-6, first begun on 36r l.11-13; plaintext 36v l.11-12)

**Arabic (record):**
احوم عليك وفي فوادي لوعة واصد عنك ووجه ودي مقبل / ومتى هممت بوصل غيرك ردني وله اليك وشافع لك اول

**English:**
- I hover around you [احوم as read; see note], and in my heart is a burning pang,
- and I turn away from you while the face of my love is turned toward (you);
- and whenever I resolve on union with another than you,
- a longing for you turns me back, and an intercessor for you who came first.

**Metre:** kamil (the first hemistich does not scan with احوم as read; see the suspected errors).

**Note:** a love lament: the lover's pretended coldness and his constancy to a first love. Source: the compiler calls it "two old verses of the Arabs"; the poet is not checked. The compiler says the first word ends in waw, which احوم does not.

**Technique note:** 36r l.10: "then he wrote the answer in two old verses of the Arabs"; 36v l.7-10: "it was solved only with great difficulty, because of its strange words, its few alifs and its many waws, especially at the end of the first word, which is rare at word end, its doubling in wa-wajh, the doubled mim in hamamtu, and other things".

## Suspected Arabic errors

None of these readings has been changed above; each is a suspicion for the next look at the master.

1. **25r.4** طلب لك: the kamil metre wants طلب اليك (ṭalabun ilayka); لك does not scan. The fit check's 3 surplus signs leave room for the two extra letters.
2. **25r.4** تطلب [?]: the metre and the rhyme sense want نتطلب ("we should seek"), the alternative the record already raises.
3. **25v.4** علمت ان غيرك: does not scan in wafir as read (an extra short syllable is wanted, e.g. علمت بان). Weak suspicion; the letter count fits as read.
4. **26r.12** الوابه [?]: the last word must rhyme in -abu with طلب; الوابه does not, so it is probably misread (the record also has 7 surplus signs).
5. **27v.3** يا من اين: the first hemistich does not scan, while the second verse scans as munsarih; the phrase is probably misread or misspelled (the record has 5 surplus signs).
6. **28v.1** يوماك: the ك is probably not a letter; يوما دهري "the two days of my fortune" gives the sense, though the wafir scansion of that hemistich is still not clean.
7. **30r.4** فقالت ان الشيخ: the last hemistich does not scan in wafir as read; one word may be misread.
8. **31r.2** the record's text has اري and splits ما الله / صانع; the corrected red-gloss reading has ارى (used above).
9. **33r.4** فجي بمثل قول الحاجري / النوم منكور: the second hemistich does not scan in basit as read.
10. **33v.6** حلي, فخد, ونظر: unpointed; فخد may be فخذ. The riddle's sense is not established, so a misreading cannot be ruled out.
11. **33v.12** كيما and تخنف: not understood in context; possibly misread.
12. **34r.8** سيأها and بما عملك سام: not understood; possibly misread.
13. **34v.6** ينيف [?] and تطيف [?]: doubtful as the record says (يضيف / نطيف).
14. **35v.8** كم [?] and بازدكرن [?] ملحلا [?]: the line end is not Arabic as read. The compiler's "superfluous ay" (vocative) is not in the reading, so a word in 36r l.8-9 is probably misread (the record says the same).
15. **36r.11** احوم: probably احنو ("I bend tenderly over you"). The compiler says the first word ends in waw (rare at word end), احنو has the same letter count (4), and aḥnu ʿalayka scans as kamil where aḥumu ʿalayka does not.
