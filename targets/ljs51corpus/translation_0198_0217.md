# LJS 51, chains 97r.1 to 106v.6 (images 198-217): English translation

Source: `chains_0198_0217.jsonl` only (master-TIFF readings), with `targets/qaysarani1350/reading.md` and
`translation_en.md` for 106v.6, as the record directs. The range has 24 chains. Twenty-two have a plaintext on the page;
98v.10 has one that does not fit its cipher, and 106v.6 has none (the volume as photographed breaks off). Seven things
need a word before they are read:

- **Red glosses.** Three chains (97r.1, 97v.2, 97v.9) carry one red Arabic letter over every sign; in all three the red
  text is the plaintext letter for letter (record). 106v.6 has red letters over its first five signs only.
- **102v.11 is not a sign cipher.** It is written in ordinary Arabic letters, as a *maqlub* ("inverted") text: each word
  is written as its even-position letters in order, then its odd-position letters in reverse, and the words are run
  together and re-spaced so that the result looks like a substitution. The rule and a worked example are under the chain.
- **98v.10 is answered only in part.** The page gives the verse in a full and a shortened form ("if the middle is dropped,
  there remains"), but the cipher fits neither: it may encode a rearranged text. Both forms are translated.
- **106r.11** is given in the cipher's own spelling, which fits the signs exactly; the page's plaintext is shorter by four
  letters. The compiler's remark on the jim/ha misreading (aflajtu / aflahtu) is translated under it.
- **106v.6** has no plaintext in the manuscript. Its reading and translation are the project's, from
  `targets/qaysarani1350`, reused here (see the chain).
- **105v.6** is a riddle (lughz) in deliberately odd words; its last line is transliterated, not translated.
- **The naming passages** in the technique notes (97r-106v), including the 102v sentence that names *Miftah al-kunuz* as
  "my book", are translated in a separate section at the end.

The Arabic is copied as it stands in the record. A slash (/) in the Arabic is the record's own line division. The
hemistich division in the English is mine, made from the metre and rhyme where they could be found. [?] marks a word
the record flags as doubtful; alternatives are only in the notes. "Translation confidence" is my confidence in the
English. It is not the record's plaintext certainty. The chains are given in folio order (the record lists 106v.6 before
106r.11).

In the headings, "the compiler" is the writer of the book (the project's working attribution is Ibn al-Durayhim; see the
last section for what the 102v sentence implies) and "the master" (al-makhdum) is the patron, here called al-maqarr
al-makhdumi al-'Ala'i (96v, 102r). In this stretch the solving runs both ways: the master solves the compiler's pieces
(97v, 99r-100r, 102v), and the compiler solves pieces from the master and from a series of visitors. Named in the record
on ff. 97r-106v: al-Sahib Taqi al-Din Ibn Murajil (99r); Shaykh Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim, "the translator" (100r-100v);
Taj al-Din Ibn al-Malih (100r-101r, 105r); al-Mawla Shihab al-Din al-Safadi, son of the late Zayn al-Din al-Safadi (101r-
101v); the vizier Ibn Zuhr (101v); Mu'izz al-Dawla (101v); Taj al-Din (son of?) Taj al-Din, overseer of Qatya (102v);
Muhyi al-Din 'Abd al-Qadir (103v); 'Amr b. Hilliza al-Yashkuri (104r); Nasir al-Din Ibn al-Tusi (104v); Shams al-Din son
of 'Ala' al-Din b. Luqman (105r); 'Abd al-Qadir b. Hasan [?] al-Mawsili, the merchant (105v); Ibn al-Qabaqibi (106v).
Places: Alexandria (99r-99v), the Quwayq, river of Aleppo (102r), Qatya (102v). Books: *Husn al-tawassul* (102r) and
*Miftah al-kunuz fi hall al-marmuz* (102v).

## 97r.1 (cipher 97r l.1-6; plaintext 97r l.8-11)

**Arabic (record):**
دع الذنب والعذر في جانب وكن صاحبا بالصفا / والصلف / فلو كان ذنب كمثل الجبال لقلنا عفا الله عما / سلف

**English:**
- Put the fault and the excuse to one side,
- and be a companion in purity of heart (al-safa) and in [al-salaf, "pride"?];
- for were a fault like the mountains,
- we would say: "God has pardoned what is past" ('afa Allahu 'amma salaf).

**Metre:** mutaqarib (both verses scan).

**Red gloss:** one red letter over every sign, equal to the plaintext letter for letter (record).

**Note:** the master's enciphered answer to the compiler's plea for pardon of 96r.2 (introduced on 96v l.11-13: "al-maqarr
al-makhdumi al-'Ala'i ... enciphered the answer to the preceding verses and sent it with them"). The last hemistich quotes
Q 5:95, "God has pardoned what is past", and the compiler "marvelled at the beauty of this verse and the quotation
(iqtibas)". The rhyme word salaf answers al-salaf at the end of the first verse, a near pun (sad / sin). Al-salaf usually
means boastfulness or haughtiness; "be a companion in purity and in pride" is an odd pair, and it may mean "in sincerity
and in sulking", that is in good moods and bad. The cipher and the gloss both have الصلف (record). The compiler solved it
at once, "because it was easy", and "rejoiced at his being pleased". Poet: the master, as I read the introduction.

**Technique note:** 97r l.7: "I solved it at once because it was easy, and may God reward [him?], and I rejoiced at his
being pleased, and it is"; 97r l.12: "I marvelled at the beauty of this verse and the quotation".

## 97v.2 (cipher 97v l.2-4; plaintext 97v l.6-7)

**Arabic (record):**
شكر الاله نعمة موجبة لشكره / فكيف شكري بره وشكره من بره

**English:**
- Thanking God is a blessing that requires thanks to Him (in turn);
- so how can my thanks (repay) His goodness, when thanking Him is itself of His goodness?

**Metre:** not scanned (short lines, rajaz-like).

**Red gloss:** one red letter per sign, equal to the plaintext; the three lines open with the same group, ش ك (شكر,
لشكره, وشكره), under the same red letters (record).

**Note:** the old theme of the endless debt of gratitude: the ability to give thanks is a further gift, which needs
thanks of its own. The device is the repetition of shukr (four times) and birr (twice), which the cipher shows as
repeated groups. The compiler wrote it to the master, who solved it and sent it back quickly (فحله). The introduction says
it was written في سعناه or معناه قدما, perhaps "on its theme, earlier" (record: the word is undotted, not decided). Poet:
not stated.

**Technique note:** 97r l.13 - 97v l.1: "and I wrote to him, may God make his shade ample, [في معناه?] earlier"; 97v l.5:
"he solved it and sent it quickly, and it is".

## 97v.9 (cipher 97v l.9-11; plaintext 97v l.13)

**Arabic (record):**
ذهاب المال في حمد واجر ذهاب لا يقال له ذهاب

**English:**
- Money gone (dhahab) in (earning) praise and (God's) reward
- is a going that is not called going.

**Metre:** wafir.

**Red gloss:** one red letter per sign, equal to the plaintext (record).

**Note:** a single verse on generosity: wealth spent for praise and for the reward of the next world is not lost. The
word dhahab ("going") is said three times, and the compiler marvels that the master solved it "despite its shortness,
the fewness of its letters and the repetition of its words: glory to the Bestower (al-Wahhab)". يقال is written with its
first letter undotted (record). Poet: not stated.

**Technique note:** 97v l.8: "and I wrote him a verse"; 97v l.12: "he solved it despite its shortness, the fewness of its
letters and the repetition of its words; glory to the Bestower; and it is".

## 98r.2 (cipher 98r l.2-7; plaintext 98r l.10-11)

**Arabic (record):**
عيد يعاد عليك من بركاته وزكا صنيعك دايما كزكاته / شرفتني وذكرتني بسمين نحرت عداك كنحره وذكاته

**English:**
- A feast that returns to you with its blessings;
- and may your good deeds (sani'uka) grow pure (zaka) always, like its alms (zakatihi).
- You have honoured me and remembered me with a fat (beast):
- may your enemies be slaughtered (nuhirat) like its slaughter (nahrihi) and its ritual killing (dhakatihi).

**Metre:** kamil (the first hemistich scans).

**Note:** a congratulation for the Feast of Sacrifice ('id al-adha), written after the master had sent the compiler a
sacrificial animal (98r l.1). It is a piece of luzum ma la yalzam: every hemistich ends in -katihi, with kaf held before
the rhyme letter, as the compiler says ("it is bound to kaf"). The puns run zaka / zakatihi ("grow pure" / "its alms",
or "its purity") and dhakkartani / dhakatihi ("you remembered me" / "its slaughter"). The master solved it "despite the
repetition of the kafs, the fewness of the words and their strangeness". The first letter of يعاد is undotted on the
page and the cipher has the ya sign (record). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 98r l.1: "he sent me a sacrificial animal, so I wrote congratulating him and mentioning it"; 98r l.8-9:
"he solved it despite the repetition of the kafs, the fewness of the words and their strangeness; and it is bound to kaf
(mulzama al-kaf)".

## 98r.13 (cipher 98r l.13, 98v l.1-5; plaintext 98v l.7-8)

**Arabic (record):**
بني بني معاوية ابن عمرو وكان ابوكم برا وفيا / فاوصيكم بضيف او بجار يجاوركم فقيرا او غنيا

**English:**
- My sons, sons of Mu'awiya b. 'Amr,
- your father was dutiful and faithful;
- so I charge you with (care for) a guest, or a neighbour
- who lives beside you, poor or rich.

**Metre:** wafir (the second verse scans).

**Note:** a testament (wasiyya) in the voice of an elder to a tribe, "the sons of Mu'awiya b. 'Amr": keep faith with
guest and neighbour, as your father did. The master sent it (introduced "then he wrote to me", with a formula of
blessing on him, see the last section) and the compiler solved it. The cipher has one letter fewer than ابوكم برا, which
it writes as one group over the line end (record). Poet: not stated; the tribe is not identified in the record.

**Technique note:** 98r l.12: "then he wrote to me, may God let Islam and the Muslims enjoy his life"; 98v l.6: "I solved
it and sent it, and it is".

## 98v.10 (cipher 98v l.10-13; the two forms of the verse 99r l.2-3 and 99r l.5-6)

**Arabic (record), full form (99r l.2-3):**
حديث قديم زوى نشر اريج شذا مندل عاطر / فحرك لي ساكنا كامنا خفوق على قلبي الطاير

**Arabic (record), shortened form (99r l.5-6):**
حديث قديم زوى شذا المندل العاطر / فحرك لي ساكنا على قلبي الطاير

**English (full form):**
- An old story gathered in (zawa) the spreading (nashr) of the fragrance,
- the scent of perfumed sandalwood (mandal),
- and stirred in me something still and hidden,
- a throbbing (khufuq) over my fluttering heart.

**English (shortened form):**
- An old story gathered in the scent of the perfumed sandalwood,
- and stirred in me what was still, over my fluttering heart.

**Metre:** not scanned.

**Note:** "of my difficult verse", the compiler says. Its device is that the verse still stands when its middle words
(نشر اريج, كامنا خفوق) are dropped, like the optional words of 94v.5. The master solved it, wrote it out and sent it,
and the page gives both forms. But the cipher does not fit either (record): its 51 signs and 12 groups match the count
of the shortened form but not its group lengths, it repeats one five-sign group exactly where neither form repeats a
word, and a letter-by-letter test fails forwards and backwards. The record suggests a rearranged text or a different
text of about 51 letters, and leaves it open. زوى ("folded up, gathered in") is as written; I am not sure of the image
(روى, "told", would make the story "tell" of the scent). كامنا خفوق is read on the master; خفوق may carry tanwin
(record). The answer stands as partial. Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 98v l.9: "and of what I wrote to him, may God make his shade ample, of my difficult verse"; 99r l.1:
"he solved it, wrote it out and sent it"; 99r l.4: "if the middle is dropped, there remains".

## 99r.10 (cipher 99r l.10-13, 99v l.1-3; plaintext 99v l.5-8)

**Arabic (record):**
لئن شاد هذا الثغر اسكندر الورى وفضله فوق الذي / كان يملك / فانت سليمان الزمان وليته فلم يبق فيه للشياطين / مسلك

**English:**
- If the Alexander of mankind built this frontier city,
- and his merit (fadluhu) rose above all he possessed,
- then you are the Solomon of the age: you were set over it (wullitahu),
- and no path is left in it for the devils.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** two improvised verses addressed to al-Sahib Taqi al-Din Ibn Murajil "when we entered Alexandria the Protected"
(heading in the last section). The conceit pairs the founder of the city, Alexander, with its present governor as
Solomon, the king who ruled the jinn and the devils (shayatin): under him no devil can find a way in. The compiler
comments after the verse (99v l.9-10) on Alexander and Solomon as the two believing rulers of the world (record; the
comment is not given in full). وفضله could also be read wa-faddalahu, "and (God) preferred him", but "his merit" is the
simpler reading. وليته فلم is read on the master (record). The master solved it and sent it back "with the man who was
with him". Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 99r l.7-9 (the heading, last section); 99v l.4: "he solved it, wrote it out and sent it with the man
who was with him, and it is".

## 99v.13 (cipher 99v l.13, 100r l.1-2; plaintext 100r l.4)

**Arabic (record):**
ليس بالاسكندرية غير اسماك طرية

**English:**
- There is nothing in Alexandria
- but fresh fish.

**Metre:** majzu' al-ramal: four feet of fa'ilatun, which make a complete short verse with internal rhyme (-riyya /
-riyya), not one hemistich as the record has it.

**Note:** a line, half joke and half complaint, "from verses I had composed in Alexandria", sent with 99r.10. The master
solved it "despite a deficiency and the fewness of its letters, and this is a great wonder". The deficiency (naqs) may be
the letter left out of بالاسكندرية and written under a divider as an insertion (record: 25 signs in the line, 26 with
it). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 99v l.11-12: "and I also wrote to him with it a line from verses I had composed in Alexandria"; 100r
l.3: "he solved it despite a deficiency and the fewness of its letters, and this is a great wonder, and it is".

## 100r.8 (cipher 100r l.8-11; plaintext 100v l.2-3)

**Arabic (record):**
غامزته بناظري ولم افه بكلمه / جاوبني حاجبه لكن بنون العظمه

**English:**
- I winked at him with my eye and did not utter a word;
- his eyebrow answered me, but with the nun of majesty.

**Metre:** rajaz-like (not scanned closely).

**Note:** a love epigram whose point is the shape of a letter: the arched eyebrow looks like the letter nun (ن), and
"the nun of majesty" (nun al-'azama) is the grammarians' name for the royal "we". The beloved answers the wink with a
raised brow, a nun, and so answers haughtily, in the plural of majesty. ولم افه is written run together as ولما فه on
the page, and the cipher groups it 3+3 (record). Shaykh Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim brought it, saying Taj al-Din Ibn
al-Malih had written it (on the wording of the heading, see the last section); the compiler solved it "at once, off the
head of the pen". The same verse returns, with two words changed, in 105r.1. Poet: not stated.

**Technique note:** 100r l.5-7 (the heading, last section); 100v l.1: "I solved it at once, off the head of the pen
(min ra's al-qalam), and it is".

## 100v.6 (cipher 100v l.6-10; plaintext 100v l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
يا ليل طل او لا تطل لا بد لي ان اسهرك / لو بات عندي قمري ما بت ارعي قمرك

**English:**
- O night, be long or do not be long:
- I must keep watch through you.
- Had my moon spent the night with me,
- I would not have spent the night watching your moon.

**Metre:** rajaz (the first verse scans).

**Note:** the lover's sleepless night, with the beloved as "my moon" set against the night's moon. The compiler solved it
at once, "because it was easy; and it is well known" (mashhur). ارعي is read from the cipher's 'ayn sign (record). From
the papers of Taj al-Din Ibn al-Malih brought by Shihab al-Din al-Mutarjim. Poet: not stated.

**Technique note:** 100v l.4-5 (the heading, last section); 100v l.11: "I solved it at once, off the head of the pen,
because it was easy; and it is well known".

## 101r.2 (cipher 101r l.2-7; plaintext 101r l.9-10)

**Arabic (record):**
يا غصن نقا قوامه مياد ايام رضاك كلها اعياد / ما اكثر حزني عندما تهجرني الا حذرا ان تشمت الحساد

**English:**
- O branch on a sand-hill (naqa), whose stature sways,
- the days of your favour are all feast-days.
- My grief is not great when you desert me,
- except for fear that the envious will gloat.

**Metre:** dubayt (ruba'i) in form: four lines rhyming aaba (مياد, اعياد, -, الحساد); not scanned foot by foot.

**Note:** a quatrain in the popular dubayt form. "Branch on a sand-hill" is the slender beloved. I take ما ... الا as
negation with exception ("my grief is not much ... except"); read as an exclamation, "how great my grief is", the الا
does not follow. The third of Taj al-Din's ciphers; the compiler notes that its alphabet is that of the two before
except for the 'ayn and the dal, and thinks the change "an oversight, not intended". The cipher has one sign fewer in
اعياد and in الحساد (record). Poet: not stated.

**Technique note:** 101r l.1: "and of what the aforementioned Taj al-Din wrote, with the explanation, too"; 101r l.8: "I
solved it at once because it was easy, and it is"; 101r l.11-12: "in all of these [?] nothing was changed except the
'ayn and the dal, and the rest of the letters are as they were; I think it was an oversight, and he did not intend it".

## 101v.2 (cipher 101v l.2-6; plaintext 101v l.9-10)

**Arabic (record):**
رمى حر قلبي بلحظ له رشا ما درى قدر ما قد رمى / وقد كان قدم احسانه ولكنه قد ما قدما

**English:**
- A fawn (rasha') shot at the heat of my heart (harra qalbi) with a glance of his,
- not knowing the extent of what he had shot (qadra ma qad rama);
- he had offered (qaddama) his kindness before,
- but he has cut off what he offered (qadda ma qaddama).

**Metre:** mutaqarib (both verses scan, with rasha' read rashan).

**Note:** a chain of puns on the letters q-d-r-m: qadra ma qad rama / rama, and in the last hemistich قد ما قدما, which I
read qadda ma qaddama, "he cut off (severed) what he had offered", against qaddama ihsanahu in the hemistich before. "But
he has not offered (it again)" (qad ma qaddama) is also possible. The cipher groups بلحظ له رشا as 4+2+3; the page
writes the ra of رشا undotted-looking (دشا) (record). Shihab al-Din al-Safadi had been unable to solve it and gave it to
the compiler, saying the verse was by the vizier Ibn Zuhr; the compiler solved it at home, quickly, at night. Poet: Ibn
Zuhr (named by al-Safadi, as reported by the compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 101r l.13 - 101v l.1 and 101v l.7-8 (last section): "he said it was by the vizier Ibn Zuhr; I solved it
at home quickly, at night, and it is".

## 102r.1 (introduction 101v l.11-13; cipher 102r l.1-6; plaintext 102r l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
رايت قويقا اذ جاوز حده له زجل في جريه وضجيج / وكان ثعال[?] جالسا بشفين فشبهته نحرا لديه خليج

**English:**
- I saw the Quwayq when it had overstepped its bounds,
- with a roaring in its course and a clamour;
- and Thu'al [?] was sitting [بشفين; the cipher has probably بشفتيه, "with his two lips" (open?)],
- and I likened him to a throat (nahr; read nahr, "a river"?) with a gulf (khalij, also "a canal") beside it.

**Metre:** tawil (the first hemistich scans with the cipher's تجاوز, not with written جاوز; the second verse was not
scanned).

**Note:** two verses "by Mu'izz al-Dawla" (so al-Safadi told the compiler), on the Quwayq, which the compiler glosses as
"the river of Aleppo" (102r l.10): a small stream, here in flood. The second verse is not clear to me. It seems to set a
man beside the river and liken him, mouth open, to a river with a canal: a joke on someone's lips or mouth, but I cannot
fix it. ثعال is written undotted-looking (نعاك / ثعال), and the cipher has four signs ending alif-lam (record). Thimal,
the name of the Mirdasid Mu'izz al-Dawla of Aleppo, would come to mind (my note; not checked), but it has a mim, and the
compiler says the cipher has "no mim in it and no definite article"; the verse as read has neither. اذ جاوز is written
اذجا وزحده, with the space in the wrong place. The cipher has five signs for جاوز, the first being the ta of رايت, so
تجاوز; and six signs for بشفين, ending in the ha sign (record). Shihab al-Din al-Safadi had been unable to solve it; the
compiler solved it "quickly despite its difficulty". Poet: Mu'izz al-Dawla (named by al-Safadi, as reported by the
compiler; not checked).

**Technique note:** 101v l.11-13 (last section); 102r l.7: "I solved it quickly despite its difficulty; there was no mim in
it and no definite article, and it is"; 102r l.10: "Quwayq is the river of Aleppo".

## 102r.12 (cipher 102r l.12-13, 102v l.1; plaintext 102v l.4-5)

**Arabic (record):**
خمر ودر وورد ريق وثغر وخد / ليل وبدر وغصن شعر ووجه وقد

**English:**
- Wine and pearls and roses: saliva, and teeth (thaghr), and cheek;
- night and full moon and branch: hair, and face, and stature.

**Metre:** mujtathth (both verses scan).

**Note:** a pure laff wa-nashr ("folding and unfolding"): three images, then the three things they stand for, in the same
order: wine for the saliva, pearls for the teeth, roses for the cheek; night for the hair, the full moon for the face, the
branch for the stature. Eight of the twelve words begin with waw "and", which the compiler notes ("the many ra's in it and the
repeated waws"; the record counts 7 ra and 9 waw). He had sent it to the master "earlier, among the first ones", from the
verses of *Husn al-tawassul*, and had not recorded it before; the master solved it quickly, "and I marvelled at that".
*Husn al-tawassul* is not identified in the record (not checked). Poet: not stated.

**Technique note:** 102r l.10-11: "and of what I wrote earlier [?] to al-Makhdumi al-'Ala'i, may God make his shade ample,
among the first ones, and did not record, from the verses of *Husn al-tawassul*"; 102v l.2-3: "he solved it quickly

## 102v.11 (text 102v l.11-12; plaintext 103r l.1-2)

**Arabic (record), the maqlub text as written:**
اتمدكل لعامنا اعهبس بيتقوام ردط لجا دالحبر فا / ان نم ذا اكترذ نقهبام قصر لمنازا صفقولهر دا

**Arabic (record), plaintext:**
دامت لك النعماء سابغة وبقيت طرد الدجى الفجر / يا من اذا ذكرت مناقبه رقص الزمان وصفق الدهر

**English:**
- May blessings stay with you, ample,
- and may you remain [as long as] the dawn drives away the dark;
- O you at whose virtues, when they are told,
- Time dances and Fate claps its hands!

**Metre:** kamil (it scans with the cipher's وبقيت ما طرد, not with written وبقيت طرد).

**The rule (record):** each plaintext word is written with its letters in even positions first, in order (2nd, 4th,
...), then its letters in odd positions in reverse order (..., 3rd, 1st). So دامت (d-a-m-t) becomes ا ت then م د:
اتمد; لك becomes كل; سابغة becomes ا غ then ة ب س: اغهبس (written اعهبس); طرد becomes ر then د ط: ردط. Then the words are run
together and the spaces put back in the wrong places (اتمدكل = دامت + لك; دالحبر فا ends الدجى and الفجر), so that the
text looks like ordinary letters standing for others, a substitution. The second line of the cipher gives the second
hemistich letter for letter.

**Note:** a panegyric couplet. The cipher has ما after وبقيت (بيتقو + ام), "and may you remain as long as (ma) the dawn
drives away the dark", which the page leaves out; the metre and the sense both want it (record; see the suspected
errors). It does not encipher the hamza of النعماء and has one extra tooth in لحبر (record). Taj al-Din (son of?) Taj
al-Din, overseer of Qatya, wrote it in front of the compiler, expecting him to take it for a substitution; it is "one of
the kinds of cipher mentioned in my book *Miftah al-kunuz fi hall al-marmuz*, from the chapter of the inverted
(al-maqlub)". The compiler solved it at once, "and he was astonished; and it is easy". Poet: not stated.

**Technique note:** 102v l.6-10 (translated in full in the last section); 102v l.13: "I solved it at once, and he was
astonished; and it is easy".

## 106r.11 (cipher 106r l.11-13; plaintext 106v l.2; the compiler's correction 106v l.3-4)

**Arabic (record), in the cipher's own spelling (42 letters, fits the signs exactly):**
احتج خصمي واحتججت بحجتي افلحت في حججي وخاب المحتجج

**Arabic (record), as written on the page:**
احج[?] خصمي واحتجت[?] بحجتي افلحت في حجي وخاب المحتج

**English:**
My opponent argued, and I argued with my proof; I prevailed (aflahtu, "I succeeded"; the compiler: aflajtu, "I won") in
my proofs, and the arguer (al-muhtajj) came away disappointed.

**Metre:** none found; I take it as a sentence of prose.

**The compiler's remark (106v l.3-4), English:** "The correct form is aflajtu, with jim; aflahtu, with the undotted ha,
is a confusion. It was only a misreading of the dots (tashif): he took it for aflahtu. And (one) says: aflaja
bi-hujjatihi ('he prevailed by his argument')."

**Note:** the piece is built on one root, h-j-j ("to argue, to prove"), and in undotted script ha and jim are one shape,
so the sentence is a game with that pair. The compiler says that the right word is aflajtu ("I prevailed", of an
argument) and that aflahtu ("I succeeded") came from misreading the dots; the idiom, as he gives it, is aflaja
bi-hujjatihi. Who "says" it, the writer or the usage, the Arabic leaves open. The cipher itself has the ha sign in افلحت,
not the jim sign, so the error is the cipher writer's (record). The cipher spells حججي ("my proofs") and المحتجج with
the doubled jim written out, where the page writes حجي and المحتج (record). The writer is not named ("and he wrote to
me"); the compiler solved it "after much trouble, until I thought I could not solve it". Author: not stated.

**Technique note:** 106r l.10: "and he wrote to me"; 106v l.1: "I solved it after much trouble, until I thought I could not
solve it, and it is"; 106v l.3-4 (above).

note). The sentence says in the first person that the writer of this book wrote a book called *Miftah al-kunuz fi hall
al-marmuz*, with a chapter on al-maqlub and one on al-ibdal. A cipher treatise with a near title, *Miftah al-kunuz fi
idah al-marmuz*, is ascribed to Ibn al-Durayhim in the modern literature (my knowledge; not checked here). If the two
titles name one work, the sentence would make the compiler of LJS 51 its author, which supports the project's working
attribution. Two things stop short of deciding it: the title here has hall ("solving") where that title has idah
("elucidating"), and the record does not say whether this is the author's own wording or a copyist's. The sentence also
places this collection after the *Miftah*, and shows the compiler being tested at court as a known expert.

## Suspected Arabic errors

None of these readings has been changed above; each is a suspicion for the next look at the master.

1. **102v.11** وبقيت طرد: read وبقيت ما طرد. The maqlub text has بيتقو + ام (وبقيت + ما; record), the kamil scans only with
   ما, and the sense ("may you remain as long as the dawn drives off the dark") needs it. Strong.
2. **102r.1** اذ جاوز: read اذ تجاوز. The cipher has five signs, the first being the ta of رايت (record), and the tawil
   scans with tajawaza, not with jawaza. Strong. In the same chain, بشفين: the cipher has six signs ending in the ha sign,
   probably بشفتيه (record). Moderate. نحرا: نهرا "a river" is expected beside خليج (record). Moderate. ثعال [?]: not
   read with certainty; the compiler's "no mim" rules out ثمال. Open.
3. **103v.11** هونتا (page, هو نتا): the cipher has هونت in four signs (record), and the ramal scans with hawwanta. Strong.
4. **106r.11** احج [?] / واحتجت [?] / حجي / المحتج (page) against احتج / واحتججت / حججي / المحتجج (cipher, which fits the
   42 signs exactly; record). Moderate to strong for the cipher's form. Separately, افلحت, which the compiler himself
   corrects to افلجت; the cipher has the ha sign, so the error goes back to the cipher writer. Strong (the compiler's
   own correction).
5. **105r.11** للجنة / للنار: the cipher has الجنة / النار (alif-lam; record), which parallels فقلت الدنيا / فقلت الاخره.
   Moderate.
6. **103v.2** برار: بوار expected for the sense, but the cipher also has برار (record), so the error, if it is one, is in
   the piece as sent. Moderate for the sense, weak as a copying error.
7. **105v.6** عبيص (عويص?), النجمت (ألجمت?), ولا الست (not understood), and العكروا, whose -وا the compiler says was added to
   hide the text. Open; the start of 106r l.7 is lost in a paper repair (physical).
8. **98v.10** the cipher fits neither the full nor the shortened verse (record); زوى (روى?), كامنا خفوق. Open.
9. **101v.2** دشا (page) for رشا (cipher grouping 4+2+3; record). Weak.
10. **102r.1** اذجا وزحده: the space is in the wrong place on the page (record). Weak (spacing only).
11. **100r.8** ولما فه for ولم افه (spacing only; the cipher groups 3+3; record). Weak.
12. **106v.6** [..ه..]: damaged, not read (project reading). Open (physical).
13. **Divergences in the cipher, not in the written text** (record): 98r.13 one letter fewer in ابوكم برا (one group over
    the line end); 99v.13 بالاسكندرية one sign short, with the missing sign written under the divider; 101r.2 اعياد and
    الحساد each one sign short; 101v.2 one interlinear sign and a missing divider between كان and قدم; 102v.11 hamza of
    النعماء not enciphered and an extra tooth in لحبر; 103v.11 and 105v.6 lam-alif on one sign (the compiler notes both),
    and سخنت منه as one group; 105v.6 written left to right with waw-alif padding; 106v.6 lam-alif on one sign.
