## 37r.1 (cipher 37r l.1-4, red gloss; plaintext 37r l.6-7)

**Arabic (record):**
قد قيل للعبد ان سيده في صدر هذا النهار قد طلبه / فاقبل العبد نحوكم عجلا فلم يجدكم فقيل العشيه

**English** (the words in parentheses marked "not enciphered" have no cipher signs on 37r; the plaintext of the
chain is the red-gloss reading, the last 11 of the 19 words, from النهار to العشيه):
- (not enciphered: It was said to the servant that his master, at the start of this) day had sent for him;
- so the servant came towards you in haste,
- but did not find you, and he was told: "In the evening."

**Metre:** munsarih (the first verse scans; the last hemistich does not scan cleanly as read).

**Note:** a verse excuse or report addressed to a superior ("you" is the polite plural): the servant answered a
summons and found no one. فقيل العشيه is rendered "he was told: in the evening"; the construction is not certain.
The record notes the first 8 words may have stood on a lost leaf, or the compiler copied only the end. Poet: not
checked.

**Technique note:** 36v l.13: "I wrote it and sent it into his presence, and I wrote the answer beneath it"; 37r l.5:
"so I wrote over it (the letters of the red gloss over the signs) straight from the head of the pen, because of its
ease, and it is".

## 37r.11 (cipher 37r l.11-13, 37v l.1-5; plaintext 37v l.7-9)

**Arabic (record):**
بزجاجتين قطعت عمري وعليهما عولت دهري / فزجاجه ملئت بحبر وزجاجه ملئت بخمر / فبذي [?] ابث حكمتي وبذي ازيل هموم صدري

**English:**
- With two glass vessels I have passed my life,
- and on the two of them I have relied all my days:
- one vessel is filled with ink,
- and one vessel is filled with wine;
- with this [?] one I spread my wisdom,
- and with this one I drive away the cares of my breast.

**Metre:** majzu' al-kamil.

**Note:** a scholar's (or secretary's) boast of his two companions, the inkwell and the wine flask. Poet: not
checked. On فبذي [?]: the record says the word is written فدي with a dot below and reads فبذي by the parallel with وبذي.

**Technique note:** 37r l.8-10: "the shaykh Shihab al-Din the translator (al-mutarjim) wrote for me what he said he had
written to the noble excellency al-Ala'i, our master, the one referred to, may God extend his shade"; 37v l.6: "I
solved it quickly, and it is".

## 37v.12 (cipher 37v l.12-13, 38r l.1-6; plaintext 38r l.8-11)

**Arabic (record):**
ركشذ زوهشو ثول هفهل سزاور هنف / اغوس ففع [?] طخهنخف ابغرا و سهنف / اوغطفذ نفد ف كبيذ غون زو شهنف / هوخف ومخ اهو ويزاهو وهنف

**English** (two verses, four hemistichs; no meaning is offered for the cant words):
- [cant] [cant] [cant] [cant] [cant] [cant]
- [cant] [cant] [?] [cant] [cant] and [cant]
- [cant] [cant] ف (Arabic "so"? tentative) [cant] [cant] [cant] [cant]
- [cant] [cant] [cant] [cant] [cant]

**Metre:** not identified (the four hemistichs rhyme in -hnf; the scansion of cant words is not checkable).

**Note:** a piece in the "Sasanian language", the cant of the Banu Sasan (beggars and tricksters), written in the
qalam qummi; the compiler stresses that al-Ala'i solved it "in spite of the strangeness of its words". The only
plainly Arabic word is the separate و "and" (hemistich 2); the separate ف (hemistich 3) may be the Arabic "so". Several
cant words begin with و (ومخ, ويزاهو, وهنف), which may be "and" plus a cant word; I have not split them. ففع [?] and
نفد are the least sure readings (record). The record's following lines (38r l.12-38v l.1) look like a word list that
mixes cant words with Arabic ones (مفلس "bankrupt", عريان "naked", اكل "eat", اشرب "drink", احصل "obtain"); they are
context, not plaintext, and are not translated here. Poet: Badr al-Din al-Jawlani wrote it (technique note); whether
it is his own verse is not stated.

**Technique note:** 37v l.10-11: "the shaykh Badr al-Din al-Jawlani wrote, by the hand of the qadi Amin al-Din, for him
(al-Ala'i) ... may God extend his shade"; 38r l.7: "he solved it in spite of the strangeness of its words, and it is
written in the Qummi pen (script)"; 38r l.12: "this is in the Sasanian language"; 38v l.3-8: "the Qummi script is not
the Pahlavi; the Pahlavi is [letter string of l.4]; most people take the Qummi for the Pahlavi, and it is not so; it is
[letter string of l.6]; the za' is missing from it, and the dad stands in for it; let him who understands it read the
two verses".

## 38v.10 (cipher 38v l.10-13, 39r l.1-5; plaintext 39r l.7-8)

**Arabic (record):**
ابا [?] سايلي عن سيد وابن سيد ومن قد حباه الله حل المترجم / ومن قد كساه الله حسنا وبهجة هو الحبر تاج الدين نجل الدريهم

**English:**
- [?] you who ask me about a lord and son of a lord,
- and one whom God has granted the solving of what is enciphered (hall al-mutarjam),
- and one whom God has clothed in beauty and splendour:
- he is the learned (al-hibr) Taj al-Din, the scion of al-Durayhim.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** praise of Taj al-Din Ibn al-Durayhim, named in the last hemistich, as the man God gifted with solving
ciphers (the record identifies him as Ibn al-Durayhim the cryptologist; mutarjam is this book's word for a ciphered
text). Poet: Badr al-Din al-Jawlani, "of his own verse" (technique note). On ابا [?]: the record says ايا "O" would fit
the sense but is not what is written.

**Technique note:** 38v l.8-9: "the aforementioned shaykh Badr al-Din al-Jawlani wrote for me, of his own verse"; 39r
l.6: "I solved it at once, straight from the pen, because of its ease, and it is".

## 39r.10 (cipher 39r l.10-13, 39v l.1-5; plaintext 39v l.7-10)

**Arabic (record):**
جذبت دواعي النفس عن طلب المنى وقلت لها كفي / عن الطلب المر / فان ابن فضل الله صار بكفه مدار حيا بالرزق / دايبه تجري

**English:**
- I pulled the soul's urges back from seeking its wishes,
- and said to it: "Hold back from bitter seeking,
- for Ibn Fadl Allah has come to hold in his palm
- the turning-point (madar) of a rain (haya) that runs constantly with provision."

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** praise of a patron named Ibn Fadl Allah (the record reads it as a name, not "by the grace of God"; who he is
is not checked), whose hand controls the flow of bounty, pictured as rain. The second verse's syntax (مدار حيا ...
دايبه تجري) is rendered loosely: دايبه ("constant", feminine) does not agree with حيا as written, and I have not
resolved what it qualifies. Poet: not checked (see the technique note).

**Technique note:** 39r l.9: "the aforementioned Badr al-Din wrote, lighairihi" (the record renders "for someone
else"; the phrase can also mean "verse by someone else", in contrast with "of his own verse" in the previous heading);
39v l.6: "I solved it straight from the pen because of its ease, and it is".

## 39v.12 (cipher 39v l.12-13, 40r l.1-10; plaintext 40r l.12-13, 40v l.1)

**Arabic (record):**
حياة المنازل سكانها هم روحها وهي جسمانها / اضات بمن حلها بهجة كما حل بالعين انسانها / وللظاعنين عن الديار كان الاحبة اوطانها

**English:**
- The life of dwellings is their inhabitants:
- they are their soul, and the dwellings are their body.
- They shine with splendour through whoever settles in them,
- as the pupil (insan, "the little man") settles in the eye.
- And for those who journey away from the abodes,
- it is as if the loved ones were their homelands.

**Metre:** mutaqarib (the fifth hemistich does not scan as read; see the suspected errors).

**Note:** on homes and their people. The wordplay is on insan al-ayn, "the pupil of the eye", literally "the man of
the eye". The last verse is rendered literally; its sense (the departed take their homeland with them in their loved
ones) is my reading. Poet: Taqi al-Din Muhammad ibn Ja'far al-Husayni, as the compiler says (40v l.2-3).

**Technique note:** 39v l.11: "a noble person of the country (or: of the city) wrote to me"; 40r l.11: "I solved it,
and it is"; 40v l.2-3: "these verses are by Taqi al-Din Muhammad ibn Ja'far al-Husayni, may God have mercy on him".

## 40v.5 (cipher 40v l.5-10; plaintext 40v l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
زدت عزا وزدت فضلا عميما وراينا عدوك المهموما / ثم لا زلت في نعيم وسعد من الله ما زال برا رحيما

**English:**
- May you increase in might, and increase in all-embracing bounty,
- and may we see your enemy stricken with care.
- And may you never cease to be in bliss and good fortune
- from God, who has never ceased to be kind and merciful.

**Metre:** khafif (the last hemistich does not scan cleanly as read).

**Note:** a benediction for the patron (al-Ala'i). The echo la zilta / ma zala ("may you never cease" / "He has never
ceased") ties the patron's fortune to God's constancy. Poet: Shihab al-Din the mutarjim, "of his own verse" (technique
note). The record says the master writes the last word like رحما and من الله joined; the cipher gives رحيما.

**Technique note:** 40v l.3-4: "the shaykh Shihab al-Din the mutarjim wrote for the master referred to, may God make
his shade ample, of his own verse"; 40v l.11: "he solved it, and it is".

## 41r.3 (cipher 41r l.3-8; plaintext 41r l.10-11)

**Arabic (record):**
من صب في خديه دمعة فانها من قلبه تقطر / لان سحب العين سيارة لكنها من حمرة تمطر

**English:**
- A tear on the cheeks of a lover (min sabbin):
- it is from his heart that it drips;
- for the clouds of the eye are travelling clouds,
- but they rain from redness.

**Metre:** sari' (the second verse scans; the first hemistich is a syllable short as read).

**Note:** tears of blood: the lover's tears are red because they come from the heart. من صب can also be read man
sabba, "whoever pours", which gives "whoever pours a tear on his cheeks, it drips from his heart"; I take min sabbin
("of a lover"), but both are possible. The opening من صب في خد يه is the five two-letter words the compiler mentions
(the cipher splits خديه as خد|يه). Poet: the writer's own verse, the heading says (the writer is Shihab al-Din of the
previous heading, by context; not checked).

**Technique note:** 41r l.1-2: "he also wrote to him, of his own verse, and deliberately put at the start of the
hemistich five two-letter words, to make it hard to solve, with no repeated letter in them"; 41r l.9: "he solved it in
spite of its difficulty and strangeness, and it is"; 41r l.12-13 and 41v l.1-2: the compiler and Shihab al-Din
marvelled at how he solved the five words; the master said he had once composed two verses made wholly of two-letter
words and sent them, but they were lost.

## 41v.8 (cipher 41v l.8-11; plaintext 41v l.13)

**Arabic (record):**
اذا قلت ما بي يا بثينة قاتلي من الوجد قالت ثابت ويزيد

**English:**
- When I say: "What I suffer, O Buthayna, is killing me
- from passion," she says: "It is lasting, and it will grow."

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** a love verse to Buthayna, the beloved of the poet Jamil (the attribution to Jamil is not checked; the record
names no poet). Her answer thabitun wa-yazidu ("lasting, and it increases") also reads as two men's names, Thabit and
Yazid; this pun is my observation and is not in the record. The compiler's own two verses of two-letter words (41v
l.3-6, not ciphered) are not translated here.

**Technique note:** 41v l.7: "the shaykh Shihab al-Din wrote for the master referred to, may God extend his shade";
41v l.12: "he solved it in spite of its shortness and strangeness, and it is".

## 42r.2 (cipher 42r l.2-10; plaintext 42r l.11-13)

**Arabic (record):**
اتتني تؤنبني بالبكا فاهلا بها وبتانيبها / وقالت وفي قولها حكمة اتبكي بعين تراني بها / فقلت لو استحسنت غيركم امرت السهاد بتعذيبها

**English:**
- She came to me, reproaching me for weeping:
- welcome to her, and to her reproach!
- She said (and there is wisdom in what she said):
- "Do you weep with an eye with which you see me?"
- I said: "If it found anyone but you beautiful,
- I would order sleeplessness to torment it."

**Metre:** mutaqarib.

**Note:** the lover's eye that sees the beloved should not weep; he answers that it would be punished only for looking
at another. The record calls it a known three-verse piece whose usual last hemistich is امرت الدموع بتأديبها; here
the cipher confirms امرت السهاد بتعذيبها. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 42r l.1: "the shaykh Shihab al-Din wrote for the master referred to, also"; there is no "he solved
it" sentence: the plaintext follows the cipher directly.

## 42v.3 (cipher 42v l.3-9; plaintext 42v l.11-12)

**Arabic (record):**
سالت خطا بالقبول يبين عن حظي لديك فزدت في هجراني / فغدوت محزونا اذوب صبابة ثكلان قد قصب [?] العدى بهواني

**English:**
- I asked for a writing (khatt) of acceptance that would make plain
- my standing (hazz) with you, but you only shunned me more.
- So I became grieving, melting with longing,
- bereaved; the enemies have [?] at my humiliation.

**Metre:** kamil (the first hemistich does not scan cleanly as read).

**Note:** a complaint to a patron who refused a request. The wordplay is khatt / hazz, written خط / حظ, the same
letter shapes with different dots, apt in a cipher piece. On قصب [?]: the record keeps it as written and calls the
sense not secure; qasaba can mean "to revile", which would give "the enemies have reviled (me) with my humiliation",
but that is not established. Poet: Shams al-Din Ibn al-Rida al-Hanafi wrote it (heading); his authorship of the verse
is not stated.

**Technique note:** 42v l.1-2: "Shams al-Din Ibn al-Rida al-Hanafi wrote for the master referred to, may God extend his
shade"; 42v l.10: "he solved it and found that he had made lam-alif a single letter (one sign)".

## 43r.1 (cipher 43r l.1-3; plaintext 43r l.4)

**Arabic (record):**
كل من مد للورى خير باع زاده الله بالمكارم طولا

**English:**
- Everyone who stretches out to mankind the best of arms (ba'),
- God increases him in reach (tul; also tawl, "bounty") through noble deeds.

**Metre:** khafif.

**Note:** on generosity. The wordplay is on ba' (arm-span, hence generosity) and طول, read as tul "length" (the arm

**Technique note:** 42v l.13: "the shaykh Shihab al-Din the astrologer (al-munajjim) wrote for the master, may God
extend his shade; and he solved it"; there is no sentence between cipher and plaintext.

## 43r.11 (cipher 43r l.11-13; plaintext 43v l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
سقوني وقالوا لا تغن ولو سقوا جبال حنين ما سقوني / لغنت

**English:**
- They gave me drink and said: "Do not sing!" Yet had they given
- the mountains of Hunayn what they gave me, they would have sung.

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** a famous verse of mystical wine poetry; the compiler names the poet as Ibn al-Farid. The record notes that
the usual form is ما سقيت ("what I was given to drink"), while the manuscript and the cipher have ما سقوني.

**Technique note:** 43r l.10: "he also wrote to him, with the explanation"; 43v l.3-10: "had Ibn Adlan or al-Kindi or
others of the ancients lived and worked at solving this single verse for years, it would have defeated them, and God
knows best; the verse before it, too, its solution is a marvel, so glory to the Giver; what the master referred to has
been given (by God) ... in a span that no one we know of reached in time past; and the verse is by Ibn al-Farid".

## 43v.11 (cipher 43v l.11-13, 44r l.1-4; plaintext 44r l.5-8)

اذا افتر من جون الغمام بارقه بكا الورق من جبت [?] / الاناعم [?] وادقه / بدمع كان الريح تنثر لولوا على خد روض سندسي / حدايقه

fortunate hand beneath them; of these is the following". There is no sentence between cipher and plaintext.

## 44r.10 (cipher 44r l.10-13, 44v l.1-3; plaintext 44v l.4-7)

**Arabic (record):**
واذا صفا لك من زمانك واحد فهو المراد وان ذاك / الواحد / واذا رايت من الزمان مسرة فاغنم فان هوى الزمان / معاند

**English:**
- If one single person in your time stays true to you,

**Metre:** kamil.

plaintext. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 44r l.9: "and of these, also" (from the volume Abu Bakr al-Dimashqi brought); there is no sentence
between cipher and plaintext.

## 45r.2 (cipher 45r l.2-6; plaintext 45r l.8-9)

رب ورقا هتوف بالضحى ذات شجو صدحت في فنن / فبكاي ربما ارقها وبكاها ربما ارقني

## 45r.11 (cipher 45r l.11-13, 45v l.1-5; plaintext 45v l.6-9)

**Arabic (record):**
شكري لفضلك يا ابن يحيى خالد فقت الاولى ومدايح / المداح / ولقد تساوى ظاهرا وسريرة متسربلا بردي تقى / وصلاح

**English:**
- My thanks for your bounty (fadl), O Ibn Yahya, is lasting (khalid):
- you have surpassed those before, and the eulogies of the eulogists.
- He (or: it) has been the same outwardly and in secret,
- clad in the two cloaks of piety and righteousness.

**Metre:** kamil.

**Note:** praise of the patron, from the compiler's own qasida for him. فضلك يا ابن يحيى خالد may play on the Barmakid
names al-Fadl ibn Yahya ibn Khalid (my observation; not checked). The subject of تساوى is not stated; "he" (the
patron) is the likeliest. The record notes that the cipher has الاوايل ("the ancients"), one letter more than the
written الاولى; the translation follows the written text.

**Technique note:** 45r l.10: "I wrote to him, may God extend his shade, from a qasida in which I praised him"; 45v l.5:
"he solved it, and it is".

## 45v.13 (cipher 45v l.13, 46r l.1-6; plaintext 46r l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
كم حاز مولاي باعفاي من وزارة الشام من الاجر / اعظم بها لكن مشقاتها مشتقة حقا من الوزر

**English:**
- How much reward my lord has won
- by excusing me from the vizierate of Syria!
- How great a thing it is; but its hardships
- are truly derived from wizr ("burden", also "sin").

**Metre:** sari' (the second verse scans; the first does not scan cleanly as read).

**Note:** the compiler thanks his master for releasing him from the vizierate of Syria. The pun is wizara (vizierate)
/ wizr (burden, sin), and mushtaqqa "derived" echoes mashaqqat "hardships" (the record notes the wizara / wizr pun).
Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 45v l.10-12: "I wrote to him after I had been asked to take up the vizierate of Syria, and I
refused ... now the non-canonical taxes (al-mukus) are abolished, and I asked to be excused and appealed to his
kindness, until the matter was settled before the noble stations (the sultan), may God perpetuate its sultanate" (the
record's reading of these lines is medium); 46r l.7: "he solved that and sent it back, and it is".

## 46r.11 (cipher 46r l.11-13, 46v l.1-4; plaintext 46v l.6-9)

**Arabic (record):**
اقول لهم لما طلبت لانال وزارة شام قول غير / مدلس / لقد هزلت حتى بدا من هزالها كلاها وحتى سامها كل / مفلس

**English:**
- I say to them, when I was asked (tulibtu) to take on
- the vizierate of Syria, words that do not deceive:
- "She has grown so lean that, from her leanness,
- her kidneys show, and every bankrupt has bid for her."

**Metre:** tawil.

**Note:** the compiler's refusal of the vizierate of Syria, quoting (tadmin) as its second verse a well-known verse
about a wasted she-camel (or woman) whom every pauper bids for (the record identifies it as the well-known verse;
its poet is not checked). طلبت is read tulibtu, "I was asked", from the context of 45v l.10; talabtu "I sought" is
also possible as written. The cipher writes لانال as لان انال "that I might take on" (record).

**Technique note:** 46r l.10: "I also wrote to him, quoting (tadmin) the second verse"; 46v l.5: "he solved that and
sent it back, and it is".

## 47r.1 (cipher 47r l.1-11; plaintext 47v l.5-7)

The cipher enciphers only verses 6-8 of an eight-verse riddle (47r l.13-47v l.7), and those three verses are its
plaintext. Verses 1-5 are not enciphered on these pages and are not translated here.

**Arabic (record):**
وما شوارب لم يغرثن من علف ولا طعام وهم يسقونها عللا / وما هروت مروت لم تضع ولدا استهلك الكرث حتى لم تدع بللا / وما فصال تبيت الليل عارية في طوله ثم يستانفنه كملا

**English:**
- And what are drinkers (shawarib) that have not gone hungry for fodder
- nor for food, while they give them drink after drink (alal)?
- And what are هروت مروت (as written, not translated) that have not borne a child,
- (that) the karth (? not understood) consumed, until they left no moisture?
- And what are weaned young (fisal) that spend the night bare
- all through its length, then begin it again, whole?

**Metre:** basit.

**Note:** riddle verses, each opening "and what are ...". Shawarib means both "drinking (creatures)" and
"moustaches", probably the point of the first riddle (not checked). هروت مروت and الكرث are not understood, and the
fourth line's syntax is a guess. The riddles' answers are in the compiler's plain answer-verses (47v l.9-48r l.6),
which the record does not transcribe; not checked. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 47r l.12: "I solved it quickly because of its ease, and found it (to be)"; then the riddle poem
(47r l.13-47v l.7); 47v l.8: "and I wrote the answer", which introduces the compiler's answer in plain verse.

## 48r.8 (cipher 48r l.8-13, 48v l.1-13)

No plaintext. The verses on 49r l.6-7 that the record carries were tested and do not fit this block (226 signs
against 72 letters); they are context only and are not translated as its plaintext. The heading (48r l.7) is one
uncertain word, وابعها [?] ("and the fourth of them" or "and he followed it with" are possible; neither is secure). The
record's note from 49r (after a further short block there): "it was not solved except after much toil, because of its
difficulty"; and 49r l.8-13: the compiler, though he thinks himself skilled in solving and has written works on it,
solved this only with effort, while the master solved the two preceding verses, harder than this, quickly; in old times
some decipherers met two verses much easier than this, made much of them and recorded them in their books, as Ibn
Dunaynir did in his book, and others.

## Suspected Arabic errors

None of these readings has been changed above; each is a suspicion for the next look at the master.

1. **37r.1** فلم يجدكم فقيل العشيه: the last hemistich does not scan in munsarih as read (the first verse does). Weak.
2. **37v.12** the cant: 97 signs against 90 letters (record); ففع [?] and نفد are the least sure words, and word
   division cannot be checked against sense.
3. **38v.10** ابا [?]: the sense wants ايا "O", as the record says; ابا is what is written.
4. **39r.10** دايبه: feminine, while حيا is masculine; either a word is misread or دايبه qualifies something not
   expressed. Weak; the cipher confirms 5 letters.
5. **39v.12** عن: the record gives it as medium, its cipher group has 3 signs for 2 letters, and the hemistich
   وللظاعنين عن الديار does not scan in mutaqarib with it. A different (3-letter) word may stand there.
6. **40v.5** من الله ما زال: the last hemistich does not scan cleanly in khafif; the master writes من الله joined
   (مزالله). Weak.
7. **41r.3** من صب في خديه دمعة: one syllable short for the sari' first hemistich. Weak; the count fit is exact.
8. **42v.3** سالت خطا: the first hemistich does not scan in kamil as read; قصب [?] is unsure in sense, though qasaba
   scans in its place.
9. **43r.6** سالت زمانا هل بفك جود: the first hemistich does not scan in wafir (the second does); بفك is unclear, and
   the record notes the زمانا group has 6 signs for 5 letters.
10. **43v.11** من جبت [?] الاناعم [?] وادقه: the record's cipher groups (4+6 against 3+7) suggest the word division is
    wrong there; and the first hemistich (... الغمام بارقه) is a syllable short in tawil.
11. **44r.10** وان: the cipher has وانت (record); the written وان scans as wa-inna, so either form works.
12. **44v.9** ومسي: the second hemistich ends a syllable short in tawil; the cipher agrees with the written letters.
    Weak.
13. **45r.11** الاولى: the cipher has الاوايل (record).
14. **45v.13** first verse (باعفاي من وزارة الشام من الاجر) does not scan cleanly in sari'. Weak.
15. **46r.11** سامها: the cipher has 4 signs for 5 letters (record).
16. **47r.1** ولا طعام written like ولالطعام and وهم like ومم (record); هروت مروت and الكرث are not understood and may
    be misread; the cipher writes يستانفنه without its alif (يستنفنه).
