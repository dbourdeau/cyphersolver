# LJS 51, chains 61r.3 to 72v.9 (images 126-150): English translation

Source: `chains_0126_0149.jsonl` only (master-TIFF readings). The range has 23 chains, and every one has a plaintext.
Two of them, 63v.1 and 63v.trees, share one plaintext: it is the couplet in the margin of 63v, enciphered once in
letter-like signs and once in the tree script. It is translated once, under 63v.1, and 63v.trees refers to it. The
only red gloss in the range is the one over the trees of 63v. Its red letters spell the couplet letter for letter
(record), so there the plaintext and the red reading are one text. Three chains need a word before they are read:

- **71r.10**: its plaintext is a reconstruction. The page writes out Ibn al-Wardi's couplet (72r l.4-5) and says
  (72r l.9-10) that the encipherer put انه in place of حالة. The cipher text is the couplet with that one word changed.
- **65v.2**: the written plaintext differs from the cipher in four words. The English follows the written text, and
  the cipher's forms are given in the note.
- **64r.3**: the master did not write the solution out in full. He marked one letter over the sign for each of a
  set of letters, and the compiler describes this on 64v (translated at the end).

The Arabic is copied as it stands in the record. A slash (/) in the Arabic is the record's own line division. The
hemistich division in the English is mine, made from the metre and rhyme where they could be found. [?] marks a word
the record flags as doubtful; alternatives are only in the notes. "Translation confidence" is my confidence in the
English. It is not the record's plaintext certainty.

In the headings, "the compiler" is the writer of the book (Ibn al-Durayhim in the record) and "the master"
(al-makhdum) is the patron who solves most of the pieces. On ff. 61r-72v the exchange widens. Shaykh Shihab al-Din
(Ahmad) the translator (al-mutarjim) sends ciphers (63v, 64r), and the compiler sends ciphers to him (65r). Amin
al-Din, qadi of Hama, sends one (71r). Several alphabets are in use (record, `style`).

## 61r.3 (cipher 61r l.3-11; plaintext 61v l.1-4)

**Arabic (record):**
صلا قطع مرانه انفع ضرابك اضحك اسربع صل اهجر رح / اغدافن ابق شر [?] خرزد انقص / خذاعط اشحط ادن اعل اسفل اشف امرض ابد عد / خن اوف اغدر لجمل [?] هزل جد اضلل الخلص [?]

**English** (following the record's by-sense division: صل اقطع مر انه ... اسر بع ... اغد افن ... خر زد ... خذ اعط ...
اجمل اهزل ... اخلص):
- Join, cut off; command, forbid; profit, harm; weep, laugh; [اسر], sell; join, forsake; go at evening,
- go at morning; destroy, spare; [شر خر?]; add, take away;
- take, give; go far, come near; rise high, go low; heal, make ill; begin, go back;
- betray, keep faith; be treacherous, be fair; jest, be in earnest; go astray, be sincere.

**Metre:** not scanned. The record says the "four verses" are written as two long lines of verse over four page lines.

**Note:** a string of paired opposites, all in the imperative. Most pairs are plain: صل / اقطع, مر / انه, انفع / ضر, ابك /
اضحك, رح / اغد (going at evening / at morning, across the line), افن / ابق, زد / انقص, خذ / اعط, اشحط / ادن, اعل / اسفل,
اشف / امرض, ابد / عد, خن / اوف, اغدر / اجمل, اهزل / جد, اضلل / اخلص. Two places are open. اسر is set against بع
"sell", which would want اشر "buy" (see the suspected errors). شر خر is doubtful (record) and is left as a gap. The
compiler calls the piece four verses by Ibn al-Majd al-Mawsili the preacher (61r l.1-2; not checked). He chose it
because the master had asked for something hard to solve, to test his mind (60v l.13). The master solved it after some
days and wrote under it that it is "more like riddles (hazazir) than ciphers". After that the record's text of 61v
l.5-10 quotes two more verses, one introduced as "the first" and the other as "the fourth, after the two mentioned":
اربي محض ودي ... مرخصي and فما انت الا انت في كل حالة وما انا الا ذاك غير مخلص ("You are none other than yourself
in every state, and I am none other than that one, not sincere [?]"). They rhyme in -si, as انقص and اخلص do here. My
reading of that doubtful passage is that the master supplied the first and fourth verses of a four-verse poem whose
second and third were the cipher. That would explain "four verses". The record's words are marked doubtful, so this is
a guess. The compiler's praise of the solution follows (61v l.11 to 62r l.2); it is translated at the end.

**Technique note:** 61r l.1-2: "... so I wrote, among (them), four verses by Ibn al-Majd al-Mawsili the preacher";
61r l.12-13: "it stayed with him some days, then he solved it and wrote under it in his blessed hand: may God not
reproach our lord"; 61v l.5-10: "this, by God, ... it is more like riddles than ciphers, and if our lord orders that
the like of it be written for him, then in God's name" (record's text and rendering, words doubtful).

## 62r.3 (cipher 62r l.3-5; plaintext 62v l.8)

**Arabic (record):**
شحطت ديار كم فعسى تخصونا بطيف كرى

**English:**
- Your dwellings have gone far away; then perhaps
- you will favour us with a phantom (tayf) of sleep.

**Metre:** not identified as read (see the suspected errors).

**Note:** the master's verse, "promised" to the compiler. The phantom of sleep is the beloved's image that visits the
sleeper in a dream. The compiler says it came after the death of the master's brother, the amir Salah al-Din (62r
l.6). The compiler could not finish it. The master told him that it contains "a word made of two words, written after
the orthography of the Mushaf" (the record takes this to be ديار كم, written apart). The compiler guessed that it
began with يا, like يا ايها. From that guess he solved ديار كم, فعسى, بطيف كرى and some letters of شحطت and تخصونا.
One sign (he calls it "the tha") fitted no letter he tried. He had not thought of a silent alif, and the master
completed the verse himself in his presence (62v l.1-7). The cipher has 32 signs for 27 letters, and the excess is
all in the first three groups (record). The last four groups match فعسى تخصونا بطيف كرى exactly.

**Technique note:** 62r l.1-2: "had such a thing been written to me I would have failed at it; the proof is that he
wrote me this promised verse"; 62r l.6-13 and 62v l.1-7: the solving story (above).

## 62v.10 (cipher 62v l.10-12; plaintext 63r l.1)

**Arabic (record):**
واذا ما رياح جودك هبت صار قول العذال فيها هبا

**English:**
- And when the winds of your generosity blow (habbat),
- the words of the censurers become, in them, (blown) dust (haba').

**Metre:** khafif (both hemistichs scan).

**Note:** a compliment on generosity. The play is habbat / haba' (هبا, written without the hamza; record). The
censurers are those who blame a man for spending too freely. In the patron's wind their words are scattered like dust.
The heading is "then he wrote to him" and does not name the writer or the recipient (record). Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 62v l.9: "then he wrote to him, may God reward him in this world and the next"; 62v l.13: "he
solved it and sent it, and it is".

## 63v.1 (cipher 63v l.1-6; plaintext in the right margin of 63v)

**Arabic (record):**
ابري كبير والصغير يقول لي اطعن حشاي به وكن صنديدا / ناديت هذا الاحور [?] فقال لي عندي بحور وثلثه [?] تقليدا

**English:**
- My needle (ibri) is big, and the little one says to me:
- "Pierce my insides with it, and be a hero (sindid)."
- I called to this dark-eyed one (al-ahwar [?]), and he said to me:
- "I have seas (buhur), and a third of it [?] by imitation (taqlid)."

**Metre:** kamil (the first verse scans; the last hemistich scans with ثلثه read thulthuhu).

**Note:** a bawdy riddle, and I do not understand its point with any confidence. The needle and the piercing are
plain double entendre. The second verse probably turns on buhur, which means both "seas" and the metres of prosody.
A short word above the margin lines was read by the pilot as والبسيط ("and the basit", a metre), which the record
says is not part of the text. Al-ahwar is "the one with eyes of sharp black and white", a stock word for a beautiful
youth. The record reads الاحور or الاحوز from the master, not الاحول ("the squint-eyed") as the pilot did. ثلثه "a
third of it" is under a smudge (record), and "a third ... by imitation" is not understood. The cipher was sent by
Shihab al-Din Ahmad the translator (the end of 63r, record). The compiler solved it and copied it again in the tree
script (63v.trees). Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 63v l.7: "I solved it, and I have written it in the tree script (al-qalam al-mushajjar) so that
whoever knows it may read it ..." (last words doubtful).

## 63v.trees (tree script, 63v below l.7, five rows; plaintext in the right margin)

**Arabic (record):** the same couplet as 63v.1.

**English:** see 63v.1.

**Note:** this is the compiler's own copy of the 63v.1 couplet in the tree script: 79 trees, with one small red
letter over each. Row by row the red letters spell the couplet in order (record), so the red letters are a gloss of the
plaintext. Whether the tree shapes encode the letters consistently was not checked (record). The compiler's stated
reason is that "whoever knows it may read it". This is the second display of one text, not a second piece.

## 64r.3 (cipher 64r l.3-8; plaintext 64r l.10-11)

**Arabic (record):**
ولها اذا رفع الازار مكلثم كالقعب كب لحاجة لم يدفع / فاذا طعنت بعض عضه مشفو واذا سللت مص مصه مرضع

**English:**
- And she has, when the waist-wrap (izar) is lifted, a plump one (mukaltham),
- like a wooden bowl (qa'b) turned upside down, not pushed away from a need.
- When you thrust, it bites [بعض] its bite [مشفو];
- and when you draw out, it sucks the sucking of a nursing mother (murdi').

**Metre:** kamil (the first verse scans; the second scans only with يعض, مشفق and يمص, see the suspected errors).

**Note:** an obscene description of a woman's sex. The compiler says its author took it from the poem هل بالطلول
لسائل رد, and quotes the model verse فاذا طعنت لعنت في لبد واذا سللت كاد يسند (64v l.1; the poem and its poet are not
checked). Mukaltham is "full-fleshed, round-faced", written with the dots of tha (record). The master confirmed the
tha by marking it. "Not pushed away from a need" (لحاجة لم يدفع) is literal and uncertain. In the second verse, بعض
is taken as يعض "it bites", the same letter-skeleton with other dots. The master's marks place a qaf in the word
written مشفو, so the word is probably مشفق ("one who is tender"). With these readings the verse runs "it bites as a
tender one bites ... it sucks as a nursing mother sucks", and it scans. This is my reading; the record keeps the
written forms. The cipher was sent by shaykh Shihab al-Din the translator and copied by the master (64r l.1-2). The
compiler solved it and noticed that waw had been given two different signs, which the record confirms. On 64v the
compiler adds that "the meaning of this cipher is more obscure and finer", and he describes how the master marked his
solution (translated at the end). Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 64r l.1-2: "then shaykh Shihab al-Din the translator wrote me this cipher, and the master copied
it"; 64r l.9: "I solved it quickly and found he had made the waw in two forms by mistake"; 64r l.12-13: "its author
took it from هل بالطلول لسائل رد".

## 65r.5 (cipher 65r l.5-8 and two margin groups; plaintext 65r l.12-13)

**Arabic (record):**
ذات هيف فيه خصال ثمان بي اليه تشوق وحنين / ابيض ناعم عريض سمين صلت ناشف حزين [?] سخين

**English:**
- She is slender of waist (dhat hayaf); in it (fihi) are eight qualities
- for which I feel longing and yearning:
- white, soft, broad, plump,
- smooth (salt), dry (nashif), [حزين?], hot.

**Metre:** khafif, judging from the clear lead-in verse; the enciphered pair is not scanned with confidence.

**Note:** the compiler's own verses, made in Aleppo, "answering what came before", that is, the 64r piece. Fihi
"in it" is masculine and has no stated antecedent. Like the 64r verse, it refers to her sex. The eight qualities are
listed in the second verse, and eight adjectives are there if حزين is counted. حزين (literally "sad") is doubtful in the
record (حزيو / حرنو) and makes no sense in the list, so it is left as a gap. The compiler gives the poem's first verse
in clear (65r l.10): هجرتني بعد الوصال وصدت ففؤادي من البعاد حزين, "She left me after our union and turned away, so
my heart is sad from the distance". He calls it a preparation (tawti'a) for these two. On the cipher: two groups each
hold two words under a drawn arc, and margin groups beside l.7-8 make up the count (record). How the arcs work is not
resolved. Shihab al-Din tired of solving it, and the master is said to have helped. The compiler's prose here is
doubtful in the record (65r l.1-4), and at l.11 Shihab al-Din "said he solved them". Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 64v l.12-13, 65r l.1-4: "then I wrote for shaykh Shihab a cipher of my own verse, made in Aleppo,
answering what came before; but it is three verses, the first with the article repeated ..."; 65r l.9: "the verse
before them is a preparation for these two"; 65r l.11: "and the two verses are the cipher; shaykh Shihab al-Din said he
solved them, and they are".

## 65v.2 (cipher 65v l.2-7; plaintext 65v l.9-10)

**Arabic (record):**
تزوجت بصبي امرد يناسبها خز[?]ج وخرج ولسعوا [?] في سباسبها / ترجع ورجع وقد حازوا مكاسبها خاسبه مثلما [?] تقعد [?] لحاسبها

**English** (the written text):
- She married a beardless boy who suits her;
- [خز?ج], and he went out (kharaja), and they [stung (lasa'u)?] in her wastes (sabasib);
- she comes back, and he came back (raja'a), and they have won her gains (makasib);
- [خاسبه, not understood], just as [?] she sits down [?] to her reckoner (li-hasibiha).

**Metre:** not scanned. All four half-lines end on -sibuha, the rhyme pattern of the mawaliya.

**Note:** the master's piece. The compiler says it was "solved only with difficulty, because of the paronomasia and
look-alikes in it". It plays on near-identical words throughout: yunasibuha, sabasibiha, makasibuha, hasibiha. The
cipher and the written text differ in four words. The cipher's form (record) is:
تزوجت بصبي امرد يناسبها تخرج وتخرج ولشعوا في سباسبها / ترجع وترجع وقد حازوا مكاسبها تحاسبه مثلما تقعد تحاسبها.
That is 90 signs for 90 letters, and each group matches one word. In the cipher's form the verbs are all feminine
present: "she goes out and she goes out ... she comes back and she comes back ... she calls him to account just as she
sits down, she calls her (or it) to account". That is clearer than the written form. The written forms (خز?ج وخرج,
ورجع, خاسبه) may be the compiler's own punning variants or slips, and the record does not decide. ولسعوا may be ولشعوا
(the cipher has the shin form), and neither is understood here. The narrative sense (a woman, a boy, going out and
coming back, gains and reckoning) is clear in outline only.

**Technique note:** 65v l.1: "then the master wrote to me"; 65v l.8: "it was solved only with difficulty, because
of the paronomasia and look-alikes in it, and it is".

## 65v.13 (cipher 65v l.13, 66r l.1-5; plaintext 66r l.7-8)

**Arabic (record):**
قالت لترب معها منكرة لوقفني [?] هذا الذي نراه من / قالت فتى متيم يشكو الجوى قالت بمن قالت بمن قالت بمن

**English:**
- She said to a friend of her own age who was with her, disapproving [لوقفني, not understood]:
- "This one we see: who is he?"
- She said: "A youth enslaved by love, complaining of passion."
- She said: "With whom (is he in love)?" She said: "With her who said 'With whom?'"

**Metre:** rajaz (both verses scan, with لوقفني taken as one foot).

**Note:** the point is in the last hemistich. قالت بمن قالت بمن قالت بمن is a question and then an answer: "She said:
'With whom?' (The friend) said: 'With her (bi-man) who said "With whom?"'". The same two words serve first as a question
and then as a relative clause. The compiler calls them "two old, well-known verses from the verses of al-Miftah". The
book is not identified in the record and is not checked. لوقفني is written as one word, and the cipher has it as one
group of six signs (record). Its sense is not clear to me ("disapproving of his standing (waqfatihi)" would suit, but
it is not the written text). The cipher drops the ya of متيم (3 signs, record).

**Technique note:** 65v l.11-12: "I wrote it (65v.2) out and wrote under it two old, well-known verses from the verses
of al-Miftah, and sent that"; 66r l.6: "he solved them quickly and sent them, and they are".

## 66r.10 (cipher 66r l.10-13, 66v l.1; plaintext 66v l.8-9)

**Arabic (record):**
ما اطيب ما يكون من تهواه في حضنك والنعاس قد غشاه / اوصيك اذا اندبلت [?] عيناه امتص [?] لسانه وقبل فاه

**English:**
- How sweet it is when the one you love
- is in your embrace, and drowsiness has come over him!
- I counsel you: when his eyes droop (indhabalat [?]),
- suck his tongue and kiss his mouth.

**Metre:** dubayt (the compiler's word; four hemistichs rhyming in -ah); not scanned.

**Note:** a love dubayt that the compiler calls "well known and old". اندبلت is unclear (اندبلت / انذبلت), and the
cipher's group has five signs, so the cipher may have ذبلت (record). امتص is beside a worm hole, and the draft's اشتر
is not what the master shows (record). The master solved it but did not write it out. Instead he wrote a joke under the
cipher (66v l.2-5): "O our lord, may God strengthen you, establish his case for us first, so that we may accept the
advice. By God, we are on the white ground (al-ard al-bayda'): no brown ones and no white ones." That is, he has no
beloved to follow the advice with. "White ground" is bare, empty land, and the pun runs on to brown and white
beloveds. My rendering of his words follows the record's text, which is partly doubtful. The compiler admires "the
beauty of this phrasing and the meanings and turns under it". The compiler gives the solution himself. Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 66r l.9: "then I wrote him two verses, a dubayt, well known and old"; 66v l.2-7: "he solved them
but did not write them out; instead he wrote in his blessed hand under the cipher ..." (above).

## 66v.11 (cipher 66v l.11-13, 67r l.1-2; plaintext 67r l.4-5)

**Arabic (record):**
ما قولك فيمن ظفرت كفاه بالقهوة والحب وما يهواه / هل يغتنم اللذة [?] من دنياه ام يحجم خوف كاشح يغشاه

**English:**
- What do you say of a man whose hands have laid hold
- of wine (qahwa), of the beloved, and of whatever he desires:
- should he seize the pleasure his world offers,
- or hold back for fear of a hidden enemy (kashih) who may come upon him?

**Metre:** four hemistichs rhyming in -ah, the dubayt pattern; not scanned.

**Note:** the master's mock fatwa request (istifta'), as the compiler calls it. Qahwa is wine here, the classical sense.
The kashih is the enemy who hides his hatred, a stock figure in love poetry (cf. 57r.2). The compiler solved it at once
and then did not know how to answer "the question being delicate". His answer is 67r.8. Poet: the master (the heading
says "he wrote to us"; whether the verses are his own is not stated).

**Technique note:** 66v l.10: "then he, may God extend his shade, wrote to us"; 67r l.3: "I solved it at once because
it was easy; it is in the form of a request for a legal opinion"; 67r l.6-7: "then I was at a loss what to write in
answer ...; I wrote it out and wrote the answer under it, as my judgment (ijtihad) led me".

## 67r.8 (cipher 67r l.8-12; plaintext 67v l.1-2)

**Arabic (record):**
من خمر [?] زكا فاق الشنا [?] كفاه مع حسن عفته [?] بمن انشاه / سلمت اموره الى مولاه لا آمر بها ولا انهاه

**English:**
- Whoever [من خمر زكا, not understood] ... the kaf of winter (?) is enough for him,
- with good chastity [?], (and trust) in Him who brought him into being.
- I have committed his affairs to his Lord:
- I neither command him to them nor forbid him.

**Metre:** four hemistichs rhyming in -ah, the dubayt pattern, answering 66v.11; not scanned.

**Note:** the compiler's answer to the istifta'. The second verse is clear: he neither allows nor forbids and leaves
the questioner to God. The first verse is doubtful in the record, and its cipher groups break the words differently
(record). The compiler's comment gives the key word as كاف الشنا: "I found that the kaf of al-shana ... gathers the
meanings he mentioned, and more, and in it is what is permitted and what is not" (67v l.5-6). I take this to be كاف
الشتا, "the kaf of winter": the comforts of winter whose names begin with kaf, which include wine and a woman as well
as lawful things like a shelter and a brazier. That explains "permitted and not permitted", and الشنا / الشتا differ
only in their dots. The written فاق الشنا would then be the same phrase, with فاق in place of كاف (see the suspected
errors). This is my reading, not the record's. The rest of the comment: "no other answer was possible, since wishing
for this is outside (the law) and commanding it is not allowed, so I got out of it this way. If I were asked why I did
not forbid it, I would say: because some of it is permitted; or why I did not command it: because some of it is
dangerous." The cipher has آمره for the written آمر (record). Poet: the compiler.

**Technique note:** 67r l.13: "he solved it quickly and sent it, and it is"; 67v l.3-8: the compiler's comment (above).

## 67v.9 (cipher 67v l.9-13, 68r l.1; plaintext 68r l.3-4)

**Arabic (record):**
ما احسن ما مشى الى الشمع وقط ما انعم خده وما انعم قط / قد خط عذاره وما يعرف خط ما اعشقني به اذا نام وخط

**English:**
- How fine it was when he walked to the candle and trimmed (qatta) it!
- How soft is his cheek, and he has never (qatt) granted a favour (ma an'ama)!
- His first down (idhar) has traced its line (khatta), though he does not know how to write (khatt);
- how I love him when he sleeps and snores (khatta)!

**Metre:** four hemistichs on the rhyme -att, the dubayt pattern; not scanned.

**Note:** a dubayt on a beautiful youth, built on full puns. Qatt is "he trimmed (the wick)" and "never". Ma an'ama is
first "how soft!" and then "he never granted a favour". Khatt is "it traced (the line of the down on his cheek)",
"writing" and, I take it, "he snored" (khatta for snoring; my reading, but the rhyme-word wants a third sense). The
down on the cheek as "writing" is a stock image. The master wrote it, and the compiler solved it at once. The cipher
has 3 signs for به (record). Poet: not checked.

**Technique note:** 67v l.8: "and he wrote me, with that"; 68r l.2: "I solved it at once because it was easy, and it is".

## 68v.13 (cipher 68v l.13, 69r l.1-5; plaintext 69r l.7-10)

## 71r.10 (cipher 71r l.10-12; plaintext 72r l.4-5, reconstructed)

**Arabic (record):**
حالة الدولاب دلت انه في فرط حزن / كان يسقي ويغني صار يسقى ويغني

**Cipher form (reconstruction, per 72r l.9-10):** انه الدولاب دلت انه في فرط حزن / كان يسقي ويغني صار يسقى ويغني

**English** (the written couplet):
- The state of the water-wheel (dulab) has shown
- that it is in an excess of grief:
- it used to give water and sing;
- now it is given water (yusqa), and sings.

**English** (the cipher form): "The moan (annat) of the water-wheel has shown that it (annahu) is in an excess of
grief ...", with the rest the same.

**Metre:** majzu' al-ramal (both forms scan).

**Note:** the plaintext here is not written out on the page in the form that was enciphered. The page gives Ibn
al-Wardi's couplet with حالة (72r l.4-5). It then says that the encipherer, Amin al-Din, put انه in place of حالة "for
the paronomasia, to make it hard to solve" (72r l.9-11): annat "moan" against annahu "that it". The record's count
fits the انه form (48 signs, 48 letters), but the word groups are not matched one by one, so the fit is established
only for the total. The conceit is that the creaking wheel once watered the garden and "sang", and now it is the one
that "is watered", dripping as if weeping, and its song is a lament. The compiler solved it and wrote under it "This is
our merchandise returned to us" (Q 12:65). He explains on 71v-72r that the conceit was his own. He had told it to Zayn
al-Din 'Umar b. al-Wardi in Aleppo, and Ibn al-Wardi then said the two verses were his. The master solved it too, but
saw the point of the quotation only later. The compiler's own longer version (71v l.8-13, not this cipher; it does not
fit the count) runs: كنت غصنا في الدوح ... / انه الدولاب دلنا انه مفرط الوجد وبالدمع يبوح / كان يسقي ويغني زمنا صار
يسقى ويغني وينوح, "I was a branch in the great tree ... The moan of the water-wheel showed us that it is excessive in
passion and reveals it with tears; for a time it used to give water and sing, now it is given water, and sings, and
wails." Sender: Amin al-Din 'Abd al-Wahhab b. Ruhban [?] al-Mizzi al-Hanafi, "today qadi of Hama" (71r l.7-9). Poet:
Ibn al-Wardi, as he claimed (the compiler disputes the conceit, not the wording; not checked).

**Technique note:** 71r l.7-9: "and Amin al-Din ... qadi of Hama ... wrote to me"; 71r l.13: "I solved it and wrote
under it: this is our merchandise returned to us"; 72r l.9-11: "but he used انه in place of حالة from Zayn al-Din's two
verses, for the paronomasia, to make it hard to solve; so he knew I had solved it from what I wrote under it".

## The compiler on his method: two passages

### The boast on 61v (61v l.11-13, 62r l.1-2; from the technique note of 61r.3)

**Arabic (record):**
فبالله العجب من هذا الذهن العظيم اقسم بالله لو عاش ابن عدلان / او ابن دنينير او ابن الرومي او الكندي وشاهدوا هذا الحل / لاعظموه [?] وتركوا جميع ما ذكروه في كتبهم وما ادعوه من [62r:] الفضيلة ... والله اني لو كتب لي / مثل هذا لعجزت عنه ودليله انه كتب لي هذا البيت الموعود به

**English:**
"By God, what a wonder is this great mind! I swear by God that had Ibn 'Adlan, or Ibn Dunaynir, or Ibn al-Rumi, or
al-Kindi lived and witnessed this solution, they would have held it in awe [?] and abandoned everything they set down
in their books and the merit they claimed for themselves. ... By God, had the like of this been written to me, I would
have failed at it. The proof is that he wrote me this promised verse" (62r.3, which the compiler then could not finish).

**Note:** the solution praised is the master's solving of 61r.3, a list of paired imperatives that he himself called
"more like riddles than ciphers". The compiler ranks it above the whole written tradition of the craft. He names
al-Kindi, Ibn 'Adlan and Ibn Dunaynir (whose book is cited at 49r), and also an "Ibn al-Rumi" whom the record does not
identify (not checked). The passage bears on the method in two ways. Piece-by-piece solving by a gifted reader is set
against the rules in the manuals. And the compiler's admission of failure leads straight into 62r.3, where he did fail
and the master finished the verse. The words between الفضيلة and والله are not given in the record.

### The red-letter convention on 64v (64v l.2-11; from the technique note of 64r.3)

**Arabic (record):**
ومعنى هذا الترجم ابهم واحسن وحله المخدوم المشار اليه / لكنه لم يكتبه بخطه وانما كتب عليه الزاي على / حرفها من الازار والكاف على حرفه من مكلثم والثا على حرفها / منه والحا على حرفها من لحاجة والجيم على حرفه منها والعين / على حرفها من يرفع والطا على حرفها من طعنت والنون والثا [التا] / على حرفهما منها والضاد على حرفه من بعض والشين على حرفه / من مشفو والقاف على حرفه منها والسين على حرفه من / سللت والصاد على حرفه من مص ثم كتب تحته بخط السعيد / هذه الاشارات كافية فلينظر لي هنا التمكن والاستعداد / والفطن وحسن التخلص والبراعة فالله يمتع الوجود ببقائه

**English:**
"The meaning of this cipher is more obscure and finer. The master referred to solved it, but he did not write it out
in his own hand. He only wrote over it: the zay on its letter in al-izar; the kaf on its letter in mukaltham, and the
tha on its letter in the same (word); the ha on its letter in li-haja, and the jim on its letter in the same; the 'ayn
on its letter in yarfa' [the plaintext has rufi'a]; the ta (ṭā') on its letter in ta'anta, and the nun and the ta on
theirs in the same; the dad on its letter in ba'd; the shin on its letter in mashfu, and the qaf on its letter in the
same; the sin on its letter in salalta; and the sad on its letter in mass. Then he wrote under it in his blessed hand:
'These indications are sufficient.' So let one see here the mastery, the readiness, the acumen, the elegance of the
way out (husn al-takhallus) and the skill. May God let the world enjoy his long life."

**Note:** "its letter" (harfuha) means the cipher sign that stands for that letter. The master wrote a plaintext
letter over one sign for each of thirteen letter-types, which gives fourteen marks, since the ta is marked twice (ṭā'
and tā'). He did not write the solution out. Every other occurrence of each sign follows from these marks, and the
letters he left unmarked (alif, lam, waw, ha, and so on) are presumably the ones the compiler would get without help.
The record notes that this is the convention the red glosses elsewhere in the book follow, and that the 64r copy itself
carries no letters over the signs. Two details matter for the text of 64r.3. The 'ayn is said to be in يرفع, where the
written plaintext has رفع. And a qaf is placed in the word written مشفو, which has no qaf as written (see 64r.3 and the
suspected errors). The record emends one word of the list, والثا to [التا].

## Suspected Arabic errors

None of these readings has been changed above; each is a suspicion for the next look at the master.

1. **67r.8** فاق الشنا: the compiler's own comment names "the kaf of al-shana" (كاف الشنا), and the permitted /
   forbidden contents he describes are those of the "kafs of winter", كاف الشتا. الشنا / الشتا share their letter
   skeleton and differ only in dots. فاق may stand for كاف (or the compiler wrote a play on it). The cipher groups of
   that line break the words differently (record). Strong for الشتا, moderate for كاف.
2. **64r.3** بعض / مشفو / مص: (a) بعض and يعض share the skeleton, and "it bites its bite" (يعض عضه) parallels "it
   sucks its sucking" (مص مصه). (b) The master's marks put a qaf "in" the word written مشفو, so the word is probably مشفق
   (the cipher group has the written word's length, so the fourth sign may be the qaf). (c) The cipher has 3 signs for مص
   (record), which fits يمص. With يعض, مشفق and يمص the second verse scans in kamil, and as written it does not. Moderate
   to strong.
3. **72r.12** فهمت: the sari' first verse scans better with فهت ("what you uttered"), and the sense is better too.
   There is a gap before لفظا on the page (record), which may mark a lost or deleted word. Moderate.
4. **62r.3** شحطت ديار كم: the first three cipher groups hold 15 signs for 10 letters (record), and the verse does not
   scan as read. The solving story says the opening has a "word of two words" in Mushaf orthography and was guessed to
   begin with يا, which the written text does not show. Strong for "the opening is not as written", weak for what it is.
5. **61r.3** اسر: set against بع "sell", اشر "buy" (ishri) would complete the pair. شر خر, لجمل (for اجمل), هزل and
   الخلص are doubtful in the record, and the run-together forms (صلا قطع, مرانه, اسربع, اغدافن, خرزد, خذاعط) are kept
   as written. Moderate for اسر.
6. **65v.2** خز[?]ج وخرج, ورجع, خاسبه: the cipher has تخرج وتخرج, وترجع, تحاسبه, which match their groups exactly
   (record). ولسعوا against the cipher's ولشعوا. The written forms may be the compiler's variants or slips. Strong that
   the two differ; whether the written form is an error is open.
7. **63v.1** الاحور [?]: الاحور or الاحوز on the master (record), not الاحول. ثلثه is under a smudge. Neither the
   point of the second verse nor the right word is settled. Open.
8. **65r.5** حزين [?]: as an eighth physical quality it makes no sense, and it repeats the rhyme-word of the clear
   lead-in verse. The record reads حزيو / حرنو. Moderate that حزين is wrong.
9. **70r.11** زرني سعدي: the first basit foot does not scan as read, and the cipher divides the first word 2 + 2. The
