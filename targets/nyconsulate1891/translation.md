# English translations of the contemporary archival texts

Parallel English renderings of the six dispatches' contemporary Russian texts in `contemporary_texts.md` (with bracketed omissions preserved). For the unedited mechanical output of the 472-entry partial table on each cipher sheet, see `3902_decode.txt` through `4166_decode.txt`.

## No. 3902 (1/13 August 1891)
In answer to your reports Nos. 266 and 267 of 22 July of this year, I have the honour to inform you that circular No. 3893, enciphered with cipher No. 336, which is in your possession, asks you to return that withdrawn cipher by a safe opportunity. As for the circular of 28 June 1888, sent twice — on 5 July 1889 as No. 3313 and on 1 July 1891 as No. 3887 — and mistakenly enciphered with key No. 356, which you do not possess, it announces that cipher No. 375 entered general use on 1 January 1889.

## No. 4945 (22 February 1895)
Key No. 381 will enter general use in July of this year. After the first of that month, you are asked to return the withdrawn keys Nos. 356 and 375 by a safe opportunity or by Russian post. *(The surviving cipher fragment on frame 732 breaks off after 60 three-digit codes.)*

## No. 5078 (22 March 1895)
Because papers enciphered abroad very often arrive without a full stop, or a full stop and dash, set at the end — which makes deciphering them very difficult, especially when there are mistakes in the text — I consider it necessary to confirm that setting those punctuation marks is strictly obligatory. Furthermore, when dividing numbers into groups of three or five digits for transmission by telegraph, one must not pad the final group with initial digits or zeroes, as is frequently done, but leave one, two, three, or four digits as they fall, adding nothing to them.

## No. 5015 (10 March 1895)
*[Continuing the French cover: "...and to draw Your attention to the fact that"]* you should not have taken it upon yourself to return keys three-five-six and three-seven-five, formerly in Mr Artsimovich's possession, since they are still in use. Mr Artsimovich would have been notified of their return in due time. You should have pointed this out to Mr Artsimovich.

## No. 519 (5/17 April 1895)
I have the honour to acknowledge receipt of the circular of 22 February of this year, No. 4945. Cipher No. 375 will be returned by me at the first opportunity after 1 July; as for cipher No. 356, [...] and in accordance with the communication of the First Expedition at the Chancellery of 1 August 1891, No. 3902, the Consulate General has not had it during my tenure.

## No. 4166 (18 February 1893)
All the old keys formerly in your possession in San Francisco, which you handed over together with keys Nos. 356 and 375 to Mr Hansen in New York for delivery to Washington — the foregoing is noted on your written declaration of 28 November 1890. *(In the French cover on frames 742–743 the Ministry recalls the promise to return those materials via Baron Schilling and asks why the annexes to circulars Nos. 3485 and 3916 were not returned; Olarovsky's reply No. 231 on frames 746–747 reports that the keys were still in the legation's fireproof safe in Washington.)*
