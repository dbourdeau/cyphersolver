# The turning grille: preregistered measure on a held-out telegram (FAILED)

## Protocol (fixed before the cipher was sent to the model)
- Held out, never sent: the whole telegram CMXV.16.1561.3-13 (Adolfo González, San Antonio, to Olliphant, 19 April 1911, 127 letters = 24 groups of 5 + 4 + 3, no gloss, no clear, not in the typed list CMXV.18.1795.1). File hashed (SHA-256 4a43a131...); a leak check (its groups and every 7-letter run of its cipher) found nothing in the files sent.
- Sent to Claude Fable 5.2 in one API run: the cipher of 1551.3-4, 1561.5-13 and 1569.3-10, the earlier failed attempts, context, and the fonds' key cards.
- Statistic car4: share of the letters of the clear produced that a maximal segmentation covers with Spanish words of four letters or more (period lexicon plus names of the traffic). The simpler statistic with short words was rejected in advance: shuffled letters of a transposition already reach 0.77.
- Controls: 200 random grilles of the same family (8 x 8, one hole per rotation orbit, random direction and order) and 200 shufflings of the held-out letters deciphered with the proposed grille.
- Pass: car4 >= 0.70 and car4 >= best control + 0.30, one run.

## Result (12 Nov 2026, one run)
car4 = 0.520; random grilles at most 0.402 (mean 0.166); shuffled letters at most 0.299 (mean 0.139); margin +0.118. **Not validated**: both thresholds missed.

Cause: the held-out telegram has 127 letters instead of 128; one letter is lost in the group of four, YPXB (20th group). The model had fixed in advance where the missing letter would be padded (at letter 64), so the first block of 64 came out intact and the second block shifted. The first block reads, with no repair: "opino armisticio equivaldria destruccion fuerzas insurgentes deben atac..." ("I think an armistice would amount to destroying the insurgent forces; they must attack ..."). The model had warned before the measure that a lost letter would put the threshold out of reach in about nine cases out of ten.

A post-hoc trial with the model's own rule for a short group (written before the measure, but kept out of the key it submitted) gives a readable second block. It was run after the measure and is **not counted**; the held-out telegram is spent. Since 13 Nov 2026 the telegram's cipher is published (issue #502, `transcription.txt`), so the measure's inputs can be compared, and the telegram is read in both blocks (see below); the failed measure itself stays reported as failed.

## What supports the grille instead (not a preregistered validation)
- Five blocks of 64 letters read as fluent Spanish with no repair at all: both blocks of 1551.3-4, the first block of 1561.5-13, the first two of 1569.3-10 (`decryption_grille.txt`), with one grille unchanged across three telegrams, 18 to 20 April and both directions.
- The nulls fall where a grille puts them: the 17 X/Z of the second block of 1551.3-4 cover the 16 rotation orbits (in the model's own simulation, 17 random cells do so 15 times in 200,000; not rerun by us); 1569.3-10 ends with xxxzzz and the run a b c d e f g h i j.
- On the held-out telegram, the first block, the only part not shifted by the lost letter, came out as Spanish, while 200 random grilles score at most 0.402 on the whole text.
- Two repairs were declared in the pull request, both chosen while looking at the text: one letter deleted in 1561.5-13 (129 letters), one restored in 1569.3-10 (191 letters). The second falls away: the telegram has IRRXNO (192 letters) and reads with no repair (below).

## Added 13 Nov 2026 (issue #502, SeldomPhooey; checked by an independent session)

**An outside check of the grille.** The Biblioteca Nacional de México holds a typed leaf headed "COPIA DE TELEGRAMA", signed "G. A. M." (Archivo Francisco I. Madero, AMA Ms.M/T.2260 c.21; image `https://repositorio.unam.mx/covers/1750/0/500.png`, SHA-256 f37754c2...ff73, checked): "Opina oficial americano inconveniente asalto Juarez por estar minado. CONvendria mas atacar incesa[nte]mente, dividiendo ejercito tres paradas ocho horas cada una. Asi lograriase rendir guarnicion cansancio. G. A. M." It is the clear text of 1569.3-10. The grille's decryption of the 192 letters of that telegram (three blocks, no repair) agrees with the copy on 174 of the 176 letters of text (edit script rerun here: t for p in *por estar*, a doubled v in *convendria*, an i in *lograriase* that the cipher lacks). The copy is a period document from another archive, not used to find the grille. The three blocks use every cell of the 8 x 8 square, so the hole positions and their order are confirmed on all 64 cells. It does not validate the readings of the other telegrams (word cuts, corrected letters, inferred words), and it does not say when or from what the copy was made (it could be a decipherment passed on). The preregistered measure is still failed; this is a different, outside check and is recorded as such.

**The held-out telegram, read.** CMXV.16.1561.3-13 reads in both blocks with a placeholder inserted at index 96 (inside YPXB); the repair was chosen by reading among the positions 94 to 98 and does not turn the failed measure into a pass. Block 1 (64 letters, no repair, not sent to the model) is the one part of the held-out text that is out of sample for the grille: under it the text scores -4.36 per letter (Spanish 4-gram, es-modern) against -6.54 for the best of 300 random grilles of the same family (mean -7.79), checked here with a decoder written from the grid. A language-model score alone does not pick index 96 for block 2; the choice rests on words.

**Transcription corrections** (compared with the CEHM images on 13 Nov 2026): 1569.3-10 has IRRXNO (two typed r's under a pencil stroke), 1551.3-4 has ERDEO (an o with a pencil stroke on its left side). The first removes a declared repair, the second a corrected letter. The third difference of the issue (1561.5-13, 16th group, XXMSY or XXNSY) was not checked on the image; the sense supports the m read in the pull request.
