It is with real sorrow that I have learnt of the slight coolness that the difference of opinion had caused between you and M. de Vaudreuil, and I would fear that the King's service might truly suffer from it, in the circumstance in which Canada finds itself, if your zeal and M. de Vaudreuil's were less known to me. I have reason to believe that this important motive will always be a point of union between you that will never suffer from any private dispute. You have just rendered too essential a service in Canada not to set yourself above anything that might diminish its glory; I count on your finding, in your prudence and in your attachment to the good of the State, the resources you may need to end the campaign as gloriously as you began it, setting aside anything that might disturb the harmony so necessary between you and the governor general, who never ceases to do your services all the justice they deserve. Moreover, I shall not fail to let the maréchal de Belle-Isle know what you tell me about your recall, and I shall agree with him on what may be most advantageous for the colony and for you. I shall answer by the first ships leaving for Canada the various letters you have written me, and meanwhile I shall make use of the observations they contain.
