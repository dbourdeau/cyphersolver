## The slip (f. 9bis, image `_c018`): clear text, read loosely
A note written in clear, apparently an addition to the letter:
> De quoy j'ay adverty messieurs de Rodez et de Lavaur, qui m'ont escript avoir bon moyen envers luy de le
> practiquer ... ; mais j'ay eu peine de gaster la practique ... ou des femmes ... ce que je feray apres, et ce que
> je y pourray faire ... Et ce faisant le mariage de **Cosme** ... la niepce(?) ... nostre sainct pere, les
> **Vitelli** ... ceste voye, car les ... a moyen de ... la ville et chasteau(?) ... de ... a ... faire ...

The Venice ambassadors Georges d'Armagnac, bishop of Rodez, and Georges de Selve, bishop of Lavaur, are named.
The "practique" seems to be a French attempt to win over someone in Florence, probably Alessandro Vitelli, who
held the Florentine citadel (the "fortezza da basso") after Duke Alessandro de' Medici was murdered in January 1537.

## English summary and context
1. Mâcon writes from Rome on 11 April 1537, during the third war between Francis I and Charles V (1536-38). The truce
   of Bomy (Picardy/Artois) came in July 1537 and the truce of Monzón in November 1537, then the Nice truce of 1538.
2. In cipher, he reports that Pier Luigi Farnese, Paul III's son, has sent men to take possession of Novara. He has
   also approached a castellan (at Pisa?) to try to buy him.
3. The pope has been offered a marriage for his granddaughter, Pier Luigi's daughter Vittoria Farnese. The groom was
   almost certainly Cosimo de' Medici, the new ruler of Florence; the slip names "Cosme". Mâcon thinks the pope may
   use it to throw himself entirely to the Emperor, while pretending not to mind and hoping for Imperial support.
4. Through Latino Giovenale Manetti, the papal agent between Rome and France, the pope has let it be known that if
   the marriages are refused he will have grounds to declare himself for the Emperor. Mâcon asks Montmorency to weigh
   this.
5. Also in cipher: Francis I's alliance with the Turk "is clearly confirmed" in Rome. This was the Franco-Ottoman
   entente of 1536-37, the year of Suleiman's Apulian and Corfu campaign, and it was damaging to France with the pope.
6. A partly read passage seems to contrast Imperial strength in Italy with the French army "amusing itself in
   Picardy", where Francis was then campaigning (Hesdin, March-April 1537).
7. In clear, Alessandro Vitelli, who held Florence's fortress after Duke Alessandro de' Medici's murder (January
   1537), is said to be unhappy with the Emperor. The French were trying to win him over (see the slip and the
   Venice ambassadors Rodez and Lavaur).
8. Council: the duke of Mantua will not host the general council summoned for Mantua (23 May 1537) without a paid
   guard. His brother, Cardinal Ercole Gonzaga, has said so in consistory. The pope is thinking of moving it, and
   the council was in fact prorogued and later sent to Vicenza.
9. The close complains of money troubles and of the cost of couriers. The dispatch is sent through Venice to save
   expense.
10. Weight of the letter: the Farnese marriage politics of 1537-38 (Vittoria Farnese, Cosimo, and Novara, later
    given to the Farnese), which pulled Paul III toward Charles V despite his professed neutrality.
