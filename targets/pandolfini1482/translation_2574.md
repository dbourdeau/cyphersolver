## Historical Notes

- **Dating analysis (`27 March 1482` = `27 March 1483`):** Florentine New Year (*ab Incarnatione*) fell on 25 March. Lorenzo de' Medici's original signed letters to Pandolfini in BNCF *Autografi Palatini* VI/51–53 (20 Mar, 28 Mar, 2 Apr 1482) place Pandolfini in Urbino and Fossombrone on 26–29 March 1482 rather than as resident *Rome orator*, while Stella Fletcher (pp. 71–72 n. 38) independently cites Pandolfini's letters from Rome on 29 March and 2 April 1483. Treating `Die xxvij Martij 1482` as a 2-day New Year slip for **27 March 1483** is an inference strongly supported by this travel and diplomatic evidence, aligning `#2574` with the **Diet of Cremona** (late Feb – early Mar 1483, *"alla dieta"*), the delayed Venetian interdict bull (*"indugiare la publicatione"*, finalized 24 May and published 25 May 1483), Andrea Jamometić's Basel schism crisis (*"il fatto dell'arcivescovo"*), and `#2559` (April 1483). See also Eleonora Plebani's study of Pandolfini's Roman embassy (`iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/952345`), against which publication priority remains open.
- **Open nomenclator candidates:** `{oo?}` = Count Girolamo Riario (seeking Faenza and dominating Pope Sixtus IV); `{B?}` = the Venetians (*i Viniziani*); `{invT?}` = a condottiere re-engaged at the Diet of Cremona (candidate: Costanzo Sforza, Virginio Orsini, or Niccolò Orsini, Count of Pitigliano); `{?grandes}` sits in a subject slot before *pigliassi* and may be a Latin cover noun rather than a pure null.
