# Alamanni to Piero de' Medici, Vigevano, 26 April 1494: English, whole letter

ASF, Mediceo avanti il Principato LXXIV doc. 96, cc. 220-221. A full translation, sentence for sentence, of the Italian
text in `aaymeloglu/alamanni-reading.md` (section "Italian (whole letter)": Andrew Aymeloglu's reading of the clear hand
and of the cipher passages with the key sheet MAP LXXII 183). The contributor's own English in that file abridges the
clear text of c. 220r and parts of cc. 220v-221v; this file gives all of it. Made 14 Nov 2026 by the project (Claude,
session of 14 Nov 2026) from the Italian alone; no image was looked at.

Same holes as the Italian: *italics* = cipher passages; `[...]` = not read in the clear hand; `[?]` = an unread cipher
group; (?) = a doubtful reading; words in square brackets are supplied for the English. Name number 22 is left as
[22] (probably Signor Lodovico; see the note in the Italian file).

## Translation

**[220r]** Magnificent Piero. I have seen what you tell me in your separate letter, and I will answer you briefly. Know
first that I have taken no displeasure at the reinstatement of messer Guido and Piero, nor have I ever disapproved in
any part of your decision etc. I only differed a little as to the manner, so as to be able at least to come to that
other part etc. As to the protest that was left aside: I should be sorry if it had been suggested to you that my advice
had entered into it; because, as I told you in my other letter, I did not see(?) the commissions until afterwards, and
there was no occasion(?) to make it. Yet this does not trouble me at all either, and I am content, if it had suited
others(?) to lay this burden on me, to bear it; because I believe [...] faith of the truth, and this matters little.
[The envoy ... had](?) had good order; and afterwards it came(?) to making the protest, as you will see from my
enclosed [...]. And I have made it and repeated it several times and in the presence of witnesses, and pricked(?) this
man with such words to move him that at another time I would have thought to make him jump. Nevertheless you see that
he has not changed. And of this I will leave the gloss to be made there by you. And about the discourse you say the
Count of Caiazzo made to your(?) ambassadors, not being informed, I cannot answer you.

About the surety to be given to this man, I have nothing more to say, because you see that we have [...].

You will see, my Piero, from the enclosed what twistings these are of Signor Lodovico, and lately what effects begin to
be seen. This morning he told these royal [envoys] the same about the galleys being made at Genoa. Of which(?) these
royal [envoys] tell me they too have notice. And His Excellency(?) having been asked that **[220v]** they, if they were
passing(?), they give me to understand that he answered he was not yet advised by his own people; but that this
Monsignor of [...] told him that on this side of [...], and already three [...]; and fifty(?) of them [...] afterwards.
So that, gathering together all these movements of his, and seeing the provisions he makes at Genoa for the fleet, and
this other(?), [I do not] know what to say. And considering the chapter you send me *from Naples* of what *the King*
says about the matter of *turning Genoa* [...](?), I am confirmed, although in my previous letter I was a little
restrained, that this would be the only medicine for the cure of *all this ill*; and that if one seeks(?) anyone to
[try?] *[22]* *[?] [?] [?]* and good counsels in the mouth(?): to repair all the harms, this is the one and only thing.
But that anyone should be for *delaying it*, because perhaps later one will want *to do it in time*, one will not be
able. And it will be necessary that we have not already run into it(?). But I would indeed remind you with affection
that entering into a thing of this nature requires going into it with such preparations, and foreseeing it so far won,
that one *went(?) to manifest victory*; and I should much like that *you here(?) you should have(?) to come into the
open* for every good reason, and it would also better serve the mind of the King [of F...](?), *that is, of Naples*, in
any event. But *under cloak* [the cipher word is read mante[?]o] rather you should offer them such help as you could,
with that supposition of drawing from it some way(?) quite particular: of those that I do not know whether they can
ever be hoped for(?). *While Genoa stays as it is now* **[221r]** and in sum, if you could be in time, or *saw [a way]
to make it(?) clean(?)*, I believe it would be the salvation *of King Alfonso(?) and ours*. And the hope they want to
have *at Naples* of seeing well the disposition of *[22]* to come(?), the truth seems to me superfluous; because
already sure of the effects [...]; and do not believe that *[22]*, even if he had more *security* than is possible,
*would ever trust the King*, nor even(?) that he should have two states; because [...], who for [...] wants *so much
greatness(?) of his*, judging it not to his purpose, nor to yours there. And there is no doubt that to come to this
effect he will try everything. And if he gets a footing *with the fleet [?] Genoa*, see where things will slide(?).
Nevertheless I defer in everything to your opinion and judgment, for I am certain you will examine well both the nature
of the matter and of the men(?), and will resolve well. But for once it could not please me more, when the [...]
necessary(?) part were recognised in it. I do beg you earnestly(?) that of this opinion of mine you speak this time to
no living soul; because I would not want the charge laid on me that I *wanted war to put the city in turmoil*. As you
know happened to me another time, that to me it seems to bargain in this way [for] both quiet and rest; and God knows
whether I desire [it] as much as another.

I am certain that you will interpret very well all the questions Signor Lodovico has put to me. It seems to me to be an
epilogue of what he said the other day on the matter of Pisa and of the [restitution?] [...] **[221v]** [...] way. And
in the end he wanted to show us three things that lie in him *to be able [?] [?] [?]*, the first *of Genoa*. The other
of France, which here they had(?) *much held [?] [?] [?] [?]*. The third *of closing to us the road* of the merchants
in the West, France, Spain and such places, believing by these means, or by putting us under suspicion, that [...]
perhaps(?) there may arise in the [city?] [ill] contentment(?), which could have some bad effect. And although I have
worked a good deal to root this out of him(?) in this part, yet I fear that he still has some hold there. And it seems
to me that in the talk of our affairs he has someone who tells him more of the particulars from there than I know
myself: which is a bad business indeed. One Paulo da [...](?), a soldier, who came here to buy horses(?), [whom] you
recommended to me by a letter of yours, [...] with these people [...] from there under [...] of buying horses. Nor do I
know whether they ever sent him to see how things stand, or to lead someone astray(?), because he has been a good while
at [...]. Nothing else. Farewell. Vigevano, 26 April 1494. Petrus Alamannus, knight etc.

With this will be a letter(?) of mine to [...]: I beg(?) you to have it delivered.
