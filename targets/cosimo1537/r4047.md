## What the cipher passages say

Cosimo writes about a "motu proprio" (an act that the pope issues on his own initiative) and about how to use it. These points come from a partial read:

- The matter "would turn to our great advantage, more than theirs".
- He hopes that they will do it, and he says that it is right to act "at the right time and place".
- He wants to make them feel "a great suspicion".
- When "this fear" is taken away, "another suspicion" stays "in this cause".

## English translation

The English of my public page, unchanged, passage by passage.

1. … the motu proprio … to do it
2. this would turn to the great … advantage of us more than of them
3. … opinion, and it is to be believed that they will do it
4. … in such cases … this … their will … help
5. so it would be good that … at the right time and place
6. motu proprio, we … driven out … finding it
7. in short, act so that a great suspicion reaches them
8. … once the fear of this is taken from us, another suspicion stays … in this cause
