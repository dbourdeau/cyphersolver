# R4039: English translation

of the marriage alliance [...] I am very sorry for the distress [...] and trouble in which I have seen you by the tenor of your letters concerning the [...] reports [...] yet I have persuaded myself that, knowing my intention and resolve in the matter of the wife, which from the first day until the present has always been the same [...] anything in this world could [...] dispose of me than in this [...] knowing the nature of the said [man] even better than I do must have spared you much displeasure.

Now whatever remained to you [...] in this letter I write to the said [man] as much as you will see by the enclosed copy, in order to cut off this negotiation entirely as too contrary [...] not only to my mind and intention but to the need [...] of my affairs at this time. I tell you that [...] both for [...] effect [...] and on your part [...] you should say [...] and show [...] that one cannot trust or rely upon the words of [...] whose nature is such as you know, for the fear he has of losing that penitentiary [office] of his [...] and the desire to win for himself [...] would not stop dreaming up whatever [...] fits that purpose [...] and putting into practice everything he dreams up.

When he passed through here [...] I made it very clear that regarding a wife [...] I was ready to speak [...] as much as His Majesty might command, but at that time not even to think of it [...] until the other negotiations reached an end such that I were reduced to the necessity of thinking of something else. In short, I did everything to cut off this talk [...] and take this negotiation out of his hands [...] all this [...] his affair, although [...] at the very least I expected something similar from him, knowing what he had done to Duke Alessandro of good memory, and remembering what he had done to me upon his arrival [...] when he sought to draw me [...] in short, his is a nature so thoughtless that one cannot imagine worse [...] I believe, as many do, that it is malice, having seen that he harms those he would wish to help as much as those he intends to harm.

It seems to me [...] to have heard something similar in the recommendation he made [...] however it may be, you have my will: go on remedying this trouble in the best way you know and can, remembering that I do not want the folly of others to harm me in any way [...] does what [...] it is right and just that at times what one expects should befall [...] this I say [...] since he has gone so far forward without purpose or any commission, I wish him to turn back rather to his own shame than to my harm.
