## What the cipher passages say

Cosimo writes about a marriage alliance and "the matter of the wife". These points come from a partial read:

- His intention in the matter of the wife has always been the same, from the start until now.
- He encloses a copy of a letter. Its aim is to cut off this negotiation entirely, because it is too contrary to his mind.
- He warns Niccolini about one man. That man is not to be trusted, and he fears to lose "his penitentiary" (an office).
- The same man acted against Duke Alessandro "of good memory", and against Cosimo at his arrival.
- Cosimo was ready to speak of a wife only as far as "His Majesty" commanded.
- He asks Niccolini to remedy the harm as best he can. He does not want "the folly of others" to harm him.

## English translation

The English of my public page, unchanged, passage by passage.

1. of the marriage alliance … it grieves me much … and the distress that they cause, in which I have seen you, by the tenor of your letters
2. yet I am persuaded that you know my intention and my decision in the matter of the wife. From the start until the present day it has always been the same.
3. … anything in this world could … dispose of me
4. that you know the nature of the said man even better than I do has spared you much displeasure
5. in this letter I write to the said man … as you will see by the enclosed copy, to cut off this negotiation entirely, as too contrary … to my mind and intention
6. say and show that one must not trust or build on the words of … whose nature is such as you know, for the fear that he has to lose that penitentiary of his
7. the desire to win himself …
8. to put into practice all that he dreamed up … when he passed through here
9. that of a wife I would speak … as far as His Majesty commanded, but at that time not even to think of it, because the other negotiations had brought me to the need to think of other things
10. in short I did everything to cut off … the talk, and to take that negotiation out of his hands
11. I expected such a thing from him, because I knew what he had done to Duke Alessandro of good memory, and I remembered what he had done to me at his arrival
12. in short, it is a nature so thoughtless that one cannot imagine more … I believe, as many do, that it is malice
13. you have my will: go on and remedy this trouble in the best way that you know and can, and remember that I do not want the folly of others to harm me in any way
14. … since he has gone so far … without any commission, I want him to go back rather …
