Source: NARA M30 roll 4, frame 0273, right-hand page (M30-004-0273.jpg). Clear text in a
clerk's hand headed "No. 59.  Trip.  London Decr. 23. 1797." It gives in words the despatch
that frames 0272 (right page) and 0273 (left page) carry wholly in code. The clerk left two
query marks that match code groups in the bare copy: "me*" with "364" written beneath
(group 364, line 22) and a "+" after "might have".

Read from a 1568-px view of the page; spelling as written, long s modernised.

Dear Sir,
This Cabinet last night after several days consideration, gave a decided negative to a
proposal of peace made by the Directory, thro' Talleyrand. The project was in detail, the
terms more favorable to England than those demanded by Lord Malmesbury at Lisle. The price
was a bribe of a million sterling to be divided among Directors, Ministers and others.
Talleyrand's department was to share one hundred thousand pounds sterling. I could name the
persons employed, the stages and every circumstance of the overture, so that you may place
perfect confidence in the information. This decision has been made at a time when it is
uncertain what part will be adopted by the new king of Prussia: when England would be glad
to make a solid peace, and if oeconomy alone had influenced her, would have paid the bribe
to obtain it, in case she could have had confidence in the corrupt agents with which she
might have + made the bargain. This and some former information have been obtained in a way
that forbids its publication in this shape or as received from me* [364]. Others as well as
myself would thereby be placed in a situation that would be insupportable.
With perfect respect & esteem
I have the honor to be
Dr. Sir, your obt. & faithful servt.
Rufus King
