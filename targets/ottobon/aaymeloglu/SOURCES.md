# Sources

## Manuscript and key

- **Target:** Biblioteca Nacional de España, **Mss/994, ff.34–38**, covering dispatch catalogued **27 April 1589**; cipher occupies ff.35r–38r. [DECODE R2252](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/2252), [public BNE viewer](https://bnedigital.bne.es/bd/es/viewer?id=91753d62-e2f8-4fcc-8947-9e56a53af1b5&page=30). Clear salutation names Pasquale Cicogna and Giovanni Mocenigo; Marco Ottobon subscribes. The enclosed reply dates itself 24 April 1589.
- **Key:** Archivio di Stato di Venezia, **B4 Reg.16 f.64, “Ziffra prima”**, [DECODE R1789](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/1789), encoder and decoder tables. The key's exact date and intended correspondents are not established here.
- **Starting reference:** Satoshi Tomokiyo, [Unsolved Historical Ciphers](https://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/unsolved.htm). Inclusion here makes no claim about prior decipherment or publication priority.

## Provenance note

The manuscript is bound in BNE Mss/994, a volume of papers of Luis Valle de la Cerda. Its title page for this item, f.34r, states that the cipher letter from Venice was given in Turin by the Infanta Doña Catalina, on the Duke of Savoy’s orders, to Valle de la Cerda, who deciphered it, and that neither key nor decipherment survives with it. The 1589 Spanish decipherment has not been located; a comparison with it, if found, would settle the remaining gaps. On f.38r the address from the outer leaf survives as a reversed ink offset.

## Possible plaintext witness

An [editorial note in *Relazioni degli ambasciatori veneti al Senato*, III/II (1916)](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Pagina%3AVenezia_%E2%80%93_Relazioni_degli_ambasciatori_veneti_al_Senato%2C_Vol._III%2C_Parte_II%2C_1916_%E2%80%93_BEIC_1906568.djvu/242) cites **Senato-Segreti, reg.87, c.46r–v**, for Francesco Contarini's commission of 27 April 1589. This is a different document, but identifies the chronological vicinity in a plausible series for a registered copy.

The official catalog has entries for [Secreti register 87](https://asve.arianna4.cloud/patrimonio/19dba575-2ea7-495c-83d9-2e09693bfc87) and [Rubrica 5, March 1560–February 1590](https://asve.arianna4.cloud/patrimonio/90cd5baf-bd49-46f3-aca0-987b7e7a344f). Neither returned attached images or child objects in the public catalog on 16 September 2026. No exact copy or folio of the Gondi correspondence has been identified.

The checked April 1589 sequence and Gondi index entries of the [*Calendar of State Papers, Venetian*, vol.8, 1581–1591 (1894)](https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.01122) did not supply a matching text. That selected calendar's silence does not establish that no registered copy survives.

A secondary witness to the substance of the reply: [Romanin, *Storia documentata di Venezia*, VI (1857), p.409](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_UZZZEKyBT1AC), summarising the Senate registers, says that Venice, asked for assistance by Henri III in 1589, granted a loan of money but excused itself from other aid because it had to watch Spanish movements in Italy, while doing every good office with Sixtus V to obtain gentler treatment for the king. His footnote cites **Secreta, 3 giugno 1589**. The loan belongs to a later deliberation than the 24 April reply, which promises nothing but intercession; the reasons given match. This confirms that the French-aid deliberations of 1589 are in Secreta register 87 and gives a second date, 3 June, to include in any reproduction request. Checked 18 September 2026.
