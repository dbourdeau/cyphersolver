# Ottobon–Mocenigo: Italian text and English translations

BNE Mss/994, ff.35r–38r. Covering dispatch, 27 April 1589; enclosed reply to Gondi, 24 April 1589. Prepared 16 September 2026 from the manuscript photographs and the separately preserved Venetian key, DECODE R1789, “Ziffra prima.”

**Substantially deciphered, with remaining lacunae and editorial repairs.** Square brackets identify doubtful readings, supplied text, or unrecovered passages. They must not be removed when quoting the result as manuscript evidence.

This reading regularizes spelling, accents, doubled consonants, and punctuation. It expands recurring honorifics and joins words across lines. It is separate from the [line-by-line transcription and literal key output](TRANSCRIPTION.md). That file preserves such spellings as *fato*, *inteligentia*, *inteponer*, and *inportant[i/a]ssimi*. Ordinary spelling modernization is not a claim that the additional letters are encoded.

The [README](README.md) identifies the manuscript and key, gives short verification examples, and summarizes the remaining gaps. The full line references below count cipher lines within each folio.

## Covering dispatch: Italian

### f.35r1–7: Gondi’s arrival and audiences

Vegnù in questa città [fin] 15 del mese presente, vi [ricevemmo] il signor Gerolamo Gondi, et il […] quale che ci espose delli travagli et bisogni presenti di Sua Maestà Cristianissima. Egli così nella sua prima audientia come dopo in un’altra che ebbe da noi, commemorandoci…

*Vegnù* is the encoded Venetian participle; punctuation and its loose forward connection to Gondi are editorial. The date **15 del mese presente** is directly recovered. **[Fin]** regularizes selected literal *fen*. **[Ricevemmo]** conveys the probable sense of keyed **recessimo**: `g20=rece` is now directly established by terminal-letter comparisons in both key halves. There is no verified contraction or extra **ve**. After *et il*, a disputed sign reads `a84/a94` (*tre/c*); the extra group remains unresolved, and the following `g1=qua` is a moderately confident selection. *Bisogni presenti* regularizes the now selected literal **bisogni presente**, preserving the agreement anomaly in the diplomatic edition. The clear Latin salutation names Pasquale Cicogna and Giovanni Mocenigo; Marco Ottobon subscribes the dispatch.

### f.35r7–35v13: French request

…commemorandoci l’amor et affetione grande che quel Serenissimo re porta alla repubblica nostra, et quanto confidi nella cordiale corrispondente del affettuoso animo suo verso di lei, ne ricercò con molta instantia di sovvenirle in tanta necessità et somministrarle alcun soccorso per resistere all’imminente pericolo di quella corona, et assicurarsi dalli nemici del[la cor]ona, voltar poi le sue forze alla recuperatione di Saluzzo, [tol]togli dal signor [duca?] di Savoia nella [gu]erra che è nota a cadauno, [in]vitandoci appresso che lo [giudi?]cassimo bene a collegarsi […] et stringersi maggio[r]mente se[co] in qualche unione di lega, assicura[ndo]ne che ne [sa/se]riano fatti tutti [quelli] larghi et avantaggiosi [par]titi che [si?] potessimo desiderare. [Alla] qual sua proposta, iscusandosi no[i]…

Three restorations at the gutter, **del[la cor]ona**, **[tol]togli**, and **[quelli]**, were proposed in the independent review: the visible groups at the start of line 2 read *o n a*, and lines 3–4 give *…gli*; one to three groups are hidden at each right edge. They are conjectures, not readings. The league clause reads *magio[r]mente se[co] in qualche unione di lega*: “bind themselves more closely with him in some form of league.” The interior **r** and binding-hidden **co** remain supplied. **Qualche** is recovered from `g7`, verified separately in the key and target. *Che lo* regularizes literal *que lo*; the following binding-hidden verb prefix remains conjectural. The transition **qual sua proposta, iscusandosi** is supported by repeated-sign controls (`g6 h16 d15 d10 g38 f63`); **[Alla]** and the final **[i]** of *noi* remain supplied at the binding. The printed *corrispondente* follows the current cipher segmentation; it has not been silently replaced by the smoother *corrispondenza*. *Resistere* modernizes the source's apparent *resitir*.

### f.35v14–36r16: Venice’s answer and instructions

…co li presenti movimenti et per altri nostri gravissimi rispetti di non [po]ter[gli?] soddisfare come volentieri vorressimo poter far, [abbiamo] risposto quello che vedrete dalla copia che vi mandiamo col […] [inclusa] nelle presenti per vostra in[forma]tione, et perché, informato di questa trattatione et della nostra intentione, in evento che ve ne sia parlato da Sua Maestà Cristianissima o da alcun de’ suoi ministri, possiate in questa occorrenza governarvi conforme al senso nostro et risponder in quella sostanza che abbiamo noi fatto di qua.

The diplomatic reading has **[po]tergo**, with `c26=go` visually supported; **[po]ter[gli?]** above is an editorial repair. Likewise **[inclusa]** is the likely intended word, but its second sign looks like `a92` (numeral 8), rather than expected `a94=c`. These anomalies have not been removed from the literal transcript.

Nel qual caso farete quelli prudenti et destri officii che vi [pare/parerà?] benissimo usare, a fine che si conoscano li giustissimi rispetti che ci hanno intrattenuto di non poter in questa occasione soddisfare a noi medesimi et alla ottima dispositione nostra verso quella Serenissima corona, congiunta con un sommo desiderio di ogni grandezza et prosperità.

### f.36r–36v: intercepted correspondence

Il mastro delle poste di Francia che sta in questa città ne ha mostrato un capitolo da lettere scrittogli da quello di Lione, che coll’occasione d’inviargli lettere sue gli manda anco le quattro sole ult[im]e righe delle vostre di 28 del passato, dicendogli che il rimanente con tutte le altre [vos]tre sono state intercette in quelle [s]trade. Continuiamo senza vostre, costre[tti] a [star bene scarsi?] d’ogni vostra notitia,

accioché possiate con tant[o mag]gior stu[di]o procura[r] una strada per inviar lettere et darci lume delle co[se che?] occorrono lì alla giorna[ta].

[Formula separata: “di nostra […]”]

Dat. in nostro Ducali Palatio die XX[VII] Aprilis, Ind[iction]e 2.da. Marco Ottobon, secretario.

The postmaster paragraph is clear handwriting until *notitia*; the instruction beginning *accioché* returns to cipher. The syntactic transition is abrupt in the surviving reading and has not been repaired by inventing a missing sentence. Only **di nostra** is established in the separate formula; *secreta* remains a contextual suggestion. The footer shows **XX** before the binding; **[VII]** is supplied from the independently established dispatch date. The word *Indictione* expands securely, and the ordinal *2.da* is right: 1589 falls in the second indiction. The earlier clear-hand date **28** is legible and refers to the previous month.

## Covering dispatch: English

**Opening and request (ff.35r–35v).** Having come to this city [by] the fifteenth of the present month, Signor Gerolamo Gondi was [received] here and described the troubles and present needs of His Most Christian Majesty. Both at his first audience and afterward at another audience with us, he reminded us of the great love and affection that the Most Serene king bears our republic, and of his confidence in the cordial reciprocation of his affectionate feelings toward it. He urgently asked us to assist him in such great need and provide some relief against the imminent danger to that crown, and to secure him against his enemies […], so that he could then turn his forces toward recovering Saluzzo, […] [taken?] from him by the [Duke?] of Savoy in the war known to everyone.

The first sentence is rearranged in English to express the likely relationship of the Venetian participle to Gondi. Its verb and the precise force of the date preposition remain editorial readings, as explained above. The anomalous group after *et il* prevents an exact rendering of that connection.

He also invited us, [if we judged it advisable?], to enter an alliance […] and bind ourselves more closely with him in some form of league, assuring us that […] generous and advantageous terms would be offered, such as we might desire. [In response to] this proposal of his, we excused ourselves…

**Venice’s response (ff.35v–36r).** In view of the present disturbances and other very weighty considerations affecting us, we could not satisfy [him] as willingly as we would wish […]. We gave the answer you will see in the copy sent […] [enclosed] with this dispatch, for your information. Informed of this negotiation and our intentions, if His Most Christian Majesty or any of his ministers speaks to you about it, you will be able to conduct yourself in accordance with our views and answer in the same substance as we have here.

In that event, employ the prudent and skillful diplomatic representations which you [consider most suitable?], so that the most compelling considerations may be understood which have prevented us, on this occasion, from fulfilling our own wishes and our excellent disposition toward that Most Serene crown, accompanied by the greatest desire for its advancement and prosperity.

**Postal security (ff.36r–36v).** The French postmaster in this city has shown us an extract from a letter written to him by his counterpart in Lyon. While sending letters of his own, the latter also sends only the last four lines of your letter of 28 last month, saying that the remainder, together with all your other letters, has been intercepted on those roads. We continue without letters from you, compelled to remain [very short?] of any news from you.

[The cipher continues:] So that you may take all the greater care to find a route for sending letters and informing us of the [events which] occur there from day to day.

[Uncertain chancery formula.] Given in our Ducal Palace on 2[7] April, [second indiction?]. Marco Ottobon, secretary.

## Enclosed reply: Italian

### f.37r1–15: heading and professions of friendship

1589, a 24 aprile. Copia della risposta fatta al signor Gondi, ambasciatore di Francia.

Signor ambasciatore, la molta affetione che in tutti li tempi passati è stata portata et tuttavia porta la repubblica nostra alla Serenissima corona di Francia è così ben nota ad ognuno, quanto sia chiarissimo il cordiale amore che portiamo et la grande stima che facciamo della particolar persona di Sua Maestà Cristianissima. Per il [che?] sentiamo quel dispiacere delle [sollevazioni?] di quel regno et delli travagli della Maestà sua che comporta la reciproca buona intelligentia, et che ci sforza l’ottima dispositione et osservantia nostra verso di lei.

The cipher reads **soletione**: `h4 c74 h32 d2`, *so-le-ti-one*. The reading **[sollevazioni?]** is an explicit editorial interpretation, requiring insertion of **va** and regularization of the ending. No *va* group is visible. The later independently encoded *sollevationi* supports the proposed sense but does not supply missing ink. *Tempi* normalizes the literal keyed *tempo* after *tutti li*; the underlying key entry remains `a90 = tempo`.

### f.37r15–37v6: inability to provide corresponding help

Quanto alle richieste fatteci da Vostra Signoria in nome di quel Serenissimo re, le diciamo che li travagli di Sua Maestà Cristianissima ne sono tanto più gravi, quanto che vediamo di non poter ora, con la nostra pronta volontà verso il servitio di lei, accompagnar pari effetti, come altre volte abbiamo fatto; [poiché?] la conditione de’ tempi l’è fatta tale, per li motivi che si scrivono da diverse parti, che fa[nno] pensare et provvedere alla conser[vatione] et sicurtà delle cose nostre.

Tutta[via], per dare a Sua Maestà Cristianissima quel maggio[r] testimo[nio] che per noi si può al presente della pro[nta] nostra et continuata volontà…

Direct key review gives `g48=scriue`; with `f20=no` the literal spelling is **scriueno**, or *scriveno* with u/v regularized. The connected reading modernizes it to *scrivono*. The source's *motivi* is retained and translated in its contextual sense of reported developments or disturbances. The resumptive *l’* before *è* is retained; its glyph matches ordinary `d62 = l`. The supplied **r** in *maggior* is inside the line, not a binding loss. Other bracketed endings here are hidden wholly or partly in the binding.

### f.37v6–25: renewed papal intercession

…nel desiderare et procurare il bene, la grandezza di quella Serenissima corona et particolar comodo et servi[tio] della sua persona, da noi tanto osser[va]ta, abbiamo deliberato, come già facessimo per occasione delle cose di Saluzzo, de interponer al presente ancor con nuova et efficace istanza presso il sommo pontefice la intercessione della repubblica nostra, perché Sua Santità si muova a dovere, con la sua somma autorità, favorire ove farà bisogno la causa giu[sti]ssima della Maestà sua, nell’acquietare le sollevazioni di quel regno et ammorzare questo incendio di guerra, il qual si vede con [certissimo] danno et maggiori pericoli di tutta la cristianità si va accendendo.

Da quei officii ne speriamo noi, per la prudenza et pietà della Santità sua, di veder partorirsi alcun frutto, et da quei…

*Giustissima* repairs the apparent literal *giussima*, explicitly supplying **sti**; the source has not been proved to encode the extra syllable. *Interponer* regularizes literal *inteponer*. The diplomatic reading is **si movi a dovere**, with `h52=vi` selected from direct numeral controls; *si muova* above modernizes that form. **Certissimo** repairs the visible literal **crirtissimo** (`a55=cri`, not `a51=ce`), rather than claiming an encoded *ce*.

### f.37v25–38r9: conclusion

…medesimi potrà Vostra Magnificentia conoscere qual sia la fede dell’animo nostro verso la grandezza et ogni prosperità di lei, et verso il bene et la quiete della cristianità; et che quello che più oltre ora non facciamo non nasce da men pronta et disposta volontà, ma da assai convenientissimi et importantissimi rispetti.

Same-hand comparisons support **quello** (`g10`) and **disposta** (`g99`). The penultimate uncertain vowel permits literal *inportantissimi* or *inportantassimi*; the reading above modernizes to the intended word without claiming the glyph dispute is settled. *Fede dell’animo* expands *fe del animo*; it does not misdivide this as *fedel animo*.

Below the cipher on f.38r, faint reversed writing is the address transferred from the outer leaf while the packet was folded. Read in mirror image: *Nobili et sapienti viro Joanni Mocenigo, oratori nostro apud Serenissimum Regem Christianissimum*, matching the salutation on f.35r.

## Enclosed reply: English

**24 April 1589. Copy of the reply to Signor Gondi, ambassador of France.**

Provisional translation of the consolidated, context-assisted reading. Paragraph references identify manuscript folios and lines. The principal unresolved expression remains in brackets; smaller uncertainties are noted below.

**[f.37r, lines 3–15]**

Sir Ambassador, everyone knows so well the great affection that our Republic has always borne, and continues to bear, toward the Most Serene Crown of France. Equally clear are our heartfelt love and the great esteem in which we hold His Most Christian Majesty personally. Our mutual friendly understanding therefore makes us feel sorrow over the [uprisings?] in that kingdom and His Majesty’s troubles, as our goodwill and respect toward him also compel us to do.

**[f.37r, lines 15–24; f.37v, lines 1–3]**

As for the requests Your Lordship has made to us in the name of that Most Serene King, we tell you that His Most Christian Majesty’s troubles weigh all the more heavily upon us because we find ourselves unable at present to match our readiness to serve him with corresponding action, as we have done on other occasions. For present circumstances have been so affected by developments reported from various quarters that we must consider and provide for the preservation and security of our own affairs.

**[f.37v, lines 3–23]**

Nevertheless, to give His Most Christian Majesty the strongest testimony we presently can of our ready and continuing willingness to seek and promote the welfare and greatness of that Most Serene Crown, and the particular benefit and service of his person, whom we so greatly respect, we have resolved once again to intercede with the Supreme Pontiff through a fresh and earnest appeal by our Republic, as we previously did in connection with the affairs of Saluzzo. We seek to move His Holiness to use his supreme authority, wherever necessary, to support His Majesty’s most just cause in calming the uprisings in that kingdom and extinguishing this fire of war, which we see spreading, with [certain] harm and still greater dangers for all Christendom.

**[f.37v, lines 23–25; f.38r, lines 1–9]**

Through His Holiness’s prudence and piety, we hope to see some fruit come of these representations. From them Your Magnificence will also be able to recognize the sincerity of our intentions toward his greatness and prosperity in every respect, and toward the welfare and peace of Christendom. You will see that our failure to do more at present does not arise from any less ready or willing disposition, but from very weighty and compelling considerations.

### Translation notes

- **[Uprisings?]** is an editorial interpretation of exact keyed *soletione* (`h4 c74 h32 d2`). It proposes *sole[va]tione*, with spelling and number regularized in the reading edition. The later independently readable *sollevationi* supports the sense, but no **va** sign is visible here; a scribal omission is a hypothesis, not a recovered group.
- “As we have done on other occasions” and its following causal connection are context-assisted readings of f.37r22–23. They fit the surviving cipher groups, but the blotted vowel in *poiche* remains less secure. The final *l’* is read as a resumptive pronoun in *l’è fata tale*.
- Several small completions near the binding, especially in f.37v1–10, remain editorial. “Most just” normalizes the surviving *giussima*; “willing disposition” follows the visually selected *disposta*. “Certain” repairs literal *crirtissimo*, with `a55=cri` established by comparison. “Move His Holiness” reflects literal *si movi a dovere*, supported by `h52=vi`. The English smooths historical spelling without claiming newly recovered cipher signs. In particular, *scriueno* (key `g48=scriue` plus `f20=no`) is regularized in Italian as *scrivono* and rendered “reported” here.
- In the last paragraph, “his” takes *di lei* to refer to the king, as in the surrounding reply. The wording is compressed, and its precise pronoun attachment is not beyond doubt. *Fè del animo* is translated as sincerity of intention; *rispeti* as considerations.
