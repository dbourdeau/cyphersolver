# Christian Pentz to Christian IV, Vienna, 26 Mar / 5 Apr 1636: reading with holes

Source: `decryptions/vienna_1636-03-26.txt` (key v1, one decode, 12 Nov 2026). CAPITALS = cipher words as decoded. Lower case = Pentz's clear German between the cipher runs: a working reading from the scan, transcribed only next to the runs and not checked by a second reader. Index numbers (62, 13) are left out.

Holes: `[168]` = code number with no value; `[...]` = clear text not transcribed; `[unread: 32 29 25]` = figures not read; `W[OLL]EN` = the decode gives `wowen`, counted not read; `[P]ERPETUUM` = first figure doubtful, counted not read.

## Notes on the reading

- BERUHREN is decoded `berhuren` (the figures for u and h stand the other way round); the first pass counted it read (S) and so does this folder. Under a stricter rule it would be a hole. [measured: decryption line "S table 1 36 33 21 30 24 21 33 17 = berhuren"]
- FRIDERICUS: probably Duke Frederik (later Frederik III), Christian IV's son, whose restoration to the see of Bremen was under discussion in Vienna in March-April 1636 (Fridericia II, pp. 17-18). [derived]
- [168] occurs three times beside DIE / DERVON and may name a person or party. Not read.
- 64, 12, 63, 11, 8, 9: a later key state (v2, `key_history/KEY_pentz_v2_b110.txt`) reads 64 and 12 as index numbers from a gloss on a sister letter, and 63, 11, 9, 8 by analogy. Not used here.
