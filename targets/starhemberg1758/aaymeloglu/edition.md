# Starhemberg, Paris, 23 May 1758

## Result and status

**Substantially deciphered, with a complete proposed reading.** The German reconstruction and English translation cover the whole letter. Remaining uncertainty concerns particular words, repairs and conventions, rather than large unread passages. A fully verified, exact decipherment is not established. The manuscript collation supplies five digits absent from the Cryptiana transcription. The historical key identifies **Farinelli** as the speaker addressing the recipient, paired with **Wall** addressing the French ambassador. The two statements were made at nearly the same time and closely agreed.

The edition below distinguishes the readable reconstruction from the literal code output. Square brackets mark material lexical uncertainties and restorations; ordinary expansion of abbreviated key entries, inflection, capitalization, punctuation and doubled consonants are editorial throughout. Eleven proposed digit-edit sites, four contextual readings of99 as g, and an interpretation of the boundary group883 remain explicit assumptions. A grammatical continuous reconstruction does not authenticate those assumptions.

## German reading

### Cleartext opening

> Hochgebohrner Herr Graf!
>
> Ew. Hochgebohrn letztere an den Hof erstattete Bericht-Schreiben durch die Post, und jenes, so Dieselbe durch einen aus Cadix gekommenen französischen Courier abgehen lassen, habe richtig erhalten, und an des Herrn Hof-Canzlers Excellenz gehörig eingeschickt, und zumahlen das letztere noch den nämlichen Tag, als ich es überkommen, durch einen eben zur Abreise fertig gestandenen Courier ohne Verzug weiters expediert.

This is a lightly normalized reading of the manuscript's cleartext, not cipher output. The handwriting of *gehörig* and some minor endings is less secure than the substance: receipt of two communications and prompt forwarding to the court chancellor.

### Encrypted text

> So [stattlich?], als ich nun den Innhalt derselben gefunden, so große Hoffnun[g] mache mir, daß [es] deroselben annoch gelingen werde, die durch das dem Stat[s]secretario Wall behändigte sehr treflich gefasste Memoir so geschickt als vor[sich]ti[g] eingeleitete Handlung nunmehro immer offen zu behalten und den spanischen Hof [bis zu] solchen Resolutionen zu vermögen, die entweder dem Krieg eine vergnüglichere, als die dermahlige Gestalt ist, geben oder uns einen erspr[ieß]lichen [†] honorablen Frieden verschaffen könnten;
>
> und gibt mir zumahlen die von dem **Farinelli gegen Euer Hochgebohrn** und von dem **Wall gegen den französischen Botschafter** fast zu gleicher Zeit gemachte, genau gleichförmige Äusserung sehr große Hoffnun[g], daß schon Minorca an Spanien überlassen würde, wozu sich Frankreich [bereit?] zeige un[d] wohl leicht [en]tschließen wird, alsdann dieser Hof an dem Krieg mit Theil nehmen und andurch den [engländischen] und preußischen Hof in sehr große Verlegenheit setz[en] würde.
>
> Da ich nun Euer Hochgebohrn durch den Courier Zinner zu erstattenden Berichten mit vielem Ver[langen?] entgegensehe, so brauche indessen nichts Be[s]eres einrathen, als in dem bereits eingeschlagenen Weeg fortzufahren und den Wall so viel nur immer möglich durch dero [g]schicktes Benehmen zu weiteren Vertrauenausserungen zu vermögen.

**†** The manuscript has a defective `119` at the recto/verso join. This edition suppresses it to connect the already-readable surrounding words; the exact lost or erroneous content is not established. The competing restoration of an encoded *en* has equal digit-edit cost. The translation does not invent an extra substantive word here.

### Cleartext closing

> Ich beharre mit aller Hochachtung
>
> Ew. Hochgebohrn
>
> gehorsamster Diener
>
> Georg Graf und Herr von Starhemberg
>
> Paris den 23ten May 1758.

The signature and date are visible. The recipient's personal name is not supplied by the address or cipher; the Rosenberg identification remains contextual rather than established from this letter.

## English translation

> High-born Count,
>
> I have duly received Your Lordship's latest reports to the court sent by post, and the one you sent through a French courier who had come from Cadiz. I forwarded them to His Excellency the Court Chancellor. In particular, I sent the latter onward without delay on the very day I received it, through a courier who was already ready to depart.
>
> [Impressive?] as I found their contents, I have great hopes that you will still succeed in keeping open the negotiation so skillfully and cautiously initiated through the excellently drafted memorandum delivered to Secretary of State Wall, and in persuading the Spanish court to adopt decisions that would either give the war a more favorable turn than its present state or secure for us an advantageous and honorable peace.
>
> In particular, the closely agreeing statement made by **Farinelli to Your Lordship** and by **Wall to the French ambassador**, at nearly the same time, gives me very great hope that Minorca would indeed be ceded to Spain. France shows itself [ready?] to do this and will probably readily resolve upon it. This court would then participate in the war and thereby place the English and Prussian courts in very great difficulty.
>
> As I await with great [eagerness?] the reports Your Lordship is to send through the courier Zinner, I can advise nothing better in the meantime than to continue along the course already taken, and through your skillful dealings persuade Wall, as far as possible, to make further confidential disclosures.
>
> I remain, with the greatest respect, Your Lordship's most obedient servant,
>
> Georg, Count and Lord of Starhemberg
>
> Paris, 23 May 1758.

This translates the **reconstructed** German, including its explicit conjectures; it is not an independently surviving plaintext. *Schon* is the present literal reading before Minorca, rendered “indeed” here. Punctuation and the relationship of the clauses remain editorial. In particular, this is an expression of hope about Spain's participation, not evidence that Spain had unconditionally promised to join the war. “This court” refers in the reconstructed passage to the Spanish court; the author writes from Paris.

## Remaining uncertainty

The photographs resolve source access, not every enciphering problem. The following prevent an exact, unqualified edition:

1. **Opening adjective:** `31159 / 31217 / 31214` supplies *so / stat? / lich*. *Stattlich* is a possible reading, not authenticated wording; the English “impressive” is tentative.
2. **Manuscript errors:** eleven proposed edit sites remain. These include *es* (`31211→31221`), two selective *s* corrections, *sich*, the *bis zu* join, the defective119, *und*, two *en* corrections, and *Engelland*. See `emendations.json`; none is called an observed correction.
3. **Four99 readings:** g is required by *Hoffnung* twice, *vorsichtig* and *gschicktes*. The numerical tables instead give Prima99=*man* and Secunda99=*er*. The g headings show letterforms resembling99 (a doubled-g variant), but this does **not** prove a universal99=g code. A plausible explanation is an encipherer's confusion between the alphabet heading and its numeric code; that is an inference. Secunda99=*er* remains consistent in *Zi+n+er*, giving *Ziner*, normalized here to **Zinner**. Historical corroboration does not prove the courier's identity.
4. **Bereit and Verlangen:** both make strong contextual sense, but the precise handwritten key alternatives remain insufficiently secure. The literal outputs retain *berg/i/t* (the margin photograph also leaves some5/6 shape ambiguity at this code) and *ver/lan-lang-lau?/en*. They are bracketed rather than silently certified.
5. **Sch[o]n and grammatical expansion:** the Minorca connective is retained as *schon*. The key's compressed roots do not separately encode every modern ending or word boundary. The German and translation are editorial readings of those roots.

## Reproduction and source handling

Run `python3 build.py` to regenerate, or `python3 build.py --check` to verify committed artifacts without retaining changes. It conserves all **1,726 manuscript digits**, separately renders the **1,721-digit emended stream**, reproduces both table switches, and checks that the emended stream has no structurally invalid groups. These checks demonstrate accounting and consistency, not the truth of the conjectures. The equality between the emended length and the old public transcription's length is coincidental.

- `manuscript-lines.json`: 21 physical cipher lines, one visual collation pass; punctuation carried over, marks interpreted explicitly.
- `collation.json`: three observed insertions totaling5 digits and caveats.
- `manuscript-tokens.json`, `manuscript-roots.txt`: source decode with selected key alternatives and explicit framing interpretation.
- `emendations.json`, `emended-tokens.json`, `emended-roots.txt`: the separate proposed repaired reading.
- `alignment.tsv`: each proposed token's physical manuscript line and digit positions. Deleted digits remain in the source transcription and ledger.
- `verification.json`: mechanical checks.
- `key-evidence.json`: new key readings, alternatives and remaining doubts.

## Evidence grades

Under the repository [conventions](../../CONVENTIONS.md), the manuscript parse has **413 units: 398 H, 12 M, 3 I**. The proposed emended parse has **404 units: 378 H, 8 M, 18 I**. H denotes historical key evidence, M uncertainty, and I editorial inference. The three manuscript I units are the two framing hypotheses and the displaced punctuation boundary. The emended I count also includes contextual g values and units affected by repairs, including boundaries after deletions. Deleted digits remain in the separate ledger. These are grades of token outputs, including controls and nulls, not percentages of authenticated prose or handwriting accuracy.

## Further work

Further investigation could include a complete audit of the key’s handwritten alternatives and extended comparison with surviving worked examples and contemporary decipherments, especially for 99 and repeated 55. These avenues have not been exhausted, but no specific comparison has been identified that would resolve those readings, and no recovery from these proposed checks has been demonstrated. Whether further work would improve the decipherment is unknown. The proposed repairs remain conjectural.
