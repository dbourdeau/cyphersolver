### p.138 (scan 0131)
Continues the distilling recipe of the aqua regis section that ends on p.137. The spirit is left to settle, the receiver is taken off and the water is poured into a flask with a still-head set in a water bath (balneum Mariae). The watery part (phlegma) is drawn off over a gentle fire until the water tastes sharp; then it goes into a flask with a long, narrow neck. One word before "fehet" (L17) has an unknown sign.

### p.139 (scan 0132)
The end of that recipe: sublimed sal armoniack is added to the water, the glass is sealed so that no spirit escapes, and it is left to stand; the text says the water gets better and stronger the older it is, and that this is the aqua regis. A new heading follows: dissolving gold ("Hungarian" gold, if the reading "ungerisch" is right) in the aqua regis. Two words at L13 ("dule") and a stray letter at L19 do not read.

### p.140 (scan 0133)
Refining the gold first: gold and lead on a cupel heated in the fire; when the lead runs clear, the gold is put in and driven off until it flashes ("blicket"), so that the gold is clean, and then taken off the cupel. The page breaks off as the next step starts (L21 has two letters that do not read).

### p.150 (scan 0143)
The end of a recipe for a "precipitate" made with spirit of wine (spiritus vini), distilled off until the precipitate stays in the fire and no longer smokes; the text calls it a true precipitate and a noble medicine, "as described above". A new heading follows: how to multiply this precipitate, by grinding it with "mercurius solis" (mercury of gold), begun "in the name of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ".
