- About 104 of the 186 judged slips in this batch are one sign decoded 'g' that stands for 'de' or 'd' (den, der, dem, anders, werden ...). The judge's notes name it as one key-level fault; it is corrected here wherever it occurs.
- Words split over a line end without the scribe's mark were joined here by sense (e.g. 80.08 su / periorum).

## Conventions

- Each line is given with its page.line number in the margin (e.g. 50.01), line breaks as in the manuscript.
- A word split over a line end is written whole on the line where it starts; the next line begins after the fragment. A line left empty this way is marked '(word continued above)'.
- ⟨word⟩ = a judged one-letter slip corrected, only where the judge's notes name that slip and the correction is obvious. The decoded form is in the footnotes under the page.
- A word with a superscript number but no ⟨ ⟩ is printed as decoded: the judge counted a slip in it, but the notes do not name that slip, so it is not corrected. The footnote gives the probable sense, which is not part of the text.
- [?] = a word judged no sense, or an unknown sign; the decoded letters are in the footnote.
- {clear: ...} = clear script (not cipher), kept as it is. [illustration ...] = an editorial note taken from the decode notes.
- Period spelling is kept (u for v, i for j, single and double consonants, -gck/-ck, uon, uor-, sol = gold). '?' doubt marks on single signs are dropped; '=' line-end marks are resolved by joining.
