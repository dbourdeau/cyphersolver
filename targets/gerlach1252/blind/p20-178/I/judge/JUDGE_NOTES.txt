At most 2 E per word; a word needing more (or unidentifiable) is all N.

p.80: Emerald-Tablet passage (it rises and descends, gains the force of upper and lower things).
      Long cooking with steady warmth thickens the matter until the bodily becomes spiritual.
      States the art is nothing but dissolving and coagulating the stone, done by putrefaction.
p.81: Latin doctrine: the work is perfect solution and coagulation; dissolve body, coagulate spirit.
      Gold and silver dissolve in their own radical moisture, the 'permanent water'.
      German: solution and coagulation are one operation by warmth; water and earth shown, air and fire hidden in them.
p.82: When the matter stops rising, the philosophical conjunction has begun; elements, soul and spirit unite.
      It becomes the true incombustible oil; a heated silver plate dipped in it is said to turn to gold.
      Warns not to open the glass then, if the work is to be completed.
p.83: This oil keeps its parts; gold is a fixed, proportioned oil that fire cannot harm.
      Its extracted fatness is a tincture belonging to the soul, drawn out slowly.
      A citrine-brown oil in the glass shows its fiery, sulphurous nature; the hidden made manifest.
p.84: Latin: the exteriors of gold are the interiors of the philosophers' mercury and vice versa.
      Hidden heat and dryness against manifest cold and moisture, citing the Turba.
      They must be brought into proportion so neither flies from the fire.
p.85: The moist and cold must take up the hidden heat and dryness to become one substance.
      The author cuts short the theory to come to the matter itself.
      Instruction to keep a steady vaporous fire at its proper degree, renewed periodically (a firing regime).
p.86: Furnace to be described later; the citrine-brown oil grows thin skins that sink.
      Continues until it all turns to powder, grey then darker.
      Finally fully black: the raven's head appears.
p.87: Turba citation: bodies dissolved by putrefaction become black earth, 'caput corui'.
      Exhortation to patient steady decoction until a black tincture shows on the water.
      Blackness means the body is liquefied; keep a gentle fire.
p.88: Blackness is destruction and corruption, and one thing's corruption is another's generation.
      Heat makes moist things black and dry things white (calor agens...).
      After solution, oil and black earth, continued warmth brings on whiteness.
p.89: Colours appear in the glass like little stars before true whiteness.
      Latin: 'our water' mortifies, illuminates, cleanses, quickens; many colours before whitening.
      The end is whiteness: the 'son' is born, called elixir; Hermes' dragon.
