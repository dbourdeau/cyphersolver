- Decoded, read and translated with AI tools (Anthropic's Claude); no person has checked the transcription, the German or the English.

## Conventions

- Faithful rather than smooth. Words in square brackets are ours: either a word the sense needs, or a short note. Figures in round brackets are the German line numbers.
- [?] = the German is unread or unsure at that point (see the footnotes in reading.md). Where a word was printed as decoded with a judged slip, the probable sense is translated and the decoded form is given in brackets.
- Latin passages are translated in the running text; the Latin itself is given in italics in the notes under each page, as it stands in reading.md.
- "Sol" and "solis" mean gold. "Waser" = water; "werme" = warmth; "corper" = body.

## p.80

(80.01-80.02) [continuing from p.79: the vapour ...] and goes down again through the dew into the lowest part. (80.02-80.08) Hence Hermes: the wind carried it [decoded 'qortauit', probably portavit], for the generation [gen[?]rdtio, probably generatio] of our begotten one takes place in the air, and being born wisely it is born in the air; likewise elsewhere: it ascends from earth to heaven and descends again to earth, acquiring the force of the upper and the lower things. ## p.81

(81.02-81.07) since our work is nothing [decoded 'nihill'] other [decoded 'aliudt'] than to make a perfect solution and coagulation; and unless everything is turned into water, one in no way attains the art. (81.07-81.12) For these two, namely the solution of the body [decoded 'corporie'] and the coagulation of the spirit, will be in one operation, and the one does not happen without the other; for to dissolve the body and to coagulate the spirit is the work of nature. (81.13-81.16) And so gold and silver are dissolved in the radical things of their own kind, and this moisture is called permanent water. (81.17-82.02) Now although solution and coagulation are distinct and happen one after the other, they have only one [decoded 'ebnige', probably einige] operation, namely the means of warmth; so that the two principal elements are shown to the eye, namely water and earth, in which the air and the philosophical fire rest hidden.

Notes:
1. Radical moisture [the basic moisture held to be the root of a substance's life].

Latin:
1. 81.02-81.07: *quoniam opus nostrum nihill aliudt est quam facere perfectam solutionem et coagulationem et nisi quodlibet uertatur in aquam nullatenus peruenitur ad artem*.
2. 81.07-81.16: *hec enim ambo scilicet solutio corporie et coagulatio spiritus erunt in operatione una et non fit unum sine altero nam soluere corpus et coagulare spiritus est opus nature et sic disoluitur aurum et argentum in rebus radicalibus sui generis et hec humiditas dicitur aqua permanens* ('corporie' probably *corporis*).
3. 81.23: *principalia elementa*.

## p.84

(84.01-84.06) which are the outer parts of gold, that is our mercury; for the outer parts of gold are the inner parts of the quicksilver of the philosophers [decoded 'elilosophorum'], and the inner parts of quicksilver [decoded 'argnti'] are the outer parts of gold. (84.06-84.08) If you understand this rightly, you understand a great secret. (84.09-84.12) For in the hidden [decoded 'obculto'] part of our stone are heat and dryness, and in the manifest part cold and moisture; (84.12-84.19) therefore it is necessary, as the Turba said [decoded 'diiit'], to hide the manifest and to make manifest the hidden, because the heat which is in the hidden is an element and a dry oil; for [decoded 'emim'] dry fixes dry, since the dryness of fire tinges. (84.19-84.26) And what [decoded 'quot'] is in the manifest is moisture and cold. But there must be a proportion among these, so that moisture and cold are tempered together [decoded 'bontemperata'] with heat and dryness, so that they do not flee from the fire [decoded 'ign']. (84.26-85.03) Make [decoded 'fab'] therefore that moisture and cold take up the heat and dryness which were in the hidden part, so that they become one substance.

Notes:
1. Turba [the Turba philosophorum, a medieval Latin dialogue much quoted in alchemy]. Tinges [gives colour, i.e. transmutes].

Latin:
1. 84.01-84.06: *exteriora auri ... id est mercurium nostrum quia exteriora auri sunt interiora argenti uiui elilosophorum et interiora argnti sunt exteriora auri*.
2. 84.09-84.26: *nam in obculto nostri lapidis est caliditas et siccitas et in manifesto frigiditas et humiditas quare oportet ut diiit turba manifestom occultire et occultum manifestare eo quod caliditas que est in occulto est elementum et oleum siccum emim figit siccum quoniam siccitas ignis tingit et quot est in manifesto est humiditas et frigiditas oportet autem habere proportionem inter ista ut humiditas et frigiditas sint bontemperata caliditati et siccitati ita ut non fugiant ab ign fab ergo ut humiditas et frigiditas* (continues on p.85; the decoded slips are listed in reading.md).

## p.87

(87.01-87.06) as the Turba says: here the bodies are dissolved by putrefaction and become black earth; and when you see the matter blackened, rejoice, for it is the beginning of the work; (87.06-87.11) and it is the black of the white and the raven's head, which flies without wings in the brightness of night and day. Latin:

## p.88
