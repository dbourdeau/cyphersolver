## Conventions

- Faithful rather than smooth. Words in square brackets are ours: either a word the sense needs, or a short note. Figures in round brackets are the German line numbers.
- [?] = the German is unread or unsure at that point (see the footnotes in reading.md). Where a word was printed as decoded with a judged slip, the probable sense is translated and the decoded form is given in brackets.
- Latin passages are translated in the running text; the Latin itself is given in italics in the notes under each page, as it stands in reading.md.
- "Mercurius solis" = the mercury of gold. "Waser" = water; "werme" = warmth; "corper" = body.

## p.70

(70.01) [... which must be brought out by the diligent spagyrist (p.69)] and must be prepared. (70.02-70.09) Then you have the right, living mercury of the philosophers, with which all your operation is to be begun and ended; for apart from this mercury all toil, cost and labour is in vain. (70.10-70.18) If you now want to bring this true matter to further degrees of perfection, then put this mercury of gold, without any addition, by itself, into a strong crystal glass, which is to be shaped for the purpose, as you [a stray letter 's'] know. (70.19-71.01) But above all consider [decoded 'bstrachte', probably betrachte] well the size of the glass, also [according to] the astrological manner, so that the matter in it can run through its sphere [decoded 'speran'] and move itself properly.

Notes:
1. 'Astrological manner' [the glass is to be shaped as a sphere, a little heaven, in which the matter circulates].

Latin:
1. 70.04 *mercurium philosophorum*; 70.10-70.12 *ueram materiam*, *geradibus der perfection* (degrees of perfection; 'geradibus' as decoded, for *gradibus*); 70.14-70.15 *mercurium solis*, *per se*.

## p.72

(72.01-72.11) proper philosophical fire, and govern this fire in an orderly and suitable way. Then this mercury of gold will begin within eight or ten days to dissolve, and by means of this vaporous and steady [decoded 'stpter'] warmth will go over little by little into a philosophical water. (72.12-72.19) Therefore note that the apparatus in which the glass stands must be set up with care, so that the vaporous fire too may work rightly according to nature, and the one can move the other. (72.20-73.01) Know also that the tripod which is in the apparatus must be wrapped round with cotton, so that the glass can rest on it nicely and softly.

Notes:

## p.73

## p.76

(76.01-76.09) For in our work putrefaction is necessary, since nothing is ever brought forth, born or growing or ensouled, except after putrefaction; and if it has not become putrid it cannot be dissolved, and if it has not been dissolved it is reduced to nothing. (76.09-76.16) But what putrefaction accomplishes in this work cannot be told enough; for the separation [decoded 'schemunge', probably scheidunge] of the elements must also be brought about by it, which cannot happen in any other way. (76.17-77.02) For the natural warmth drives the elements on [for[?]], so that one turns into the other; and this separation is nothing other than a change of their nature, so that out of water air [decoded 'lut'], out of air fire, out of fire earth again is generated.

Latin:
1. 76.01-76.09: *nam opere nostro putrefactio est necesaria quia nunquam fit aliquid natum uel crescens nec animatum nisi post putrfactionem et si putridum non fuerit non poterit solui et si solutum non fuerit ad nihilum redigitur*.

## p.78

(78.01-78.05) warmth or putrefaction the philosophical solution takes place, and the body gives itself to water, the philosophers have said: only one water throughout. (78.06-78.11) For the moisture from the spirit is all water, and the moisture from the soul is all an oil; hence they say: an unctuous vapour is our stone. (78.11-78.16) Therefore the soul is a bond of the spirit, just as the spirit is a bond of the soul; nevertheless this bond is nothing against the bond of nature. (78.16-78.18) Note that it must all become [decoded 'werkn'] water, if you mean to proceed rightly. (78.19-78.23) Hence the philosophers: dissolve the bodies into our quicksilver, which is the water of the mastery, and it becomes permanent water. (78.23-79.05) For just as at the creation of the world all [all[?]s] was water and the spirit of God hovered upon it, so [decoded 'alo'] it is also at the beginning of this work in the solution; for it rises up and goes down again until it has wholly dissolved its body into a beautiful, transparent, crystalline water.

Notes:
1. The clear-script mark 'NB.' stands in the left margin at 78.14.
2. Permanent water [aqua permanens: the 'water' that does not evaporate in the work, the dissolved matter itself].

Latin:

2. 78.09-78.11: *unde dicunt uapor unctuosus est lapis noster*.
3. 78.19-78.23: *unde philosophi soluite corpora in argentum uiuum nostrum quod est aqua magistery et fit aqua permauens* ('magistery' for *magisterii*; 'permauens' as decoded, for *permanens*).
