UNIT RULES
- 1 unit = 1 decoded letter a-z, or 1 unknown sign '_' / '#' (always N).
- '?' after a letter is not a unit: the letter before it is judged like any other.

- S = letter belongs to a word reading as sense in 16th-c. German or Latin (u/v, i/j, y=ij, dropped e as in
  'gburlich', 'ggen', 'gs-chicht', doubled letters as in 'weitter', 'herlichenn', 'einigett' accepted as period spelling).
- E = wrong letter that is plausibly a one-letter slip (substitution or intrusion). Where a letter is missing
  so the word no longer reads ('lut' for luft, 'alo' for also, 'd' for de, 'se?ra' for spera) one letter of the
  word is marked E. Two slips in one word = two E ('inmerthalb', 'anfengcllich').

- Each letter is checked by script: the letters of the segmented reading must equal the letters of the decode
  line exactly (assert passed on all 218 lines). Counts are made by script from per-letter tags.

GISTS (3 lines per page, recipe level only)
p.70  Opening of the work on the philosophers' mercury: the living mercury from which the whole work starts.
      Treats the mercury of gold ('mercurius solis') as the matter to be brought to perfection.
      Describes the kind of crystal glass vessel it is to be sealed in.
p.71  Short page: sealing the glass with the Hermetic seal.
      Setting the glass into its apparatus.
      Leads into the philosophical fire (continues on p.72).
p.72  The philosophical (vaporous) fire and its regulation.
      The mercury of gold begins to dissolve into a philosophical water.
      How the apparatus and its stand are to be prepared to hold the glass.
p.73  Continued gentle heating until the mercury becomes a clear crystal-like water.
      Latin maxim 'unica per totum aqua' (one water throughout, by cooking and recooking).
      Warning not to open the glass before or after the solution.
p.74  The glass stays in the apparatus under the same regimen of fire.
      Latin sayings: one stone, one vessel, one fire for all the operations (distillation, sublimation, etc.).
      The true solution: what dissolves becomes airy and rises.
p.75  The rising matter settles like dew on the top of the glass.
      This is the 'solutio remota' of the philosophers; the body dissolves, sublimes and revives itself (Turba).
      Latin: our dissolution is done by digestion/putrefaction, not by hand.
p.76  Putrefaction is necessary: Latin passage that nothing is born or dissolved without it.
      Putrefaction effects the separation of the elements.
      The elements are turned one into another (water to air, air to fire).
p.77  Aristotle quotation on converting the elements to have the whole art.
      The elements are changed, not truly parted; nature reunites them.
      Body, soul and spirit are separated; the stone is of body, soul and spirit (Latin).
p.78  By steady warmth or putrefaction the body becomes water ('unica tantum aqua').
      Spirit and soul as bonds of each other; all must become water.
      Latin: dissolve bodies into our quicksilver, the permanent water; comparison with the Creation.
p.79  The matter rises and falls until the body is wholly dissolved into a clear crystalline water.
      Latin: the whole operation is the extraction of water from earth and its return until the earth putrefies and is cleansed.
      Our stone is a vapour that rises from the bodily mercury within the sphere of the glass.
