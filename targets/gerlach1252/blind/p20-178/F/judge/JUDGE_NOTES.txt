UNIT RULES
1. Unit = one decoded letter. '?' marks dropped and the letter judged as it stands. '_' and '#' = 1 unit each, always N.
   '=' (line-break hyphen) and the dividers ; . , are not units. Text in { } excluded (Practicam, Sol, Leo).
2. Each decoded token was given one intended reading (period German or Latin), then aligned letter by letter
   (Levenshtein, by script):
   matched letter = S; substituted letter = E; inserted (surplus) letter = N. Letters the reading needs but the
   decode lacks cost nothing (units are decoded letters only).
3. Strictness cap: if a token's E+N exceeds half its units, the whole token is N (so "p?ri" for qui, "arf" for aus,
   "p?rotp?re" for quodque are N; "rnd" for und, "p?ria" for quia stay at 1 or 2 E).
4. A token with no defensible reading = all N: wegcg (40/21), af?m (41/03), farf?t (43/01), pl (45/25), file (46/04).
5. Accepted as sense: period spellings (dieweill, domit, vill, werdenn, verstandenn, denn, gebirende, oleitet),
   the abbreviation bne (bene), parca (real Latin; pauca probably meant), gafen (gaffen).
6. Readings marked [unverified] because no source could be opened: iximidrus (Turba speaker, 40/24), de massa solis
   (45/08), directa (45/20), filium (49/10), stucken (41/19), kulen keller (48/10-11). Judged as if right.
7. judge.tsv: segmented reading shows the intended word, then <decoded letters> where any letter is not S;
   [letters:N] = whole token N.

GISTS (3 lines per page, content level only)
p.40 Preface: the author sets out to explain the practice of the philosophers' stone and the parables of the
     philosophers, as a warning so the reader keeps to the ground of truth. All true philosophers describe one way
     to the end of the art. Cites the Turba philosophorum.
p.41 Latin sentences (Turba / Diomedes / Geber): nature is amended only by its own nature; add nothing foreign.
     German: do not chase many materials or switch from one to another against the philosophers' meaning.
     The art does not consist in a multitude of things; the stone is one thing.
p.42 One medicine in which the whole mastery stands; nothing foreign is added except in its preparation.
     Read the philosophers by agreement of their sense, not the bare letter; their circumlocutions keep the
     unworthy out. Persevere diligently in their writings.
p.43 With practice the reader will see the writers agree and their many names mean one thing.
     The names come not from the matter but from the changes in the work from beginning to end.
     What the true matter of the stone is stands in all the philosophers' books, though many do not understand it.
p.44 Consider human generation: like begets like, you reap what you sow (Latin tags).
     So too a medium to purify or change the metals; the word Sol is explained.
     Sol is "above all shining", the perfect body, to be kept in view.
p.45 A small error at the start becomes a large one at the end (Latin tag).
     The nature of Sol from a libellus "de massa solis": gold is the perfect body, lord of stones, not
     corrupted by earth or fire, of tempered complexion with nothing superfluous or lacking.
p.46 Gold's substance described (quicksilver with a little pure red sulphur).
     The philosophers' stone, being tempered and perfect, removes impurities from human bodies and metals.
     Therefore the stone must be made materially from the body of gold: every tincture proceeds from its like.
p.47 This needs suitable means: the body of gold must be broken to bring out its tincture.
     Announces the procedure that follows (heading Leo).
p.48 A recipe for preparing a philosophical mercury from refined gold (via aqua regis, crystals, putrefaction and
     sublimation). Said to break the body of gold for the first time and bring it near the prima materia.
p.49 Latin principles: everything resolves into what it came from; all metals can be reduced to mercury.
     Conversion of the metals' ash into spirit, making it volatile, i.e. the philosophers' mercury.
