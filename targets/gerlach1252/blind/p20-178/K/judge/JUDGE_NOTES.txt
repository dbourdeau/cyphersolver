UNIT RULES
- Unit = one decoded letter (a-z) on the line where it stands. '_' and '#' = 1 unit each, always N.
- '?' after a letter: the letter itself is judged; the '?' is not a unit.

- Word breaks were re-segmented by the judge; words split over a line end are shown with '-' in judge.tsv and judged as the whole word, each letter counted on its own line.

- E = wrong letter where one substitution (or one extra letter) restores the word. Omissions (e.g. philsophis, almechtigeit) cost nothing because there is no letter to mark.
- N = letters that make no sense and are not a one-letter slip: the stray 'cg' / 'cl' pairs tacked on to complete words, plus '_' and '#'.
- Where a 'cg' stands for 'ck' (dicger, glucglichen, gedancget, dancg), c is S and g is E.
- Notation in judge.tsv: ^x = letter x judged E; ~x = letter x judged N; all other letters S.

GISTS (level of 'a recipe for X' only)
p.100: Continuation of the preparation of the high medicine (the philosophers' stone). It is joined with a further prepared mercury of gold, sealed and returned to its vessel and fire. It dissolves into a clear crystalline water.
p.100a: The stages of the work over successive forty-day periods. An oil appears, then an ash-coloured earth. The black stage ('raven', 'caput corvi') is reached.
p.102: The 'peacock' stage of many colours follows. After it comes the white stage. The white philosophers' elixir is produced.
p.103: The white elixir is said to turn base metals into fine silver ('luna fixa'). Yellow colours and then red follow. The perfect red stage is described as shining by night and day.
p.104: A Latin quotation praising the red stone. Instructions for 'ingress' and the first multiplication of the medicine with gold calx. The medicine is said to turn base metals into gold.
p.105: Further multiplications of the medicine, each said to increase its strength. Praise of God's wonders. Many philosophers had the stone, few knew the ingress or multiplication.
p.106: An exhortation to keep the secret, citing Solomon and Proverbs. Why the philosophers hid the knowledge from the unwise. Thanks and praise to God; end of the section.
p.107: New section 'in the name of Jesus Christ': an explanation of putrefaction, the chief step of the art. A recipe for building a divided oak box. The box is split into two compartments.
p.108: The two compartments described. Preparing fresh horse dung. Filling the boxes with it.
p.109: Heaping the dung so it heats strongly. Setting the glass vessel into the hot dung. Repeating with the second compartment; a margin note on the third day.
