UNIT RULES
- One unit = one decoded letter. '_' or '#' = 1 unit, always N.
- Not counted: '?' markers (the letter before it is judged), ',' ';' ':' '.' '=' and spaces, and anything in { } (clear script: p.114 L24 '{&}', p.118 L02 '{S:S:S:}').
- S = letter in a word that reads as sense in 16th-c. German or Latin chemical terms. Period spellings accepted:
  u for v (uol, uitril, puluer), hot (hat), seint (sind), domit (damit), tagck/dreisigck/gnugck/ewigck, nach (noch),
  mol/moll (mal), iha (ja), leidt (liegt), uire (viere), machett, fulet/fullet, rosmist, mercury (gen.).
- E = wrong letter where the intended word is clear (one-letter slip). Each wrong letter is marked separately,
  so a word with two or three slips gets two or three E. Extra letters are E.
- N = letter in a stretch with no recoverable sense.
- Missing letters cannot be scored as units. They are listed below and NOT counted
  (nac[h]lasen 113 L13, quecksilbe[r] 115 L03, walden[u]rgisch 116 L01, gere[i]nigetes 116 L07,
  besch[u]t[t]e 118 L13/14 (the u is scored E as 'b'), durchfr[e]sen 119 L09).
- Markup in judge.tsv segmented_reading: [x] = E letter, <...> = N stretch, everything else S.
- Code check: for every row, the markup-stripped letters were asserted equal to the decode's letters, and S+E+N = letter count.

GISTS (recipe level only)
p.110: Continuation of a horse-dung (Rossmist) heating method: a glass vessel is moved from one dung box to a second.
       Explains when the dung has heated and how to seat the glass in fresh warm dung.
       Two short stretches unread.
p.111: Same method continued: replacing the spent dung in the first box with fresh horse dung.
       The glass is moved back and forth between the two boxes on a fixed cycle.
       No unread stretches.
p.112: Same method: the cycle of a day and a half, refilling the boxes with fresh dung.
       Advice to watch the heat and leave the glass longer while the dung stays hot.
       No unread stretches.
p.113: Same method: move the glass only when the heat starts to fall, and check often.
       Several glasses can be set at once; the dung must be clean, without straw.
p.114: Closes the dung-heat method, praising it as the true philosophical 'vaporous' fire in which putrefaction happens
       and the philosophers' stone is made.
       Ends with a prayer of thanks.
p.115: Heading: how to make all metals as fluid as wax by means of quicksilver (Mercurius solis et lunae).
       Opens with an invocation of the Trinity and the start of a recipe using calcined vitriol.
p.116: A recipe for vitriol oil distilled over quicksilver, referring to a 'great book', giving a calcined mercury
       ('mercurium calcinatum') that is kept for later use.
p.117: A recipe for layering thin silver or gold sheet with calcined-mercury powder in a crucible
       ('stratum super stratum'), with a note that it is written with three S signs.
p.118: Same recipe: sealing the crucible and building a tile-and-charcoal fire around it, then heating until it glows.
       One short stretch unread and one unknown sign.
p.119: Same recipe: letting it cool, called cementing; the metal is found brittle and eaten through.
       The layering and cementing are repeated until the metal flows like wax or oil.
