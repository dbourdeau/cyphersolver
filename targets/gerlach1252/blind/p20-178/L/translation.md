## p. 115

How to make all metals as fluid as wax by means of quicksilver [heading]

*Mercurius solis et lunae* [mercury of gold and of silver]

In the name of the holy, highly praised Trinity, God the Father, God the Son, God the Holy Ghost, amen. Take vitriol [a sulphate, here probably iron or copper vitriol], one pound or eight [pounds], calcine it [heat it to a powder],

Latin: *mercurius solis et lune* (mercury of the sun and of the moon, that is of gold and of silver)

## p. 116

and put it into a Waldenburg vessel [stoneware from Waldenburg in Saxony], and make a vitriol oil [sulphuric acid] as is written in the great book. But into the receiver [*furlage*: the flask that collects a distillate] put one pound of common, purified, living quicksilver; seal the joints with all care and distil for three days and nights with a strong fire, [so that] the vitriol oil comes over onto the quicksilver. Let it cool, take the receiver off and pour the oil out. You will then find the quicksilver calcined at the bottom, like chalk. Take this out, make it quite dry and keep it with all care. This is called *mercurius calcinatus* [calcined mercury].

Latin: *mercurium calcinatum* (calcined mercury)
