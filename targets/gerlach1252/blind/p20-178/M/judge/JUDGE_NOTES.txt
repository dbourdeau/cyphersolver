UNIT RULES
- One unit = one decoded letter a-z, or one unknown sign '#'/'_' (always N).
- '?' after a letter is not a unit: the letter itself is judged.
- Punctuation (';' '.' '=' ',') and spaces are not units. No clear-script { } text occurs on these pages.
- S = letter belongs to a word that reads as sense in 16th-c. German (period spellings and Latin chemical terms allowed:
  uiola, uol, uirten, uon, tzwe, ligen, schirben, krentzlein, circkel, -gck/-igck as in uirtzigck/genugck, mercurium solis,
  spiritum uini, putrefaction, sigillum hermetis).
- E = the single wrong letter in an otherwise readable word (each wrong letter is one E; a word with two wrong letters gets two E).
- N = unknown sign, or letter in no-sense material. Every word here was readable, so the only N units are the 5 '#' signs.
- Omitted letters are not units and are not penalised (albalde for alsbalde, nich for nicht, uiol for uiola); noted only.
- Words split by line breaks are judged as whole words; units are counted on the line where they stand.
- Check: a script verified that the letters in every judged reading match the decode line letter for letter (no mismatch).
- In judge.tsv, segmented_reading shows lowercase = S, UPPERCASE = E, '#' = N, '-' = word continues on the next/previous line.

GISTS (recipe level only)
p.120: Close of a recipe for preparing an oil or wax in a viol (glass vessel) with a sharpened tartar spirit.
       A general caution on how full the viols may be filled for this work.
       The rule is restated so that most of the vessel stays empty.
p.121: Opens a recipe titled "sigillum hermetis": how to seal a viol hermetically before putrefaction.
       Describes setting the vessel up and heating its neck with a ring of fire.
       Ends as the neck begins to melt and is closed with a heated tool.
p.122: Continues the hermetic sealing: finishing and cooling the sealed neck.
       Explains what "sealing a glass with the seal of Hermes" means.
       A test for whether the seal is sound.
p.123: Continues to the putrefaction of the sealed viol in dung.
       Says silver or gold oil or wax turns to living quicksilver, called mercurius solis or lunae, mercurius philosophorum.
       Begins the recovery of the product from the opened vessel.
