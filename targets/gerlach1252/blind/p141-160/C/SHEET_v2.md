## How to write them

- One sign = one label, separated by single spaces, in reading order (left to right).
- Word divider (the comma-like mark in the manuscript) = ` , ` (comma with spaces). Full stop = ` . `, semicolon = ` ; `, colon = ` : ` where the hand shows them.
- `=` at the end of a line: the word runs on to the next line (hyphen mark).
- `?` straight after a label (`4R?`): doubtful sign; give your best label.
- `#`: a sign not on this sheet; describe it in the notes column.
- `[...]`: untranscribed, damaged or illegible; `[...3]` if you can count about 3 signs.
- The three figure-4 shapes are different signs: 4L open top, 4R closed with the stem through and a pointed foot, 4U closed with a flat foot. Look at each one.
- Clear (Latin-letter) writing, marginal numbers and headings in ordinary script: copy them as written inside `{ }`, e.g. `{3}`. Do not convert them into labels.

## Look-alike sets: check each one (v2)

These shapes were confused in an earlier blind pass. Zoom in and decide by the feature named.

- **4L / 4U / 4R** (figure 4s). 4L: top OPEN, two strokes that do not meet. 4U: a small CLOSED triangle with a flat foot under the stem; this is the ordinary printed "4" and the commonest figure-4 on the later pages. 4R: a kite, the stem runs through the closed part and the bottom ends in a POINT. A closed "4" with a flat foot is 4U, never 4L or 4R.
- **ARW / OAR / SIG followed by SEVEN.** ARW: o, level bar, FLAT-topped 7 hook. OAR: o, bar, POINTED arrowhead or peaked hook. SIG then SEVEN: a sigma whose tail rises steeply, then a separate 7, even when they touch: write two labels, `SIG SEVEN`.
- **MM / S3.** MM: rounded cursive m, arches, nothing on top. S3: angular pi-like legs with a stroke rising through the top (often crossed, like "ホ"). Any m-like sign with a stroke or tick through the top is S3.
- **THE / DOT.** THE: horizontal bar across the circle. DOT: a dot in the centre.
- If you cannot decide inside a set, give your best label with `?` and name the set in notes (e.g. `4U?` notes "4U/4L").
