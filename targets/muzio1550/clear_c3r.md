# Muzio, cc. 3-4: the text in clear, and what the other pages hold

Read on 12 Nov 2026 from the Estense IIIF images (c. 3r closing lines at 35% scale; cc. 3v, 4r, 4v at 12.5%). Abbreviations are kept; `[...]` = not read.

## c. 3r

Heading, above the cipher block:

> Ill.mo et Ecc.mo S.

Closing lines, below the cipher block (the same hand):

> Si intende che questi R.mi fanno aprir il Conclavi dalla parte del Belvedere per pigliar aere; Il che vuol dir che hanno voglia di stare anchora un tempo a risolversi. A V. Ecc.a bacio con riverenza le mani.
> Di Roma a gli XI. di Gennaio del M. D. L.
> Di V. Ecc.a
> S.or humiliss.mo Il Mutio

English: It is understood that these Most Reverend lords are having the Conclave opened on the Belvedere side to take the air; which means that they intend to stay some time yet before they settle. I kiss Your Excellency's hands with reverence. From Rome, 11 January 1550. Your Excellency's most humble servant, Il Mutio.

## c. 3v and c. 4r

Blank. Only show-through from c. 3r and the library stamp are seen. The catalogue entry "cc. 3-4, Lettera cifrata" is one page of cipher, on c. 3r.

## c. 4v

Address panel and docket, no cipher:

> Allo Ill.mo et Ecc.mo Principe, et S.re / S. Don Ferrando Gonzaga [...] / Luogotenente [...] S.or mio oss.mo

Docket, written sideways at the top: "Mutio XI di Genaro". Paper seal.
