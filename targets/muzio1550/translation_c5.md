# Girolamo Muzio to Ferrante Gonzaga, Rome, 12 Jan 1550, cc. 5r-5v: English translation

Same marks and holes as reading_c5.md. `⟦ ... ⟧` = deciphered from cipher.

## Translation

Most Illustrious and Most Excellent Lord,

I wrote two letters to Your Excellency yesterday, and because I wrote so briefly of having spoken with the lord Don Diego, I now say again that I tried to make sure whether the news I had been told was true, and I recalled Your Excellency's interest. He answered that the news was not true, and that nothing would be left undone so that no such harm should be done to Your Excellency.

From the conclave come various reports, which agree in this sense: that ⟦[Your Excellency] and [?Don Diego] had taken up the protection of Salviati, and (?) in order to become by that favour, the one [?Duke of] Milan, and [?Don Diego] master of Siena (?); that the [?Duke of] Florence and the [?Duke of] Alba have disclosed this scheme to [?His Majesty]. From which has followed his exclusion, made by the [?]; that for this reason [?Don Diego], to cover himself, has not only drawn back from it, but has worked against it [?] vigorously [?]⟧; that likewise ⟦our [Cardinal] has cooled [?]⟧. And that if he had kept on with the work already begun again, the plan would already have succeeded. And I have to tell Your Excellency that when this college of cardinals is well examined, no one is found who by general opinion is fitter to govern this machine, and who has fewer objections against him, than the one Your Excellency has undertaken to favour. I have spoken with some of his own enemies, who say that they would not want him, but that for all that they know no person fitter than he. Being such, then, and since in the interest of Christendom conscience must first be satisfied, I do not see why anyone should draw back from him for any consideration, above all since it is known that the Emperor had already consented to him, and that the lord Don Diego has vouched that he was not distrusted by His Majesty. Nor has any new occasion been able to arise since that would make him distrusted. And the orders that came afterwards are known not to be of his free will. So that if this work were continued, I do not see how His Majesty could by right hold himself aggrieved. I pass over the interest of honour, and a thousand other considerations that come into it, which I leave out for brevity. Thinking over these things I have been about to ⟦write to our [Cardinal]; but then, having no answer to what [Your Excellency] has sent⟧. And being persuaded that there is time enough, I wished to give Your Excellency notice of this talk, and then to write you (?) these words, and to kiss your hands as usual with reverence.

From Rome, 12 January 1550. Your Excellency's most humble servant, Il Mutio

## Notes

- "Salviati": Cardinal Giovanni Salviati, a candidate in the conclave of 1549-50.
- "Don Diego": named in clear at the head of the letter; taken as Diego Hurtado de Mendoza, the Emperor's ambassador in Rome. The code value "Don Diego" for the group 1o is our proposal.
- "the one ... has undertaken to favour": the candidate is not named in clear; from the cipher passage he is Salviati.
- The sentence in cipher on c. 5v breaks off: the clear text goes on with a new sentence.
