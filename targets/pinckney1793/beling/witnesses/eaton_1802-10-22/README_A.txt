IMPORTANT — THE PAGE CARRIES A CONTEMPORARY DECIPHERMENT
  On frame 0320 a second hand (not the letter-writer's) has written ordinary English
  words in small script above the code groups, from the first group to the last. It is
  evidently a recipient's interlinear decipherment. The transcription in the TSV is of
  the numerals only and was made from the shapes of the digits; the interlinear words
  were NOT used to choose between digit readings. Because those words would spoil a
  held-out test, they are not quoted in the TSV or in this README. My rough reading of
  them is in interlinear_words_A_SPOILER.txt for whoever is entitled to open it.
  The interlinear writing is also the main cause of damage to the numerals: its
  ascenders, descenders and several blots run into the code lines above and below.

LAYOUT OF THE CODED PASSAGE (frame 0320, single page)
  The code starts in the middle of manuscript line 11, directly after the clear words
  "not to receive a negative." and runs to the end of line 19. The next paragraph
  ("The Dane has again this summer, made peace with Tripoli —") is clear.
  Line numbers in the TSV count every written line of the page from the dateline (1).
  Line 11 is given as two rows (clear part, then code part).

NOTATION USED IN THE content COLUMN
  .      period after a group (end of word)
  -      dash joining groups; a dash at the end of a line means the word runs on
  +      small cross written just after a group at the baseline (see below)
  ?      after a group: the reading is doubtful; alternatives are in the notes column
  *      inside a group: a digit position that cannot be read at all (blot/smudge)
  [.]    no separator can be seen on the film at this point (hidden by a blot, an
         ascender or a long tail); a period is the likely separator but it is supplied
  No ^ (caret beneath), ins: (interlinear number) or sup: (superscript number) occurs:
  I found no numeral written above the line, and no mark beneath a group that I could
  accept as a caret (see "possible marks" below).

LOWER-LEVEL DOUBTS (not marked ?; I believe the reading given)
  0 versus 6 in this hand. A non-initial 6 has a clearly rising ascender (1546, 856 x2,
  1476, 1563, 1362 x3, 1064, 1465); a 0 is a full-height oval that sometimes ends in a
  small flat closing tick. On that test 1100 (line 13) and 1300 (line 19) are zeros
  (alt 1106/1166, 1306) and 856 (lines 14 and 17) ends in 6 (alt 850).
  1 versus 7. Every 7 here has a long descender well below the line; linked 1s (1104,
  1107, 611, 1110) have a hairline across the top but no descender, so they are not 7s.
  4 has two shapes: a "u" with tall right stroke, and an "h"-like form with tall left stroke.
  line 13  512     the 5 has an extra crossing stroke (possibly altered).
  line 16  1348    2nd digit partly overlaid; 3 probable.
  line 18  1554    3rd digit very heavy, perhaps written over; flag of a 5 visible.
  line 19  614     top of the 6 merges with interlinear writing (alt 814 not favoured;
                   the 614 on line 11 is clear).
  line 17  1517    a short cross-tick on the first 1, judged stray.
  line 15  1244    2nd digit confirmed as 2 at high magnification (not 5).

THE + MARKS
  Written by the same pen as the numerals, immediately after the last digit at the
  baseline and before the period. Two are a plain small cross (1258+ line 14, 1110+ line
  16); two look like a double cross, two short uprights on one bar, like "#" (620+ line 13,
  1258+ line 16). All four are recorded as a single +.

STRUCK OUT / BLOTTED

CLEAR TEXT
  All 19 clear lines of page 1 are transcribed in the TSV (the whole page is short).
  Spelling as written ("stile", "Capn. Bounds", "re established").

METHOD
  sips only. Frame 0320 is 2352 x 3584 px; the code block lies at about x 440-2060,
  y 1400-2080, written lines about 72-78 px apart and rising about 30 px left to right.
  Pass 1: each line cut into overlapping windows at 3x and 5x, doubtful groups at 8x.
  Pass 2: every line re-cut with different offsets at 4x and re-read against the first
  reading; remaining doubts at 10-14x. Final check of the finished rows against fresh
  2.4x strips. The film is high-contrast, so fine hairlines and pale strokes are lost and
  blots are opaque: the flags above cannot be improved from this scan.
