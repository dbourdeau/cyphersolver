DE SEURE, LISBON, 1558 — KEY AND DECIPHERMENT

BnF français 3151, nos. 39–41 and 43–44, December 1558.

Letters from Michel de Seure, then Henri II's ambassador in Lisbon. No. 39 is addressed to Florimond Robertet, sieur de Fresne; the others
are addressed to the king himself.

The cipher has 118 sign types, including letter, syllable and word signs, 21 nulls, and a cancellation operator, across 9,405 characters in 397 rows. The key was partially recovered from discovered plaintext: three short sections that had been deciphered on an adjacent leaf in the manuscript. These supplied cribs which we used to run a simulated annealing process after best-efforts transcription. This process was only partially successful, as a result of several sets of similar-looking signs that had been mistaken thought to be a single character. Repeated comparison with the scans. Manual checks across the whole corpus allowed us to spot new distinctions and slowly correct the whole key.

The same key covers all five letters. No. 40's enciphered passages have surviving clear copies;
no. 43's postscript repeats no. 44 C. 

In the letters are reports of Spanish treasure fleets from Peru and the state of feeling in Portugal as well as numerous appeals for financial help.

N10_4 remains unassigned (context suggests vostre); longs_x = escuz is
also conjectural. Some damaged or unidentified signs and scribal errors
remain.

KEY.svg identifies the signs. KEY_AND_DECRYPTION.txt gives their values
and every transcribed position's output, grouped by manuscript row.
