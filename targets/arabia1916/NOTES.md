# German Foreign Office to Bernstorff, 5 December 1916, on the Arabia: the cipher text was never kept (catalogue 635)

Status: attempted, open: the groups are in no digitised source; nothing to attack. Not deciphered.

Catalogue 635 (class B, priority 4.1, "Help solve"). A frame check of 2 Nov 2026 (claude frame check 12) had already
found that the NARA copy has no groups; this session (6 Nov 2026) re-viewed the whole run of frames, searched the
printed sources and closed the entry as attempted, open.

## The document

- Grew (Chargé, Berlin) to the Secretary of State, telegram **4691**, 5 Dec 1916, 6 p.m., received 6 Dec 11.45 p.m.,
  "Green and German cipher", via Copenhagen. The Foreign Office asks him to transmit through the Department to Count
  Bernstorff "the following cipher telegram which is said to relate to the case of the ARABIA and to contain
  instructions to Count Bernstorff to endeavor to settle the matter by conversations in Washington". Then:
  "(Quote German cipher). End quote. GREW." FRUS 1916 Supplement, doc. 424 prints it with the footnote that the
  German cipher text is not printed.
- NARA RG 59, 841.857/244, microfilm **M580 reel 191, frame 0111**
  ([naId 580879489](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/580879489)). A handwritten note in the margin: "delivered to
  Ambassador by direction of the Secy 12/7/16". The groups went to the Ambassador and were not kept with the file.
- Same day, same channel: Grew's **4692** (841.857/243, frame 0110, in clear): the Foreign Office's confidential oral
  statement on the Marina (his 4655 of 27 Nov) "applies equally to the case of the ARABIA".
- The cable was in a German diplomatic code sent over the American channel; the code is not identified here (no groups
  to measure). The catalogue's guess (13040 / 0075) rests on the channel, not on any group.

## What was done (6 Nov 2026)

- Read the catalogue entry, its solve page, and the earlier frame check.
- Viewed M580-191 frames 0109-0113 at 1400 px (IIIF `/full/1400,/`): 0110 = 841.857/243 (4692, clear), 0111 =
  841.857/244 (4691, placeholder only), 0112 = /245 (Havre consul no. 480, a British steamer mined in the Havre roads),
  0113 = /246 (Hibbard, YMCA, 6 Dec 1916, on Paul Danner, "the only American passenger on the Arabia"). No sheet in the
  run carries German code groups.
- FRUS 1916 Supplement doc. 424, and FRUS 1914-1920 The Lansing Papers vol. I, doc. 547 (Bernstorff to Lansing, 8 Dec
  1916). He writes that he had word from Berlin about the Arabia late on 7 Dec, that Lansing already had it through
  Grew, and that his government instructed him to place himself at Lansing's disposal for a speedy, satisfactory
  settlement of the Marina and Arabia cases. This is the nearest plaintext: a paraphrase of the instruction, not the
  telegram.
- Bernstorff, *Deutschland und Amerika* (Berlin 1920), full text from the Internet Archive OCR
  (in.ernet.dli.2015.509083): "Arabia" occurs only for the 1915 *Arabic* case (p. 176 ff.) and in a report of the
  1916-17 peace action (p. 341). The telegram of 5 Dec 1916 is not printed.
- Earlier sweeps in `targets/bernstorff1917/NOTES.md` section 10: RG 59 M367 and M580 OCR searched for German code
  sheets (and for a shared-group match with catalogue 657). Incoming ciphers left the Department, so only
  Bernstorff's outgoing messages survive in the file.
