# Français 3516 — two System A readings and six corroborated groups

## F. 87r, the missing negative

The passage after *ce party* on cipher lines 5–6 reads *qu’ilz n’ayent occasion de s’en cacher*. The `n` is an ordinary letter sign and the contemporary decipherment has *nayent*. The 1656, 1819 and 1875 editions omit the negative, reversing this part of the advice. The passage follows the point where the project’s f. 87r reading breaks off. [Manuscript](https://openapi.bnf.fr/iiif/image/v3/ark:/12148/btv1b9059630p/f150/full/1700,/0/default.jpg) · [1656, p. 72](https://books.google.com/books?id=yahDAAAAcAAJ&pg=PA72) · [1819, p. 222](https://books.google.com/books?id=10cUAAAAYAAJ&pg=PA222) · [1875, p. 64](https://archive.org/download/negociationsdiplomatiquesetpoli/page/n89/mode/1up) · [Project reading](https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/jeannin1607.html)

Three barred values absent from the public chart recur with matching interlinear readings. Barred `3` reads *qu’ilz* (ff. 87r, 112v and 116v), barred `50` reads *j’ay* (ff. 113v, 114v and 116v), and barred `97` reads *occasion* (ff. 87r, 115v and 116r).

Three dotted groups also recur. Dotted `15` reads *Roy d’Angleterre* (ff. 112v and 113v), and dotted `20` reads *archiducz* (ff. 112v and 115v). Dotted `11` refers to the Spanish side. The glosses use *le Roy d’Espagne* on ff. 88r and 116r and *Espagnolz* on ff. 112v and 115v. The supplied files locate each example and keep the two contextual expansions distinct.

## F. 116v, the Dieppe shipping clause

I transcribed 41 cipher signs at the top of Gallica view 201, from the last group of cipher line 4 through line 6. This leaf is listed among the untranscribed pages of the *jeannin1607* target. Applying George Lasry’s System A chart gives the Dieppe shipping clause, with one group requiring an inferred value. [Manuscript scan](https://openapi.bnf.fr/iiif/image/v3/ark:/12148/btv1b9059630p/f201/full/3400,/0/default.jpg) · [Target write-up](https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/jeannin1607.html)

> Si tost que nous scaurons que leurs [nauire]s seront partiz pour les venir charger.

- Line 5 has barred `56` plus `=`, yielding *leurs*. The same sequence gives *leurs* before *partisans* on f. 86r, cipher line 17. For comparison, the *les* on f. 116v line 6 is barred `54` plus `=`.
- Barred `90` precedes a separate letter sign for `s`. The period interlinear and the 1875 print support *navires*, suggesting *nauire* as the code stem. This is a single-attestation proposal.
- The 1656 and 1875 editions both print the clause and have *les navires*. The contribution is the sign-level reading of this witness, the possessive variant and a proposed chart entry. F. 116v visibly carries a period interlinear decipherment, as does f. 87v. [1656, p. 96](https://books.google.com/books?id=yahDAAAAcAAJ&pg=PA96) · [1875, p. 88](https://archive.org/details/negociationsdiplomatiquesetpoli/page/n113/mode/1up) · [f. 87v scan](https://openapi.bnf.fr/iiif/image/v3/ark:/12148/btv1b9059630p/f151/full/1700,/0/default.jpg)

The accompanying transcription and Python verifier give every sign, including the published-key output with barred `90` left open. A visual screen of the top 20 shape matches per view across 15 scans recovered the original occurrence but yielded no clear second one.
