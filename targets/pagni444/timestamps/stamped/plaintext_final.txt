# ENGLISH [derived; interpretive]
[...so that you would understand how] I have stayed on here until now at the request of the Marquis of Sarria {Q}, to see where the negotiation with this Pope was heading. In short, although there seems to be some agreement, that friend who knows part of the Frenchmen's secrets tells me plainly that our side will be deceived. Even so, a league is being negotiated, and a plan to pledge Cervia and Ravenna to the Duke of Ferrara to raise money; and the same Monsieur de Lansac, who {d} who came yesterday evening, is pressing it hard. The Cardinal of Lorraine will come; I wrote to him. Although these French ministers have let it be understood that they sent to meet him and tell him that his coming is no longer needed, [yet] it may well be that they have settled matters with the Pope so that nothing more is needed, and that he is delaying his coming to lull our side for some days, to see whether Duke Ottavio, with the troops they have planned to raise, would take part in putting Siena in straits, so that it would have to come to an accord and the Pope be made its depositary, in the belief that the Duke [Cosimo] would like such a scheme; and meanwhile to give orders for a fresh effort next season, whatever happens to Siena. And know that in these past days the Pope has made himself understood so openly, with harsh words and with visible demonstrations and preparations, that the French were displeased, since it seemed to him that he would see {m} flooded by Imperial troops before they had made their preparations. In short, the Pope is the bitterest enemy of the Emperor and of the Duke, and [therefore?] he will use all his power; already one sees money being spent, and a large sum, so reckon on this. Horse are coming from Mirandola, and likewise from Parma. The Pope, I say, will disarm, and the French will make use of those soldiers. I do not know when [...] to be allowed to go to {n}. I ask you to keep me in the Duke's good grace and to give me your commands.
