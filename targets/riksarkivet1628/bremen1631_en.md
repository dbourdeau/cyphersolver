## Translation

### R4330, Bremen, 27 November 1631

By the grace of God Johann Friedrich, elected and postulated archbishop and bishop of the sees of Bremen and Lübeck,
heir to Norway, duke of Schleswig-Holstein.

Our gracious greeting with especial well-inclined goodwill first, noble, steadfast and most learned, especially dear
[friend]. We do not doubt that you will have duly received our last letter, and what we asked in it about the succour
that might be sent to us in haste, you will have learned from it. When we are then informed whether [?the Landgrave of
Hesse] should, by [?His Royal Majesty's order], with his army from the Westphalian [...]. And [?therefore] not [?...]
[?now] the town [would be] summoned.

And we then [?...], because the Count Gronsfeld's [troops] at [31] and the Minden folk and Cologne (Colonien) troops otherwise have no
resistance to fear on that account, [?as] ours are also advised from Minden, that they will [?join forces] and also
press upon us, whereby [?the Landgrave] [18] [82] would go, and [we] would come into the utmost danger.

If not [?thus] [...] the actual help follows in haste, especially since we would be surrounded by enemies on both sides.

Therefore it is herewith our [?gracious] request to you that, in this consideration, you will promote with especial
diligence and [?order] and [?arrange] the most gracious provision in haste, as soon as ever possible, that we be not
abandoned but helped out with speedy help and succour, so that General Tott's and Colonel Dumeine's troops be sent over
now without delay and sent to us with all speed, so that the work be not made harder by delay and we [over that ...]
[82] with [?pressing].

We have exerted ourselves so far and brought it so far that our own and [42] troops have at the same time attacked the
sconce at the castle and are engaged in the work; also, as we now receive report, [it is] already occupied. [?We]
cannot, however, advance further unless we receive assistance.

Therefore we earnestly desire that Salvius will take it to heart that the succour not tarry too long but follow with
all speed. We rely wholly on you for this and remain in return well disposed to you with especial grace in all good
things. Given in our town of Bremen, 27 Nov. 1631. Friedrich.

### R4331, Bremen, 2 December 1631

[Title as above.]

Our gracious greeting with especial well-inclined goodwill first, noble, steadfast and most learned, especially dear
[friend]. We have no doubt that you will have received our last letter about speedy succour and troops'
help-sending, since the sconce castle has now also been occupied and taken. And although we have quartered the
cavalry there, Reinach nevertheless raids hard here and there through [?our] lands and lays waste one place after
another, and we cannot prevent it.

For we must secure ourselves of the passes along the Westphalian borders first of all, of necessity, against the feared
invasion of Bönninghausen's and Gronsfeld's troops, of which a strong [rumour] is spread, so that no greater [86] and
[82] may befall and come upon us from that quarter.

But as we take every comfort [?in this] and rely on it, that we would be seconded, as promised, with some thousands and
helped out, so we still have the firm confidence that one will send us in haste some troops to our help and [?not]
leave us so any longer, or hold us off only with good promises, whereby we, together with land and people, would be
wholly endangered and might come into misfortune [?utterly].

And we therefore, because the highest need demands it, graciously request of you once more that you will make the
definite arrangement and order and [?provision] that, if the full number of which you lately wrote to us cannot be, at
least some foot and partly dragoons, as many as one can at all muster in haste, be sent to us with all speed at the
first opportunity for succour, and [?usefully] come to us, so that we see ours, that [...] succour is nevertheless at
hand, and do not let our hope sink greatly; also that Tilly's [men] in [...] be not made bolder. And we remain
graciously disposed to you with all grace and especially well-inclined goodwill. Given in our town of Bremen, 2 Dec.
Anno 1631. Friedrich.

### R4331, postscript

Could Salvius also, since he is anyway, as we hope, [?now] [?present] at Celle, [and] so dispose and direct the work with Duke Christian
and Duke Georg that we [?therefore] in that quarter not only may expect all good assistance and have cause to rejoice,
but also may surely obtain that the enemy, where [he would come] [?down] from Neuenburg and otherwise over the Aller,
through the land of Lüneburg, [be] granted passage of war, [but rather be] [?blocked], [lest] from there harm of war
[82] and danger befall and meet us and also Minden, [?all the less] if the garrison of Neuenburg should march out on
accord, [?it be] not allowed to come into our archbishopric, but rather that we be included under the accord on that
account, we would graciously [?like it]. And it would be especially dear and agreeable to us, and we have therefore
graciously [?to desire] in this, as we also do not doubt that you will take it especially to heart to promote it.
Otherwise it is also our [?gracious] wish that Salvius come as soon as possible nearer to [?Stade] and to us in person.
That will be done to our [?gracious] pleasure.
