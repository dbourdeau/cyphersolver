# The Mute Book of Granada

Sacromonte Lead Book 17, *Ḥaqīqat al-Injīl* (the Essence of the Gospel), is catalogue entry 2941. The book was found in Granada in 1597. Its Arabic seal legend can be read. Our tests of the cryptic writing produced no verified plaintext. Van Koningsveld and Wiegers suggest that the makers may have devised the signs to represent a secret revelation. The tests below leave that interpretation open. [Critical edition, pp. 113–115 and 348](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/181372440/9789004685277-68485.pdf)

## Testing the title and opening formula

The editors deposited photographs of the plates alongside the edition. The deposit includes 30 images labelled XVII, extending the material available for comparison beyond the printed illustration of folio 3b. We use the editors’ folio labels throughout. [Photographic deposit](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24138933)

On folio 2a we tested whether thirteen visible groups could spell the twelve letters of the Arabic title. We grouped strokes by appearance and allowed one group to drop out or two adjacent groups to merge. In both directions, with either spelling of alif in *al-Injīl*, at least one repeated letter still required different shapes. This result depends on our grouping and on the assumption that a letter retains its shape.

On the first line of folio 3b we allowed 32 ways to divide the strokes and tested both directions. The full Arabic opening formula *bism Allāh al-raḥmān al-raḥīm* failed the repeated-letter test in every window. One interior window in each direction fit the title when we distinguished hamza. These two matches carry little weight. Of 13,860 control patterns with three repeated pairs, 8,420 also fit somewhere on the line. In each title match, closer inspection distinguished two strokes that the coarse grouping had treated alike.

Run `python test_title.py` and `python opening_cribs.py` from this folder to repeat the counts. The scripts include the hand-assigned shape groups. Their output tests the consequences of those assignments. Reading the plates independently requires the photographs.

## A line with a stated length

Book 16, folio 6a, describes a lamb with an inscription of 22 letters on its crown. The editors associate its script with Book 17. Figures 29 and 35 in the edition reproduce the same photograph (the PDF uses the same image object on printed pages 332 and 498). We located the illustrated passage on the photograph of XVI 6a. The boundaries between the 22 letters remain uncertain. The stated length supplies a check on a future transcription of this line, but gives no alphabet size or letter values for Book 17. [Critical edition, pp. 56, 332–333 and 498](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/181372440/9789004685277-68485.pdf)

## Remaining gaps

Pettorano’s Latin version of Book 16 survives in Harley MS 3500, folios 102r–141v. A drawing in the passage about the lamb could show how an early copyist separated the inscription from the surrounding prose. The catalogue does not say whether he drew it, and we have not inspected those folios. A decipherment would require consistent sign values that predict a reading in a second passage. [British Library manuscript record](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002049331)
