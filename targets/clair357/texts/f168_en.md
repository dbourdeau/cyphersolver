# Clairambault 357 f. 168r, 28 Oct 1586: the clear copy of f. 167r up to the cross, English translation

I have received your two letters at the same time, which I have communicated to Monsieur de la Forest and Monsieur de
Corbet. Charry … being very vexed at the good state in which your affairs are, and that you will have got back in as
soon as possible [?]. Unwell with an illness that has troubled me for nearly two months, which has prevented my writing
to you. During that time, which was the reason that the clerk to the Abbot of La Couronne, by the means [by which] he was
to come to an understanding … of Monsieur de Nemours [?], and to hand over twenty [?] of their troops [?], who would take
advice from me, rather through you the marriage carried out, at which I showed much regret, … did not keep me from
telling him that the journey and negotiation he was undertaking [?], being contrary to that of last year, would be found
strange not only by his friends, the Catholics of France, but by foreign princes. Whereupon he brought all the reasons
he could. The best and most plausible [?] was … of the Queen Mother, from whom he gave support to whom he pleased, who
promised him the assurance [?] that she wished to make peace only on condition that there should be only the Catholic
religion in France; and he went further: that if he had been on the terms he had arranged with you, he would not have
undertaken the journey; that everyone had done his own business, and he had been left alone, abandoned. The rumour that
ran everywhere these last days [?] that you wished to come has indeed reached me. … the almoner, this [?], who was coming
to this country, … company and … will feel [?] richly … that gives me … thought … all this rumour that you recommended …
as it did not seem to me proper to do it. Meanwhile neither pressed nor included [?], which are the only causes that
should hasten you to it. I believe that by pressing for the other places that have been taken from you, you will
be able to have them, or at least make it known that you give up neither the pursuit nor the hope of the
reconciliation of the King of Navarre and you, which I hold very difficult because of the character each of you has
taken, the one of protecting the Catholics, the other the Huguenots, without the other, which comes first. ✝
