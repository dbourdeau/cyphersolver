## 2. What would settle them

- **g/q class (13 units).** Reader 1 reads one sign as g (= c) before theta (h) in sich, noch, machen, auch, nichts,
  solches, mochten, etliche, commendiret; reader 2 merged that sign with q (= d) by mapping; reader 3 saw a straight
  tail (q) in some of these places on the crops. Reader 3's adjudication (b137) judged that the hand does not keep g/q
  apart reliably. Settling it needs a person to compare the tails at full size, or a fourth reader given one reference
  crop per key label.
- **Reader 1's emendations (7).** Unkosten (an extra e), Signoria, eine, auf, künftige, Speranz (sign n has no value),
  currentis: each needs one sign read differently. They stay counted not read.
- **pi/Z/n class (4)** and **c/e (3).** Readers 2 and 3 used different classes for the pi-sign, the n-form and the
  omega-like sign, and did not see an eye in the first sign of wochen, wollen, worden.
- **Codes 117 (x2), 118.** No code value is known. 118 is the place of writing (Venice is the likely reading, not proved).
  The 1628 chancery decipherment sheet at image 447 (Schultetus's number code) does not list 117 or 118.
- **Other single signs (3).** heute (m), umb (s/f: readers 2 and 3 read f), anno (d/u).
