Quem novit [flourish]

## English [derived]
Most gracious King, H. lets Y(our Majesty) know how H., to his great vexation and displeasure and at considerable cost, has now had to wait here into the third week, because H. could get no galley; and although some small galleys were on hand, nobody, 117 in particular, would advise H. to risk himself on them. So the Signoria has now assigned a large one, which arrived here yesterday, and on it H. will set out this very day, in God's name.
Further, because he was held up so long and for other necessary outlays, H. has had to borrow another 3,000 thalers here; he hopes Y(our Majesty) will be content and have it paid, and will gladly let it be deducted in future.
For the rest, 117 has so far resolved nothing on H.'s proposal, but I have good hope that W. may yet declare themselves somewhat better. Dated at 118, 20 July of the current year.
"Whom you know."

## Not settled [unverified]
- Who H., E., W., 117 and 118 are. "Seigneria" and galleys point to Venice (118 = Venedig?, 117 = the Doge or the Collegio?); not proved. E. is the addressee, addressed as king.
- The year. The leaf lies between papers of Sept 1628 (image 442) and Schultetus's letter after 24 July 1628 (image 444): 1628 is likely, not shown.
- The writer. Hand differs from Schultetus's; "Quem novit" stands in place of a signature.
- Lead on identity [unverified]: one article (Bohemiae Occidentalis Historica 2/2019, otik.zcu.cz/bitstream/11025/42879/1/BOH_2_2019-87-112.pdf, read through a summariser only) says Christian Wilhelm, Administrator of Magdeburg, sent to lead the Danish army in Silesia, reached Venice in early July 1627, lingered there, and wrote from Alba Iulia on 18 Sep 1627. A three-week wait at Venice for a galley on 20 July fits that journey, so the letter may be of 20 July 1627 and "H." may be the Administrator. Not tested against any dated source.
