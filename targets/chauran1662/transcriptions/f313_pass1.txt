## Notes
- Cipher type (A). Highest number read 235; lowest 1. 45 cipher lines in all (21 + 21 + 3). No gloss on
  any line, so every G line is "(none)". The position rule for the gloss could not be tested here.
- Not transcribed, as instructed: the facing left-hand page of image 1 (it carries a later pencil
  docket "Holland 1662 June 20/30" and the address panel of the previous item) and f. 315 on image 3.
- Digit forms in this item: 2 is written like a modern "2" or "z" with a long tail (never "v").
  3 has a flat top, a diagonal and a lower bowl ("ʒ"); 5 is a smooth long-s shape; where the two were
  hard to tell apart the group carries "?".
- 4 versus 9, the main source of doubt. I separated two shapes: (a) closed, rather angular head with a
  STRAIGHT descender, often thick and ending bluntly or in a small hook to the right = 4; (b) round or
  open ("y"-like) head with a tail CURVING to the left = 9. On that rule 219, 209, 190, 199, 139, 19,
  39, 69, 79, 97, 98, 99 are nines and 134, 154, 164, 174, 141, 114, 24, 84, 54, 64, 47, 34 are
  fours. The two shapes are clearly distinct in most groups (for example 141:B1 beside 191:O, 134
  beside 139:B1 in f. 313r L05-L06, and 149 with both shapes in one group), but in perhaps one group
  in ten the descender is neither clearly straight nor clearly curved. I marked the worst of these
  with "?"; unmarked 4/9 readings are confident to roughly nine in ten, not better. If the key test
  fails mainly on groups containing 4 or 9, this rule is the first thing to re-examine.
- Marks. B1, B2, M, O, S1, P are clear almost everywhere. S2 is written as two long forward-leaning
  strokes that often come down to the line between the digits; in 210:S2, 126:S2, 206:S2, 216:S2,
  17:S2 and 219?:S2 the strokes run through the place of a middle digit, so a short "1" could be
  hidden or imagined there (flagged where I was unsure). D3: the three dots are tiny at this
  resolution; in several groups (notably many of the 190:D3 and 185:D3, 10:D3, 17:D3) I could make
  out only two dots for certain and took the third as merged with a digit; I did not see a clear
  case of a deliberate one-dot or two-dot mark, but a D3 here should be treated as "dots, most
  likely three".
- Marks not in the list (each once): a thick bar with three dots above it (f. 313r L01, over 9); two
  short backward strokes like a doubled S1 (f. 313r L12, over 13); a bar crossed by a short upright
  stroke, perhaps a badly formed O (f. 313r L03, over 1); a small circle with no bar visible (f. 313r
  L19, over 24); a small short stroke or blot (f. 313r L10, over 1). In f. 313r L01 the B2 over 85
  has a third short stroke or dot above it.
