# Spanish letters of April to June 1662 in a Spanish-vocabulary number cipher of Williamson's informants (TNA SP 84/165, Chauran's papers)

Status: in progress. Next step: the period decoding table at Kew, **SP 106/6 no. 34**, has not been seen; it would
settle every unread group (section 9).

Contributed by Andrew Beling, with AI agents, 15 November 2026. Catalogue entry 870; leads 1154 and 1803 are related
(section 8). Key rebuilt from the period interlinear decipherment of two sibling letters, frozen and tested. Five bare items are
in the key and decoded, but by the repository's rule none is yet read (holes in most sentences): one of them
(ff. 245r-v) is identified, against print, as a Spanish translation of a known treaty article. Four further items are
not shown to be in the key and are not read. **No person has
checked any of it against the images.** The site page and the ledger rows are left to the maintainer.

## 1. Source and how it was read

- **Archive.** The National Archives (Kew), SP 84/165, State Papers Foreign, Holland, 1662: ff. 187-189, 231, 243-246,
  260, 264-265, 294-295, 306, 313-314, 315-316, 317-318 (catalogue items SP 84/165/112, /113, /134, /141, /149,
  /152, /171, /177, /181, /182, /183; TNA Discovery, e.g. [SP 84/165/112](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C7306704)).
- **Images.** Copies from Gale's State Papers Online, saved on site at the Library of Congress on 14 November 2026 by
  Andrew Beling. They are licensed and are **not included**: anyone checking needs State Papers Online or a copy from
  Kew. Each image is a microfilm frame of an opening (two pages, about 3900 x 2600 px).
- **Who read it.** AI agents (Claude, Opus) on 15 November 2026: every item transcribed group by group from the images
  twice, blind of each other; the two passes aligned and decoded; a separate verifier (Claude, Opus) then transcribed a
  fixed sample of 140 groups blind and rechecked the tests (section 7). No person has compared any of it with the images.
- **Transcription conventions** (`transcriptions/`): groups as `number:mark`, marks `B1` one bar, `B2` two bars, `B3`
  three bars (rare), `D3` three dots (`D1`/`D2` where fewer were seen), `M` an "m", `O` a bar with a small circle,
  `S1` one backward stroke, `S2` two strokes, `P` a bar with two dots (./.); `X` before a struck group; `?` and
  `[alternative]` for doubtful numerals. On the glossed letters `Lnn G` is the Spanish written above cipher line nn.

## 2. The cipher and whose it is

- **System.** Numbers from 1 to about 240, almost every number under one of nine marks; the same number under another
  mark is another group (about 2,000 possible; 1,036 distinct groups occur in about 10,600 transcribed). Values are
  words, names, syllables and single letters in one list, in Spanish, with homophones (several groups for "que", "de",
  "y"), numbered in no alphabetical order. A one-part list as used here; the key at Kew is described as a decoding table.
- **The key at Kew.** Satoshi Tomokiyo, *Cryptiana*, "Some Orangist Ciphers (1662)"
  (<http://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/chauran.htm>, 2012): "PRO SP 106/6 no. 34 is a decoding table of a similar cipher
  with a vocabulary in Spanish", with nine series 1-211 (no mark), 1-210 (bar), 1-211 (double bar), 1-211 (three dots),
  1-210 ("m"), 1-220 (crossed circle), 1-240 (grave, "un tiret a rebour"), 1-239 (double prime, "deux virgule"), 1-234
  (./., "une barre et deux points"). His "English Ciphers during the Restoration Period"
  (<http://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/charlesii2.htm>, 2024) gives it as DECRYPT record **R510, image 0104**. The highest
  number seen here under each mark (215, 210, 210, 219, 212, 220, 240, about 239, 234, with stray higher readings) fits
  those ranges. We take this cipher to be that table's (inference: the table is unseen).
- **Not a Spanish royal cipher.** The layout is that of the French-vocabulary cipher of Williamson's informants
  Chauran and Wickfort (SP 106/6 nos. 32-33, Tomokiyo), not of the Spanish chancery's ciphers of the period.
- **Whose.** The catalogue of Chauran's letters in the same volume: SP 84/165/35 (5 Feb 1662) "Spanish cipher is
  inadequate: [Chauran] will devise another"; /54 (26 Feb) "Sends cipher"; /70 (19 Mar) "Insists on payment before he
  sends remaining Spanish papers ... Additions to cipher"; /114 (f. 190, 7 May) "his work on the Spanish cipher".
  Wickfort, another informant, wrote on 14 May (SP 84/165/127) of a new cipher and of sending "quelque chose espagnol".
  The best reading is that this is the cipher Chauran made for Spanish papers; Wickfort is not excluded (inference).
  Tomokiyo says only that Chauran "apparently he also tackled French and Spanish ciphers", and does not say whose
  no. 34 is.
- **What the items are.** Real Spanish diplomatic letters in Spanish (ff. 245r-v match a printed treaty text; names and
  dates fit), put into an English informant's cipher and, on six items, glossed between the lines by someone holding
  its table. So they are copies made for Williamson and re-enciphered for the post, not Spanish ciphertext intercepted
  in transit, and not exercises. How the copies were obtained is not known; Gamarra's embassy at The Hague, which
  received copies of the King's despatches to Fuente, is the likely place (inference). The headings over the first
  cipher line of ff. 189 and 260 are written partly in French in the gloss hand ("le 27 Abril du Rey d'Espanna a
  Mansera"; "Paris du Mr de la Fuente a Dn Est. di Gamarra le ocho Juin"), so the decipherer was probably
  French-speaking: Chauran himself or Williamson's office (not settled).

## 4. The key (`key/`)

- `key/table_v1.tsv`: built only from ff. 189r and 260r-v, each transcribed twice blind (f. 189: 504 groups, numerals
  agreeing 99.6%, marks 97.0%; f. 260: 674 groups, 98.8% and 98.1%; `tests/blind_agreement_ff189_260.txt`), the
  period gloss aligned to the groups by program (`key/alignment_auto.txt`) and corrected line by line
  (`tests/scripts/make_table.py`). 392 groups; **355 settled** (tiers firm 143, mixed 27, single 185) form the table,
  37 unsettled are not used. Frozen before any test: sha256 `9619f6ef0896b5efd0b0b2a6b14dddd305df983b6b022249ac8b176c8e9cd029`.
- `key/extension_v1.tsv`: **47 groups** added after the tests while decoding, each with its basis and evidence, frozen:
  sha256 `46c78b731043f7674cfa04471fd32120767286ffe28fb0c947d58b62083eb809`. By basis: 7 from period glosses on the
  opening of f. 187r (independent of the passages read: Gamarra, Fuente, despacho, inclusa, copia, dando, mismo);
  20 from the printed French text of the treaty article (Dumont, section 6), of which 6 recur outside ff. 245r-v where
  they fit (Paris, abril, imposicion, veinte, subdito, general) and 14 rest on f. 245 alone; 20 from context only, set
  from the same bare passages they are then used to read (guesses, e.g. 117 "bri", 2:S1 "forma").
- `key/chauran1662_key.tsv`: both in one table with a column `source` (`frozen`, `frozen (unsettled, not used)`,
  `extension`). Hashes in `key/SHA256.txt`.

## 5. Tests (plan `tests/TEST_PLAN.md`, written before the runs; one run each)

**Test A: does the frozen table predict the period gloss on letters it was not built from?** Pass mark: overall hit
rate at least 70% and above all 200 shuffled tables (values permuted among the same groups); ff. 264 and 294 each at
least 60%. Result (`tests/test_a_result.txt`): f. 264 81.5%, f. 294 85.1%, f. 187 opening 81.6%, f. 243 opening 88.9%;
**overall 83.7%** (970 of 1,159 table groups on glossed lines); shuffled tables mean 20.4%, **maximum 26.5%. Pass.**

these items, not the words of a reading.

**ff. 245r-v, decoded, not read; identified** (`chauran1662_f245_reading.md`, a joined Spanish text and English, with
every supplied word marked; it has holes or supplied words in most sentences, so by the rule of `profile.schema.json`
it is not yet a reading). A Spanish translation of the
"Article séparé touchant l'imposition de cinquante Sols par Tonneau" of the Franco-Dutch treaty of Paris, 27 April
1662, printed in French by Jean Dumont, *Corps universel diplomatique* VI part 2 (1728), pp. 418-419 (no. CLXII; read
on the page images of Internet Archive item `corpsuniverseldi06dumo`, n1029-n1030). A heading (L01-L04) calls it a
translation of the separate article on the fifty "placas" a ton on foreign ships leaving French ports, to be sent to
Fuente with a despatch. The text follows the French clause by clause: equality of the two allies' subjects with natives
in duties under article 20 of the treaty of alliance shall not derogate from the fifty sols a ton laid in France on
foreign ships; Dutch subjects pay it like other foreigners unless the King decides otherwise on their representations;
orders to take it once a voyage, on leaving port, not entering; salt ships pay half; a like Dutch duty may not exceed
what Dutch ships pay in France; article 20 otherwise in full force; dated Paris, 27 April. Signatures follow in
syllables (de Lomenie, "le jeune", Beuningen, de Huybert, Boreel). In the reading 55% of the words are read from the
frozen table, 28% are supplied (`[?word]`, many from Dumont's French and so not independent), 17% are unread.
Read from the frozen table alone: "ha estipulada", "igualdad", "no derogara", "cincuenta placas por tonelada
establecida en Francia", "su Mad", "en lo venidero", "cargados de sal", "el dia de hoy" among others. On a test fixed
before decoding (verifier), the frozen table alone shows 9 of 17 named items of the first two clauses (mark 10:
a near miss); with the extension 14, but those extra items come from the crib.

**Decoded, not read** (the decryption files are complete; the passages below are the parts that build sentences; the
English is a gist):
- **ff. 187r-188r.** Philip IV to Fuente, Aranjuez 26 April 1662 (lines 1-5 glossed in the period hand): Gamarra has
  been told to correspond with Fuente on the enclosed copy of the despatch. Then a copy of the King's letter to Fuente
  of 13 April: *"En una de vuestras cartas de [...] del passado he visto ... que ... del trattado de liga entre Francia
  y Holanda ... de la imposicion de cincuenta placas por tonelada"*; Spain is to seek inclusion in the Franco-Dutch
  league (*"procurar que yo [...] incluido en la liga que se trata entre [Francia] y Holanda"*), Gamarra is given the
  same orders and the two are to keep each other informed. Then the King to Gamarra, acknowledging his letters of
  22 March: the league, the States General, and *"Luis Felis de Longas para consul de ... Mi[d]de[l]bo[r]g en
  Zelanda"* on conditions (a salary of not over fifty [...], no other occupation).
- **ff. 317r-318v.** Fuente to the King, Paris, [May or June]: protests over a forcible act on the Flanders frontier and
  an *arresto* (a French arrêt); the resident of Luxembourg; *"del [...]r de Linquen"* (Fort Linck by our inference;
  the catalogue describes ff. 264 and 293 as about the "Governor of Lingen" and the "Governor of Linguem": SP 84/165/152
  and /171, TNA Discovery records C7306744 and C7306764, seen 16 Nov 2026); *"el conde de [?Bri]enne y su hijo"*; arbitration *"desde el de
  Chateau-Cambresis"*; ends with a copy enclosed. Fort Linck and Brienne are identifications from syllables (inference).
- **ff. 313r-314r.** [Gamarra] to the King: *"aora no se offrece cosa digna de la noticia de V. M."*; the league he has
  been ordered to further; *"los estados de la provincia de Holanda"*; *"el residente del elector de Brandeburgo"*;
  letters from Paris.
- **f. 231r-v.** [Gamarra] to [Fuente]: English news: *"el duque de Jorc"*; *"la imposicion sobre las [chi]mineas"*
  (probably the hearth tax of 1662, inference); an oath refused by most of the preachers (*"juramento de
  fidelidad"*); the Anglo-Dutch talks over the seas of Europe; the States to fit out twenty frigates against
  privateers.

The four "not shown" items (ff. 243r-244r, 244v, 306, 315) are transcribed once (`transcriptions/`) and not decoded here.

## 7. Independent check (verifier, 15 November 2026)

A second agent, given only the images and the conventions, wrote its sample rule and pass marks first, transcribed
60 glossed groups (ff. 189r, 260r, lines 2-10) and 80 bare groups (ff. 245r, 313r, lines 2-8), and froze them before
opening any of this work.
- Glossed groups: numbers agree with the transcriptions here in 58 of 60, number and mark in 55. The verifier's own
  attachment of the gloss to single groups agreed with the table in only **28 of 60, against a mark of 80%: a miss**.
  The misses are shifts in the verifier's alignment (the first words written over a line belong to the end of the line
  before, as the table shows by decoding those line ends); all ten six-group runs decode into the Spanish written over
  them (counted after the miss; not a test fixed in advance).
- Bare groups: numbers 95-98% and number and mark 86-89% the same as the two passes here (mark 85%: reproduced). 42 of
  the 80 groups have a frozen-table value; all 42 give the same word as the decryption here.
- Tests A and B rerun: identical outputs. Treaty article against Dumont: section 6.
- Files: `tests/verifier/` (rule and marks, both samples, the expectation written from Dumont before decoding, their
  sha256 with times, and the helper's transcription of Dumont's French).
