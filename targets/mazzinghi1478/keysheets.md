# Key sheets checked against the Mazzinghi 1478/79 cipher (6 Nov 2026)

Question: is any Medici key sheet of 1475-1485 the key of this cipher (LXXII 170, LXXXIX 142, CXXXVII 252), and does
any carry its code numerals (10, 12 = Zanobi, 30, 40, 51, 231, 10/3)?

Test signature (from `key_refined.tsv` and `glyphs.md`): one sign per letter for most letters, letter-like signs,
word division kept: **a = +, e = χ (x/y), i = s/5, o = r, t = H (double cross), r = 6, d = o, c = σ, l = 2+ (hook + cross),
n = n, s = 4, m = 8, g = m, u = q, b = =, p = p / barred p**. A sheet is "this cipher" only if several of these agree;
a = + and b = = alone are not enough (they recur in several unrelated Laurentian keys, see 115, 186, 188).

Images: full server size from the ASF MAP viewer, in the session scratchpad
`C:\Users\dbour\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\C--Users-dbour-cypher\018b508d-1df1-440f-b1ab-1675801c41a6\scratchpad\keys1478\`
(file names `f<filza>_d<doc>[_c._<carta>]_<r|v>.jpg`; not committed). Rectos and versos of every document were viewed.
For sheets judged "another", the sign values given are the first rows of the alphabet read at 1-2.6x: enough to
exclude the sheet, not a transcription.

## Result

**None of the sheets is this cipher's key, and none has its nomenclator.** Every alphabet fails the test on e, o, t
and most other letters. No sheet pairs 12 with Zanobi or 10 with a Duke; the numeral lists that exist give 10, 12, 30,
40, 51 to other names. The cipher's code list is not in MAP LXXII 178-189 bis or CXIX 115-127.

New sibling found by the way: **MAP CXXXVII 63** (inventory "Poscritto cifrato anonimo. s. d.", lead row of the first
ASF sweep) is written in **this** cipher: with `key_refined.tsv` it gives `8χoχ4s8χ n+qχ` = medesime nave,
`+mχnrq+` = a Genova, `osmχnrq+` = di Genova, `s4H58+6χ` = istimare, `fr+ngχ4s` = francesi, `in =+σ+or6s` = in[m]bas[ci]adori,
`χ4o#rqr` = (v)escovo. Eight lines, bare, clear words between ("volere papa", "parlino", "nostro", "ragione", "modo"),
one numeral **60**. Image: `keys1478/f137_d63_r.jpg` (1264 x 437). Not transcribed here.

## LXXII 170, line 24: the gloss over 10, re-read at 6x

Crops at 4x, 6x, 7x and 9x of page px (180-345, 1158-1202) of `img/f72_d170_r.jpg`.

What the ink shows: three letters, then **Duc(h)** (D, u, c clear; then a looped ascender, h-like), then four or five
small letters ending in a nasal stroke or flourish (…a l? a n/m~). The two tall strokes that were read as the "b r" of
"d'albr" come down from the line-23 cipher signs above (descenders at page x ≈ 265 and ≈ 298): they are not gloss
letters. The first word is n-shaped, a letter with a descender, o: most likely **nro** (nostro, mercantesca r with a
descender) or lo; "lo" needs an l the ink does not show.

Reading: **"n(ost)ro? Duch(a) …al…ñ"**. "Duca" is secure. "di Calabria" is **not** supported letter by letter (no C or
Ch, no b-r-i-a); "d'Albania" fits the a-l and the nasal end but is not certain. The identification of code 10 stays
open; the gloss proves only that 10 is a duke.
