# Glyph inventory: MAP LXXII 170 recto (with MAP CXXXVII 252, MAP LXXXIX 142)

Source images: `img/f72_d170_r.jpg` (1272 x 1801). Coordinates are page pixels of 170 recto, given as
(x, y) at the centre of the sign; they come from word spans estimated off 3.5x strips and are good to about
±10 px. Each example is labelled `l.<line> w.<word> s.<sign>` and matches `t170.tsv`. The sheet
`crops/glyphs.png` shows every example in its word context, with a red triangle over the sign.

A cipher line is about 10 px high at x-height, so most signs are 8-14 px across. Ascenders and descenders
reach about 10 px beyond that.

## Look-alike pairs kept apart (decide only with care)
- `H` vs `#`: same shape, different height. In 2/2 both occur in one word with values t and l.
- `b` vs `6`: a straight ascender (f) vs a curved one (r). Several readings depend on it (9/7 'ascrivere' needs b = r).
- `q` vs `9` vs `g`: a straight tail (u), a curved tail (q), a looped or very long tail (s/ss).
- `4` vs `2 +`: a closed loop (s) vs an open hook followed by a separate cross (l). In 4/5, 8/6, 30/8 the split between them is uncertain.
- `x` vs `y`; `s` vs `5`; `t` vs `+`; `r` vs `2`; `n` vs `u`; `o` vs `{sg}`.
- `o`: reads d in initial and medial position in most words (dirvi, della, dare, dipoi, adosso, di). In palagio (6/8), antonio (10/4) and ridono (20/2) a plain-looking o reads o. Either two signs (perhaps a larger, rounder one) are merged, or the writer mixed plain letters in.
- `d` (∂): its values scatter (h, d, a). It is surely more than one sign: a large ∂ with a curled stem, and a small ꝺ with the stem up-right. They are not separated in the TSV.
- `f`: f and long s look alike (see above).

## Other features
- **Word division**: words are spaced, but some phrases run together ('incastello' 2/2). A word can run on to the next line (11/8 + 12/1 'intendere').
- **Dots**: a raised point `·` separates many words. A single dot or two dots (diaeresis) often sit over a
  word-final `r` or `s` (1/3, 1/4, 1/10, 3/7, 4/1, 18/2, 29/4, 30/3). They are probably word-end marks; they are not transcribed as tokens.
- **Numerals as codes**: clear numerals stand in the cipher stream and get name glosses: `12` with gloss 'Zanobi'
  (13/3), `10` with gloss '?o Duca d?albr?' (24/2, Duca di Calabria?). `5 1` in 3/5, 14/6 and 22/7 may be code 51; '18' at 19/6.
  The bare letter 142 also has `· 10 ·`. Treat numerals as nomenclator codes, not as letters.
- **Superscripts**: a small `m` with a dot over numerals in 30-31 ('50 m', '40 m': migliaia?). The overline in `n~`, `q~` is an ordinary clear abbreviation.
- **Clear text** is mixed in throughout ('Solo', 'apunto', 'como', 'quello', 'tutti', 'e cosi trovarete...'), and lines 21 and 31-32 are almost all clear. The glossator repeats clear words in some places.
- **Glosses** are a continuous running decipherment written above the line. They are much more compact than the cipher, so they **drift left** (by 100-400 px towards the right end of a line). Pairs were aligned by order and by key consistency, not by x position. A few glosses sit below the line (3 end, 17/8-9). `g170.tsv` lists every gloss segment with its own x-range.
- **Bare letters** (252, 142): no sign was seen there that 170 lacks, except two variants: a `p` with a bar under the descender, and a double cross with a bar beneath, both in 142 line 2 (around x 520-600, y 210). 252 has a triple stroke `≡` (around 110,205). The group `olop`/`rlop` recurs in 252 and 142, and `d e l o p` appears in 170 11/5: possibly a code word.

Other observations from 252:
- **`2 +`**: in 252 the hook's foot always runs straight into the bar of a cross whose vertical has a descender
  (and often a short ascender). It reads as one sign everywhere (1/1, 1/2, 2/4, 2/5, 4/9, 5/7, 6/2, 8/1, 8/4, 8/5).
  Transcribed `2 +` as instructed; it looks like a single sign `{l}`.
- **`2` with a top bar** (z-like, Ƶ), sitting on the line with no tail: 252 2/9 s4 (1005,205), 4/5 s5 (575,293).
  Transcribed `2` with a note; could be a distinct sign.
- **`#`** proper (two long verticals passing above and below the line) occurs in 252 4/1, 8/6, 9/4, 11/10.
- **`t`/`+`**: crosses with a long left bar and a vertical that drops below the line are frequent; transcribed `+`
  unless the top is forked/raised (then `t`), with a note.
- Rare 170 tokens used in 252: `i` (3/10, dotted stroke), `a` (10/5, 11/9: a bold ɑ), `~` (6/6), `u` (3/8), `l` (7/4: a large
  cursive looped l; 1/7 and 10/6 'rlop' have a plain tall stroke there instead, transcribed `l` with a note).
- `s` vs `5`: the 252 hand writes this sign with a flag on top and a tail below; the split is made on the flag
  (straight and angular = `5`, rounded = `s`) and is often uncertain. Both read i in 170.
- **Interlinear**: one mark only, a double-barred cross written between lines 13 and 14 over 14/2 (175,738). No
  decipherment or gloss anywhere on 252.
- **Line 3** of 252 (from x 500) is written in a looser cursive with large looped descenders (g, y, {h}); several
  groups there look like clear Italian (`ogne...`, `mamada`?) and are transcribed sign by sign with low confidence.

Other observations from 142:
- `{Hp}` (12 uses), `{dbar}` (11), `#` (19) and `2 +` (42 tokens) all recur. The hook and cross of `2 +` always join here too.
- Small **rings** instead of dots over a short stroke (ı°: 5/13, 9/2, 13/4) or over g (15/8, 16/5); like `{T}` they
  often stand alone as one-sign words (nulls or abbreviation marks?).
