[p.436]                                                          [8 March 1799]
  (editors' bracketed date = date of the Mary's action; the letter itself is dated 19 March)

To the Secretary of State from David Humphreys, U. S. Minister to Madrid, Spain

(Nº 188.)                                                  MADRID March 19th 1799.

SIR, I have learned from various quarters that the gallant defence made by our armed merchant vessels against French
Privateers in several instances has greatly tended to inspire a respect in the crews of the latter for our flag.
Captain Gamaliel Bradford, commanding the Ship Mary of Boston, carrying 25 men and 10 four pounders, has acquired
much reputation by repulsing two French Privateers of Superior force and entering safe at Malaga a few days ago with a
valuable {cargo of} fish and sugar. He had not a single man killed or wounded. The French Privateer soon followed him
into the same port, having had two men killed and thirteen wounded — some of them mortally. Letters from Algeciras
advise that the French Privateers which have so long infested the Streights of Gibraltar are now very cautious in not
approaching near American vessels until it be ascertained, whether they are well armed or not. I have expected this
{resolute conduct on our} part would have excited great irritation among the French and occasioned complaints from the
Ambassador to this court against our armed vessels in the ports of Spain. He has hitherto been silent; and I know not on
what principle to explain a late change in his public conduct towards me — he has invited me (with the Diplomatic
Corps) to dine with him tomorrow, it being, I understand, a national feast. I have excused myself from attending. —
Indeed {I} had made a previous arrangement to go to Aranjuez in order to confer with Mr de Urguijo on several points of
business, and especially {to} remonstrate against the conduct of the Governor of Malaga, who {has} manifested in a late
instance unfriendly dispositions in regard to {the} citizens of the United States. — A conduct I am persuaded, which
{is} contrary to the intentions of his Government. — Within a short {time} past I have experienced more prompt attention
than heretofore {to} the applications which I have had occasion to make to the Depa[rtment]
[p.437]
of foreign affairs. The King has lately ordered his Ambassador at Paris to reclaim of the French Government the
American Schooner, Alert, Jacob Oliver master, captured near St Sebastian in voilation of the Spanish Territory and
carried into Bayonne. His Majesty has likewise, in consequence of a pointed representation from me, ordered the embargo
to be immediately taken off from a cargo of Cod fish at Alicante (belonging to Messrs Thomas C. Amory and Isaac Winslow
junr) which the Governor had caused to be seized upon information of their being the produce of the English fishery.
This prompt decision saves the almost ruinous delay and expence which must otherwise have resulted from a trial before
the Supreme Council of War. For however well that Tribunal might be disposed to hasten their proceedings, it is
impossible they should for years finish what is now before them. — At this critical conjuncture when it is well known
that the French Ambassador Guillemardet and Mr de Urguijo are far from being on friendly terms, I entertain hopes of
being able to draw some informations from the latter which may be useful to us.
  I have lately received an office from Mr de Urguijo announcing that the port of Gibraltar is considered as blocked,
and that the intercourse of Neutrals between that port and those of Spain will be regulated accordingly.
  Captain Ingraham, who arrived from Tripoli at Carthagena a few days ago and is now in quarantine, has written to me
that he has Dispatches for me which it is necessary for him to deliver with his own hand. I have replied that I conclude
he did not know of the arrival of the Consuls for Tunis and Tripoli when he wrote, but that being now apprized of that
fact as well as of the contents of the Dispatches of which he is the bearer, he would be able to judge of the expediency
of his coming to Madrid — and I added that since my residence at this Court I had not been invested with the management
of Barbary affairs, as a farther circumstance to enable him to make his arrangements.
  I transmit herewith the copy of two communications from the Swedish Chargé des Affaires to me on the subject of my
application (mentioned to you in a former letter) that Swedish armed vessels would take under their convoy merchant
vessels of the United States which might be sailing in the same direction with the Convoy. In a posterior conference
with that Chargé des Affaires, I have answered his question respecting the authority by which Danish vessels of war were
in the habit of convoying American Merchantmen, that I only knew the fact without being able to ascertain the source
from whence that protection originated, but at least that it must be by the approbation of their Government.  *  *  *
                                                                    ^^^^^^^^^ OMISSION MARK
  Calepio, the Cisalpine Minister at this Court is recalled, to be Minister of foreign Affairs at Milan. No successor is
named.
  I send to you by way of curiosity the Pope's Bull allowing the subjects of His Catholic Majesty in Spain and the
Canary Islands to eat Meat in Lent during the present war with England.
  With sentiments of great respect and esteem
    I have the honour to be,
      Sir,
        Your most obedt and most humble Servt
                                                            D. HUMPHREYS
[p.438]
  P. S. I enclose an account of expences incurred by Mr OBrien for the support of American Seamen, & take occasion to
reiterate my reccommendations of that Gentleman to the honorable notice of Government
                                                            D. H.
The SECRETARY of STATE, &c &c &c
