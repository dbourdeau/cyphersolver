# vibejuel1651 - reading of the cipher passages (Danish, spelling of the letters)

Letters of Peder Vibe to Peder Juel, 1649-1655 (Arkivalieronline unit 261583, images 62-93). Read by sense from `decryption.txt`
with `key.tsv`, after the system was validated (`validation.txt`). The re-reading of the held-out sets was done AFTER the
measurements; the measurements were not run again.

Marks: CAPITALS = cipher words, certain (every number read, the word is Danish of the time); {X} = probable (one number corrected
with a reason taken from the image, or an old spelling; see the list); [x] = restored (word supplied from the sense, not counted);
<...> = unresolved; #211 = nomenclator code, not identified; lower case = clear text of the letter, abridged with "...". Word
division and punctuation are ours. Spelling of the time kept (u for v, w for v, ff, hafuer).

## What the dossier says about the cipher, in clear

- Image 62 (11 Jan 1649): the new alphabet ("dett ny Alphabet") will be sent; meanwhile Vibe writes "wed woris forige gamble
  Ciffres". This is the change from the old to the new cipher that the decode shows.
- Image 80 (end of N. 334, 6 Jan 1653): two numbers are to be added to the common nomenclator ("sub N° 349" and "N° 561"); Juel
  writes his numbers in letters ("dine bogstaffuer ... denotere tallet") and is asked to write t and k more clearly.
- Image 93 (P.S., 1655): the last cipher sent by Erich Krag had no code for the States General nor for the Øresund.

## Why the holes

The cipher words in letters read to 97.5%. What does not read are the 143 nomenclator codes: no list of them is in the unit, the
interlinear gloss of N. 319 covers letters only, and only two codes are defined in clear (349 and 561, on image 80). Some can be
guessed from the sense (211 the king? 770 Sweden? 222 Queen Christina?), but a guess is a restored word, so they stay holes.
