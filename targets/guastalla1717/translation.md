## Cipher text

1. In substance I see that [the Prince?] Trautson had come back from the family estates.
2. [Count?] Starhemberg and Trautson [?] were expected the following week.
3. They were waiting for my answers on the content of the earlier letters written to me.
4. On these, as far as I find that I have only answered them by the last post, I do not fail to send them again [?].
5. I do not know what more precise answer to give, unless I am told clearly whether the claim of the reversal letter ("lettera reversale") has been dropped or not.
6. That letter has the very same effect as the eleventh article of the declaration, and is even worse.
7. It amounts to a tacit abdication of my rights, to which I will never agree.
8. I will not do myself and my house such an obvious harm.
9. So I will wait for you to speak to me clearly, in positive terms, about this reversal letter.
10. Without that I do not know what answer to give [?].
11. You have written to me that nothing more is claimed than what is commonly practised in [the investitures?] of the other fiefs.
12. I reply to you that, if [the investiture?] that is meant to be granted to me follows the form [?] of the old investitures usually granted to my forebears, the owners of the territory that is to be ceded to me,
13. and so without the least harm to me and to my posterity, I will have no difficulty in accepting it.
14. But if they wanted to attach new conditions to it, I could put my thoughts on them to you with all the greatest care [?].
15. [Passage not fully read.] It will be to my advantage [?], and I could add nothing more on the matter [?].
16. It suits me to wait from you for the precise answers and decisions on the difficulties raised above.
17. I will not lose a moment in answering them, and I will not fail to put them up again for consideration.
18. The revenue of the territory named to me in the list you sent is so small and poor, compared with the whole [value?] of [?],
19. that it never deserves that I let myself be tempted into the least harm to myself.
20. I declare again that whatever I do, provided that no harm to my rights comes of it,
21. will always be out of regard for the high merit of [code 92 93: the Emperor?],
22. for whom I will always have the most dutiful veneration,
23. in the hope that, in time, I will enjoy the acts of that most generous munificence and [?] justice.

## Clear closing

I hear with much regret of the bad health of the young Count, your son. I understand the distress of mind that you feel. I pray the Lord to give him back his former health, and to you every true good. Guastalla, 24 September [written "7bre"] 1717. Antonio Ferdinando Gonzaga.

## Gist

The duke writes to an agent at the imperial court, or in touch with it. The court has offered the duke a territory. The duke refuses to sign a "reversal letter" that would give up other claims. The duke will accept the territory only on the old terms of investiture. The territory's income is too small to justify any loss of rights.
