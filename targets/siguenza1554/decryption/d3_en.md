It is true that having no money, nor anywhere to get it from, is what gives the most trouble. (It will soon be known whether it comes or not.)

The Prince of Salerno is still at Castro; it is said that he wants to come to Sermoneta (to follow) more conveniently from there his plots and intrigues, (in which he puts all his trust and spends his time. I hope in Our Lord that, since he has so little foundation, all of it will do him little good.)

The little remedy there is for fulfilling it; and the trouble is that nobody is to be found who will buy anything.

No more than two thousand five hundred ducats have been sold, at ten per cent redeemable, in payments on the fiscal revenues, (and in other things of small importance), which will come to the sum of twenty-five thousand ducats; and at this price I should be glad to find someone to sell to.

I would like to be able to fulfil it, for I lack neither diligence in seeking expedients nor goodwill; (but in fact) I see no order nor way of being able to get it now. (Your Lordship will do me the favour,) since you know the need there is here.
