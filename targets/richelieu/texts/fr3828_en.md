## CXIX — fr. 3835 no. 15, to Rancé, [15 (?) February 1629]

… His Majesty wishes the Queen to tell him this news and to give him the brevet ⟦binding⟧ the word ⟦of {the Cardinal
de Bérulle}⟧ that he ⟦will not unite⟧ any ⟦benefice in his gift to his congregation⟧. ⟦{The King}⟧ wishes this of his
own accord.

His Majesty gives La Valasse to M. de Metz, and Saint-Faron of Meaux to M. de Moret. …

The letter that ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ wrote ⟦to the Parlement⟧ is put in terms not well chosen, in my opinion; I fear
it may have ⟦a bad effect⟧ for ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧, and it would have been better to spare ⟦her authority and⟧ the
goodwill of ⟦that company towards {the Queen Mother}⟧. Speak of it only to ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧. ⟦{The King}⟧ may well
write ⟦so⟧, but it was not fitting that ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ ⟦should use⟧ such terms.

As for ⟦Saint-Malo⟧, since ⟦{the Keeper of the Seals}⟧ asks for it, I think ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ should grant it to
him. ⟦{The Queen Mother}⟧ does me more honour than I deserve in wishing for my advice; ⟦La Racinière⟧, an old servant,
would well deserve a pension of ⟦a thousand livres⟧.

⟦{The Queen Mother}⟧ may allow ⟦Madame de Vendôme⟧ to go ⟦and sleep at the Bois de Vincennes⟧ more often, since she
wishes it.

## CXXXV — fr. 3825 no. 28, to Rancé, Susa, 21 March [1629]

… I am too anxious that ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ be told of everything that happens … Last night ⟦{Mme de Longueville}⟧
received a courier, who came riding his horses to death, saying that ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ has had ⟦the {Mme de
Longueville} and {Princess Marie} put in the Bois de Vincennes⟧. We are waiting for the lame man before we believe this
news … ⟦{The Queen Mother}⟧ will surely, I am sure, have seen at the same time to ⟦softening the mind of {Monsieur}⟧.

… Before learning the news brought by the courier of ⟦{Mme de Longueville}⟧, which we do not yet believe, I was in
doubt whether ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ should have the Te Deum sung … to stifle with this joy the fears that might arise
here ⟦discontented⟧. Meanwhile one must speak of the King's victories with ⟦modesty⟧ so as ⟦not to provoke⟧ ⟦{Spain}⟧
beyond measure.

From Susa, 21 March.

## CXLVI — fr. 3828 no. 8, to Rancé, 14 April 1629

I am extremely sorry for the headache of ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧. … The honour I have long had of being ⟦near her⟧ makes
me think that the displeasure she has at the affair of ⟦{Monsieur}⟧ is partly the cause of her illness; I wish with all
my heart that this affair were ended to the satisfaction of Lysandre [the Queen Mother]; one must try to end it;
⟦the people of {Monsieur}⟧ will now see that ⟦this marriage⟧ is no longer a matter he can think of, and so they will
be readier ⟦to bring him to⟧ satisfy ⟦{the King} and {the Queen Mother}⟧.

I did not know for certain of the pregnancy of ⟦the {England}⟧ [Avenel: the Queen of England]. If ⟦{the Queen
Mother}⟧ sends there, as I think she may, it will be right to send a man ⟦capable in affairs, of acting and
speaking⟧. I think the sieur ⟦de Meaux⟧, who has been there before, would be fitter than another. Besides, I think
⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ should wait until the beginning of next month, by which time we shall know how things stand for
⟦peace or war⟧; and between now and then I shall send you a short instruction for ⟦the said de Meaux⟧.

I beg you from now on to acknowledge receipt of my letters by such and such a gentleman courier.

I wish M. de ⟦Marillac⟧ every satisfaction, but I do not think ⟦{the King}⟧ will consider the matter you write of
until the whole ⟦council⟧ is ⟦together again⟧, and then ⟦{the Keeper of the Seals}⟧ will press it.

You have not told me what you did about the letter I wrote to you for M. ⟦de Brassac⟧. From now on I beg you to answer
point by point on whatever concerns business.

## CXLVII — fr. 3828 no. 10, to Rancé, Susa, 20 April [1629]

I have received your letters of the 9th and 11th of this month. M. ⟦de Brassac⟧ will prepare for his ⟦embassy⟧ and,
without yet letting the matter out, he must leave at the beginning of ⟦[?September]⟧ so as to arrive ⟦at the end at
Rome⟧. The favour ⟦{the King}⟧ does him will not stand in the way of the other he wants. …

I greatly wish, for the service of ⟦{the King} and {the Queen Mother}⟧, that the affair of ⟦{Monsieur} be settled⟧.
But it is impossible to give precise and particular advice from afar. …

From Susa, 20 April.

## CLIII — fr. 3828 no. 17, to the Queen Mother, Susa, 29 April 1629

(pp. 291–292: the state of the troops, in clear, no cipher.) … Several well-founded suspicions of ⟦the temper of
Savoy⟧ have led the King to leave me here with his army. It was to be feared that, ⟦keeping {the King} occupied here,
he⟧ might want to make him ⟦lose the time for⟧ subduing the rebels of Languedoc.

His Majesty, having arrived at Valence, intends to besiege ⟦Privas⟧ and then to take a number of small places ⟦of the
Cévennes⟧.

At the same time he means to have ⟦the devastation of {Montauban} made by {Monsieur le Prince}⟧, who is ordered to go
there by ⟦Bordeaux⟧ and to take ⟦{the Duc d'Épernon}⟧ on the way; that ⟦of {Castres}⟧ by ⟦Estrées, and of
{Nîmes}⟧ by ⟦{Montmorency}⟧.

⟦The peace with {England} is made⟧, of which, if you please, nothing must be said until ⟦the [?20] May⟧ is past; it is
the greatest ⟦affair⟧ that ⟦{the King}⟧ could ⟦do⟧, which will deal a great ⟦blow to the Huguenots and⟧ will prevent
⟦{Spain} and⟧ those who would like ⟦to stir trouble from doing anything⟧. It has been found that ⟦Savoy⟧ wanted ⟦to
prevent it⟧; but in this its efforts were in vain. That is all I can send Your Majesty; …

From Susa, 29 April 1629. Your Majesty will, if you please, have this letter deciphered by M. de Rancé.

## CLIV — fr. 3828 no. 18, to Rancé, Susa, 30 April 1629

Monsieur, I have learnt nothing whatever of what the sieur de ⟦Nogent⟧ has done about the affair of ⟦{Monsieur}⟧; … Nor
have we learnt how the pardon of M. ⟦de Vendôme⟧ went through, …

⟦The peace with {England}⟧ is ⟦signed⟧ on both sides and the ⟦proclamation⟧ will be made ⟦on the [?20] May⟧. For this
purpose the ambassador of ⟦Venice⟧, named ⟦Contarini⟧, who resides in ⟦{England}⟧, will write to ⟦{the Queen
Mother}⟧ before that day that he has ⟦the signed articles of {England}⟧ in hand.

When you have received this letter, ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ will not fail, if she pleases, to have ⟦the peace proclaimed
in Paris⟧ solemnly; and at once a dispatch must be made to do the same throughout ⟦France⟧.

Word must also be sent on my behalf to all the ports of the maritime provinces, to give the same notice; and that from
now on they receive ⟦the {English}⟧ like the French.

As soon as this is received, you will send word to Calais, Boulogne and Le Havre that no ⟦harm⟧ be done ⟦to the
{English}⟧.

The same has already been done ⟦in {England}⟧.

By agreement between the two ⟦crowns⟧, two ambassadors extraordinary are to ⟦cross on the⟧ 1st of ⟦June⟧.
⟦{England}⟧ asks for ⟦{Mme de Chevreuse}⟧; we shall take good care not to do so, for the reasons ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧
knows. We have sent word that we need to send a man who understands ⟦affairs⟧; I think ⟦Châteauneuf⟧ will do this
business well. But it is not yet settled, though ⟦here⟧ we see no one else. ⟦{The Queen Mother}⟧ will, if she
pleases, accept that so pressing an occasion has not allowed ⟦{the King}⟧ to learn her opinion, … From Susa, 30 April
1629.

## CLV — fr. 3828 no. 19, to Rancé, Susa, 1 May 1629

Since writing to you ⟦the peace with {England}⟧, the ambassador of ⟦Venice⟧, being in doubt whether ⟦{England}⟧ will
not want two ⟦ambassadors, one great and one⟧ other, sending two ⟦in exchange⟧.

I thought it right that you should know that if ⟦{England} wants⟧ two, the intention of ⟦{the King}⟧ is to send
MM. ⟦d'Elbeuf and Châteauneuf⟧. That is why, if ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ receives news ⟦from Contarini in {England}⟧ that
they ⟦[?want]⟧ two, ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ will, if she pleases, promptly tell the said sieur ⟦d'Elbeuf⟧ to ⟦leave at
once⟧ ⟦to go to {England}⟧.

Meanwhile ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ will also know, if she pleases, that the courier who is to leave ⟦{England} on the⟧ 1st
of ⟦May⟧ will bring her news whether the said sieur ⟦Contarini⟧ has ⟦the signed [?articles] of the peace, and⟧ whether
⟦it is to be proclaimed in Paris on the [?20] May⟧.

From Susa, 1 May 1629.

## CLVIII — fr. 3828 no. 21, to Rancé, Susa, 2 May 1629

The dowager ⟦of {Lorraine}⟧ has no right, not even an apparent one, to ⟦the Montferrat⟧. The investiture of this
⟦duchy⟧ prefers males to all females, and if women came to it, she whom ⟦the son of {M. de Mantoue}⟧ has married is to
be preferred to any other; so that not only ⟦{M. de Mantoue}⟧ of the present, but his ⟦daughter-in-law⟧ exclude
Madame ⟦de {Lorraine}⟧ altogether.

M. ⟦de Piémont⟧ asks ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ to tell ⟦La Bernet⟧, when she leaves Paris, to behave towards ⟦his wife⟧
as ⟦he⟧ shall wish. ⟦{The Queen Mother}⟧ must instruct ⟦La Bernet⟧ to tell ⟦the Prince⟧ that she has ordered her so.
And meanwhile to order her, in fact, to serve ⟦her [?mistress]⟧ as she ought.

⟦{Monsieur}⟧ has sent for ⟦Le Coudray and⟧ others so many times that he and some others ⟦have gone⟧. It is rather
vexing to know nothing at all of what is happening in this affair, and of what ⟦Nogent⟧ has done.

Hurry, if you please, the castings of Dupré, to whom I am writing on the subject.

You will have 900 livres paid ⟦to Scot and⟧ 500 livres ⟦to Dumoulin⟧.

From Susa, 2 May 1629.

## CLXVII — fr. 3828 no. 26, to Rancé, 25 May 1629

I have written several times to ⟦{the Cardinal de Bérulle}⟧ that from afar I gave no advice. I have also written that
in general my thought was that ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ should ⟦soften towards {Monsieur}⟧, and that, he being her ⟦son⟧,
as he was, ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ would be inclined to it by her good nature, … that it was necessary ⟦to release
{Princess Marie} and {Mme de Longueville}⟧. Had I given that advice, I would also have written that it must be done on
condition that ⟦{Monsieur} gave up the marriage and that {Princess Marie} went away⟧.

If anyone says otherwise (which I do not believe), I beg ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ to have my letters shown to her.

Meanwhile, seeing that the bad outcome [of an affair] might perhaps be laid at my door, in which others can see more
clearly than I, and in which perhaps ⟦{Calori}⟧ would not be given ⟦the credit⟧ he deserves (which I cannot believe of
the ⟦goodness of {the Queen Mother}⟧, whatever is written here from various places), I beg Nestor [the Queen Mother]
to take no account of what I wrote by ⟦Saint-Germain⟧ on the particulars of this affair.

I confess that if ⟦{the Cardinal de La Valette}⟧ does not desire the satisfaction of ⟦{the Queen Mother}⟧ in this
⟦marriage⟧, I should be utterly mistaken, …
