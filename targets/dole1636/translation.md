# English translation of the ciphered passages (each passage is a phrase inside a letter written in clear)

## fol. 191r-v (views 332-333), A. Brun, [Dole, 1 July 1636]

- P1: the sooner you can come, the better
- P2: the cavalry relief sent by His Most Serene Highness
- P3: to go and join you at Salins
- P4: to delay as little as you can, and to expect no new help other than that relief
- P5: [that it is] a great risk to let four or five days go by without your coming to our gates
- P6: the diversions
- P7: the signals, the liveries (recognition marks)
- P8: the communications of that enemy
- P9: both more honourable and more useful by daylight than at night
- P10: [rations], flour and corn
- P11: with tools to reap the harvest there
- P12: that they burn Rigny, Talmay, Pontailler, Mirebeau
- P13: that they destroy outright the forge of Drambon and the others where the bombs thrown at us are made
- P14: [raids] into the Duchy [of Burgundy] as well as Bassigny and Bresse

## Summary

On 7 June (fol. 127) the Archbishop and the court urge Conflans to attack quickly; a letter they had addressed to him “was a trap for the enemy”.
On 25 June (fols. 181, 183; clear on fol. 182) the commis describe the French quarters: on the side of the bridge, a separate attack with three or four hundred horse and six hundred foot; elsewhere, an attack of twelve hundred men.
On 1 July (fol. 191) Brun writes that they must come as soon as possible with the cavalry relief sent by “His Most Serene Highness” (not identified here), without going to join it at Salins; that letting four or five days pass is a great risk; he recalls the diversions, the signals and recognition marks, the cutting of the enemy’s communications; an attack by day would be more honourable and more useful than by night. They are to bring rations, flour and corn, with tools to reap; the Gray garrison is to burn Rigny, Talmay, Pontailler and Mirebeau, and above all destroy the forge of Drambon and the others where the bombs thrown on Dole are made; raids are to go into the Duchy, Bassigny and Bresse.
The cipher was broken at the time. Antoine Rossignol deciphered the intercepted dispatches in Paris; in July Richelieu sent him to Condé’s camp with “the decipherment of the letters that those of Dole were writing to the Marquis of Conflans” (Longin, no. CCLXXVIII). In late July and early August the court warned Conflans three times that “the enemy has found the key of the cipher”; the letter of 12 August (fol. 106) says so again and changes to another cipher, which we have not broken.
