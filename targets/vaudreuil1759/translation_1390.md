## 1390 — Montréal, 16 April 1759 (ANOM COL C11A 104, ff. 75-76)

My Lord,
Fearing that next year we may find ourselves in the same dearth as this one, I have urged the contractor [munitionnaire] to order
his correspondents in France that, should they learn of the capture of some of the ships they have loaded with provisions for
him, they replace them at once and send him new cargoes from any port whatever.
I have the honour, my Lord, to inform {you} of this arrangement and to beg you to be good enough to {have} the contractor's
correspondents supplied with the ships they will need, should they be unable to find any; the contractor will pay the King in
Canada the freight of these ships at this year's rate. The risks are too great for him to pay that freight at any price
whatever, which obliges him to write to his correspondents that he does not mean to pay the freight anywhere but in the colony.
For the rest, my Lord, I can only repeat my wish that you add a supplement to the shipments of provisions that {will be} made to
us this year; on this above all depends the preservation of the colony, and without it it would fall of {it}self.
I am with very deep respect, my Lord, your very humble and very obedient servant. Vaudreuil [in clear]
