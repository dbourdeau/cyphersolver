## Documents

Wiesbaden, Hessisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Abt. 171 Nr. S 995: letter 1 = fols. 20r–21v; letter 3 = fols. 41r–41v; letter 5 = fols. 44r–44v (cipher part); postscript = fol. 53r. Digitisation on Archivportal-D, not reproduced here.
Date: spring 1620 (February–May): the neighbouring letter in the file (fols. 22r–23v), same channel, has “1620” in clear and answers a letter of Johann of 23 February; Willem Lodewijk died at the end of May 1620. The dates of the letters are in cipher, with number signs we could not resolve.
Sender: Willem Lodewijk of Nassau-Dillenburg (Wilhelm Ludwig), stadtholder of Friesland; signature in clear “E. L. trewer bruder Wilhelm Ludwig graff zu Nassaw”. Recipient: Johann VII of Nassau-Siegen, his brother (“E. L.” = Euer Liebden, Your Dilection; letter 5 says “euch”).
Letters 2 (fols. 22r-23v, dated 1620 in clear) and 4 (fols. 42-43, La Haye, in clear) of the file are not part of this contribution.
The 1609 letter of the file (img 00007, grid B) is already deciphered by a contemporary list and printed in clear by Groen van Prinsterer
(2nd series, vol. II, letter CCCLVI): not counted here.

In the spring of 1620 the Bohemian war was spreading into the Empire. Spinola was gathering in the Netherlands the army that would invade the Palatinate at the end of the summer; the Protestant Union was preparing its defence. Willem Lodewijk of Nassau (1560–1620), stadtholder of Friesland, wrote from Leeuwarden to his brother Johann VII of Nassau-Siegen (1561–1623), the theorist of territorial defence by militia (Landrettungswerk), then commissioned to the defence of the Palatinate. The letters are almost wholly in cipher, in a nine-cell grid with dots whose key survives in a family key book. They carry no contemporary decipherment and, to our knowledge, have not been edited. We publish three letters and a postscript: three pieces read beyond the 95% threshold we hold ourselves to, the fourth (letter 3) only with the restored words, with the reservation on prior work set out below.

## System and key

nine-cell grid (AKT · BLV · CMW / DNX · EOY · FPZ / GQ · HR · IS): each letter is the shape of its cell, with no, one or two dots; plus a nomenclator of signs and capitals for names. The key is written in the key book Abt. 171 Nr. C 667, image 55 (upper grid, “1620”).
The cover of S 995 says "Die Schlüssel sind zu C. 667 gelegt"; C 667 image 55 holds two 3x3 grids, a worked example dated 1620 and the
nomenclator. The upper ("natural") grid H reads the 1620 letters; the lower one (= Z 1807 img 0003) the 1606/1609 letters. `key.tsv` gives
grid H. Validation (`validation.txt`): 0.860 on 11 glossed words vs 0.185 for the other grid and at best 0.442 for 20 permuted grids.

Content in brief: Johann VII, commissioned to the defence of the Palatinate, thinks of resigning his command. Willem Lodewijk ("[A] and I")
urges him to keep the general command (letter 1, "beim commendo maintenirt"), but if age weighs on him, to withdraw as an "emeritus miles", as
he does himself; he calls a plan for the command of the relief forces "nicht practicabel vnd gantz immature". Letter 3 answers Johann's defence
plan for the Lower [Q] (very probably the Lower Palatinate): militia plus a regular corps, muster by Circles rather than one assembly of the
Union, no invasion towards the Weser expected this year, provisions first. Letter 5 hopes he will continue in his charge and finds it
dangerous to write such things "ohne chifer". The postscript is about officers' pay fixed by the late Elector and table money in the field.

## Limits

- Nomenclator not resolved. The capitals and symbols ([A], [A̅], [A·], [A°], [As], [Am], [B], [Q], [M], [O], [⊙], [2̲], [∇], [V], [W]) are printed as they stand, with no conjecture in the text. From the sense only: [A] and [B] stand for persons or authorities; [Q] “Lower” occurs twice, feminine, and very probably means the Lower Palatinate (Unterpfalz); [O] and [⊙] seem to be rivers; [∇] precedes a number (“[∇] gulden”) or a rank. The list of capitals (C 667, images 20–54) has not been seen.
- Capitals and dates. The dates are in cipher (“Datum [D] den [W]/[W] [S]. [y x° w]”) with unresolved number signs; the dating to spring 1620 rests on the context and on the neighbouring letter dated in clear.
- Word division by sense. The transcription of letter 5 (fols. 44r–44v) does not record the spaces between words: the word division was made from the sense on the decode.
- Letter 1, fol. 21v, lines 23–24. These two lines were not redone at high magnification; the transcription may contain doubled signs there; “vorzustellen” is restored.
- Postscript, line 16. The last line runs to the edge of the leaf: “behört zu werden ‹vz› in ‹lsterip…›”; its continuation (verso or a neighbouring leaf) has not been seen.
- Probable words. Most come from a dot misread in the transcription. For letter 3 a visual re-reading of the shapes (22 questions) confirmed 21; the 22nd (“were”, fol. 41r, line 14) became unresolved. The other pieces had no such re-reading.
- No images. The HHStAW digitisations were used for research only and are not reproduced here.

## Prior work (reservation)

We publish before finishing the prior-work checks; they are under way and this card will be updated. What we know: (1) a pencil slip inserted in the file (fol. 1), probably in a modern hand, with no name, date or reference, gives one or more grid shapes against the letters a…x, with several wrong or double equivalences and no decoded word: it is a partial reconstruction of the alphabet, not a decipherment; its author and date are unknown. (2) Willem Lodewijk’s 1620 letters to Johann VII are not in Groen van Prinsterer, Archives … de la maison d’Orange-Nassau, 2nd series, vol. II (1858), which prints in clear a 1609 letter from the same file; we found no edition of them in the Briefe und Akten zur Geschichte des Dreißigjährigen Krieges, in the editions of Willem Lodewijk’s correspondence or in the online finding aids; the file is not on DECODE; Daniel Bourdeau’s catalogue lists it as unsolved (no. 566). (3) Not yet seen: K. Wolf, “Aufbau eines Volksheeres in den Gebieten der Wetterauer Grafenkorrespondenz” (1937); L. Paul, Nassauische Unionspläne (1966); W. Hahlweg, Die Heeresreform der Oranier. Das Kriegsbuch des Grafen Johann von Nassau-Siegen (1973); and the origin of the pencil slip, to be asked of the Hessisches Hauptstaatsarchiv. If any of these holds an earlier reading, we will say so here and credit it.

## Method

Method: (1) the key was not reconstructed: it is written in the family key book (HHStAW Abt. 171 Nr. C 667, image 55), to which the cover of file S 995 points (“Die Schlüssel sind zu C. 667 gelegt”); (2) the upper grid was frozen (SHA-256), then validated on cipher words in the file that carry contemporary glosses in clear (fols. 25 and 29: “vnion”, “werbung”, “monatlich”, “künfftig”, “feindt”…): mean agreement 0.860 on 11 words, against 0.185 for the second grid and at best 0.442 for 20 permuted grids; (3) the shapes of each page were transcribed sign by sign from the digitisations, and each transcription was frozen by SHA-256 before any decoding; the key was never touched; (4) mechanical decoding, then reading by Daniel Bourdeau’s rule, with the statuses certain / probable / restored / unresolved; (5) for letter 3, a pre-registered targeted visual re-reading of doubtful shapes, answers applied mechanically; (6) final count redone by a script on the text published here. The pencil slip (fol. 1) was compared with the key only after the readings: no contradiction.
