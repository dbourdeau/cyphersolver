# English translation — Letter 5, cipher part, fols. 44r-44v: Willem Lodewijk of Nassau to Johann VII, [spring 1620]

Working translation, close to the text; […] = unresolved word; nomenclator signs stay in brackets.

Now this has come to me almost against all expectation […], because in your last letter you made known quite a different opinion; and although, after [A] and I had read your letter and your reflections, one cannot blame you for seeking to free yourself from so doubtful an affair, yet since the common good, the [B] and your reputation are notably concerned, we should like satisfaction to be given you and you to continue in the charge entrusted to you, which we consider to be for the greatest service of the [B], and right too that they should meet you fairly in it and that you enjoy the fruits of the services faithfully rendered for [W] years now. We will nevertheless think further about the matter and send you our advice at the earliest. We find it very dangerous, however, to write such important and far-reaching matters over here without cipher (“ohne chifer”); otherwise everything here stays on the former terms; sure news that the [V] Portuguese mentioned before are coming into the [Q]; [that] the [B] also strengthens himself with troops in time, to be a match for the enemy.
