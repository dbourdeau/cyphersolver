# English translation — Letter 1, fols. 20r-21v: Willem Lodewijk of Nassau to Johann VII, [spring 1620]

Working translation, close to the text; […] = unresolved word; nomenclator signs stay in brackets.

[A] and I have read Your Dilection’s letter with care and attention and weighed it thoroughly, [to see] what we might best advise you in such weighty matters. We hold, first, that no one is fitter or more capable for this service than Your Dilection, since you understand the work of territorial defence (Landrettungswerk) thoroughly, have put it into practice for many years in a praiseworthy and most profitable way, and know better than anyone the state of the country and the temper of the whole levy (Auszug) and of the other levies, on which the preservation of the Lower [Q] will partly depend; without your presence, direction and government little could be expected, and the common good, and the [B] above all, might suffer irreparable harm, which could be laid at Your Dilection’s own door, as if in the hour of need you had despaired of the common good and abandoned the [B] for private considerations — which we should be sorry to see. For [A] and I hope that the [B] has not the least thought of taking the general command from Your Dilection in the absence of the [A̅] and the [A·], after declaring you [∇] in your commission themselves; not only in view of equity and of the great difference in experience of war between Your Dilection and the others, who cannot be supposed to be preferred to you, but above all in view of necessity: the [B]’s own service and profit depend in the highest degree on Your Dilection being maintained in the command (“beim commendo maintenirt”). We also think that [A] and [A°], because of their particular interest, since they cannot be assisted by anyone as well as by Your Dilection, for the reasons given, must not be wronged. Therefore [As] and I hold that Your Dilection’s person and presence cannot be done without, without notable harm to the common good, and that the [B] will face the matter and give Your Dilection, for your own profit, a fair satisfaction; in that case we do not doubt that Your Dilection will weigh everything in good conscience and on your side know how to comply for the common good, since the other burdens are such that you can accommodate yourself to them; [As] and I are subject to the like ourselves, but with discretion and patience have overcome much and brought it to the best. Above all, if Your Dilection, in remonstrating your case, has done what the matter required, you cannot fairly be blamed in your person if the lord’s own service suffered and no hand was offered to you, or if it must be laid to the lord himself, who will not give better order, or to impossibility. Yet if, against all expectation, Your Dilection’s [∇] were not granted outright in the absence of the [A] and the [A°], neither [A] nor I could hold it against you that you keep to your resolution and resignation (“vfkündigung”), the more so as it would be not only most belittling for you but impossible to discharge your service properly, and you alone would remain answerable for what had been badly done, had turned out ill and might be another’s fault. If Your Dilection, because of age, from weakness of memory as well as infirmity of body — as I find in myself — felt too weak for so laborious and difficult a work, or noticed that the [B] or other directors of this work had doubts about it, I would advise you to do as I do myself: to count yourself an emeritus miles and withdraw in time from such danger and reproach, from which one must expect far more than thanks or reward, and leave the toil to those who want the thanks all the same. That the [A] will grant Your Dilection no command beyond having the mere watch ordered is signum imperitiae, and in keeping with the custom and capitulation of the old Germans, by which we make ourselves unfit for the service of all lords; yet they had to dispute the like with the Prince of Parma himself; this can be remedied with discretion, and these times will give occasion to correct many things. Your Dilection’s proposal concerning the command over the relief forces of these parts, [A] and I find impracticable and wholly premature (“immature”); and he who will command over the [A] might already be […] of the [Am] himself; they will gladly acknowledge Your Dilection in your charge and obey you. This, from good brotherly intent, as the advice of [As] and myself, I wished to convey to Your Dilection herewith, as quickly as was possible.
