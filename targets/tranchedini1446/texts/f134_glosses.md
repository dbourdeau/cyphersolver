# f. 134 (DECODE R7905 P1), Nicodemo Tranchedini to Francesco Sforza, Florence, 27 Nov 1446: cipher runs and interlinear glosses

Image: `img/IMG_R7905_I35651_P1.png` (git-ignored; DECODE R7905 image P1). Pencil "1446 27 Nov" and mount number (54).
The letter is clear Italian with six short runs of graphic signs. Over several runs a **contemporary hand, smaller than the
text, wrote the plain words between the lines** (the decipherment of the run). Crops: `crops/f134_run1.png` ... `run6.png`
(git-ignored, made with `crops.py`). Readings of the glosses below are from the image at 3-4x and are mine; `?` marks a
letter or word I could not settle. No sign-by-sign transcription was made, so no value is assigned to an individual sign yet.

| run | where | signs (descriptive, left to right) | gloss above it | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | line 2, after "mandando luy el" | about 11: hooked-bar, b, lollipop-stem, v-with-bar, caret, caret-with-dot, triangle-with-bar and slash, o-bar-o, A (filled triangle), 8, S, two linked circles | "gente d'?lce in Tostana" (read: *gente d'arme(?) in Toscana*) | the gloss ends over the last two signs |

What this fixes:
- The 1446 hand uses the same nomenclator family as Domnina's 1449/54 key and Lasry's 1452 key: **non** is the same sign (glossed).
- Words and phrases (gente d'arme, stantie, Fiorentini) are carried by word signs or runs of syllable signs; the glosses are
  the key's only direct witnesses in 1446.
