and coming to the particular of the determination with which [?you remain], from [?there], to arrange and to conclude terms with the [...] Ottavio over the enterprise of Parma, some of the considerations that occur to us on it will be touched on here, so as to let you understand our intention further below, and [?...].

Having regard to the little reliance that can be placed on the person of the said [?Duke] Ottavio for an enterprise such as that of Parma, both from what is known of it, and his having no means nor money, being in disgrace with the [Pope]; and because, even if he did not lack it, [...] he has where to draw [...] from, there being other [?neighbouring] lands there from which he could get it, unless from those of the Church, of [...]; and that in this [?same way] you [?would be] [?forced], to carry the matter forward, to intervene and declare yourself on our part, which would be nothing other than to [?break] into war against the Church, His Holiness holding Parma, as he now does, in the Church's name and not that of the House of Farnese;

and considering likewise that if this enterprise is made in favour of Ottavio, as it clearly would be by concluding terms with him, it would seem that one wished to uphold and approve his right as better than that of the Empire, [?it being] so that, holding [?this] equally in Parma and in Piacenza, we have never until now chosen to undertake anything by way of open war, it is clear that the right claimed on our part would come to be [?weakened];

besides which, by concluding terms with the said Ottavio, and the [Pope] dying, one would at once come to blows with his [?successor], unable to avoid putting to judgment the dispute over the right claimed by the parties; and if it came in the end to giving compensation to the Church, should the right to the said lands be found to be hers, the Duke would claim on the other side a reward by reason of what had been agreed with him, so that we should find ourselves embarrassed and burdened on both sides.

And because finally, beyond what is considered above on the little reliance to be placed on the said Duke Ottavio, it is to be believed that out of sheer impossibility he [?would] have to come to terms in the end with his [?grandfather], unless the enterprise were taken up openly in our name, which would not be fitting for the reasons always given, we do not see that it is [?fitting] to enter for now into capitulation with him, both for the little [?fruit] [?that comes of it], and for the occasion the [Pope] might take from it to go on more shamelessly in giving Parma to Orazio, as [?is expected], and to put himself [?inside] in the name of the French so as to make [?from] there a [?frontier], as has always been suspected and [?they] have said in [?Rome] several times, besides the [?harm] that the said Duke Ottavio would do, imagining that in the end we could not fail our grandsons, at least after his days.

For all of which, [?after] having examined it well, leaving aside the private settlement between you and Ottavio, which indeed, when it [?can] be made to your satisfaction, we cannot but approve, it has seemed [?to us] that for the rest you must not in any way enter at present into capitulation with the said Ottavio, and so we charge you with all force; but you are to take very great care to learn the [?outcome] these differences [?will have], so as to [?inform us] of everything, and to see whether the said Duke by any way has another, more apparent form than is now seen for entering Parma; and in accordance with that, and with what will be learned anew from [?Rome], which is awaited every day, to see what can and should be done according to the opportunity and the present state of affairs public and our own.

But in case Camillo Orsini should wish to play some trick in the vacancy of the see or in the [Pope]'s lifetime, as you point out in your first letter, and it should be evident to you that Parma is out of the hands of [?the Church] and of her obedience, we leave it to you [?to do] in that case what seems best to you, not doubting that you will take care to undertake nothing that is not, as you say, fully justified, and that has not a very sure and easy way out, because of the harm and loss of reputation that could follow from the contrary.

And likewise, in case, as is suspected, by agreement between the [Pope] and the King of [?France], or in some other way, Orazio should enter Parma, or another person in the name of someone other than [?the Church] or Duke Ottavio, with whom the truce was made, on the pretext that it had not been observed and that the land was in the hands of one who could not fulfil it, we too leave it to you to think and consider by all possible ways and means how this might be prevented, and to try to make yourself master of the said city, with the dexterity and dissimulation we trust you will know how to use, according as you see the moves of the other side; so that, to settle our mind, our intention and aim is that for the present, Parma being in the hands of the [Pope] and through him or the said Duke, nothing be [?altered] nor undertaken against it except in the two cases said above.
