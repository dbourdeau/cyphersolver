# Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga, 30 Sep and 20 Nov 1549: transcription jobs

Both letters carry a contemporary decipherment in the margin beside every cipher passage. They are not breaks. The text below is the marginal
decipherment, in my reading, with abbreviations expanded (`clear_margins_charles.txt`). The cipher is in `transcription_ch1549_0930_1120_blind.txt`, one blind
pass, and its decoding in `decryption_ch1549_0930_1120_blind.txt`. That decoding is unrepaired and has errors. It is not yet a line-by-line edition.

## 30 Sep 1549, Brussels, cc. 50-1r to 50-3r (leaves numbered 50-01, 50-02, 51)

Spanish, from the margins:

1. y aunque yua con fin de tractar al Prior de Lombardia, castellano que es del castillo, de tal manera que fuesse tanto como echarle de ally por meter dentro a Mos. de Brissiu
2. Veays que lo del tractado de aquella ciudad
3. Una carta que el Conde de Tenda screuia al Condestable, la qual se hallo en poder de vn Frances que Ingleses mataron en vna escaramuza junto a Bolonia, que ellos mesmos nos la han traydo, y
4. que se tenia sperança del dicho tractado de Niça
5. y buena guarda de aquel castillo y lo demas lo que vieredes ser menester y conuenir para obuiar a los inconuenientes
6. se ha entendido que las personas de las que les se tenia sospecha que interuiniessen en el dicho tractado
7. ciertos auisos que han venido de Francia cerca del frayle que teneys preso, porque scriue nuestro Embaxador que seruiran mucho para effecto de su deposicion

English:

1. and although it was aimed at dealing with the Prior of Lombardy, who is castellan of the castle, in such a way that it would amount to throwing him out of there, in order to put Mons. de Brissac inside
2. See that the matter of the plot on that city
3. A letter that the Count of Tende was writing to the Constable, which was found on a Frenchman whom the English killed in a skirmish near Boulogne, and which they themselves have brought to us, and
4. that hope was held of the said plot on Nice
5. and good guard of that castle, and for the rest what you see to be necessary and fitting to prevent the troubles
6. it has been learned that the persons who were suspected of taking part in the said plot
7. certain advices that have come from France about the friar you hold prisoner, since our Ambassador writes that they will be of great use for his deposition

The cipher differs from the margin at passage 2. The signs at K-06 and K-07 give `lo del tractado de aquel [ca]stillo`. The margin says "aquella ciudad". The first
sign of K-07 was checked on a zoomed crop. The signs were read by a reader with no table and no clear text, so this difference is not steered.

Contents: a French plot on Nice, the Duke of Savoy's stronghold, aimed at getting the castellan out and Brissac in. An intercepted letter from the Count of Tende to
Montmorency came to Charles by way of the English at Boulogne. An Italian copy headed "Transcritto de la lettera del Conte di Tenda al Conestabile" follows on cc. 51-52.
Its date looks like August 1549 at Marseille; I saw it at contact-sheet scale only.

Context: Boulogne was held by the English and besieged by France in 1549. Nice mattered because Charles needed Savoy's coast between Genoa and Provence. Ferrante had
to watch it from Milan.

What the clear text hides: the clear text speaks of the Duke of Savoy's wish to go to Nice and of the need for vigilance. The method of the plot, the name Brissac, the
intercepted letter and its route, and the evidence against the imprisoned friar are in cipher only.

## 20 Nov 1549, Brussels, cc. 64r-65r

Spanish, from the margins:

1. a Parma en nombre de la yglesia
2. mueua ninguna cosa en Parma de nuestra parte ni en otro lugar
3. que no queremos mouer guerra a la yglesia
4. offender y occupar en esta occasion el estado de la casa Farnesa por la vezindad que tiene con el del Duque de Florencia y
5. tentar en tal tiempo
6. pareciere otra cosa y dieren occasion se podra facilmente hazer

English:

1. to Parma in the name of the Church
2. let nothing be moved in Parma on our part, nor in any other place
3. that we do not wish to make war on the Church
4. to attack and occupy on this occasion the state of the House of Farnese, because of its neighbourhood with that of the Duke of Florence, and
5. to try at such a time
6. if something else should seem fit and they should give occasion, it can easily be done

Contents and context: this is the reply to the news of the death of Paul III (10 Nov 1549). The clear text deals with the conclave, the election of a pope, and Don Diego
de Mendoza at Rome. The cipher repeats the order of 14 Nov in a new situation: no move on Parma or elsewhere, no war on the Church, while conceding that it could easily be done
later if occasion arose. Passage 4 follows a clear phrase on the opportunity the Duke of Florence might have. I saw that phrase at contact-sheet scale only, so who proposed the attack on the
Farnese state is not shown here.

What the clear text hides: the clear text is about the sede vacante and a free election. The refusal to move, and the option kept open for later, are in cipher only.
