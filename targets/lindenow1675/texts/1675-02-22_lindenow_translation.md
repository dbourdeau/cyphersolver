> It turns out that the draft treaty which Count Niels Brahe has presented is

wholly to the advantage [?of] Sweden, without there being the least substance in it for His Majesty,

> providing in substance that the two Kings shall keep a perfect understanding together in the present conjunctures, work in concert for the advancement of peace and the preservation of the Protestant cause, and join their counsels and opinions for the upholding of the Peace of Westphalia; and in case one of them should find himself obliged, in carrying out so salutary a design, to undertake something, and on that account were attacked by any other whoever it might be, the other would be bound to assist him with all his forces and to run the same fortune as the one attacked, or, to use his own words, [...]. That moreover all the earlier treaties, and in particular that of Roskilde and that of 1660, shall be confirmed anew, and by name also all that was stipulated in them in favour of the Duke of Gottorp, so that what had not yet been carried out in his regard would be so at the earliest, as if Sweden wished to charge His Majesty with having failed the said treaties on this point, which nevertheless can never be shown. The one who was here on behalf of the said Duke went back when it was least expected, without having answered anything solid to the openings the Grand Chancellor made to him, namely that if His Highness believed His Majesty could do him some service in the difference he still had with the house of Plön over the counties of Oldenburg and Delmenhorst, His Majesty would gladly apply himself to it, but that he did not see [?in] what it would be good to use the mediation of Sweden, since he did not recall that there was any longer any dispute between His Majesty and the Duke on this subject. The Swedish Ambassador Extraordinary himself did not approve this sudden departure. Mr Block, councillor of the Duke of Hanover, has arrived here but has not yet had audience. I am. P.S. It is said that Sweden goes on assembling many troops on the side of Norway. Mr de Klingenberg has arrived in Holland, and it is left to your judgement to keep up with him such correspondence as you think fit for His Majesty's service.
