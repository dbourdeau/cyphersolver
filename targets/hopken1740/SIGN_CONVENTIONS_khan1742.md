# Sign conventions (catalogue 2244, Höpken-Carlson volume, Constantinople, 1742; volume I/9/28, R0003637)

Set before any transcription and applied to every transcription in this folder. Shapes only; no values. They follow the
conventions of `targets/hopken1740/SIGN_CONVENTIONS.md` so that the files can be compared.

- The cipher is written in Arabic digits in four-digit groups separated by spaces (sometimes run close together; a group
  is always four digits). Transcribed as written, one row of cipher per line, labelled A01.. (image R0003637_00227) and
  B01.. (image R0003637_00229).
- On these pages a contemporary decipherer struck each group through and wrote the French plaintext above it. The strike
  is not recorded. The interlinear French is not transcribed into the cipher rows; it was read afterwards into
  `gloss_khan1742.tsv`, after the digits were settled.
- A short stroke written above or just after a group (an acute- or grave-like tick, or a raised comma) is recorded as
  `'` after the group (e.g. `1006'`). Its meaning was not assumed while transcribing.
- Points, commas, colons and semicolons written between groups are recorded as `.`, `,`, `:`, `;` where they stand. A
  short dash at the end of a row is recorded as nothing (line-end hyphen) and noted in the file header.
- Plain (unenciphered) words written in the cipher rows by the encipherer are recorded as `[clear: ...]`.
- `?` after a group: one digit could not be settled on the image (the likelier reading is given; the alternative is
  named in the file header).
- Digits that look alike in this hand and were checked on 2x crops: 3/8 (closed upper loop = 8), 5/6, 2/7 (open lower
  stroke), 7/1, 0/6 when blotted, 4/9.
- Added after pass 3 on images 227 and 229, before image 228 was transcribed: in this hand **8 is often written with an
  open, z-like top and a looped foot**, so that it looks like a 2 with a loop at the base; a plain 2 has a flat or open
  foot. The looped form is transcribed as 8. Labels C01.. (image R0003637_00228, left page) and D01.. (right page).
