## The letter

BnF Espagnol 318 no. 5, ff. 5–6v (canvases 32–35 of ark `btv1b52503046q`). Federico III of Naples to Ferdinand and
Isabella, San Germano, 11 Jan 1497; Italian, partly in cipher. Its own first clear line names the ambassador: *Da poi
de havere resposto ad micer Joan Scrivà, oratore de le Maiestate Vostre*.

## The existing decipherment

Ivan Parisi, *Un cifrario in prestito per una lettera segretissima di Federico d'Aragona re di Napoli ai Re Cattolici
nel BnF, Espagnol 318*, "Studi di storia medioevale e di diplomatica", n.s. IV (2020), pp. 157–177; open access,
DOI 10.54103/2611-318X/14378. The point of the article is the anomaly: the king of Naples had his secret letter
enciphered **in the Spanish ambassador's own cipher**, by the ambassador's hand. Parisi pins the cifrista by a Spanish
speaker's mistake — the failure to use the double-*s* cipher in *possano*, *obedientissimo*, *promesso*. Contents:
Federico confirms the pre-agreement for the marriage of his nine-year-old son Ferrando, duke of Calabria, to one of
the Spanish infantas.

The key is Escrivà's cipher no. 1, printed by Parisi in 2004 (*Pedralbes* 24, 55–116, appendix II). The nomenclator
elements Parisi lists are legible on the leaf at 3000 px and match what one reads there directly: `qix` del, `qao`
duque, `qav` de, `qae` con, `qed` cosa, `qie` como, `rae` el, `rof` en, `ru` mensajero, `saf` Italia, `sul` la, `tad`
no, `tay` parte, `vee` que, `vet` si, `vog` sobre, `xer`/`xoq` Vuestras Altezas, `xit` una, `pam` bien. Note the band
of initials: p, q, r, s, t, v, x. That band is what excludes this key for nos. 93 and 94 (see `esp318no93`).

## What was done here

Only the search (the shelfmark rather than the cipher found Parisi's article) and the check of his nomenclator list on
the leaf. No transcription and no decipherment were made here; the key was not applied.

## Remaining gaps

None in the text: Parisi prints the whole letter. Not re-checked sign by sign here.
