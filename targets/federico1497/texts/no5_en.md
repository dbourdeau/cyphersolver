# Federico III of Naples to the Catholic Monarchs, San Germano, 11 January 1497: English translation

BnF Espagnol 318 no. 5 (ff. 5r-6v). Translates the text printed by Ivan Parisi, "Un cifrario in prestito per una
lettera segretissima di Federico d'Aragona re di Napoli ai Re Cattolici nel BnF, Espagnol 318", *Studi di storia
medioevale e di diplomatica* n.s. IV (2020), Appendice, pp. 173-174 (open access, DOI 10.54103/2611-318X/14378). It
is Parisi's decipherment and edition, not one made here; the Italian text is not copied into this folder.
Translation: Claude (session of 14 Nov 2026).

What is clear and what was cipher: Parisi prints the deciphered part in italics. On the leaf (f. 5r, scan in
`targets/esp318/full/f005r.jpg`) the clear text runs from the opening to "me havea declarato" and resumes at "Et
perché longamente ho parlato"; everything between is cipher. The cipher part is between asterisks below.

Parisi's square brackets mark what he supplied where the leaf is damaged (a stain runs down the middle of the
page) or where the cifrista left out a letter; they are kept here only where a whole word depends on them. His
notes record these emendations of the cipher, all "probable errors of the cifrista": *summa* for *sumia*, *nello*
for *nelli*, *similmente* for *similcente*, *fe* for *fi*, *seguire* for *sequire*, *qual* for *quel*, a second *si*
where the cipher has the group *vog* (sobre), *confirmassero* where the signs give *confirmarese* with one sign
unreadable under the stain, and the group *xie* read as *vostre* (in Escrivà's other letters he had read it
*socorro*). In the Italian edition the nomenclator words of the Castilian cipher are given in Italian (*che* for
*que*, *Maiestate Vostre* for *Vuestras Altezas*, and so on).

[Your] Majesties,

After having replied to micer Joan Scrivà, ambassador of Your Majesties, concerning what he had declared to me on
their behalf *about the marriage of one of your infantas, daughter of Your Majesties, and of the duke, my son, I was
told by micer Gonçalvo Ferandiz the very same thing that this envoy had said to me, at which I took as much
pleasure as I could have taken at anything I might have heard, since it seems to me that Your Majesties are
confirmed every hour more in wishing to embrace me and to hold me and my affairs as their own, as they deservedly
may, since I have once and for all given myself to Your Majesties as a most obedient son. And as they are heads,
protectors and lords of this kingdom, of me and of my sons, and although, holding to this firm purpose, I had
resolved to make no marriage for my son unless I had first learned their will about it, now, understanding
concerning the aforesaid matters what they have reported to me on behalf of Your Majesties, I am left with the
greatest consolation, and I have replied and promised them on my faith that I will make no other marriage for the
said duke, my son. And for greater assurance I have given my word on it, and likewise by this present letter I give
it to Your Majesties. And let them be certain that I never did anything with a gladder and better heart, both to
obey promptly in what they command me, [and] because I [hope] that from this will follow the good and repose of
all Italy and, I do not doubt, no small service to Your Majesties, which I desire no less than my own good.* And
because I have spoken at length with the said Consalvo Ferrandis and the ambassador, I shall not trouble to extend
myself further in this letter, but refer myself to what they will write to Your Majesties, praying our Lord God for
their prosperous state and long life. I commend myself continually to Your Majesties. From San Germano, 11 January
1497.

King Federico.

Vitus Pisanellus
