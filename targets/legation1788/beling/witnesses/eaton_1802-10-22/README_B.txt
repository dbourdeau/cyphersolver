WARNING: THE MANUSCRIPT CARRIES ITS OWN DECIPHERMENT
  On frame 0320 a second, smaller hand (evidently the recipient's office) has written plain
  English words in the interlinear space above most of the code groups. That is not my
  decoding, it is ink on the page. Because this is a held-out test I have kept those words
  OUT of transcription_B.tsv and out of this README, and put my best-effort reading of them
  in interlinear_B_SPOILER.tsv. Do not open that file if the plaintext must stay unseen.
  I did not use the interlinear words to choose any digit. (One observation in that layer
  bears on the doubtful first group of line 19; it is stated at the end of this README in
  a way that reveals no words.)
  The interlinear writing is also the main cause of the doubtful digits: lines are only
  about 75 px apart at full resolution and the added words and their blots run into the
  tops and feet of the numerals. The scan is near-bitonal, so blots are solid black and
  gamma changes recover nothing beneath them.

  0327  blank verso with docket "William Eaton 22d Oct. 1802". No code.
  So the letter of 22 October 1802 occupies frames 0320-0327 (text on 0320-0326, docket on
  0327); it begins on 0320, not on an earlier frame. Page continuity was checked by
  catchwords (ships / crew ... / So long as we | confide / They | have / propor-tion /
  from / re-claims). The only numerals elsewhere on 0317-0327 are dates and sums of money
  in clear text (e.g. 150,000 dollars, 8,000, 75,000); none is code.
  No other code-bearing letter or passage sits on frames 0317-0327.
  Folio numbering jumps from 484 (0318) to 489 (0320); folios 485-488 are not on these
  frames. I did not fetch anything outside 0317-0327.

THE CODED PASSAGE (frame 0320, single page)
  It is one unbroken stretch. It starts in mid-line on page line 11 straight after the clear
  words "not to receive a negative." and runs to the end of page line 19, followed by a new
  indented clear paragraph "The Dane has again this summer, made peace with Tripoli".
  Groups per manuscript line:
      line 11:  4      line 14: 11      line 17: 12
      line 12: 10      line 15: 11      line 18: 11
      line 13: 11      line 16: 11      line 19: 11
  Total 92 groups on the page (and in the letter).
  Lines 12, 13, 15, 16 and 17 end with a dash, i.e. the dashed word runs on to the next line.
  No interlinear numerals (ins:) and no superscript numerals (sup:) were found.

METHOD
  Frame 0320 is 2352 x 3584 px; the code block lies at about x 440-2080, y 1380-2080.
  First pass: each code line cut in overlapping tiles and enlarged 6x (sips, then a small
  CoreImage crop tool for Lanczos enlargement), doubtful groups re-cut at 8x-10x.
  Second pass: every line re-read from fresh 3x strips (p2/) against the TSV row; the
  readings stood, with the period/dash and underline notes refined.

NON-SPOILING NOTE ON LINE 19, FIRST GROUP
  The group at the end of line 11 is a clear 614. The first group of line 19 has the same
  outline apart from its obscured first digit. The interlinear hand wrote a DIFFERENT word
  over each of the two. That favours the alternative 814 for line 19 more than the shape
  alone does; I have nevertheless left 614? as the reading from the pen strokes and give
  814 as the alternative. The same reasoning cannot be applied to 611?/811.
