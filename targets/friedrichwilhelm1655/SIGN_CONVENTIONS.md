# Sign conventions (2539, Culemborg inv. 1257, scans 715-819)

Shapes only, no values. Filed after pass 1; the conventions were used from the start (see NOTES.md).

- The cipher signs are Arabic numerals, one or two digits, in the same hand and ink as the clear text.
- A dot (sometimes a comma) follows each number; numbers are transcribed dot-separated, as written.
- A wider space between runs is a word gap and is kept as a space. Gaps inside long runs are not always visible.
- Clear German words stand between the runs; they are transcribed in double quotes. Some runs carry one or two clear
  letters (e.g. a number group followed by "in" or "ier"); these are quoted inside the run.
- Two dots (a diaeresis) over a number are written N: . A bar over a number is written N^ .
- 3 written twice (3.3) and the single number 33 are kept apart as written; they are hard to tell apart where the
  dot between them is faint.
- A line that runs into the binding of a left-hand page is cut with | ; the facing scan shows the same edge, no more.
- Blots, strokes and words written in the margin or between the lines in another hand are notes in square brackets,
  not text. Glosses of the time are kept in separate files.
- 1 and 7, 2 and 7, 10 and 16, 14 and 19 are the shape pairs most easily confused in this hand.
