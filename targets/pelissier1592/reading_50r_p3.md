## Summary

This is the last page of Pelissier's letter from Burgos, 13 September 1592. The cipher opening warns that "they"
(the Spanish ministers) are slowly undermining someone's authority, very probably Mayenne's. They first took away
control of finances and military command, then used intrigue to erode his credit, aiming to bring him as low as
they can ("Dieu veulle que je me trompe").

In clear text Pelissier says that someone "has a flea in his ear" and keeps his thoughts to himself. He learned this
from that man's Jacobin (Dominican) confessor. The confessor's name reads *Cornent[e]*, which is doubtful. This
confessor is following the court on his own business.

The States will not answer to what code group 310 claims. The ambassador of 310 has been sent back without answer to
his demands. The cipher then names the governor of Milan and a "don ...", ambassador at a court. The context points
to 310 being the Duke of Savoy.

Pelissier then pleads for union. Treatment is equal everywhere, so no one should be jealous. People "ought to
recognise each other better", and the wisest should make the first move. Everyone should give up some private
interest and "unite in so close a union that it is known everywhere that an offence to one is felt equally by the
others". That would be the true means "to change the resolutions taken over here" and make them act with more
respect than they now show in keeping their promises at Madrid.

He is staying at court for a final answer on the 50,000 écus the two Idiáquez keep promising. He had set out to take
leave of the King and go home. Since he has been given means to stay, he will wait out the month. If he is not paid
in that time he will take his final decision, seeing that the words he was given and his complaints have come to
nothing.

The postscript reports that "le Baston" has been dispatched to Palencia, well pleased. The King's welcome went as far
as sending him horses "from the lodging of don Fran[cisco]" when he went to audience. This makes Pelissier suspect
the man handled "other business than his own".

## Code group 310

C14: `X 310 w II q oo y T J f n D X Q 6` reads "[null] 310 et l'ambassadeur", following the clear text "…ne
correspondront pas a ce que pretend". So 310 is a party with "pretensions" in France, with an ambassador at Philip's
court who has just been sent back without answers. The next cipher passage (C16) mentions the governor of Milan. Of
the League-era claimants, the Duke of Savoy best fits "pretend", an ambassador at Madrid and the Milan link. Savoy
claimed Provence and a share of the crown, and the governor of Milan was his Spanish neighbour and paymaster. The
Duke of Lorraine is the alternative. 310 is not in cands.txt (the decoder shows [C310]), and no other occurrence has
been found in nos. 22/45/46, so this identification is from context only.

## The confessor's name

C10 ends `… z 4 HH oo g | s6 Lo r 4 E` and C11 is `4 z4 L`, read as "nommé | c o r n e | n t e/u". The flourished
s6 is c; the bold ⊥ is o; r is r; 4 is n (or m); E is p/e (only e makes a name). In C11 the first 4 is heavily
over-inked and may be a correction. The second 4 has the hooked lead-in (z4 = t), and the last sign is a looped ℓ
(u or e). The candidate forms are **Cornente / Cornentu**, or **Cornete** if the over-inked 4 is a cancelled sign.
None is a name I can confirm. The name should be checked against the Dominicans at Philip II's court in 1592 before
it is used. Crops: crops/p3_50r_name1.jpg, crops/p3_50r_name2.jpg.
