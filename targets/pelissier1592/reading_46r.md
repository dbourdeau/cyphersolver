## Summary

Pierre Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, writes to President Jeannin from Burgos on 13 September
1592. He reached Valladolid just as the King was leaving, and Don Juan de Idiáquez put business off until Palencia.
There, in cipher, Pelissier again pressed his own case. He had too little money to stay in Spain without the King's
help, and his credit was gone because he had not repaid money borrowed in Madrid. The King's long silence showed that
his request was not being welcomed and that his many appeals were resented. So he had come to take leave of the King
and of Idiáquez and go back to France. He asked Idiáquez to obtain this for him, because every day's delay made the
journey harder and the length of his stay was using up all his means. Philip's answer was that he had his own
ministers in France, so Pelissier could go or stay as he chose. Pelissier replied that he had come on Mayenne's orders
and would like to leave on the same kind of order. The King should at least bear witness that he had given no offence
beyond his repeated requests, since they had produced so little that everything he had proposed stood where it had on
the first day. The answer was "yo lo creo". Pelissier asked for a private hour to present his information and learn
when he could put it before the King. At his lodging Idiáquez asked for news from France. Pelissier described the
League's weakness and the enemy's control of the countryside, called the peace rumours false, and said Mayenne was
heading for Champagne and the Estates-General. The King now wanted Pelissier to wait a while longer for news. For
that he had granted him 500 ducats, about 450 écus "or more if he wished", payable in Madrid, where Pelissier could
withdraw with the ambassadors and other agents of foreign princes. Idiáquez would write to Francisco de Idiáquez to
have the sum paid. Pelissier thanked him and asked to discuss the state of France and the troubles that would follow
if nothing was done. Even if the King had good information from his ministers, many things never reached them.
Idiáquez answered that this was hard to do while the court was on the move to Burgos. With Don Martín de Idiáquez,
Pelissier began on the Estates-General and the weakness of the Catholic party.

  cross (null), so the opening reads "e i peu", not "e i a peu".
- C4: "sommes" over `dz F oo 4_ oo 4_ g tt`. Agrees.
- C7: "con(ge)" over `z 4 8 12`. Agrees.
- C12 (new): the gloss "…ilz…que…" over `D L o 80 Z8`. It fits "sur quoy": the barred 8 = q.
- C31: "et des incon" over `oo xx 6 t 80 ph 4 s6 o s`. It now decodes exactly once 56 is read as the flourished
  s6 (c) and "dz" as o + s.
- C16/C17, C26/C27, C33/C34: further faint glosses. Where legible ("…que…", "…a leur…") they agree.

## Notes on the key (pass 3)

1. **∞ at the C30 row end and the C31 start = et**, confirmed by the C31 gloss.
2. **The serifed plain I (tokenised I) is p/l throughout**: par (C2), peu (C6), repliquay (C12), pouuois (C18), puis (C36).
3. **7 = b** in foiblesse, nonobstant, bien and bons aduis, but **h** in "catholic" (C37). So context decides, as on f.47v.
4. **The barred 8 (Z8) = q**: quoy (C12), quelque (C25), quatre (C26), fruit qu- (C17), qu'il and que les (C34).
   The pass-2 transcription had these as I, F or z.
5. **Dagger on a delta (#o)** is a null, except in "estoit" in C32, where it has to be o.
