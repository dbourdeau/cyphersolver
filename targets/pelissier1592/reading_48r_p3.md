## Summary

Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, sends Jeannin his analysis of the League's position. He argues that new
counsels are needed, a "third means", because the harm has sunk too deep and past efforts have only lowered the League's (or
Spain's) reputation abroad: the great means spent have borne no fruit and those they were meant to help are nearer ruin than
on the first day. He does not think now is the time to assemble the Estates General to name a Catholic king: everyone has
lost heart since the last army came to nothing and left things as weak as before, and the people, the clergy and the
nobility are worn out. They would sooner accept an accord that sets up a king against the one "who claims to be" king
(Henri IV) and to be recognised as such, inside his own party and beyond. The conflict can end only with the extermination
of one of the two royal lines, so bloody is the hatred, which spares neither age nor sex, or else in the ruin of both sides.
He therefore backs a plan already put to Philip II: keep two armies in the field. One would face the enemy, push him back
from the Seine to the Loire and carry the war onto his lands and against the help that comes to him from Germany and
England. The other would take the Seine towns, free Paris and Rouen, and then reduce the provinces one after another. He
answers the cost objection (nearly 300,000 écus a month): the effort would last only a few months, the enemy would be
outmatched and forced back onto the defensive, and after a success the armies could be cut back. Whoever wants to end the
war must seek battle; but the King should be warned how dangerous it is to risk a battle with an army made up of foreign
nations raised in the wars of the Low Countries, on whose safety the preservation of [?] depends. The page ends by asking
that His Majesty have a force made up to oppose [the enemy] (the sentence runs onto 48v).
