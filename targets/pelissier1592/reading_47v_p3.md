## Summary (English)

Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, is reporting to Jeannin on his audience with the King and his exchanges
with the King's principal minister at Burgos. The minister said that history would show no other king who had given such
powerful and lasting help with such sincere affection, which was enough to shut the mouths of those who claimed Philip
acted for his own interests. If the help had not brought what was hoped for, the fault lay with those it was sent to,
some of whom had acted too slackly on their own part. The Spanish side rejected Pelissier's arguments for putting off the
assembly of the Estates and his other remedies: these would only raise suspicion that it was a trick to hinder them.
Pelissier praised Philip's zeal for the Catholic cause, but warned that if his words reached anyone other than the
minister it would seem very strange to the Spanish nation. He feared the French of the League would take it very badly
that the Spanish were delaying the one thing that would bring the Catholics peace: the election of a legitimate king to
oppose the heretic and tyrant (Henri IV). Pass 3 recovers that the minister cited the time of the Cardinal de Bourbon,
the League's elected king ("esleu", Charles X), when Philip had asked that everything else be put aside and the forces
committed "pour sa deliurance" (the Cardinal was Henri IV's prisoner). The minister told Pelissier to be careful who
learned of this and would not say how the King would take it. The King's affection was restated, with the condition that
the Estates would not be assembled so soon unless there were enough forces to back them, withdraw them to safety and keep
them free. Philip would always provide what the cause needed. Pelissier left it to the minister to inform the King, and
felt he had discharged himself by clearing things up with him. The minister had an interest in whatever touched his
reputation, and the Spanish ministers "have delicate ears when one tells them" things: even good advice is suspect to
them.
