## Summary

This page narrates the 1591-92 war in France (cipher) and then argues a point (clear text). Henri IV ([315]), encouraged
while [someone] lay ill at Reims, gathers his forces and besieges Chartres, which he takes along with other towns; this
makes him master of the Beauce(?), a fertile country whose people now live "arms in hand". He does the same at Noyon, in
sight of [the League army], whose relief was frustrated by the Prince of Ascoli. Some say it would be better to "give" the
kingdom to the heretic than let him conquer it piece by piece. Forces from Germany and England join Henri long before the
League army appears; unguarded towns want to stay "free" and treat with the enemy. A commander (Mayenne) gathers 15,000
foot and 3,000-4,000 horse and wants to act in Picardy(?) while waiting for "Son Altesse" (Parma), but is refused; another
lives so dissolutely that everyone cries out. Then the 1592 campaign: Caudebec is taken; Henri turns and camps next to the
Catholic army, offering battle daily; lack of supplies forces that army back across the Seine to near Château-Thierry;
Henri, deserted by most of his Germans and Swiss at Châlons-en-Champagne, still holds the field. After two powerful armies
came to nothing there is no hope of setting things right by war. The clear text blames Parma for passing up chances to
fight, which lets enemies spread rumours that Philip II defends the cause not for religion but to keep the French busy so
they cannot help his rebels in the Low Countries. The page ends on "the good and repose of [Christendom?]" and a warning
against fomenting one of the parties.
