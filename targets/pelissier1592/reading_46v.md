## Summary

Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, reports what passed with the Spanish ministers once the court had
reached Burgos. The page opens in the middle of a conversation (probably with Don Martin de Idiaquez, named at the foot
of f. 46r). Idiaquez urged that the Leaguers be pressed hard, spoke of the taking of Pont-Audemer (reading uncertain)
and of "our forces superior to the enemy's", and said that holding back the Estates was a great mistake while great
levies were being raised in Germany. Pelissier answered that he had understood the deputies were to assemble the
previous month. He was told the Estates had been put off until the King arrived at Burgos. There Pelissier saw Don
Juan (de Idiaquez), who gave him a private hour. Pelissier argued that nothing matters more to a state than its
reputation, which frightens enemies and keeps subjects in their duty. He recalled that the Catholics "from then on
recognised that their safety depended chiefly on the aid and forces of His Majesty". The Duke of Parma's army had been
so flourishing that the enemy expected it to continue. Most of the royalist Catholic nobility went home, and several
towns began to treat. Had the army wintered in France, code-group [315] (the Béarnais, Henri IV) would have been
ruined, and the Germans would never have dared to come to his aid. Its withdrawal to the Low Countries ended all these
dealings and let the Catholics lose heart. Many League towns, fearing exposure if the enemy returned, began their own
negotiations. The page ends on the losses in Picardy: something near Corbie and Amiens (new in this pass, doubtful),
and what would have happened at La Fère and Soissons had they not been provided for.
