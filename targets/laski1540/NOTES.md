# Hieronymus Łaski to Ferdinand I, Constantinople, Nov 1540 – May 1541 (catalogue no. 497)

**Outcome: read from existing decipherment.** The letters were deciphered at the time (interlinear, on the
originals) and printed in full by Anton von Gévay in 1842. Nothing about the text is new here. The finding is
Michael Brackx's (issue #172, 28 Oct 2026); this folder records it as a target. Session of 29 Oct 2026.

## The documents

Ciphered reports of Łaski's last embassy to Süleyman, sent while he was held in Constantinople. Each is in a sign
cipher except the address ("Aus dem, die Adresse ausgenommen, durchaus in Chiffern geschriebenen Originale", Gévay).

## System and key

A sign cipher. Not rebuilt here. Łaski's 1531 key to the Bavarian dukes (`kaa4591`) uses a different alphabet.

## What is open, and why

- No sign-by-sign transcription of the cipher and no key table. Both could be made from the AIS images, using the
  interlinear gloss and Gévay's text as plaintext (method: key from adjacent plaintext). I did not examine the images.
- The read share (1.0) is estimated from Gévay printing every letter, not counted from a reading file.
- I have not checked Gévay's print against the interlinear glosses.
