## Doubts, line by line
- A1.02: all four p's (#3, #12, #18, #20) have their descenders crossed by the strike-through of the struck interlinear words below, so p or px can't be told apart (marked px?). #21 is the same case for a q (qx?). #29: a looped stroke plus an x after g, read as the short form of bx (bx?).
- A1.02: a "+" in the left margin, above the start of the line and in front of the interlinear words, is left out. It matches the "+" markers the interlinear hand puts at the start of its lines on B1.
- A2.04: #4 d whose ascender meets a bar that may be the interlinear's (dt?). #12 is a heavily inked, double-stroked long s with a crossbar, possibly overwritten (f?).
- A2.07: #7 and #19 are q's whose descender is crossed by an interlinear bar (the "ff" of "fantiff" and the t of "fanti"): qx?. The line ends with "n" and then a large ink blot or cancelling stroke that covers at least one sign (only an ascender shows), transcribed "?". There may be more signs under the blot.
- A2.08: #9 is a d whose ascender runs up into the crossbar of the interlinear "ff" (in "esspanyoli"). I transcribed plain d, though it could be dt. #12: q crossed by the interlinear line (qx?).
- A2.09: #5 is a hooked stroke with an x in front of A, partly tangled with an interlinear descender (bx?). #10 is a plain d (the bar above it is the underline under the interlinear "xxxx"). #14 and #15 (q, p) and #27 (p) have descenders crossed by interlinear strokes (qx?, px?). The last sign is dot / long stroke / dot, but the long stroke may be the ascender of the interlinear "deliberà" (dsd?).
- A2.10: a tiny speck between rc and p (after #12) is taken as a paper mark, not a dot sign.
- A2.05, A2.06, A2.10: the three Nx could be two signs, N + plus, which would add 3 to letter A's count.
- B1.01: #3 is a closed small bowl with a stem running up into the interlinear "presa". It may be a d, or an o with an interlinear descender through it (d?).
- B1.02: #15 q and #16 p are crossed by the strike-through of the interlinear word "to" (qx?, px?). #2 is a sure plain q.
- B1.03: #15 p and #16 q are crossed by the struck interlinear "tanto" (px?, qx?).
- B1.04: #5, #16 and #21 are q's crossed by interlinear strokes (qx?). #6 is read as pc: the bar on the ascender is centred on it, although interlinear "ff" bars are close by.
- B1.05: #1 lies partly under the paper repair at the left (read as bx, hooked form). #10 and #12 are separate single dots at mid-height (dot).
- B2.01: #10 is a lambda with a long horizontal foot to the right (lamf). I kept it apart from lam.
- B2.03, B3.03: the bar over pc (#2) and over dt (#5) touches the interlinear "agio dat°". In B2.03 I'm fairly sure both bars are the cipher's own.
- B3.01: #16 has an ink blot over its bowl. An ascender and an x-stroke show (bx?), and a crossed vertical below it could make it a crossed q or p instead. Two small specks (an apostrophe after rc #6, a dash before 7 #13) are left out. The final dsd has an extra dot at the foot of the stroke.
- B3.02: #3 is a heavily inked, overwritten long-s-like sign with a crossbar (f?, like A2.04 #12). #6, #8 and #10 are q's crossed by interlinear strokes (qx?). Both #17 and B3.03 #24 are read as I (foot on both sides), not L.
- B3.03: the line starts under the interlinear "a la M.ta". #1 "+" could be an interlinear marker (plus?). #2 and #4 are small reversed-c signs at cipher height; the superscript a of the interlinear "Ces.a" sits just below #4, so I treat #4 (and likewise #2) as cipher. #7 is an open pointed form, between a and lambda (a?). #8 is mostly hidden by the interlinear "agiunto" (g?). #11 is a large looped sign with a bar across its ascender and a tail to the lower left (dt?; it may be its own type). #15 q and #20 p are crossed by interlinear t-bars (qx?, px?).
- B3.04: #6 is struck through with several hatching strokes (read f?, like the other overwritten long-s signs). #12 is a clear f. #13 is a small sign filled with ink, illegible (?). A tiny dot after it, on the baseline, is taken as a speck.

## General observations (shape only)
- "7 pc" occurs six times (A1.01, B2.03, B2.04 twice, B3.01, B3.05), and in B2.04 and B3.05 it ends the cipher run.
- Telling c from rc at low zoom was the main risk; every line was checked at 2.5x-5x.
- Interlinear words (second hand) and their strike-throughs often cross the descenders of q and p, which is where most doubts come from. A line-by-line image check of the "?" cases against a sharper scan would settle most of them.
- Fold and damage: letter B's opening has the central fold and dark gaps between B2 and B3, but no cipher line crosses them. A3 has a horizontal tear with lost text (see above). A2.07 has the large ink blot at its end. B1.05's left end is under a paper repair strip.
