"… since I wrote to you; and just now I have been told by a person who has in the past told me things that proved true, that the pope has
been informed from the court of ‹T…› that this duke had received letters from the King of [Spain] asking him resolutely to make war on the
King and openly declare himself protector and defender of Marshal de Montmorency: a thing that seemed to me very hard to believe, above all
at this time, and I was much astonished at it. Nevertheless, thinking it over, I considered that perhaps this marshal had written about what
Marshal de Joyeuse was doing against him in defence of the places that the said Montmorency was seeking to put into his own hands, chiefly
Clermont-de-Lodève, and that he feared that more would be done against him, and that this was the answer he had received to what he had
written to [Spain]; but also that with the arrival of Monsieur de Poigny, who brought the King's intention, the marshal's suspicions might
have vanished and the rest been settled, just as the levy of four thousand foot that was being raised in the State of Milan these last months
has also vanished, which by common opinion was to help the said marshal when he needed it. I thought I should write these things to you,
and [that] ‹…›"

Context (not checked in sources): Henri de Montmorency-Damville, marshal and governor of Languedoc, in conflict with the Joyeuse family in
1584-85; Jean d'Angennes, sieur de Poigny, was one of Henri III's envoys of those years. The page is undated; 1584-85 by the file.
