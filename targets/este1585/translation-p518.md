# este1585 - English translation of p. 518 (working translation of reading-p518.md)

Read in part as a page (94.4%); with p. 517, the letter is read (95.9%). ‹…› = unresolved.

"[… I thought I should write these things to you, and that you should] refer them to His Majesty, being of such ‹…› nature as they are;
so that it seems to me I must believe they are not likely to succeed ‹…› the pacification and reconciliation of the said marshal, as
people say. Since then, having written up to this point, and seeing that the hour of the audience with the Pope was drawing near, I
wanted to go there before ‹closing?› my letters; and so, discussing with [His] Holiness the affairs of Languedoc, ‹…› of so much the
greater suspicion, and [to show] that the above is true: [His Holiness] has ordered me to do all I can with the King so that he comes
to terms in every way with Marshal de Montmorency, to calm the troubles which are in such ‹…›"

The cipher ends there; a postscript in clear Italian follows on the same page (not translated here).
Context (not checked in sources): Henri de Montmorency-Damville, governor of Languedoc, was in open dispute with the crown and the
Joyeuse in 1584-85; Gregory XIII pressing Henri III to settle with him fits the papal policy of those months.
