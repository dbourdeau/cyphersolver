1. f. 1, address: "Mr **Gen. Don Félix Díaz**."
2. f. 1: "A month ago today, having come from there, we put into the hands of those designated the **documents** of
   which we were made the bearers."
3. f. 1: "The indescribable enthusiasm at the sight of **orders** and the immediate **warlike** preparations made me
   suppose that by this date all would be a **terrible conflagra{b/c}tion**, and so, in reporting in my previous letter
   of the 25th of last month, which I confirm, in spite of difficulties that I felt at first hand, I supposed that this
   second one would be the bearer of so fine a reality."
4. f. 1: "But... Oh disappointment! All those eager for those **orders**, when they were communicated to them, turned
   out not to have the proper preparation, and all, save the honourable exception of **Mr. Lic. Pimentel**, came to
   the **junta** asking for thousands of pesos, and there was a fool who, when asked for the **fifty men** whom he has
   **{d/a}rmed** and **supplied with ammunition**, and whom he had offered, asked for $20,000.00, saying
   "**my life I sell, but dearly**"."
5. f. 1: "But, passing over these defects proper to our very Mexican character, and doing justice, I must tell you that
   much has been done and a great deal achieved, for the few **movements** already under way answer your call, and if
   they have not grown stronger, it is the work of the **lack of money**, because, except for don **Aristeo**, always
   ready, who is the one who has met many expenses, other persons have made their offers come to nothing."
6. f. 1: "**Don Pablo** himself spoke to us of many **thousands of pesos** that never arrived, and even gave us exact
   dates for **movements** that did not take place."
7. f. 1: "And if these **movements**, small for now, do not get ahead, they will gain strength on feeling the crushing
   force of the body that **Lic. Pimentel** has carefully organised in his native State, and which, given its
   preparation and the seriousness and prestige of the **organiser**, I trust and confess is for now the only thing
   that keeps alive my faith as a sincere believer."
8. f. 1: "I know, and I am assured, that there are many others, but as I do not know them, I speak only of that of
   **Oaxaca**, because of the close contact I have the honour to have with **Mr. Pimentel**, who has kindly chosen me to
   be the intermediary between you and him, communicating to you urgently whatever deserves special consideration, now
   that he is to leave the **capital**, which today I do judge to be early and profitable."
9. f. 1: "I have spoken and argued much that the work should begin [struck letters] in the **north**, so satisfying
   the wish you expressed, but it seems that circumstances decide the contrary, and perhaps very soon you will learn
   that it was begun on the opposite side."
10. f. 1: "I tell you this because some days ago I wrote to **Gen. Ismael Hernández**, who answers me by word of
    mouth, and tells something about Doña Mercedes and a very early visit to you."
11. f. 1: "As some delay follows from this, it is not rash to predict that the work in **Oaxaca** will come first,
    since **Mario Ferrer** has gone ahead by launching a **manifesto** signed by him, which document and arrangements
    have been put into practice without even consulting **Mr. Pimentel**, for it seems that **de la Hoz Escobar** and
    **Mata** pass over so worthy and [struck words] competent a gentleman; a circumstance that has delayed the work
    because it subjected it to changes."
12. f. 1, signature: "Your ever loyal partisan, who esteems you greatly, greets you with affection and hopes soon for
    your orders. **C. M. Argume{d?/d}o**."

## Notes

- Sentence 4: "**mi vida la vendo pero carita**" is literally "my life I sell, but at a good price"; "dearly" keeps the
  pun. "junta" is the directing board, left as "junta".
- Sentence 7: "no aventajan" is taken as "do not get ahead" (do not gain ground); I am not sure of the shade.
- Sentence 8: "lo que hoy sí juzgo pronto y provechoso" refers to Pimentel's leaving the capital, which Argumedo now
  expects soon and thinks useful.
- Sentence 11: "de la Hoz Escobar" may be two persons, as the reading notes; the English keeps the run as one.
