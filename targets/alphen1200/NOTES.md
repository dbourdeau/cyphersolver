# Alphen aan den Rijn sword, inlaid blade inscription (Archeon collection)

Status: attempted, open. Not solved.

Photographs used (published, not freely licensed, so not reproduced): Madoc 2014 afb. 1 (Restaura, van Hasselt) and Wagner 2013
picture 1 (J. Worley). Wagner/Worley/Holst Blennow/Beckholmen 2009 (WKK 51, DiVA diva2:289664) and Wagner 2013 (WKK 55,
diva2:652689) are open on Uppsala DiVA and give the sibling readings below.

Sword blade found 2002 in the Rhine bed at Alphen aan den Rijn (Provincial Depot Bodemvondsten Zuid-Holland no. 8888),
silver-inlaid engraved letters on both faces, 285 mm per face, letters tapering 15 to 12 mm. Layer after the 1122
damming of the Kromme Rijn; script 12th or early 13th century (Hedlund, Bornschlegel, Koch for sibling blades).
Letters in `inscription.txt`, from van Hasselt's drawing in Madoc 28/2 (2014), which I checked against the published
photographs (Madoc afb. 1, Wagner 2013 picture 1). Forum transcriptions (Peters 2011) differ and are superseded.

## What exists already (checked 9 Nov 2026)

- Marc van Hasselt (Archeon), 2012: BENED(ict)O CH(rist)O F(i)L(ius) I(e)S(us) S(an)C(tu)S ..., "doesn't make a lot of
  sense"; MTINIUSCS = Martinius Sanctus (St Martin), three times on the blade; St Mauritius floated.
- Matthijs van Halteren 2014: "Benedictus Sanctus Martinus Sanctus", source not given.
- Craig Peters 2011: BENEDICE with interrupting characters; OXO possibly symbolic.
- Parallel: River Witham sword (see witham1250), Berlin and Swedish blades; Wagner/Worley on "talking swords".

## Measured (9 Nov 2026)

- Vowels: face A 12 of 38 (32%), face B 17 of 41 (41%); Latin running text about 40%. Unlike Witham (17%) this is not
  a pure initials string; it has Latin-looking words (BENED-, -IUS, DIC-, DIO) amid junk.
- Longest repeat across faces: MTINIUSCSD (10 letters, A pos 26, B pos 16); also MTINIUS+ES/CS twice more on B
  (variant MTINIUSES), DIO x2, DIC x3, CMT x2. A repeated formula, not a cipher text.
- No monoalphabetic or other substitution is indicated: the vowel rate and the repeated words are what unsubstituted
  Latin with abbreviations and garbled/unlettered inlay would give. Ulfberht-type blades are known for such
  invocations with copied, partly meaningless letters.

## Assessment

No cipher is shown. The reading "Benedicto Christo ... Sanctus Martinus Sanctus Domini" is the abbreviation/pious
invocation family; MTINIUS(CS) as MARTINIUS is a reasonable expansion, the rest (ISSCSDRRISCDICEC) has no verified
expansion and the transcription is van Hasselt's published drawing (Madoc 2014), checked against the published photographs. Not solved: no ciphertext
reliable enough, no key, no clear text.

## Remaining gaps

Beyond the formula parse (section on sibling alignment): F T, RR, EC, NI, ICCC, II Z I, DIO/IO have no expansion.
Outside blocker: the Restaura conservation report and Provincial Depot photographs are not online, and no agreed
expansion of the SDX/DIC/NED formulae exists in the literature (Wagner 2009/2013, Gumbert, Latinists consulted by
van Hasselt in 2014).

## Escalation

Siblings: done (Wagner et al. 2009 readings of Fyris 74, Karlstad, W 1830, W 879, W 897, Marburg, St Omer, Finland
aligned above; Witham has no key). Clear pages, known keys, print: Wagner/Worley and Madoc read in full. Key rebuild:
no key exists. Retry: only with the Restaura report or Wegeli 1904 / Post 1918-20 drawings of the family.

## Language check (9 Nov 2026)

`lm.best_language` on both faces joined (79 letters): Latin first (la -4.06 per char), then fr-1600-letters -4.17,
la-letters -4.57; the margin over French is small, so only weak support that the letters are unenciphered Latin. A
substitution would have scored near the bottom (de/nl -5.8 to -6.4). Consistent with the abbreviation/garbled-invocation
reading; gives no key and no further expansion.

## Sibling alignment and formula reading (9 Nov 2026)

Wagner et al. 2009 read the SDX family: SCS = S(an)c(tu)s (abbreviation stroke seen on Fyris UMF/B 74), D = Dominus,
X = Christus, IS = Iesus (ligature), O invocative, DIC maybe Dominus Iesus Christus, MTINIUS = M(ar)tinius or Mauricius.
Longest shared blocks, measured with difflib against their readings:

- Face A with Fyris 74 "...ERNISSCSDX...": ISSCSD (6 letters).  Face B with W 1830 "...ISROXMTINIUSRN": OXMTINIUS (9).
- BENED with the Finnish sword "BENEDICATNTVS..."; DIC with the same sword (DICIT) and the DIC group.
- W 1830 and Fyris 74 give the Alphen formula pieces in other orders, so the Alphen text is the same invocation in
  another arrangement, not a different cipher.

Segmentation, with their expansions applied (each one is theirs, tentative):
A  BENED(icat) | O X(riste) O | F T | IS(us) | SCS D(ominus) | RR | IS C | DIC | EC | MTINIUS | CS D | NI
B  DIO | X | MTINIUS | ES D | IO | MTINIUS | CS D | ICCC | MT | DIC | II Z IS(us) I
Gloss: "May ... bless; O Christ ... Jesus; Holy Lord ... Jesus Christ; Dominus Iesus Christus ... Martinius, holy Lord ..."
Letters assigned to a recurring formula element: 56 of 79 (30 of 38 on face A, 26 of 41 on face B); F T RR EC NI ICCC II Z I and the DIO/IO groups stay
unexplained, and the expansions are the sibling authors' guesses, so this is a family-level parse, not a decipherment.
Wagner 2013 itself lists Alphen as showing SCS, DIC, BENE and NED groups at once and calls the classification unsound.

## Dating and what stays unexplained

Dating: stratigraphy after 1122; van Hasselt links it to the Loon War flight of 1204 (his inference, unproven).
Unexplained groups above; no key exists because nothing is substituted: the invocation is written in abbreviations
and name-like groups. Outside blocker for a full reading: the Restaura conservation report and Provincial Depot
photographs (not online), and an agreed expansion of the SDX/DIC/NED formulae, which Wagner et al., Gumbert and
several Latinists (Jan-Apr 2014) have not reached.
