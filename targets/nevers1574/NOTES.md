## Result

The catalogue premise that these are two unread cipher letters is only half right. At f. 21 a ciphered flap is folded across the same recto that contains a complete fair copy in clear Italian. One image shows the flap over the text, the next shows it folded away. Renato Birago writes from Lyon on 28 September 1574. He had expected Nevers for the feast of St Michael; the bearer has arrived at the worst possible political moment and returns with an answer; Birago will discuss Piedmont orally; Their Majesties wish to provide Nevers with a government and whatever else he deserves.

The duchess's two-page letter at f. 15 is likewise not bare cipher. A contemporary or near-contemporary reader supplied a word-by-word French decipherment in the spaces between the lines. It begins “Monseigneur, je ne veulx faillir à vous donner …” and is addressed to “Monsieur le duc de Nevers, pair de France.” The cipher continues to the foot of f. 15v and is followed by a clear closing on f. 16r: it is midnight, the writer's eyes hurt from weeping, and she wishes herself in paradise rather than suffer as they are being made to suffer. The closing dates the letter Lyon, 16 September; 1574 is inferred from its archival context. Much of the gloss is individually legible, but it is too crowded for a defensible continuous transcription in this pass.

## The cipher and the misleading sibling

Tomokiyo correctly notes that ff. 15 and 21 do not use the Nevers key copied at ff. 12-13 of this volume. Their alphabet is very close to Nevers collection no. 2, issued in 1584 and now BnF fr. 3995 f. 16, but is not identical: here `F` gives *e*, `9` gives *q*, and x, y, z remain clear. The 1584 alphabet was fetched and inspected at full resolution (`nevers_no2_alphabet.png`). It explains the family resemblance but is unsafe as a decoding table. The target's own gloss and parallel clear copy are better evidence.

## f. 21 reading

Full semi-diplomatic transcription: `f21_transcription.md`. The clear copy is continuous and the date and signature are explicit: “Da Lione alli xxviii di settembre 1574 … Renato Birago.” The first sentence contains one uncertain expression written approximately `saluato suo`; it may be a name.

In substance:

* Birago expected Nevers in Lyon for the feast of St Michael but now hopes he will arrive shortly.
* The bearer's business is ill-timed: affairs of the kingdom make virtually anything else more urgent.
* The bearer returns with the dispatch that will explain the answer.
* Birago avoids writing more about Piedmont and will speak in detail when he sees Nevers.
* “Lor Maestà” are well disposed to give Nevers a governorship and every other thing he merits; Birago has performed, and will continue to perform, the offices of a true servant and friend.

## Prior art checked

* Tomokiyo's Cryptiana Nevers page describes the two letters and the cipher's relation to no. 2 but supplies no decipherment.
* The Gallica notice and the BnF manuscript catalogue were checked; neither prints a reading.
* Ernest Lavisse, *Histoire de France*, VI.1, prints Henri III's emotional appeal to the duchess **before he left for Poland**. Despite some similar language of distress, this is not the 1574 letter. The attempted crib was discarded.
* No DECODE record was needed: the primary manuscript itself preserves the decipherment and clear copy.

2. Read the f. 21 fair copy against the cipher slip and transcribed it end to end.
3. Cut f. 15r-v into overlapping bands and half-line tiles, made a second visual pass at native resolution over the interlinear hand, and located the clear closing on the adjacent f. 16r.
4. Fetched the similar 1584 Nevers no. 2 alphabet from BnF fr. 3995 f. 16 and tested its relation to the target. It is demonstrably not the exact key.
5. Searched printed literature and identified a superficially similar Henri III passage in Lavisse and the Lyon palaeographical volume; its date and sender exclude it as a crib for this letter.

## Independent verification by Brice Dudout (PR #572, 14 Nov 2026)

Brice Dudout (PR #572, `VERIFY_dudout_20261114.md`, `verify_dudout_20261114.py`) enciphered the reading back with
`f15_key.md` and aligned it with the signs of `f15_signs.tsv`: 942 of 978 collapsed signs agree on f. 15r (0.963),
637 of 781 on f. 15v (0.816; ll. 2-5 unread), 587 of 620 on f. 16r (0.947). Re-run here: same figures. So "gloss and
decode agree word for word on f. 15r and f. 16r" holds for the words, not for every sign: about fifty places differ by
a letter or two, mostly letters the sign transcription does not show (*veu[l]x*, *fai[l]lir*, *vou[s]*) and one gap
in the transcription (16r l. 6).

Two regular usages were missing from `f15_key.md` and are now listed there under "Homophones and traps":
*gouvernement* is written `0 8 2 …` in all five places (n sign for u), and the d sign `b` stands for u/v three times.
Checked here in `f15_signs.tsv`: the five lines and the three places are as he says. He saw 15r ll. 2 and 8 on the
Gallica image (btv1b90600800 f17) as transcribed; the image was fetched here but those two lines were not read again.
f. 15v ll. 2-5: he re-read them at full size and recovered nothing new; the gap stays.

Read share `v10_relabel.measure` 0.941, as recorded; read in part stands. No change to a letter value.

## Escalation

- [x] siblings: checked the neighbouring fr. 3315 leaves and Tomokiyo's description; ff. 12-13 have a different 1574 key, while the f. 21 cipher flap carries its own Italian interlinear reading.
- [x] clear-pages: found the clear ending of the duchess's letter on f. 16r and Birago's complete clear copy on f. 21r beneath the flap; f. 21 was read in full.
- [x] known-keys: inspected the 1584 Nevers no. 2 alphabet in fr. 3995 f. 16 and Tomokiyo's comparison; it differs at F=e and 9=q and is not an exact decoder.
- [x] print: searched exact wording and historical editions; rejected the superficially similar Henri III appeal printed by Lavisse because it predates this letter and has a different sender. No published edition of f. 15 or 21 was found.
- [x] key-rebuild: tested alignment of the enlarged f. 21 signs with its interlinear Italian gloss; crowded glyphs and uncertain word boundaries prevented a reliable complete table. No uncertain mapping was used to fill f. 15.
- [x] retry: revisited f. 15r-v in overlapping native-resolution crops after the clear ending and f. 21 parallel were found; the opening remained secure, but the ciphered middle still could not be transcribed continuously.
- [x] retry (22 Oct 2026): gloss and cipher signs transcribed line by line, alphabet rebuilt from their alignment (`f15_key.md`); f. 15 and its f. 16r continuation read, only f. 15v ll. 2-5 and single groups open.
- [x] key-rebuild (22 Oct 2026): the f. 15 alphabet rebuilt from gloss-sign alignment, every value attested in several words.

The useful next escalation is palaeographic: edit the small interlinear French on f. 15 from the native Gallica scan, using the larger cipher only to check alignment. If a mechanical reading is required, first align the f. 21 signs with their own Italian gloss to rebuild the exact 1574 alphabet, then apply that table to f. 15. Do not use the 1584 no. 2 table unchanged.

The atlas has no edge for either letter: both originate in Lyon, but the duke's location when he received them is not established. The f. 21 cipher was not transcribed sign by sign, so a reveal animation would imply precision the work does not have.
