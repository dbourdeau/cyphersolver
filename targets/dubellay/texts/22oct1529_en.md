## Translation

My lord, wherever I might wish otherwise, I could not keep from granting the cardinal of York his request, which he
has had made to me by an Italian servant of his, the only one left faithful to him out of so great a number; but
first I will ask you to understand [?how] his affair [stands].

On Tuesday the seal was taken from him; an inventory was made of all his goods down to a crown; an order was given to
all who [?have] handled anything of his these twenty years to render account of everything, which troubles them
greatly, for since not a sixth part of [?what] was hoped for is found, they are sure to get many a turn of the rope.
Then he was given the choice of answering before [?the King] or the Parliament. He chose [?the King], thinking, as is
true, that the lords would have put [?judges] into the Parliament at their pleasure. However, since he hopes nothing
from the mercy of [?the King], [?complains] […] so hotly that he is no more than nothing, so that he expects nothing
less, after the loss of all goods and honours, than […] at the very best a harsh and [?perpetual] prison, confirmed by
Parliament, and […] thereby irrevocable, neither by the King nor by anyone else.

The points on which he is pursued are the plunderings he has made, but all that would not bring him to his death.
They [?press] for [ꝏ] lese-majesty, that he […] of the legation more than ten years against the laws of the land;
that at Amiens he agreed to bring the Duke of Ferrara into the league without the knowledge of [?the King], and that
he gave [?the King] a bond in his own hand without the command, in no way, of the said lord; and as for […] since,
he had war declared on [?the Emperor] against his [the King's] intention, [and this is] the least of these points.
In sum, they mean to make him lose his head, and I well believe that unless the King and Madame help him it is in
great danger of staying there. There may well be other points I do not understand, but that is what I understand of
it.

Thereupon he asks that they send a gentleman in all haste to remonstrate with [?the King], who will entreat you most
[…] by these means, without going further into particulars, nor […] will dare acknowledge in any way in the world that
it was so, knowing [they] do not consent to this request, on pain of having him put to death at once. He begs [?the King] and
Madame, for the honour of the Passion of God, [?assurance] by this means, that the fury of his enemies, who have
thus carried [him] away, have but […] to a poor old age, to the most shameful and [?miserable] end that ever befell a man,
which he sees so near and before his eyes, unless they take pity on him.

As for me, although it displeases me to meddle so far of the two, his advice, what […] I would wish you of it,
I could. For the [?growing] of the goods to which one has affection […], pity [?reigns], good judgement […] turned in its
entirety.
