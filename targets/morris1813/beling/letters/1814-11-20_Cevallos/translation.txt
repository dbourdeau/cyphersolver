My dear Sir — The King my master, to whose knowledge I have [?raised] the paper which you addressed to me under date of the 17th of this month, commands me to answer you that His Majesty keenly resents the conduct which the government of the United States has observed towards the one which, during His Majesty's absence, governed Spain in his royal name. [370] The slight done to His Majesty by the American government in not recognising as minister of Spain the person who was sent with that character to the United States in the year 1809 is not repaired by the appointment which the same States have now made of [?Mr] Erving to reside with the same character near His Majesty. [370] Moreover, the hostile conduct which the American government has observed towards the Spanish provinces bordering on its territory during the last [?period] does not show on its part the disposition to keep up a good understanding with Spain which the appointment of Erving seems to declare. [370] His Majesty [?has] therefore every right to demand full satisfaction from the American government before admitting the minister named by it to reside near his royal person; and this satisfaction must begin with [?the] recognition of His Majesty's minister to the United States. [370] Notwithstanding this declaration, which I make to you in the name and by order of His Majesty, you may deal with me in the matters that may arise and concern either of the two States, for His Majesty has authorised me to do so. [370] I take this occasion to offer you my particular wishes to be of service to you, and I pray God to keep your [?life] many years. [370] Palace, 20 November 1814. Your attentive and sure servant, who kisses your hand, Pedro de Cevallos
