Morris to Monroe, Madrid, 27 October 1814, headed No 19. NARA M31 roll 14: page 1 in clear on 0137 (filmed again on 0138; other copies 0146, 0148); the coded passage on 0139 (base text, left page top), 0147 (Duplicate) and 0149. Catalogue 650 groups 0139 with No. 18; it is the continuation of this letter. No contemporary decipherment on these copies. One reader.

Summary and context: Page 1, in clear, says that with no letters from Washington, and with reports of British troops landing in Florida, Morris came to Madrid to seek talks with the government, arrived on the 9th, and on the 13th called on the Duke de San Carlos to explain his mission. The coded passage says the duke received him coldly and promised an answer he has not given, that the state of opinion is hard to judge, and that the press and the post are policed against anything unfavourable to the government or to Britain. The coded heading reads "Madrid, October twenty-seventh, 1814".

Conventions: *italic* = Morris's own words in clear. `[370]` = the unidentified sign that follows full stops. `[575.1060]` = groups left unread. `[?word]` = inference.

Madrid, October twenty-seventh, 1814.

*(Page 1 in clear, ending: "... appeared to me to render such a communication peculiarly requisite.")*

The duke received my communications without any expression of [?approbation] or any cordiality of manner, and confined his reply to an assurance that I should hear from him, which assurance he has not yet complied with. [370] It is very difficult to give any satisfactory account of the general state of the national sentiment as to its support of or opposition to the [?actual] government. The press is [?care]fully guarded against any expression of sentiments in the smallest degree unfavourable either to it or to the British government, and the post office also [575.1060]ously guards against the introduction of foreign papers. [370]
