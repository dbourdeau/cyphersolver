Morris to Monroe, Madrid, 30 December 1814, No. 24 (the number and date are in the coded heading). NARA M31 roll 14: top of frame 0220 (base text, clear hand); copies on 0205 (right page, dark film) and 0196 (right page, very dark, with a clerk's decipherment between the lines, transcribed in clerk_decipherment_0196.txt). Wholly in code. One reader.

Summary and context: Morris reports, from a source he trusts, that the Council of State is preparing a report to the king recommending a declaration of war on the United States over its conduct on Louisiana and Florida. He adds that 47,000 soldiers and 5,000 officers have come back from France as prisoners of war, that Spain has at least 160,000 men under arms, and that two expeditions of ten thousand each are fitting out at Cadiz, one under Field Marshal "Linan" said to be for Mexico, the other under General "Murillo" for Buenos Aires, to sail early in January. His next letter, No. 25 of 6 January 1815, says the news of peace may change these "intended measures".

Conventions: `[370]` = the unidentified sign that follows full stops. `[?word]` = inference. Names are spelled as the groups give them.

(Number twenty-four) Madrid, December thirty, 1814.

[?Sir] — I am informed today from a source in which I have considerable confidence that a report is preparing by the Council of State to H. M. recommending a declaration of war against the [?United States], founded upon the grounds generally of our proceedings [?relative] to Louisiana and Florida. [370] The number of prisoners of war returned from France amounts to forty-seven thousand soldiers and five thousand officers, and the whole force of Spain at present under arms is not less than 160 thousand. Two expeditions of ten thousand each are preparing at Cadiz; one under Field Marshal Linan, said to be destined to Mexico, the other under the command of General Murillo to Buenos Ayres. They are expected to sail early in January. Anthony Morris
