(No. [1520 seventeen; number unresolved]) Madrid March 24th 1815.

Sir — No variation has taken place in the posture of our relations with this government since my last to you of the 27th January. *The position in which* I am here *will more particularly appear by reference to* my former [?letters] should they have been received and to Mr Erving's received by me which I extract for your information and *which also explains the situation of* Mr Brent.

(Extract Mr Erving to Mr Morris dated Paris February 20 1815)

"*On the authority of a gentleman who arrived here some time since from* the United States *it has been reported that* a secretary of legation to Spain has been appointed &c *and has arrived* in Spain and as *in such case he must have been* appointed before our government became *aware of the difficulties in the way to* my going thither [?it] may be proper to anticipate by suitable instruction any inconvenience either to him, or to yourself or any *prejudice* to our *affairs in their present delicate state, which may otherwise arise from his presence* or interference, if then he should arrive in Madrid please to lose [?no] time in acquainting him with *the causes which for the present prevent* my going thither he *will then doubtless see that it will be wholly* improper for him to present his commission, or to make his character known to the government, or to mix in the *affairs which must remain* as our government has placed them under your [572.1001] until my arrival, and pursuant to *the manifest intention of the* secretary of state in his last letter to you the scrupulous observance of *which intention is made the more* necessary *by what has occurred since it was* expressed."

Mr Brent in pursuance of the instructions contained in the above extract *left* Madrid *on the 13th instant* (B: *12th inst*) *for* Valencia. Although I did not *consider his departure* necessary yet I saw no inconvenience or impropriety in it. [370]

I have as yet been unable to obtain more certain and specific information as to the (B: their) views and plans of this cabinet in relation to our affairs. A special embassy has been contemplated, but its precise objects are not known. It is however presumable rather with a view to complaints of supposed injuries than to an equitable adjustment of existing differences or an amicable arrangement of [1466]. Nothing more has been said for some time as besides the result of the expedition to New Orleans yet unknown here, and which will give rise to new views in that quarter the the attention of the government is too much engrossed by its internal concerns to permit its being turned to any foreign relations that are not pressed upon it.

The number of the discontented is encreasing greatly and their murmurs daily become more audible. The enthusiasm with which the king was received on his arrival in his dominions has subsided and numbers begin to reflect with bitterness upon the difference between their real situation and that which they had anticipated. The discontented besides complaining of the continuance of former abuses and [?despotism] allege that the confidence of the king and his absolute power is possessed entirely by a few palace favorites without talents or [?education], without the public confidence and which have never merited it by rendering any distinguished services to the king or to the nation; they represent the system recommended by these men and adopted in the administration as distinguished alike by severity and weakness. It has (they say) decided the loss of America, irritating the Americans by their imprisonment of their [?representatives], and occasioned general ruin and misery in the peninsula by a number of arrests, exiles and other punishments so great that there is scarcely a family in Spain which does not mourn the fate of some imprisoned friend or some banished relation: they complain that the king's favorites confer employments on persons totally unfit for them and that many notoriously addicted to the prince of peace and instruments of his most obnoxious measures, are appointed to situations of great influence and emolument while [?those] [?who] by their patriotism and [?attachment] to the [?cause] of the nation are [?most] worthy of employment appear to be the only ones entirely excluded.

All the ayuntamientos appointed under the regency and the cortes were removed by one general order, by which those in office in 1807 and who had been nominated principally by Godoy, were restored. Corruption and venality in the tribunals is also loudly complained of as well as the protection given to informers and the facility afforded to unprincipled and malicious men of ruining their enemy by secret accusations. The greatest dissatisfaction however is [1407[555]^] with the department of the treasury, and the opponents of the government accuse it of continuing the abuses and perplexity heretofore prevailing and of injuring the resources and credit of the nation by the restoration of the estates of the convents, and the abolition of the system adopted by the cortes without substituting any less onerous or equally productive. They assert that the nobility and clergy whose injurious privileges have been restored have done nothing in return for these favors to relieve the nation from its embarrassments. The government left without resources even for its [?ordinary] expences is unable to pay the army and the discontent has extended itself to the [?troops] as well as the citizens.
