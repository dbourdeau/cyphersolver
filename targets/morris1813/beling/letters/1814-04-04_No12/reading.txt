Morris to Monroe, Cadiz, 4 April 1814, No. 12 Duplicate. NARA M31 roll 14, frames 82-83; second copy of the cipher, "No 12 Cadiz April 4th Extract", frames 87R-88L. [?word] = inference; [370] = unread group. Spelling follows the code values.

Summary and context: Morris, an unofficial US agent in Spain, reports that East Florida could be bought on moderate terms, partly paid in US produce, but only with about ten thousand dollars in bribes; he is still waiting at Cadiz for a reply to his proposal. Curry (1888) reports the same advice in substance.

On the important subject of the acquisition of East Florida, I have reason to believe that it might be purchased on moderate terms and a considerable portion of its price be paid in the articles of the produce of the United States, particularly beef, pork and tobacco, but some presents or douceurs would be indispensible to the amount of perhaps ten thousand dollars: the departments of the Spanish Government are not yet sufficiently regenerated, I fear, to have great hopes of success without means of this description. [370] I have waited [?here] in [?expectation] of a reply to my proposition before referred to until this time; indeed the difficulties and even dangers of the tour to Madrid have delayed my departure until circumstances should have occurred [?rendering] it more essential than any which have yet appeared. [370] Anthony Morris
