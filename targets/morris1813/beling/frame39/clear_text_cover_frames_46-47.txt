Cadiz4th January1814
Sir
Since my last of the7th Ult the enclosed extraordinary proposals have been made to the Spanish Govt relative to Louisiana and Florida. It is of a very recent date and its author and supporters are not yet known. It was presented in secret by a certain Rivane [name uncertain] a Native of Maryland now a Spanish Citizen under the new Constitution. He is a Son in law of Luther Martin of Baltimore. I do not think there is the least reason to apprehend its adoption or any sudden change of measures by the Spanish Government relative to these Territories as the Council of State has recommended to the Cortes that no change should be adopted in consequence of the possession of Mobile but that every thing there should remain in its present state. This state will probably continue until time is afforded to hear what shall be the determination of the United States relative to the recognition of the Spanish Govt. On this point all the pride and sensibility of the Nation is excited. I expect soon to hear from Madrid and shall regulate my proceedings accordingly. It appears to me that a prudent Policy on the part of the U.States would induce them to have always a respectable force in the vicinity of Florida to counteract any attempts of the British in that quarter. An offer was made by them last year to send Troops there under the pretence of preserving the Territory to Spain but probably with the intention of holding it, which disposition under present circumstances will no doubt be increased should a change in the Regency take place. Their views will be facilitated and no means will be left untried to effect this change.
