# Sign conventions, catalogue 1447 (DLI.4.634.1) and sibling DLI.3.524.1

The cipher is written in ordinary Arabic numerals, two digits per group, groups joined by hyphens,
embedded in clear Spanish text. The signs are the ten digits 0-9; no other cipher shapes occur.

- A group is transcribed as its two digits exactly as written, e.g. `61`.
- Groups are separated by a single space in the transcription; the writer's hyphens are not kept.
- A run of groups between two clear words is one cipher word-run; clear words are kept in `<...>` on their own.
- Line ends of the manuscript are marked `|`.
- An uncertain digit is written with the alternatives in brackets, e.g. `1[3|0]` (13 or 10).
- An unreadable digit is `?`.
- Struck-out clear words are kept as `<~word~>`.
- Punctuation after a group (`:` `.` `;`) is not transcribed.

Shape notes (no values):
- The writer's `3` sometimes has an open, flat top, close to a reversed `C`; it is separated from `0` by the
  closed lower bowl. Where this is not clear the group is marked uncertain.
- `7` has a horizontal cross-stroke in some places; `1` never has one.
- `4` is open-topped.
