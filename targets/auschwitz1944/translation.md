# English translation — clandestine message from Auschwitz, 4 September 1944

Working translation. Same marks as `reading.md`: `{word}` probable, `[x]` supplied or gloss, `‹…›` unread.

7) Examine well the route of Paprzyca – whether it would not be possible that way to carry out, if need be, {larger} {consignments} –

8) Does Jasia from the TWL. Rajsko work in the service of the SS men in the building next to the Gärtnerei (market garden) at Rajsko, and does she have the contact with the Lager through Józek, the gardener {there}; what contact can she have with you. (*jaki* also allows "… Józek, the gardener {there}, who can have contact with you".) –

9) Send the [clandestine] press – Do you already have with you explosives prepared, [coming] from ‹…› ?

Warm greetings — [signature]

Notes. Paprzyca: Paprzycy is the genitive of this surname; Sadzikowska 2022 (p. 120) names Ludwik Paprzyca, a released prisoner. TWL: Truppenwirtschaftslager, as expanded in Sadzikowska 2022 (p. 120 and note 240), who quotes point 8 in German from Paczyńska 2013 (p. 439). "What contact can she have with you" is the sense the print takes (same place).
