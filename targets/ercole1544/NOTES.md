### Key

The letter table is `key_frozen.tsv`, unchanged, and no key was built from these letters. Two tests, on pass 1 and pass 2:

- `scripts/fit_1554.py`, frozen table against 500 shuffled tables per page, bigrams trained on cc. 104r and 107: every one of the ten pages beats all 500, for example c. 119r -2.488 against a maximum of -3.169 in the rerun. The word-hit count is weak on short pages: on c. 116r it only ties the shuffled maximum.
- `scripts/hillclimb_1554.py`, ciphertext-only, one-to-one, no table as input, trigrams from clear Italian: on all 6,858 letters together it recovers the table on 20 of 20 signs. By letter: 20 of 20 on five; 18 of 20 on c. 114r and 13 of 20 on c. 116r, where it scored above the true table because the text is too short. A first design, which let several signs share one letter, beat the true table and agreed with it on almost nothing. It was dropped as overfit.

Both tests read signs that were transcribed with the table known, so neither measures the transcription. They show one substitution, and that it is this table.

Seven signs are new here and open in `key.tsv`: 13, 26, 29, 36, L^3, L^7, L^8. Context suggests 43 = sua santita at c. 113r and c. 120v; it stays open. A group read z5 (H15, J14) is unexplained, and `L^?` at A08 is a blotted superscript. Many words in these files decode to forms one sign off from a word: g/t, z/g, 14/19, h/u, c/p, c/e, f/s, r/e, d/q, 5/s. Decryptions keep the form the signs give.

## Marks in the reading files

`reading_*.md` holds the Italian, `translation_*.md` the English, block by block with the same holes. An unmarked word is verbatim from the signs, in their spelling; only word division and punctuation are editorial. `word°` is a repair: the letters differ from the signs and `editorial_*.md` gives the signs. There are 18 (3 and 15). `[?word]` is a proposal from context, or a form the signs do not give. `[...]` is unread and may hide more than one word. `[L^30]`, `[43]` are codes not identified. `scripts/check_repairs.py` aligns each reading with its decryption letter by letter and fails if a departure is left unmarked; it reports 0. The earlier claim "Spelling is that of the signs" is withdrawn in its old form: it now holds for unmarked words only, and only as far as the transcription is right.

## The system

A plain one-to-one letter table with a small code list. It is not a homophonic cipher. Early notes suspected a stronger system because of the letter counts; that came from misread signs (c/e, g/q/z, n/r/u).

The letter table is a reciprocal half-alphabet on the 22-letter Italian alphabet:

    plain   a b c d e f g h i l m
    cipher  n o p q r s t u x y z     (and the reverse: n=a, o=b ... z=m)

Plain z alone breaks the pattern and is written k. There is no word division.

- Doubled letters are numbers: 5 cc, 8 bb, 10 dd, 11 ff, 12 gg, 14 ll, 15 mm, 16 nn, 17 pp, 18 rr, 19 ss, 20 tt, 21 uu, 22 zz. A ligature (S in the transcriptions, shaped like 1 plus a hooked 9) also gives ss.
- Nulls 24, 25, 27, sometimes inside a word (`pnf 27 nyr` = Casale).
- L alone is "et".
- About 30 code numbers for common words and names (30 che, 53 non, 39 questo, 40 quello, 57 cosa, 59 quanto, 64 perche, 35 Vostra Excellentia), and L with a superscript for persons and places (L^10 papa, L^n imperatore, L^r re di Francia).

Numbers are not unambiguous shapes: 6 can look like b, 0 like o, 2 like r, 4 like q, and 61 written fast looks like b1 (E02, F20). Cipher t and y joined look like "td"; that is gli. The superscript blots at D11 and D12 are scribal corrections, not signs L^x.

`key_frozen.tsv` is the table as frozen. `key.tsv` adds what came later, with a status column and use counts recounted on the third state. L^o has 12 uses. F17 and F23 were rechecked on a sheet of all 21 superscripts: both are L^10, so F17 reads "li dissegni del papa", which also removes the doubt raised by the final check. 44 and L^o stay doubtful: context only.

## How the key was obtained

c. 103r against c. 104r (4 Jul 1544). c. 103r was transcribed twice from different crops; the passes differ on 34 of 1,166 signs, re-read on 2x enlargements. Pass 2 was not blind. Global alignment against c. 104r (`scripts/step1_diff_align.py`) gives the counts behind `key_frozen.tsv`. Code values come from the same alignment; many have one occurrence and are marked weak.

Table rebuilt with no table as input (`scripts/step4_rebuild_freeze.py`, output `validation/key_rebuilt_103_104.tsv`): runs of 11+ letter signs on c. 103r are slid over c. 104r and a place counts only if the run maps one-to-one. 25 of 40 runs hit uniquely (393 letters). 19 of the 20 signs that get a vote agree with `key_frozen.tsv`. The exception is k, with a single vote against two alignment places for k=z. The script cannot give S, L or code values. The independent check got the same result with its own script.

The freeze rests on file times only (table 21:16:33; first c. 109 tile 21:16:49). The table-free rebuild does not depend on those times.

## What the letters say

### cc. 78r-79r: Mantua, 4 May 1544

Date from the clear docket on c. 79v ("44 / Car.l di Mant.a 4 di Maggio"); c. 79v also has the address to Ferrante.

Three weeks after Ceresole (14 Apr 1544). Ercole, regent at Mantua for his nephew, also governed Monferrato. When the master of the horse was sent to Casale it was thought the French would follow up the victory, and Casale was seen to be in great danger. Ercole explains ordering victuals and passage for the French, as help could not be had, and that no imperial troops were to be admitted without his will. He says he would give his life rather than fail his duty, weighs losing Monferrato against risking Mantua, and fears seeming a friend of fortune and the blame when the duke comes of age. If he were free like other servants of [?his Majesty] the world would see his will; even so, that consideration "non sera tale che faria che manchi mai" to his duty as a vassal. He then passes on news: the count of Mirandola went to the cardinal of Ferrara at Bondeno, who could give no answer until he had negotiated with the Signoria; Piero Strozzi and the count have some six thousand and await a commission; the talk points to Tuscany; two unknown men came from Pisa and Livorno. All of it has been told to [L^30] and the duke of Florence.

### cc. 174r-175v: undated

No date. "fatte in Busseto" puts it after June 1543. Farnese sent to "those parts", the diet, England, Ferrante's journey ("prima che partisse per Inghilterra", G16-17) and the Turkish fleet suggest late 1543 to early 1544; that is a proposal. Ferrante's English mission is from general knowledge and was not checked here.

Ercole reports intelligence on the Pope gathered at his own cost and at risk to his informant, poorly received by the Emperor's ministers, who think him deceived. He asks Ferrante to tell him, without betraying any secrets, whether to go on. He relays the Pope's complaint over the diet, the use of Lutherans and the promises made at Busseto; the Emperor's answer; a report that [43] sent letters to the French king urging an army for Italy. c. 175r seems to go on to a plan around Milan and Tuscany, "li dissegni del papa", a letter of the wife of Pier Luigi, and money for the Emperor, but those lines are decoded, not read. c. 175v turns to the sequestration of the revenues of cardinals who serve the French king, after French action against the cardinal de la Baume, Salviati's case, thanks for an office done with [?his Majesty], and the count of Mirandola. The text stops in mid sentence.
