# Catalogue 1554, letter 2, c. 114r: English translation of reading_0114r.md

Same holes as the reading; probable words are not marked again here. Sender, date and addressee are not on the leaf.

The count of Mirandola has let me know that, count Giovan Tommaso having learnt something, as he judges [...]
by way of don Diego that [<44>, perhaps His Holiness] be [asked?] to accept him into his favour, making the said
count Giovan Tommaso yield to the claims that [...] has had in Mirandola, [...] to say that [...] and that first
[...] and the greatest fortress there is in the world; that he is resolved to do this, and to come to court and
disturb this negotiation [...] matter [...]. [<55>] I let Your Excellency know it, so that you may know with what
intent count Giovan Tommaso comes, so that [... about eight words ...] he attends to the negotiation [<28>].

Context (proposal): the Pico of Mirandola dispute; Galeotto II Pico held Mirandola (under French protection from
1536) against his kinsmen, among them Giovan Tommaso Pico; "don Diego" is probably Diego Hurtado de Mendoza,
imperial ambassador. Not checked in print.
