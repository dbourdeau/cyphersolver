# Online search for additional Moray evidence, 18 September 2026

No additional same-key ciphertext or omitted Donibristle key image was recovered. The search did identify a much more specific archive route. Published decipherment and key remain unchanged.

## Donibristle: primary report and current archive

- [HMC Sixth Report (1877), p.638](https://archive.org/details/reportofroyalcom06grea/page/n663/mode/1up) records seven cipher papers, two entitled “The Quein's Cipher,” and says the accompanying plate represents two of the set.
- [Facing plate](https://archive.org/details/reportofroyalcom06grea/page/n664/mode/1up), downloaded and visually inspected: one alphabet/nomenclator with nulls, and one dot-based system. Neither matches our recovered Moray alphabet (for example, the first plate's triangle means Edinburgh Castle; ours means a). The plate also explicitly mentions another Queen's cipher partly composed of Arabic numerals, without reproducing it. Thus five of the seven papers are not reproduced here. This does not establish that all five survive today or were used by Moray and Wood.
- [NRAS217, Stuart Family, Earls of Moray](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrasregister/details.aspx?reference=NRAS217), inspected through Playwright after direct retrieval was blocked. Catalogue updated 4 September 2026. Access restricted; enquiries to the NRAS Registrar, **nras@nrscotland.gov.uk**. Twelve volumes of detailed catalogues are available for consultation on request.
- Crucial catalogue detail: a portfolio discovered in 1911 in the estate office at St Colme contained documents selected for the HMC report; these are **now in Box 45**. The original inventory covered boxes at Darnaway. **Box 45 is an inferred starting point for the cipher enquiry, not a confirmed item-level location for all seven keys.**
- [NRAS access instructions](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrasregister/welcome.aspx): full catalogues are online only with owner permission; staff can answer specific existence enquiries but cannot undertake research. No enquiry sent.

Best next request: identify current references and availability of scans for the seven cipher papers described at HMC Sixth Report p.638 and its plate, particularly the five not reproduced; ask whether they are among NRAS217 Box 45.

## Additional Moray–Wood correspondence

1. **31 July 1568:** Throckmorton to Wood, Hatfield, forwarded a Moray letter received through Leicester. [Christie's catalogue](https://onlineonly.christies.com/s/valuable-books-manuscripts/sir-nicholas-throckmorton-1515-16-1571-24/93084) explicitly says that enclosure is no longer present. Evidence of another letter, not a recovered ciphertext.
2. **25 August 1568:** Wood to Cecil, Bain *CSP Scotland* II no.785, [p.490](https://archive.org/details/CalendarStatePapersMaryQueenOfScotsVol2/page/n534/mode/1up), forwards Moray's letter to himself. No.784, Wood to Leicester, pp.489–490, mentions the same circulation. No enclosure or cipher image located. Bain's table assigns the unmarked entries of this date range to the Scotland State Papers volume XV; **SP 52/15 is a collection-level lead, not a verified folio identification**.
3. **6 September 1568:** Sussex to Cecil, Bain II **no.805**, [p.501](https://archive.org/details/CalendarStatePapersMaryQueenOfScotsVol2/page/n545/mode/1up), confirms forwarding a packet from Murray to John Wood. It does not print the packet's contents. Marginal source is C.P., vol.I; Bain's abbreviation table defines this as *State Papers (Queen of Scots)*, not Cecil Papers at Hatfield. Modern collection [TNA SP 53/1](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C3828761) covers May–September 1568. No precise folio or surviving enclosure established.

TNA's SP 53/1 page offers a copy quotation and says the record cannot be downloaded there. [Gale State Papers Online Part II](https://www.gale.com/product-catalog/primary-sources/state-papers-online/state-papers-online-part-ii) provides the relevant Scotland manuscript collections; its indexed content list includes all SP 52/1–72 and SP 53/1–23. This is a potential institutional-access route, not evidence that the missing enclosure survives or contains cipher. No authenticated Gale manuscript was opened.

No.804 on the same p.501 is the already-tested Wood→Cecil cipher: DECODE R2989, BL Add MS4136 f.33. Its different key remains excluded. OCR misreads nos.804/805 as 304/806; checked against page images.

## Strength of the lead

NRAS217 Box 45 is a specific location lead, but a weak lead for solving this
cipher: no matching date, correspondents or symbol set has been identified.
The enclosure references likewise establish correspondence, not surviving
same-key ciphertext. These findings do not justify calling another archive
request a strong route to the last word. No enquiry was sent.

Source links above are the public evidence. Downloaded source images and
catalogue snapshots remain in the research workspace; DECODE manuscript
images are not redistributed in this repository.
