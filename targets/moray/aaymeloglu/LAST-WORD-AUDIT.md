# Moray last-word audit, 18 September 2026

**Outcome: no secure decipherment of the last word.** A new candidate is
*knauis* (knows), but it requires an unproved null/cancellation or transcription
change. Do not promote it into the published key or describe Moray as solved.

## Correction to the previous elimination

The earlier preference for `Zz=t`, based on eight local attestations of *lattis*
versus none of *lakkis*, does not exclude `Zz=k`.
[DOST, Lak v.](https://dsl.ac.uk/entry/dost/lak_v) explicitly attests *lakkis*
in its sense of being absent or lacking, including Henryson and Dunbar.
Thus *thair lakkis na mair to spil ...* remains a serious alternative to
*thair lattis na mair to spil ...*. A limited corpus's zero count was being
given too much weight. Neither assignment is independently established.

## Candidate: knauis

[DOST, Knaw v.](https://dsl.ac.uk/entry/dost21033) records *knau(e* and the
inflected *knauis*, including a 1549 example. The possible closing phrase
*oure freindis knauis* would mean “our friends know.” This is a contextual
conjecture, not a mechanically recovered reading.

| Position in L4 | Published label | Proposed treatment | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Zz | k | Also produces *lakkis* in L3; t remains possible. |
| 2 | x3 | n | Could be an xd-family variant; not established. |
| 3 | A | a | Existing secure value. |
| 4 | Xs | u | Resembles X; the long adjacent stroke may belong to position 5. |
| 5 | Z2 | null, or cancelled/superfluous character | Essential unsupported assumption. Must distinguish this long-stroked sign from the o-sign elsewhere. |
| 6 | N | i | Existing secure value. |
| 7 | Zh | s | Existing secure value. |

This gives `k n a u [null] i s`. Merely assigning the three uncertain key
values does **not** give *knauis*: preserving the published `Z2=o` gives
*knauois*. No attestation of *knauois* was found in this search. It must not
be passed off as an established historical spelling.

An alternative deletion of position 4 and assignment of position 5 to w
would produce *knawis*. That likewise lacks evidence of an actual deletion.
The heavy strokes in position 4 resemble the established u-sign, so calling
them a cancellation is not independently justified.

## Manuscript and context limits

Rechecked the original DECODE P4 image and the existing line/focus crops.
The last z-like sign has a long stroke descending to the left; the o-signs
in *of* and *to* have a short upward extension. This warrants reconsidering
the transcription, but does not supply a plaintext value. Treating it as
the y-sign also fails to give an independently supported word.

The preceding `haill e(t)` remains unresolved. A restoration such as
“the whole, as our friends know” would add another unsupported reading;
it is not evidence proving the final word. No continuous translation of
the final sentence is claimed here.

The independent [Bourdeau notes](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/blob/main/moray/NOTES.md),
checked during this pass, contain no later solution or independent manuscript
transcription. They suggest sibling correspondence and the unreproduced
Donibristle keys as possible sources of additional evidence.

**Next discriminating evidence:** another use of the long-stroked L4 sign,
an original key identifying nulls, or independent palaeographic evidence
for cancellation. Further word guessing alone cannot distinguish these hypotheses.
