# Sign conventions (shape names only, no values)

Source image: plate of ELTE G. 4 Fol. Tom. V p. 1461, printed in Donath 1964, Magyar Konyvszemle 80/1, p. 57
(EPA PDF MKSZ_EPA00021_1964_80_01_047-073.pdf, PDF page 11 = index 10). Halftone print; strokes thin.

The cipher itself has no word division, but the writer leaves spaces between words and writes commas and points in
clear. Spaces and punctuation are kept in the transcription. Each sign is one token, separated by a single space; a
word gap is ` / `; punctuation is kept as `,` `.` `;`. In the pass files, `?` after a token means the shape was unsure.

This file was first written before the transcription, with names for the shapes expected from a first look at the
plate. It was brought into line with the transcription after the blind review (12 Nov 2026): the first table now
lists every token that occurs in `transcription_p1461.txt`; names that were drafted but never used are listed at the
end. No token in the transcription was renamed.

## Tokens used in the transcription

| token | shape |
|---|---|
| `5` | figure five |
| `oo` | horizontal double loop on the line (like a small infinity sign), with a lead-in stroke |
| `w` | omega-like, two open bowls (looks like a cursive w) |
| `q` | q-shape whose descender is crossed by a short bar |
| `gx` | q-shape whose crossed descender ends in a curl or loop turned back under the line |
| `dg` | upright stroke crossed by one or two bars, often with a long descender (dagger, crossed thorn) |
| `+` | small cross, upright, sometimes with a small loop at the top; smaller than `dg` and without its long descender |
| `3` | figure three, flat top (z-like) |
| `pi` | two-legged pi with flat top |
| `m` | three-legged m with a flat top bar (looks like a script m) |
| `oo-o` | two circles joined by a bar (dumbbell) |
| `^` | caret, inverted v (lambda) |
| `Y` | small y or gamma: a v-shaped fork on a short stem that slants down to the left |
| `y` | Cyrillic che shape (h-like, four-like with open top); one place, L05 |
| `c` | reversed c, open to the left |
| `8` | figure eight |
| `2` | figure two |
| `d` | d-shape, bowl left, ascender bending back |
| `o-` | small circle with a tail to the right |
| `o` | small circle with a dot in the middle (in one or two places with a short stem instead of the dot); no tail |
| `+o` | small cross followed by a small circle, written as one sign |
| `6` | six-like, round bowl with a stroke rising from it and curling over (Cyrillic be) |
| `4` | figure four, cross shape with a loop |
| `x` | x-cross; when followed closely by a stroke it may look like `\x` |
| `7` | hook, a seven-like stroke with its foot turned (lambda reversed) |
| `III` | three short verticals with a dot (Cyrillic sha with dot); one place, L09 |
| `z` | two loops one above the other (figure eight with open waist, stem below) |
| `&` | ampersand-like loop sign (L01 in the opening group, and before `dg` at the start of L07) |
| `1` | short upright stroke like a figure one; only in the opening group of L01 |
| `f` | long s or f shape with a long descender curling at the foot; only in the opening group of L01 |
| `A°` | capital A with a superscript o and point; end of the opening group in L01 |
| `[574]` | not a shape: three small signs (`5`, `7`, `4`) written above L02 with an insertion mark, kept in brackets where the mark points; `scripts/decrypt.py` decrypts each sign inside the brackets |

## Tokens in key_frozen.tsv only

These are shapes of Donath's printed table (1964, p. 58) that were given names when the key was mapped. None of them
occurs on p. 1461.

| token | shape |
|---|---|
| `\|o` | short vertical stroke and a circle (bar-circle) |
| `-\|` | short bar and a vertical stroke |
| `D` | triangle (delta) |
| `vv` | double vee |
| `[]` | small box |

## Names drafted first and not used

`b/` (b with a stroke through the stem), `v` (vee open at the top), `p` (p with a loop on the stem) and a bare `?`
(sign not identified) were drafted before the transcription. None of them occurs in the transcription.

Superscript corrections written above the line are recorded in brackets as described for `[574]`, and in a note at
the foot of the transcription.
