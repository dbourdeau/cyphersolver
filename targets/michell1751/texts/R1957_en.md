5263. To Secretary Michell in London.

Berlin, 28 December 1751.

I have received your dispatch of the 14th of this month and am, like you, of the opinion that the matter of the King
of Sardinia entering as a contracting party into the treaty being negotiated at Madrid between the courts of Spain
and Vienna will not fail, although I am persuaded that General Wall predicts quite rightly on that.

As I have just been informed in secret of a plan which the courts of Vienna and Petersburg have formed to have
Prince Charles of Lorraine succeed to the crown of Poland when it falls vacant, I presume that it is with this in
view that the court of Vienna has tried to make its treaty of neutrality for its possessions in Italy with Spain and
the King of Sardinia, so as to have its hands free on that side to pursue its design on Poland. So my intention is
that you are to use all your care and skill to find out, as far as possible, whether the court of London is informed
of that design, or whether perhaps the court of Vienna has come to an understanding on it separately with that of
Russia, without communicating it to the King of England, which I nevertheless find hard to believe; on the contrary,
I almost presume that it is perhaps on the advice of the latter that the Empress-Queen has lent herself to making
new cessions to the King of Sardinia, being given to understand that it would be in the design on Poland that she
would find herself compensated for these cessions. Although I am not unaware of the difficulties you will have in getting
to the bottom of this mystery exactly, you must nevertheless do what is possible and at least give me your
reasoning on how far you believe that the people who are at the helm of England's affairs, and whose system you
know, being on the spot, might fall in with the aforesaid design.

Moreover, tell me also whether you believe that the present pacific system of the English ministry will still hold,
and for how long you believe it will be continued, supposing that the King of England remains alive a few years
more. Make me your report on that, but note that you must make it, on all of the above, to me alone, directly,
without sending duplicates of it to the department of foreign affairs. For the rest, as the Queen of Denmark has
been dead since the 19th of this month, let me know whether this death has affected the King of England and whether
he has grieved at it, and likewise about the dangerous illness from which the Duke of Cumberland has just recovered.

Federic.
