# GLC02414.339, Camp Piatt to "Operator" (for Scammon), 1 July [1863], signed by code as Toland: reading in part

No decipherment of the time has been found in the collection. This is the one telegram of the lead whose text was not already read in 1863. It is read in part.

Arrangement used (B, chosen at packaging on 13 Nov 2026 by sense; an independent second reader reached the same arrangement). The 42 cells fill exactly only if the darker-ink "&", the margin "when", the interlined "not", the overwritten word ("amore") and the struck word ("bent") each count as one word. Three of the cells counted as read (&, when, not) are later additions in other ink or another hand.

[Camp Piatt], July first. Scammon, Charleston. [Virginia] I saw [Missouri] last night before answering, & [gaol, or the plain word "gave"] our united opinion. If the movement is important, please direct when at/as shall start [bent]. [nile, or the plain word "will"] not this interfere with the proposed [amore] on [away, or "way"]? Signed Toland.

Under the second reader's "gave" and "will": 37 of 42 (0.881), 34 of 42 (0.810) without the additions. Holes: Virginia, Missouri, gaol, bent, the first nile, amore and away. "might" is written for night. "at/as" is a plain word either way (the earlier emendation to "it" is dropped). The readings gaol/gave, nile/will, at/as and away/way were looked at again on the image after the independent check and are not settled.

What the context suggests (guesses, not values; made after the test): on 4 July Halleck told Kelley to "push forward the expedition planned for General Scammon", and Kelley ordered Scammon to send a force to cut the Virginia and Tennessee Railroad (Official Records, Series I, vol. 27, part 3, p. 528). On 8 July Scammon put the 34th Ohio (mounted), the 2nd West Virginia Cavalry and two companies of the 1st West Virginia Cavalry into a brigade under Toland (p. 614); a telegram of 23 July reports that the party sent to cut the railroad fell back, "Colonel Toland killed and Colonel Powell mortally wounded" (p. 750; the volume's index lists the operation as the expedition from Fayetteville to Wytheville, 13-25 July 1863). The last sentence may therefore ask whether "the movement" would interfere with the proposed raid on the railroad or on Wytheville, with [amore] for raid or expedition and [away] for the place; and "our united opinion" may be Toland's and another commander's, possibly Col. W. H. Powell of the 2nd West Virginia Cavalry ([Missouri]). None of this is tested; the code words stay holes.
