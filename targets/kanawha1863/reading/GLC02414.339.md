# GLC02414.339, Camp Piatt to "Operator" (for Scammon), 1 July [1863], signed by code as Toland: reading in part

No decipherment of the time has been found in the collection. This is the one telegram of the lead whose text was not already read in 1863. It is read in part: 35 of the 42 grid cells carry a plain word or a value; one of the 35 (Camp Piatt) is inferred from the heading slot and the printed form, not from a pair, and the signature value (nile = Toland) was learned from .185 after the test.

Arrangement used (B, chosen at packaging on 13 Nov 2026 by sense; not checked by a second reader): every mark on the form is counted, the margin "when not" and the darker-ink "&" included. The 42 words then fill the 7 x 6 grid exactly.

[Camp Piatt], July first. Scammon, Charleston. [Virginia] I saw [Missouri] last night before answering, and [gaol] our united opinion. If the movement is important, please direct when [it] shall start [bent]. [nile] not this interfere with the proposed [amore] on [away]? Signed Toland.

Holes: Virginia, Missouri, gaol, bent, the first nile, amore and away are code words with no value. "might" is written for night; "at" is taken as a slip for "it" (an emendation, not a reading of the form).

Round 2 arrangement (A): without the "&", the second column taken off by the route is one word short and row 7 column 2 is empty. It reads: "... I saw [Missouri] last night before answering the [gaol] our united opinion: if when movement is important please direct not at shall start [bent]; [nile] more this interfere with the proposed [ ] on [away]. Signed Toland." Arrangement B reads better in rows 3 to 7 and needs no empty cell; both are kept in `decryption/GLC02414.339.txt`.

What the context suggests (guesses, not values; made after the test): on 4 July Halleck told Kelley to "push forward the expedition planned for General Scammon", and Kelley ordered Scammon to send a force to cut the Virginia and Tennessee Railroad (Official Records, Series I, vol. 27, part 3, p. 528). On 8 July Scammon put the 34th Ohio (mounted), the 2nd West Virginia Cavalry and two companies of the 1st West Virginia Cavalry into a brigade under Toland (p. 614); a telegram of 23 July reports that the party sent to cut the railroad fell back, "Colonel Toland killed and Colonel Powell mortally wounded" (p. 750; the volume's index lists the operation as the expedition from Fayetteville to Wytheville, 13-25 July 1863). The last sentence may therefore ask whether "the movement" would interfere with the proposed raid on the railroad or on Wytheville, with [amore] for raid or expedition and [away] for the place; and "our united opinion" may be Toland's and another commander's, possibly Col. W. H. Powell of the 2nd West Virginia Cavalry ([Missouri]). None of this is tested; the code words stay holes.
