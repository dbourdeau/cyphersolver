# GLC02414.341, White to Scammon, Fayette, 2 July 1863: reading

Deciphered in 1863 at Scammon's headquarters: the grid GLC02414.114 (catalogue: "possibly a copy in General Scammon's hand"). This is the crib from which the route was recovered; the reading below is the frozen route and key applied to the cipher, which gives the .114 text.

Fayette, July second. Scammon, Charleston. Just reported we from outpost: Mrs. Miller, wife [of a] soldier [of the] thirteenth Virginia, is there from [the] head [of] Paint [Creek]; says forty-fifth and twenty-second were to leave Lewisburg yesterday. Truly yours, White.

Notes: "ms" = Mrs.; "va" = Virginia. "Truly yours" stands for two cipher words read "Italy" or "truly" and "Venus" or "Agnes"; the readings are uncertain, and if the first is "truly" it is a plain word. Two words in the grid ("out", "from") are under a paper repair patch on the cipher form and are supplied from .114.
