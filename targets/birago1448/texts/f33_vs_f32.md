# f. 33 (cipher, decrypted) against f. 32 (clear), word by word

BnF italien 1585, Andrea da Birago to Francesco Sforza, Pavia, 28 Feb 1448. The decryption is
`texts/f33_decryption.txt` (from `decode.py` over `texts/f33_cipher.txt`); the clear text is `texts/f32_clear.txt`.
The two say the same thing throughout. Every cipher sign matches the key (513 signs, 22 distinct, none off the
key, 2 uncertain shapes). The cipher is not a copy of f. 32's spelling. It has its own Lombard spelling, drops
Latin-style doubled consonants, and in a few places uses a different word.

## The two points asked about

- **Francesco Piccinino.** f. 32 l. 06 spells out "ad francisco piccinino"; the cipher (L07) writes "a" + the
  single code sign FP (an 8/ɣ-shaped sign with a stroke through it, the "Francisco Piccinino" sign on the key).
  The name is not spelled out in letters anywhere in the cipher.
- **The hard phrase.** f. 32 l. 08 "Senza [?] de la quale" is enciphered (L10) as `s e n z a | c a r i g o | d e l a | q u a l e`:
  **"senza carigo de la quale"**, i.e. *senza carico della quale*, "without whose commission/charge". The clear
  word on f. 32 is then best read **"incharicho"** (= *incarico*, same sense). Its long-shafted initial and the
  "ri" (written like "u") make it hard to read in the clear. The cipher has the simpler form *carigo* (c→g voicing, as in Lombard).

ha, et, conforta, caxa (cipher), lui, fare, per andargli, domandare, per questi famigli, gli, dare, adiuto, et,

The salutation, date and signature are in clear on both slips.
