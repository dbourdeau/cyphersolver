# Vienna working decipherments and two numerical corrections

Inventory 204A is a second set of contemporary papers. Scan 253 right begins with the end of 14 March, followed by No. 23 of 21 March. Scan 254 left carries the end of No. 23 and the text of No. 26, dated 28 March. Scan 254 right instead begins a Munich letter. After both March transfer tests, two independent readers examined the four Vienna corrections on full-size manuscript crops without seeing the older numerical identifications or our candidate key. These are readings of period notes. A struck word, an inserted word and a number may describe successive stages in the clerk’s work. | Place | Manuscript annotation and parallel evidence | Editorial conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| 21 March, scan 253 right | Both readers prefer 238 with a cross below a struck word. Both read inserted *on*, followed by *leur donnera*. One reader allows 230 and qualifies the attachment of the cross. In the cipher itself, the blind pair agree on 238 with cross at slot 309. The inventory 248 fair copy reads *On donnera*, visibly omitting *leur*. Both earlier computer alignments independently of these numerical annotations assigned slot 309 the word *on*, but the group occurred only once in the training letter and was excluded from the 93 entries. | The two readers re-examined the same 204A annotation as the old survey. The separate evidence is inventory 248, where slot 309 aligns to *on* in the fair copy. The two fit settings agreed on that reading. No change was made to the key or score. |
| 21 March, scan 254 left above the No. 26 heading | Both readers prefer 210 with a ring and read *ni*, inserted before *ses esperances* over a canceled mark. In the cipher ledger, slot 685 is a hole; both first choices are 210 with ring, but a reader qualified the last digit. Both fits place *ni* at the hole. A second secure 210 with a ring at slot 622 aligns differently. | The note supports *ni* at this place. The conflicting other occurrence prevents a universal assignment. These annotations belong to the close of No. 23. |
| 28 March, scan 254 left, after *garantir* | Both readers prefer 642. An upper ring and a lower cross-like feature make the purpose of the marks uncertain. The short word immediately before it is canceled. The clean copy goes directly from *garantir* to *Il se peut aussi*. The cipher letter has two secure instances of 642 with a cross, at slots 67 and 146. | No secure word value. A cancellation and the absence of a word at this place do not establish a general null. |
| 28 March, scan 254 left, near *justement* | The preferred reading of both is 710 with a cross. The first reader also allows 713 or 718 and qualifies the mark. The mark is below a cancellation on the line of *justement*. *Du jour* occurs on the following line. The cipher manuscript independently contains 710 with a cross at slot 154, between known prospective matches *que* (151) and *le* (158). | Withdraw the old claim that this note attests 718 with a cross for *du*. Neither the numeral nor its placement supports quotation as an attested key value. No alternative plaintext assigned. |

The older note also gave 227 for *armée*. That entry is in the Munich letter on the opposite page. No reading derived from the Munich item entered the Vienna key. The differing pronoun in *On donnera / on leur donnera* is direct evidence that the working and fair copies are distinct textual witnesses.
