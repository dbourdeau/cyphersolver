## Vincent’s mission

- **10 January, No. 2.** In the lower half of scan 134 left, Van Spaen says that foreign newspapers had announced “l’envoi du Général Vincent au quartier général de l’Empereur des François” before Vienna heard of it. He adds that Vincent had yet to leave Vienna when the news appeared. This dates Van Spaen’s report of the announcement. He gives no advanced knowledge of Vincent’s instructions here. [Prose dispatch, scan 134 left](https://service.archief.nl/api/file/v1/default/d863bec4-6673-400c-b787-cc316c429d22)
- **10 January, No. 3.** The receiving office’s French copy ends “Le fond de cette mission est toujours couvert, au moins ici, d’un voile épais.” The preceding sentence names General Vincent. The mission reported in prose and the uncertainty recorded in cipher thus occur in separate dispatches dated the same day. [Start of French copy, scan 445 right](https://service.archief.nl/api/file/v1/default/409eb78f-7d0e-4624-9853-e05f7bdc057d) · [End, scan 446 left](https://service.archief.nl/api/file/v1/default/1fb34e36-2c79-4366-b9fa-c99414261b6b)
- **24 January, No. 7.** In its first paragraph Van Spaen considers whether Vincent carried an answer to proposals brought by Montesquiou or to an imperial letter. He suspects the French ambassador had been bypassed and expressly calls his account a “Conjecture”. [French copy, scan 106 right](https://service.archief.nl/api/file/v1/default/0b2cef40-76a9-4098-9e04-d73651647395)
- **31 January, No. 9.** The next week’s French copy begins with a report that Vincent had been instructed to press at Warsaw for “la restitution de Braunau”. The sentence introduces this as a suspicion. In the following lines Van Spaen calls such reports conjectures circulating in Munich and Ratisbonne. These passages document competing accounts of the mission. Its actual instructions remain unverified. ## Lucchesini’s visit

No. 6 opens with the marquis’s arrival in Vienna, “Samedi soir, 17 de ce mois”. The next morning, Van Spaen says, the marquis wrote to the French ambassador before visiting him and requested “des passeports pour l’Italie”. Van Spaen then distinguishes the sensation caused by his appearance from the competing interpretations of his journey. No. 7 takes up that last point in its second paragraph, observing that the public watched and interpreted his every word. The two dispatches share an episode and a reporting date. The exact arrival date and passport request come from the prose original. [No. 6, first paragraph, scan 109 right](https://service.archief.nl/api/file/v1/default/c28a4231-c5c3-4b91-98c6-c4bab395b86d) · [No. 7, second paragraph, scan 106 right](https://service.archief.nl/api/file/v1/default/0b2cef40-76a9-4098-9e04-d73651647395)
