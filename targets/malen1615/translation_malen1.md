# Translation of letter 1 (English), same holes as the reading

Brother. [...] I thank you very much for the good news. As for the matter of Jülich, I think the dispute will
[never] be settled except by force of [arms]. [...] for to think that 60 or 59 will let themselves be crowned by
68 out of regard for their confederation is a great mistake. We know that they threaten us with the breaking of
the truce, as if indeed; and what do they think, that we tremble at their threats? No, no: let them only begin,
and they will see whether we have a heart in our belly; and what is more, our camp is waiting for nothing else.
I think there will be no difficulty about the restitution of the town of [Rees], and others. All that you tell me
of the embassies of the baron of [Anholt] are inventions and practices of those gentlemen of Holland. And as to
their presupposing that the [sieges] are made in the name of His Majesty 59, they are right. I do not think they
would give them anything to understand [by it], notwithstanding the league that the marquis [...] of Brandenburg
and others have made. I have already done what you ordered me toward the Spanish ambassador, and my [...] very
humbly asked to give you his compliments. And as to what you tell me of the safe-conduct and letters of
recommendation, [...] you that whatever [you] ask, I will have it delivered to you at your first request without
fail. [...] the least doubt. [...]
From London, 28 December.

Numbers 60, 59 and 68 are code numbers in the letter; their holders are not identified.
