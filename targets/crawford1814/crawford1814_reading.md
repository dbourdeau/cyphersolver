## 0064R (after the clear account of the Decatur affair on 0063R-0064L)

I am inclined to believe that this government has come to a determination to sacrifice every thing for the
friendship of England. The use of the Telegraphs has been granted to Lord Wellington, to enable him to ascertain with
the greatest dispatch what passes in Belgium. The King, so far from viewing with an eye of jealousy the augmentation
of British troops and others in British pay in that country, looks to that force for protection against his own
people. It is reported and believed that a conspiracy has been discovered, at the head of which they have placed
Savary. Those opposed to the government [say] that this conspiracy is the creature of the police, fabricated to
countenance the arrest of about thirty persons, of whom the court is afraid. [A] report also prevails to day that the
discussions at Vienna have assumed a hostile tone [...]

## 0065R (after the clear account of the war minister's orders and the Duke de Brancas at the barracks)

[...] His grace made a precipitate retreat. Things are approaching [a] crisis which must be decis[ive] of the fate of
the King and right[s] of the nation. It is impossible to form a correct opinion of the result of this struggle. It is
indeed most probable that the rights of the nation will be lost without [a] struggle. This however is not the common
opinion, among many of the best friends of the new order of things. Many believe that the royal family will again be
expelled, but none of them appear to have any distinct view of the means by which it is to be effected.

## 0066L

The army would be for recalling the Emperor. The republicans will with difficulty [be brought by] that circumstance to
consent to this. The great mass of the senate would be compelled to emigrate if any struggle is ma[de]. Those who make
it will form three parties. I have already stated the designs of the army. Such of the new nobility as may be opposed
to the views of the army will be for the King of Rom[e] with [a] regency, or they will be in favor of elevating the
Duke of Orleans to the throne. The great difficulty will be to reconcile [these] three different parties. The genuine
republicans will unite in either of the la[s]t speci[fied] projects, if [any] effort should be made.
