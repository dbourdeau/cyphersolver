## Reading

{Bombay} April 28 1808.

To His Excellency the Rt. Hon. Lord Minto, Governor General.

My Lord,

I have the honor to enclose the duplicate of my address No. 2 to your Excel. from ‹to› Roads, and to inform you of
my arrival here on the 24 inst. The inclosed copy of a letter No. 2, addressed by me on the following day to the
Hon. the Gov. here, will communicate to your Excel. the resolution I formed on hearing that Brig. Gen. Malcolm had
previously to my arrival proceeded into the {Persian} Gulph under a special authority from your Excel. The Hon.
the Gov. in Council having favored me with the perusal of the papers which I requested, I lose no time in
submitting to you the ideas which occur to me relative to the situation in which I find myself placed, premising
that I wish to shape my conduct in such manner as may be most beneficial to the public service and agreeable to
your Excel.

I think it is to be lamented that Gen. Malcolm, as I must believe him aware that the Sapphire could not be long
before she arrived at {Bombay}, had not thought it expedient to have suspended his departure hence for a few days,
since, had he even ultimately thought it his duty to proceed on his mission, I presume it might have o[cc]urred to
him that, from my being in possession of the [s]entiments and wishes of His Majesty's Ministers and the Hon. the
Secret Committee on the subject of our relations with {Persia} and the eastern part of Tur[k]ey, the a[d]vantage he
would have derived from a personal communication with me would more than have compensated for the trifling delay
in his departure from {Bombay} which would have been necessary to procure it. Although it appears clear to me from
the ninth paragraph of your Excel.'s instructions to Gen. Malcolm that it is not your intention his mission should
suspend, much less supersede, a mission under the immediate sanction of His Majesty, yet for reasons which must be
obvious to your Excel. I consider it most prudent to suspend my departure for {Persia} until I am honored with
your Excel.'s commands; for surely nothing could be more impolitic than to suffer even the appearance of clashing
authorities in {Persia}, and nothing more unjustifiable than putting the public to the expence of separate
missions to the same court.

I do not perceive the smallest disadvantage to the public service that can possibly arise from the line of
conduct I have adopted; and though I should not think myself authorized under any circumstances, except those of
receiving His Majesty's commands to do so, to annihilate or relin[q]uish the mission of which he has been
graciously pleased to honor me with the charge, yet I thin[k] His Majesty's Ministers cannot blame me for
suspending it to the extent I now do, in deference to your Excel. and for the weighty and solid reasons mentioned
in the preceeding paragraph.

I was informed that copies of my full powers and of my instructions have been transmitted to your Excel., and I
therefore consider it unnecessary to trouble your Excel. with a repetition of them, or to do more than barely
mention that since the {French} have appeared at Tehran the {Persia}ns are aware that national Ministers can only
be accredited by His Majesty.

In closing this letter I must avail myself of the opportunity of repeating that I am on every account fully
determined to pay the most respectful obedience to your Excel.'s orders, as far as can be compatible with those
instructions which I have received from His Majesty's Ministers and the Hon. the Secret Committee. In great
sincerity, it will be highly flattering and consolatory to me to find that my conduct meets your Excel.'s
approbation, and in the hope of being early honored with your commands,

I have the honor to be &c. [in clear]

Har(f)ord J(o)nes [signature in another figure cipher with nulls 1 and 2, probable]

(True Copy) Jas. Morier, Priv. Sec. [in clear]
