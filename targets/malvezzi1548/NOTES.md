# Ferdinand I to Johann Malvezzi, Augsburg, 23 January 1548 (DECODE R366)

Reading: read (key R367; measured 509/514 = 0.990 of ciphered plaintext words as Latin sense, 5 Oct 2026)

Hadtörténelmi Levéltár (Military History Archive), Budapest, 1548/3 · DECODE R366 · 8 pp. · Latin ·
homophonic cipher with graphic signs · DECODE status "partially decrypted".

Ferdinand I writes to Giovanni Maria (Johann) Malvezzi, his envoy at the Porte, in reply to four of
Malvezzi's letters. The letter is mostly in clear Latin, with long ciphered passages on pp. 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7
(about three pages of cipher in all).

## The key

The key is on DECODE itself, filed at the same shelfmark: **R367** ("Rex Ferdinandus I. ad Malvezium, a. 1548",
Military History Archive 1548/3, 1), a four-page later reconstruction giving Tabella I and Tabella II with the
letter alphabet, the doubled-letter numbers and two lists of code syllables. **R386** (National Széchényi Library,
Quart. Lat. 2254. 15, "Chiffre Johann Malvezzi's") is a second copy of the same alphabet. DECODE R368 at the same
shelfmark ("ad 2") is a further short ciphered slip, not a decipherment.

System: monoalphabetic with homophones, graphic signs (alchemical and planetary signs, Greek and Latin letters
used with other values). Doubled letters are numbers (13 = ss, 14 = rr, 16 = nn …); 50 = bellum, 51 = pax.
Nulls are whole Latin-looking words dropped into the cipher — *etiam, idcirco, atque, cum, Porro, Dar, Der, spo,
spu* — plus loose letters (b, d, n, z, π). The code syllables cover the principals: ki = Rustanus, ka/ke = Passa,
le = Charles V, Bi = the king of France, Nas/Ras/ye = the Sultan, zes = Queen Isabella, zos = Frater George,
Cad = Constantinople, Eb = orator.

## What the cipher says

The ciphered passages are exactly the politically delicate ones; the clear text around them is the routine
complaint about border raids.

**p. 1** (read): *sermones Rustani Passae tecum habitos, quod uidelicet ingens odium propter tractatum nobiscum
initae pacis, tanquam illius pacis constituendae autor, nunc uero conseruandae promotor et defensor, sibi
conciliarit; et praeterea quid tu ad quaeuis eiusdem Rustani Passae obiecta, quaesita et interrogata responderis*
(clear:) *et quid pro eiusdem pacis conseruatione faciendum censeas* -- Ruestem Pasha has drawn great hatred on
himself for the treaty, as the author of the peace and now its defender.

**p. 4 A** (read): *Galli tam multipliciter et dolose hanc pacem subuertere conantibus, qui sane id non ob aliam
causam faciunt quam ut, ex quo Rex Galliae et sui similes sacram Caesaream et catholicam regiam maiestatem,
fratrem et dominum nostrum charissimum, uiribus suis lacessere non audent, imperatoris Turcorum sumptibus …
contra maiestatem suam Caesaream … Sed magnitudo eius atque etiam ipse Rustanus Passa dubitare* (clear:) *minime
debent de syncera fide et animo et Maiestatis suae Caesareae et nostro erga hanc pacem.*

That is the secret of the letter: the French are working to wreck the Habsburg–Ottoman peace, and they do it for
one reason only — not daring to attack Charles V with their own forces, they want the Sultan's resources turned
against him. The Sultan and Rüstem Pasha are not to doubt that Charles and Ferdinand mean the peace sincerely.

**p. 4 B** (read): Malvezzi's pay. *Quo uero ad intertentionem, tibi per proximas literas nostras significauimus
nos decreuisse ut tibi pro tua ordinaria intertentione et pro reliquis extraordinariis sumptibus, quos pauci
momenti esse posse nobis ostensum est, tibi mille quingenti ducati annuatim numerentur, exceptis sumptibus …* —
1,500 ducats a year, continuing on p. 5.

**p. 5** (read in full): how the money is to reach him. Ferdinand will pay separately for couriers sent express
(*mittendis tabellariis propriis*), and has given orders to **Justus de Argento, his secretary, who is setting out
shortly with the money** and is to take Malvezzi's account of receipts and expenses. By the same Justus he sends
*summam illam duorum millium et octingentorum ducatorum* — the 2,800 ducats Malvezzi had written that he was owed,
spent not only on his own upkeep but on other business of the King's — *necnon mille et quingentos ducatos pro tua
intertentione ad futurum unum annum a prima die mensis Martii nunc uenturi computandum*: so Malvezzi will receive
**4,300 ducats at once**. Beyond that, Ferdinand is writing to Gulielmo [S…] and to Argento that, if a sudden need
arises of which Malvezzi cannot warn him in time, they are to lend him up to **3,000 ducats** more in the King's
name, in one or more instalments, at a fair rate of interest to be agreed between Malvezzi and Justus.

**p. 6** (read): the lender *dare non recuset, et securus esse debeat se, ad exhibitionem chirographi tui [..]
nominatione nostra, solutione Venetiis indubitanter habiturum et accepturum esse*. Ferdinand requires that the
money be spent only on what is necessary or useful (*exponas atque impendas*). He will find **a master clockmaker**
(*magistrum horologiorum inueniendum curabimus*), whom the secretary coming with the money is to bring with him.
Then the border: *si fortasse Rustanus te interrogaret de gentibus nostris in confiniis, ad id respondere poteris
nos in eisdem confiniis non alias gentes quam quae sunt necessariae pro eorundem tuitione, tum ad coercendos [v]
compescendos latrones et uiolatores pacis, tum etiam ad conseruandam et manutenendam obedientiam a subditis nostris
nobis debitam* -- the King keeps on the frontier only the men needed to guard it, to put down robbers and breakers
of the peace, and to hold his subjects in obedience, *non aliam ob causam* (p. 7).

**p. 7** (read): the captives the Sultan has freed. *Praeterea de captiuis quos iussu Imperatoris Turcorum liberatos
esse scribis, si necesse fuerit, respondebis nobis id auditu gratissimum fuisse, nos uelle etiam uicissim libenter
curare ut, sicubi magnitudinis suae subditos captos fuisse intellexerimus et de quo apud nos conquestum fuerit, eos
liberari et dimitti faciamus, atque adeo nihil omittamus quod ad mutuam seruandae pacis sinceritatem pertinet*,
before the clear close *Et haec sunt quae in praesentiarum ad literas tuas respondenda duximus*, dated Augsburg,
23 January 1548, signed Ferdinand.

## Prior art

Malvezzi's correspondence of 1548–1552 is printed in *Austro-Turcica 1541–1552. Diplomatische Akten des
habsburgischen Gesandtschaftsverkehrs mit der Hohen Pforte im Zeitalter Süleymans des Prächtigen*, ed. Srećko M.
Džaja with Günter Weiß, prepared by Karl Nehring and Ernst D. Petritsch (Südosteuropäische Arbeiten 95, Munich
1995). Checked 21 September 2026: the edition is not online (no full or snippet view found), so this letter's own
number could not be confirmed. Its neighbours can be placed from citations: Ferdinand's instructions for Malvezzi,
24 January 1548, are cited as no. 62 (search-result summary, unverified), and Ferdinand to Justus de Argento,
Augsburg, 13 February 1548 — the secretary this letter says is bringing the money — is **no. 68, pp. 213–15**
(J. D. Tracy, "The Logic of Kleinkrieg", *Austrian History Yearbook* 52, 2021, n. 34). Malvezzi to Ferdinand,
26 Aug. 1547, is at p. 178 (Gürkan, "Representation and Self-Consciousness…", n. 43). So the 23 January 1548 letter,
if printed, sits between nos. 62 and 67 (c. pp. 190–212), presumably from the Vienna registry copy in clear. The
print could not be seen, so it neither confirms nor corrects the reading here. No decipherment of this manuscript
was found on the web, and DECODE still carries it as only partially decrypted.

## Second pass (5 Oct 2026)

Re-transcribed from the full-resolution images (`img/p1.png`, `p4.png`-`p7.png`, crops 1800 px across) every stretch
the first pass left open: p. 1 cipher lines 1-10 (the first pass had read only from *praeterea quid*, and the old
measure left lines 1-8 out), p. 4 l. 1, the *sese*/*ulciscantur* lines and the last line, scattered words on p. 5,
p. 6 l. 14 and the foot (ll. 15-26), and all of p. 7. What moved it:

- **Variant sign values the first pass had not used.** The g-like sign with a closed loop (§) is **d** in this hand
  (*ad*, *adeo*, *ad id*, *eisdem*, *debitam*); the stemmed circle with a loop at the foot is **q** before u (*de quo
  apud nos*; R386 draws its q the same way) and **r** elsewhere; the Π with feet is **g** (*gentibus*,
  *horologiorum*); P with a small circle below is **o** (*latrones*, *eorundem*, *liberatos*); the pointed cap Λ serves
  for **l** as well as f (*uidelicet*). With these p. 7 and the p. 6 foot read straight through.
- **Code words** as in R367: *Nas* = Imperator Turcorum (p. 7), *ki* = Rustanus (p. 6), *Mis* = Justus de Argento.
- **Writer's slips corrected where the context forces them** (9 words, marked {...} in `reading_full.txt`): *pacr* for
  *pacis* (p. 1), *Turcrum* (p. 4), *uiciscantur* for *ulciscantur* (i-sign for the l-sign), *ordinaria* (odd g-sign),
  *comutum* for *computum*, *factorum* for *facturum*, *expona* for *exponas* (s dropped before *atque*), *manutenea*
  for *manutenendam*, *faciam* for *faciamus*.
- First-pass guesses the images correct: p. 4 *exceptis sumptibus qui in [mittendis tabellariis]*; p. 5 *expositos*,
  *honesto*, *de quanto*, *Argento, litterarum huiusmodi exemplo* (no gap); p. 6 *accepturum*, *atque impendas* (not
  *aeque*), *magistrum horologiorum*, *si fortasse* (not *Si h[..]asse*), *de gentibus nostris* (not *designo*).

## Measure

`python measure_sense.py`. The old 0.83 (21 Sept) was a mark count over `read_A.md`/`read_B.md`: a word counted as
read unless it carried a [?] mark. It did not test whether the reading makes Latin, and it left out p. 1 ll. 1-8.
The new measure counts a plaintext word as read only when it is marked read in `reading_full.txt` and, for a non-code
word, it is a Latin word form under the shared `la` model (in the corpus vocabulary, or above the 2nd percentile of
rare corpus words) and the 9-word window around it scores above the 2nd percentile of random 9-word corpus windows.
Result, 5 Oct 2026:

| page | sense / words |
|---|---|
| p. 1 | 41/41 = 1.000 |
| p. 4 | 114/114 = 1.000 |
| p. 5 | 156/158 = 0.987 (*dabit* fails the word test though it is plain Latin; *Semaslia* unread) |
| p. 6 | 134/136 = 0.985 (a group before *nominatione*; a lone *v* between *coercendos* and *compescendos*) |
| p. 7 | 64/65 = 0.985 (a lone *v* after *debitam*) |
| **all** | **509/514 = 0.990** |

Without the 9 slip-corrected words: 500/514 = 0.973. Control: the reading with letters shuffled inside each word
scores 19/514 = 0.037 (the code words only). The old mark-count method applied to the new file gives 0.992.
