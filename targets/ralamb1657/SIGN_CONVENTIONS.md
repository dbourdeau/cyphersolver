# Sign conventions (catalogue 2258, Rålamb 1657)

Written before any key was applied. The signs are Arabic numerals in the clerk's ordinary hand; no other cipher shapes occur
in the runs read here.

- A code group is a number of one to three digits (seen: 2-108). It is copied as written, digit by digit.
- Separators: in the letter of 27 May (images 22-23) and the copy of 22 May (image 75) groups are separated by commas and
  a word ends with a point. In images 104 and 114 the clerk puts a point after every group and leaves a wider space between
  words. In the transcriptions a lone `.` token stands for the word end (comma letters) or the wider word space (point letters).
- Digit shapes to watch: the clerk's 5 has a long flag and can look like a long s; 4 is open-topped; 7 has a hooked foot;
  9 has a looped head and a tail below the line; 1 is a plain stroke, sometimes with a lead-in.
- `?` after a group: the reading of that group is doubtful (overwritten or partly hidden).
- `[gutter]`: groups lost in the binding of the volume (the line runs into the fold on the left-hand page of image 114).
- Clear words next to a run are given in `<...>` only where they help place the run; they are not part of the cipher.
- Interlinear decipherments (images 22, 23, 75) were covered while copying the numbers and are kept on separate `G` lines.
