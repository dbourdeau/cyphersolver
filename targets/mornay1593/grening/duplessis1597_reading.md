# Duplessis to Cargroy, Saumur, 19 January 1597: reading and translation

*Mémoires et correspondance de Duplessis-Mornay*, vol. 7 (Paris, 1824), letter LXVI, pp. 124-126.
Cipher words are in **bold**. Marks: `[?word]` a doubtful reading, `[...]` not read. The clear text is the 1824
print's; only the sentences that carry cipher are given. The three cipher runs of the next letter (LXVII, to his
wife, W1-W3) are another system and are not read.

## French

1. (p. 124) Le subject dont j'avois à conferer avec vous, est qu'ung de mes amis **du [?parti] [?contraire]**, assisté
   de quelques ungs, vous doibt mener **un prisonnier de qualité** pour estre conduict ici par commandement tres
   expres du roy.
2. (p. 125) Celui que dessus aura **pour signal une eguillete bleue au pourpoint ou a la mandille**; mais il sera
   besoing qu'aussitost j'en sois adverti, pour pourvoir aulx inconveniens.
3. Je n'ai oublié de faire vive instance pour vous faire entretenir vostre trouppe, **vos gens de Poitou dient** que
   vous estes **[... 1]**.
4. Voyez si vous pourrés les faire consentir que vous eussiés celle qui avoit esté accordee par mon entremise à
   **Courbejoliere**, lequel s'en est desporté, pour n'attirer **ruine sur sa maison**, et de deçà j'y tiendrai la
   main de tout mon pouvoir.
5. (pp. 125-126) J'ai esté fort aise d'avoir cogneu M. de **Monbarot**, et l'estime fort.

## English

1. The matter I had to discuss with you is that a friend of mine **of the [?opposite] [?party]**, with a few men, is
   to bring you **a prisoner of quality**, to be brought here by the king's very express command.
2. The man named above will have **as a sign a blue lace on his doublet or on his mandille** (a short loose coat);
   but I will need to be told at once, to provide against mishaps.
3. I have not failed to press hard to have your troop kept up; **your people of Poitou say** that you are **[... 1]**.
4. See whether you can bring them to agree that you have the one which had been granted through me to
   **Courbejolière**, who gave it up, so as not to bring **ruin on his house**, and from here I will do all I can.
5. I was very glad to have met M. de **Monbarot**, and think highly of him.

## Notes on the reading

- "du [?parti] [?contraire]" (C1): 105 107 24 gives "du part" exactly, and 9 98 42 75 gives "contrai". Between
  them the print has 11 where "parti" wants 5, and the passage stops at "contrai" with no "re". The group is a clear
  119 on the page image. "du" counts as read; the two words are marked doubtful.
- "[... 1]" (C5): 75 87 95 66, eight figures (a/m/y, i/u, b/n/z, a/m/y, c/o, i/u, l/x, l/x). I have no reading. The
  hole is counted as one word; that is an estimate.
- The filling e: C2, C3 and C6 have an odd number of letters and each ends in an extra 1 (e), so that the last
  group is 11: "qualite e", "mandille e", "Courbejoliere e". C4, C7 and C8 have an even number and no filler. In
  Loménie's letter of 1595 an odd last letter stands alone.
- "5. 10." is printed as two groups in both places where i/u is followed by d/p (C3 "au pourpoint", C4 "Poitou
  dient"), not as 510.
- "dient" is the period form of "disent".
- Courbejoliere and Montbarot are named in clear elsewhere in vol. 7 (three and six times in the archive.org text).
  The cipher has no t in "Monbarot".
- Every figure stands for two or three letters, so each reading is a choice among the letters the key allows. The
  choices are listed figure by figure in `duplessis1597_decryption.txt`.
