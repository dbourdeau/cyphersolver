# Sign conventions (inv. 25071 and its glossed siblings 25067, 25069)

Written before any key was applied to 25071; shapes only, no values.

- The cipher is written in Arabic figures in long runs inside the Spanish plain text. Figures are read in pairs
  from the start of each run (two-figure groups). A run starts after plain script, a comma or a point.
- Figure shapes in this hand: 1 is a short stroke, often with a dot or a hook (it can look like i or j); 4 has a
  closed or open head and a cross-bar, and a crossed 4 can look like a 7 or a t; 6 has a tall looped ascender; 0 is
  a small round loop; 9 has a long tail (like g); 5 has a flat top stroke; 2 has a flat foot.
- A group that stands alone and is written larger, sometimes underlined (72, 67 on 25071), is a code group.
- Tokens in the transcription files: `NN` one two-figure group; ` | ` a comma, point or clear gap between runs;
  `<...>` plain script copied through (read for context, not letter by letter); `=` the run breaks at the line end;
  `NN?` a group whose shape is only near the reading (the decryption prints its value in round brackets);
  `NN/MM` a group whose second figure could be read two ways (the first is used).
- Glosses (on 25067 and 25069 only) are written over the runs in a smaller hand; they were covered when the signs of
  25071 were copied.
