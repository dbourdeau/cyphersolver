# Sign conventions (HStAD D 4 Nr. 487/4, images 119-121)

Shape names only; no values.

- The cipher is written in Latin lower-case letters (the clear text around it is German Kurrent).
  Each Latin letter shape is transcribed as that letter, a-z.
- `d` covers both the plain Latin d and the looped, upright d (a d whose stem curls back to the left,
  like the letter eth). Both shapes are written `d`.
- `z` is the Latin z with a descender loop; `j` and `i` are told apart by the descender, not the dot.
- `y` and `g` are told apart by the closed bowl of `g`.
- `•` is a heavy round dot standing before a run on 119 (before `aytl...` and `amvr...`). It is not
  counted as a letter; it is kept in the transcription as `•`.
- `[abc]` letters struck through by the writer. They are kept in the transcription but are skipped
  when the key is applied (the writer struck them before going on).
- `?` an unreadable letter; `(a|b)` a letter that may be a or b (first choice first).
- A run is the unbroken stretch of cipher letters between two pieces of clear text. The run is the
  unit of the transcription: runs are numbered per image (`119.1`, `119.2`, ...).
- Overwriting on 120-121 by a second hand (key letters written above, strokes linking letters) is
  not transcribed as cipher. It is described in NOTES.md.
