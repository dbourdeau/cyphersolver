# Independent Dutch-source prior-art audit

Scope: 25 December 1623 Batavia cipher, Nationaal Archief (NA) 1.04.02 / 1080 scans 0376 and 0380. No Claude output, cipher/plaintext transcriptions, or 1079 manuscript margins used or included. Research model: GPT-6.2 Sol. This audit distinguishes a same-letter edition, a printed extract from a different part of the letter, an independently published reading of the target lines, and an actual key.

## Same-letter evidence and its limits

- **W.Ph. Coolhaas, Generale missiven, I (1960), no. III, printed pp. 129–130:** The national scholarly edition identifies the letter as Batavia, 25 December 1623, signed by Pieter de Carpentier, Frederick de Houtman, Jacob Dedel, Martinus Sonck and Jacques Specx. It cites old shelfmark 991, ff. 124–126 and a copy in the same old shelfmark, ff. 61–63. On p. 129 it flags an opening section as “deels in cijferschrift”; on p. 130 it flags a later section with the same wording. The intervening prose is a selective edition, not a reproduction of either cipher passage; the flagged passages are represented with editorial summaries/notes, not cipher transcription, decoded text, or a key. Exact official Huygens pages: [p. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html), [p. 130](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0130.html), [edition portal](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/vocgeneralemissiven). This is a firm prior description of cipher use in the same dated letter, not a publication of the target cryptogram or a cryptanalytic result.
- **Nationaal Archief, modern item-level references:** The [original December missive](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348/74911950-ab66-102e-8314-0050569c51dd) is called “Originele generale missive” and catalogued in 1079, pp./ff. 124–126; the [copy entry](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348/7490be1a-ab66-102e-8314-0050569c51dd) bundles copies of the February and December 1623 letters at 1079, pp./ff. 57–64. The [official concordance, rows 991 and 992](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/download/xml) maps old 991 to modern 1079, and old 992 to modern 1080. Coolhaas therefore does not directly cite 1080. The [1080 scan 0376 viewer](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1080/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1080_0376) and [scan 0380 viewer](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1080/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1080_0380) are separate witnesses/locators. The public index search for [generale missive 1623 1080](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348?searchTerm=generale%20missive%201623%201080) found no result, not proof that the facsimile is unrelated or absent. Cite each witness by its own identifier; do not infer cipher-line identity from the date alone.
- **J.K.J. de Jonge, De opkomst van het Nederlandsch gezag in Oost-Indië, V (1870), document V, pp. 22–24:** Coolhaas cross-refers to p. 23. The facsimile shows document V begins on printed p. 22, continues through p. 24, and contains omissions selected by the editor. The document heading and signature dating (“In ’t fort Batavia, ady 25 decemb., a°. 1623.”) agree on the 25th, despite the contents giving the 26th. The extract covers the material Coolhaas cross-references and neither supplies an actual key nor prints the two flagged cipher passages. [Facsimile of volume V](https://www.cortsfoundation.org/pdf/OPK5.pdf). This is same-letter publication, but not publication of the cipher content at issue.
- **Shaogang Cheng, De VOC en Formosa, 1624–1662: Een vergeten geschiedenis (Leiden dissertation, 1995), vol. II appendix, p. 27:** [Tonio Andrade, “Pride and Prejudice,” n. 8](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/abs/pride-and-prejudice-the-role-of-policy-and-perception-creation-in-the-chinese-revolt-of-1652-on-dutch-formosa/95CCCDA4774D80F5A0BECDFB5D4D5FA9) links that page to Coolhaas p. 129 and 1079, ff. 124–126, using “printed in Cheng, Vergeten Geschiedenis, 27”. [Andrade, “Political Spectacle and Colonial Rule,” n. 19](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/abs/political-spectacle-and-colonial-rule-the-landdag-on-dutch-taiwan-16291648/E59AE3E268E9F0459E73AD7085B026E1) and [“The Company’s Chinese Pirates,” nn. 17–18](https://historycooperative.org/journal/the-companys-chinese-pirates-how-the-dutch-east-india-company-tried-to-lead-a-coalition-of-pirates-to-war-against-china-1621-1662/) corroborate it. Cheng’s own bibliography gives the scope of the appendix in [“The Dutch Deserter Hugo Rozijn,” bibliography](https://www.ostasien-verlag.de/zeitschriften/crossroads/cr/pdf/CR_16_2017_141-160_Cheng.pdf); Leiden confirms [his 12 December 1995 defense](https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/en/staffmembers/leonard-blusse-van-oud-alblas). The Taiwanese museum identifies [the 2000 Chinese edition](https://tlvm.nmtl.gov.tw/zh/Theme/ExhibitionArticleCont?Exbid=238) as a selection of Formosa-related reports. Direct page 27 and the corresponding Chinese printed page were not openly accessible in this audit. This is a genuine same-letter extract lead, not evidence that Cheng printed either exact cipher segment or a key. Do not claim exhaustive novelty while page 27 is uninspected.

## Other editions and specialist coverage

Coen: Bescheiden omtrent zijn bedrijf in Indië (1919–1953), I–VII:** [DBNL bibliography](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_ned017197901_01/_ned017197901_01_0013.php) gives “7 dln. in 8 bdn.” Keyword and contents checks in accessible [vol. This is a catalogue and scope check rather than a full-text negative for II–VI.
- **J.E. Heeres, Dagh-register gehouden int Casteel Batavia, vol. I (1896), printed p. 1:** The [volume OCR](https://archive.org/stream/daghregistergeh01kologoog/daghregistergeh01kologoog_djvu.txt) opens with “tsedert p° January a° 1624 gepasseert” (OCR normalized), and [ANRI’s published volumes list](https://sejarah-nusantara.anri.go.id/daily_journals_volumes/) identifies it as volume 1624–1629. It cannot have a same-day 25 December 1623 entry; this does not exclude later references to earlier events. [NA Dagregisters inventory, 1.11.06.01, printed pp. 5–6](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.11.06.01/download/pdf) distinguishes early manuscript copies from published volumes. No independent cryptogram, target decipherment, or key was identified there.
- **K.M.M. de Leeuw, Cryptology and Statecraft in the Dutch Republic (2000):** [UvA repository record](https://dare.uva.nl/search?identifier=2d5163aa-e69b-4c90-8624-1bd4b5a04d0d), [full thesis](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/3074957/12760_Thesis.pdf), and [chapter 2, printed pp. 6–51](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/3074969/12766_UBA002000046_06.pdf). Chapter heading describes the work as “mainly during the 18th century” (normalized OCR). Full-text/chapter search for VOC, Oost-Indische, East India Company, Carpentier, Batavia, 1623 and Cole(n)brander found no match relevant to the target; apparent matches concern other names, Dutch politics or cipher literature. Target page and key: unavailable. This is comparative Dutch statecraft, not affirmative VOC evidence.
- **Nationaal Archief and DECODE catalogue searches:** The NA VOC item index terms [cijferschrift](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348?searchTerm=cijferschrift) and [cijfersleutel](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348?searchTerm=cijfersleutel) returned no records; [geheimschrift](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348?searchTerm=geheimschrift) yielded the separate 1674 Ramanacoil item. These are indexing negatives, not archival absence. [DECODE “VOC”](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsList?cmd=search&search=VOC) yielded Ramanacoil as the only VOC item; searches for [Carpentier](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsList?cmd=search&search=Carpentier), [Batavia](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsList?cmd=search&search=Batavia) and [1623](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsList?cmd=search&search=1623) yielded no positively identified record for the target. Broad searches constrained by text metadata and public availability do not establish that no record or key exists.

## Independently documented cipher comparisons

- **23 and 24 January 1674, VOC, Rijckloff van Goens Sr. to the Governor-General at Batavia and to the Heren XVII respectively:** Jörgen Dinnissen and Nils Kopal, “Island Ramanacoil a Bridge too Far,” HistoCrypt 2021, [paper with facsimiles, pp. 48–57](https://ecp.ep.liu.se/index.php/histocrypt/article/download/156/112/55), [conference TOC verifying pp. 48–57](https://ecp.ep.liu.se/index.php/histocrypt/issue/download/15/28), [NA item record](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348/765aa738-ab66-102e-8314-0050569c51dd), [DECODE scans, record 1564](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/1564). Cipher and archived key: NA 1.04.02 / 1292, ff. 544–563; the paper’s Fig. 1 is a facsimile of the key. Cipher model: “monoalphabetic substitution cipher” with 24 single-letter symbols, five doubled-letter symbols and seven nomenclature entries (36 key signs); a sign used was not accounted for in the key. It is 39 encrypted pages overall, and independently archived matching cleartext for 23 January is in 1298, ff. 300–340, and for 24 January in 1298, ff. 246–256. This confirms later VOC cryptography, not the 1623 key or model. [Nils Kopal’s public facsimile post](https://www.kopaldev.de/2023/12/10/from-ceylon-to-amsterdam-the-10000-km-journey-of-an-encrypted-dutch-east-india-company-voc-message-from-1674/) links official DECODE metadata and provides original key and cipher-scan images; [Guido van Meersbergen, “In goede en vertroude handen,” De Zeventiende Eeuw 27.1 (2011), fig. 2 and n. 9](https://www.academia.edu/5292064/In_goede_en_vertroude_handen_Communicatie_en_beleid_bij_de_VOC_tijdens_de_Hollandse_Oorlog_1672_1678_) independently discusses the archival item and illustrates the key.
- **1 February and 4 May 1620, Dutch diplomatic, not VOC, Cornelis Haga to the States General:** NA 1.01.02 / 6894, [inventory](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.01.02/invnr/6894); [DECODE 2113, 1 February, 3 pages](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/2113); [DECODE 2115, 4 May, 4 pages](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/2115); [DECODE 2118, separate surviving 2-page 1620 key](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/2118). Catalogue model: “Simple substitution, Nomenclatures”; recorded symbol sets are graphic signs and numerals, and the key catalogue lists over 100 entries. Direct image access requires authentication; no exact folio stated in the accessible DECODE records. These primary catalogue records establish the contemporary Dutch model and dates, not a VOC link or the 1623 key.

Bottom line: an early publication of selected same-letter material and a clear scholarly notice of cipher usage are verified. No independent published decipherment covering both exact 1080 passages, matching published 1623 key, or externally established cryptographic model for those pages was verified; the open lead most worth checking physically is Cheng II, p. 27.
