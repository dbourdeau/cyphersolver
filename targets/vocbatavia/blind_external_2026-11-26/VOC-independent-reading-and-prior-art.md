# VOC cipher letters: blind readings and prior art

26 November 2026 | Nationaal Archief, The Hague, VOC 1.04.02

The three accompanying UTF-8 text files are readings of manuscript signs, not decryptions. For the 1623 letter, the important distinction is between an early marginal explanation, an editor reporting that it exists, an edition printing it verbatim, and a publication of the key. The first two are documented: Coolhaas identified the cipher, summarized its subject matter, and explicitly reported that explanations were added in the Netherlands. His entry does not print those explanations or a key ([Generale Missiven I, p. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html); [p. 130](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0130.html)).

## How to use the blind files

- **Batavia, 1080_0376:** Eight physical rows, plus a separate left-side insertion on row 02, keyed by crosses. `M02` identifies the insertion without silently moving it into the main text; the crosses and paired horizontal strokes are transcribed rather than interpreted. `B` identifies a detached figure well below the cipher; do **not** put it into a ciphertext frequency table. The figure corresponds to the accounting amount Coolhaas printed in running prose, not to another cipher row ([original scan](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1080/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1080_0376); [Coolhaas, p. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html)).
- **Batavia, 1080_0380:** Seven rows between passages of prose. Use the dots and slashes as transcribed, not an assumed rule of one separator between each pair of groups. The last horizontal line is a pen stroke, not a string of minus signs ([original scan](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1080/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1080_0380)).
- **Hooghly, 1575_0038:** Twenty-two rows. `<over>…</over>` denotes a bar associated with a group; it does not assert that the stroke spans the full width of every digit. `<sup>…</sup>` is a raised sign; `<rule/>` is the closing horizontal stroke. Letter-like groups are retained. On row 20 the characters `32134` are not silently split into `32:134`. The catchword below the block is not a cipher row ([original scan](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0038)).

The identifier before the first tab or line-label colon is editorial. Inside a row, `[?]` qualifies the immediately preceding reading or sign; `[illeg]` records unresolvable ink, not a guessed number of missing characters. Spacing and the length of drawn rules are not to scale. Retain the diplomatic files unchanged if making a normalized version for computation.

## Independence and attribution

Only the three specified facsimiles went to the image readers. No 1079 scan, margin, Claude transcription, proposed plaintext, OCR or HTR was used in their readings. The literature researchers worked separately. Bibliographical findings were not passed to the image readers; a published description of the margins is evidence for priority, not an input to the transcription. No attempt was made to reconstruct a key.

## Batavia: what has and has not been published

On **0376**, both blind readers independently recovered the same visible body sequence and the seven-number left-side insertion. The right fold is the limit: row 03 probably ends `15`; on row 05 only the initial `4` of the final group can be defended, on row 06 no complete sign follows the slash, and on row 07 only the initial `2` survives clearly. Gemini tentatively suggested `3` on row 06 and `8` after the `2` on row 07; neither is supplied in the delivered text. Outside the cipher, Gemini read the initial loop of the accounting figure as a currency sign; Sol read a digit `6`. The delivered figure follows Sol, independently supported by Coolhaas's printed `63568.11.12`; only the separator ink remains doubtful ([scan 0376](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1080/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1080_0376); [Coolhaas, p. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html)).

On **0380**, the two diplomatic passes agree on all 127 numeral groups; they differ at two separators. Row 02 shows `28-50` (Gemini: `28.50`); row 04 ends `35.15./` (Gemini: `35.15/`). The enlarged image supports a short level stroke in row 02, and a point before the slash in row 04. Both readers gave `/3` rather than `13` after `72.22.` on row 04, and `50` at the tight right edge on row 01. Minor alternatives proposed by Gemini are preserved in its first pass ([scan 0380](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1080/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1080_0380)).

- **The precise witness matters.** Coolhaas's no. III cites old inventory 991, ff. 124–126 and a copy at ff. 61–63. This is modern **1079**, not 1080. His notes identify shipping movements near the opening, and ship cargoes later, as partly ciphered; his words include “de verklaring is in margine in patria bijgeschreven” (p. 129). Knowing **what** the concealed passages concern is different from possessing their text or number-to-meaning mappings ([Coolhaas, p. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html); [p. 130](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0130.html); [NA catalogue of the original](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348/74911950-ab66-102e-8314-0050569c51dd); [official concordance](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/beschrijving/appendices)).
- **An earlier edition of the same letter.** J.K.J. de Jonge, *De opkomst van het Nederlandsch gezag in Oost-Indië*, V (1870), document V, pp. 22–24, prints a selected passage involving the English, not the two cipher blocks or their key. The contents give the 26th, but the document itself is dated the 25th; Coolhaas directs readers to De Jonge p. 23 for the English material, not for a cipher solution ([De Jonge, vol. V, document V](https://www.cortsfoundation.org/pdf/OPK5.pdf); [Coolhaas, p. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html)).
- **Most important unfinished publication check.** Shaogang Cheng, *De VOC en Formosa, 1624–1662* (1995), vol. II, appendix, p. 27. Tonio Andrade's note 8 connects the same December letter and archive folios to that page: “printed in Cheng, Vergeten Geschiedenis, 27”. The citation is verified; **whether p. 27 prints the protected lines is [unverified]**. A full-text prior-art claim should wait for an image of p. 27 and its continuation, not merely another citation of it ([Andrade, “Pride and Prejudice,” n. 8](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/abs/pride-and-prejudice-the-role-of-policy-and-perception-creation-in-the-chinese-revolt-of-1652-on-dutch-formosa/95CCCDA4774D80F5A0BECDFB5D4D5FA9)).
- **Colenbrander/Coolhaas, *Jan Pietersz. Coen: Bescheiden*, I–VII.** Public OCR of I and both parts of VII yielded no verified matching ciphertext, decrypted passage or key. II–VI were not available for a complete public OCR check; those volumes remain **[unverified]**. The references to Coen IV, p. 29 ff., and Coen I, p. 758 ff., in the December 1623 entry are citations of other documents, not evidence that those pages print the ciphertext. No page is assigned to a negative search ([vol. I](https://archive.org/stream/janpieterszcoenb01coen/janpieterszcoenb01coen_djvu.txt); [VII/1](https://archive.org/stream/p1janpieterszcoen07coen/p1janpieterszcoen07coen_djvu.txt); [VII/2](https://archive.org/stream/p2janpieterszcoen07coen/p2janpieterszcoen07coen_djvu.txt); [Coolhaas, pp. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html) and [130](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0130.html)).
- **Batavia journal and Dutch cryptographic history.** The first printed *Dagh-register* begins 1 January 1624 (p. 1): it is not a journal witness for 25 December 1623, although retrospective references remain possible. K.M.M. de Leeuw's *Cryptology and Statecraft in the Dutch Republic* (2000), especially chapter 2, pp. 6–51, yielded no positive identification of the target key or text. Dutch-language searches on `cijferschrift`, `geheimschrift`, `cijfersleutel`, VOC and Oost-Indische Compagnie yielded the later Ramanacoil comparator, not a verified 1620s–1630s match. Unprinted or differently indexed material remains **[unverified]** ([Dagh-register, vol. I](https://archive.org/stream/daghregistergeh01kologoog/daghregistergeh01kologoog_djvu.txt); [series listing](https://sejarah-nusantara.anri.go.id/daily_journals_volumes/); [de Leeuw, chapter 2](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/3074969/12766_UBA002000046_06.pdf); [NA cipher search](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/index/nt00348?searchTerm=geheimschrift)).

For the Cheng check, seek the **1995 Leiden dissertation, vol. II**, not just the Taiwan book: Andrade specifies that the latter appeared “without the Dutch transcriptions” and cites p. 27 for this very letter. That narrows the search without assuming the Formosa passage and the cipher passage are the same ([Andrade, *How Taiwan Became Chinese*, introduction, notes 51 and 53](http://www.gutenberg-e.org/andrade/pdfs/Introduction.pdf)).

## Hooghly: what the manuscript establishes

The translated letter begins on scan 0027, f. 1589r, with the place and date, “den 11: maert 1696”. Scan 0037, f. 1594r, carries the numerical catchword `258`, repeated as the first group on scan 0038 (f. 1594v). On scan 0039, f. 1595r, the Dutch prose resumes, and the copy gives the surname Martin after the words for “was signed”; the attestation below is to the translation, **not a French autograph** ([opening, scan 0027](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0027); [preceding page, scan 0037](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0037); [cipher page](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0038); [closing, scan 0039](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0039)).

François Martin is the stronger identification, but the forename is **[unverified]** for this particular letter. The text mentions Jean-Baptiste Martin in the third person; independent French records distinguish J.-B. Martin at Surat from F. Martin at Ougly. Those are reasons for an attribution, not a substitute for a forename or a matching French original. The 11 March date belongs to the letter; the date, place and circumstances of its interception remain **[unverified]** here ([Dutch copy, scan 0036](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0036); [French references, nn. 20 and 25](https://books.openedition.org/pur/47212)).

## Hooghly: French prior art

- **Yes, there is independently catalogued cipher correspondence involving Martin.** On **10 May 1689**, Jean-Baptiste de Lagny wrote to Martin, head of the Pondichéry factory, with “instructions sur le commerce (en partie chiffrée)”: Archives nationales, **MAR/B/7/60, f. 73v**. In the same register, **f. 201**, a **12 September 1689** dispatch from Lagny to the directors at Pondichéry is also explicitly partly enciphered. These are French letters **to** Martin or his establishment, not the March 1696 letter **from** Martin. The entries do not publish his key, identify the number system, or say the keys match. The May entry is the best specific comparator for a follow-up comparison of actual signs ([FranceArchives, 1689 analytical register, ff. 73v and 201](https://francearchives.gouv.fr/fr/facomponent/01d7233706d432e7320e60bf71ac8a1bd4a266e6)).
- **Martineau's edition of the *Mémoires*.** The catalogue title says 1665–1696, but the actual title page of vol. III reads “FONDATEUR DE PONDICHÉRY (1665-1694)”. The closing chapter concerns the voyage to Bengal in January–February 1694. This rules the memoir narrative out as a publication of a letter written in March 1696; it does not rule out cipher use mentioned under an earlier date ([Martineau, vol. III, title page and chap. XXXIII, pp. 366–394](https://www.persee.fr/doc/sfhom_1961-8166_1934_edc_3_3); [BnF biographical account](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-southasia/francois-martin)).
- **ANOM, COL C² 64.** This is the best identified French correspondence volume, not an identified French exemplar of the target. There are securely cited later Ougly letters: 10 July 1696, Martin and Deslandes, **f. 143**, and the 21 November 1696 Bengal general letter, **f. 169**. The short reference “Lettre de Martin et Deslandes” belongs to July, not March. Inspect neighbouring letters, enclosures and references to duplicate packets before asserting a file-level absence; correspondence in other series also survives. No folio has been verified for the 11 March letter in C² 64 ([ANOM fonds guide](https://recherche-anom.culture.gouv.fr/archives/archives/fonds/FRANOM_00003/open:all); [OpenEdition, chapter V, nn. 17 and 85](https://books.openedition.org/pur/47208?lang=fr); [Indrani Ray, notes on the French Bengal correspondence](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001946467100800103)).
- **An independent route outside C².** The Paris foreign missions archive, AMEP 956, indexes Martin writing from Ougly, for example to Delavigne on 28 October **1695**, manuscript p. 223. Its heading about the Dutch knowing what passed in Company councils makes this a useful control for Martin's correspondence, **not** a March 1696 match. No cipher key is established by that catalogue entry ([IRFA, AMEP 956 analytical inventory](https://irfa.paris/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/AMEP_table_analytique_956.pdf)).

- **French cryptographic histories are not an identification.** Hervé Lehning, “Strasbourg, témoin de l’évolution de la cryptologie du 16e au 17e siècle,” *La Revue de la BNU* 13 (2016), pp. 46–57, §7, describes numerical dictionaries encoding “des syllabes et des mots”. The article treats other correspondents, not Martin or the Company; resemblance to a numerical system is not evidence that the March page uses the Rossignols’ Grand Chiffre ([Lehning, §7](https://journals.openedition.org/rbnu/1501?lang=en)).
- **Company practice, independently documented.** Paul Kaeppelin describes dispatches sent from India as “souvent chiffrés en temps de guerre” (1908, p. VII). This is direct published evidence of wartime encipherment, but not publication of the March letter or a key; the July report he cites on p. 330 n. 5 about Dutch ships leaving on 11 March does not date the interception ([Kaeppelin, *La Compagnie des Indes orientales et François Martin*](https://archive.org/details/lacompagniedesi00paulgoog)).

The official ANOM inventory, printed p. 12, gives a practical locator for the C² search: **COL C2 64, 1692–1698, consultation copy 203 MIOM 62**. The inventory describes a microfilmed correspondence volume, not a letter-by-letter negative or a published key ([ANOM, *Correspondance à l’arrivée*, p. 12](https://recherche-anom.culture.gouv.fr/data/files/anom.diffusion/pdf/inventaires/FRANOM_00005_all.pdf?1746169387)).

## Martin: decisions that affect reuse

Both readers give the same sequence of overbarred groups across all 22 rows. The disputed items below concern digit shapes, a possible separator or additional pen marks, not an inferred plaintext. The three especially important places to recheck against the [original cipher page](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0038) are:

| Place | Delivered reading | Evidence and alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Row 15, final group | `306[?]` | Sol saw `30` followed by an incomplete stroke; Gemini preferred `306`. The raised third stroke is compatible with `6`, but the fold hides part of it. If using only certain signs, encode `30` + unknown. |
| Row 18, group 10 | `39[?]` | Both initial passes preferred `89`; Sol's same-hand comparison shifted its reading to `39`. The comparison crop in the bundle allows either judgment to be audited. Run analyses with both. |
| Row 20, group 4 | `321[?]34` | The actual ink looks like `32`, one continuous vertical stroke, then `34`, with no visible pair of colon dots. Both readers transcribed `32134` literally. The marker flags the middle stroke's identity; it is not an instruction to insert a colon. Preserve this as one graphic group in a diplomatic count. |

Other small differences: at the end of row 07 Sol saw a faint raised stroke over `61`; it is marked doubtful. On row 14 there is an additional low dot beyond the colon after `h`. The repeated letter on rows 14–15 is provisionally `L[?]`, with `P` among the alternatives. On row 19 a small raised diagonal sits above the gap before `40`; it is preserved separately from the baseline colon. On row 21 both read `49`; a further trace at the fold is uncertain, not a confidently supplied terminal colon. None of these marks is assigned a cryptographic function ([scan 0038](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0038)).

## Priority verdict

- **Batavia:** Do not claim discovery of an unknown cipher or first historical decipherment. Coolhaas explicitly records the earlier annotations; a full printed reading of these exact passages and a matching published key remain **[unverified]**. The next publication check is Cheng II, p. 27 and its continuation, followed separately by a witness-by-witness comparison with 1079, without overwriting these blind files ([Coolhaas, p. 129](https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/retroapp/service_generalemissiven/gm_01/html/gm_1_104_0129.html); [Andrade, n. 8](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/abs/pride-and-prejudice-the-role-of-policy-and-perception-creation-in-the-chinese-revolt-of-1652-on-dutch-formosa/95CCCDA4774D80F5A0BECDFB5D4D5FA9)).
- **Hooghly:** A published text, decipherment or key for this exact cipher page was not verified. Check C² 64 for a French duplicate or enclosure before making a priority claim; compare the actual signs on MAR/B/7/60, f. 73v, before proposing any connection between the keys. These are distinct evidentiary tests ([Dutch cipher page](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/1575/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_1575_0038); [ANOM inventory, p. 12](https://recherche-anom.culture.gouv.fr/data/files/anom.diffusion/pdf/inventaires/FRANOM_00005_all.pdf?1746169387); [FranceArchives, f. 73v](https://francearchives.gouv.fr/fr/facomponent/01d7233706d432e7320e60bf71ac8a1bd4a266e6)).
