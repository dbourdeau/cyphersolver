# wackerbarth1717 - reading of the letter of 24 April 1717 (French), Wackerbarth to Flemming, Vienna

Status: read from the decipherment of the time, which renders the whole cipher. The reading below is the check of that sheet
with the rebuilt key on views 0037-0039: 193 of 207 cipher words = 93.2% under the one rule applied to the whole target at
integration (12 Nov 2026, `integration_recount.py`); 188 of 207 = 90.8% by the contributor's count.
**This reading is not blind**: we had seen the decipherment sheet before reading.

Letter no. 15 ("Monsieur") of Wackerbarth to Jacob Heinrich von Flemming, Vienna, 24 April 1717. Dresden, HStA 10026 Loc.
00713/01, ff. 29-32 (views 0037-0040). A decipherment of the time is on a separate sheet, ff. 33-33v (views 0042-0043),
headed "Le C.te de Wackerbarth du 24. d'avril 1717". **That sheet renders all of this cipher.** It begins with "vues du Roy de
Suede sur la Pologne", which is the start of the cipher on f. 29 ("les vues du ‹187› sur la Pologne"). It does not give only
the last passage. Our transcription of the sheet is `frozen/releves/A-FEUILLE-0042-0043.txt`.

**Note on Anne-Simone Rous's paper.** Her paper on the Wackerbarth - Flemming cipher correspondence lists a letter of
"24th July 1717" (Loc. 00713/01, ff. 29-33) as not deciphered. We think it is this letter of 24 April. In the volume as
digitised, the sheet at ff. 33-33v renders its cipher from the first words to the last, in agreement with our decoding by an
independent key. We mention this as a finding, and we may have missed something in her account. She knows this corpus
far better than we do.

The letter is covered by the key test (B', Relation XXII) only by extension: same system S1, same key c. Its views 0040 and
0042-0043 were used up by the first test A, which failed. Our reading of 0037-0039 is a check against the sheet, not a new
decipherment. Marks as in `reading.md`.

**188 / 207 = 90.8%.** All 7 unresolved words are nomenclator codes that the sheet of the time resolves: 187 = le roi de
Suède (4 times), 314 = Danzig, 198 = Stanislas, 364 = "10e" (the 10th), 356 = "second" (Ferdinand II). The sense agrees with
the sheet over the whole length. There are two small differences: the cipher says "au sujet du comte de Hanau" where the sheet
has "avec le Comte de Hanau", and "les sentiment" where it has "le sentiment".
