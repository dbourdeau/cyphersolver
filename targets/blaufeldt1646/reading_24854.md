# Reading of inv. 24854 (writer signing in cipher "Blaufeldt" to Ottavio Piccolomini, [February?] 1646)

Status: **read in part**. The letter is short: about five lines of mixed cipher and plain Italian, the courtesy close
in plain script, the cipher signature, and a cipher date line at the foot. Counts are printed by
`scripts/reading.py check` (see NOTES.md).

Image: Vlnas 2017, appendix zip P_lohy_A_a_B.zip (theses.cz/id/p3p2kb), frame 24854.jpg (photograph of a
microfilm-reader screen). Key: `key_frozen.tsv`. Mechanical decryption: `decryption_24854.txt`.

Notes on the marks:
- L1 `nott{[?50]/e}`: the group after the barred 19 (tt) reads 50 on both passes; 50 is not glossed on the sibling
  letters. "notte" needs e; 50 may be an e homophone (the table has homophones in the 40-80 range, see key.tsv) or
  a code group (for instance "io") with the e left out. Both are open.
- L1 `[?partito=?ar???o]`: seven groups; only the 2nd, 3rd and last (a r ... o) are settled on the image. "partito"
  (left) fits the count and the sense with "di Giessen"; it is a guess, counted as a hole.
- L1 `G{[?70]/i}essen`: 70 is unglossed; i is what the name needs. The bar over 74 (s) doubles it (ss). The same
  name, spelt with 24^ (ss), is glossed "Gissen" on 24852_1 and ends the date line L8 here with known groups.
- L2 `{[?86]/b}uona`: 86 is unglossed (or a misread 31 = b on a weak photograph); "buona risolutione" is the
  phrase of 24852 ("con buona risolutione"). The third group of L2 is the crossed 44 (n), as on 24853.
- L3 `g{[?73]/r}an`: 73 is unglossed (or a misread 23 = r).
- L4 `d{[?60]/i}ffi{r/c}olt{?a}`: 60 is unglossed (i needed); 16 under a bar = ff; the 5th group reads 23 (r) where
  "difficolta" needs c (32 or the homophone 52): a misreading of the photograph or a slip of the writer; the last
  group is doubtful between 30 and 80.
- L4 `hav{[?80]/u}to`: 80 is unglossed (u needed; 10 = u stands before it).
- L4-L5 "resolver il negotio": the signs give "resoluer" + "ilnegotio"; no letter is added.
- L5 plain script `[?ogni] [?fatica]`: not certain on the photograph. The blind reviewer (12 Nov 2026) saw the last
  word at 6x as more like "[d]ifficolta" than "fatica" and could not settle the word before it; the sense is close
  either way. These are plain words, not cipher words, and do not enter the word count.
- L8: the first group is under an ink blot (a day number or a word); the groups after "feb" are not settled; the
  line ends "in Giessen" in known groups. "febraro" is a guess from the sibling 24852 ("alli cinque del febraro
  1646").
