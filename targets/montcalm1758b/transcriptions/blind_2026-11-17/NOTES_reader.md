# Reader notes, Montcalm to Bougainville, Montreal 21 Nov 1758 (image/page.jpg)

Reader: Claude Opus 5.5 (subagent), 17 Nov 2026. Blind transcription, no decoding.
Method: whole page viewed, then 3x crops of each figure line in three overlapping pieces (crops/R*, crops/B*), read twice from two different crop sets; doubtful groups re-read on 5x crops (crops/Z_*).

Layout: 8 figure lines in two paragraphs (P1 = 4 lines, 55 groups; P2 = 4 lines, 57 groups; 112 groups in all). Groups separated by dots. A trailing dash at the end of P1L1, P2L2 and P2L3 is a line-filler, not a figure. In P1L2 a short dash joins 568-657; read as a separator.

Underlining: one continuous rule under P2L2 "435+ 368 637 449" and a separate rule under the first group of P2L3, "38" (the underlined passage seems to run across the line break).

"+" marks: 434+ (P1L1, P1L3, P2L4), 435+ (P1L2, P2L2), 433+ (P1L3, P1L4, P2L2), 568+ (P1L4, P2L2). In P1L4 the + after 568 and 433 stands after the dot, as a cross-like sign; recorded as 568+ and 433+.

## Digit forms of this hand
- 1: plain upright or slanted stroke.
- 2: small looped head with a flat foot (like a cursive "ɛ" closed at the base); see 293, 427, 240.
- 3: flat-topped, Ʒ-like, often with a heavy dot at the lower end.
- 4: open, ℒ-like: tall slanted ascender, small loop at the base (74, 448, 240). Easy to confuse with 6 at speed, but the 6 is a closed round bowl with a curled top.
- 5: two forms: a hooked, "h"/"§"-like 5 (511, 591, 35) and a printed 5 (568, 588).
- 6: rounded bowl with curling top stroke.
- 7: ")"-like with a hooked top and long descender (74, 427, 37).
- 8: two stacked loops, sometimes with a dot in the middle (588).
- 9: small loop with a long descending tail (589, 39, 519).
- 0: small round o.
Clear prose numbers for comparison: date "21 9bre 1758", "du 8. et du 11." in line 1 of the letter.

## Doubtful groups
- P1L1 42|48: second digit has a looped head and a closed foot; it matches the 2 of P1L2 "42" more than the two-loop 8, but the lower loop is not open.
- P1L3 340|360: middle digit at a glance looks like 6; on the 5x crop it is the same open ℒ form as in 240 right after it, so 4.
- P2L4 262|263: last digit has a tall looped stroke rising from the 6 and a heavy blotted foot; reads as 2 but a 3 is not excluded.
- P2L2 "1 18": a dot stands between the 1 and the 18 (1.18.); read as two groups, but 118 is possible if the dot is a pen rest.
- P1L1 666: third digit has a rather closed top; read 6, slight chance of 8 (not marked in figures.txt).
