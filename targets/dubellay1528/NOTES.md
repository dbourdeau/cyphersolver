# Jean du Bellay (London) to Montmorency, 28 October 1528 — BnF fr. 3077 no. 23, pp. 145–147 (DECODE R4254; copy Clair. 328 f. 291, R2287)

Split on 14 Oct 2026 from `dubellay` under the one-key rule (George Lasry, 14 Oct 2026). Catalogue item 4 joined
this letter with du Bellay's letters of 1529, which use a different cipher ("Bayonne's cipher (1529)"); those keep
the old entry: [`targets/dubellay/`](../dubellay/NOTES.md), page `dubellay.html` ("a different cipher, with its own
page"). This entry is du Bellay's *first* cipher, of 1528.

**Outcome: read from existing decipherment.** Bourrilly and de Vaissière print the whole letter in clear, the
cipher passages in italics, with a key they recovered themselves (*Ambassades en Angleterre de Jean du Bellay*,
1905, no. 156, pp. 430–434). George Lasry solved the same cipher again in 2022 (Tomokiyo, Cryptiana GL.htm, "Bayonne's
Cipher (1528-1529)"). Nothing about the text is added here; no key was applied. Work: 17 Sept 2026 (identification,
leaf seen at Gallica), 9 Oct 2026 (DECODE records), 14 Oct 2026 (split, DECODE images, Bourrilly's note read).

  143–145); DECODE R4254 ("Non-decrypted", 12 images: three pages and crops). A copy is BnF Clairambault 328 f. 291
  (DECODE R2287, 5 images). L&P IV 4879; Le Grand III 188–192 (Bourrilly's source note).
- Images fetched 14 Oct 2026 with the shared DECODE cookie; git-ignored, in the main checkout's
  `targets/dubellay/img/decode/` (`IMG_R4254_I25496_P1` … `P12`, `IMG_R2287_I16207_P1` … `I16211_P5`).
- Opens in clear: "Monseigneur, je croy que me pensez si peu paresseux es affaires du Roy …" (the money du Bellay
  cannot get, Montpezat's coming, Campeggio's reception by Henry VIII, the two legates with Queen Catherine). The
  cipher runs, about fifteen lines on p. 146, concern the Emperor's peace offers and the Queen's view of the divorce.

## The cipher (from Bourrilly's note 6, p. 432)

"Ce chiffre est le premier employé par Du Bellay": in the editors' material only this letter and one of 28 May 1529
use it. They recovered its lost key but could not identify with certainty the persons meant by some signs that
occur once. The ciphered text is broken at intervals by groups of meaningless signs or by uncoded phrases on other
business, each enclosed between two fixed marks that act as brackets cancelling their content. Their key is not
printed. Lasry's key (Tomokiyo, GL.htm) was solved on Clair. 329 f. 9, a letter annotated as Bayonne's, and,
Tomokiyo writes, "the same key also fits Clair. 328, f.291" (this letter's copy). Tomokiyo notes that it differs from
the cipher of Bayonne's 1529 letters (Clair. 329 f. 139); that 1529 cipher is the one in `targets/dubellay/`.

## What the cipher says (Bourrilly's text)

After a clear sentence on the Emperor's two envoys with peace offers (one by land, one by sea), the cipher runs,
read together with the clear words set between them, say that du Bellay does not believe peace will come through them if the marriage (the divorce and remarriage) goes ahead: he has it from a good place that the
Queen said privately that, if it does, the Emperor will never stop until he has ruined the King of England, even if
he has to give the King of France back his children on reasonable terms for it. He then suggests something might
be worked up through the Duke of Gueldres or Margaret of Austria, and warns against trusting the Pope.
