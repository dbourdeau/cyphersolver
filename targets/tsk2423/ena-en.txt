Translation and commentary on the surviving groups

1 — Two groups of three cipher signs, each with its Hebrew-letter gloss. Plain-script Ehyeh follows on the same baseline.
2 — Two repeated groups. The first occurrence is overwritten. Provisional consonants only.
3 — Provisional sequence. The stand-alone bet could finish something lost at the end of the preceding line.
4 — The first sequence is uncertain, especially its middle letters. No translation assigned.
5–6 — Divine address and an affirmation, “you have no partner.” Each slash marks a new manuscript line.
7–8 — “By the power of this name … [I charge you] to answer my request …” The second-person plural addresses the executors of the spell.
9–10 — “My need … and [that] you cause ophthalmia … and sever the lids of his eyes …” The causative verb independently explains the title. The object of that verb falls beyond the cut.
11–12 — A relative clause and “there be for him …”. The lost end of line 11 could have contained a negation governing line 12. The word קרא may anticipate the fully written קראר on line 14. A continuous clause would require restoration.
13–14 — “Nor in the daytime, nor … / nor rest [qarār], by the power of this …”. The final resh of קראר is present on line 14. The gap after line 13 precludes supplying its paired time expression.
15 — Three visible urgency words, with al-ʿajal between two instances of al-waḥā. The left edge cuts off any continuation.
verso, two lines beside the diagram, read after rotation — Provisional reading: “the incense: sandarac and sal ammoniac / and wild costus …”. The diagram and note are on the opposite face from the eye-disease heading. The final short group and the note's connection to any particular recipe are unresolved.
Reverse, separate cipher inscription (Bohak, p. 15) — … iron … written [on the] day … . The surviving words leave the day unspecified.
