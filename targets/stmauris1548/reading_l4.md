## English

(9r) There is talk of some [?Swiss]; however I do not know it for certain, and it will be that the infantry stays in this place by the command of the said lord king with other ambassadors, through which I [...] that use be made of your [...] while they are in Piedmont, where he can easily go, since the ambassador of Venice, with whom he was daily, follows the court.

They say here that they will prevent the passage of monseigneur our prince; but I hold, moreover, that they could not do it, that they would not undertake it, but will live in good friendship. Even so, one must not trust them too much, and must avoid surprises of towns, and Your Excellency should be on guard for your person. It is said that this journey is made to stand up to monseigneur our prince, and to give heart to those of French sympathy in Italy and keep them from despair.

And that [...] thus to advise; with whom they have here negotiated a defensive league, and already to furnish money on both sides, which they may wish to use next year [...].

## Notes

- "monsieur nostre prince": Prince Philip, whose passage from Spain through Italy (autumn 1548) the French were said to want to hinder.
- "ce voiage": Henri II's journey to Piedmont, July-August 1548, the subject of the clear text above the cipher.
- "ligue deffensive": apparently a negotiation of the French with another power; the partner is lost in the hole. Not verified.
- "surprinses de villes" and "aller d'austant que" read since `Le` (l + c, ll) was split from `Lo` (l + o, nn).
- `{x/?}` etc.: signs first seen in this letter, or split after the test, have no frozen value; their letters are repairs.
- All historical notes here are checked only in outline and must be verified before publication.
