English: Now that the [Ambassadors] are leaving, people here begin to spin a softer thread. Yet it is done so that nobody is meant to notice, for business is carried on as if one feared nobody in the world. But you know that those who have served their apprenticeship laugh at tricks of this kind. I hold as certain the [351: rupture or war?] between [France] and England, and that up to now England has no friend but the Bishop of Munster. So you will understand what [reason] they have here to play the knowing one, even though all may go as the King of England wishes inside the [kingdom], as may be hoped. But if things went badly, I do not know whether one defeat would not be enough to put the malcontents under arms, chiefly the Scots, who in general are very [151].

More troops are already being sent to Guernsey and Jersey, which lie nearer France than England. I imagine the recall of the [French king's ambassadors] may delay it further if it does not break it off entirely, for I imagine [the King of Denmark] will want to tell [the Most Christian King] of his steps; his ambassadors leave fully persuaded that [the King of Denmark] is a most affectionate friend of their [master], but that he needs a sum of money.
