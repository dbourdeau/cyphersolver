## 2. Notes per line

Recurring non-anchor shapes, named once here and referred to below:

- **tail sign (ϙ/ρ)**: a small "c" or "9"/"5"-shaped head with a long straight tail running down to the lower left,
  well below the baseline. Open head = koppa-like (ϙ first); closed loop head = rho-like (ρ first).
- **angle sign (κ/ζ)**: a large "<" (two strokes meeting in a point on the left), in L02 attached to an upright stem
  (then it looks like a "k"); in L03 and L08 without stem. Read as a kappa with or without its stem; ζ as the
  alternative for a zigzag.
- **h sign (η/ν)** and **N sign (ν/η)**: two uprights joined by a short diagonal. With the tall upright on the left
  and a hump on the right it is "h" (η first). With the tall upright on the right it is a mirrored "N"/"4" (ν first).
  They may be one sign.
- **hook-dash (ι/ل)**: a short hook or short upright whose foot runs left as a long flat baseline dash. Not an anchor
  shape. Either a iota with a joining stroke or an Arabic tooth/lam with a lengthened join.
- **ح-shape (ح/ε)**: a small hook or "S"/"3" head sitting on a baseline stroke that runs on to the left, like
  Arabic initial ḥāʾ. Distinct from S (no tall stem). ε given as the Greek alternative (a reversed cursive epsilon).
- **big slanted loop sign (D/φ)**: a very thick stroke leaning far to the right with a long narrow loop at the foot,
  twice the size of the ordinary D. By the anchor definition it is a D; flagged because of size and the narrow loop.

### L01

  Not enough for a sign.

- `G`: bold "v".
- `S/D`: tall, nearly straight stem with a small bowl at the foot and a foot stroke that runs a little to the left of
