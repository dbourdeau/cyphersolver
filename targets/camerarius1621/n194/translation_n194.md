Most noble Count, my obedient and most willing service and best diligence first. Gracious Lord. By the last post I
[sent?] Your Grace, through Herr von Dohna, a letter. I await [?...] with longing, and especially to hear more of
Prince Maurice after his raid into Brabant, which went off so well; and that something may be attempted [?towards the
end] of this year.

How things now stand in ⟨England⟩, and what is to be expected from there, above all from the ⟨translation⟩ [of the
electorate] and from ⟨Brussels⟩: I am much afraid when I observe the strange ⟨proceedings⟩ of the ⟨King of England⟩.

From the Palatinate there is nothing certain here, and letters come slowly. From Kassel and Hanau it has been written
that Count Mansfeld beat the Bavarians near Ellwangen [?...]; I cannot well understand that, ratione loci, unless a
Bavarian relief force had come down from above.

From Duke Adolph I have this sound information: that [?his] horsemen are mustering in the [?Franconian] Circle, and
that the [?Imperial] [conjecture: governor] Prince Liechtenstein has been ordered to pay them a month's wages, with the addition that
His Majesty will then send a commissary and have the horsemen led into the Upper Palatinate. But since it [?...] and
the duke's [?resignation] and commission, we must wait and see how he behaves.

⟨Camerarius⟩ has word through the councillors of the ⟨Archbishopric⟩ and of ⟨Bremen⟩ that he [the archbishop] is not
at ease, because he furthered the said ⟨recruiting⟩ and now has the ill report, and from the ⟨Emperor⟩ the thanks,
for having granted the ⟨Count of Oldenburg⟩ [?...] ⟨[?1513]⟩. For this reason the said ⟨Archbishopric⟩ has summoned
[?its] friends to ⟨Hamburg⟩; what was decided there I shall learn by the next post.

The diet in this ⟨Archbishopric⟩ is over and, God be praised, through ⟨Camerarius⟩'s good groundwork it came to this:
the ⟨estates⟩ have refused three times over the [?intolerable] [?levy] and triple aid that the ⟨Archbishop⟩ pressed so
hard; and so that the ⟨Archbishopric⟩'s ⟨envoys⟩ at the coming ⟨circle diet⟩ of the ⟨Lower Saxon Circle⟩ [?...] in
this point and others, they have attached to them two of their own number.

And since the ⟨Emperor⟩'s ⟨envoys⟩ who are to come to ⟨Lüneburg⟩ demand a heavy ⟨contribution⟩ from the ⟨Lower Saxon
Circle⟩, they [the estates] are to grant it [?not] otherwise, in eventu [?...], but simply on this condition: that
first there be peace in the Empire and the Elector Palatine be restored.

This is no small thing, for the said ⟨Archbishopric⟩ has on its side and as neighbours the ⟨Dukes of Mecklenburg⟩,
⟨Holstein⟩ and ⟨Lauenburg⟩. The ⟨towns⟩, which cannot be brought to any outlay anyway, side with them; and especially
those in ⟨Thuringia⟩ will complain bitterly that ⟨Electoral Saxony⟩ has quartered his troops on their necks and in
their villages.

⟨Camerarius⟩ hopes his letters sent to the ⟨Duke of Brunswick⟩ and the ⟨Duke of Lüneburg⟩ will also have good effect,
and meanwhile he does not neglect the correspondence he has, and so will await the outcome of the said ⟨circle diet⟩,
hoping that nothing adverse will follow, if only God [...] gives good success; that would do much. For these people
go only by fortune, look only to prudence, and have so much to do with themselves that they will not heed the public
good for [?...] their private interest.

The ⟨King of Denmark⟩'s ⟨envoy⟩ has now been with ⟨Electoral Saxony⟩; I am trying to obtain his dispatch, which would
throw much light, though I fear little advantage will come of it, as also from the ⟨legation⟩ to ⟨Vienna⟩. For the
⟨King of Denmark⟩ will not act manfully either; he is too deep in private matters, has now obtained the bishopric of
Osnabrück in expectancy, and they are now bargaining over Verden, where Lüneburg may raise a strong obstacle. The
⟨King⟩ is to be in Holstein in a few days, but it is thought he will not stay long, because in June the
⟨[?Reichstag, i.e. the Norwegian assembly]⟩ will be held in ⟨Norway⟩, which he must attend in ⟨person⟩.

With ⟨A[ndreas] Paul⟩'s coming here, [?I fear] it will be in vain, if only the ⟨[?King of Bohemia]⟩ writes to the ⟨Lower
Saxon Circle⟩ and others, violentia zuerhalten [conjecture: to keep off violence]. (The gloss over the code reads
"K. i. B."; if it were "K. in D." the writer would be the King of Denmark.) Duke Christian of Brunswick [?...] now far out, has obtained from the
Eichsfeld 100,000 Reichstaler, [?the] passage, and free fodder and meals, some [?...]; he has also got 100,000 thalers in the
land of Brunswick from the Landdrosten, for which, however, there is no [slip] ground. It were to be wished that, if
[slip] to secure, the [?motus] belli [slip] in [slip], whereby the ⟨Upper Palatinate⟩ [?with longing] [slip]. Although ⟨[?Bethlen
Gabor]⟩ has nothing certain in these parts.

If Herr ⟨Grand Master⟩ [conjecture: i.e. Solms himself] as well as ⟨[?von] Dohna⟩ [conjecture: go] out, and [?...], I beg most earnestly to
be told. Should he [conjecture: go] to [?...], I might well go along, for I have a great longing to see the few still at
⟨Heilbronn⟩ in perpetuo metu & angustiis. The matter cannot [?be sent] via ⟨Nuremberg⟩ because the roads are unsafe.

⟨Camerarius⟩ has written several times to the ⟨King of Bohemia⟩ but gets no answer; if Your Grace would [?send] this, or
an extract, to His Majesty in Your letter, that is at Your pleasure. To Herr von Dohna I do not write now, as I know
no more, and Your Grace can also inform him from this.

Commending Your Grace most faithfully to God's protection, and myself humbly to Your lasting favour. ⟨Bremen⟩, 24 May /
3 June 1622. Your Grace's most humble obedient servant.
