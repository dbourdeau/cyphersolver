## f. 298r

From the letter [?of Suardino], dated [?at Vitoria], 26 February.

[...] [?the lord Chancellor], when I had told him all the above things, was very pleased to hear them, and His
Majesty [?(the state ?)] of [?the Marquis]; telling me how [...] still ours very glad, and that he found himself as [?well
served] as by anyone else who had done him service [?in Italy], and that he was always ready, whenever occasions
arose, to work for his benefit with good will; and now, news of the death [?of Signor Prospero] having arrived
by this last (post), although by [?His Lordship] [...] I had been answered in the matter of [?Carpi], as by my other
(letter) I wrote many days ago, I thought it right to raise it again, saying that by His Lordship I was told that
[?the Emperor] [?had] given [?to Signor Prospero] Carpi [?for him] and for the heirs; but that he had not [...] made the
investiture; and because news of the death had now arrived, [?the Emperor] might wish to draw from it some [?sum] of [?money] in this need of [?Italy], and then compensate the heirs [?of
Signor Prospero] with some [?other] thing. And whenever this messenger (came) to His Lordship, he would know what I
had said to him in past days; and it would be reasonable that [?the Marquis] be preferred to anyone else; for which reason I
ask, as a favour of His Lordship, that he be willing to lend his support in this business with [?the Emperor] whenever I
should speak to him, as I had a commission to speak of it. [?From] the said lord [?Chancellor]

## f. 298v

I was answered, as he had told me in past days, what had been settled: namely that [?the Emperor] had given Carpi [?to
Signor Prospero] and to his heirs, and this would not change [?because of his death], even though the investiture
had not been made. True, it had not yet been determined whether Carpi had been taken away by right or not; and if it
were found that it had not, it might be that something else would then be done with it at the pleasure of [?someone].

## Notes

- Victorie (doubt): the key gives `uf[n]fctorie`; the court was at Vitoria from 5 January to 6 March 1524, which
  makes the place plausible but does not settle the signs. Suardini rests on q = a, a value found in this word only.
- The top stroke marks the probable Emperor and the right stroke the probable Marquis. Their exact titles and the syntax after the second mention of the death remain uncertain. [All six signs and both occurrences of *inteso*](audit/recheck-2026-11-17.md)
- Prospero Colonna died at Milan in late December 1523. The grant of Carpi to him without investiture is already in
  Petrucci's life of Colonna (DBI 27, 1982).
