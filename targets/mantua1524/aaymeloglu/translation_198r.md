Translation of the running text in `reading_198r.md`, with the same holes. The leaf is clear text except for two
cipher lines (lines 17-18: "se retirerà questo exercito de fiume in fiume, et poi in Pavia, Cremona et Milano,
secondo dice Barbon"). English inside `[? ...]` translates words that are `[?word]` in the Italian: a doubtful clear
reading, or a cipher proposal from a forced or lenient segment (a hole in the count). Words in round brackets are
supplied for the English only. "hoi" is read as h[omin]i.

## f. 198r

From the letter of Master Cappino, dated at Robbio, 8 April 1524.

Today the enemy camp went into Novara; all the while our light horse, and above all those of the magnificent Paulo
with his own person, were at their tail. The rearguard was 2,000 Swiss; very few of their horse appeared, and those
that did appear were beaten by fifteen [?or so] [?men-at-arms] and archers, and were taken. I do not know what
prisoners the magnificent Paulo has, for I have not seen him this evening, although he came to find me after he had
come back from the skirmish. I had gone to meet messer Ricardo, the English orator, who was to arrive this evening
according to the reports we had, and he has not come. If there were a way to keep up the war, I believe that now it
would begin. The enemy have gone into Novara, in the judgement of men who have judgement of war, not in order to flee
so easily but to wait for their relief, of which these lords have no other report that it has set out except what I
have given them [?from Mantua] (and) [?from Marin]; but they believe that I mean the Grisons [?captains] who are
coming from Bergamo, and they think that these Swiss will not arrive within these fifteen days. At [?its] arrival
the truce of Spain will not have come first, through the negotiation of the Archbishop of Capua; (then) [?this army]
will withdraw from river to river, and then into Pavia, Cremona and Milan, according to what [?Bourbon] says.

The enemy have also abandoned Mortara today, according to what a light horseman of l Zuccaro reports, and two
Neapolitan prisoners were taken, who will go to die in the galleys, if that is what the Lord Viceroy has ordered.
Yesterday morning at dawn, by the news that has just come, Signor Giovanni de' Medici took the bridge [?of Bia] by
force, and 200 men died on the one side and the other; and having taken it he has broken it and has gone [?to Bia].
Now as I write, 12 men-at-arms are brought in from below, whom the Spaniards took at the inn of the Trecca. Tomorrow
we leave here; we go to within 4 miles of Novara, on the side [?of Verze].

## Notes

- The cipher lines are a conditional, prospective retreat inside a clause about the truce, not a report of an
  itinerary followed; "questo exercito" most naturally means the writer's own force, against the enemy in Novara.
- The cipher gives Cremona sign for sign (`7 8 2 e+ 9+ x 9`, all frequent signs).
- Nicolaus von Schonberg, Archbishop of Capua, was negotiating with Charles V in April 1524. Bourbon served the
  Emperor in this campaign.
