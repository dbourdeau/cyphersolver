## What this suggests

- The note is about persons ("the aforementioned") and a payment ("to each ... pounds"), so the coded groups may carry
  names, ranks or sums, not the letters of running prose. Only 26 distinct values in 119 groups fits either a letter
  substitution or a short code; the clear words do not decide between them.
- Two clear words sit in the middle of coded rows ("each", "as previously written") and four groups stand inside
  closing line 1 (38 75 34 49): the groups next to "did not give them" are the best crib candidates, as figures or
  names of the men in question.
- The phrase "as previously written" (row 5) says the coded part repeats or refers to an earlier letter or telegram,
  which is the thing to look for in the Wingate Papers (SAD.250/1/528-606) and in the four "Arabic cipher letters" of
  Nov 1884.
