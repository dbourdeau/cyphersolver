# Sign conventions, catalogue 1500 (Varela to the Consul General of Paraguay in Rio de Janeiro, Asunción, 13 Feb 1850)

The cipher signs are Arabic numerals written in the same hand and ink as the clear letter, set inside clear
Spanish sentences. Shape names only; no values here.

- A cipher run is a sequence of numbers with a dot (or a short dash) after each one: `9.13.1.37`. A run may be
  broken at a line end; the break is written ` / ` in the transcription.
- Digits as the hand makes them:
  - `1`: a single stroke with a small hook at the top and often a short foot; it can look like a `4` at low
    zoom. Told apart from `4` by the missing closed bowl.
  - `3`: flat top (like a Z with a curved lower bowl).
  - `4`: open cursive four with a loop on the left; the stroke from the left often runs on into the next digit.
  - `5`: a bowl with a flag on top, like a small `б`; sometimes the flag stands well above the line.
  - `7`: crossed seven (a bar through the stem).
  - `9`: round closed loop with a straight tail.
  - `8`: two loops (only in `98`).
  - `6`: only in `116`.
- Single numbers that stand alone inside clear text (`25`, `1`, `116`, and `98` inside the last run) are given as
  they stand; they are recorded as separate items N01-N05.
- One non-numeral sign: a circle with a vertical bar through it and a short cross-bar at the top (a barred circle,
  like a Greek phi with a cross). Written `PHI` in the transcription (item S01).
- Doubtful digits are written with the alternatives in the pass files (`43|49`); the reconciled file gives the
  settled reading and a comment on the line.
