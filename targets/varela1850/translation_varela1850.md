# Translation of catalogue 1500 (Benito Varela to the Consul General of Paraguay in Rio de Janeiro, Asunción, 13 February 1850)

English translation of `reading_varela1850.md`, with the same holes. Words that stand in cipher in the letter are in
italics. [25], [1], [116], [98]: lone numbers of the letter, not identified. [...]: cipher not read.

Long live the Republic of Paraguay! Independence or death.

Asunción, 13 February 1850, year 41 of liberty, 40 of the explicit recognition of independence by the Government of
Buenos Aires, and 38 of national Independence.

Ministry of Foreign Relations of the Republic of Paraguay. To the Most Illustrious Consul General of the Republic at
the Court of Rio de Janeiro.

The undersigned has received the order of His Excellency the President of the Republic to acknowledge to Your
Lordship receipt of your notes of 17 October and 21 November last, with the newspapers enclosed with them; that he
has taken note of the news you were good enough to pass on to him; and above all of the English *contract* [...]
Montevideo [...], and that that one received the *pesos*.

His Excellency was anxious about the letter of 12 November last that Your Lordship announced to him in the above
letter of the 21st, and at last Hopkins arrived, from whom he received it with the newspapers mentioned in it. His
Excellency does not understand in what sense the news of the concentration that Your Lordship reports will please
[25], unless [25] wishes to dream of the *occupation* of [Rio] Grande, which he must not think of.

His Excellency has seen in the printed papers the business of *Olinda* with [1]. It does not deserve to be spoken
of. It is not the first perfidy of that [?ayer] towards you. What is strange is that *Paulino* should consider
himself embarrassed by the *declaration* of *Olinda* to [1] that [...] (the barred-circle sign), as to the *occupation* [?of the
Province] *of* [Rio] Grande, would be [116]. One must recognise that he is tainted with the *ministerial* perfidy
[98] *of* [...].

On this occasion the undersigned has the honour to greet Your Lordship with distinguished consideration and
esteem. God keep Your Lordship many years. Benito Varela.

Notes. The cipher gives only "Olinda" and "Paulino", and the clear text only "Hopkins". Identified by the
contributor as: Olinda, very likely Pedro de Araújo Lima, Viscount (later Marquis) of Olinda, Brazilian foreign
minister until late 1849; Paulino, very likely Paulino José Soares de Sousa, who followed him in that office;
Hopkins, very likely Edward A. Hopkins, the United States agent in Paraguay. These are identifications from context,
not from the cipher. The lone numbers [1] and [25] could stand for persons (for example the Argentine envoy in Rio, or Rosas);
nothing in the letter settles them, so they stay open.
