# Joel Barlow to the Duke of Bassano, Paris, 25 October 1812: reading

### NARA RG 59, Despatches from US Ministers to France, vol. 13, microfilm M34 roll 16: copy A on frames 0240-0241, copy B on frames 0246-0247 (both enclosed to Monroe). Code: 46 numbers in copy A, 47 in copy B, in two sentences. Files: transcription_1812-10-25_A.txt, transcription_1812-10-25_B.txt, collation_copyB.md, decryption_1812-10-25_A.txt, decryption_1812-10-25_B.txt, key.tsv. No decipherment on the film; an extract without the coded sentences is printed in American State Papers, Foreign Relations 3:604.

Barlow's last note to the French foreign minister before he set out for Wilna, where the Duke of Bassano had
invited him to finish the negotiation. He accepts, says the treaty of commerce and the convention of indemnities
are ready, and warns that the President will take a rejection as a refusal of justice. The note Bassano received
was presumably in clear. In the two copies sent home the sharpest words are in code: that the project would cost the imperial treasury little or nothing, and that the President could see in a rejection
nothing but a determined refusal of justice by the Emperor, which would embarrass the American government in its
dealings with England as well as France. Barlow died on the road back from Wilna on 26 December 1812.

The clear text is copy A, in full; copy B differs from it in about 24 small places (collation_copyB.md). The
coded words follow copy B in three places where copy A is short: copy A has no "of" before "justice", spells
"justice" with a number that has no value, and writes "Imperial" before "Majesty" without its third
syllable (im/per/l). "Treasury" follows copy A, which alone has its third syllable (copy B writes tre/as/y). In
"regard" the last syllable is one number in copy A and a different one in copy B, and neither has a value. Each
case is set out in NOTES.md, section 6. It is English, so there is no translation.

## Marks

The clear text is plain. Words written in code are in italics. A word in square brackets after a question mark is
one where a code number has no value in any decipherment of the time and we supply it from the sense
(proposals.tsv): it is a proposal. In wel{+l} the key gives "wel" and we add the second l. Stops and capitals inside the coded words are ours. The numbers and the value
each one gives, without any repair, are in the decryption files, line by line.

#### Example: *[?determined]* ([706]/er/min/ed: 706 has no value in the key).

## Reading

Paris 25th October 1812.

Sir,

In consequence of the two last letters you did me the honor to write me on the 11th of this month, I accept your
invitation and leave Paris tomorrow for Wilna, where I hope to arrive in 15 or 18 days from this date. My
Secretary of Legation and one servant will compose all my suite. I mention this to answer to your extreme goodness
in asking the question and your kind offer of finding me a convenient lodging. I hope the trouble you will give
yourself in this will be as little as possible.

The negotiation on which you have done me the honor to invite me at Wilna is so completely prepared in all its
parts between the Duke of Dalberg and myself and, as I understand, sent on to you for your approbation about the
18th of the present month, that I am persuaded, if it could have arrived before the date of your letter, the
necessity of this meeting would not have existed, as I am confident that His Majesty would have found the project
reasonable and acceptable in all its parts and would have ordered that Minister to conclude and sign both the
Treaty of Commerce and the Convention of Indemnities.

From a private letter of the President of the United States communicated by me to the Duke of Dalberg, the
principal points of which he has doubtless imparted to Your Excellency, and from other circumstances well known to
you, you will perceive the urgent necessity of all possible dispatch in this business, as well for the interest of
France as for that of my country.

I have been extremely mortified and my Government greatly surprised that it has not been finished long ago. And
if the present project (whose execution would really cost *[?little] or nothing to the imperial treasury* and by
which it would immediately gain great sums and secure a permanent and increasing gain) should be rejected, the
President can see in it nothing but a *[?determined] refusal of justice on the part of his Imperial Majesty,*
which must greatly embarrass the Government in its operations *in [?regard] to England as wel{+l} as France.*

I cannot press this subject too seriously on Your Excellency's attention, and I beg you would have the goodness
to prepare the business before my arrival that it may be concluded with as much expedition as its extreme urgency
demands.

I write this in such haste as leaves me no time to measure my expressions or to multiply the arguments that
naturally grow out of the subject. I beg Your Excellency to perceive in it an additional proof of my honest zeal
to promote the harmony and best interests of the two countries.

Receive etc. (signed) J. Barlow

His Excellency the Duke of Bassano, Minister of Foreign Relations etc.
