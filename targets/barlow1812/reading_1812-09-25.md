# Joel Barlow to James Monroe, Paris, 25 September 1812: reading

### NARA RG 59, Despatches from US Ministers to France, vol. 13, microfilm M34 roll 16, frame 0232 (right page) and frame 0233. Code: 64 numbers in one paragraph. Files: transcription_1812-09-25.txt, decryption_1812-09-25.txt, key.tsv. No decipherment on the film; not found in print (print check of 21 Nov 2026, NOTES.md section 8).

A short letter in a secretary's hand, signed by Barlow, the American minister in Paris, to the Secretary of State.
It acknowledges Monroe's letters and reports on the negotiation with France. Only the middle paragraph is in code:
there Barlow says that indemnities for past spoliations (the American claims for ships and cargoes seized by
France) are at last under discussion, that the men about the Emperor have been won over, and that the treaty will
follow once the form of the indemnity is settled. Barlow left Paris for Wilna a month later to conclude it and
died on the road back, on 26 December 1812; nothing was signed.

The whole letter is given. It is English, so there is no translation.

## Marks

Barlow's clear text is plain. Words written in code are in italics. A word in square brackets after a question
mark is one where a code number has no value in any decipherment of the time and we supply it from the sense
(proposals.tsv): it is a proposal. A bare number in square brackets is a code number with no value at all.
Letters in curly brackets with a plus sign are given by a number that is blotted or miswritten on the film and
that we read from the word: {+s}poliations (the group is blotted), {+exp}ected (written like 1553), mod{+e}
(blotted). *[?the]* before United States stands for a number written like 1780 where "the" is 1700: a whole word
cannot be confirmed by its neighbours, so it is marked as a proposal. Stops
and capitals inside the coded words are ours, except the full stop after "opinion", which is in the code (177).
The numbers and the value each one gives, without any repair, are in the decryption file, line by line.

#### Examples: *[?indemnities]* (in/dem/[815]/es: 815 has no value in the key), *[163]th* (163 unread), *{+exp}ected* (a miswritten number).

## Reading

Paris 25th September 1812.

Sir,

I have lately received your letters of the 3d and 14th July, and still more lately the one of the 23d April. But
those of the 21st March and the 6th May, alluded to in some of these, I have not received. This is to be
regretted, as it seems they were charged with documents relating to subjects on which I have had nothing
official.

The great object of *[?indemnities] for past {+s}poliations* is under discussion and is *[?making] [?progress]* on a
footing more *favorable than* I ever *{+exp}ected.* The views in which the principle is presented in my *note of the
[163]th June* have convinced every person in the *imperial confidence here*; and their arguments aided by the
attitude now taken by *[?the] United States* will probably bring the *Emperor* to their *opinion. His decision* is
expected in a few days, and my *hopes in a result* acceptable, if not satisfactory, are now *stronger than* they
have been. The *Treaty* will suffer no difficulty when once the *mod{+e} of [?indemnity]* is agreed upon. The
observations contained in your letter of the 23d April will be attended to, and your ideas probably all adopted.

The particular cases recommended in your several letters have been most of them taken care of before, and none
of them shall be neglected.

I have the honor to be, Sir, with great respect, your obedient servant

J. Barlow

Honorable James Monroe, Secretary of State

(Copy) I have given to different American ships the eight commissions of letters of marque you sent me, and
could very well place as many more if I had them.
