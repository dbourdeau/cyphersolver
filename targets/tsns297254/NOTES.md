# T-S NS 297.254 · A hooked sign in a daily account

**Status: open.** The Princeton Geniza Project transcribes the sign as لى and tentatively expands it as *addā* (أدّى, “paid”). This approximately twelfth-century Arabic bifolio uses Greek/Coptic numerals and names the officials Qayyim al-Dawla and ʿAfīf al-Dawla. The question is whether the hook marks a payment. [PGP 24835, catalogue and both faces](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/24835/)

## Transcription doubts

In the body of the letter, a separate silver entry may read من الورق ماية خمسة وستين درهم […], provisionally “of silver, 165 dirhams […]”. The unit word appears to be خمسة (five). The figure needs an independent reading. No surviving line has been identified that explains the two-dirham difference. [Silver line](https://images.lib.cam.ac.uk/iiif/MS-TS-MISC-00028-00232-000-00002.jp2/pct:2,22,88,13/1800,/0/default.jpg)

## Comparisons

A similar sign occurs on an instruction beginning يدفع الشيخ (“the shaykh is to pay”) and on a receipt for rent already collected. The instruction could have been marked after payment. Neither document dates the act of marking. [Payment instruction](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/42497/) · [Rent receipt](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/42179/)

In the T-S AS 178 series, comparison of 19 slips with OpenCV feature matching and visual inspection identified mirrored writing from .138 recto on .140 verso, and from .140 recto on .139 verso. Compare the reflected central writing and the upright at its right. The hook on .138 was present when the sheets touched. This dates the mark relative to physical contact only. [Source face .138r](https://images.lib.cam.ac.uk/iiif/MS-TS-AS-00178-00138-000-00001.jp2/full/1000,/0/default.jpg) · [Reflect .140v for comparison](https://images.lib.cam.ac.uk/iiif/MS-TS-AS-00178-00140-000-00002.jp2/full/1000,/0/default.jpg)

## Remaining gaps

- **Expansion.** Neither ledger occurrence has a written gloss or an adjacent amount. Retain لى as the diplomatic representation. A parallel ledger alternating the hook with an explicit verb in the same position would help test *addā*.
- **Accounting function.** The photographs establish different positions in the ledger and letter. The same function in both has yet to be demonstrated.

Codex assisted with image inspection, catalogue searches, and the receipt comparison. The proposed expansion and the annotated sum of 167 come from PGP.
