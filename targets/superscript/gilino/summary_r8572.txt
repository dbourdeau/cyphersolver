R8572 - "el Gilino" to Francesco II Sforza, Duke of Milan (at Cremona), Paredes de Nava, 15 September 1527
BL Cotton Vesp. C IV ff.214-218. Deciphered with DECODE R5258 (ASMi Carteggio Sforzesco cart. 1591 no. 24) plus
context values. About 96% of the significant cipher tokens read as sense (reading_r8572.txt). The cipher text runs
P1 > P2 > P7 > P3 > P5 > P4 > P6 > P8 > P9 (folios 214r-218r); the DECODE image order is not the folio order.

CONTENT (cipher passages; the clear passages are in r8572_clear.txt)

1. Gattinara out of favour (214r-v). Gilino has been told "by many" that the Grand Chancellor (Gattinara, just landed
   at Barcelona, per the clear text) stands in poor favour with the Emperor because of two of his letters,
   intercepted, to the Viceroy of Naples: they asked the Viceroy to send the Naples galleys to the relief of Genoa
   and told Lope de Soria to spend what was left of the 100,000 ducats of the exchange on the city, for cash and
   for victuals, or all would be lost. His detractors, now in the open, are the Emperor's confessor and the
   secretary "Lalemant" (Jean Lalemand). They claim that on this information Lautrec sent part of his troops, meant
   for elsewhere, to the siege of Genoa, and that the city was therefore not defended. Gilino thinks the
   Chancellor will stay in favour, given the Emperor's need of him. He urges the Duke to write to "the Bilia" to
   keep on good terms with Gattinara; since Bourbon's death only Gattinara's good offices are left to hope for.
   Gilino will ride three days to meet him, and the confederate (League) ambassadors will not take it amiss.
2. The Portuguese ambassador and the Pope (215r-215v). The new Portuguese ambassador did not come mainly on the
   Pope's behalf, as the Datary wrote to the nuncio. The Pope asked him to carry a brief giving him powers to
   negotiate with the Emperor, who would then free the Pope and restore him to the friendship of the Emperor and to
   the old state of the Church. The Emperor did not do this, and the ambassador is thought "a light man" in Italy
   and in Spain. He leaves court tomorrow for Portugal. He brought a letter in the Pope's own hand to the nuncio,
   with credence for Saporito(?), the Pope's chamberlain, who died at Genoa. The letter is wisely written and urges
   the nuncio not to fail in his duty. There are also two briefs to the Emperor asking him to take the Church under
   his protection and not crush it.
3. The Abbatino and the Pope's negotiation (215v-216r). The Abbatino (an agent) returns with all he asked for. On
   many points the Emperor has referred him to the Viceroy of Naples, who knows Italy better, and asked that "the
   said marquis" be kept content. The agreement ("concerto") with the Emperor has gone ahead, although "he" (the
   Pope?) had been negotiating elsewhere through the cavaliere Casale (Gregorio Casale, the English agent). The
   Emperor heard of this agreement through the Duke of Ferrara's advice and the nuncio's work. Gilino thinks the
   marquis, bound to the Florentines, will not side fully with the Emperor, but that Paolo Luzzasco will go over to
   the imperial service with 300 light horse. The Abbatino leaves for Italy in all haste, and Gilino has written by
   him under cover of the Marchioness of Mantua.
4. Milan in the peace talks (216v). The French and English ambassadors (clear text: Francis I's letters of 29 Aug
   announcing Portofino, the surrender of Genoa and hope of Alessandria; peace on the basis of the Treaty of Madrid)
   have sworn to Gilino that Francis I insists the Duke stay in the state of Milan. The Venetian ambassador and
   Gilino asked the papal nuncio to go to Palencia while the French ambassadors are there and find out what they
   negotiate, above all about the Duke. He has gone, and Gilino trusts him out of regard for the Duke, for Italy
   and for his friendship with the Venetian ambassador.
5. Genoa, the Pope's liberation, Ferrara and the Colonna (217r). On hearing of the surrender of Genoa the Emperor
   sent a new dispatch to "Monsignore di Milia(o)", not yet embarked for Rome. Gilino is told the Emperor is
   revoking the Pope's liberation because of the news from Lombardy. He blames the Ferrarese ambassadors and the
   Colonna agents, who call the Pope a traitor, a false pope, a bastard and a poisoner. Ludovico Catti is to send
   off Troilo Negrono. The Emperor promises favours to the Colonna. The Lombard difficulty causes fear that one
   ruin (Lombardy) will bring the other, the Kingdom of Naples.
6. Andrea da Burgo's advice; the Infante for Milan (217r-218r). Letters from Andrea da Burgo to the Emperor and
   (Alfonso de) Valdés are full of counsel "pro opprimenda Italia" (for crushing Italy) and of plans to keep the
   state of Milan for the Emperor. In the end it was decided that the Infante (Ferdinand?) should hold and defend
   it. The Emperor has sent orders for the Infante to come down to Italy, so that all Italy is a prey to the
   Germans. The Emperor shows more spirit than ever, and the confessor(?) has left. The loss of Genoa spurred the
   Emperor to war. By three couriers he has sent the investiture of the state of Milan to the Infante, to come down
   and defend it, with 100,000 ducats. The loss of Genoa had been held up by the lord of Monaco, whom the Emperor
   pays to stay loyal.
   Clear passages also report: the Emperor sold the Count of Caiazzo's state in the Kingdom of Naples for 28,000
   ducats to the son of the Count of Monteleone; the bishop of Tarbes gave Gilino copies of intercepted letters;
   Marco Pio is well disposed.

SIGNATURE: "Bilia" vs "Gilino"
- The cipher text names "il Bilia" as a third person. In P2.16 (f.214v) Gilino urges the Duke "che ordini per sue
  letre al Bilia che se intertengh[a] bene con esso" (to order the Bilia by his letters to keep on good terms with
  Gattinara). The writer is therefore not Bilia: R8572 is by Gilino, who signs in clear "Servo el Gilino", with "G"
  and "1527 15 Sept paredos Gilino" in the margin.
- R5287 (cart. 1591 no. 53, 7 Nov 1525) lists "Cavallero billia" A28 and "Ghilino" A29 as two people. Neither A.28
  nor A.29 occurs in R8572, and no A-series name code does (only A.30-A.33, all letters). The R5258 sheet itself has
  a nomenclator entry "Ore Veneto" written over a struck name read as "Gilimo/Gilino" (A.73). A.73 is also in the
  sheet's null row and does not occur in R8572.
- So R5258 ("Cum equite Bilie / extracta ex cifris") is a key worked out from the Bilia correspondence, and Gilino
  used the same cipher, probably as Bilia's colleague at the imperial court. The key fits Gilino's letter (96%) but
  is not his own. The docket name describes where the key came from, not who wrote R8572.
