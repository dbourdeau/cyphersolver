## Translation

⟨Most Excellent Lord:⟩

I [received] what Your Highness commanded me to [write] in [cipher] concerning the negotiations of **King Don [Ferrando]** *[Ferrante I of Naples]* regarding the affairs of **[Roussillon]** and Cerdagne; and had I [known] more particulars than those I [wrote] in [cipher] about those negotiations and secret understandings, I would not have failed to [write] them, whomever they [might concern]. [But] that [was] [written] [by] me and to my secretary Ariño, and in the manner in which it was written I made [it] known to Your Highness; and since Your Highness says that it is true, if you know anything for certain, do [not] fail to punish it for anything, according as you best judge will [fulfill] your service.

And [as] [to] what concerns King Don Ferrando, in summary I will repeat the reply I [gave] here [to] his [ambassador] and to the [Queen] my [sister] [?fiyees], saying: that it [pleased] me that the said King should act as mediator of the peace and [concord] with the King of [France], and that [the] [said] [counties] of [Roussillon] and Cerdagne should pass into his custody [under] [two] conditions: first, that before all else the intention and sum which the King of [France] [claims] is owed to him must be ascertained and declared; and second, that once [ascertained], before taking custody of the counties of [Roussillon] and Cerdagne he must give valid security that whenever Your Highness or I pay him the [verified] sum, he will restore the said counties to us. And I added (*subiungia*) that of [this] Your Highness would know nothing, yet if King Don Ferrando did it in this way, I [would work] for it and believed Your Highness would be content. And I told him further: that through other channels I was negotiating an accord with the King of [France], and that for the one negotiation I ought [not] [to] drop the other, [save] to take whichever [suited] me best. From this reply it does not [seem] to me that any [harm] can follow, nor does it seem to me that I [ought] to refrain from answering in this manner — especially since I [knew] that he had, as he still has, his own dealings with the King of [France] through the persons of whom I wrote; and that if the business were [agreed] between them, it was worth more that it be done in the [aforesaid] manner with our [consent] than otherwise [without] our [knowledge] and against our [will] — [which] Your Highness knows I cannot [prevent] him from doing.

And [as] to what Your Highness [writes] to me concerning the lieutenancy of the Infante, I kiss your hands and most humbly beseech you that you [be pleased] to have it carried forward thus, for without any [doubt] what best fulfills your estate and [service] will thereby be accomplished.

[As] to the affair of Modica (*Camodica*), I do not know why Your Highness has [ordered] the Count [of] Cardona to return from Sardinia to Sicily; for since he had entered into that [negotiation] of [Sardinia] and left behind that of Sicily, it would have seemed better to me, under pretext of the affairs of [Sardinia] (*so color de los fechos de [Cerdenya]*), to order him to come to {oo?} rather than make him go back to Sicily. And in [?nebe] the coming to that of the [Castellan], I do not know what good delaying it until we [meet] can [do]. Yet believing that Your Highness knows what best [fulfills] your estate and service, I am pleased to conform to your judgment, having seen [in] the letters [which the] Count of Cardona wrote to [Your] [Highness] concerning his arrival in [Sardinia], and likewise those of the Viceroy of [Sardinia], and [how] Mossén Pachs and Micer Vilanova [acted] on their own toward the said Count. I do not know [whether] Your Highness does not [wish] the affairs of Don Leonardo to be settled by [way] of an accord solely so as not to displease [the] [Viceroy] — [who], if it [suits] him [well], will strike his own bargain or [marriage] with the said Don Leonardo: I am informed that talks are underway to marry the eldest son of Don [Leonardo] to the daughter of the said Viceroy, and [then] he will deem the talk of an accord good, and the two of them would remain lords of [that] kingdom.

Truly, Most [Excellent] Lord, I cannot [bear] with patience that Your Highness, out of the private passions of some of your subjects, should wish to persecute others, and that those men for [whom] Your Highness puts your own estate and [consequently] mine in [danger] [should have] such [presumption], or should not [allow] Your Highness or me [to] [refuse] any terms of their adversaries, while they make their own [bargains] as [it may] seem good [?be] to them, and expect us to accept them as good. I have spoken my mind; let Your Highness consider and determine, [?Your] [?Highness], whatever you best [judge] fulfills your [service], for with that I shall conform.

Of Your Royal Majesty, the humble and obedient son who kisses your royal hands, The King. J. Ariño, secretary. [Verso docket: Cipher of the Lord King of Castile, 1478. ## Historical & Textual Notes

- **Date and court context (Seville, 9 June 1478):** Written 21 days before the birth of Prince Juan of Asturias (30 June 1478) by King Ferdinand II (styled *Rey de Castilla* in the verso docket) to his 79-year-old father King John II of Aragon (who turned 80 on 29 June 1478 and died in January 1479), countersigned **`J. Arinyo secr.`** (the cipher and subscription supply only the initial `J.`, commonly identified in historiography with royal secretary Juan/Gaspar de Ariño).
- **Roussillon and Cerdagne escrow plan (`L01–L33`):** King **Don Ferrando** is **Ferrante I of Naples**, married in September 1477 to Ferdinand's sister **Queen Juana of Aragon** (*la Reyna mi hermana*). The dispatch reveals Ferrante's proposal to act as third-party escrow holder (*intervenidor*) of French-occupied **Roussillon and Cerdagne** while auditing **Louis XI's** debt claims, while Ferdinand simultaneously pursued direct Franco–Castilian talks that led to the Treaty of Saint-Jean-de-Luz (9 October 1478). On `L21`, imperfect/pluperfect *subiungia* permits either 1st or 3rd person; the surrounding first-person report favors *"I added"* (García Larragan's 16 July 2026 reading takes 3rd-person *él* / *añadía*).
- **Lieutenancy of the Infante (`L33–L36`):** Refers to **Enrique de Aragón y Pimentel** (*el Infante Fortuna*), nephew of John II. Because the cipher writes only *lugartinencia del Infante* without naming the territory, and Enrique held the lieutenancy of Valencia in 1478 before being appointed Lieutenant General of Catalonia in 1479, no territorial gloss is imposed on the text itself.
- **Sardinia, Sicily, and Don Leonardo de Alagón (`L36–L63`):** *Camodica* is contextually associated with the **County of Modica** in Sicily. The **Count of Cardona** is **Joan Ramon Folch III de Cardona**; his presence at Trapani, Sicily, 37 days later is attested by Cat. `#2855` (`AHPZ C_CRAR/000001/0026`, 16 July 1478), though whether that journey directly obeyed this specific recall order requires further archival evidence. **Don Leonardo** is **Leonardo de Alagón**, Marquis of Oristano in Sardinia (defeated at Macomer on 19 May 1478), locked in conflict with Viceroy **Nicolás Carroz de Arborea**. The cipher gives the names **`mossen Pachs`** and **`Micer Uilanoua`** directly (`L46`); their identification as royal commissioners rests on external historical context, and the truncated verb stem `fizi-` (`[fiziessen]` vs live alternative `[fizieron]`) remains uncertain. On `L58–59`, *escusar partido* is rendered here as *"refuse any terms"* (an alternative interpretation is *"avoid/bypass an agreement"*). On `L61`, the smudged second address `[?uuestra] [?alteza]` is kept uncertain (García Larragan reads *lo que*).
- **Open nomenclator `{oo?}` (`L40`):** Appears once (*"mandarle uenir a `{oo?}` que fazerle boluer a Sicilia"*) and remains open; García Larragan (16 July 2026) proposes *aquí*, while *acá* or a Crown of Aragon court destination remain unverified possibilities.
