# rucellai1482: what the cipher passages say (read in part)

Bernardo Rucellai, Florentine orator in Milan, to Lionardo Spina in Pisa (Lorenzo de' Medici's cover address), June-September 1482, during the War of Ferrara. Only the cipher runs are read here; each letter also has clear words between them. **Partial key, not validated: these are partial readings, with the doubts marked in reading/.** Words in braces are probable, ‹…› unresolved.

**196 (3 July 1482).** About Ascanio Sforza and his conditions: "le {conditione} sue", "{Ascanio} fu confinato", "e confini suo" (Ascanio, Ludovico's brother, had just escaped from his confinement at Naples and was in Rome). A place: "quando {era a} Castelnuovo"; "per … in {Parma}"; "el conte {Giovanni} … {Bologna} … el {marchese} di {Monferrato}"; "dentro e fuora"; "{morbo}" (plague?); "noi dubitiamo non …".
*About Ascanio's conditions and his confinement; Castelnuovo, Parma, a Count Giovanni, Bologna and the Marquis of Montferrat are named.*

**200 (19 July 1482).** "Luigi, uomo del duca {di} {Calabria}, {venuto} {per} altra …"; "onore {Ascanio}"; "guerra"; "[che] duca {di} {Calabria}"; "questo {Luigi}"; "se non per {forza}".
*A Luigi, a man of the Duke of Calabria (Alfonso of Aragon), came on other business; Ascanio's honour; the war; "unless by force".*

**202 (July 1482).** "Monferrato" (twice), "di {costoro}", "{contenti}", "{ridurre}", "a {sospetta}", "Francia", "Ascanio".
*Montferrat, France and Ascanio are named; people are (dis)contented; something is to be brought back (ridurre).*

**206 (4 September 1482).** "da Gienova non … fa per non …"; "a dire [che] non avevano più … [che] non per loro {reputatione}"; "… genti di {Monferrato}"; "costoro {sono} male contenti"; "si tiene … fatto del … da Ferrarese".
*From Genoa; they no longer had …, not for their reputation; the troops of Montferrat; these men are discontented; something done by the Ferrarese side.* The letter opens in clear on the marquisate ("Languisco del marchesato …", earlier reading).

**211 (June 1482).** "segni", "notare", "[che] scrivere", "{quanto} a {fatto}", "{questa}", "lettere pare [che] {arguisca}", "[che] {presupposto}", "dal canto nostro", "con questi …", "con {piacere}", "a questa …", "{essere} in errore", "pure … voluto".
*Negotiating language: signs to note, letters that seem to show, a premise, "for our part", "with pleasure", "to be in error".*

**207 (late August or September 1482; key pair).** Its contemporary decipherment 208 is the source; the key renders it as in reading/lxxii207.md: Ludovico ("Il S. Lo") was at first glad of Ascanio's return, then, seeing that he did not come freely and had not broken with the Venetians, changed his face; he told Bernardo he had to think of his own affairs.

Historical points (secondary sources: DBI, M. Pellegrini, "Sforza, Ascanio Maria", 2018; R. M. Comanducci, "Rucellai, Bernardo", 2017; Warwick index of Lorenzo's Lettere): Ascanio left Naples about 12 June 1482 and was in Rome on 20 June; the Otto wrote to Rucellai on 27 June that he had fled there; he went on to Venice and came back to Milan on 17 September. Alfonso, Duke of Calabria, led the League's war against the Pope in Latium that summer. These agree with the readings but do not prove the key.
