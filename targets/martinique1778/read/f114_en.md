I was to sail [...] with the Amphion and some frigates and troops to capture a convoy that the English were assembling at Tortola [...]. I am prepared for every eventuality: I have had time to put all the king's colonies in a state of defence; they are perfectly supplied with munitions of war and provisions, having no less than six months' food; I have sent reinforcements of troops and munitions of every kind to all of them. You will judge from the state of the garrisons that I send you that, apart from Martinique, where only two thousand regular troops remain, but for which I have [no] fear, they all have sufficient and proportionate garrisons. Grenada alone would have needed two or three hundred more men, and to make up for them I have sent the ship the Experiment, which is of no use here and whose troops and crew can join the garrison in case of siege.

Guadeloupe, attacked by ten thousand men, must [hold out] at least two [...] three months. [Two ships, two frigates and a cor]vette are to have left Saint-Pierre today to cruise to windward at the latitude of Barbados until [...]. Even if I do not manage to carry a relief of a thousand to twelve hundred men there, which seems easy to me and which should save it from being taken. Dominica should hold as long as Grenada, except that it would be easy for me to throw relief into the first and almost [impossible] to get any into the second. Saint Christopher can only be taken by famine and [has] six months' provisions in the fortress. The small islands of Saint Vincent and [...] require a siege, a long and murderous operation in these countries; but I cannot [expect] to save them if they are attacked, without running the risk of greatly compromising the relief that I wish to carry there, which would not be of great [...].

[In answer to your despatch of 10 October and to inform you of] the dispositions I have made [for the defence]: two ships, two frigates and a corvette are to have left Saint-Pierre today to cruise to windward at the latitude of Barbados until the twentieth of this [month], in order to intercept enemy vessels that may be separated from the convoy of [...] and [...] expected from New England, laden with troops and munitions of war. At that time two frigates will cruise to windward of [...], off Saint Giles point; they will communicate, by means of one or two small intermediate vessels, with that island, where I shall send word to the vicomte d'Arnot, who commands there, of the news I shall receive that may concern the squadron and the convoy. As for the two ships, they will continue their cruise until the twentieth or twenty-fifth of January or until they meet the squadron, which they will join on the order of the French officer commanding it. I keep the Amphion and some frigates here for use as circumstances require; I shall send a frigate commanded by an intelligent officer to Spanish Trinidad, and I shall write to the governor, a man of great merit in whom one can have every confidence, as well as all the [measures] for the safety of the convoy's landfall and every means suitable to inform the French officer commanding the escort. [...] must assemble [...] so that I decide to send to Spanish Trinidad the vessels I intend to carry M. de Vaudreuil's despatches and the advice for M. de Solano, asking the governor of that island to find them a [pilot] and the means of fulfilling their mission. You will judge from the state of the troops of the [islands] that the force intended to defend them is fifteen men weaker than it was at this time last year, since when two islands have been brought under the king's rule, Saint Christopher and Saint Eustatius, which require a garrison of sixteen men between the two [...]; the king may be [...] on his colonies [...] and may be assured [that I] shall do all that men can do. If I am not employed in the operations that are to take place at [...] and if, after the arrival and passage of the sea and land forces in these seas, I find myself in the position of having [nothing] to attack or to defend, which is very likely, my presence here becomes useless.
