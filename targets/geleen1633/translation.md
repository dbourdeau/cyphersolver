# English: summary of the sense (certain and probable parts)

A word-for-word translation is not given: about 12% of the cipher words are unresolved, most of them lost in the binding.

Hildesheim. The two Electors share Geleen’s view: everything must be done to relieve Hildesheim, still besieged, and keep it from further violence. They have advised Major-General Bönninghausen — although the enemy is strong, Kniphausen having joined the Hessians — and will do all they can to send Geleen as soon as possible at least a thousand horse, to carry out the relief more conveniently. They ask him to weigh what the loss, for want of troops, of so considerable and so near a town would mean.

Money. The three thousand reichsthalers paid in Cologne should by now have reached him by bill of exchange; a much larger sum (“zehnere”, tenfold?) is being raised, counted out to Lerchenfeld (Maximilian of Bavaria’s envoy at Cologne) and meant for the army. Any indispensable expenses Geleen advances will be repaid without fail once he names them. This page has the most gaps: its line ends are in the binding.

The League. Meanwhile he is to spare nothing for the common good, with the valour he has shown so far: his merits and faithful services will be rewarded by the Electors and by “all the united Catholic Estates” (den sambtlichen unirten catholischen bundsstanden), that is the League, with real gratitude.

Hamburg. In clear, the Electors thank Geleen for passing on what the Hamburg correspondent Menselius (in cipher) sent him; and since Menselius complains bitterly about a Jew held by Geleen (in cipher), Geleen is to see that this merchant and Menselius himself are fully satisfied. Menselius may be the imperial commissioner resident in Hamburg mentioned by Aitzema (not checked).
