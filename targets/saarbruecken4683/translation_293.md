# Translation: copy of a letter received on 23 Jan 1674 (fol. 293r-v, first 41 lines)

Holes as in reading_293.md. The copy repeats, number for number, the letter fol. 279r-281r, whose decipherment of the time is in the file: this translation is not a new text. The German is a long chancery period and the translation follows its sense, not its syntax.

I received in all humility Your Gracious Highness's most gracious command of 18 December and at once [...]; the continued godless proceedings against [His Grace] at Saarbrücken are seen with {grief}, and the {need} [...] is to be attended to in the proper places. Since everyone everywhere feels not only compassion but also [alarm], as Prince Schwartzenberg too was alarmed [...], yet nobody can yet find the fittest way to meet it, although all now advise to complain in good time to the Empress and the Empire of this evil action [and] bring it forward. Though it seems dangerous, and a still harsher treatment of the high person there and of the lands is to be feared, the prejudice there, if one {kept silent}, would be still greater. And since I [could] get no other reason for this proceeding than that the {demanded} revers was not given, [...] the origin and text of that demand without cipher of the Metz matter [...] Fürstenberg [...]. If it is thought good to bring this before the arbitration [at Cologne], it is still to be seen in what state it may now be, since its members [327] and [330] behave with partiality in those [...], and these {favoured} the Emperor's party, and [314] would regard the arbitration all the less; but if [303] [...] would no longer hold to the party, then not much more was to be hoped, so it could do no harm [...] in particular [...].
