# saarbruecken4683: captivity of Count Gustav Adolf of Nassau-Saarbrücken, 1673-74 (Landesarchiv Saarland, Nassau-Saarbrücken II Nr. 4683)

Status: no write-up (found read from an existing decipherment: fol. 290r-v carries the interlinear decipherment of the time, and fol. 293 is a copy, number for number, of the glossed letter fol. 279r-281r, so its text is known from that gloss; nothing new read. The work added here is the key rebuild (systems A and H). Outcome method: read from existing decipherment; decided by the owner, 27 Nov 2026, recorded at the integration of PR #1008 on 28 Nov 2026).

## Document
Landesarchiv Saarland (Saarbrücken), Bestand Nassau-Saarbrücken II, Nr. 4683: a file of 344 leaves (finding aid) on the arrest of Count Gustav Adolf of Nassau-Saarbrücken (1632-1677). French troops under the Marquis de Rochefort occupied Saarbrücken on 2 Dec 1673 (Köllner 1841, p. 348). The count would not give a written revers not to act against the King. Rochefort rode off to Nancy and left the brigadier de Pilloy as commandant, with a written order to take the count prisoner to Metz if he did not submit (Klageschrift of Countess Eleonora Clara, 2/12 Mar 1674, ed. Mitteilungen des Historischen Vereins für die Saargegend 9, 1909, pp. 271-279; Ruppersberg 1910, p. 133). Pilloy had the count arrested: on 11 Dec 1673 in the evening and taken away the next morning according to the town court record quoted by Köllner (p. 348), on the 10th and taken away on the morning of the 11th according to Ruppersberg (p. 133); neither says whether the date is old or new style. He was taken directly to Metz (Klageschrift; Ruppersberg p. 133); Köllner's record and the ADB (E. Joachim, ADB 10, 1879, pp. 187-188) have him go by Nancy, probably a confusion with his steward von Rüdesheim, who was taken to Nancy. At Metz he could send no letter the commandant had not read first (Klageschrift), a likely background for the cipher in his relatives' correspondence. He was released through the mediation of the Swedish plenipotentiary Count Tott (Ruppersberg p. 136) and came back from Metz on 13 May 1674 (Köllner p. 348; Ruppersberg p. 137). Our first note said no source names Rochefort, our second that Rochefort had him arrested: the arrest was made by Pilloy on Rochefort's written order. The clear text of the copy fol. 293r, like the gloss of fol. 279v, says he had not given a "zugemutheten Revers", which agrees with Ruppersberg and the Klageschrift. The file is the correspondence of his elder brother, Count Johann Ludwig of Nassau-Ottweiler (1625-1690), with relatives, agents and the Nassau houses (Usingen, Idstein, Weilburg) from December 1673 to March 1674. 47 double pages (fol. 159v-340r) were photographed by the archive and sent on 26 Nov 2026; the inventory of what each shows is in the pull request's working notes. Credit line required by the archive for any reproduction: "Landesarchiv Saarland, Nassau-Saarbrücken II Nr. 4683, fol. ...". No image is committed; their SHA-256 are in `frozen/IMAGES-SHA256SUMS.txt`.

## System and key (`key.tsv`)
- **System A** (all cipher passages of the file except fol. 290): numbers 40-199 are letters, on a table of 24 cells repeated every 24 numbers: letter = table[n mod 24], table from residue 0 = `z n b s e c s d h l i p k t w u m q a i g o r f` (so e = 52, 76, 100, 124, 148, 172; n = 49, 73, 97, 121, 145, 169). Residue 6 is voted s by the glosses; x would be expected, it is rare. Numbers from 200 up are a nomenclator: 50 values recovered from the glosses (236/248/260 Graf, 241/253/265 Kaiser, 266/278 König, 290 Frankreich, 284/626 Reich, 282/306 Memorial, 389/401 Schreiben, 622 Saarbrück, 345 Maintz, 334 Trier, 332/344/356/368 Köln, 552 Hocher, 745 Fürstenberg, 807 Montecuculi ...). The numbers are often written without a stop but split without ambiguity (a group starting with 1 has three digits, 4-9 two).
- **System H** (the letter from The Hague, fol. 290r-v): a = 1, b = 2, ... (i 9, k 10, q 16, u/v 20, w 21, x 23, z 24), with codes 70 England and 180 Prince of Orange (glossed). 22 occurs three times, always after e (keyserlichen, beystehen, verzeyhen): most likely y, not a second i (a conjecture on three instances; key.tsv unchanged). 15 is p, but the glosses read it n in nicht, sondern, genereux; 23 is x (expressen) but also stands in genugsamb.
- How the A key was obtained: 2,439 number-letter pairs from the interlinear decipherments of the received letters and the cipher margins of the drafts (`frozen/working/T1-T4.tsv`), majority per number (`frozen/CLE-K.json`), then reduced to residues mod 24 (`frozen/CLE-K24.json`, built by `frozen/scripts/mesure3b.py`).

## Validation (`validation.txt`)
Three pre-registered tests on system A (one failed, one not run for lack of material, one passed: 0.815 against a bar of 0.664 and a best control of 0.330), one on system H (0.948 against 0.301). The failed test and the deviations are stated there.

## Reading (`reading_290.md`, `translation_290.md`, `reading_293.md`, `translation_293.md`)
- Fol. 290r-v, The Hague 2/12 Feb 1674, signed "X X": recounted on the edited text (27 Nov 2026, second recount, one code one word): **285 cipher words, 246 certain (86.3 %), 26 probable, 9 restored, 4 unresolved**; strict count, with the 35 certain words that contain a sign marked doubtful in the transcription moved to probable: 211/285 = 74.0 %. Read in part. Earlier counts (286 words; 247/24/10/5, then 246/27/8/5) counted "sondern" twice and followed the gloss in places. The reading now follows the cipher where it differs from the gloss: was (gloss daß), dies (gloss diesen), darahn, innehmen, izige, Comendant, keyserlichen, Underthanen, sol; andern, den (geringsten) and französisch are marked probable (one sign corrected). The interlinear decipherment of the time covers the letter; our reading agrees with its sense and adds nothing to its content. The writer calls Gustav Adolf "our brother": a brother of the captive in the Netherlands (Walrad of Nassau-Usingen, then lieutenant-general of cavalry in the service of the States General, Ruppersberg p. 130, is a hypothesis, not checked; the address "Ew. Ex." is unusual between brothers). The letter expects "in wenig Tagen den Frieden mit Engellandt": the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of Westminster was signed on 19 Feb 1674. "Duc de Bourneuille" is Alexandre-Hippolyte, prince de Bournonville, the imperial general. Homburg (the Hohenburg in the Saar) fell to Johann Ludwig at the partition of 1659 but was held by Lorraine from 1641; on 27 July 1671 Duke Charles IV handed it over to the Elector of Trier, Karl Kaspar von der Leyen, as imperial commissioner (Köllner pp. 330, 337-338; Ruppersberg pp. 117, 119, 130): the "Churfürst von Coblenz aus" of the letter is the Elector of Trier, and "ohne Ordre von i[hrem] Churfürsten" refers to his commandant. The lines on the fiefs of Metz and on vassal and subject answer Rochefort's claim that Saarbrücken was a fief of the bishopric of Metz (Ruppersberg pp. 130, 134; Klageschrift p. 275).
- Fol. 293r-v, a copy received on 23 Jan 1674, with no decipherment: first 41 lines (465 numbers) read in part, 56 of 80 cipher words certain (70 %; count made before the comparison and not redone). This reading is **not new**: compared at Daniel Bourdeau's request, fol. 293r ff. is a number-for-number copy of the glossed letter fol. 279r-281r (signed "Johann Melchior ...", 14 Jan 1674, received the same day). Of 54 runs of three or more numbers noted on 279r-v, 41 recur identical and in the same order in the copy, 10 with one difference, 3 with two; controls: 0.08 on average in a shuffled number stream of the copy, none in the outgoing letter fol. 297. The comparison was not pre-registered and the key was partly built from these glosses, so decryption_293 is a check of the key against that gloss, not a first reading. It corrects five places of our reading (see reading_293.md): "rozan" = codes 464 548 *Fürst Schwartzenberg*; "aller Arten" = *alle rahten*; "418" = 148, giving *Metzische Sach*; 744 = *Fürstenberg*; the restoration "M[ajestät]" is withdrawn (the original has 283 *Kaÿserin*, 284 *Reich*). The heading of the copy carries *Wien* in cipher (62 58 52 49) and "d. 4ten Jan." with an uncertain year (1673?); the original is signed 14 Jan 1674 by our survey. The ten days are the gap between old and new style: perhaps the copyist turned a Vienna date in new style into old style. This is a hypothesis, not checked. Whether the groups also stand in the same order in the copy was not tested separately. "Metzische Sach" is the question of the Metz fief (see above).
