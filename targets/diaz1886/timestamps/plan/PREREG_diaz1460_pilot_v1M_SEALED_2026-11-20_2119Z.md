## 5. Hypotheses and bars
- **H1, the 1885 table holds.** On the 3 held-out leaves of 1885, in the blind copy's first pass: of the items the frozen tables value, at least 90% are placed on their translation by the scoring script, on at least 20 valued items in all, and the share is above the 99th percentile of 1,000 dealt tables. Fewer than 20 valued items: "not tested", which is not a pass.
- **H2, the 1886 table holds.** The same on the 16 held-out leaves of 1886, with the same three numbers, counted apart from H1.
- **Printed beside H1 and H2, outside the bar:** the same shares in the blind copy's other two passes, and the three shares of this chat's own copy. A pass that holds only on the first pass is printed as such.
- **H3, the seven targets of October 1886 are in the 1886 key.** Both tests of the key-test script must pass on them together, on the first pass:
  - **numbers:** more of their four-figure groups are valued by the 1886 word table alone than by the 1885 word table alone, with the script's exact one-sided p below 0.01;
  - **letters:** their runs of valued syllables score higher under the letter model than at least 990 of the 1,000 dealt lists. Under 40 letters in runs the test is not made and H3 is "not tested".
  - The letters test asks whether the 1886 values make letters more like the translations' Spanish than 99 in 100 dealt lists do. That is another question than the first draft's test, which set the 1886 key against the 1885 key's shift; here the numbers test is the one that sets the two keys against each other.
  - Both tests are run again on the other two passes, and the report says for each whether the bar is met there too.
- **The 1885 target has no bar of its own for its key.** DLI.2.397.1 is one leaf with 14 four-figure groups; neither test can carry a bar on that. Both are run and printed. Its key rests on its date, 26 November 1885 in the listing, inside the span of the 1885 building leaves.

- **What is scored:** every group the frozen tables value, word or syllable. Words in clear are not scored; they only hold the order. Numbers of one or two figures, signs and unvalued groups are not scored.
- **Spelling is folded** on both sides alike: accents and case dropped; j as g, v as b, z as s, y as i; h dropped.
- **A word item agrees** with a whole word of the translation if the two begin with the same letter and one of three things holds:
  - the translation's word has three letters or fewer and its letters occur in order in the entry (the translation abbreviates: usted and Ud., que and q.);
  - the entry has three letters or fewer, and the translation's word begins with the whole entry and is at most two letters longer (de and del, su and sus);
  - both have four letters or more, and their longest common subsequence has at least 0.6 of the shorter's letters (tener and tiene).
- **A syllable item agrees** with the letters at a place if they are its letters. After a placed syllable the next syllable of the run must begin where that one ended.
- **The order:** places go forward only. Between two placed items no more whole words may be passed over than there are rows between them that could stand for a word, plus one.
- **The count:** the script takes a laying-out with the most valued items placed. An item it cannot place counts as not agreeing, whatever the reason.
- **Dealt tables** show what the same rules give a wrong table of the same make: the values are dealt out anew among the numbers, 1,000 times.

## 7. Open groups and signs
1. **The reading that stands first is the one decoded and scored, so the order of the readings carries weight.** The rule for the order was fixed before any decode. On the targets the first is the blind reading's where a blind reading exists and differs from the first reading, because it was made by shape alone by a reader who had not seen the system; else it is the first reading's. On the held-out leaves it is the reading the blind reader gives as the likelier by shape. Every bar is on that pass, `p_first`. Version 3's note said the order carries no weight; that sentence is withdrawn (version 4 of the copy).
2. **Each result is printed again under the other readings.** A group is open when it has at least one other reading on record. In the pass `p_other` every open group takes the first of its other readings; in the pass `p_dropped` an open group is valued by nothing. For H1 to H4 the report says whether the result holds in each. A hit that holds only on `p_first` is printed as such.
3. **A sign is never taken as a word in clear.** Version 3 of the converter folds every token first (Unicode NFKC, so that a raised letter is the plain letter) and then takes as a sign: (a) a token whose letters are cs, anywhere; (b) a token of two letters ending in s that stands directly after a number of one or two figures, whatever its first letter; (c) a token with no digit and no vowel (a, e, i, o, u, y), which is the sign the copies write `q'` if it holds a q. Any other token in square brackets is a sign, and any other token is a word in clear, without its `(?)` wherever that stands in it.
   - **In the converter's own numbering:** the folding is its rule 0, (a) its rule 3a, (b) its rule 3b and (c) its rule 4. Rules 0, 3b and 4 are the desk's; 3a is version 2's rule for cs, with the desk's widening to a mark between the letters (its verdict on ask 75, point 7).
   - **Why.** In the scoring script a word in clear lets the syllable after it stand inside a word, and lets one more word of the translation be passed over. A sign does neither. Version 2 took q only alone or with an apostrophe or a point, so a sign written `qs` became a word in clear, and that leans toward a hit (the desk's verdict on ask 76, entry 465, which corrects point 7 of its verdict on ask 75).
   - **Where rules (b) and (c) come from,** in the desk's words, printed as it asks (its record, entry 467):

     > The desk wrote them after seeing the forms in the answers, and before any decode or score, which it has not made. Each rule can only take more tokens as signs. A sign makes the score stricter: the next syllable must begin a word, and no word of the translation may be passed for it. So neither rule can move the test toward a hit.

   - **By the desk's count,** in its words: "under v2, 20 sign tokens in the two answers would be words in clear"; "under the desk's ask 76 rule, 12 would still be"; "under the two rules below, none would be". Its two rules are (b) and (c) here. This chat has seen none of those tokens.

4. **A row on which two readings differ in kind** (`q'` against `91`) counts the same under either: a number of one or two figures is never looked up and is not counted among the groups, and a sign is not looked up. 5. **Every row that is not a valued syllable group closes a run.** So an open sign cannot join or split a run under one reading and not the other.
6. **A group beside an open sign is valued by its own digits alone.**
7. **No script reads a mark after a group or a stroke above it.** So the open point after 194 on DLI.5.924.1, line 9, changes no count however it is taken.
