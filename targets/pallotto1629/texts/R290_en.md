# R290 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 10 August 1629: English translation

Translation of `R290_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. Paragraphs 1-3 follow the Roman
decipherment printed by Kiewning (1897), Nr. 156; paragraph 4 is read from the figures and the clear words on the sheet.

---

‹In accordance with this, on the following day› Questenberg came to me. Besides what I had said to His Majesty and to the
Prince, I pointed out to him, regarding His Majesty's remark about the audience given to me, the reflection I made after
returning home: that 27 March, falling on a Tuesday, which is not a day of ordinary audience, made it clear that Our Lord
had given the imperial ambassador an extraordinary audience for this affair, and that he had therefore not delayed even
one day in hearing him. I asked him to tell this to His Majesty, as I am willing to believe he will have done, and I
shall do so myself at the first opportunity.

Today the said Signor Questenberg sent me His Majesty's second answer; from its copy Your Most Illustrious Lordship will
see what has been changed from the first. I consider some insistence well spent on this, because besides what has been
gained in making sure that these truths have been well understood, we are clear of the prejudice that the wording of the
first answer could have carried in men's opinion, and indeed in the memory of posterity, to which the wording of the
current papers in so grave an affair will probably be handed down; and of what could have resulted from multiplying other
papers, which it would have been thought necessary not to omit for the clarity of the truth.

Speaking with the Lord Prince on this occasion about Our Lord's request, and weighing the reasons that recommend it, His
Excellency said that His Majesty has resolved in this business to decide the case by justice, but that the French must
first withdraw from Italy, and therefore there is no need to ask for plenipotentiary powers. To this I replied that this
same thing might perhaps have been obtained by way of negotiation, if ministers of His Majesty and of the two crowns could
have been brought together in Italy close at hand, whereas concluding it while it had to be handled from afar was
altogether impossible. The Prince added that His Majesty is utterly resolved not to admit negotiation of any kind unless
the King of France first leaves the posts he occupies in Italy. The Prince also said in conversation that he had told the
Emperor he hoped God would be pleased to turn all these preparations for a lamentable tragedy into a most joyful comedy,
and that His Majesty's arms, in a number however suitable and moderate, would serve only for the dignity and escort of his
person, taking this occasion to set out for Italy; and, so as not to go to Rome, knowing that Popes do not like it, to beg
His Holiness to be pleased to take the trouble of coming to Bologna or Ferrara and there receive the imperial crown from
His Beatitude, renewing the memory of the reverence due to the supreme pontiffs; and that the pious and heroic deeds done
by His Majesty in the service of God and of the Catholic religion well made the Emperor worthy of being called and crowned
as such.

Sabran left yesterday very ill satisfied, and [?wholly] set and resolved to spur the King to war, for which every haste is
being urged here on the General; but I hear that he was held up on his [?journey] by a touch of gout. Today there was talk
of some […] concerning the son of the King of Denmark; if it is confirmed I shall report it. The Magdeburg business is not
yet settled, but it is thought it will be; meanwhile it is not a thing to be made light of. I have word from a good source
that the [?Elector of Saxony] is [?very] ill satisfied, and that he not only [?will not] obey [?the] edict on church
property but has other suspicions besides, which the conjuncture of affairs might make more serious. I shall deal with His
Majesty about the church property of the Palatinate and the other property that is to be recovered under the edict, when
he returns from outside the city, and with the Father Confessor I shall make use of the communication of these orders,
according to Your Most Illustrious Lordship's [?intention]; and with the next post I shall make up for what I cannot do
with this one.
