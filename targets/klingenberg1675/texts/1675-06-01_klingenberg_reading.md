# Biermann to Klingenberg, 1 June 1675: German reading, holes in place

Built by rule from the decryption and the judge's word list (gloss), not polished: a word is printed plain only when its cipher groups decode to it letter for letter under `key.tsv` (signs, table, word map v4); `[?word]` is a word the judge intends where the decode differs (each one is a row of `emendations.tsv`) or a word-code value standing outside any glossed word; `[n]` is a code with no value; `[...]` is one or more signs or syllables not made into a word. Lines beginning `>` are the clear text of the copybook between the cipher passages, read approximately and not counted. Spelling is the cipher's; no punctuation or capitals are supplied in the cipher passages (434 is the writer's own stop). The second section, `Punctuated reading`, repeats the text with punctuation of ours added (b113). Count of record for this letter: strict 90/94 tokens under map v3 (see `counts.txt`); this file uses the later map v4, so it shows more words than that count credits.

## Reading

> Meines Hochgeneigten Herrn Estats Raths Relation vom 4 Junij/25 Maij ist bey des Herrn Reichscantzlers Hochgräfl. Excellentz heute Ihr Königl. Mayt. in dem geheimen Rath vorgetragen worden, und haben Ihr Hochgräfl. Excellentz mir darauf gnädig befohlen meinem Hochgeneigten Herrn Estats Rath die begehrte Liste von den regimentern und Compagnien der

## Punctuated reading

The reading above, line for line, with punctuation of ours added to the cipher passages (12 Nov 2026) so that it can be read as sentences against the translation. Only commas, semicolons, colons and full stops after words are added. No decoded letter, capital, hole (`[...]`, `[?word]`, `[n]`) or word division is changed: a script strips our marks and compares the result with the reading above, token for token. The word division of the cipher passages is the rule-built one above, taken from the gloss; it is ours, since the cipher marks none, and this section supplies no further division. A standalone ` .` is the writer's own stop (434). The clear-text lines (`>`) are unchanged.
