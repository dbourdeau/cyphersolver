## How the readings are made

- Source, for each passage: the frozen first transcription, the single decode with map v4 and the ledger, all already in `texts/`. One ledger word unit is one word of the reading. No key value is added and no unread code is guessed: a code with no value stays `[n]`, a blot stays `[blot]`, an unmapped sign stays `<x>`.
- S units are printed as decoded. Every E unit is printed as decoded; the ledger's intended word is added after it, as `decode[?word]`, only where the emendation is obvious by one fixed test (no code without a value, no unread sign and no blot in the decode, and difflib similarity of at least 0.75 after ä/ö/ü to a/o/u, ß to ss and c taken as g). Otherwise it stays `decode[?]`, and the ledger's word, if any, is in the list at the end of the file. For 27 March 1675 the six `[0]` (the O sign written as the digit 0) are taken as a for the test.
- E units over all 22 passages: 6 + 9 + 33 + 47 + 51 + 39 + 56 + 118 + 34 + 30 + 71 + 10 + 15 + 43 + 14 + 24 + 10 + 9 + 28 + 8 + 21 + 57 = 733. Printed with an emendation: 3 + 4 + 10 + 12 + 16 + 11 + 15 + 35 + 11 + 8 + 12 + 2 + 2 + 11 + 4 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 10 + 5 + 4 + 20 = 210. Left as decoded: 733 - 210 = 523.
- Clear (uncoded) text is copied from the braces of the transcription into lines beginning `>`, as the first reader wrote it (approximate). Braces that are only the first reader's notes (insert marks, struck text) are given as `> [note: ...]`. Where a brace falls inside a word's token range (4 places), it is given after that word and the reading says so.
- The punctuation is ours: commas, semicolons and full stops after words only. A script stripped it and compared the result with the unpunctuated reading, unit for unit, for all 22 files.
- The English keeps every hole in place. A script checked, line by line, that every `[n]`, `[blot]` and `[~decode]` of the reading stands in the English in the same order, and that the number of `[?word]` in each English line equals the number of printed emendations in the German line. `(?)` marks a doubtful rendering. English by Claude, not checked by a German reader.

## Fragmentary passages

No passage is too fragmentary to translate as a whole: each gives connected sense over most of its length. The gists summarise the reading (with the subject lines of the FILE_NOW notes); where a gist names something the reading leaves as an unemended decode, the decode is given with the name as a query. 16 cipher lines in 10 passages have holes in half or more of their units (lines of 3 or more units); there the English is word for word and gives no connected sense. Each translation file lists its own such lines at the end; they are also in the last column above.

## Decode and ledger checked against each other

For all 22 passages: the ledger units cover every cipher token once, in order (first token 0 to the last token of the transcription); the decode of each unit joined in order equals the decode file with its clear-text inserts removed; and the word-unit file equals the ledger's intended words (S plain, E with `:n`, no word as `?n`). One difference of form only: where the word map gives ß, the decode file prints SS (upper-casing in the decoder) and the ledger ß; this occurs in 12 passages (2 + 1 + 2 + 1 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 5 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 24 places: 260196-10R, 260196-235R, 260196-251R, 260195-50R, 260195-57R, 260195-68R, 1675-07-24, 260195-85R, 260195-104R, 260195-149R, 260195-168R, 1675-03-27). No disagreement of substance was found.
