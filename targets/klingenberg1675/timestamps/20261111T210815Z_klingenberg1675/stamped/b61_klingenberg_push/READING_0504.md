## English gist

The King read the joint report of 6 May (N.S.) from Klingenberg and the envoy Høeg "with no small surprise". The allies (the Dutch States and their partners) hold that Denmark is bound to break with Sweden, yet they will give no subsidies for the Danish fleet. Their reason is that the treaties can hardly be made to compel it.

The treaty point about the fleet is admittedly conditional. Even so, the nature of the case means a fleet is now unavoidable. By the allies' own admission, Sweden must be hit hardest at sea, and that cannot be done without a fleet. Sweden will certainly fit out its own fleet. Denmark must therefore be ready with hers as soon as the break comes. War against Sweden without a fleet is impossible, unless Denmark wants to bring every kind of harm and danger on itself and leave Sweden a free hand to do as it likes.

Besides the troops stipulated in the treaties, the King has a larger force under arms. He is content for the allies to carry out their muster, and Klingenberg should give them due notice. The muster will have to take place in the King's own lands.

The King has also told the (Brandenburg) Resident Brandt how sorry he is that the allies have not backed the Elector more seriously. The King may take this as an example of how the allies will treat him in like cases. The allied ministers in Copenhagen are reminded that if their principals carry on like this, the King and the Elector, who are still (thank God) in good shape, can at any time leave the affair with advantage and reputation intact.

Klingenberg already has the King's will on co-signing the declaration of war against Sweden from the previous letter. He and the envoy are to keep out of it with good manners.
