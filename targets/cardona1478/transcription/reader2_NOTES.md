# Notes, helper2_2855: blind sign-by-sign transcription, AHPZ Zurita C_CRAR/000001/0026 (Cardona to John II, Trapani, 16 Jul 1478)

Read from img/c26/im01_1704x2368.jpg only, using my own crops (crop.py, tiles.py, *.jpg here). I did not open im02, any other helper folder or any transcription file. I made no attempt to decipher, and left out the interlinear words (the second, browner hand).

## Counts
- 17 manuscript lines with cipher: L01-L04 (the top block, after "S. Mat"), L05-L12 (the middle block), L13-L17 (the lower block).
- 403 cipher signs. This includes the dot signs: dd 14, trid 7, and the two dotted p-signs.
- 41 sign types. Most frequent: t 44, rho 35, g 31, s 26, ae 19, c 19, hy 18, xo 17, v 16, 3 16. Signs seen only once: ls2, qx, qx2, 8d, delta, o, Lq, rhoo, ppd, pxd.
- 8 signs marked doubtful or illegible: 2 illegible, 6 doubtful.

## Which line a run belongs to

- L10 starts with "plus? r a s" low, under the interlinear "al don Gismundo".
- L11 starts with "rho ls" low, under "la senyor de Ramazaro".
- L14 starts with "ae t v m c g hy t" low and runs on into "3 rho gz xo ...".

- L17 starts with "a rho" low.

## Clear words inside lines
Short words between cipher runs were taken as clear text and not transcribed: lo, del, q-with-bar (que), ha fera la mort, no callare, y vergonya, e lo q ha fer en lo, and others. If any of these short words (lo, del, y, e) are really cipher, the counts would change.

## Doubts
- **L02 #5:** a sign blotted or overwritten with heavy ink, illegible (?). The v before it is sure.
- **L02, the dd after ls (#20):** a paler, reddish dot below it may make it a trid; I took the pale dot as later ink or a stain. A similar reddish-brown dot appears in L11 between s and sig.
- **L07 #1 (ae?):** an ae-like sign with a long stroke down to the left, on the brown stain at the left edge; a small white loss and a red-brown mark sit just below it.
- **L08 #5:** an m hatched over with several strokes (cancelled or overwritten?), transcribed "?".
- **L08 #27 (trid?):** three dots sitting high between the last ae and the clear "que donaza"; they may be an interlinear mark.
- **L10 #1 (plus?):** a cross with a small hook on its left; the ascender of the interlinear "la" runs up into it.
- **L13 #2 (t?):** an L/t-shape whose apparent crossbar is the underline of the interlinear words above it.
- **L15 #14 and #32 (ppd?, pxd?):** a row of two or three dots sits directly above a p-sign. I treated the dots as part of the sign, not as separate dot signs. A similar row of three dots also shows between L14 and L15, above #14.

## Shape notes
- In L01 the descender of the first 7 runs down into the L02 t2 below it; the bar it seems to carry belongs to that t2, so it is read as plain 7. The second 7 in L01 has its own bar (7x).
- In L02 the apparent cross under g (#17) is the top of the L03 t2 below it, so g is plain. hy #15 has its own bar (hyx).
- Small brown "#" or "‖" marks in the interlinear ink stand after the last cipher sign before clear text in L04 (above the last ae), L12 (after the last xo) and L14 (after the last xo, a superscript mark). I took them as marks of the interlinear hand, not as cipher.
- Three stretches read almost the same: L12 "ae t u m c g hy t 3 rho gz xo g rho s xo", L14 "ae t v m c g hy t 3 rho gz xo g rho s xo" and the end of L17. In L12 I read u where the others have v; worth a second look, since the two shapes may be one sign.
- "6" and "sig" are close; I separated them by the direction of the stroke (ascender rising from the left for 6, stroke to the upper right for sig).
- Tiny specks at the right end of L06 (two very small dots near x 1560) are taken as paper marks.
