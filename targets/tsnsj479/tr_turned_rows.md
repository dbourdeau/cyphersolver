# T-S NS J479: the open rows re-read for orientation (20 Nov 2026)

This pass started from the finding in `tr_hard_rows.md` that S1L03 is an alphabet written with the page turned 180°.
Each open row was checked to see whether it, too, was written turned. Then it was re-read sign by sign in whichever
orientation the evidence supports. Sources: `img/full1.jpg`, `img/full2.jpg` and the crops in `img/rows/`. Enlargements
(contrast-stretched, both orientations) are in the session scratchpad `j479_turned/` and are not kept.

**Diagnostics used.** Read in the right orientation, the signs take the shapes in `glyph_chart.png`. The useful tests
are the signs whose 180° turn is not itself a chart sign:

- X (ק) has its dot **below**.
- 7 has its blob at the top left, a bar to the right and the diagonal running down to the left.
- < opens to the right, with its point on the left.
- N is an arch with a bowl at the lower left; turned, it looks like a check mark (√).
- C (ש) has its small inner curl at the top.
- w has its humps closed at the top and a lead-in at the lower left.
- 4 has its triangle at the top and its stem running down; turned, it gives the "h" of S1L03.
- c, turned, gives the "z" of S1L03.

The pairs 6/9 and p/d turn into each other, and O is nearly symmetric, so these three tests decide nothing.

Hebrew letters are given in the scribe's order: left to right in the photograph for a main row, left to right in the
turned crop for a turned row. `?` marks a doubtful letter and `[...]` the hole.

**Reading tests.**

- **הדה** = هذه / هذا, "this". It reads. It is the same group the earlier passes found, because הדה reads the same
  either way and the signs N A N look much the same in both orientations.
- **אלפרא** = الفرا. The letters are secure. It could be the noun الفراء al-farāʾ "fur", or الفرا "the wild ass",
  as in the proverb كل الصيد في جوف الفرا "all game is in the belly of the wild ass". Neither makes a sentence with
  the rest. The user's suggestion of אלפרג, al-farj, rested on the blotted round sign. That sign belongs to the S1V02
  column, and the line has no ג (no E-shaped sign) here, so al-farj is excluded.
- **קרשכד** has five secure or near-secure letters. One possible split is قرشك "your qirsh (piaster), your money",
  followed by ד. With sign 7 read as 4, the word would be קרשכו. Neither is Arabic as written. Egyptian كده
  "thus" would need ה after כד and would leave קרש alone. The qirsh coin is Ottoman and later, so this guess would also
  bear on the date. **Not adopted.** The meaning of قرش and the date of the coin in Egypt were not looked up in a
  dictionary in this pass.
- **[...]?ל**: the word's beginning is in the hole.

**Result:** the letter values change for the whole line. The old main-orientation letters (אפרטא הדה כהכרפ ק טכ) were
the turned signs read upside down. Only הדה gives sense. Unread: 12 of 15 signs. The earlier count of 16 signs
included the S1V02 column's blot.
