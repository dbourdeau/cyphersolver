# T-S NS J479: is there a year on the folio? (20 Nov 2026)

Brief: find where the date formula of S2L02-03 (גרי דלך פי אכ̇ר גמאדי אלתאמי) continues, read every candidate sign
group under the key, check side 1, and give the year if one survives. Read-only pass. Images: `img/full1.jpg`,
`img/full2.jpg`. Enlargements in the session scratchpad folder `j479_year` (`s2l01_x3.png`, `s2_ar_x3.png`,
`s2u03_rot.png`, `s2u01_x2.png`, `s2u01_rot_x2.png`, `s2_rmargin.png`, `s2l04_x2.png`, `s1l13_tail.png`,
`s1ar_rot.png`, `s1ar01.png`, `s1l12.png`). Coordinates are full-resolution pixels of `full2.jpg` / `full1.jpg`.

## Result

**No year survives. The formula goes on one more line, to "סנת אר" (sanat ar-, "the year [fo]ur..."), and stops
there.** This is the same text PGP gives, *sanat ar[...]*. The number word was never finished. Nothing is lost
in a hole: the space after אר is taken up by the upside-down alphabet line S2U03, which comes right up against it.
No other place on either side carries a year in cipher, in Arabic or Hebrew letters, or in numerals.

What "ar-" allows: in Arabic the units come first ("sanat arbaʿ wa-...", "sanat arbaʿīn wa-..."), so the year either
ends in 4 (x04, x14, ... x94) or is a round x40 (arbaʿīn alone, then the hundreds). With the PGP and Burda floor
(after c. 648 AH / 1250 CE) that still leaves dozens of possible years (654, 664, ... 704, 740, 744, ... 804 ...).
The month is Jumādā al-Ākhira, at the end of the month. **No Hijri or CE year can be given.**

Confidence: about 75% that the formula goes on to S2L01 + "אר" as read below; certain that no further number signs
stand there or anywhere else on the leaf.

## 1. Where the formula continues

As photographed, the side-2 text lines run left to right and follow one another **upwards**: the Burda verses
are 1a S2L09 (y 1545-1760), 1b S2L07 (y 1420), 2a S2L06 (y 1235), 2b S2L05 (y 1040), then the date line S2L02
(y 730-880). This is what the page shows turned 180° (the scribe's view, lines right to left from top to bottom).
In the scribe's view:

- S2L02 ends at the left margin, and its last word, מי of אלתאמי (S2L03, x 2770-2880), is raised above the line
  end. Carrying the end of a word up the margin like this is ordinary in Arabic-script documents.
- The **next line** in the scribe's view lies just below S2L02 at the right margin, where a line starts. In the
  photograph that place is **just above S2L02, at the left**, and S2L01 stands there (x 947-1282, y 590-720), written
  the same way up as the cipher text.
- The line goes on (photographed to the right, in the same band) to x ~1370. From there the band holds S2U03, an
  alphabet written with the page the other way up.

Other places checked for a continuation, with nothing found: the right margin below S2L03 (`s2_rmargin.png`: only
מי, then the start of S2L05); the interlinear pair S2L04 (see 2c); the band above in the photograph, which is the
next line in the scribe's view (S2U02, a full alphabet row, all signs in chart order); the note at the top right
S2U01 (see 2d); the far end of S2L02 itself (the stroke after אלתא and the leading stroke `\` are line marks, not
signs).

## 2. Readings

### 2a. S2L01 = סנת (sanat)

Signs, photographed left to right (`s2l01_x3.png`):

1. A wave: a lead-in rising from the lower left, a dip, then a flat stroke with a downward hook at the right. A dot
   stands under the dip. tr_side2.md read this as `-.` (י with a dot) because of the hooked end. It has two humps,
   though, and is longer than any `-` on the page. It is the **w (ס)** wave of the alphabet rows, with a hooked
   finish. Turned 180° it is still a wave (tr_hebrew_side2.md calls it "a dotted w" in that view), and a wave reads
   the same both ways up. The dot is not explained: it may be a stray, or a mark the scribe also puts on other
   letters.
2. A loop on the left of a stem that runs below the baseline: **q (נ)**.
3. A small closed loop with a long tail rising to the upper right: **a (ת/ة)**.

→ **סנת** = سنة / sanat "year (of)", in the construct spelling with ת that Judaeo-Arabic dating formulas use.
Read as י נ ת it gives no word. PGP's scholar read *sanat* here, which supports the ס.

### 2b. The first two signs of the S2U03 band = אר (ar-)

After a gap of 87 px, about one word space in this hand (`s2_ar_x3.png`), photographed left to right:

4. A tall, slightly slanting stroke: **I (א)**. It looks the same either way up.
5. A dot at the left, a stroke rising to the upper right, then a stroke back down to the left that ends in a small
   loop at the bottom. This is the lying ꝛ, **s (ר)**, which the text lines use often (אלריח S2L06, גרי S2L07).
   tr_side2.md labelled it `2` because it read the band upside down. Upside down it matches no sign of the key.
6. (Doubtful.) Two tall stems joined at the bottom, the left one with a hooked foot: a cup with a tall right stem.
   In the photograph it is not a key form (U = ב is the bar with a hanging loop; it is closest to S = צ or a turned
   N). Upside down it is tr's `n`, a variant arch that also turns up in the alphabet columns S2V01-02.

→ **אר**, possibly **אר[ב]** if sign 6 is an odd ב. This is PGP's *ar[...]*: the beginning of ארבע / ארבעין.

From sign 7 onwards the band reads only upside down, as the alphabet in chart order. Turned 180° and read left to
right, it gives S | w O 6 S X 9 C = צ | ס ע פ צ ק ר ש. That is seven letters in exact alphabet order, which
coincidence cannot produce. So these signs belong to the alphabet and not to the date. Read the photographed way
up, they give א ר ? ע פ ק ? ר ע ס, not a number. S2U02, the alphabet line above it, also ends at ש (C) with no ת.
So S2U03 most likely ends at C too, and signs 4-6 ("n 2 I" in tr's upside-down listing) are not alphabet letters:
they are the date's letters, written the other way up.

The two sign groups touch: ר (x ~1410-1503), sign 6 (x ~1530-1603), ש of the alphabet (x ~1627-1700), with gaps of
only 24-27 px. Either the alphabet was there first and the scribe ran into it after "סנת אר", or the scribe stopped
after "אר" and someone later wrote the alphabet tail up to it. Either way **no number word stands after אר on this
line**, and there is no hole here.

### 2c. S2L04 (interlinear above אלברק, x 1565-1690)

A lying ꝛ joined by a horizontal stroke to an upright 8 (`s2l04_x2.png`): ר ר as a monogram (tr: `8 N`). Neither
orientation gives סנה/סנת, a number word or a numeral. Not part of the date.

### 2d. S2U01 (note at the top right, x 1840-2440, y 255-370)

- Turned 180° (`s2u01_rot_x2.png`), left to right: short heavy hooked dash, 7, P, open ↄ, U, O, 9 = י/ת ת מ י ב ע ר;
  right to left ר ע ב י מ ת ת/י. With the side-1 habit 7 = ב: ב ב מ י ב ע ר.
- As photographed ("Goaoc8<<", `s2u01_x2.png`), left to right: 6-like, O, a, open c, 8-like, <, < = פ? ע ת ? ר? כ כ;
  right to left כ כ ר ? ת ע פ?.

None of these contains סנה/סנת, ארבע (the ב ע in מיבער is followed by ר, not preceded by ר), סבע, תמאן, מאיה/מיה
or any other number word. Read as abjad, no letter run gives a plausible Hijri year, and the signs are not Arabic,
Coptic or Hindu-Arabic numerals in either orientation. In the scribe's view it also stands at the bottom left of
the page, which is not where a continuation line would start. Not a date.

## 3. Side 1

- **S1L13 tail** (`s1l13_tail.png`, x ~1550-2675, y 3080-3250), photographed left to right: crossed triangle (h,
  outside the key), triangle on a stem (A? ד), crossed loop 8 (ר), wave w (ס), then the tighter group I, a
  curl-topped stem (7, or 9), 4. The "174" impression comes from this last group: I = א, 7 = ת (or ב on side 1),
  4 = ו. Read as cipher it is אתו / **אבו**. As numerals, "174" cannot be a Hijri year (174 AH = 790 CE, five
  centuries before al-Būṣīrī). Western-shaped 1-7-4 digits would also be out of place in a Mamluk-period Geniza
  jotting. It would need the thousands left out of some era (Jewish minor era (5)174 = 1413/14 CE; Coptic 1174 AM =
  1457/58 CE), and nothing on the leaf supports either. All three signs are ordinary key signs, standing after a
  row of single practice signs (h, A, 8, w). **Not a year.**
- **S1L12** (`s1l12.png`): X X X (ק), w (ס), a knot of interlaced loops, a dash. Pen trials, no number.
- **S1AR01** (top line, Arabic script, `s1ar01.png`): كا لا ططم ياعاس لا للطم لا, repeated لا/ط letter trials. No
  numerals, no سنة.
- **S1AR02-03** (petition, upside down, `s1ar_rot.png`): يقبل الارض بين يدي المواقف العاليه اعلاها الله تعا وشرّف /
  ـلاده محمد [...]ـه امين وينهي انه مقطوع الحاكـ[م]. Only the opening of a petition survives, and a petition carries
  its date at the end, not the opening. No date here.
- The rest of side 1 (S1L01-11, S1L14-16, S1V01-02) is the erotic prose, the signature, letter play and alphabet
  rows (`reading.md`). No סנה/סנת, no number word.

**Year: none.** The scribe wrote *jarā dhālika fī ākhir jumādā al-thāmī sanat ar-* and broke off, or was stopped by
