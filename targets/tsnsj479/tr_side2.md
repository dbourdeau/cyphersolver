# T-S NS J479, side 2: shape transcription

Source: `img/full2.jpg` (3069 x 4319). All coordinates are full-resolution pixels (x, y from top left).
Labels follow `glyph_chart.png` (I U E A N 4 c B d - < p P q w O 6 S X 9 C 7) plus the new labels listed in
section 3. Shapes only; nothing here is a decipherment. Scratch crops (line crops, rotations, comparison
sheets, `newglyph_<label>.png`) are in the session scratchpad folder `j479_s2`.

Conventions
- Reading order: first token = rightmost sign once the line is upright. Signs separated by a space, words by ` | `.
- `?` illegible sign, `X?` doubtful reading, `[...]` hole or covering with possible loss.
- `X.` = dot under the sign. Where a dot stands beside a sign at mid height (after `<` twice in L10, after `p`
  in the bottom alphabet row) it is also written as a following `.` and flagged in the line note.
- Word division is by spacing only and is often uncertain: gaps of 40 px or more were taken as word spaces,
  gaps under 30 px as none, 30-39 px judged by eye. The alphabet rows have even spacing and are given as runs.
- d vs q: `d` = loop with the stem rising above the loop and ending at the baseline; `q` = loop on the left of a
  stem that descends below the baseline, whether or not it also rises above the loop (the alphabet rows L12, L14
  write q both ways, so the two forms are kept together).
- p vs P: the alphabet rows (L1 upside down, L12, L14, L15) show that the scribe's `P` is the loop-on-stem
  sign with an extra curl or hook on the LEFT of the stem below the loop. That shape is frequent in the text
  and is written `P` everywhere; a plain loop on a stem without the left curl is `p`.

## Orientation of side 2

Three items at the top of the page are written upside down (the page was turned): the short note at the top
right, the first full line, and most of the second line. Upright after 180-degree rotation they read as
alphabet lists in the chart's sign order. The two vertical columns at the left are written top to bottom with
the signs turned 90 degrees clockwise (rotate the crop 90 degrees anticlockwise to read them); both are also
alphabet lists. Everything else is upright. No line in Arabic or Hebrew script was found on side 2 (see the
note on S2L13).

Notes on the upright lines
- S2L01: upright reading `a q -.` (the `-` has a long lead-in stroke rising from the lower left, dot under its
  hooked end). It is separated by 87 px from the upside-down text of S2U03. Read upside down it would be a
  non-chart loop, a p-like sign and a dotted `w`; the upright reading gives common signs, so it is taken as
  upright, but this is not certain.
- S2L02: the last token `I?` is a long slanting stroke at x 890-950 that may be a separator or a stray stroke
  rather than a sign. `p.` = loop on stem with dot under.
- S2L05, S2L06, S2L07: the very frequent `P` here is the left-curl P (see conventions). In S2L06 `X.` was first
  read as `O.`; it matches the X of the alphabet rows (round with inner loop, dot under). Same in S2L07.
- S2L06: `U` in `a U N` is the bar-with-hanging-loop shape; `e` is a one-off shape (section 3).
- S2L07: `q` near the right end is borderline between d and q (stem descends 50 px below the loop).
- S2L09: `y` (two parallel slanting strokes) stands above `q`, written between S2L07 and S2L09 at y 1547-1600.
  The leftmost word `t p`: `t` is d with a crossbar through the stem, at the edge of the large hole.
- S2L10: x 1440-1700 is crossed by ink runs (vertical brown smears). `9?` (x 1541-1608) is smeared; a smear at
  x 1447-1480 was not counted as a sign. `N?` is a small arch. `I?` at x 983-997 is a stroke at the hole edge.
- S2L11 / S2L12: isolated signs between S2L10 and S2L15. Rotated 180 degrees the `2` would look like a bar with
  a curl hanging at its right end (U-like), so their orientation is open. `g` is a spiral with a tail.
- S2L13: smaller and lighter than the main hand; read as cipher signs. A long arc rises from the `d` and runs
  over the rest of the word to the right. I could not read it as Arabic; worth a second opinion.
- S2L14: open 4 (angle at the lower left of the stem); a horizontal bar above it with a dot above the bar.

- S2L16: a loop on a stem whose loop curls inward to a dot; written `p`.
- S2L17: `<.` twice: a dot stands just right of `<` at mid height (before `p` in word 2, before `-` in word 6).
