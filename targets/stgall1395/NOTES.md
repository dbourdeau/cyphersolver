# St. Gall Incantations, Cod. Sang. 1395: cryptic colophon

Status: written up (docs/stgall1395.html)

Scope: the only "St. Gall" cryptogram found for the goal "solve The St. Gall Cryptograms" (9 Nov 2026). No target of that
name exists in the repo, DECODE-derived leads, or the web; the user's intended text was not identified.

Ciphertext (Thesaurus Palaeohibernicus II, p. 290, as on Wikisource/maryjones.us; not re-seen on the e-codices image):
`PreCHNYTΦcANϖMNYBVc:- KNAATYONIBVS:- FINIT`

Reading already in print (Stokes & Strachan, Thesaurus Palaeohibernicus II): *prechnytosan* = Latin *praedicent*,
then *omnibus nationibus* (Matth. 28:19 / Luke 24:47 type formula). Checked here: `ϖMNYBVc` = omnibus (ϖ=o, Y=i, V=u,
c=s, i.e. vowels written as Greek/odd letters and lunate sigma). `KNAATYONIBVS` fits *nationibus* (Y=i, V=u) with the
leading K and doubled A unexplained; `PreCHNYTΦcAN` vs *praedicent* does not align letter for letter (12 vs 10 letters).

Seen on the image (e-codices csg-1395 p. 419, IIIF crop pct:8,33.1,90,2.4 of csg-1395_419.jp2, 9 Nov 2026): the line sits
between the Irish incantations, in capital-like letters with Greek/odd forms (round e, phi, pi-like glyph, lunate sigma),
ends `... KHAATYONIBVS : FINIT`; the Thesaurus transcription above matches it at the level of letter count and word breaks.
Not tried to explain: doubled A in the last word could be `aa` for a (nationibus = N-AA-T-Y-O-N-I-B-V-S with leading K).
Cod. Sang. 459 p. 58 (computus) checked 9 Nov: Easter-table page, no cipher entry (a search summary claiming one was wrong).
