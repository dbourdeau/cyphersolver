## Marked text (line by line against the signs)

Marks: `{x/y}` the signs give x, read y; `{+a}` letter added; `[Name]` code group. Plain script in `<...>`.

```marked
L01: <... che quantunque non molto> buoni {a/p}er
L02: gli interessi del [Re] <sono tuttavia da me tenuti nella ...>
L03: <... Mi dispiace non poco> d'udir che le cose della Fiandra
L04: vadino così male <, e che> ne siano in gran parte
L05: cagione i disordini dell'indisciplin{i/a}te {l/g}en-
L06: ti del [Duca di Lorena] <; perche potrebbono, come V.E.> prudente-
L07: {o/m}ente discorre, tirarsi a{+d}dosso <anco> mag-
L08: gior rovina <. Dio N.ro Sig.re ...>
```

Repairs: L01 the sign read as 2 (a) has a loop that may be the closed loop of Q (p) (transcribed `2/Q`); L07 a
cross (o) where the sense wants the figure 4 (m); L07 one d added (the writer gives `adosso`, a common spelling of
the time, so this may not be an error at all). L05-L06 `indisciplinitelenti` is read as `indisciplinate genti` with
two repairs in one phrase (y = i read as a; the c sign = l read as the tall sign = g). This is the weakest point of
the reading: the sense ("the undisciplined troops of the Duke of Lorraine") fits the context, but two changed signs
in one phrase are a conjecture, not a reading. Treat "indisciplinate genti" as doubtful.

## Italian (cipher in normal type, plain script in italics)

*Ricevo con la lettera di V.E. dei 7 la continuatione de gl'avvisi di coteste bande, che quantunque non molto*
buoni per gli interessi del Re, *sono tuttavia da me tenuti nella conveniente stima, per venirmi dalla natural
cortesia dell'E.V., a cui ne rendo perciò le dovute gratie. Mi dispiace non poco* d'udir che le cose della Fiandra
vadino così male, *e che* ne siano in gran parte cagione i disordini dell'[?indisciplinate] [?genti] del Duca di
Lorena; *perche potrebbono, come V.E.* prudentemente discorre, tirarsi addosso *anco* maggior rovina. *Dio N.ro
Sig.re sia servito di porvi opportuno rimedio ... Inspruch a 25. di 7bre 1646.*

The plain-script passages are a reading of the clear text around the cipher, given for context; they were not
transcribed letter by letter.

English: `translation_24800.md`.

## Summary and context

A short letter of thanks for news from Flanders, written in the weeks when the French under Enghien were besieging
Dunkirk (taken in early October 1646). The ciphered sentences say that the news is bad for the King of Spain, that affairs
in Flanders go badly, largely through the disorders of the Duke of Lorraine's troops, and that, as Piccolomini
himself wrote, this could lead to still greater ruin. Code groups: 37 = Re, 18 = Duca di Lorena.
