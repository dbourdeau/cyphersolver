# Check of the contributor's key and reading (8 Nov 2026, repository commit af80f5e)

What an AI model (Claude) checked of the submission by Prabal Gupta (issue #330). The CEHM scans are not reachable from the
project's environment, so **no transcription and no key card was compared with a scan by the project**. No human has repeated
the check.

## 1. The key

`key.tsv` has 26 cipher-to-plain rows, one plain letter per cipher sign and one cipher sign per plain letter (26 distinct each,
ch and ll counted as single signs). Read in the order of the card, the cipher signs p q r s t u v x y z a b ch d e f g h i k l ll m
ñ n o stand against the plain alphabet a b c d e f g h i j k l m n ñ o p q r s t u v x y z: a plain alphabet shifted against
the cipher one, as the contributor says.

## 2. The decryption

The ten coded words of `transcription.txt` (potdltk, aydtsp, otdtipb, xlltilp, chflyd, chybylpi, rfdlip, sypo, gitrpllryfdtk,
mtdup; 65 letters, ll read as one sign) were decrypted again with `key.tsv` by an own script. All ten agree with
`decryption.txt` letter for letter: azentes, kineda, zeneral, huerta, motin, militar, contra, diaz, precauciones, venfa. Seven
words are Spanish as spelled (z standing for the sound of g or j in "azentes" and "zeneral", as the contributor notes) and
one is a name, so nine of the ten make sense, and the tenth, kineda, does not settle.

## 3. What the project could not check

- The transcription against the scan, and the key card (CMXV.16.1557.1, scan 7) against the scan.
- The reading of the next day's telegram (CMXV.18.1777.5-6) against its pencil gloss, which is the contributor's check that the card is the right one.
- Statistical validation: ten words are too few for a language-model test, so none is recorded.
- The history in the contributor's notes (the dates of Díaz's resignation and of the coup against Madero).
