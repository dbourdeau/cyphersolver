# mexico1911: a coded telegram to González Garza warning of a mutiny, 19 May 1911 (CEHM-Carso CMXV.18.1770.2-4)

Status: read in part (8 Nov 2026). Contributed by Prabal Gupta (issue #330; repository `prabal-rje/branicki-1757`, folder `mexico-1911`, commit af80f5e, CC BY 4.0): a ten-word coded stretch of a Spanish telegram, read with the shifted-alphabet key card that the same fonds holds for one of the signers. Checked here against its own files only (`VERIFICATION.md`); the CEHM scans are not reachable from the project, so no transcription was compared with a scan here.

## The document

CEHM-Carso, Mexico City, Fondo CMXV (Archivo Federico González Garza), CMXV.18.1770.2-4: a Western Union form of 19 May 1911 from Mexico City to "W. Olliphant" (a pencil note adds "F. Gonzalez Garza") at Missouri 1008, El Paso, Texas, signed Fausto E. Miranda and S. Herrera Moreno, received at 10:55 pm. Catalogue 1462 lists the Maderista junta's undeciphered telegrams of this fonds and carries this one as "partly read" from the archive's record ("Texto en clave y palabras descifradas", subject "Armisticio fue firmado"); no decipherment stands on the sheet and the pencil notes do not touch the coded words.

`transcription.txt` is the contributor's typed copy of the form, `reading.md` and `translation.md` his reading in Spanish and English, `decryption.txt` each coded word with its letter-for-letter reading, `key.tsv` the key card's alphabet and `contributor_readme.md` his own notes.

## What it says

Ocurra [a] Julio Peña [para la] traducción. Last night three agents [of Pineda?] left for the north. General Huerta told one of the writers (the verbs are first-person singular) that a military mutiny against "usted" is feared in the capital on account of Díaz's resignation; the writer thinks precautions necessary, "or do not come". The contributor translates "causa renuncia Díaz" as "because of Díaz's resignation"; the resignation followed six days later, so the wording may refer to the one then awaited (the record's subject is "Armisticio fue firmado"); this is not settled by the sources. "Usted" may be González Garza or Madero, for whom he acted; the telegram does not say. The contributor notes that Díaz resigned on 25 May 1911, six days later, and that Huerta led the coup against Madero in 1913 (history from his notes; not checked here).

## The cipher

A 26-sign cipher alphabet (it has ch, ll and ñ, and no c or j) set against a plain alphabet of 26 letters (a to z without w, with ñ): cipher p q r s t u v x y z a b ch d e f g h i k l ll m ñ n o stands for plain a b c d e f g h i j k l m n ñ o p q r s t u v x y z (`key.tsv`). Only ten words are coded (65 letters: agentes, [?]kineda, general, Huerta, motín, militar, contra, Díaz, precauciones, venga); the rest is clear Spanish. The key is the card headed "Herrera. Moreno." in the same fonds (CMXV.16.1557.1, scan 7), one signer of the telegram being S. Herrera Moreno: the key is the archive's own, not recovered from the ciphertext.

## Checks

The contributor reports that the same card reads a telegram of the next day by the same signers (CMXV.18.1777.5-6), glossed in pencil by a hand of the time, and all ten coded words agree with the pencil gloss (general Reyes, científicos, patrios, nacional, sacrificio, personalidad, Reyes, Habana, Limantour), one with a wrong letter. The project did not see either sheet; see `VERIFICATION.md` for what it re-checked.
