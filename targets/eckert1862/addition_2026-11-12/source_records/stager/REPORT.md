# Stager locality-index sweep: No. 10 trail

12 November 2026. **Completed the digitized locality index of Huntington mssEC 36 (also catalogued EC 50). No No. 10 vocabulary table or table locator was found. The index does supply a useful, directly pictured historical connection: George W. Baldwin at Baltimore held Nos. 9 and 10.** U3's heading names Baldwin. This supports a recipient/operator-location hypothesis, but supplies none of the six missing codeword values.

This is a follow-up to the earlier stage of [U3_search.md](../../U3_search.md). This portable copy adapts the local report’s file references; historical inspection claims are unchanged. The frozen contribution and its keys/readings were not changed. Research is context-assisted, not blind decipherment. No external communication, order, login or repository publication was made.

## Scope and source

[Huntington item 70](https://hdl.huntington.org/digital/collection/p16003coll11/id/70), *Memoranda Giving Location and C. of Cipher Keys arranged by Col. Anson Stager*, [IIIF manifest](https://hdl.huntington.org/iiif/info/p16003coll11/70/manifest.json), [catalogue record](https://catalog.huntington.org/record=b1801980). Saved metadata dates the volume **1865–1867**, describes 332 pages with scattered text on 56, and identifies it as a ledger of which keys were held geographically and by whom.

The locality section is itself an alphabetically tabbed holder register, not an index pointing into the Eckert telegram ledger. I visually inspected **all 58 digitized locality canvases, 4 through 61, labels p[7] through p[321]**, including blank dividers and the already sampled first locality page. This completes the available digitized locality-index sweep. It does **not** mean all 332 physical pages were available or read. A one-page boundary check at canvas 62, p[324], identifies the following section as cipher-book stock accounting; its remaining pages were downloaded with the batch but not inspected, and no claim is made about them. Covers and the earlier blank/title pages were not re-reviewed.

`PAGE_REVIEW.tsv` records every inspected page with its manifest label, canvas, local file, image URL, and concise observation. The observations identify useful numbers/holders, rather than claiming a complete diplomatic transcription of all handwriting. Uncertain names and overwritten numbers should be checked on the linked images before a biographical or chronological claim.

The Beckwith entry, **p[113], canvas 21**, lists Nos. 9, 1 and 2 with later delivery/return annotations concerning 2–5; it does not list No. 10 there. This does not contradict Plum's description of an earlier combined three-colour 9/10/12 key: this register is later, amended over time, and not necessarily a complete history of each operator's possessions.

## What changes for U3

U3 remains a partial reading with `Pauline`, `Quarrel`, `Ink`, `Rodneyed`, `Abel`, and `Viola` unvalued. Baldwin/Baltimore is now a source-supported historical hypothesis for the outer heading's operator/destination. It does **not** prove `Ink = Baltimore`, `Quarrel = General`, a signatory, or any other codeword assignment. The telegram is dated February 5, 1864; the register's catalogue date is 1865–1867. The time difference and changing possessions prevent simply projecting every entry backwards.

No explicit page, shelfmark, or vocabulary table was found to follow. Thus there was no additional table image to acquire from an index locator. The useful output is a finite list of historical holders/transfer clues for a future archival catalogue query, especially Baldwin/Baltimore and Culgan → David/Pittsburgh, plus central Stager/Eckert custody. These do not themselves establish a surviving public No. 10 table. The prior NCM VF69-62 issue-identification lead and Plum colour fingerprint remain as previously documented; this sweep neither resolves nor repeats them.
