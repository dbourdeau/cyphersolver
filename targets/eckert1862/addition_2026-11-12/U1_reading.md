# Eckert ledger U1: the missing key is Book No. 2

Local research, 12 November 2026. This fills a repository reading gap; it is **not a first historical recovery**. The period sender and recipient could read it, and its clear text was printed in Official Records, series I, volume 33, p. 486. Current repository NOTES and its U1–U3 file were checked before this work; U1 was explicitly unread with Book No. 9.

Source: Huntington mssEC 19, p. 4, second telegram, 1 February 1864, [image 8896](https://hdl.huntington.org/digital/collection/p16003coll11/id/8896). The ledger is already in reading order, before route transposition. The ledger image reads `Word`, as the volunteer/repository transcript has it. `World` (Book No. 2 World = West) is our emendation of the clerk's slip, used in `transcription/U1.txt`; an earlier note here said the image reads World, which a second look on 13 Nov 2026 did not confirm. `Jaunts` is a possessive without apostrophe. The ending is separated from the message body: `Tobsy` is a variant of the time code `Topsy` (12:30 p.m., verified below), while `nasty day this` remains unresolved check/null material.

Key: [Huntington mssEC 47, Cipher Book No. 2](https://hdl.huntington.org/digital/collection/p16003coll11/id/596), particularly pp. 11–12, 17–21, 24–25. `key_book2_selected.tsv` is a **partial**, image-read extraction; it is not a full transcription of that volume. The key's catalogue date is later than the telegram; this demonstrates matching vocabulary, not that every amendment in the surviving copy existed in February 1864.

## Cipher and code substitution

```
For Nestor It is the opinion of
Midas that Early is patenting on World
Bermuda and the Bard & Biscay Walnut
with a large salem quickened from Jaunts
Peru Wiley Lady Tobsy nasty day this
```

```
For [John Sedgwick] It is the opinion of
[B. F. Kelly] that Early is [advancing] on [West]
[Virginia] and the [Baltimore] & [Ohio] [Rail-road]
with a large [force] [detached] from [Lee]'s
[Army] [Signature] [H. W. Halleck] [12:30 p.m.; Tobsy/Topsy] [check/null tail unresolved]
```

The key explicitly allows adding `ed` or `ing` to the marked arbitrary words: patent-ing → advanc-ing; quicken-ed → detach-ed. No ciphertext-fitted meanings are needed. Proper-name spelling is normalized only in the readable rendering.

## Readable text

For Sedgwick. It is the opinion of Kelley that Early is advancing on West Virginia and the Baltimore & Ohio Railroad with a large force detached from Lee's army. Signed, H. W. Halleck.

The [Official Records scan](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion0133rootrich/page/486/mode/1up) supplies the rank “General” before Kelley and a 12:30 p.m. heading. The ledger has no separately encoded rank before Midas. A subsequent image check of Book No. 2 p. 1 resolves `Tobsy` as the spelling variant of `Topsy`, **12:30 p.m.**; the final `nasty day this` remains a check/null tail. This time table differs from Book No. 1, where Topsy means midnight. The OR text was found **after** the Book No. 2 reading of this telegram; key selection itself was exploratory using other already-seen material, not a preregistered blind test.
