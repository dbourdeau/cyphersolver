# Eckert ledger U2: Book No. 1 recovers the address and heading

Huntington mssEC 19, p. 6, lower telegram, [image 8898](https://hdl.huntington.org/digital/collection/p16003coll11/id/8898), 5 February 1864. The handwritten operator heading itself ends in `1`. The repository marked this telegram unread using Book No. 9, although it already contains the necessary No. 1 key transcription (`key_book1_mssEC43.tsv`). This is a message-specific application and correction to the broad claim that Book No. 1 does not fit this ledger, not a new cryptographic key.

Root checked the original telegram and four original No. 1 key pages in mssEC 43, item 428: image410/p14, image415/p19, image396/time, image421/numerals. Downloaded copies are in the survey raw folder. These agree with the repository's prior key extraction:

| Code | Meaning | Key location |
|---|---|---|
| Grapes | Washington | p.14 |
| Plaster | 5 | numeral flyleaf |
| Emily | 10 a.m. | time page |
| Paradise | Colonel | p.19 |

The rest of the sentence is already ordinary English. `unity` following McCallum is not assigned a meaning here; the word is retained as unresolved punctuation/check material. `Nothing about Devereux` follows the signature and may be check/null wording; it is **not promoted to part of Anderson's message**.

```
Grapes Feby Plaster. Emily For Paradise Mc
Callum unity I leave here to day
and will report to you soon as
possible sig A. Anderson Nothing about Devereux
```

Readable message, with editorial punctuation: **Washington, February 5, 10 a.m. For Colonel McCallum. I leave here today and will report to you as soon as possible. Signed A. Anderson.**

Prior reading: the period operators had the key; the body was already legible in the volunteer transcript. No matching printed copy was found in the limited search of OR I/33, but this is not proof of nonpublication, especially for military railroad correspondence. Our addition is four verified heading/address substitutions. U3 remains a separate key problem, headed `10`; neither Book No. 1 nor No. 2 fits its Ink/Rodneyed words.
