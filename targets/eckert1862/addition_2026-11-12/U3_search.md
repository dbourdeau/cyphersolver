# U3 / No. 10: bounded source investigation, 12 November 2026

The source mssEC19 p7 (Huntington canvas8899) visibly heads the lower telegram **10**, underlined. Its clear frame concerns prisoners leaving Cleveland and passing through a city, with precautions against communication. `Pauline`, `Quarrel`, `Ink`, `Rodneyed`, `Abel`, `Viola` have no verified No.10 values. The full source, literal gaps and a readable clear frame are saved separately; no context-derived value is entered into a key.

## Actual primary sources checked

- [Plum, Military Telegraph during the Civil War (1882), vol.1 p52](https://archive.org/details/cu31924092908742/page/n65/mode/1up), image visually checked. It describes Beckwith's combined keys: No.10 in red, No.9 in blue, No.12 in black. As a useful identification test, Asia/Adam means McClellan in red, Lincoln in blue, Halleck in black. This identifies a promising **form** of No.10 source, not its present location or the six values needed here. The text says several copies were issued. Page-mapped scan and metadata are hashed in `source_records/U3_SOURCE_URLS.tsv`.
- Huntington's saved 67-item Eckert public inventory includes explicitly named Books1/2/5/9 and several other manuscripts, but no expressly titled No.10. This bounded inventory result is not a claim that no No.10 survives or that all later catalogue descriptions have been searched.
- **mssEC67, No.9**, canvases1729/1733 (pages9/13), viewed in colour. The inspected entries have the No.9 assignments, including Adam/Lincoln; no matching red No.10 layer identified on this sample. The earlier No.9 table gives Quarrel/Pemberton and Ink/Abingdon and cannot simply be transferred into this message. The entire book was not re-examined for colour layers in this continuation.
- **mssEC38, S. B. Fairchild's Generals and Places key**, [manifest185](https://hdl.huntington.org/iiif/info/p16003coll11/185/manifest.json). Inspected owner page160, instructions164, and the seven vocabulary pages166–172, plus several blanks. This is an actual handwritten Gulf filling, not just a generic blank-book description. Examples: Anderson/Agnew = Grant; Agnes/Ashland = Halleck; Rodney/Raymond = Wing (canvas171). It does not supply the No.10 six-token reading. Auction descriptions associating blank templates with 9/10/12 are not proof that this particular filled key is No.10.
- **mssEC36, Stager memoranda**, [manifest70](https://hdl.huntington.org/iiif/info/p16003coll11/70/manifest.json). The follow-up inspected all 58 digitized locality canvases 4–61 and the account/end canvases 62–66. The register dates 1865–1867. It lists Baldwin at Baltimore holding Nos.9 and10 (canvas5), and a No.10 transfer from Culgan to Capt. T.B.A. David at Pittsburgh (canvas47); Eckert held copies of each (canvas59). No vocabulary table or direct table locator was found. This completes the digitized substantive sections, not every physical page of the 332-page volume. No codeword value follows from custody: U3 is earlier, dated February5,1864. See [page-level sweep](source_records/stager/REPORT.md), [coordinator account-page review](source_records/stager/ROOT_ADDENDUM.md), and [source URLs/hashes](source_records/stager/SOURCE_URLS.tsv).
- **Official Records, series II vol.VI** ([IA cu31924079575274](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079575274)), full OCR acquired. Targeted search around 4–6 February1864 and Cleveland/Lockwood/prisoner-transport wording found no exact U3 match. Printed pp875–876 contain related January25 transport plans from Sandusky via Pittsburgh to Baltimore; pp919–921 contain 4–5 February correspondence. These are contextual parallels, not cribs or evidence that Ink means Baltimore. OCR errors and incomplete search prevent an absence claim.

The catalogue description does **not** establish that this is No.10 or a combined three-colour copy. No corresponding page images were located in this search. This is a holding to clarify through catalogue/reference access, not a proven No.10 source or an order already made.

Targeted web searches for an explicit No.10 book and Beckwith's colour key supplied historical descriptions, not an accessible matching table.
