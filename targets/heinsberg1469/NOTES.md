# heinsberg1469: two cipher letters of the lordship of Heinsberg, 1469 (catalogue 1618, archive request ar-776)

**Source.** Landesarchiv NRW, Abteilung Rheinland (Duisburg), AA 0582 Herrschaft Heinsberg, Akten, B 2, fol. 120r-v
(letter 1) and fol. 121r-v (letter 2). The archivist's summary on fol. 109r ("Heinsberg 1469") ends with "Zwei
chiffrirte Schreiben an Johann". Both letters are addressed on the back in clear: "Unsern lieben getruwen Johanne von
Russeb[ruck]" (fol. 120v), so they are letters *to* Johann von Russebruck, the lord's officer, not to Count Johann.
The "we/us" style and the address fit Count Johann II of Nassau-Saarbrücken, lord of Heinsberg, as sender (probable:
the cipher does not name him). Johann von Russebruck and Johan Hucking appear together on fol. 112v of the same file.

**Photographs** by Elke Schwedmann (WGfF, Bezirksgruppe Ruhr), taken for this project on 16-17 Nov 2026, with thanks to
the Landesarchiv NRW. They are held privately for study and are not reproduced here; only text transcriptions are.

**System.** Simple substitution of invented signs, word division kept; 15th-century German (Early New High German) chancery language with Rhine-Franconian
features (daz, zu, zit, uf, uch). Key: key.tsv. One sign gives d and b, one h and ch, one u and v.

**Method.** Two blind transcriptions by two independent AI agents (transcription_*_passA/B.txt; codes mapped in key.tsv,
SIGNS.md). Preregistration frozen by SHA-256 before any key search (heldout/PREREG.md): letter 1 for training, letter 2
held out. The key came from letter 1 by frequent words (und, uns, die, daz, du, wir) and was frozen with the lexicon and
the measure script (heldout/KEY.sha256). One measure on letter 2: lexical coverage 0.580 / 0.548 (passes A / B)
against at most 0.073 for 200 permuted keys: preregistered verdict PARTIAL (threshold 0.85 not reached; the lexicon,
letter-1 words plus a hand list, misses the new words of letter 2, whose decryption reads throughout). heldout/
keeps the files as frozen (the scripts expect the original file names TRANSCRIPTION-A/B.txt).

**Reading.** reading_120.md (546 words, 99.1 % read by sense, strict), reading_121.md (88 words, 98.9 %); translations in
translation_120.md and translation_121.md. Independent AI review: grade A for both (profile.json).

**Content.** Letter 1: the lord's debt to Krumel (deferment of a month or six weeks, or repayment against the return of
the letters of debt with a sealed receipt; Electors' gulden struck before the letter's date, four gulden per hundred as
surcharge), new lenders' securities, a possible loan from Godart von Flodrop for a redemption (losunge), and the Vogt of
Roermond's money. Letter 2: Russebruck and Hucking the rent-master to be at Aachen on Friday evening or Saturday morning
with the money and letters; Krumel to come; the lord's envoy will be there; Heinsberg to be kept safe meanwhile.

**Open.** Letter 1 r18 ("3rude", "sen"), r35 ("gedin"); letter 2 l. 9 ("dame"). The d/b strokes of the transcriptions.
