# Letter 1 (fol. 120r-v): English translation

Working translation of reading_120.md; [?] marks a probable word, [...] an unresolved one. The sender is not named in the
cipher; the address on the back ("To our dear faithful Johann von Russebruck") and the "we/us" style fit the lord of
Heinsberg, Count Johann II of Nassau-Saarbrücken, writing to his officer.

Dear faithful one. As you lately wrote to us about all the [?hindrances] concerning the money and the exchange, and that
Krumel had spoken with you on the way, that he would gladly stay there; and since the time is now short and might well
need longer, we think it good that you spoke with him about the matter he discussed with you. You were to consider these
things: whether he would let the matter rest for a [?month] or six weeks, honestly and in good faith, and put it off; but
if we could not agree on the time within which we should then give him his money, he should take it without
[?prejudice] and give us back our letters, and at once give a letter and receipt for it under his seal, so that we are
secured. And so that you secure yourself in this and earn no ingratitude from us, please write us at once his offer and
intention. In the meantime we shall come close [?at hand] ourselves, and once we are together we might come to a further
agreement than you can now negotiate by messenger across the country.

And should you come to terms with him on this, it would be good that someone sealed with him, and [...] here in [...]
the most suitable way to serve to that end; and none the less to arrange the money as we wrote to you before, and to
bring it together, and not to rest day and night in it.

We also think it good that you spoke with him, because his letters stipulate Electors' gulden struck before the date of
the letter, and you suspect that we would have to exchange them at a loss. If he would now take, on every hundred in
other gold and money, four gulden as a surcharge, that would still be money just as good to pay out, and no one would
leave it untaken from him on that account; so he would have the surcharge on it now and hereafter. And it would be as
good that he took the surcharge as the money-changers do. Or if that cannot be, then at least he should take other
gulden and Electors' coin as good as those struck before the date of the letter, and whatever is worse, the surcharge on
it in good faith; and that this too be expressly stated and provided for in the aforesaid letter.

You may also put it to him that it would make us more willing towards him if we heard that he would [...] [?keep] it
fairly with us, since his letters are drawn up with many hazards and had to be sealed that way at the time; then we
should be together, so as to know how the hazards may be removed. And see whether you can achieve anything in this; and
if that happens, all matters should rest in good order until our further instruction.

You were lately parted from us [with the charge] to let us know what bond and security those who now lend us money want
to have; also whether Godart von [?Flodrop] would lend us something, should need come upon us, for the redemption. Of
that you have written us nothing; so write us every circumstance. And as you wrote to us about the Vogt of Roermond,
please speak with him not to leave us without the money at that time; afterwards we might come to a further agreement
with each other than we can do now.
