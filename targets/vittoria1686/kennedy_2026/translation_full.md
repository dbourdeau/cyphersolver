# Catalogue 1570 (`vittoria1686`) — Complete 4-Letter Translation (English, Matthew Kennedy, Nov 2026)

## Letter 1 — `cc. 3r–4v` (Naples, Santa Chiara, 2 April 1686)

### c. 3v

...concerning Don Paolo Ruffo, we are desperate in our own way; he wants to lift the sequestration from San Paolo and we fear [that] he will proceed to the sale of the property; we have begged him by letter and in person, and we have accomplished nothing.

The Marquis of Casalnuovo was negotiating in the Vicaria to dispatch a writ of execution (*secutorio*) against San Paolo for his revenues; Your Excellency has kept [us unaware] of this matter—we know nothing of it. Please do us the favor of letting us know what is going on.

We have already reached the point [that] all the dogs have awakened together; there is no other remedy [than] a safeguard from Spain to settle everyone for at least 10 years, just as many noble houses enjoy it, and in particular [the Prince of] Cariati and many others. This Lord [Viceroy Marquis del Carpio] knows not how to do a favor for anyone, and the more prominent the Houses are, the more he strikes them down, just as a great part of the foremost houses have died in the castle [prison], and the rest, if God does not provide, will follow after; thus there is no person one can trust.

### c. 4r

In the Collateral Council, since [Regent Galeota?] passed away, they are all Pulcinellas [buffoons/puppets], and they do not remember friends, and they do not let any [decrees] go out anymore; and from experience we know that after granting the favor and taking the money, they revoke it. ...but not like the Regent of Sanctoro, because San Paolo is slipping out of our hands; one would need to move in haste (*di volata*) ... For the love of God have pity on us, for we cannot trust anyone, we are nuns ... and thinking that something might happen [that] is not to your liking pierces our soul.

*(Lower half of c. 4r — not included in PR #226:)* ...this gentleman of Rome is the most gracious and virtuous son one could wish for, how attentive he is to expenses, to the accounts—he is a marvel of Nature. Your Excellency kindly tells us [that] you wish to provide for him so he has need of no one. We thank you infinitely, yet we beg Your Excellency for charity's sake to let us see the effects, since our sleep is always interrupted by distress over every peasant of San Paolo. [Every peasant] has the means to live, and this son does not have a single crust of bread assigned [to him].

When he was in Naples he spoke these words to us: "I pray God shorten my own years and add [them] to my Lord Father, and grant health to my Most Serene Lady that [she] soon feel/hear the birth of a little Prince, so that I may have the honor of being his servant; and when I see myself at his feet, I know well that he will not cast me away, at least out of his generosity."

## Letter 2 — `cc. 11r–12v` (Naples, Santa Chiara, 13 April 1688)

### c. 11r

Antonio sent me just as I was writing a summons (*citatione*) from Don Paolo Ruffo [for] the 1,000 ducats of Bisceglie together with the interest at 5 per cent.

## Letter 3 — `cc. 22r–23v` (Naples, Santa Chiara, 4 September 1691)

### c. 22r

Friar Basilio has remained and went to San Nicandro because the bishop wanted to imprison him. Don Giovanni has written to Monsignor of Molfetta...

### c. 22v

It is true that with the bishop it is a wretched life. The Patron of Marsilia...

### c. 23r

And if I did not have the bridge [dispute/toll] [that] troubles me, I would enjoy a few months of peace.

He is here in Naples and is completely in league with the Lieutenant of the Chamber and [?letters/writs] [of] injunctions (*precetti*); I could not afford to alienate him.

## Letter 4 — `cc. 20r–21v` (Naples, Santa Chiara, 16 October 1691)

### c. 20r

...this lay within his discretion, [so as] not to disrupt the shipment/charge, [which] would have been the greatest loss [and] injury to [Your Excellency]. Pompeo then had the affidavits drawn up...

### c. 21r

I say that Your Excellency should investigate the entire charge and then make a clean cut. Enclosed here are 4 bank vouchers...

The [Duke] of Torremaggiore [Paolo IV di Sangro] had Pompeo write to me, [that] he wants [that] I procure for him 50 wagonloads (*carre*) of grain export licenses, and I replied with polite formalities and [that] for the present the export licenses were not open, and the Royal Chamber kept them behind iron gates, as indeed is true, reserving them for the State of Milan. I am suspicious by nature, Your Excellency knows it, and so I form the impression [that] he is in need of money, even though he summoned his agent...
