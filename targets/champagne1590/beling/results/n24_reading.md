Gist [I]: a newsletter from the royal camp before Paris, 11-15 June 1590: no hope of peace; Guise's secretary refused;
copies only through La Guiche, who is ill at Compiègne; the King's night march to stop Saint-Pol at Meaux; trouble at
Senlis over a commission refused as onerous; famine in Paris; La Chastre said to be negotiating through Madame la
Princesse and Beauvais-Nangis; the failed surprise of the Vincennes keep, foiled by Givry; the army's quarters around
Paris on the 14th; Richelieu (the grand prévôt) dead and his office given to Fontenay; Brigard (procureur of Paris)
refused a passport to Mayenne; Mayenne's failed siege of Crécy-en-Laonnois; Vitry and Tremont come over to the King.

## No. 60, f. 88r (slip, undated here; 7 June 1590 per the target's notes)

1 Ce que je vous ay escript du s[ieu]r {Ludouic} (19 56 93 38 67 93 53 37; glossed "L u d o u ic" over 56 93 38 67 93 53 37)
2 est vray, et apres avoir veu l'extraict de vos lettres,
3 ausquelles je satisfairé plus a loisir, [?il] me l'a
4 confirmé et dict qu'il [?s']en a dict. Et dira ce qu'il
5 en est aussi {de Launan} (39 38 43 56 35 94 62 32 64).

Glosses over the l. 5 run [V]: e (43), l (56), a (35), u (94), n (62, written like p), a (32, with a stroke), and over 64 a
y-shaped letter with a dot. So the decipherer wrote **"Launay"**, though 64 is *n* in key no. 25. Either the encipherer put
64 for 96 (*y*), or the decipherer wrote the name as he knew it [I]. Read "de Launay".
At the right edge of ll. 1-4 there are marks in a greyer ink (like "ce", "7", "est", "pro", "No"), over a stain; not read,
possibly a later hand [I].
Gist [I]: what I wrote to you about the sieur Ludovic is true; having seen the extract of your letters, which I will answer
at more leisure, he confirmed it to me and said that he had said so; and he will say how things stand with [de] Launay too.
