What I gave you notice of by the despatch of the [said] s[ieu]r was contained in that of the 7th, which one who belonged to the s[ieu]r de Chazeron will have delivered to you. I informed you of the little hope of peace, of the name of the secretary of {the duc de Guise}, the refusal made to him, the impossibility of having the copies you desire. The only means being through that of {M. la Guiche}. But his violent illness, which keeps him at Compiegne, makes it impossible. Brandon was obliged to leave in haste to hear what he had to say to him, he having sent for him for that purpose. He begs you to excuse him if he has not sent you a despatch.

What concerned the affair of Balagny [?Sire] that Selincourt had told me of it: how it would not be possible to have the capitulation not kept at Sens, and the truth of the affair {of Ludouic}. The rumour of the [?blowing-up] of the League's powder and the reversion of the Bishop of Langres, and how much the journey to [?Chauny] had set back the hope of the enterprise of [?Paris]. The {intelligences} that are there, which are said to grow from day to day.

Now, to tell you of this dash, rather than journey, of the King would be to wrong Monsieur de [?Lamay], who will be able to testify to you that the number of horse the King had [?that same day] comes near that of the battle, within five hundred. It is held for certain [?that] M[onsieu]r de Mayenne, after a ban and arriere-ban proclaimed on his behalf throughout the Beauvaisis, has not been able to gather more than four gentlemen who came to join him.

The King returned on the 11th very late, and on a warning that S[ain]t Paul, arrived at Meaux, meant to pass, wished to pass that same night to go to Paris. He remounted, marched the army into the plain of [?Roissy], having sent ahead {the comte de Soissons} com[panies] to go to Dammartin to cut off his passage from Meaux. But the King found him at Senlis, whence there has been sedition [?or] some discontent, and in fact he stayed at Senlis ill disposed: {the King ha[d]} refused {hi[m]} this commission as burdensome. But he pointed out to him that it belonged to him as leading the vanguard. The march of o[u]r army broke S[ain]t Paul's design. Another report came the next day that it was that our reinforcing troops left their quarters and went as far as Montfaucon. But nothing was undertaken that day either. Ten or twelve prisoners were taken.

We have seen here some of the bread that is given to the poor in Paris. It is a wretched thing to tell, so poor it is and pitiful to see. These prompt moves of the prince have broken S[ain]t Paul's designs. He has crossed [?the] Marne and entered Brie, where he has forced some quarters, which he put to the fire rather cruelly. It is said that {M. de la Chastre} is being worked on, as much by means of Mad[am]e the princess as through the mediation of Beauvais Nangis, who has come to the camp.

An attempt by 4 soldiers of the wood of [?Vincennes], who had seized the keep, came to nothing through the s[ieu]r de Givry, who had been warned. Those inside hanged them and threw them, in his presence, through the battlements. On the 14th the army left Gonesse. The King lodged at Aubervilliers, the artillery at Le Bourget, the others at La Villette, Montmartre and La Chapelle.

The s[ieu]r de Richelieu is dead; his office of grand provost has been given to Fontenay du Val, otherwise [?Esperon], who lends 5000 livres, with which the King secures his creditors. To that of captain of the guards no one is appointed. Brigard came to the King to have a passport to go to the duc de Mayenne. It was refused him, and he was told that when he spoke of returning to the King's obedience, he would be heard for himself and the city of Paris, of which he is procureur. It is said he answered that before that happens the inhabitants will set fire to the four corners.

Crecy-en-Laonnois was battered with seven cannon or artillery by the duc de Mayenne as soon as the King had moved away; [?the year] it withstood two assaults, in which three hundred men were killed; the rest took fright at a relief that came to the town from the [?neighbouring] garrisons, and raised the siege. Today also Vitry and Tremont saw the King near Paris and spoke with him a long time. [?The League] [...] him two pieces of cannon to fire into Paris; it will be on the hill of Belleville if it can be done.

Of 15 June 1590.
