# No. 32: English translation of the working edition

This translates `EDITION.md`, including clear-witness assistance. It is not a translation independently obtained from the cipher. Square brackets identify uncertainty or supplied context; A–O match the edition. The text starts in the middle of reported conversations whose complete speaker framework belongs to the accompanying diary. It is therefore deliberately cautious about pronoun referents. Period terms are explained at the end.

**A — Denmark and the Lower Saxon Circle.** On the occasion of the [hard-fought battle?], he had also asked the Mecklenburg envoy what he thought would come of it: since the Swedes were thus continually growing stronger and might eventually oppress Germany altogether, what should the Imperial Estates do? Should the Lower Saxon Circle in particular put itself into a position of readiness, especially since the House of Brunswick-Lüneburg still had the means and opportunity, yet was now abandoning Denmark entirely? He had replied that, as far as he could see, Brunswick-Lüneburg would no longer fight Sweden, while Mecklenburg and the other Lower Saxon Estates had no resources. [Clemi?], the Danish secretary, then indicated that Denmark would eventually make peace with Sweden on the best terms it could obtain, although Cranius, the imperial envoy, had only recently reproached him [adverb unread, G02] for such peace negotiations.

**B — a limited peace and restitution.** He informed me in particular, in strict confidence and on the authority of the Lübeck envoy, that the deputies and the other envoys associated with them inclined only toward a superficial peace. The doctor [Olhafen?] from [Nürnberg?], for instance, had indicated that he would be satisfied with restitution to the year [72? in the fair copy; 27 in the revised draft]. Consequently, the Protestant side should not rely heavily on their assistance and should take greater care in sharing its deliberations. The draft also specifies Franconian deputies. The numerical discrepancy is not resolved into a calendar year.

**C — the archbishop's intervention.** Since he also mentioned the intercession of the archbishop of Magdeburg, I briefly suggested to him on this occasion that I did not believe His Princely Highness would knowingly and freely have allowed himself to be used in such an unaccustomed, or indeed [unjust—draft-assisted; cipher word unsettled, G06], undertaking. Since, however, it was a weighty matter on which the welfare or misfortune of Electoral Saxony and the neighboring territories depended, in my humble opinion the elector of Saxony could not be blamed for first giving it mature consideration.

**D — reporting, neutrality and Magdeburg.** Lampadius considered these arguments sound and well founded. Both he and the Mecklenburg envoy had therefore advised me, in strict confidence, to report them respectfully and fully, with all their circumstances. The Mecklenburg envoy in particular thought that these arguments were being advanced with a certain exceptional prudence precisely so that this report would inform Electoral Saxony, and convey the impression that [unresolved connective, G07] Sweden intended to induce it to adopt different counsels, and ultimately neutrality, through kindness and mildness rather than force or compulsion. The Swedes would have ample means of coercion, especially after this victory granted by God.

Lampadius further mentioned that, if the [undertaking?] could be secured through the archbishop of Magdeburg, and particularly the restitution of the fortress of Magdeburg—as a defense and support not only of the archbishopric but of all Lower Saxony, and because [a clause concerning Bavaria, Electoral Saxony and the Elbe is unread, G08]—the House of Brunswick-Lüneburg would gladly help. Although it could accomplish little with the [emperor?] in this matter, it would willingly lend the archbishop, on request, several companies as an initial garrison, just as it had previously supplied the duke of Mecklenburg with a whole regiment for Rostock. “Lend” follows the clear copy's *herleihen*; the inherited cipher literal instead resembles “help.”

**E — Salvius in writing.** And indeed, as the Mecklenburg envoy informed me, [this came] from Salvius in writing. The short note's referent belongs to the surrounding report; the supplied English does not identify a separate document that has been found.

**F — six reasons for declining.** His principal reasons for refusing had been these:

1. One-sided [unread noun, G09] would not be compatible with the cooperation offered on both sides. “Offered” follows the revised draft; the tentative fair-copy reading was “agreed on.” The noun is not translated as military arming or partisan attachment.
2. Brunswick-Lüneburg had neither been constituted nor authorized to negotiate in the name of all the Estates.
3. Such proceedings were dangerous, as the examples of the Peace of Prague showed.
4. Electoral Brandenburg had already been deputed in the year [blank or unresolved]; the arrival of its [representatives?] should be awaited and their opinion heard. The wording leaves uncertain whether Brandenburg had been appointed or had sent representatives; the translation does not resolve that syntax.
5. It was not enough to deliberate or undertake something: there also had to be practical effect and force behind it.
6. Although Brunswick-Lüneburg still remained in a state of readiness of sorts, it alone was far too weak to undertake and sustain a difficult enterprise. If other Estates joined and took it up together—especially Electoral Brandenburg, the archbishop of Magdeburg and the dukes of Weimar, concerning whom he had specific instructions—he himself would wait no longer. He would willingly begin the business with united intentions and counsels and do what his instructions, which were sufficiently broad, and the needs of the situation required.

The diary on Nr. 540 frame 0646 supports Lampadius as the reported speaker of these reasons; that identity is documentary context, not newly deciphered from this heading. The revised draft’s “deputed to the negotiations” language does not settle item 4’s actor syntax or missing year.

**G — Augsburg and negotiations.** The Augsburg [envoy] had indeed hoped to find the negotiations begun and some progress made. He, however, had answered that they should thank God and good people for having held the business back thus far. Nothing had yet been missed; everything remained open, and every Estate could still express its views and requests, both generally and specifically. Otherwise, whatever had once been agreed, or even merely proposed, would probably have to stand, and new articles or proposals could not be made every day.

**H — Württemberg, Constance and amnesty.** Both had also reported the miserable situation higher up in the Empire, and especially the pressure on Württemberg, whose representatives could hardly say a word freely. Nevertheless, despite this and the fact that most Estates of the Swabian Circle were Catholic, Württemberg and Constance had not only summoned the Circle's assembly; it was [subsequently?] decided to send representation to the negotiations on behalf of the Circle, even against the emperor's will. Württemberg had also raised the matter of the general amnesty. Constance, for substantial reasons, had judged it more advisable that the amnesty—although they otherwise willingly wished it for the Protestants and wanted to promote it—should not be expressly mentioned either in the instructions or in the proposition. Both were instead framed in purely general terms.

This translation makes the reported sequence intelligible without treating the copy's irregular connective syntax as a newly recovered, regular cipher sentence.

**I — Bavaria and Salzburg.** The Bavarian [envoy] too had professed that he wished likewise to summon a Circle assembly and have it deliberate on sending representation to the negotiations. Salzburg, however, as joint convener, had prudently declined, fearing that bad neighbor: if Bavaria learned of Salzburg's counsels, it might lodge a complaint against Salzburg with the emperor and seek an opportunity to harm or harass it. The referents Bavaria and Salzburg in this last explanation are interpretive expansions of the Latin pronouns, not additional cipher text.

**J — the Danish secretary's questions.** [Clemi?], the Danish secretary, had likewise tried to probe and discover various matters from him, perhaps prompted by the imperial party. He had rebuffed the secretary briefly and directly with approximately the following questions and answers.

**K — question 1.** What did Lampadius himself think of this Swedish success and the outcome of the war?

Answer: Long experience had taught him that outcomes seldom corresponded to plans. He would therefore make no further judgments about the future, but entrust the whole matter to God.

**L — question 2.** Should not the Imperial Estates, especially the Protestants, come to the emperor's assistance?

Answer: In the present circumstances this could be done only through counsels of peace.

**M — question 3.** But they ought to take the matter into their own hands and do something different about it.

Answer: He had offered his services to the imperial party. Since they did not wish to accept them, he was much more inclined to thank them than to press himself upon them further. The cipher-oriented reading leaves the Latin form *Caesareis* uncertain; the clear copy has *Caesarianis*.

**N — question 4.** What, then, was a suitable remedy, especially since the emperor was in danger of losing his whole position and the Turk also threatened?

Answer: The best remedy would be for the emperor to adopt more moderate counsels, make peace, sincerely reconcile the Imperial Estates, and lead both armies against the Turk.

**O — question 5.** Would the Swedes really do that?

Answer: He could not promise it with certainty, but thought that they would, or at least that, once peace had been made, they would disband their army. And since payment of the soldiery would in any case proceed slowly, those troops [could—the uncertain cipher-oriented reading; would have to—the clear witness] meanwhile be employed for that purpose.

## Period glosses and translation limits

*ReichsStände* means the Imperial Estates, the political members of the Empire; it does not mean modern nation-states. *Creyß* is an Imperial Circle, and *Creyßtag* its assembly. *Beschicken* here means send representatives to, not simply write to. *Posture* and *in postur* describe military readiness or capacity. *Restitution* concerns restoration to an earlier legal or territorial condition, but the disputed year in B is not inferred. *Vires* means resources or strength; *res in integro* means the matter remains open or unprejudiced; *taliter qualiter* means after a fashion; *satis larga* means sufficiently broad. *Päbstisch* is the writer's confessional label, translated “Catholic.” *Turca* is the period expression “the Turk,” referring to the Ottoman adversary; the wording reflects the document. “Both armies” in N is retained without supplying army identities not expressed in that answer.
