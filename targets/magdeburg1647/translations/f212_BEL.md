# No. 31 — English of the source-assisted working edition

This follows `EDITION.md`; clear-copy expansions and all uncertainties remain source assistance. The annex is attached to the diary closed at Osnabrück on 12 March 1645. Several opening pronouns depend on that diary; they are not silently assigned to named speakers. Plans, accusations and secret reports below are what the document reports.

**α.** Especially since they are Imperial Estates, and many among them are Protestant, who can expect little from the French.

**β.** Perhaps this was because correspondence with the emperor was still being conducted weekly from Wolfenbüttel. That might arouse suspicion and prejudice the princely houses of Celle and Calenberg or Dr. Lampadius, or make them answer for it. Lampadius himself had made this quite clearly understood, in approximately these words: “Why should I or my lords have to answer for Duke August of Wolfenbüttel's counsels?” The clear copy supplies these names; the old cipher connective remains imperfectly read.

**γ.** He also reiterated his heartfelt wish for the archbishop of Magdeburg to come here. His Princely Highness—particularly if Electoral Saxony's neutrality took effect and respect for his father no longer stood in his way—could do much for the Protestant cause, and would undoubtedly do so. “Come here” explicates the copy's compressed wording; it is not an additional deciphered word.

**δ.** He considered it certain that, if the fighting and ruin of the imperial army continued, this would not only advance the peace negotiations in general but also give Electoral Saxony even more reason to become neutral. The sentence does not itself identify the battle.

**ε.** This was also one of the reasons why, to avoid all suspicion, he did not yet converse much with the Swedes. He also repeated the matters in paragraph δ. The final cross-reference already appears in ordinary writing in the cipher witness.

**ζ.** The Mecklenburg envoy reported, on the authority of [Klein?], secretary to the king of Denmark:

1. To avoid a greater evil, the Danish king would satisfy the Dutch.
2. Cranius, the imperial envoy, had reproached [Klein?] because the Danish king was contemplating peace with Sweden contrary to his agreement with the emperor. The secretary replied: (a) These had only been negotiations, without a definite conclusion or alliance. (b) The imperial assistance under [Gallas?] had harmed his king more than it had helped him. (c) The imperial party could not prevent even the elector of Bavaria from employing various expedients to preserve his territories and entering negotiations or alliances with one party or another without consulting the emperor.
3. Among the articles of their instructions, the French also had the principle that they should make haste slowly in both places.
4. The French were contemplating—something he reported to me as an exceptionally deep secret—taking the succession in the German Empire away from the House of Austria and conferring that dignity on the Bavarian. The Bavarian would scarcely accept it himself because of his advanced age; but what if they brought matters to the point where his son, or another male relative of his house, were made King of the Romans?

The secretary's surname is still bracketed; his full identity is not claimed as a cipher discovery. “Both places” is retained without supplying town names absent from this clause. “King of the Romans” is the title in the text, not a paraphrase as an already reigning emperor. The proposed succession scheme remains reported intelligence.

**η.** During a recent visit Salvius had suggested that Torstensson would join forces with the Austrian peasants, arm them, then proceed directly into Styria, likewise relieve Olomouc, and hopefully see Electoral Saxony's neutrality secured on his side. These are reported expectations, not a claim that each action occurred. The clear copy supplies the named actors and places; the corresponding cipher wording is still imperfect.

**θ.** If only they would append the general clause that this principal proposition should take nothing away from, or prejudice, the Imperial Estates' particular interests and private requests: he had shown himself inclined to this in dealings with both the Hessian and Mecklenburg envoys. A cancelled phrase in the clear copy and an additional sign in the cipher prevent treating every word of this sentence as a settled exact collation.

Period vocabulary: the Imperial Estates are the political members of the Empire; *neutrality* here is a proposed contemporary political arrangement; *desideria* are requests or desired terms; *succurs* is military assistance; *agnatus* is a male-line relative. The historical territorial forms Celle, Calenberg, Wolfenbüttel and Styria translate the copy's Zell, Calenberg, Wolffenbüttel and Steuermarck. No modern geographical boundary is implied.
