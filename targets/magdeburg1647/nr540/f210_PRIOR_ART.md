# Prior-art and identity search, 12 November 2026

This records a bounded continuation, not an exhaustive literature search. No claim that the annex was unread or unpublished follows from negative searches.

The University of Bonn identifies Tina Braun's 2015 dissertation, *Der welfische Gesandte Jakob Lampadius auf dem Westfälischen Friedenskongress (1644–1649)*, as a study based on Lampadius's weekly reports and his work with other representatives. It is therefore a strong next source for the March 1645 conversation and its prior treatment: [official repository record](https://bonndoc.ulb.uni-bonn.de/xmlui/handle/20.500.11811/6360), [Bonn research description](https://www.zhf.uni-bonn.de/forschung-am-zhf/abgeschlossene-projekte/fertiggestellte-dissertationen).

The old `https://hss.ulb.uni-bonn.de/2015/4104/4104.pdf` address was tried with curl. The sandbox attempt failed DNS; an approved read-only network retry returned an HTML migration page, not a PDF. The current repository record appeared in search, but direct web opening returned an Anubis access-denied page. An earlier CORE PDF URL returned 404. **The dissertation full text was not obtained or searched in this pass.** This is an access limitation, not an exhausted source. No anti-bot challenge was bypassed.

A useful contemporary context source surfaced: [G. Keller to Grotius, 30 March 1645, edited correspondence no. 7393](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/groo001brie16_01/groo001brie16_01_0321.php). The indexed text mentions arrival of Württemberg, Nürnberg and Ulm representatives and editorial identification of Tobias Oelhafen. This makes the proposed Nürnberg/Olhafen reading contextually plausible, but does not independently establish the uncertain letters in our annex or its restitution year. The direct page was not successfully retrieved; no extended quotation or page-level collation was used.

Maria-Elisabeth Brunert's study of the Sachsen-Altenburg instructions also identifies Oelhafen with Nürnberg and the Franconian representation: [journal PDF](https://nasg.publia.org/nasg/article/download/1137/953/998). Its focus is later instructions, and no exact edition of the March No. 32 annex was established from it. The contextual identification remains explicitly tentative in the edition.

Searches did not settle the Danish secretary's name or the 27/72 discrepancy. Search snippets are discovery aids, not a substitute for checking editions.
