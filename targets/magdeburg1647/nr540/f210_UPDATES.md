## δ coverage: numbering jump, not an omitted physical line

The original cipher page, LASA A 1 Nr. 530 frame 0240, f. 210r, has **13 δ text baselines**. The old transcription also contains thirteen δ records, labelled 25–36 and 38. Its missing label 37 was therefore a numbering jump, not evidence for an additional wholly untranscribed manuscript line. `delta_crosswalk.tsv` maps physical D01–D13 to those legacy records; it retains each inherited cipher string exactly.

The full-annex package's explicitly marked missing-slot placeholder can be **retired in a future edition**, with this explanation preserved. Its 104-slot inventory remains frozen and unmodified. Removing the artificial placeholder gives 103 inherited transcription records for that assembled inventory; this does not make their letters complete or correct. In particular, physical D13 / legacy38 is only partly transcribed and extends into the top of f. 210v. Its inherited literal ends roughly “... die sonst,” while further letter groups and the precise Elbe clause remain unsettled. No fluent cipher completion has been substituted for that gap.

## γ adjective: draft reads “unbillig”

Nr. 529 frame 0093, left-margin γ, `crops/draft_gamma.jpg`, supports the proposed phrase:

> ... in dergleichen ungewohnten oder gar **unbillig werck** ...

English: “... in such an unaccustomed, or even unjust, undertaking ...” (*Unreasonable* is another possible contextual rendering of *unbillig*.) The coordinator independently saw “oder gar unbillig werck.” This resolves the word at the **draft-witness** level. The fair copy's corresponding form and cipher group still need exact collation, so it is not promoted as a newly verified cipher word. Cancelled and inserted draft material is not silently merged into a diplomatic reading.

## δ Elbe clause: actors clearer, verb still open

The fair copy and revised draft footnote both support a clause involving **Bayern** and **ChurSachsen**, with **die Elbe** visible in the fair copy. `crops/fair_elbe.jpg` and `draft_delta_foot_wide.jpg` isolate the comparison. Neither reader resolved the remaining verb securely. The earlier full edition's G08 therefore remains open; no historical proposition about who controlled, crossed, disturbed or secured the Elbe is asserted.

## ζ1: first noun remains open

The revised draft at Nr. 529 frame 0094 clearly supplies *utrinque offerirten Cooperation*, improving on the tentative fair-copy reading *conferirten*. The first noun, previously proposed as *Beywaffnung*, is still doubtful; the draft/fair-copy letter shapes also invite *Beyhafftung/Beyhaltung*. These alternatives are not sufficiently established to translate the noun definitively as military arming or partisan attachment. A cautious interim rendering is: “The one-sided [unread noun] would not be compatible with the cooperation offered on both sides.” The fixed-key literal is preserved; there is no key tuning.

## ζ4: revised deputation wording, not a recovered year

The revised draft at Nr. 529 frame 0094 has a heavily altered passage naming ChurBrandenburg and visibly mentioning **“zu den tracta-/ten ... deputirt”**. It does not securely provide the missing calendar year of the fair-copy *Anno* passage. This supports a deputation/negotiation context but does not decide whether the copy means Brandenburg was deputed, or had deputed representatives. Neither the actor syntax nor a date should be repaired silently. G10 remains open, now with this precise draft comparison.

## Diary context for α and ζ

Nr. 540 Bd. 1 frame 0644, f. 595r, under **15 March**, says the writer met the Mecklenburg envoy, who reported *ex relatione* of the Danish royal secretary. The passage contrasts external ceremonies with private conversation and ends with an **α** reference. This supplies a documentary frame for the annex: the Danish secretary's conversation is relayed by the Mecklenburg envoy. It is not a newly deciphered speaker name. See `crops/diary32_alpha_context.jpg`.

Frame 0646, f. 596v, describes Lampadius coming to the writer and recounting discussions, including a proposed cooperation involving the Swedish envoys, with **ε** and **ζ** references. This supports Lampadius as the person whose reasons for declining are developed in ζ; the cramped intervening syntax is not fully transcribed here. See `diary32_zeta_full.jpg`. No unexpressed direct quotation has been invented from that context.

The adjoining No. 31 clear annex now offers better comparative forms of the Danish secretary's surname, provisionally **Kleins/Cleino**. A modern Osnabrück museum exhibition identifies a Danish resident Johann Leonhard Klein, which is contextual support, not a secure reading of the No. 32 name: [Dänisches Quartier](https://www.westfaelischerfrieden-os.de/stadtrundgang/d%C3%A4nisches-quartier/). The full identification remains tentative.
