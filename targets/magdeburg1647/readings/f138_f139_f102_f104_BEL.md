## Existing period decipherment is not a new solution

The left slip on 0161/0162 has **two cipher lines and two clear Latin lines**. The latter are an existing period gloss/key instruction: new signs refer, first, to a new imperial envoy, and, second, to religion and liberty. The clear abbreviation `pri(us)` is preferable to reader A's *utriq(ue)*, and `novum` to A's *notum*; the final imperial adjective remains an expansion alternative. This is evidence already in the manuscript, not plaintext newly recovered independently by us.

The cipher line contains adjacent crossed-loop/upright and Γ-shaped marks in the position corresponding to *Legatum Caesarianum/Caesareum*. That combined phrase correspondence does **not** by itself prove a one-to-one assignment for either sign, or prove identity with similar crossed-loop signs on other pages. The note's instruction about “former/latter” new signs cannot safely be turned into “this drawn crossed loop = this named actor” without determining what signs it refers to in its original context. No such symbol key is added here.

The clear gloss also exposes shared A/B errors: both transcribed open-c-shaped glyphs as `e`, producing literal *duae* and *duidem* where the period gloss has *quae* and *quidem*. The frozen alphabet already contains cipher `c`→plain `q`; this is a transcription/convention issue, not grounds to change the key. *signz* versus *signa*, and *posteriora* versus the gloss's *posteri(us)*, are likewise visible. `shared_error_ledger.tsv` records these differences. Agreement between readers is not proof of a correct word; the period gloss must not be counted as fresh validation data for a reading made while looking at it.

## Editorial ledger and conventions

Every row below contains complete frozen cipher, unchanged-key literal output, proposed German/Latin and English. `⟨A ∥ B⟩` retains unresolved source variants. Clear text in braces and graphical descriptions in `[S:...]` pass through the decoder unchanged, so they are not its output. `[?...]` marks supplied/repaired readings, including context-assisted clear Kurrent. Cancelled material is retained as cancellation; a black blot is not automatically a code symbol. Capitalization, spaces and routine u/v presentation are editorial.

Important repairs remain explicit: No.3's recent reminder/fifth-point phrase; No.20's *serius apprehendiret*, instructions/orders, and lost mediator; No.22's *Gegensatisfaction*, *ad dies vitae*, *declinanda sua aliisque concilianda invidia*; Appendix's *Reformierten*, *Wesenbecio*, *schärfer* (literal *scharfner*), place-name readings, and broken final alpha sentence. Latin *quae/quidem* on the instruction comes from the visible period gloss; the literal anomalies remain beside it. English translates the proposed reading with holes and uncertain constructions, and is not evidence independent of that proposal.
