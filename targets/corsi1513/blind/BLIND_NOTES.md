# Blind transcription notes (map137, Corsi letters 1513)

Transcribed from the four images and SHEET.md only. No key used, nothing deciphered.

## BLIND.tsv format

- One row per line segment of a cipher run. Where a run carries on to the next line, the next row's 4th column reads "(cont. from Lnn)".
- Lines are numbered from 1 at the first text line of each image (folio numbers and dockets are not counted).
- Superscripts: assigned to the sign whose upper right they sit over. Where a digit sits between two signs, the assignment is doubtful (marked "?" where it matters).
- A run is closed by clear Italian. The 4th column gives the clear words immediately before the run (my reading of the clear text, approximate).

Runs (continuation rows merged):
- 1329: 9 runs: L11-14, L15-16a, L16b-17, L18-19, L20, L21-23a, L23b-24a, L24b-25, L26 (L26 continues onto 1330 L1).
- 1330: 1 row (L1), the end of the 1329 L26 run.
- 1341: 4 runs: L2-3, L4-8, L11-14, L18-22.

## map137_A0001329.jpg

- What it is: front page (recto) of a Corsi letter, text starting mid-letter ("Abbiano non le piglierebbe ...") and running to the foot. 26 text lines. Lines 1-10 are clear. Lines 11-26 are mostly cipher with clear stretches.
- Numbers visible: "628 bis" (struck-through older number?) and "126" at top right; "92" at bottom left.
- No contemporary decipherment between lines or at the foot.
- Show-through from the verso is visible but faint.
- Difficulties:
  - Superscripts are small. Several faint ones may be show-through (e.g. L11 "s" before "o t 8 #23", read without superscript).
  - Descenders from the line above cross some signs (L23 "q1?" after a7 has a stroke through it, probably the L22 "q" descender).
  - "12" (flat 2 on the baseline) and "13" (3 written as "ʒ" with a descender) look alike at low zoom. I checked each at high zoom: #12 = flat, #13 = descending.
  - L1 "ne fu R/to" and L8 "Fu R/to" are clear-text abbreviations with a crossed R. L20 "ho R/to. et" is the same (clear), just before a run.
  - L10 ends "degli altri .d." Possibly an abbreviation. Treated as clear.
  - L24 "ed·" (written "zd·", with a looped d) stands between two cipher stretches. Read as the clear word "ed", but it could be a cipher group (NEW3). 1330 L1 ends the same way ("q3 ed. Ad alt° salvo ...").
  - L25 "e+ &" before "dalle giornate" read as clear "et ... (che/et)".
- Base forms used (my conventions): t = the "ε/ŧ"-shaped t; r = "ɤ" (v with a looped bottom), distinct from v (clean v, L15 "v9"); P = flat-footed P ("Ⲣ", almost always "P3 f"), distinct from p (ordinary p with descender); W = ϖ (barred omega shape), w = plain ω; 8 = closed figure-eight with a flick at the top; d = round "∂".

## map137_A0001341.jpg

- What it is: front page (recto) of a second Corsi letter, 27 text lines. It opens "porti seco la copia della altra mia de 27. Come di sopra ho detto," and ends "a nessuno di questi altri agenti".
- Numbers visible: "631 bis" and "130" at top right; "97" at bottom left.
- L2 starts "~. M. ha da sapere ...". The "~." is probably a clear abbreviation (V.M.?) and is not given a row.
- Clear lines: 1, 9, 10, 15-17, 23-27, plus the openings of L2, L4, L11 and L18. L15 "d 29. del passato" is a clear date, not cipher.
- No contemporary decipherment.
- Difficulties:
  - L12 "b1 y r1" after "K1" is struck through with a horizontal line (a cancelled group). Kept in the row, marked "?".
  - Number groups are written without separators in places: L2 "R/ 1417 19" (read #14 #17 #19); L6 "22 1815" (read #22 #18? #15?); L7 "R/ 1 b 1" (read #161?: the 6 has an ascender like b).
  - Figure-eight "8" here usually has a dot beneath it (L11 twice, L13, L20). The dot is recorded as part of the sign, not as a separate ".".
  - "d" (round ∂ with the loop bent left) occurs at L5, L14, L21, L22. L13 "d?" is a q-like form with a left-curving descender.
  - "z?" marks a flat, 2-shaped sign on the baseline (L7, L8, L14, L18, L20; also 1329 L23). It is not part of a number. Plain "z" is the ʒ form.

## NEW shapes

- NEW1: a short isolated minim stroke "ı" at x-height. It follows q or L (1329 L19 "q ı c", L21 "L1 ı u", L23 "q ı g"; 1341 L5 after m7, L19 "s ı o"). It may be a lowered superscript 1, but it stands at letter level.
- NEW2: an s/5 body with a tall slanting ascender ("ƽ" with an ascender), as though a superscript 1 were fused to the top. 1329 L20, L21, L24.
- NEW3: possible reading of "ed·" ("zd" with a looped d) if it is cipher, not clear. 1329 L24, 1330 L1. Not used as a token in the TSV.
- NEW4: a ξ-shaped sign (epsilon with a descending curl). 1329 L13; 1341 L6 (×2), L7, L8. Possibly what SHEET.md calls "E/" or "sg", but I cannot confirm.
- NEW5: a square with a cross inside (田), at the end of 1341 L3, just before clear text.
- NEW6: a π-shaped sign (1341 L8 "w π f").
- NEW7: a β-shaped sign (B-like with a descender). 1341 L7 "z β m", L8 "z β f".
- "sg?": a flat-topped 5/s with a g-like descending loop (1329 L11). It differs from NEW4.
- "T?": a full-size "7" standing on the baseline as a sign (1329 L13 "c3 T? m", L20 "D1 T? m"; 1341 L13 "P3 T? K"). Labelled T as the nearest letter. It could be a numeral 7.
- "Ri1?": 1329 L12, "ı" + R-like sign + small 1.
- "E/?": 1341 L6, an E with a small cross at the upper right.
- "bt": 1341 L3, a b with a horizontal stroke through the ascender.
- "A": 1330 L1, 1341 L5, L11, L18, an A with a bar over it.
- "R/": a crossed R, 1330 L1, 1341 L2, L7, L14, L22. In 1341 it is mostly at the end of a run or before numerals.
- An "o" followed by a long horizontal tail appears often (o—x, o—X, o—R/). I transcribed it as plain o. It might be the "~" mark.
