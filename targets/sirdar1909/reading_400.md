Cipher: `Vdugk cesux yBypg exyBp xdwet jBpef pzyot Bpdyv` (40 letters).

Frozen key v1 applied as it stands: `·kigdlywiremeagyremarksyoumayha‹z›etomake·`

Reading: (V null) **kindly wire me any remarks you may have to make** (v null). 38 letters and two nulls, the same
null pattern (V first, v last) as HIL/64/402 and the first run of HIL/64/239.

Counted words (10): sure 5, {probable} 5, [restored] 0, ‹unresolved› 0.

- sure: me, you, may, to, make
- {kindly}, {any}: the letter read g stands for n (n is q in the key); g and q are hard to tell apart in these clerks'
  hands, and HIL/64/402 writes q where 239 writes g
- {wire}, {remarks}: the "4"-shaped letter taken as x (= r); both blind passes read x
- {have}: z = v is not in the key; from sense only

All ten words read as sense (100%), half of them sure. **The passage is 40 letters, under the 300-character length of
the read bar**, so this is a short reading, not a reading that meets the bar on its own.

Context: Wingate, as Sirdar, forwards a request from the widow of Colonel Hassan Bey Tewfik that her son, who has passed
the medical examination, be admitted to the Military School, and asks Herbert for his remarks.
