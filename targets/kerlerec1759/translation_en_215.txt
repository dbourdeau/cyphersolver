I have the honour to report to you that nearly half of the [in clear up to here] Choctaw nation is altogether resolved to receive English
traders among them. I have used [every means] to close the way to them through the Alabama nations; for more than two
years I have pulled every string imaginable to this end, but the want of trade goods has at last decided the first of
them to take this course; and according to the latest letters from the commandants of Tombigbee [Fort Tombecbé] and the
Alabamas, the said English traders were on the march to enter the Choctaw nation, and it is much to be feared, my Lord,
that in that case these traders will induce some warriors to strike at the French. A war with that nation would follow,
which we are in no state to sustain for want of goods, and the colony would be in the greatest danger. I have [??]
represented our needs on this head without cease, and I see nothing arrive. We are [??] short of nearly nine hundred men
of the full complement of 2,000. As for gunpowder, we are on the point of running out of it altogether [??]. That is a
most crushing situation for a governor.
