The greatest chance gives me only a moment to take the opportunity of a Spanish friar who is about to sail for Havana.
All my previous letters have announced to you what is happening today: the standard of revolt and independence is
boldly unfurled by the underhand intrigues of an infamous cabal, of which I have given you the details, at whose head
are Messrs de Rochemore, Estrenan [Destréhan] the treasurer, the family of Mr Belile [Belle-Isle] the major, made up of his
son-in-law d'Orville, assistant major, Grondel, Swiss captain, Messrs d'Erneville and d'Autrive, captains, Mandeville
and others, whom I shall name by the first opportunity, which will be soon, giving you the details of the horrors that
have just been committed against me, which could not be put into cipher, since this friar leaves within the hour, and
if anyone suspected that he carried letters from me they would [take] them from him. A strong frigate and 300 [?]
Swiss troops are absolutely needed here, with the strictest orders to the governor to send the rebels back to you on its [return? / board?], but to be punished with long years of prison on the account that I shall shortly send you of what has just
happened on the part of a section of the officers. Mr Volant is furious with Mr Grondel, but to spare bloodshed he
felt, as I did, the need for wise and prudent moderation, which was very necessary in this cruel situation; to act
otherwise would have been a fine bait for the English. If you do not, my Lord, order the officer you entrust with your
despatches to deliver them to the governor in person, they might well not receive them. There are no
goods at all for the Indian trade, and [libre?]; we receive none from one day to the next. We must not be [surprised?] to see
within three months some very unfortunate upheavals on their [the Indians'] part.
