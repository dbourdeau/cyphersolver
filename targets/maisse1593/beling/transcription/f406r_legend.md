# f. 406r cipher transcription: legend (blind reader a)

Andrew Beling's agent (Claude) transcribed this on 11 Nov 2026 from `sources/raw/repo_help_2026-11-11/maisse1593/leaves/f457.jpg` (right page). The transcription goes by shape alone. The key (`beling/key/tomokiyo_maisse2.tsv`) was used only to name signs. Crops are in `work/a_406r/`.

## Line numbering
- Lines are counted from the top of the letter text, with l. 1 = "Monsieur de Maisse, J'ay tousjours ...". The recto has 43 lines.
- **WAINUT's numbers do not match this count.** WAINUT's f406 l.16 (the line where the cipher starts after "apporter") is leaf l. **23**. WAINUT's l.17 is leaf l. **24**. WAINUT's numbers run 7 lower throughout, and the TSV note column gives the WAINUT-style number as W<n>. I checked the count twice: once on a margin crop of ll. 1-24 and once on a projection profile of the cipher block.
- In leaf numbers, the cipher block runs from l. 23 to l. 43 (W16-W36). It fills every line to the foot of the recto.
- There is also one short cipher run at the end of clear l. 13 (W6). The rough overview position given in the brief (y 350-360) falls on l. 11, but the run of figures is on l. 13, at overview y ≈ 396.

## Counts
- 22 rows: the l. 13 run (18 signs) plus 21 cipher lines (ll. 23-43, 903 signs). That is **921 signs** in all, 832 of them in ll. 25-43 (W18-W36).
- 46 signs are marked `?` and 3 have alternatives `a|b`. One sign is illegible (`[?]`, at the crease at the end of l. 34).
- Each line was read in four overlapping 2x crops. The overlaps were matched by native x-position so that no sign was counted twice or dropped.

## Conventions added
- `^X`: sign X is written above the line at this point, perhaps as an insertion or correction. This occurs three times as `^8` (ll. 30, 35, 40) and once as `^d` (l. 32).
- `III`: a long thin slash followed by two short minims (`/ıı`). It occurs 7 times (ll. 25 ×2, 28, 31, 37, 40, 41). I read it as the key's "three strokes", with the first stroke drawn long. Twice the two minims stand without the slash; there I write `II` (ll. 30, 43).

## Key names used for less obvious shapes
- `E~`: the very frequent long-s-like sign with a looped top and a bottom curl to the left. This follows WAINUT's use of E~.
- `que` vs `q~`: both are a 9-shape with a long tail. `q~` has a **crossbar on the tail** and `que` has a plain tail. I followed WAINUT's q~ in l. 23.
- `9`: figure 9 whose tail slants down-left (l. 31). It differs from `que`.
- `de~` vs `w`: `de~` is an omega with a tall loop rising on the right; `w` is a plain omega.
- `rho`: a closed loop on top, with a tail that descends and curls left.
- `desp`: a p-shape, often with a crossbar through the stem.
- `Z2`: a z/2 head with a long descender hooking left.
- `6b`: a 6 with a hooked flourish on top.
- `s5`: an h/5-like sign with a tall hooked ascender. It is larger than the figure 5.
- `16` is written "ıb". A dot or short stroke before a b-shape is therefore read as the 1 of 16.
- `tri`: a closed triangle (l. 30). `tri?` in l. 26 is an A-shape with a crossbar.
- `alpha?`: a bold double-looped sign (∝/ꝏ) that differs from the thin `x`. The key's `alpha` (h) and its p-sign `x` are drawn almost alike in Tomokiyo's image.
- `eps`: a C with a short inner bar (l. 31). `cdot`: a C with an inner dot (ll. 38, 40, and `cdot?` 30).
- `44`: the "u4"-like figure in l. 39.

## Recurrent doubts (shape pairs that were hard to tell apart)
1. **`y` / `uy` / `1 y`**: whether a short stroke or dot stands before the looped y. This is the commonest doubt.
2. **`z` / `2`**: an angular flat-topped z against a rounded 2. Small angular signs late on the page (ll. 40-43) are read `z`.
3. **`2+` / `+`**: in l. 24 I read `2+` where WAINUT read `+`.
4. **Spacing of figures**: `24` vs `2 4`, `26` vs `2 6`, `14` vs `1 +`, `25` vs `2 5` (ll. 13, 27, 29, 37, 41).
5. **`6` / `6b`**: whether the hook or arc above the 6 is attached (ll. 35, 41).
6. **`f~` / `f_` / `T`**: whether the long s or f has a crossbar.
7. **`x` / `alpha`**: thin x against bold looped ∝.
8. **`ck` / `x+` / `ct`**: see the table above.
9. Blots and the dark crease at the right edge (ll. 30-34, 42-43), and the BN stamp at the left of ll. 28-30, may hide or distort single signs.
