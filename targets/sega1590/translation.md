# English translation

I myself, and outside the parties, have some information on the bishop's bad nature and his leaning towards the opposite party, not so much for reasons of modern politics as from a limping religion. I shall go on temporising as much as I can, making outward shows of feeling for and against him, but slow in execution, meanwhile stirring the parties to spew out all they know and can, [in clear:] so as to be able to inform [Rome] better and resolve more maturely what is fitting in so notable an incident. The last enciphered sentence ("<canonizatorio> della festa ... se non è più che caduto") remains obscure and is not translated.

## Second letter: Paris, 16 January 1591 (f. 71r-v)

[f. 71r] ... and what I wrote to you of having discussed with Mayenne about the service of the Pope, I did not think it necessary to have it sent in duplicate ... He wrote to His Holiness ... without even mentioning you, despite my first advice, already approved as good by His Excellency himself: a proof of the reading he makes ... and of the application of his mind to affairs; but let it pass. The provincial wrote to me that Mayenne himself told him he had written something or other good for me to Rome; but he writes it so obscurely that I do not understand it exactly ... What Mayenne wrote, I do not know. ... not finding that I need, with the Pope, a greater, more powerful and more timely intercession than the one which the lord Duke of Bavaria, of his own motion and as a good father, takes under his protection, and which I promise myself, in due time and place, in the favour of His Highness ... [f. 71v] ... that fruit ("quel pomo") whose proper season I have awaited with singular patience and dissimulation for so many years ... the freedom to speak the truth, which this cause particularly needs, represented as it is by both sides far from what it really is.
