# AGAD 1/338/0/1/1398: English translation

This follows the French reading, with the same holes. `[795]` is an unread code group, `[?word]` is a doubtful reading, and a letter in square brackets is supplied.

## Page 1

From each place I have the honour to send you my deep respects [795]. And I believe my letters written from Vilna have reached your hands. [775]

I am now with Mr Siruc, Castellan of Witebsk, my father-in-law, six leagues beyond Kowno.

The news from this place is that Count Apraxin is still at his headquarters at [?Ludwinow], seven leagues from the frontier of Prussia, and it is believed he will not enter Prussia so quickly.

The shortage here is extraordinary, especially of f[l]our, and even all the pastures around where the army passed, for three leagues, are completely ruined by a great number of horses.

Great provisions are carried continually from the general magazines of Kowno, of Skorule and of Wilkomierz to the Russian army. This transport is devastating the country, taking horses and men without pay to carry its provisions.

Men are b[ea]ten, and it is very hard to get satisfaction for the damage done by the Cossacks and Kalmyks.

## Page 2

At this moment, as I write this letter, news has come that Count Apraxin is to come to Wierzbołów and camp there, half a league from the limits of Prussia.

Deputies of the towns of Prussia have come to Field Marshal Apraxin, submitting to him and imploring his mercy and military discipline. The towns are named Gumbin, Stolupiany and the [?Wrontyszki]. Their requests are granted: to them [?the excise] and the [?tax], for the town of Memel.

Yet nothing is said except that Count Apraxin has ordered the contributions and the provisions. He has even obliged the Castellan and the Marshal of Kowno to try, after the harvest, to collect all sorts of provisions.

From this [?one] concludes one of two things. Either another Russian army will come here to reinforce this one, which has only sixty thousand men. Or the present army, if the treaties are concluded, will come here for winter quarters. But it is to be feared that then there comes what reform of our state.

Prussian officers have talked with persons of trust, both about the extraordinary retinue and about the succession to the throne for the royal family. But this point needs confirmation, because only the ladies have said it.

They say that the Stolnik of [496] will marry the only daughter, a very rich heiress, of Field Marshal Apraxin.

As to the army of the King of Prussia, I believe His Excellency the Grand General of the Crown has enough reports. It is now under the town of Pillau, or [?...] of Königsberg. This town is [?already] closed, and the fortifications are enlarged.

Rumour says that the Prussians have mixed poison into the flour and the bread to [?poison] the Russians. It also says they have [?received the order] of the King their master to resist the Russians only at the town of Königsberg.

I also heard that [?it is] from Petersburg that word has come not to speak ill of the King of Prussia. I [?beg] you to prostrate me at the feet of His Excellency the Grand General as my only protector. I am to the last breath of my life, Sir, your most faithful client Matusewic.

The twenty-fourth of July, from Jaswojnie.

The memorial that I sent you from Wilno, of the Castellan of Brest, which His Excellency [296] sends on to Petersburg, he recommends to His Excellency the Count de L'Hôpital [?...] of France.
