# The number code

The letters use one code table. Its numbers run in alphabetical order, with a block of number words near the end.

Each value comes from these four letters. The evidence is clear text, repeated context and the alphabetical order. The book in `README.md` supports some of them.

## Number words

Numbers 805 to 824 are 20 number words, in order.

| Numbers | Value |
| --- | --- |
| 805 to 813 | 1 to 9 |
| 814 to 822 | 10 to 90, in tens |
| 823 | cent |
| 824 | mille |

The letters use these:

- 805 in 1399: "au [805] d'août prochain", the first of August.
- 806 and 807 in 1399: "tous les [807], les [806] Bohuszowie", three and two.
- 809 in 1399: "[809] seigneurs", followed by exactly five listed shares.
- 806, 823, 824 in 1400: "[806] [823] [824] francs", two hundred thousand francs.
- 810, 811, 818, 823, 824 in 1400: the date line, "mil sept cent cinquante six".
- 816 in 1399: "en datte du [816] du passé", the thirtieth.
- 819, 824 in 1398: "[819] [824] hommes", sixty thousand men.

## Open

These stay unread: 195, 296, 367, 385, 386, 480, 496, 580, 775, 795, 841.

385 may be a "Grand" title, since it follows Grand Général. But 1400 has "une revue [385] de la noblesse", so I leave it open.

## Not words

- The series 830 to 850 falls at clause ends, and it acts as punctuation.
- Numbers from 851 up look like nulls.

The readings leave these out. The decryption files keep them.
