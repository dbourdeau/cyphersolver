Sir,

I have received yours of the 6th of this month. I would very gladly have answered at once, if time had allowed it in any way, but I was still travelling when the post went out, and we were hardly left the time required, so I could not do as I wished.

I have however made up for this by having my master do what I would have done myself, if time had allowed: I got him to send you a copy of the letter from Mr [195], dated the 30th of last month, which was not answered by this post. When it is, I will not fail to have a copy sent to you.

If the reply holds anything of interest, I will also be very exact in telling you whatever deserves it, so far as my reports do not go against what I owe to God, my master, my conscience and my honour.

We left Grodno on the 4th of this month and arrived on the 6th at Zdzięcioł, at the home of the Dowager Princess Palatine of Nowogród, a worthy daughter of our province, who hardly consoled us on the sad state of our present affairs.

There we only planned to gather our friends on the twelfth of this month at Iwie, at the home of Mr the [385] of Lithuania, to decide what to do in so thorny a crisis.

We spent only the night at Zdzięcioł, and arrived the next day at Korelicze, on our own lands. As soon as they knew we were there, the unfortunates came to find us, among them the Abramowicz gentlemen, all three, the two Bohusz and the Ciechanowiecki gentlemen, led by Mr the Prince [480] and the Strasznik of Lithuania.

The pitiful looks and gestures of these wretches seemed to reproach us for our lack of firmness. We sent them back to the congress of Iwie, where they went that same day.

We left Korelicz on the 10th before noon, and came to sleep at Sielup at the home of the Palatine of Trok, whom we also persuaded to go to the congress of Iwie, where we arrived on the evening of the 11th. There we found the Abramowicz, Bohusz and Ciechanowiecki families.

The next day, the twelfth, there arrived Mr the Palatine of Trok with Mr the Strasznik of Lithuania, his son; Madame the Princess Palatine, dowager of Nowogród, with Mr the Prince [841] of Lithuania, her son; and Mr the Prince, Grand Ensign of this Grand Duchy.

Since arriving, these gentlemen have already held several conferences, both at the home of Mr the [386] and at the Bernardines' of Iwie. So far I do not know the result, but it will surely reach me, and I will not fail to inform you.

By this post I am setting up a new correspondence at Mitau, to be told exactly what is happening in Russia, and especially on the frontiers of [?Livonia] and [?Courland]. I will take care to let you know in time, so that you can pass it on where we agreed.

But, Sir, subject to your better judgment, when I discover some [?event] of importance whose nature absolutely requires that it be known quickly, would it not be better, to avoid all delay, for me to go directly to the minister of [?France]? For I will find the means to write to him and to receive his orders without arousing the least suspicion in anyone.

It is not that I wish, God forbid, to hide my discoveries from you, and I will always inform you of them with the utmost exactness. It is to cut short such circumstances where the case may require it. Please tell me frankly what you think.

Just before our departure from Iwie, I learned the result of the conferences, and I do not fail to send it to you in this letter too. I do not believe I betray my master by letting you know, since I see that he has no secrets from his Excellency the Grand General of the Crown, and he may tell him the result himself.

So it was agreed to deposit at Koydanow, with Mr the General Major [?Trabiwski], the sum of one hundred and forty-six thousand francs, to be shared out across all the palatinates and districts of Lithuania, to secure in our favour the success of the coming dietines, both for electing the deputies to the Diet and the deputies to the Tribunal of 1757.

From this general sum, six thousand francs have already been drawn for Mr Zabiello, territorial marshal of Kowno, and eight thousand for Mr de Tiezenaus, palatinate ensign of Wilna.

Five lords have undertaken to provide their shares by the 1st of next August, to make up this sum, namely:

- the Prince Grand General, fifty-three thousand;
- Mr the [385] of Lithuania, twenty-five thousand;
- the Prince Grand Ensign, fifty thousand;
- Mr Pociei, Strasznik of Lithuania, ten thousand;
- and Mr Sottohub, General of Artillery, eight thousand.
