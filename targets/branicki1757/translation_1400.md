Nieśwież, 11 September 1756

Sir,

Every day we hear here very tragic news of the dietines of our Lithuania. Whichever way one turns, one meets only the corpses of gentlemen massacred at these riotous assemblies, which are carried to the courts and to the Tribunal to be presented there: the deadly result of this hatred that is tearing apart the Republic.

This time at Brest of Lithuania, for electing the deputies to the general Diet, there was a dietine of the most riotous kind. Everything there breathed only rage, fury and carnage. Brother against brother, kin against kin, were let loose with the utmost inhumanity.

Count Sapieha, the Palatine, was there to keep good order, and to try to bring about a peaceful choice of deputies in this palatinate who could serve our aims. But alas, this lord, whose protests made not the least impression on the furious nobles, is lucky to have escaped their hands safe and sound.

All of them, except a very small number, were of the opposite party, which arbitrarily elected as deputies Messrs Oziemblowski and Suzin, the lieutenant of Mr Sosnowski, who was struck down by the known decree. Apparently this was to get the Prince Grand General to relax somewhat the severity of the sentence passed against him, although this election, being illegal, will not hold.

A dozen gentlemen, who will not fail to go to Warsaw as well to block the work of these illegally elected deputies, have solemnly protested against this act at the court of Bielsk in Podlachia.

At the head of the mutineers was Mr Buchowiecki, territorial notary of that palatinate, once one of our friends, but who has since turned his coat. At this dietine he made plenty of work for Mr the Palatine.

This troublemaker still claims he will bring this palatinate to a review [385] of the nobles, the first step toward a confederation. But Mr Wereszczaka, ensign of this palatinate, backed by Mr the Palatine, strongly opposes it.

All this uproar comes from the generosity of the opposite party, which has scattered two hundred thousand francs across all the palatinates and districts of Lithuania. So Mr the Palatine, foreseeing that the sum we were about to pay out to this palatinate would achieve nothing with nobles long since won over by our adversaries, judged it best not to risk it for nothing.

I am, Sir, your very humble and very obedient servant Boeven.

P.S. At Nowogród it proved impossible to get the nobles to include the rehabilitation of Sieur Abramowicz in the deputies' instructions. Most of them, who have lawsuits before the Tribunal, fear that [?the Familia] would make them pay dearly for such a step.

Mr Oziemblowski, one of the deputies illegally elected at Brest of Lithuania, a towarzysz of Mr the Palatine, has left military service.
