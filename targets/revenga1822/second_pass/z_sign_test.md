# Revenga 1822: the z sign, the j sign and the stops (second pass, 17 November 2026)

The questions are the ones NOTES.md leaves open: which sign stands for plain z, which for plain j, and whether a
final z or k after a word is a stop. Answers from the settled transcription (`transcription_settled.tsv`) and the
frames. "Both transcriptions" means the first pass (`../transcription.txt`) and the blind second transcription
(`blind/transcription_blind.tsv`), made without sight of any key, decryption or earlier transcription.

## Plain z: written with the sign of plain i

The letter has four words with a plain z. In all four, both transcriptions read the cipher sign `g`, the sign of
plain i, at the place of the z, and the settled text keeps it:

| word | group | frame, line | cipher (settled) | mechanical decryption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cádiz | B04 (C04) | 0057R 4-5 | `yvzgg` | cadii |
| pobreza | B20 (C20) | 0058L 2 | `opxmtgv` | pobreia |
| diez | B25 (C25) | 0058L 12 | `zgtg` | diei |
| forzoso | B42 (C42) | 0056L 4-6 | `upmgpllp` | forioso |

No other sign stands at these places on either reading, and no sign outside the key is used for z anywhere in the
letter. The block pattern of the key that NOTES.md points to predicts z -> g, and g is what is written. So the writer
enciphered plain z with the same sign as plain i. Limits: these four places were not looked at again on the frame
on their own, because the two transcriptions agree there; the blind transcriber noted that g and q are hard to
separate in this hand, so a separate q-like z sign cannot be ruled out from the shapes alone.

## Plain j: a w-like sign, not u

Where the frame is clear, the sign for plain j is a double loop like a w (a u closed by a v-loop). The blind
transcription writes it `w`: Ejecutivo `atwty` (B51, 0056R 1-2) and mejor `dtwpm` (B53, 0056R 7-9). In objetos
(B27, 0058L 14) the same form is read `uv` (`pxuvtlp`). In consejo and aconsejó (B54, 0055R) it reads as u on the
frame at the size available, a weak reading. Cipher u (plain f) is clear on the frame in Orlof, perfecta and
franceses. So the j sign is close to u = f and easily read as u, uv, ur or v. With w = j, Ejecutivo and mejor
decrypt cleanly. The sign is not in `key.txt`.

## Final z and k: stops

Inside words, cipher z is plain d throughout. At the end of a word, a z stands where the clear text needs a stop or
a comma: after desastrosa, septentrional, nacional (before "Dispuestos"), europeos, derecho, uno (before
"Determinamos"), tropas, pesos (where a semicolon belongs, with a correction squiggle), sistema (end of the letter),
before "Hoy" (B11), after Andalucía (B13) and after apresurarse (B42). k stands after declare (B33) and encuentra
(B39), and inside the run of B54 after todo, where a comma belongs. The positions are from the settled text; that
they are stops is our inference from where they stand, and it is strong. The comma-like crossed mark (`,`) is a
third punctuation sign. A doubled word space `aa` occurs three times (collation items 15, 18 and 145) with no
clear function.

## The doubled g next to c (-ción)

The doubled `gg` before -ción (`yggpe`), which the first pass read as a reading slip, is on the frame: in B10
(constitucionales) the first g carries a dot, and the same `yg` + `g` is in B13 (Andalucía). Eight of the ten
-ción places keep gg in the settled text. It is the writer's habit, not a misreading, so corrections.md is right to
read it as one i, as an emendation of the writer's spelling.

## Other signs

`q` appears once (B41, "también"), as both transcriptions read it. A pointed s-like sign stands where plain p
(cipher o) is needed in privadamente, porque, público, partido and presentes; this hand's o also has a lead-in, so
the shape cannot separate o from s there, and it was settled as o from the sense.
