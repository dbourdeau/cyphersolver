Polish letter to Hetman Adam Mikołaj Sieniawski, probably from Stanisław Chomentowski (envoy at the Porte 1712-14). Not printed.
Transcribed from DECODE images IMG_R7476_I33805_P2 (p. 31), P3 (pp. 32-33), P1 (p. 34); native-resolution strip crops in img/crop/sien_R7476_*.jpg.

Conventions
  C: code numbers as read; clear words in "quotes". "?" = number unreadable.
     N* = the digit 7/4 is ambiguous in this hand and was settled by the gloss (e.g. 171* looks like 141 and is glossed "na").
  G: interlinear gloss (second hand), one item per number; "-" no gloss; "[?]" gloss present but unreadable or not alignable.
     Glosses often sit between two numbers. The alignment is a best effort, so a gloss may be off by one position.
  K: value from sien/syllabary.tsv (first value listed); "x^" = value not in syllabary.tsv, taken from the R7476 gloss;
     "?" = unknown. XIII and XVIII are Roman-numeral code groups (XIII glossed "Flota").
  This is a first pass. Reading the glosses is the weak point (small, faint brown ink); closer crops could recover many [?].

== English summary ==
The envoy reports his dealings with the grand vizier and his kehaya: a present was accepted, and an audience and dispatch
were promised. He describes a dispute over precedence and memorials in visits with other envoys, and the question of a
convoy that would let a sovereign (evidently Charles XII) return "through Poland to his states", with news touching King
Stanisław, an amnesty, the Crimean Han, the kalga sultan going to the Circassians, and Tatars. He adds that the Ottoman fleet
has put out, somewhat larger than usual (with about thirty galleys, possibly for Azov). The Swedish king's creditors keep
pressing him for money while he "firmly sits here". A man (code XVIII) was brought in and shut up in Yedikule on old
suspicions about Muscovy and the failure to deliver provisions to Chocim, and the Sultan's mother is interceding for him. A
clear postscript reports the kapudan pasha seizing grain and oil ships, the French envoy's request, and unusual cold.

== Dating suggestion ==
Chomentowski's embassy (1712-14), at a time when Charles XII is still in Ottoman territory and refuses to leave ("firmiter tu
siedzi"), his creditors are pressing him and his convoy through Poland is still undecided. Autumn-winter 1712/13 fits
best: unusual cold, the Turkish war scare of Nov.-Dec. 1712, before or around the Bender kalabalik of Feb. 1713. The
Swedish king's return through Poland and the references to Stanisław also fit the Adrianople negotiations of spring 1713
(siblings R7484/R7485). Suggested date: c. Dec. 1712 - spring 1713, Constantinople/Adrianople. Check it against
R7484/R7485 and the fleet and Yedikule references.
