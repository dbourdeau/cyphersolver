# Catalogue no. 2886: two cipher lines on the title page of ad-Dani's at-Taisir (Berlin, Ms. or. oct. 3629, 615 AH / July 1218)

Status: in progress. Attempted 16 Nov 2026 from the printed plate; not solved. The manuscript leaf itself has not
been seen.

Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin, Ms. or. oct. 3629, f. 1a, between the title and the author line: two lines of separate
signs, which Sellheim (VOHD XVII A 1, 1976, no. 12, p. 23) counts as "43(?) Buchstaben in Geheimschrift", partly
smudged, seven of them repeated in the margin of f. 72b. He gives no reading and points to his Abb. 92 for comparison.

## Findings from 22 November 2026

The colophon dates the copy to July 1218. Sellheim printed 1217. The date and authorship of the cipher lines remain uncertain. The seven signs he reports on folio 72b provide the strongest independent check on a proposed reading. Searches using a provisional 43-position transcription and comparisons with historical alphabet tables yielded no verified plaintext. ## What Abb. 92 is (read here, 16 Nov 2026)

Abb. 92 (Ms. or. oct. 3938 f. 1a, about 1800) is an owner's note with the same words beneath it in cipher. Checked
sign by sign against the clear text above it: each letter is its abjad value written as an Arabic digit with a small
loop beneath it for each zero (mim = 4 with one loop, shin = 3 with two loops, ya = 1 with a loop, ha = 8, ta' = 9,
waw = 6 bare). The cipher line begins "Muhammad ash-Shanawani" and continues "al-Khatib bi-l-...", right to left.
This is a system, not a key: it has no secret part, so if the title-page lines used it they should read at sight.

## Transcription

`signs.txt`: 40 signs in two lines (21 and 19), six of them present but not classed, 18 working classes. Sellheim's
43(?) fits if two or three signs are lost in the smudged gap in the middle of line 2 (x 400-470). Both lines open
at the right with a cluster of three or four dots, and line 2 closes with one at the left. Many signs carry one or
two dots or a short dash beneath them. The classes are shape groups made on a blurred halftone and several are
unsafe: D covers four "loop with a tall stroke on its right" signs of which one has a tail and one a dot in the loop;
N covers a "flag over a loop" and a "triangle over a loop"; E covers two signs that mirror each other; the three A
signs of line 1 differ in what hangs below them.

Counts: A x4 (a "9" shape), D x4, H x3, I x3 (a zigzag with a dot under it), K, N, Q, C, M, E x2 each.
Line 2 opens A B C C D with the two C signs adjacent, the first with two short marks under it.

## What was tried (16 Nov 2026), none of it confirmed

1. **Arabic digits with zero marks, abjad values (the Abb. 92 system).** Several signs fit digit shapes of that
   plate exactly: A = 9, B = 8 (an arch), J = 6 (a hook), K = the old 4 shaped like 'ayn, R = 1 with a loop (the ya'
   of Abb. 92). On that reading line 1 could open m-l-k (40, 30, 20: "milk", the opening of the Abb. 92 note), but
   the next signs then give no word (a 1-with-loop followed by a bare 9 would be ya' then ta'; A as bare 9 four times
   is four ta' in forty letters), and line 2 would open ta', ha'. Not a reading.
2. **Coptic cursive (zimam, "epact") numerals with abjad values.** Compared with the tables of Pihan 1860, Mallon
   1956 and Megally 1991 as reproduced in Pandey's Unicode proposal (WG2 N3843) and with Noto Sans Coptic. Some
   signs fit (C = 30, O = 10, D = 60, N = 70, R = 8, K = 100), and line 2 could then open "... al-Latif" with line 1
   ending "'Abd", but that needs three signs read against their shape, and the right-to-left order of the supposed
   ta'-ya' pair is wrong on the plate. Not a reading.
3. **Coptic letters** (the "9" as fai, K as hori, N as cima, S as ti, Q as ksi, D as alpha, J as gamma), read left
   to right as Coptic or as Arabic in Coptic letters, or right to left as Greek-order numerals with abjad values.
   The shapes are close, and three of them are letters only Coptic has. No Coptic word, Arabic word or number
   sequence came out of either direction; with Greek-order values the "9" would be sad four times.
4. **Disguised Arabic letters, mirrored or turned upside down.** Both views looked at; nothing reads.
5. **Cribs by pattern.** The scribe's name (Abu Sa'id ibn Humaid ash-Shafi'i al-Muqri'), "ibn ... ibn", "bi-llah",
   "wa-llah", "'Abd Allah", "'afa llahu 'anhu", "ya kabikaj" (seven letters, as many as the signs repeated on
   f. 72b), "wa-ma tawfiqi illa bi-llah", "milk al-faqir". "A I" recurs eight signs apart in line 1 and line 2 opens
   with A and a doubled sign, which suits "... bi-llah" (A = ba', B = alif) or "... wa-llah" (A = waw), but B then
   stands for alif and occurs once or twice in forty signs, and neither reading can be carried through the rest.
6. **The scribe's name as a crib on test 1 (second pass, 16 Nov 2026).** With the Abb. 92 system, line 1 can be laid
   out as m-l-k | alif-b-w | s-'-y-d | (four faint marks) | h-m-y-d, "milk Abu Sa'id [ibn] Humaid", the name Sellheim
   gives for the scribe. For it: K is the 4-with-loop of Abb. 92 (mim), N is its 2-with-loop (kaf), H is a bare
   stroke (alif), Q is a hook over a loop (6 with a zero, sin), and the pair A I stands at the end of both names
   (ya', dal). Against it: the "9" has to be ya' where Abb. 92 has it as ta' and writes ya' as R; R has to be
   'ayn; P does not look like a 6; the two "ya' dal" pairs are eight signs apart where "Sa'id ibn Humaid" needs
   six; and the thirteen signs that follow do not align with "ash-Shafi'i al-Muqri'" (alif would be three different
   signs). A hypothesis to test on a scan, not a reading: about half of its sign values are forced.

## Remaining gaps

- The whole text, 40 to 43 signs: not read. Blocker: illegible at this resolution (needs the leaf itself). The
  halftone does not separate a zero dot from a speck, a loop from a blot, or one sign from its neighbour in the
  smudged stretches (line 1 x 345-445, line 2 x 355-520), and the sign classes above cannot be made safe on it.
- The system: not identified. Three families of sign fit in part (Arabic digits, Coptic numerals, Coptic letters)
  and each fails on the frequency of the "9" sign or on the opening of line 2.
- F. 72b margin, seven signs: not seen. Blocker: needs the leaf or a scan.

## Prior work

The 9-like sign there represents ṣād, and the peaked sign represents tāʾ. Applied to the opening of Berlin line 2 (A B), those tentative identifications give صت. The doubled C that follows has no secure match, and the small under-marks on the Berlin plate remain unexplained. The source provides an 18-letter partial alphabet from another codex. This comparison does not establish shared cipher values.
