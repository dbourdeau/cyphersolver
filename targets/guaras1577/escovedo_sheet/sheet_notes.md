## How the syllables are built

Every syllable is the consonant's own alphabet sign followed by a vowel mark joined to it:

- a: a short upright joined by a hairline (looks like `-1`; with 1 or 11 it makes an H) → token `1`
- e: a 6-shaped hook → `6`
- i: a small cross → `t`
- o: no added sign; the bare consonant sign, usually with a tick above right → `'`. For b, f, m, n, r and s the
  tick is not on the sign but appears as a comma after the plain label (bo, fo, mo, no, ro, so); for c and g there
  is no tick at all. So in letters the o syllable may look like the bare consonant.
- u: an e-like curl with a tail below the line (ę) → `e`. Note this is the same letter token as the G sign `e`
  (gu = `e e`); the u mark always has the descending tail, the G sign does not.

The same marks are used on the E sign (ea = `0 1` ...), and on qu (`b`). L and V use the same 8-with-open-top
shape `g`: small on the line for L, large with a loop dropping below the line for V.

Clusters: l-clusters put a dot above the consonant sign, r-clusters a dot below, and ch is the c sign (a) struck
through; then the same vowel marks follow (bla = `3* 1`, bru = `3. e`, cho = `a-'`). Doubled letters (bb ... tt)
are the consonant sign with a circumflex above.

Alphabet homophones: A has three signs (4, ε, P), E three (0, a gamma-like r/y with a long tail, a large e),
O three (9, 5, n). I has one sign (a squarish, retouched g) and no syllable row; U is not listed separately (V row).
