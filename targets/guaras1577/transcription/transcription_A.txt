## Sign shapes the notation does not cover (tokens I used for them)

- "u T" cluster (every line): a u-like double minim joined to a tall descending stroke with a crossbar; it is followed
  by P (loop/flag), t (small cross), i (plain short stroke) or 6. Written as separate tokens u T P / u T t / u T i /
  u T 6. Where the tall stroke has no crossbar it is written "u y" (e.g. P1L04 "u y h 4", P1L08 "o o z u y").
  Second readers may wish to treat "uT?" as one sign.
- Small cross "+" ligatured after a letter: written "t" (7 t, 6 t, d t, 9 t, x t, S t, b t, z t). The notation only
  defines B for b-with-tail; I wrote B where the b has a mid-height tail and "b t" where a separate cross follows
  (P1L29, P2L08).
- Crossed yogh/3 shape and crossed y shape: both written "4" (e.g. "3+" P1L01, P1L02, P1L11; "y+" P1L19, P1L23,
  P1L24; "dy+" P1L09, P1L13). Uncrossed yogh stays "Z". Where the cross is clearly a separate small stroke I still
  folded it into 4; a/b alternative would be "Z t" / "y t".
- Epsilon-shaped e ("e"), distinct from 6: P1L04 "d v e", "g e"; P1L20 "e 9 Z"; possibly more instances read as 6.
- Large Q-like sign: "Q" (P1L28 "9 Q", P2L13 "Q m").
- R-like sign with a descending leg: "R" (P1L28 "a u y R", P2L14 "S t R").
- r-like sign with a descender: "r" (P1L02 "b b r 9").
- Small 5/s shape on the line: written "5" (P1L03 "5 d t", P1L07 "z 5", P1L14 "o 9 5", P1L28 "5 d 6", P1L29
  "9 P n 5", P2L01, P2L09, P2L10, P2L12). "S 6" (P1L02, P1L09, P1L31) is a taller s-like form followed by 6/e.
- h-like sign (H with a tall left stem): "h" throughout (h 4, h 9, h g).
- Superscript mark over a letter (small n/w shape) written as "~" after the letter: P1L03 "9~", P1L16 "9~", P1L19
  "d Z~", P1L21 "9~", P1L22 "d 9~", P1L24 "u~ y", P1L26 "u~ T P", P1L29 "u~ T 6", P1L32 "m u~ T P", P2L03
  "d u~ T P". In P1L12 "9 9 4~" the 4 carries a heavy horizontal bar (struck through?).
- Commas/ticks after signs (e.g. P1L02 "x ,", "b Z ,", P1L10 "z ,", P1L14 "u ,", P2L10 "z ,", P2L11 "z ,") are omitted.
- Stray single dots not forming #n are omitted: P1L11 after "7 P", P1L25 after "1 P", P1L27 at line end after
  "6 4 9", P2L01 line end, P2L03 between "m 7" and "S t", P2L04 between "8 z" and "1 6" (only a leading dot, so
  not written #16).
- Drawn strokes: vertical bar between "b 6" and "x 9 P" in P1L10 (kept as "|"); slash between "9 9 4 P" and "d 4"
  in P1L13; slash between "g x b" and "Q" in P2L13; a horizontal stroke through "x~ a u T t v t" in P1L21 and
  through "n d" in P1L24 (possible deletions); "6" in P1L20 "o d 6 o" has a bar through it; ink blot over the first
  sign of "u T 6" in P2L14; ticks above the "6" in P2L01 "7 6 1 6 4 9".
- Margin note at the left of P2L11 (about "6D+" / "dd+"), not transcribed into the line.

## Doubts worth a second look

- 9 versus g (straight versus looped descender) was judged sign by sign and is the most likely source of
  disagreement; also 6 versus o versus b in small round signs (P1L13 "9 6 Z | 6 7 t", P1L30 "g 6 o z", P1L20 "9 b m 9").
- Numbers: P1L18 "#36" has the trailing dot only clearly visible; P1L28 starts "20." with only the trailing dot (the
  previous line ends with a dot); P1L17 "h 9 #59" and P1L21 "9 #43" could also be read as 9.59 / 9.43.
- Underlines: P1L11 "x < 8 P >" and P1L14 final "x < 8 P >" are faint/low and may belong to the line below;
  underlines that may cover more signs: P1L03 "< 5 d t >" (may run under d 6), P1L22 start "m 4 P" (faint stroke).
- P1L33 ends with a faint damaged sign "?" at the torn right edge.

## Line-numbering notes

- Lines slope down to the right by about one line spacing on f. 93r. The right-hand ends of many lines (P1L01,
  P1L02, P1L07, P1L10, P1L14, P1L18, P1L19, P1L20, P1L21) sit level with the start of the next line, so in the
  b-half strips the end of line n is shown in the row below the tick, and the tick row of strip n+1b shows the
  tail of line n. I followed each line by its baseline; numbering follows the overviews' left-hand starts.
- On the P1 overview the tail of line 33 ("x P 9 m d u T 6 ... d 4") drops onto its own row next to the folio
  number "93"; it is the end of line 33, not a 34th line.
- P2 numbering matches the overview; P2L14 ends in clear Spanish (the closing courtesy begins there).
