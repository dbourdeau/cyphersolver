# mendoza1548: Diego Hurtado de Mendoza to Antoine Perrenot de Granvelle, bishop of Arras, Rome, 29 May 1548 (BNE MSS/20212/29/1)

Catalogue #1268. Madrid, Biblioteca Nacional de España, MSS/20212/29/1 (BNE Digital bdh0000172694, the first of six
letters under MSS/20212/29/1-6). The cipher was transcribed sign by sign, and the key was rebuilt from two sources:
the decipherment written between the lines at the time, and an alignment of the other lines with the text they
carry. The key gives a value to 99% of the signs (`decryption/d1_decode.txt`); the reading supplies in brackets
what neither the decode nor the gloss gives (34 places, see Reading).

**Catalogue corrections:**
- **Date:** 29 May 1548, not 29 March. The clear closing on f. 1v reads *en Roma a 29 de Mayo 1548*; the stroke under
  the *y* can be taken for the cedilla of *março*. The endorsement and the printed edition also give May.
- **Decipherment:** the letter is partly deciphered at the time. Faint interlinear syllables stand over L01-L04 and
  L21-L25 of f. 1r and over M1-M2 of f. 1v. "No decipherment noted" and "no interlinear text" are wrong; the script
  is faint at overview size.
- **Incomplete letter:** the BNE record notes that the letter is incomplete and that its first part is MSS/7919/75
  (not on BNE Digital). This explains why f. 1r starts mid-sentence (*… puedan quedar con ella …*).
- **Already printed:** M. J. Bertomeu Masiá, *La guerra secreta de Carlos V contra el Papa. La cuestión de Parma y
  Piacenza en la correspondencia del cardenal Granvela* (Valencia/Murcia 2009), doc. 34, pp. 267-270, "(BNM, ms.
  20212/29-1 [7919/75])", prints the letter in clear with the cipher passages deciphered; the letter is discussed on
  pp. 154 ff. It was found during this attempt, after the transcription and the gloss pairs, and used to align the unglossed lines.

## The letter

- **f. 1r:** the date at the top right, then 25 lines wholly in cipher (L01-L25). L20 ends with a flourish; L21 starts
  a new paragraph.
- **f. 1v:** 6 cipher lines (M1-M6), then the clear closing (*Guarde Nuestro Señor … en Roma a 29 de Mayo 1548*) and
  Mendoza's signature. f. 2r is blank.
- **Address leaf:** to the bishop of Arras; endorsed *Don Diego de Mendoza, 29 de mayo 1548*.

**Content:** Piacenza, the Pope's dealings with France, and the Interim. Farnese will settle for any compensation
for Piacenza. Mendoza tells Granvelle that he may show the report to "Monseñor" (Nicolas Perrenot de Granvelle, his
father) and, if need be, to the Emperor. The papal side means to use the Interim as a bargaining point: Sfondrato is
to offer the legates on condition that they examine the Interim. The Pope's haste over the judgement on the
translation of the Council is for show. Mendoza has read the text of the ratification and finds Spanish turns of
phrase and faults of Latin grammar in it. He will send his notes by the next courier.

## The system

A Spanish chancery syllabary (Bucuré's "cifra de Nápoles 1547"). It has:
- consonant signs with a vowel mark: the *t* family `∇̇` *ti*, `∇ꝶ` *tu*, `∇o` *to*; the *m* family `ꝓ` *me/mu*,
  `ꝓ̇` *ma*, `ꝓo` *mo*; the *p* sign `V`, `V̇`;
- signs for syllables: `Xo` *de*, `X` *se*, `∂` *ne*, `⊥` *tra*, `M` *pu*, `8ꝶ` *cu*, `ꝶ4` *me*, `Ẍ` *pri*;
- single letters with homophones: *e* = `ʃ`, `ω`, `5`; *a* = `n`, `ꝺ̂`, `6`; *s* = `ꝺ`, `mo`; *r* = `d` and the
  `no`-shaped sign; *n* = `T°`;
- double consonants: `∠` *ll*, `─` *go* here (*ss* in Bucuré);
- code groups: `dide` Su Majestad, `mta` Su Santidad, `ritib` Vuestra Señoría, `nego:` franceses;
- nulls marked with dots (`ö`, `o̤`, `n̤`, `d̤`, `ɑꝶ̤`, `δ̣`; Bucuré: two or four dots);
- dots, which mostly mark off a sign and are not word dividers. `ꝺ` followed by a dot is *que* or *da* (not separable on the scan), and `e .` is *lo*.

135 kinds of sign as transcribed over 702 tokens. Many are a base with a mark. The vowel marks are 2-3 px on this
scan and most are not read, so a base sign often stands for several syllables in the transcription (`V` *pa/po/pu*).
The key with every sign and its pairs is in `key_1548.md`.

## How it was read

1. **First look:** the faint interlinear syllables over L01-L02 were found at 3-4x. Read by position over the signs,
   they gave the first pairs (`T°` *n*, `ʃ` *e*, `β` *y*).
2. **Bucuré's table:** the key matched it (`n` *a*, `ω` *e*, `∠` *ll*, `M` *pu*, `Xo` *de*), and its code words
   occur in the letter (`dide`, `mta`, `vitis` written `ritib`).
3. **Transcription:** all 31 lines, on slope-following crops at about 3x. The gloss resumes at L21 and on f. 1v
   M1-M2. A first pass had missed it as too faint.
4. **Gloss pairs** (`key_pairs_gloss.txt`): L21, L22, L23, L25 and M2 align sign for syllable; L01-L04 and L24 align in part (L04 only its first words).
5. **Decoding by context** (no outside text): the gloss pairs plus the sign families gave words in the unglossed
   lines:
   - *desta manera* (L04, after the gloss's *Placencia*);
   - *no lo escrivo en ella*, *en particular y parte*, *destos*, *de V.S. mostrar*, *menester a Su Majestad*;
   - *publicación*, *traslación*.
   Two other readings of that stage were wrong: *pues ha de ver* (L16, now *después a[probar]lo*) and *de partir*
   (L19, now *poco de porta[da]*).
   Two transcription faults were found and fixed: a large 8-loop (`β` *y*) had been merged with the small *m* sign
   `ꝓ`, and lines 5-19, read before the glossed lines fixed the sign names, were read again.
6. **Search for the edition:** the BNE record cites Bertomeu Masiá 2009. Its Google Books preview gives the letter as
   doc. 34, dated 29 May. The date was then checked on the image (*Mayo*).
7. **Crib pairs** (`key_pairs_crib.txt`): the unglossed lines (the rest of L04, L05-L20, M3-M6) were aligned sign by syllable with
   the text it must carry, checked against the edition. This fixed values the context had not settled:
   - `de` *lo*, `no` *r* (two glyphs shaped like clear words), `e .` *lo*;
   - `ȧ` *ha*, `ꝶ4` *me*, `ɔo` *fo*, `─` *go*, `Ẍ` *pri*, `5` *e*;
   - the name *Fernés* in L02 and the code group *franceses* in L01.
8. **Second look at the brackets:** the places where the reading needed brackets were re-read on the image. Four
   signs had been misread: in L06 a crossed *y* (`ÿ̶` *se*, giving *ser*), not `β`; in L10 `ꝓo` *mo* (*Monseñor*),
   not `ꝓ`; in L12 `7̇` and the dotted big loop `ꝸ̇` *ra* (*barato*), and `ꝗ̈` *cri* (*escriviendo*), not a null.
   The dotted `ꝺ .` turned out to be *da* in three places (*queda*, *desasida*, *portada*) as well as *que*.
   Brackets went from 38 to 34.
9. **Consistency:** over the 618 pairs (120 sign types), 77% of pairs take their sign's most common value. Most of
   the rest are base signs whose vowel mark is not seen (see `key_1548.md`, Ambiguities).

The crib step depends on the edition; steps 1-5 do not. The phrases listed in step 5 were decoded before the
edition was seen, and all of them are in its text. The decode in `decryption/d1_decode.txt` uses both sets of
pairs, so on its own it does not show that earlier state. About two thirds of the pairs (424 of 618) are crib pairs.

## Reading

`decryption/d1_reading.md` is the reading in Spanish and `decryption/d1_en.md` the English. `decryption/d1_decode.txt`
gives, line by line, the gloss of 1548 (where there is one) and the decode by `dec1548.py`.

Sources of the reading:
- **L01-L04 (L04 only its first words), L21-L25, M1-M2:** the decipherment written between the lines in 1548, with
  the decode where the gloss is faint or missing (L03 start, L24 middle, M1 middle).
- **L25 *algunas*:** the gloss's first two syllables were read *ni n*, but the same signs (`n ─o oꝶ`) are glossed
  *a l gu* in L22 and decode *al*; the reading follows the decode.
- **The rest of L04, L05-L20, M3-M6:** the decode. Where a sign has two values or its vowel is not seen, the value
  was chosen by context and checked against the edition.
- **Brackets:** `[ ]` mark every word or part of a word that neither the gloss nor the decode gives (34 places, for
  example *[sola]*, *[Monseñor]*, *[aprobarlo]*, *[y] [no] [querría]*; a word is bracketed whole when any part of it is supplied). They were supplied by context and checked
  against the edition, and they show where the reading depends on it.
- **Spelling:** follows the decode and the glosses (*escrivo*, *hazer*, *Placencia*, *menudencias*). Accents, word
  division and punctuation are this project's own.
- **Not in the reading:** `δ ꝶꝶ n̈` at the end of L10 decode *me su fi* but make no word there, and the last 17
  signs of M6 come after *reputación*. Both stand at a break in the text, so they are probably nulls.
- **Start:** the letter begins on MSS/7919/75, so the reading starts mid-sentence.

## Remaining gaps

- **Vowel marks:** at 2-3 px on the scan, most are not seen. 178 tokens print two values; the reading chooses by
  context.
- **Unread signs:** 10 tokens of 10 sign types have no value (`key_1548.md`, Ambiguities): one each in L01-L04,
  L09, L11, L17 and M1, and two in the end group of M6.
- **MSS/7919/75**, the first part of the letter, was not seen.
- **Human check:** Michael Brackx checked L21, L22 and the first half of L23 against the image (sign counts and the
  place of each gloss syllable): confirmed. He read the last syllable of L21, over `n̈`, as *fa* or *fu* rather than
  *fi*, and in L22 *vi* as possibly *ui*. He also checked the unglossed L06-L07 (sign counts and shapes only):
  confirmed, with remarks on letter forms that do not change values (L06's *no* sign, first transcribed `∞o`, is written `ɔo`, an open loop: the transcription now names it so, and a
  comparison found the same form in L09 and L13 and a different, closed `oo` in L09 *negocios*; `ÿo` has no dots;
  `T°` looks almost like *I*; in L07 the dot after `β` sits closer to the following `V`). The rest of the letter was
  not checked by a person.
