# mendoza1548: the key as rebuilt

The cipher is the Spanish chancery syllabary that Bucuré 2025 reconstructs from Neapolitan letters of 1547 (Pedro
de Toledo, Charles V, Diego Hurtado de Mendoza) and that Bertomeu Masiá 2009 places also in the correspondence of
Granvelle, Mendoza and Ferrante Gonzaga. Its signs: a consonant sign with a vowel mark (most marks are 2-3 px on this
scan and are not read), signs for syllables, single letters with homophones, signs for double consonants (`∠` ll),
code groups for names and frequent words, and nulls marked with dots (two or four in Bucuré; two seen here).

The values come from two sets of pairs, kept apart:

- `key_pairs_gloss.txt`: each sign read against the syllable written over it by the decipherer of 1548 (L01-L04,
  L21-L25, M1-M2);
- `key_pairs_crib.txt`: the unglossed lines (the rest of L04, L05-L20, M3-M6) aligned sign by syllable with the text they must carry,
  checked against the printed decipherment (Bertomeu Masiá 2009, doc. 34). The edition's text is not copied here.

`dec1548.py` takes each sign's most common value over both sets; a second value prints as `a/b` when it has two or
more places and at least a third of the first value's count. Signs in neither set take the value Bucuré gives, or
the value of their sign family (marked below). The *p* sign `V` is the exception: its pairs spread over *pa*, *po*,
*pu*, *pla* and *pe*, and the decoder prints *pa/po* for it, ignoring the pairs.

Code groups (Bucuré's vocabulary, confirmed in place): `dide` Su Majestad (L10), `mta` Su Santidad (L16), `ritib`
(Bucuré `vitis`) Vuestra Señoría (L09), `nego:` (Bucuré `nego`, Francia) franceses here (L01, after *en liga con*).

Two glyphs look like clear words and are cipher signs: `de` is *lo* (L08, L16) and `no` is *r* (L06, L14, L17, L19,
M4, M5).

## Ambiguities

- **Vowel marks not seen.** `V` is the *p* sign (*pa*, *po*, *pu*, *pla*, *pe*); `ꝺ̇` and `ꝗ` are *d* signs (*da*,
  *de*, *do*, and `ꝗ` *u* in *aunque*); `ÿ`, `ÿʃ` take *s*, *v* and *b* syllables; `ꝓ̈` is *cri*/*ri*/*mi*. The
  decode prints the two commonest.
- **Homophones of *e*:** `ʃ`, `ω`, `5`, and `ꝓ` in places; of *a*: `n`, `ꝺ̂`, `6`.
- **`aꝶ`** is *o* or *n* in equal numbers: probably two signs that the scan does not separate.
- **`ɔo`** (an open loop like an inverted *c*, then *o*; Bucuré's *no* sign `∞o`) is *fo* (*información*,
  *Sfondrato*) and *no* (L06): the three are written alike. A closed double loop `oo` in L09 (*negocios*) is a
  different sign, without a value.
- **Unread:** 10 tokens of 10 sign types: `ꝺꝶ` (L01, before the code group), `∇` (L02), `Ω` (L03), `Ṫ` (L04), `oo`
  (L09), `6̈` (L11), `Π°` (L17), `⊥°` (M1), and `8̣`, `&` among the last 17 signs of M6, which come after
  *reputación* and are probably nulls (`ʒ` there decodes *jo* but makes no word). A comma in L11 and the flourish ending L20 are not
  transcribed.
