[...] to Messer Matteo [...] the discussion held with [...] Most Serene Lord and with [...] by letters the scruple of the obligation [...] in cipher that the aforesaid Lord has [...] Lady of Germany, and likewise the resolution taken regarding the negotiation of our marriage alliance and the plan adopted for the journey of Monsignore of Nitra to Rome [...] when His Majesty is certified that His Holiness the Pope is well enough to attend to dispatching business. Having arrived [...] in the absence of Messer Matteo [...] according to the order of the latest ciphers [...] in the March [...] in Ancona Monsignore of Nitra, we opened them ourselves and understood [...] what is contained in them. We received double pleasure: the one for the resolution [...] aforesaid Most Serene Lord [...] the conclusion of the alliance, in which matter the desire is mutual in us as well, nor could we have anything more welcome than to see this negotiation soon concluded so as to confirm with the bond of kinship our love [...] for his dignity and immense virtues. The other pleasure is that [...] we see confirmed [...] what we already believed of the sincerity and religious spirit [...] of His Majesty, and that every rumor spread by others that he has an obligation to another marriage is vain; which thing, even though we did not believe it [...] nonetheless we rejoice to have this testimony [...] so that when the time comes it may be the better refuted [...] that some in order to hinder the negotiation [...]

[...] persisting therefore in his purpose, the aforesaid Most Serene King [...] this our [...] and we too persist in our purpose as most content, and we accept it on the conditions negotiated, namely that once the dispensation is granted we shall give him fifty thousand ducats, and then the rest [...] up to two hundred thousand ducats when he brings the [...] counting in this sum ten thousand ducats [...] Since the Bishop of Nitra must therefore come to make the conclusion, and since it does not please His Royal Majesty, as you write, that this matter be negotiated at a border place, desiring instead that it be negotiated in Rome for greater dignity, and since he must send his ambassador amid this [...] of the Pope's illness, we have thought fit to send this courier back to you with speed without waiting for anything further from the Most Reverend Monsignore our brother, because nothing more could be written by him than what we are doing in this matter; and having sent him your ciphers, if he sees fit to signify anything to you either on this or on another matter, we shall send you the reply [...] by another special courier.

We tell you therefore, with regard to the Pope's health, that it is true His Holiness is indisposed, and the illness is caused by burning of the urine, of which it is not yet fully certain whether it proceeds from a stone or from an ulceration of the bladder; and although from whichever of these causes the illness may not in the end have a good outcome, nevertheless it does not appear to the physicians to be such that His Holiness cannot still linger for some time, nor does he cease on this account to attend to business and give audience to ambassadors [...] and especially at present when the Prince of Capua has traveled to Rome and has had and continues to have frequent audience with His Beatitude; which demonstrates that although this illness of his, as aforesaid, at times suffers some aggravation beyond the usual from some new episode, as is customary in long infirmities, he does not on that account cease to attend to the dispatch of affairs [...]

It seems to us therefore that the coming of Monsignore of Nitra should not be delayed on this account, because as he comes through Germany we shall station persons at Padua and wherever necessary to await his arrival and inform him day by day of what we hear regarding the Pope's condition; and if the Pope is in no worse state than at present, he will be able to continue his journey to Rome, where we too shall have Messer Matteo to conclude the matter in the manner pleasing to His Royal Majesty; and if the Pope is found to be worse and unable to conduct business upon the Bishop of Nitra's arrival at Padua, we shall have him come here to Milan where the matter can be settled [...] and once that is done, depending on what is heard of the Pope's condition, the Bishop of Nitra will be able to continue his journey to Rome, where as far as the authority of the Most Reverend Monsignore our brother and the name of this state extend, this Most Serene King may promise himself [...] in the dispatch of his affairs [...] and even if the Pope's condition should worsen, he will be able to choose the course most convenient to His Royal Majesty's affairs. You will therefore make known to His Majesty [...] our firm and free good disposition in accepting the match under the [...] conditions, and likewise the manner of concluding it.

[...] at Rome or here in the manner stated above, showing him that for this reason [...] with speed [...] the Bishop of Nitra should not hold back on account of the Pope's illness, because [...] His Majesty's desire to negotiate and conclude this matter at Rome will be followed; should he worsen, it will be done here [...] we believe that for dignity and honor no other place in Italy should be preferred to it. Once you have set forth what we write, you will learn what decision His Royal Majesty takes and inform us with speed, so that we may in time station at Padua and wherever needed the persons who will wait [...] for the Bishop of Nitra to pass, in order to advise him of what he is to do [...] As for the promise of the benefice, you need not be in doubt nor fear that delay [...] because we are very pleased [...] of honor [...] that this promise should take effect, and we shall do so most willingly to reward your virtue as its merit calls for. Attend therefore with the diligence and prudence you have used up to now to this affair, and advise us of the [...] arrangement that is adopted, for on our side you will not lack recognition and proof that we esteem and hold dear your virtue.

Here many reports are circulating about the Turk's preparations against that kingdom, and therefore we were expecting some news of them in your letters; but since you have written no particulars of them, our desire remains just as great, and therefore we urge you to advise us diligently by the returning courier concerning these matters, both of the sound provisions we do not doubt this Most Serene King must make and of the nature of the Turk's attempts. Duplicate, at Milan, 20 July [...] Ludovico Maria Sforza, Duke of Bari [...]
