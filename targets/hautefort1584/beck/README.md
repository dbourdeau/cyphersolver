## The two page turns

The last signs on 208r include our `B single eq A X`, registered as *que [single]cau*. Brice groups the middle marks as `12` = *vous*. His frozen key on `B 12 G X` gives *que vous au*. The next leaf starts *es entendus d’icelui*. These join inside *aués* (modern *avez*). The continuation reads *Il le conseilla d’en avertir le [roi de Navarre]*, “He advised him to inform the [king of Navarre].” The next turn joins *c|idevant*, “previously”, across 208v/209r.

The strongest test of a previously proposed code is on 209r.17-20. In normalized French, *car il [est] fort à craindre qu’il n’en […] rien, tandis qu’il [sera ?] en espérance d’avoir l’une des infantes d’Espagne*. English: “For [there is] good reason to fear that he […] nothing of it while he [still?] hopes to have one of the infantas of Spain.” The missing verb affects what the writer fears.

## Word counts and scope

The archive at the earlier issue was downloaded and replayed before the comparisons. The initial 207r word measure is 160/198 fitted word slots. The measures on 207v and 208r are respectively 494/749 and 546/798 signs in editorially accepted words. Neither denominator measures whole-letter word coverage. Brice’s blind result applies to 207v.1-11 only, 318 signs, dictionary-coverage score 0.879 against a best shuffled 0.536. His strict word count there is 52/73 (71.2%), with about 85% sure or probable.

Eight previously withheld word positions gain second-key support. No new positions can be certified against interlinear plaintext in the supplied photographs. Three earlier words gain further support from the paired-3 test, and one new occurrence appears on 209r.17. These sets measure different things. A whole-letter percentage requires an agreed segmentation through the last cipher word. The available sign transcription ends on 209r.25 at *… le rendront peut estre un peu pl…* mid-word. No date, signature or final formula has been verified from the image. The linked reading marks that gap.

## Source photographs and the missing check

Français 15567 and Français 15568 use the cipher in different hands. The right-hand photograph shows 158r with 26 visible cipher rows. Brice’s sign transcription records the first 20. The other photograph is the left side of the same opening, with faint show-through. The cipher rows can be compared at this scale. The scattered interlinear strokes are too faint to identify a continuous French gloss with confidence. These photographs therefore support comparison of sign shapes, including `15 17`. They do not supply an independent plaintext label for either sign.

The most useful single addition for the recurring codes would be **one sharply focused, overlapping close-up of a contemporary plaintext gloss and the cipher beneath it**, preferably containing `15`. Start on the right of [15567, view 164, f. 158r](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b90639282/f164.item), lines 12-14, right end of 13 and start of 14, or the middle of line 24. Keep one cipher row on each side of the annotation. For a genuinely separate test, use the right side of [view 166, f. 160r](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b90639282/f166.item) or [view 167, f. 161r](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b90639282/f167.item), starting with lines 1-6 and including each readable gloss and adjacent cipher. The surviving gloss words and their line numbers on 160r could not be verified here. The earlier pointer to 15567 f. 207 was incorrect.

For the endpoint, [15568, view 217](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9063927n/f217.item) has 208v on the left and 209r on the right. Photograph the **right page, line 21 to the paper’s lower edge**, including the full right margin, any writing below line 25, date, signature or blank space. For a direct visual check of the first join, the matching strips are 208r lines 24-26 (right of view 216) and 208v lines 1-3 (left of view 217). Gallica denied direct image access during this comparison.

## Reproduction

From the repository root run `python3 targets/hautefort1584/beck/compare.py`. The script applies the original key, coordinate-specific 207r amendments and published word boundaries. It verifies all 77 mechanical lines and the three original denominators. It checks the SHA-256 of Brice’s frozen key, all 14 hook coordinates, the contrasting reading in *venir*, both page joins, and the paired-3, title and 158r examples. The eight word gains measure agreement between two readings; the computation cannot authenticate a period gloss.
