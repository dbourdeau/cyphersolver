## Reading

I have received since my last pretty full accounts from Mr Lovell of the proceedings at Philadelphia. I cannot
conceive how it coud have been agreed to receive an anonymous Letter of abuse against two of their commissioners.
Anonymous abuse may be from a notorious Enemy, an interested or an unworthy person, & therefore deserving of contempt
instead of credit. — not seeing any probability of doing good here, I, will probably return to you in the spring. I do
not know whether without any all instructions was calculated to prevent [...] from[...] but certainly it must have
that effect.

Mr S. Adams has informd me that Mr J. Adams wrote him very warmly in my favor, & he convinced me before his
departure that he had done so here. I have sent you Copies of his Letters by a former opportunity which will be the
more important after the wicked manoeuvre of M. Gerard. The Supplies I see are announced as
arrived, but no mention made of the senders. It is by these omissions that the Public at large, are unacquainted with
our Services, while they are imposed upon by the boastings of our Enemies to give all the merit to them. I hope you &
Francis being at home will enable them to counteract the machinations of their Enemies, to whom absence gives an
infinite advantage. For my part I suspect [...] exceedingly. His design is you may depend to be alternately governor
& delegate, to wch he must think you are an obstacle & therefore wish to destroy our influence. Till you make it a
point, as they do, to get places of [...], as well as influence the struggle will be in vain. [Gerard? Thomas?] will come
immediately to you, & I think his plan should be to marry as soon as possible the best fortune he can find & commence
merchandise in the manner that yr observation of things shall point out as the most proper. A little more knowledge
of the world & marriage will be of service to him. If Arthur comes, Ludwell will also, if the former should be obliged
to stay he will send the latter in the Spring, who will be of use to his Father & studying the Laws of Virginia for a
year before he begins to practise as i[t] be necessary. This winter will I think decide on peace, or a long [war?] by
the interference of other powers among wch Russia is most to be feard. Farewell.

## Summary

Lee protests that Congress received an anonymous letter of abuse against two of its commissioners, says he will
probably come home in the spring, and calls Gérard's conduct a "wicked manoeuvre". He urges his brothers, at home,
to counter their enemies in Virginia, warns of a man (the name group is unread) who wants to be "alternately governor
& delegate", and gives family plans for two of the sons (the first name is a doubtful group, Gerard or Thomas; then Ludwell). Peace or a long war
will turn on other powers, Russia above all.
