## Reading

I have it from very good authority that Gerard, is suspected here of hav[in]g been concernd with Deane & others, in
merchandise, Contracts &c & that he imposed upon the minister here so as to obtain the Letter for [...]n of wh he
boasts so much. Send me what facts appear on yr side to support this. My opinion is that the Doctor, the French
consul, with you, Gerard, Deane, Chaumont [&] Beaumarchais are all interested, together. There is apparently great
roguery in the business, which time, observation & enquiry will discover. This court, is in a much distress about
supportg the war & mean to call on you for vigorous [exertions?]. But I am afraid they cannot furnish you. Money.
Adieu

## Summary

Lee reports that Gérard is suspected in Paris of having been concerned with Deane in merchandise and contracts, and
gives his own opinion that "the Doctor", the French consul, Gérard, Deane, Chaumont and Beaumarchais are all
interested together; he asks his brother for facts to support it. He adds that the French court is short of money
for the war. The accusations are Lee's, written privately to his brother.
