# Letter 1: Arthur Lee to Richard Henry Lee, [Paris, 29 March 1779], letter book image 0019

Conventions for all six readings: the clear text is Lee's, as transcribed (his spelling kept); the words that stand
for code groups are written in plain type when the group is firm. A doubtful group is printed in brackets with a
question mark, with both candidates where two are open: `[vendible? venerable?]`. An unread group is `[...]`.
Letters or words supplied in the clear text are in brackets (`[thi]ng`, `[you?]`). An ending Lee did not write is
in brackets (`minister[s]`). The code gives only Christian names for persons; who is meant is not in the code and is
never added inside the reading. Group-by-group table: `../letters/l1_decryption.md`.

## Reading

The Chevalier de la Luzerne is named to the place of Gerard, who will a[...]ot leave till the arrival of this
successor. He is a respectable person, brother to the [Bishop?], who is a duke. He was before minister at
Bavaria. I am afraid the [former?] will have done all the mischief, & I foresee much, before his departure. The
[vendible? venerable?] friend, is doing all he can here with his assistants to promote Deane's [charges?]. They are
joined in the same cause & equally wicked. Tho' perhaps I do the latter injustice, in saying so. Certain it is that
such a set capable of every[thi]ng that is villanous, was hardly ever seen before.

I wou'd hint to you, that Frenchified [French?] faith, & friendship, are the same & nothing material to be hazarded
on either. Interest, is the sole guide & the sacrificing every profession & every engagement to this is the
unvariable rule. The nation, begins already to talk of against the alliance, & in a little time, if they continue
losers & no Spanish junction, which is sure more uncertain than ever, this [will] be the language of the
minister[s]. Be therefore on your gaurd. I assure [you?] the cause, was never more critical than at this moment. I
would not be so profuse in praises nor too liberal of confidence, unless I cou'd violate both as they, do, without
remorse or shame. These are the soberest & best weighed opinions I can form, & think the scales are without an
exception.

The suspicions & discontents which seem rising to great [considerableness?], on both sides the water must have very
bad, consequences if they go on. We must look well to the great cause, or bad men will betray it.

## Summary

Lee tells his brother that the Chevalier de la Luzerne is to replace Gérard as French minister to the United States.
He writes that the "friend" (the adjective before it is doubtful) and his assistants are promoting Deane's [charges?] (doubtful since the double reading of 15 Nov),
that French "faith & friendship" (the first word is written as "Frenchified") are worth nothing beside interest,
that the French nation is beginning to talk against the alliance, and that the cause has never been more critical.
These are Lee's private opinions to his brother, not established facts. Who "the former" and "the friend" are is
not in the code.
