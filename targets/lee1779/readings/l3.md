## Reading

The part of my answer to the Observations which relates to the old Doctor, is so contrived that it may be presented
or not as you judge proper. The propriety of presenting or making public the whole or any part of what I have
written, I leave entirely to your judgment.

Since Gerard was recalled the conversation runs freely about his connexion in jobs with Deane, Beaumarchais & others,
& his [chaplain? character?], is exceedingly reprobated. Mr Ford will give you particulars of things in-

I can only say of certain persons, what many others have said, that if it remains long in their power, it is much to
be feared that they will proceed from jobbing the state secrets, to job the state.

As to the favor & influence of a certain person here, I who know that instead of having influence to act for us, he
is a mere instrument in the hands of those whom the court, set over him, can see a very good reason why they should
wish him to remain. If there is such a high opinion of his political wisdom, as is pretended, his advice must of
course have been taken, since our alliance, & the very weak & unwise measures wch have raised the enem[ies], & almost
[...]d the French were his. Either his advice was not worth asking or if given, was very weak; I know that he neither
has any political knowlege nor have I any reason to believe his advice was ever asked. If Congress, are satisfyd of
the injustice of the attacks upon us, they should resolve accordingly. Some reparation must be made or I do not see
how any gentleman will ever be safe in the public service. The Chevalier de la Luzerne & the Secretary of the
Embassy, M. Marbois, who are to replace M. Gerard will not disturb us by exciting Parties or making disgraceful
connections. They seem to be well acquainted with Gerard's, having engaged unduly with certain persons.

## Summary

Lee writes that since Gérard's recall people in Paris talk freely of his "connexion in jobs" with Deane and
Beaumarchais, and that "a certain person here" is a mere instrument of those the French court set over him. He asks
that Congress, if satisfied that the attacks on the Lees were unjust, should resolve accordingly. The code hides the
names and the hard words; "a certain person" and "his political wisdom" are left unnamed in clear. These are Lee's
private charges, not established facts.
