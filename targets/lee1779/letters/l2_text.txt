R. H. Lee   Dear Br.   Paris April 22d 1779
I enclose you an answer to one of the Pieces sent me, by the Com-
mittee, which you will present or not as you judge proper.
If Mr Deane's piece should have made no impression perhaps
it may be as well to withold this answer, as doing too
much is often doing mischeif. We are here without a Syllable
from any of you since the 10th of Decr when I had a Letter from
London. Some resolutions we have seen serve only to increase
our embarrassment & surprise. I beg that both in the
answer I now send you & that addressd to Congress, you will
correct or erase whatever you think proper; & if it shd
appear to you & the other real friends to the public, that a farther
discussion of this matter at present will be detrimental to the pub-
lic cause, I shall most willingly acquiesce in suspending it. For
I am entirely of opinion with our Brother London in his short
& very sensible address to the Public, that I had rather suffer
myself than hazard injuring the Public by my justification.
[interl. line: The Success of the Enemy in [India?] has raised their credit & their friends.]
Otherwise things remain exactly as they were, & without any
appearance of any event of consequence happening soon. I beg
you will present my Compts & thanks to Mr Payne. I have it from
very good authority that 455a14, is suspected here of hav[in]g been concernd with 115b38 & others, in
merchandise, Contracts &c & that he imposed upon the 243a17 here so as to obtain the Letter for
[...]n of wh he boasts so much. Send me what facts appear on yr side to support this. My opi-
nion is that the 134a6, the 170a24, 101a35, with you, 455a14, 115b38?(116), Chaumont
[&] Beaumarchais are all 213a8-d, together. There is apparently great roguery in the
business, which time, observation & enquiry will discover. 374b12, 108a9, is in a much
{G}2b29, about supportg the 416b19 & mean to call on you for 392b13, ?2b9-s?(blot). But
I am afraid they cannot furnish you. 246b25. — Adieu
