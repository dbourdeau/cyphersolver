The Honble Richd H. Lee,   Dear Brother,   Paris, Feby 29th 1780
I expect to embark very shortly in the Alliance, & hope soon
to have the happiness of seeing you once more. Ludwell comes
with me, & Tom goes in a private Ship from Nantes, care having
been taken to engage all the passage in the Alliance, before I was in-
formd of her destination. This comes by the Marquis de la Fayette
who returns into our service, & will I hope be followd by a force suf-
ficient to strike some decisive blow. It is safer to leave the details to him
as I presume he will write you on his arrival. We are in great expec-
tation of the spanish fleet intercepting that of Rodney in his return from
Gibralter. Should this happen & his fleet receive a blow, it will crip-
ple the Enemy in their naval operations, on which all others depend.
Should Rodney escape, so hardy an enterprize happily atchieved will
raise their credit, reanimate their efforts, & do us most essential in-
jury. In that case too, the force which [struck word] [interl.: on] his failure, they must
send to protect their Islands will be turned against us. The uncertainty of these events
gives me great inquietude, & suggests a repetition of what I have always ad-
vised that you shoud remitt of no exertion on your part, that may render
us as independent as possible of events in Europe. My love to yr family; to
London & all our friends. Do you think it practicable to get 251b12, appoint[ed]
from yr 354a36, to 99a17. It will be of great importance. Think of it, in time.
                                                   Adieu
