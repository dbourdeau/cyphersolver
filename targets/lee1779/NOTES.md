# Arthur Lee to Richard Henry Lee, Paris, 1779-1780: six letters in Entick's dictionary code, read in part

Status: in progress (folder only, no site page in this commit; target files and checks only)

Class: read in part (read after matching with key from external sources). Andrew Beling, with AI agents,
14 November 2026. The code system and the book were known before us (Burnett 1917, Weber 1979, Tomokiyo), and
Burnett's typed partial key to the Lee code (filmed in 1966 on roll 1 of the Lee Family Papers microfilm) gives a
value for 39 of our 119 distinct groups. The **received copies of letters 2, 3 and 4 survive with an old decipherment
interlined** (Lee Family Papers, University of Virginia; microfilm roll 6), which we found only after our reading was
made; as far as we found it has never been printed. Ours is an independent reading of all six letters from the letter
book, with the 1776 dictionary setting and the counting rule established. Scored against the interlining before any
regrading (`received/`), it agrees in 63 of the 72 groups where the interlining can be compared (95 groups in the three
letters); the nine differences are guesses by the interliner, not dictionary words. For letters 1, 5 and 6 no
received copy was found on the Lee Family Papers microfilm or in its index, the American Philosophical Society
calendar or Harvard's calendar, and the online catalogues of the Virginia Historical Society, the Library of Congress and
Harvard show nothing for their dates. Of 166 code groups, 148 are firm, 14 doubtful and 4 unread after every figure
was read again by two blind readers on 15 November 2026 (145 / 16 / 5 at first; 151 / 11 / 4 after the received copies
settled six groups and the full-size image a seventh). Only our own AI agents have read the manuscripts; no person has
checked them.

## How sure the reading is

- **The key.** The system (Entick's *New Spelling Dictionary*, "page, column, line" = the Nth headword entry down the
  column) was identified by Burnett and Tomokiyo. The London 1776 printing and the counting rule were fixed here on
  their anchor groups; a blind re-derivation gave the same word in 43 of 54 groups; controls (page +1, page -1, other
  column, the 1781 printing) give 0 or 1 hit in 71; the old interlined decipherment on the received copies of letters
  2-4 agrees in 63 of 72 comparable groups; R. H. Lee's printed letter of 22-23 May 1779 gives the printed word in
  14 of 15 groups.
- **The weak step is reading the numerals.** Three held-out tests on letters glossed at the time all missed their 80%
  mark (54 of 71, 55 of 71, 10 of 19), and in each most misses were misread figures, not wrong look-ups. On the letter
  book itself, two blind readers double-read all 166 groups: our transcription was wrong in 1 group (0.6%) and right
  only as its alternative in 1 more; four groups that rested on one reading or on the other copy are now doubtful. A
  figure misread the same way by every reader would not be caught.
- **Grades.** Firm 148: in 146 of them at least two independent readings of the figures agree and the word fits; 2
  (distress, politicians) rest on one full reading, the received copy, with the letter book agreeing as far as it can be
  read. Doubtful 14 (printed in brackets with a question mark, both words where two are open). Unread 4.
- **Who read it.** All image readings, ours and the checks', were made by AI agents. No person has checked them.

## Source

Record Group 360; microfilm M247, item 102 vol. 3, beginning on roll 128), National Archives Catalog 502968528,
https://catalog.archives.gov/id/502968528, 227 images. The catalogue titles the volume "Transcripts of Letters from
Arthur Lee, William Lee, Ralph Izard, and the Joint Commissioners of the United States to France, 1776-80: Volume 3";
it is in fact Lee's book of copies of the letters he sent (to Vergennes, Franklin, the Committee, his brothers and

Letter-book page 39 (image 0052) completes the last sentence of letter 4 ("he being now in England") and has **no

21st 79". It is the same letter: its text and code groups follow the letter-book entry to "he being in their power"
(the letter book has "he being now in England"), then go on for about three more pages (Adams at parting, Paine,

size, then all 227 at 1000 pixels, the code pages at 1610 pixels). A letter to "The Honble R. H. L." on image 0041 was
full of bare number groups, with no decipherment anywhere in the volume.

## The code

Each code word is written as page, column, line: "243, a, XVII" = page 243, left column, 17th entry = *minister*.
The book is John Entick's *New Spelling Dictionary*. Endings Lee wanted are written after a dash (`115b38-s` =
Deane's; `322a7-sia` = Rus-sia). Persons are coded from the dictionary's list "The most usual Christian Names of Men"
(pages 454-455, five columns lettered a to e), so the code gives only a Christian name: `455a14` Gerard, `454b14`
Benjamin, `455b11` John + `454a5-s` Adam-s, `455d27` Samuel, `455a9` Francis, `454b4` Arthur. **Who is meant is
never in the code.**

## Edition and counting rule

- **Dictionary copy used:** Entick, *The New Spelling Dictionary*, London: Edward and Charles Dilly, 1776, Internet
  Archive `bim_eighteenth-century_the-new-spelling-diction_entick-john_1776`
  (https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-new-spelling-diction_entick-john_1776), a scan of a
  microfilm (source reel IA40310418-69 in the item's metadata; the library that holds the printed copy is not
  named there). Printed page = scan leaf + 2. Local copies of the pages used are in the project's raw downloads
  (`sources/raw/entick_dictionary/`, with checksums), not in this repository.
- **Why the 1776 printing.** The 1766, 1772, 1773, 1776 and 1781 editions on the Internet Archive were compared. The
  1772 and 1781 editions are paged differently (minister, Congress and loan fall elsewhere). The 1773 and 1776
  printings share page breaks on pp. 166, 196 and 229 but differ elsewhere: on p. 108 col. a, *Court* is the 10th
  entry in 1773 and the 9th in 1776. Lee writes court as 108a9 four times (letters 2, 3 and twice in 4; `decoding.tsv` ids 55, 71, 85, 117; the
  check's notes say "five times", which the claims review could not reproduce), so the **1776 setting fits Lee and the 1773
  does not** (independent check, `check/NOTES_verify.md`). Tomokiyo's twelve directly read groups (from Jay's and
  Lee's letters) fall on the stated word in the 1776 copy ten times; the misses are Jay's court 108a10 and told
  378a07.
- **Rule:** count headword entries from the top of the column; do not count the running head, letter-section
  headings ("I.") or the indented run-over lines of long entries. Fixed on loan 229a37, I 196a28 and friend 170b15,
  which are right only under this rule.
- **No second scan of the 1776 printing was found** (15 November 2026): the Internet Archive has only the copy used
  here; Google Books, viewed in a browser, shows no 1776 or 1777 copy (its full views start in 1780 and are paged like
  1781); HathiTrust refused a script at the first request (403) and was not tried further; Eighteenth Century
  Collections Online was not open in a signed-in browser. Instead 22 more pages of the same scan were fetched (local
  copies in the project's `sources/raw/entick_dictionary_2/`, not in this repository), so every page the tests needed
  has an image.
- **1777.** Tomokiyo (from Jay's usage) and Burnett (1917, "probably") name a 1777 edition. No 1777 copy was found
  online (Internet Archive, HathiTrust catalogue blocked, Google Books not checked by ordinary viewing). No difference
  between the 1776 setting and any 1777 printing has been shown.

**Lee's instructions of 3 June 1776** (item 83 vol. 1 images 27-28; image 28 is the small original slip, image 27 a
copy). Read on the images by the worker; my reading: "This Book is better than the last I sent you. It is to decypher
what I write to you & for you to write by. This is done by putting the page where the word is to be found and the
letter of the alphabet corresponding in order with the word. As there are more words in a page than letters of the
alphabet the Letter must be doubled or trebled to answer that. As thus, to express the troops, you write 369 kk 381,vv
— ing, ed, s &c must be added when necessary and distinguished by making no Comma between them and the figures. Thus
for betrayd put 33cd[?]" (the last figures unclear), then the 26 letters a to z, and the signal "If you have both Books
say the Children are well". So in 1776 Lee prescribed a page number and a letter (a-z, then doubled, then trebled) for
the word's place counted down **the whole page**, with no column, in a book he does not name. That is not the
1778-80 system (page, column a or b, Roman numeral line). The examples do not fit any Entick printing we hold: in the
1776 setting *The* is on p. 371 and *Troop* on p. 384, *Betray* on p. 63; in 1772 *The* is on p. 397. So the 1776
book was another book or another edition, and none of the 1778 glossed groups follows that method.

**Second gloss test** (`gloss1778/TEST_PLAN_gloss2.md`, `TEST_RESULT_gloss2.md`; 14-15 November 2026; the second
attempt, after the first failed). Plan written and hashed first. The figures were read by a helper who never saw the
glosses, from strips cut to hide the interlined words (checked on contact sheets; all 71 groups tested), and looked up
by a second helper who saw neither glosses nor letters; each batch carried three seed groups with known answers and
both passed. **Result: 55 of 71 (77.5%), below the 80% mark, so the second test also fails.** Controls: page +1 1 of 71
(itself a misread figure), page -1, other column and 1781 printing 0 of 71. Without the six anchor occurrences 52 of
65 (80%). Of the 16 misses, nine are the new transcriber's misreadings of the figures; the other seven are the same
seven as above (three decipherer's words, Lee one off twice, succours, quarrel). Taken together, the two tests suggest
that the rule is right where the figures are read right, and that reading the clerks' figures is the weak step; they do
not pass the mark set before them.

**Secondary evidence: the reader's frozen test** (`tests/`). Written and hashed before decoding (`tests/FREEZE.txt`):
on a fixed random sample of 30 groups, the frozen look-up fits the clear text in 27 of 30, against 0 of 22 (page + 1),
1 of 22 (page - 1), 0 of 23 (page + 7), 0 of 28 (page - 7) and 1 of 25 (1772 edition, same coordinates); 30 of 150
control crops could not be cut automatically and were left out. It passed its written mark. **Its weakness:** the same
agent extracted the words and judged them; the judging sheet shows decoded neighbours in the contexts, so a right word
is easier to accept; and the controls are random dictionary words, which almost never fit, so the test could not fail
by much and says nothing about the doubtful or emended groups. It is kept as run.

`scripts/verify.py` reproduces the counts of this folder and checks the frozen hashes.

## What the letters say, in Lee's words

These passages make private charges against Franklin, Deane, Gérard and others. They are what Arthur Lee wrote to his
brother, not established facts, and they are given here in his words with the brackets.

- Gérard "is suspected here of hav[in]g been concernd with Deane & others, in merchandise, Contracts &c"; "My
  opinion is that the Doctor, the French consul, with you, Gerard, Deane, Chaumont [&] Beaumarchais are all interested,
  together" (letter 2). Since Gérard's recall people talk of "his connexion in jobs with Deane, Beaumarchais & others"
  (letter 3). "the wicked manoeuvre of M. Gerard" (letter 5).
- "Never will our affairs go well, while Benjamin is here" and a new man should be sent; "In one word he is the
  servant of this court, not of his country" (letter 4, referent not in the code). The successor of Gérard "has often
  declard his opinion here of the old Doctor's unfitness to be minister, here" (letter 4).
- On the French: "Frenchified [French?] faith, & friendship, are the same ... Interest, is the sole guide"; "The
  nation, begins already to talk of against the alliance" (letter 1). He does not want "2000 French [troops?]" brought
  over: "I am by no means satisfyd they do not wish for a footing" (letter 4).
- Whether to present his piece against "the old Doctor" "will entirely depend upon the part taken by John Adams"
  (letter 4).
- Family and Virginia: the man who wants to be "alternately governor & delegate" (name unread); "places of [...], as
  well as influence"; plans for the sons; and in 1780: "Do you think it practicable to get myself, appoint[ed] from yr
  state, to Congress" (letters 5, 6).

  (https://archive.org/details/per_lee-family-papers_the-lee-family-papers_1742-1795_6). Internet Archive image

  with the letter book. The group-by-group reading and score: `received/received_copies.tsv`,
  `received/TEST_RESULT_received.md`.
- **The interlinings: two layers.** (a) Darker, ink-like marks of one to three letters: in letter 3 a skeleton of
  first, middle and last letters over nearly every group ("s-t-e" over 354,a,XXXVI, "w-o-m" over wisdom, "G R D"
  over Gerard's), in letter 4 single initials ("o", "c", "f", "t", "p") and a few short words ("ships", "line",
  "meannesses"). (b) Fainter, thinner full words in an upright hand, often queried in the manner of an editor ("Court
  (King?)", "base (?)", "(successor?)", "Izard?", "[Franklin?]", "R. Morris" in the margin): all of letter 2 and most
  full words of letter 4. On the black-and-white film ink and pencil cannot be told apart for certain. Layer (b)
  writes words the dictionary cannot give at those places ("sail" over 315,b,XXXV = Return; "ruled" over
  314,b,XXXVII = Restrain; "service" over 330,b,XIII = Secretary; "Izard?" over 196,a,XXVIII = I), and writes

> follow his Mandates ... I have no doubt that [Dean] [115 b XXXVIII] will [come] [92 b XXVII] — [off] [259 b XXXII]
> with [eclat] [140 a XXVIII] — which wd. not have been the case had [you] [426 b XXVIII] taken care to [give]
> [177 a XVI] [us] [415 a X] timely [advice] [39 b II] of his [recal] [305 b XXX]. ... one in particular [the]
> [373 a XXXIV] [nephew] [253 b XXI] of [Frankl-in] [169 b XXI in], I am sure in the above time has made £40,000 Ster. ...

Deane, recalled from Paris, will "come off with eclat" before Congress, which William Lee thinks would not have
happened had Richard Henry Lee given the commissioners timely advice of the recall; Franklin acts as if Congress must
follow his orders; Franklin's nephew (Jonathan Williams, as Ford's note has it) has made £40,000. The code hides
charges against Franklin, his family and Deane in a letter sent by post. Group by group, with the controls and the
earlier readings: `william_lee_1778/groups.tsv`.

**What was known before, and what this adds.**

- *Print.* Worthington C. Ford, *Letters of William Lee* (Brooklyn, 1891), II 491-493, prints the letter with every
  group left as figures: "I have no doubt 115 b xxxviii will 92 b xxvii — 259 b xxxii with 110 a xxviii — which
  would not have been the case had 426 b xxviii taken care to 177 a xvi, 415 a x timely 39 b ii of his 305 b xxx" (II
  492, read on the page image, Internet Archive `letterswilliaml00leegoog`; the last i of "xxxii" carries a stray
  mark). The first group, "169 b xxi in.", is on II 491, also read on the image. The last three groups ("373 a xxxiv,
  253 b xxi of 169 b xxi in") are on II 493, whose image could not be had (two requests returned server errors); they
  are from the text layer only. He supplies three names in footnotes from the sense: "Benjamin Franklin" (II 491),
  "Deane" (II 492) and "Williams, nephew of Franklin" (II 493, text layer). In a note at II 417-418 (page images) he
  says that the dictionary cipher "baffled all my efforts", gives "110 a xxviii" as his example, and says the only
  edition of Entick he could find, of 1783, does not fit.
- *The typed notes on cipher filmed on roll 1* (the material attributed above to Edmund C. Burnett; Internet Archive
  roll 1 text layer) copy this passage from Ford's text and write a word over each group: Deane, come off, criticism?,
  you, give, us, advice, recall, the nephew, Franklin. So **11 of the 12 different words (12 of the 13 groups) were
  already written in, from the sense** and from Burnett's partial key, in an unpublished typescript.
- *Added here.* (1) Each group is identified as the entry at that place in the 1776 dictionary, so the readings are
  look-ups and not guesses from the sense. (2) **One word is new: 140 a XXVIII = "eclat"** ("come off with eclat").
  Both blind readers read the manuscript figure as 140. Ford prints 110 (on his page image), and the typed notes,
  which cite Ford II 492, write 110 a XXVIII with "criticism?" over it. 110 a XXVIII in 1776 is "Crinkle" (27 Crink,
  29 Crinose), so "criticism?" is not the 1776 entry at the figure it stands over; with its query mark it reads as a
  word supplied from the sense.
