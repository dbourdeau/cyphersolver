# English: summary of the sense (certain and probable parts)

A word-for-word translation is not given: about 11% of the words are unresolved (count of 11 Nov 2026). This summary follows the reading.

F. 67r. Longlée takes an opportunity towards Bayonne to send the duplicate of his last dispatch and report what has happened since. The Catholic King is in good health; Longlée sees no other movements or preparations for war than the army of sixty or eighty ships already reported, ready to oppose {the English}, should they have any design to harass “ces costes de deça comme ils ont acoustumé”; Flanders is named about the rest of that army (passage partly unresolved).

On the 21st “of this month” there arrived at Madrid the sieur Tornasoni, a Florentine gentleman, nephew of the sieur Pietro Paule {Tousinet}, with two or three others, sent to the Catholic King by the Duke of Mercœur (“duc de Mercure”). It is conjectured that he is to ask the King to declare openly against Henry IV, to forbid trade to Frenchmen who are not of the League, and to have their persons, goods, merchandise and ships arrested; and he will ask for money, which is also to be asked by Roissieu, expected any day on behalf of the Duke of Mayenne. Longlée does not vouch for this news, which does not come from a sure enough place; even if the requests are made, he sees no reason they should be granted; as for money, some will always be given “pour entretenir la guerre”. He will try to find out more. Then, partly unresolved, Portugal and the Low Countries; Savoy {asks for} relief.

F. 67v. The business that “comes upon his arms”; what was taken from him (unresolved passage); {Lesdiguières} is named. The Duke of Sessa goes to Rome after setting out his reasons, [as] ordinary ambassador; the Count of Olivares, who resides there, will withdraw; Longlée says he is {pressed} by this courier (passage partly unresolved). He has received no dispatch from the King since 3 November. He does not see that Madrid is any better disposed towards Henry IV’s right: to any proposal, {without objection}, the only answer is “la defence de l’eglise catholique”; “{qu’on} ce {lassera} {bientost}” of anything but the defence of the Church (they will soon tire of it); then “{de me voir} ici si [affaires]…” (passage partly unresolved). He waits every day for His Majesty’s commands.
