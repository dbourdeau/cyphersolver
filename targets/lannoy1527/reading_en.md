# Lope de Soria to Charles de Lannoy, viceroy of Naples, Genoa, 7 March 1527: translation

## f. 9r

Most Illustrious and Excellent Lord. The last letter I wrote to Your Illustrious Lordship was of the 23rd of last month, by way of ⟨Ferrara⟩. Captain Bracamonte will carry this one. With him, on the 6th of last month, I received Your Excellency's letter of 3 December, the most recent I have from you, which I have answered in many others. I have told you how the said Bracamonte took on here, with the galleys that went to Corsica, almost all the biscuit he had and the saltpetre, as Your Lordship will see from the certificate he carries of what he has consigned. A little biscuit remained in Corsica, and I have promised that it be sent to me for the provisioning of the galley of the gobo.

Before the said saltpetre arrived I had sent to the Duke of Bourbon, who was near Piacenza, seven mules loaded with cannon powder, and he received it. Afterwards he charged me to send the said saltpetre to Pavia, and so I sent almost all of it with twenty mules, in the charge of a carrier of this country. I ordered him to go by the Lomellina, because of the danger there might be on the Voghera road now that our camp had left the neighbourhood of Piacenza. And that rogue of a carrier, when he came to Sale, to save the cost of the mules put the saltpetre on the water, together with more than 25 bales of silk he was carrying for other people, and taking it all without escort, the boat in which he carried it was taken by the enemy at the mouth of the Ticino. Your Excellency may well imagine how desperate I am at this, more for the lack the saltpetre will cause than for its value; the said Bracamonte will inform Your Excellency of everything. I will try to recover its value from the carrier, since he disobeyed my order, though I think he will hide from me. There is a great lack of saltpetre, and Your Excellency should order a good quantity sent.

Here we have heard various news these days that grieved us, because the enemy gave out much more than there was. I hope in God that victory will remain with Your Excellency as in the past.

Here we are now well off for wheat, because a cog loaded with it has come in, and we expect others to arrive at any hour, and it still comes from Lombardy,

## f. 9v

so that for now we are out of famine. But ⟨there is some kind[?] of talk among these citizens of making the union that began in the city in recent days: to do away with the factions, to govern themselves as a republic with the citizens and not with the Pope alone as hitherto, and to be neutral, so as to free themselves from the expense and hardship they bear. Only two or three citizens are in this talk with the Pope, and the matter is kept very secret. These days they called me and told me of it, and they say they will do nothing against His Majesty's service. I told them that any change would be odious to His Majesty. I think they will discuss it and not carry it out until they see how the [mur]s[?] fare; and if our affairs were somewhat out of favour[?], I think they would carry it out, and it would be tacitly to withdraw obedience from the [rez] ... this[?] pope. I will hinder it all I can, and so will I in person; but I do not think I can prevent it if our affairs do not prosper, because everyone looks to what suits him in particular.⟩ I have informed His Majesty of everything, and Your Excellency will be informed of what is happening; but ⟨it is not necessary to talk about it, so as not to wake anyone who might be asleep.⟩

As we have understood here, ⟨in the agreement that was being negotiated in [lir], among other articles there was one saying that whoever holds anything in Italy shall keep it; and they are [tmi] very scandalised at this, and fearful, because the enemy holds Savona and all the coasts (riviere), and Genoa alone stands by itself; and if it remained like this, this city would be ruined and would be forced to change sides. It is necessary that its coasts be restored to it, and therefore it is necessary that [?] take care that, if any settlement is made, it be declared that Savona and the coasts return under the dominion of Genoa as they were before ...⟩

## f. 10r

This lord Duke [of Genoa] and the members of the Commune have spoken to me about what they have thought and decided for the remedy of this land. Considering that, as things are now, with the French fleet at Savona doing all the harm it can, this city could not hold out, the remedy is to have many galleys, so as to be strong at sea and let their ships and merchandise come safely. The decision is that, ⟨if His Majesty is willing to contribute half the cost,⟩ the prince will arm three galleys he has built and will set about making up to fifteen new hulls to arm them; with these three, three others he has armed and His Majesty's three that are here, there will be nine, and they will try to do something good with them and damage the French. For this they have told me that, considering that ⟨the [?] towards [lax pax] of Naples, the galleys of [zaz] and of [giq], which will be 21, and that there are now[?] only six of His Majesty's, the six cannot do anything against the 21; so it would be better for the six of His Majesty's that are [xup] to come and join these nine. With fifteen galleys together here, they would without doubt break those of France, even if they were in any port at all. The six could very well come, leaving by night, and once they had left the enemy's behind, they would have no [fub]; and with[?] these of France routed they could all go together, with some others that would have been armed, towards [lax pax] or wherever else it might suit. And it is certain that with the six at Naples and these others alone[?], neither the one group nor the other can do any good, while together they could achieve good results. This matter of the galleys is a most important point for this service of His Majesty, [bel] if these cannot form a strong fleet.⟩ The said Duke and the Commune have told me that, for this to be done well, ⟨His Majesty must contribute half the cost of arming the said galleys, because they have no means of sustaining so great an expense; and that I should make it known to His Majesty and to [?] and to the lord [?] of Bourbon, so that they may order provision and give order for the money that will be needed.⟩

## f. 10v

I have written about it to the Duke of Bourbon and await his answer, and I am also writing it to His Majesty, and Your Illustrious Lordship will be informed of all this. If it seems right to you, order ⟨that the six galleys that are at Naples come and join these nine that we shall have here;⟩ and order also provision ⟨for this expense, of some money or credit, so that it can be raised. The preservation of this city is very important to His Majesty's service, and these citizens know it, and they think His Majesty should bear all these expenses; and they talk in [lex] in such a way that it seems it will have to be done by force, [or] they will change sides. There are bad people among them who do not wish His Majesty's service and want an excuse to make a change. In truth this commune is very poor, and they are tired of so many hardships, damages and expenses, and they say they suffer it all for serving His Majesty, because if they chose to withdraw from it they could live in peace and do their [gas]s; and the bad ones spread this among the populace so that it may rise.⟩

The Duke of Bourbon with the army arrived near Reggio on the 27th of last month, and they say the Duke of Ferrara will join him there and they will decide what to do. But he is still resolved to march towards Florence, and if need be towards Rome. They have a very powerful army ⟨but very little money.⟩ Antonio de Leyva remains in charge of the guard and government of Milan. The enemy had already entered [?] with about a thousand men, and Antonio drove them out again, and Monza was sacked by our men when they recovered it. And the Count

## f. 11r

of Lodron goes about Monferrato with his little force doing some damage. In Asti there are about two thousand men and a hundred French lances, and from France there is no news of troops crossing for now. The King has gone to Picardy to conclude his marriage with the King of England's daughter, ⟨and he has sent money to fortify Savona and to keep it under his dominion, as it is already; and the people of Savona have invited him with [?] ... to be under ... of this city; and he orders that Count Pedro Navarro [go] now[?] with the galleys, and that the ships[?] and galleons go towards Naples.⟩ Nor is there any word that ⟨the Swiss are moving.⟩

Since the said Bracamonte is ready to leave at any hour in a carrack going to Sicily, and I had written this letter for him to carry, and since it can be sent by this route, he will carry the copy. There is nothing more to say, except that from letters from France of the 3rd of this month it is understood that ⟨the King[?] and the Venetians were offering [riz] the kingdom[?] of Naples, and he would not accept it unless they also give him the state of Milan.⟩ I hope in God that he will provide better and give victory to Your Excellency, whose life and estate may our Lord God keep and increase as you desire. From Genoa, 7 March 1527.

Your Illustrious and Excellent Lordship's most faithful servant, who kisses your hands, [Lope de Soria].
