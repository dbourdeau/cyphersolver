# A3. Verifier's view of No. 35 before opening the reader's alignment (15 Nov 2026)

**What the layout shows.** The office copy (SP 94/205 f.268r-v) has 62 numbers in 11 rows (4, 7, 6, 6, 6, 6, 6, 6 on
the recto, catchword 2898; 6, 6, 3 on the verso). A second, upright hand has written the English as running text in
the spaces between the rows: seven lines, 54 words, the first in the space under row 1, the last ("should be appointed
to reside there. —") in the space under row 7. Each English line fills the width of the page whatever stands beneath
it; no word is centred on a number. The spaces under rows 8 to 11 carry pen strokes only. The same hand treats No. 36
(f.269r-v, next in the volume) and the private letter of 10 April (f.185) the same way: there 108 English words stand
over 93 numbers and the last 14 numbers are under strokes.

**What that says about English and numbers.** The clerk entered the finished plain text in the space he had, starting
at the top, and stopped when the text was done. The English therefore gives the order of the words and nothing more:
a word stands at or before its number, increasingly before as the passage goes on. The strokes under the last rows are
line-fillers, not a statement that those numbers mean nothing: in the 10 April letter 108 words cannot be carried by
the 79 numbers that have English over them. So the 15 numbers on the verso of No. 35 (rows 9 to 11) are not "unread";
part of the 54 words must belong to them. Number and word can be paired only by counting in order, helped by numbers
that recur elsewhere with the same word.

**One check of order I could make blind.** No. 35 has ... 3239 1855 672 454 3522 2236 1661 371 507 3637 423 ... and
No. 36 has ... 3239 1851 899 3115 1855 3522 2236 201 842 2983 485 1661 371 507 423 ...; both English texts have
"for the establishment of the Packet(s) ... Vice Consul ... Agent" in that stretch. Eight numbers recur in the same
order where the same words recur in the same order. That supports reading in order; it does not by itself say which
number is which word.

**Nulls, as I would call them blind.**
- Opening: 1516, 903, 1260, 2120 (the whole of row 1) and probably 3205. Reason: 717 stands where "I"/"me" stands
  three times in No. 8 (row 19 third, row 11 first, and near "I can" in row 4), and the text of No. 35 begins "I have";
  717 is the sixth number. No. 5 of 22 January likewise seems to need five numbers before "I should not". Caveat: 1516
  also occurs in mid-sentence in No. 8 (row 10), so either nulls are used inside passages too or 1516 has a meaning and
  my count of five is one too many.
- Closing: I cannot name them from the layout. 54 words with several that a code would have to spell or build
  (Florida Blanca, Santander, Packet boats, Vice Consul, establishment, Sentiments, communicated, appointed) need about
  as many groups as there are left after five opening nulls (57). I would therefore expect few closing nulls: the last
  row of three (120, 1612, 893) at most, and I would not be surprised by none.
- Inside the text: none that I can identify blind. 3637 stands between 507 and 423 in No. 35 but not in No. 36
  (507 423), so 3637 is a candidate for a null or a short word.
- My blind total: 4 to 8 nulls of 62. A claim of many more than that would need the table to show that most words are
  single groups.
