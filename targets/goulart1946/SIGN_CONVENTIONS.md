# Sign conventions: goulart1946 (CPDOC GV c 1946.03.09/2)

Written before any key was applied.

The telegram text is printed by the receiving teleprinter on gummed paper strips pasted on a Departamento dos
Correios e Telégrafos form. The signs are the strip capitals of the Latin alphabet, the digits 0-9, `/`, `-`, `(`,
`)`, `=` and the ordinal sign `º` (written `9º`, a small o over a bar). There are no cipher figures, no symbols and
no number groups. The text is in plain-language words; any code is "open code" (cover words standing for names).

Transcription rules:

- one strip = one line, as pasted on the form, prefixed `L01` ... ;
- words are kept as printed, in capitals, without accents (the teleprinter has none);
- `PT` is the telegraph word for a full stop and is transcribed as printed;
- `=` is the teleprinter separator before the signature and after the address, kept as printed;
- a strip letter that is doubtful would be written `X?`; none was needed;
- handwriting on the form (pencil and ink) is not part of the transmitted text; it is listed separately at the end
  of the transcription under `# hand`.
