## Short answer

**I found no edition, calendar, study or decipherment of Cosimo III's letters to Montauti (Penn Ms. Codex 533, 531, 534), and
no study of their cipher.** I found no publication that uses the Penn Montauti volumes. Apart from the catalogue records, the
only description of them in print is the Zacour-Hirsch catalogue (1965), which I cited from Penn's record and did not open.
I found no modern study of Medici grand-ducal ciphers of 1680 to 1700. **Key material exists in two collections, but neither is published or online:**
Penn Ms. Coll. 180, Folder 114 ("diplomatic cipher codes, 1689 and 1694"), and Stanford M0320, Box 1, Folder 1 ("Ciphers").

## 2. The cipher and Medici ciphers of 1680-1700

- **No study or edition found** of this two-digit cipher with the w-shaped sign, or of any Tuscan grand-ducal cipher of 1680 to 1700.
  Searches tried: cifre medicee, cifrario mediceo, "segretario delle cifre", "Mediceo del Principato" with cifrari/chiavi, and crittografia diplomatica medicea.
  The only crypto-historical work these searches turned up is Marco Vito on the fifteenth century, which is out of period.
- **Office:** Apollonio Bassetti (1631-1699) was appointed **Segretario della Cifra** by Cosimo III in 1670. In Florence that title
  meant the chief secretary of state, not a code clerk, and he held it "for the following thirty years", which covers the whole Montauti period.
  Source: Gloria Moorman and Ingeborg van Vugt, "Medici Rule Reimagined: Cosimo III, the Dutch Republic, and Grand Ducal Aspirations
  for Seventeenth-Century Tuscany (c. 1667-1723)", *Erudition and the Republic of Letters* 7.4 (2022), author's manuscript pp. 7 and 39.

## 3. Biography

- **Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani: no entry for Anton Francesco Montauti.** DBI vol. 76 (2012) has only "MONTAUTI, Antonio (Giovanni Antonio)",
  the Florentine sculptor (1683-1746), by Sandro Bellesi: https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/antonio-montauti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/ (cited only, through a search snippet).
  The guessed URL anton-francesco-montauti_(Dizionario-Biografico) returns no entry (opened).
- **Best available reference for his mission:** the Penn Ms. Coll. 180 record (opened). It calls him "Abbot and Marquis", special envoy for Cosimo III at the
  imperial court in Vienna **until 1699**, then **Secretary of War 1699-1708**, with postings elsewhere in Europe. The catalogues use "Abbe Marquis" and "Marchese".
  The Codex 534 dispatches run from 17 Sept. 1689 to 14 Nov. 1699, which fixes the Vienna mission at about 1689 to 1699. Stanford's "March 24, 1670 to Oct. 31, 1723"
  matches the span of Cosimo III's reign and is the span of the papers, not of the mission. I found no Repertorium der diplomatischen Vertreter (vol. 1, 1936) entry online.
- **DBI, Cosimo III:** Elena Fasano Guarini, "COSIMO III de' Medici, granduca di Toscana", *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*, vol. 30 (1984).
  https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/cosimo-iii-de-medici-granduca-di-toscana_(Dizionario-Biografico)/ (opened). It does not mention Montauti or ciphers. Points relevant to these letters:
  - Royal treatment: Cosimo sought "trattamento regio" after Louis XIV granted it to Victor Amadeus II in 1690. **The Emperor conceded it in 1691** and Rome in 1699; France and Spain never did.
  - Contributions: **in 1691 the Empire required Italian princes to side with Austria or pay contributions as imperial vassals.** Cosimo paid, but negotiated a large reduction by arguing that the dues fell only on feudal territories such as Pontremoli and Lunigiana.
  - Neutrality: Livorno's neutrality was proclaimed for the first time in 1692.
  - Marriages: Ferdinando married Violante of Bavaria in 1689, after a Portuguese match failed in 1685-86. Anna Maria Luisa married the Elector Palatine in 1691. Gian Gastone married Anna Maria Francesca of Saxe-Lauenburg, widow of the Count Palatine Philipp of Neuburg, in 1697.

## What bears on what Cosimo would put in cipher to Montauti, 1692-1700

From the catalogues and the DBI, the confidential business between Florence and Vienna in these years was:
1. **Imperial war contributions** (the main subject of Codex 534): how much Tuscany would pay towards the imperial troops quartered in Italy, and the claim that only feudal territories (Pontremoli, Lunigiana, Filattiera, Licciana) were liable.
2. **Gian Gastone's marriage**, 1694-97: the Saxe-Lauenburg inheritance, terms with the bride's side and the Emperor's consent. Penn says the Cosimo letters "mostly" concern this.
3. **Royal treatment and precedence** (granted by the Emperor in 1691) and how it was applied at court.
4. Contacts through the Elector Palatine and Anna Maria Luisa at Düsseldorf. The Electress was the go-between for the Lauenburg match.
5. Tuscan neutrality (Livorno 1692) and relations with Rome, where Cardinal Francesco Maria worked on imperial business.
Likely cipher words: contribuzione, quartieri, truppe, Imperatore, Cesare, Elettore Palatino, Elettrice, principessa, matrimonio, dote, feudi, Pontremoli, Lunigiana, trattamento, Spagna, Francia, Papa.
**This list is my inference from the subject descriptions. No source says what was enciphered.**
