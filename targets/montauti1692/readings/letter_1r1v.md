# The first letter of Codex 533 (ff. 1r-1v): Cosimo III to Montauti, undated

University of Pennsylvania, Kislak Center, Ms. Codex 533, ff. 1r-1v. Autograph of Cosimo III, Grand Duke of Tuscany,
to his envoy Anton Francesco Montauti. **No date line**: the text runs to the foot of 1v and breaks off; f. 2r begins
another letter. Images: Penn's IIIF manifest for Ms. Codex 533 (`sources.md`), canvases "1r" and "1v".
Small crops of the cipher lines: `../images/c533_1r_*.jpg`, `../images/c533_1v_*.jpg`.

## What the letter says, in one sentence (the verifier's wording)

> In an undated letter partly in cipher (after the 1691 marriage), Cosimo III tells his envoy Montauti that the Elector
> Palatine has let him know of a report, written to the Elector, that Cosimo's daughter had written to Cosimo asking
> permission to return home and separate from her husband. Cosimo says such talk shames and offends his and his
> daughter's reputation, speaks of 'such calumnies', and asks Montauti to use all his skill against it, if need be by a
> journey to Düsseldorf.

## What this does and does not say

- It is **a report at second or third hand** that Cosimo passes on and treats as calumny: the Elector Palatine tells
  Cosimo ("Mi fa sapere") that something was written to the Elector ("li fu scritto"), namely that Cosimo's daughter
  had written to Cosimo to ask leave to return and separate.
- **There is no explicit denial among the words read.** Cosimo's rejection is carried by "ci fanno vergogna" (1r l. 14),
  "ci offen[...] ... reputatione" (1v l. 1) and "simili calunnie" ("such calumnies", 1v l. 4). No sentence read says
  "this is false".
- **The person who wrote to the Elector is unread** (1r l. 5: "d[e]l? un? amico" or "del March[ese]?", neither secure).
- **The letter has no date.** It is after the marriage of Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici to Johann Wilhelm, Elector
  Palatine (1691), and before his death (1716). Nothing on the page narrows it; the volume's legible years are 1692-1693.
- **The clear text at 1r lines 5-6 and 11-13 needs a palaeographer before anything is quoted.** So do 1v lines 1 and 3.
- Nothing here says that the Electress asked to leave her husband. Do not write that.

What each mark rests on:
- **68 read as 6w** (1r a, t*): Cosimo's w written as an 8-like loop. The verifier calls this a reading of the w form,
  not an emendation.
- **The second 64 of 1r a**: the image shows a 6-shaped sign entered by a hairline from the crossbar of the 4 (r);
  read as w it gives w4 = e and "Elettore". Either way one e of "Elettore" is not written. Unsettled; it does not
  change the identification.
- **Final ww of 1r a** (o+): the verifier reads the same strokes as 4 8, emended to 40 = o. A context value or an
  emendation: either way not key v3.
- **96 / 92 read as 56 / 52** (m*, h*): the "9" is Cosimo's hooked head with a descender. The verifier found the same
  form in undoubted 5s (55 = l), so these are readings of one letter form; marked anyway.
- **06 read as 66** (1r c, s*): an oval joined by a hairline to the next 6; no ascender of its own.
- **w2 = a at the end of "permission-"**: written where e is needed; not a listed confusion. The e is not on the page.
- **w4 = e in "figlea"** (1v a): written where i is needed; not a listed confusion.
- **The context values change no word's identity**: every word they touch is fixed by its other letters
  (r i t _ r n a r e; m a r i t _; _ u a after "ritornare"). "separarsi" is key v2 alone, without emendation.
