# Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici and Johann Wilhelm: what the literature says (1691-1700)

Web check only, 14 Nov 2026. Nothing was posted, emailed or ordered. Done by script (curl, honest User-Agent, paced) and WebSearch/WebFetch; Chrome was not used. HathiTrust was not touched, because the Acton search there had already been blocked earlier today (see prior_work_round4.md, block log).

## Short answer

1. **No source I could read mentions a wish, request, plan or rumour in 1691-1700 that Anna Maria Luisa would go back to Florence or separate from Johann Wilhelm. None mentions a calumny about the couple passing between Florence and Düsseldorf either.** The modern literature says the opposite: a close, affectionate marriage. The Codex 533 passage ("ritornare qua e separarsi", "offesa") therefore has no published parallel that I found. That may be because the story was a false report that the courts quashed. I did not search the archival correspondence itself (Mediceo del Principato), and that is where any trace would be.
2. **Montauti did go to Düsseldorf.** Hermine Kühn-Steinhausen (1938), working from the Electress's letters to Cardinal Francesco Maria, names "Abbate Antonio Francesco Marchese Montauti" with Fra Tommaso del Bene as "Gesandten ihres Vaters" (her father's envoys) at the Düsseldorf carnival. Montauti's appearance is dated by her letter of **9 February 1697** (ASF, Mediceo del Principato 5835, doc. 63). What he was sent to do is not stated (see section 3).
3. **Corrections to the brief:**
   - The church wedding was at **Innsbruck**, not Düsseldorf. The proxy marriage was in Florence on 29 April 1691, and the couple entered Düsseldorf on 19 July 1691.
   - The biographer is **Hermine** Kühn-Steinhausen, not Hermann.
   - Her book on Johann Wilhelm is dated **1953** in the NDB. The 1958 item is her edition of Rapparini.

## 2. Did any source mention a separation, return or calumny story, c. 1691-1700?

**None that I read.** I checked:
- **DBI, Anna Maria Luisa** (Gencarelli, vol. 3, 1961). Nothing between the 1691 departure and the 1710s succession question.
- **DBI, Cosimo III** (Fasano Guarini, vol. 30, 1984). Nothing.
- **Casciu 2006.** I read the whole essay. It stresses the happy marriage and mentions no crisis.
- **Verga 2006.** I read the whole essay. Nothing for the 1690s. It mentions her 1701 letters with "maliziosi pettegolezzi sulla vita di corte" (ASF MdP 5853).
- **Galluzzi 1781, vol. IV.** I searched the full text for Elettrice/Palatino/separar/calunn. No such story; the only "calunniato" (l. 8314) concerns a general.
- **Kühn-Steinhausen 1938** (festivities article). Nothing.
- **NDB, Johann Wilhelm.** Nothing.
- **English, German and Italian Wikipedia.** Nothing.
- **Web searches** (Italian and English) for separazione/calunnia/"tornare" with Elettrice. No hits.

**My inference, not established:** Kühn-Steinhausen writes (1938, p. 72) that the Elector had the Electress draw her father's envoys into the carnival "auf den besonderen Wunsch des Kurfürsten, der wohl den Hofleuten seines Schwiegervaters zeigen wollte, daß die Tochter ihres Herrn sich in einer reichen und frohen Umgebung befinde". That is, at the Elector's special wish, presumably to show her father's courtiers that their master's daughter was living in rich and happy surroundings. The "wohl" (presumably) shows this is her own reading. If a story that the Electress was unhappy and wanted to come home had reached Düsseldorf, such a display before Cosimo's envoys, Montauti among them, would be a natural answer. That would fit the Codex 533 cipher passage, in which the Elector tells Cosimo he had been written to that the daughter had asked leave to return and separate, and which speaks of an "offesa". This is a possible connection only. No source makes it.

## 3. Montauti and Düsseldorf

- **Established:** Kühn-Steinhausen 1938, p. 72. The Electress got "die Gesandten ihres Vaters, den würdigen Prior Fra Tommaso del Bene und den Abbate Antonio Francesco Marchese Montauti" to join the carnival. She wrote to her uncle that she had danced with del Bene (letter of 4 Feb. 1696, ASF MdP 5834, doc. 6) and that Montauti had cut a very good figure in his mask costume (letter of **9 Feb. 1697**, MdP 5835, doc. 63). Del Bene was the Tuscan envoy resident at the Electoral court in 1696-97 (Casciu n. 40: his report of 30 Nov. 1697, MdP 2669; Kühn-Steinhausen n. 19: del Bene to Panciatichi, 4 Feb. 1696, MdP 2868).
- **Not established:** whether Montauti had a formal mission to Düsseldorf or was passing through, and what his business was. February 1697 falls while the Saxe-Lauenburg marriage was being arranged. The bride was at Düsseldorf, and the wedding there followed on 2 July 1697 (Wikipedia, citing Acton pp. 208-211). Since Montauti was Cosimo's man in Vienna and the match needed the Emperor's goodwill, a Lauenburg errand is plausible. **That is my inference.**
- Codex 533 f. 57r ("viaggio" + cipher "Dusseldorf"; see NOTES_montauti_round4.md) may refer to this journey or another. Its year is not fixed, so this is not established.
- Acton (1958) has 0 hits for "Montauti" (prior_work_round4.md).

## 5. Best references for her marriage

1. **Hermine Kühn-Steinhausen, *Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici, Kurfürstin von der Pfalz* (1939; Italian edition 1967).** It is the archival biography built on her correspondence and is still the standard reference (Casciu 2006, n. 6). Pair it with her edition of the letters, *Düsseldorfer Jahrbuch* 40 (1938).
2. **Stefano Casciu, "Principessa di gran saviezza", in *La principessa saggia* (2006), pp. 30-57.** It is the best modern synthesis that is readable online, with full archival notes (marriage articles: ASF Misc. Medicea 2, ins. 7; correspondence with Francesco Maria: ASF MdP 5828-5855).

For an English reader, Acton's *The Last Medici* (pp. 181-182 and 208-212 of the 1958/1980 text) is the usual citation.
