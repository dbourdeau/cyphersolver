## Findings of this pass

- **The bare Λ is a doubling mark.** It doubles the letter that follows: que[Λ]la = quella (L9), rimase[Λ]ro (L9),
  ce[Λ]sarò = cessarò (L11), pare[Λ]se = paresse (L12), tu[Λ]te = tutte (L15), que[Λ]li = quelli (L16). The dotted
  Λ̇ in "principalmente" (L2) and the free-standing Λ in L18 ("è") are E.
- **"far" is not always CHE.** In L6 and at the start of L15 the same-looking group is three letters, f a n = NTO:
  "altrotanto" (L6) and "mancame|nto" (L14-15). This removes the first pass's "altro(?)ta(?) che ho" and "che ponendo".
- **y = B in "bono proposito"** (L10), as in "d'Urbino". Elsewhere y = N.
- **ρ = F in "fine"** (L18). In L3 the ρ of "mai" reads M. The two uses stay separate.
- **h = O** in "opinione" (L14). This is the only place where it has that value. Elsewhere h = B.

## Italian text (clear parts in italics)

*Syre: Sapendo* il timore hano Venetiani di la pace, principalmente per non esere astreti contra il Turco, il che mai
farano, [rr] heri cum il duca d'Urbino et providitore fece l'oficio de vostro bono servitore, in asecurar lei che mai
Vostra Maiestà abandonerà la sua Signoria, et altrotanto dise dil duca di Milano, il quala [cum] gran pena mai si
asecurerà, etiam cum l'andata di [48]. Manco li doi signori, prometo quella, rimasero asai satisfati(?). [rr] dirò
bene ch'io facio de le pratiche da me a bono proposito, de le quale mai cessarò s[e]in che viva, né[Λ?] vogli no tacere
che fuo [......](?) [cherì](?) in consiglio paresse, che se fuose concluso de provare de s[ρ]forcire Milano. Mia
opinione è che fu(?) li farà per loro mancamento, ponendo a campo a tutte hore dificultate per le [..] quale durano,
che noi seiamo quelli dicano de prima. De non farsi il che, non li andarà: fato se[Λ?] [48] ne(?) crede com[e](?)
[se terrò](?) farà, et il tuto, Sire, è a uno fine non bono.
*Syre, io suplico V[ost]ra M[aes]tà ... Datum in campo in la pieve de Loccate ali XV de Giugno 1529.
Le vostre humile et obeyent servitor et subject Galeazo Vesconte.*

## English summary

Visconti writes to the King from the League camp at the pieve de Locate in mid-June 1529. The Venetians fear the peace
(the Cambrai talks, with the Treaty of Barcelona just concluded between the Emperor and the Pope). Above all they fear
being bound to act against the Turk, "which they will never do". Yesterday [rr] did the office of the King's good
servant with the Duke of Urbino and the Venetian proveditor. He assured them that His Majesty will never abandon the
Signoria, and he said as much of the Duke of Milan. The Duke of Milan, Visconti adds, will hardly ever feel secure,
even with the arrival or departure ("l'andata") of [48]. Visconti assures the King that the two lords were well
satisfied all the same. [rr] will report favourably on the negotiations Visconti conducts, to good purpose. Visconti
will never stop them while he lives, and he will not keep silent. He had urged in council that, once terms were
concluded, the army should try to force Milan. In his opinion the others will fail through their own shortcomings,
putting difficulties in the way at every hour. Those difficulties last, and Visconti's party are the ones who spoke of
them first. If Milan is not attempted it will not happen. If [48] does not believe it, the outcome is foreseeable,
"and the whole thing, Sire, is heading to no good end." The letter was written days before Saint-Pol's defeat at
Landriano (21 June 1529).
