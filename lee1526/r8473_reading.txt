KEY OBSERVATIONS / CORRECTIONS TO THE TABLE
- d-shaped sign (with ascender) = u (7ɬ⊥dʒ "about", 4÷d90v "shulde", cvdvx "neuer", ɔdʒ÷v "mutche").
- 0-shaped sign = d (0⊥⊥ "doo", 0⊥c "don", 0v "de", 0d6v "duke"). The worked check "Δ⊥⊥δ = good" fits (Δ⊥⊥ = "goo" in B7).
- T-shaped sign in running text = w (ϒ): Tv99 "well", Tx⊥ʒv "wrote", Ϯ7xv "were". (T also stands alone as [they].)
- ÷ is used for ch AND in sh/h positions: 4÷d90v "shulde", σ÷x-4ʒvc0⊥ɔɔv "chri(s)tendomme"; ɔdʒ÷v = "mutche".
- 9 serves for both l and q (ʒx7c9d-99-ʒv "tranquillite").
- ɬ (b-like) = b (ɬv "be", ɬvΔωcv "begynne", ɬdʒ "but", ɬd4-cv "busines", ɬdxΔ⊥ωcv "burgoyne");
  the same or a very similar small sign also stands alone where Tuke has "to"/"the".
- ı (short stroke) is used for [that], and also where Tuke has "to"/"the" (Tx⊥ʒv ı ‡ "wrote to your highnes",
  ɬω ı ɔ-997c⊥-4v "by the Myllanoyse"); I cannot separate two signs reliably at this resolution.
- ‡ = [your highnes] (not "the French King" as the table suggests): i ‡ "in your highnes", ∂ ‡ "of your highnes".
  The French King appears as ı ‡ with a distinct form in B4 ("that the Frenche king") -- uncertain which variant is which.
- ∂ used as [of] and also as the ss/s sign inside words (σ⊥ɔɔ-∂-⊥c "commission", ɬd4-cv∂ "busines").
- Code ʘ = [have/hath]; = [and]; ф = [wolde/wol]; ℮ = [if]; H = [he]; ∴ = [shal]; Ⱶ (4-like) = [the Emperor];
  S-like sign = [not]? (B5, Tuke "wol not deliver").

CONTINUOUS READING (modernized punctuation)
Top block: "... and that they would be high or low with your Highness as their matters do follow in France. This I cannot
write sure[?] your Highness for certain, but well I know they much doubt of your [peace, Tuke], and now begin to
devise that they have made a default, insomuch that if this marriage were to do, many men think how[?] it should
never be done, they reckoned upon things not likely to follow. In your Highness it is much now to be a stay to the
tranquillity of all Christendom, which much may be to your glory."
Bottom block: "I wrote to your Highness of request made by the Milanese and the ambassadors of Italy here, that the Emperor
would call home the Spaniards being about Milan. Now word is come that there hath been business, and many Spaniards
slain. The Prince of Orange hath written to the Emperor that the French King hath sent into Burgundy four hundred
men-of-arms and five or six thousand footmen. And Monsieur de Rieux[?] writeth that the Frenchmen will not deliver
Hesdin. I hear that Don Hugo de Moncada hath commission to take money for Burgundy; after he hath suppressed the
Moors in Valencia he shall go into France and from thence to Milan to entreat with the Duke there, if this new
business hinder not."

DIFFERENCES FROM TUKE (f. 246, image 4)
- Tuke "in somewhat that if": cipher 4⊥ɔdʒ÷v = "somutche" (insomuch that if).
- Tuke "iiij^C men of armes and v or vj^C fotemen": cipher spells "foure hundred ... fyue or sixe thousande footemen".
- Tuke writes a first line "it shulde neuer be" and strikes it through (the cipher has it once).
