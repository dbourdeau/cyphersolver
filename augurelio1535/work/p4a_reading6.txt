ENGLISH SUMMARY
The upper two thirds of the page (lines 01-23) now read continuously, and the sense is a
covering letter to the Dukes of Bavaria about an informant's news and about the letters
themselves. The writer speaks of something handed over ("uberantwort"), of what he has
learned from the man ("uon ime erfaren werden"); the man has said there is good fortification
("er habe guten bauw"); Your Princely Graces' letters ("eur F.G. brieuen") are mentioned,
and something seen, "auch zu d[em], dieweil jetzt männiglich mit" -- since everyone now
deals with the enemy, letters go back and forth between them ("feind ... brief einander"),
and no one is detained ("keinen der aufenthalt"). He therefore sends a separate copy
("sonder copeien"), explaining why, out of dutiful obligation ("aus schuldiger pflicht[ig]"),
and that no one else is told of it ("kein ander der hand kund[e]"). Then the clear Latin
insertion "Quid autem actum sit". He goes on: he is content with the outcome ("und es nach
guet"); he has shown himself [to be] of the high[born] prince['s party]; the Meister, on
behalf of Your Princely Graces, [reports] that despite everything they are agreed with the
Turk ("doch so uerein mit dem turcken"), and that against this they will have the peace
("dagegen den frid haben"); those related to Your Princely Graces would nevertheless
undertake it ("uerwant seind doch nemen thun") when the messenger has [come] ("wann der
poten hette"); and there is talk of these and other places ("diese der und orten").

The lower third (lines 24-31) is still largely unread. What can be made out is compatible
with the rest of the file and with the P4b slip: something is "uermaint" (supposed/claimed),
Your Princely Graces are to be told ("eur F.G. sagen"), there are causes/recruiting
("[u]rsachen ... werben") with "bei gedach[ten]", and near the end "der turcke zei[ten] hat
zu gewerben als ue[r]de" -- the Turk has at times to recruit. The last line is clear:
"[word sign] zeichen und was dort gehan[delt]" -- signs, and what is being negotiated there.

The names and figures the file is known for (Palota, Laszlo More, Ofen, Wien, Pressburg,
King Ferdinand and King John, the 50,000 Turks from Belgrade) are on P3 and on the P4b slip,
not on this page: P4a is the covering argument, not the news itself.
