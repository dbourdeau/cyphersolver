# R8358 — BL Harley MS 260 ff. 262r–262v (Burghley to Walsingham, Westminster, 5 July 1572)

Catalogue item 127. Session 2026-09-22.

Status: attempted, open. The letter is clear English and in print (Digges 1655); its cipher is a handful of
boxed name codes, one five-letter run and one monogram, and no key survives. One code is fixed by context.

## What the record is

DECODE R8358 ("William Burleigh to unknown recipient", Jul 1572, 2 pp., "Short parts encrypted only") is two
images of the fair-copy **letter-book of Walsingham's Paris embassy** (running head "July 1572"):

| image | folio | content |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | 262v | end of a letter signed "William Burleigh", "Westminster the 5th of July 1572"; then the address of the next, "To the right honorable and my very good Lord the Lord of Burleigh" (Walsingham's letter to Burghley, begins "Your Lordship's of the second and 5th of July sent by the Aunta servant I received the 8th ...", i.e. Walsingham → Burghley, ~13 July) |
| P2 | 262r | end of an earlier Burghley letter ("... I am sorry for them, and so would you also if you thought the suspicion to be true"), signed "William Burleigh", addressed "To his very friend Mr Frauncis Walsingham Esquire Ambassador Resident for the Queen's Majesty in Fraunce"; then the start of **Burghley → Walsingham, 5 July 1572** ("My Lord Ambassador, this day my Lord Admiral came to the Queen's speech ...") |

DECODE's P1/P2 are in reverse reading order: P2 (f. 262r) comes first and runs on to P1 (the verso, f. 262v). The
recipient is Walsingham; the author is Burghley.

The 5 July letter is printed in Dudley Digges, *The Compleat Ambassador* (London 1655), in the July 1572 section
(`burghley1572/lit/digges1655.txt` line ~17687 in the shared checkout), with the cipher left as figures. CSP
Foreign x (July 1572, BHO pp. 140–163) does not calendar it. So the letter was known; its cipher was never read.

## The cipher passages (transcription from the scans)

f. 262r–v, Burghley → Walsingham, 5 July 1572:

> I am sorry that the opinion groweth here of the French King's recoil from the Flanders enterprise, it
> breedeth coldness here. Some that are come home bear us in hand that **[3]** reneweth the **m o R. d A.**
> **[4]** whereby the last matter **⅌B** **[5]** is hindered. I marvel much thereof, indeed **[3]** is more given
> to Milan **[4]** then to **[2]**. And I am commanded to write to you hereof and to require some answer of your
> opinion ... I see no means by worldly means ~~A~~ **A** but Florence with **F.** **[2]**, God send it some way.

Digges prints: "[3 | reneweth the in o A. D. F. [4] whereby the last matter Bl5! is hindred ... indeed 3| is
more given to Millane 4] thentoſs| ... bur Florence in|; |".

Tokens (`transcription.txt`, measured): 15 — [3] ×2, [4] ×2, [2] ×2, [5], ⅌B, A, F, and the run m o R d A.

Walsingham's answer (Paris, 13 July 1572; Digges line ~17845): "To come to your letter of the fifth of this
month, I find the conjectures touching the [3] reneweth the in o A D F [7] altogether false and vain [Digges:
'Fain'], whereof I am assured of the contrary many ways". This confirms the phrase is a rumour about one party's
intentions, and that Walsingham, in Paris, could judge it: the party is at the French court.

## Reading

- **[3] = the French King (Charles IX)**, grade C. Both uses sit in a paragraph opened by "the French King's recoil
  from the Flanders enterprise"; the rumour is that [3] renews something that hinders "the last matter"; and
  Walsingham, at the French court, answers that he is assured of the contrary. (R8361's [3] = the Queen is a
  different letter, four months later; the book's copyist is not consistent with boxes, see r8361 NOTES.)
- "Milan [4]": a box after a word marks a cover-name (r8361 NOTES, key-rebuild), so [4] here and after "m o R d A"
  is probably the marker, and "Milan" a cover-name, perhaps for Spain; not graded.
- [2], [5], ⅌B, A, F and the run m o R d A: unread.

Cipher tokens read: 2 of 15 (13%).
