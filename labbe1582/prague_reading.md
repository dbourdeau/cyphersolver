# Desiderio l'Abbé to the duke of Nevers, Prague, 2 March 1577

Working reading of **BnF fr. 3198 no. 30**, beginning on fol. 62 (Gallica canvas f63) and ending on fol. 64 (canvas f66). Clear handwriting is readable throughout. Numerical cipher is inserted in short runs, most densely on canvas f65; no contemporary decipherment has been found in this item. This is a content reading from the source images, not a continuous diplomatic edition. The names and exact amounts below should be checked against the manuscript before quotation.

**23 September reassessment:** this remains a provisional content outline. No cipher text was recovered. See `REASSESSMENT.md` for the cipher-dispatch reference, corrected details, expanded inventory and unsuccessful tests.

## Page sequence and clear text

1. **f63, opening of fol. 62.** L'Abbé says he presented two of Nevers's letters on 27 February. He reports difficulty obtaining the imperial court's response after its return, his own fever and nineteen days confined to his room, and disruption caused by new troubles in France. He seeks resolution of Nevers's business and refers to money expected through the queen; he has not been able to obtain a free answer from one intermediary. Seigneur Piva has left Prague, and Prospero Gonzaga has arrived to pay his respects. The longest part of this page gives news of Stephen Báthory's contest with Danzig: L'Abbé doubts reports that the city will yield under the offered conditions, describes its defensive preparations, and says its merchants and trade face increasing pressure. A short numeric insertion follows a named correspondent's letter about Danzig.
2. **f64, first continuation.** He elaborates on Danzig, Báthory, and the Polish diet at Toruń. The opinion of several informants about Báthory is enciphered in short passages. The clear text says Báthory remained firm at Toruń with some 200 persons. It then describes the dispute over property of the collegiate church of Saint Lawrence in Toruń: the Catholic service was restored and Mass celebrated after a Senate response invoking the time of Sigismund and Stephen. The nuncio of the Pope and Count Krzysztof Rozdrażewski (?) are mentioned. L'Abbé says Báthory lacks money; Poles do not want to take arms against Danzig, arguing that Báthory had promised to confirm their privileges, and one important person's opinion on this is hidden by a numeric run. An ambassador from Báthory and a man from Transylvania have reached Prague and been heard by the emperor.
3. **f65, fol. 63, cipher-dense continuation.** The two envoys' representation to the emperor is reported with many numeric insertions alternating with clear syntax: they carry letters, ask for a hearing and a reply, and expect satisfaction from the emperor. Much of the sensitive description of the requested terms, Báthory's reaction, and the Polish estates is in cipher. The clear text then states that many Poles think Báthory will not live long, that individuals are pursuing their own advantage, and that a voivode has received an imperial pension of 500 talers a year on condition he act neither against the Habsburg house nor its interests. L'Abbé reports that the voivode hopes to return to his homeland after resolution of Polish affairs. Sborowski has left with 500 talers for his journey and 3,000 in assignments in Silesia; Łaski has not yet received money.
4. **f66, continuation and close.** A final numeric run precedes news that a French claimant or pensioner did not go to Poland, that a count named “Hierome Ras…” was reportedly going to Báthory instead of Breslau, and that the emperor's reported route through Lusatia, Silesia and Moravia is uncertain. He says the emperor is expected first at Vienna and Hungary, with court funerary obligations also in view. The emperor sought 200,000 talers each year for the stated years from the estates for defence of the Hungarian border and asked to extend ordinary contributions two years. L'Abbé reports the estates' resistance, especially their wish to know what would happen to their religion before granting more money. He notes a count's arrival from Spain and an Italian noble who will attend the empress's coronation; they lodge with the Spanish ambassador's circle. The prisoner Landolfi is waiting for safe conduct to Italy.

The final page (canvas f66, right leaf) is entirely clear. It reports word from Flanders that peace was proclaimed in Brussels on the 19th of the previous month, though some doubt it because soldiers were still being recruited. Two imperial ambassadors set out for Italy to profess obedience to the Pope: a prior of the Order of St John (name to recheck) and imperial counsellor Dr Tomer. L'Abbé says the Pope gave the empress 100,000 escus from the revenues of the archbishopric of Toledo; she sent a man to recover it, needing funds. The Swedish king's ambassador is still at court and intends to dine with Łaski. The ambassador has letters of recommendation from the empress to the king of Spain for recovering what she said was owed in Naples. It is said that Sweden and Báthory have formed a confederation. A returning gentleman from Constantinople has heard that peace between the emperor and the Turk would be confirmed and that the Turkish army is being readied against Persia. The dispatch is dated in Prague, 2 March 1577. A postscript says L'Abbé has sent papers through a courier, has not yet obtained the requested imperial council decision, and expects an answer the next day.

## Cryptanalytic status

The cipher is intercalated in otherwise clear French, using handwritten Arabic numerals, isolated figures, and marked figures. It is not decoded by the 1580 “Chifre avec Madame” in BnF fr. 3995 fol. 1: common 1577 groups include `02`, `03`, `04`, `13`, `35`, `43`, `45`, and a pointed `6`, whereas the later table assigns many of those positions to whole-word names or gives no alphabetic value. Its application produces no continuous French in the known grammatical contexts. The Breslau letter of 2 May (no. 37) and a near-verbatim copy (no. 38) repeat a shorter passage in the same-looking number hand; see `breslau_cipher_draft.md`. Whether the two letters use the exact same key remains unproved.
