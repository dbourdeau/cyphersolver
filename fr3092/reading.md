## f. 101r (no. 43, DECODE R3699) — the chancellor [Duprat] to the King, Calais, autumn 1521

On ne se devoit glorifier jusques a la fin, et que tant de choses restoient encor a faire entre vous et eulx et en
divers lieux que leur gloire pourroit retourner a honte et confusion, et que en telz actes le peu braver et bien
executer est a louer. Sur quoy nous dit que disions verite, et que souventefois on avoit veu advenir telle chose.

Nous avons mis peine de scavoir de divers lieux ce que le Cardinal avoit fait en Flandres, et trouvons que ce qu'il
nous a dit est veritable, et que sans point de faulte il s'en estoit venu fort mal content. Nous essaierons encores si
pourrons tirer de luy ou de son maistre quelque ayde par promesse ou autrement, et si cela se povoit faire serviroit de
beaucoup, comme entendez tro[p] mieulx. La chose qui nous sera plus contraire a l'obtenir sera l'estimation qu'il a
raporte di celuy ‹N2› [Empereur], et de sa tante, et puis la royne d'Angleterre.

Nous entretiendrons les afaires jusques au dixiesme de ce mois, ainsi qu'il vous a pleu nous mander, et garderons a
nostre povoir que riens ne se innovera contre vous.

L'evesque de Lin, en conduisant moy chancelier par la ville, entre autres propoz tumba sur le ‹N4›, et dit que c'estoit
ung merveilleux esprit, et que le Cardinal et ceulx du conseil d'Angleterre avoient sceu qu'il faisoit conduire par
autre main et a part d'avoir paix et amitie avec vous. Laquelle chose je ne deniay ne confessay, ains par admiration luy
demanday comment le scavoit. Il me dit : « Nous le scavons, et d'autres choses », et ne tira le propoz plus avant. Le
Cardinal ne nous en a parle ; peult estre le garde pour une autre fois.

**Translation.** [The English said] one should not glory before the end, and that so much remained to be done between
you and them, and in various places, that their glory might turn to shame and confusion, and that in such deeds little
boasting and good execution is to be praised. To which he told us we spoke the truth, and that such a thing had often
been seen to happen. We have taken pains to learn from several quarters what the Cardinal did in Flanders, and find
that what he told us is true, and that without fail he came away from it very ill content. We shall try again whether
we can draw from him or from his master some help, by promise or otherwise; if it could be done it would be of great
use, as you understand far better. What will most stand in our way is the esteem he has brought back of this
‹N2› [Emperor], and of his aunt, and then of the queen of England. We shall keep the business going until the tenth of
this month, as you were pleased to order us, and shall see to it as far as we can that nothing new is done against
you. The bishop of Lin[coln?], taking me the chancellor through the town, fell among other talk upon the ‹N4›, and said
he was a marvellous mind, and that the Cardinal and the council of England had learnt that he was having it
negotiated by another hand and apart to have peace and friendship with you. I neither denied nor confessed it, but in
surprise asked him how he knew it. He said "We know it, and other things", and took the talk no further. The Cardinal
has not spoken of it to us; perhaps he keeps it for another time.

## ff. 103r-104r (no. 44, DECODE R3700) — the commissioners [at Calais], autumn 1521

Ung serviteur du grenetier de Dieppe a este icy et a dit a plusieurs noz serviteurs que le duc d'Albanye secretement
estoit alle en Escosse, et estoit monte a Honnefleur, et que son maistre avoit fait advitailler et equiper son navire.
Des l'heure que l'avons sceu, l'avons envoye querir, mais estoit party. Monsieur du Tour a parle a ung autre serviteur
d'iceluy grenetier, qui luy a dit qu'il n'estoit rien plus vray, que l'esquippage et advitaillement avoit este fait et
par ses mains, et que s'il n'estoit party, est d'advis qu'il partira de brief.

Nous ne scavons si ce sont choses veritables ou controuvees, et si elles sont veritables, si c'est du consentement du
‹N3› [roy] ou a son deceu. Mais tant y a que s'il est vray et les Anglois le scavent, se declaireront contre le
‹N3› [roy], qui peult bien penser que le dommaige [de] celuy seroit plus la multiplication des ennemis que par ce moyen
aura. Lesquelz Anglois ne s'estoyent declairez, et combien que leur peuple y soit assez enclin, neautmoins ne
l'oseroyent entreprendre sans le ‹N3› [roy] et Cardinal, lesquelz sont encores pour dissimuler quelque temps pour
veoir et entendre quel chemin prendront les affaires du ‹N3› [roy] et de l'Empereur ; pendant lequel temps s'affaire
du ‹N3› [roy] eust peu prendre quelque fin, par paix, tresve ou guerre. La ou, si ses Anglois se declairent, ne fault
esperer ne paix ne tresve, ains la guerre, que sera plus grosse que n'est a present, tant pour la force de gens qu'ilz
gecteront sur terre en quelque endroit du royaulme que pour l'argent que pourront fournir a l'Empereur.

Ce que avons bien voullu escripre afin que, s'il n'estoit party, soit empesche, et s'il estoit party, soit envoye icy
quelque gentilhomme par devers ‹N1› [le roy d'Angleterre?] et Cardinal pour excuser ‹N3› [le roy] de ceste allee, si
on se veult ayder d'icelluy d'Albanie ; sera assez temps quant les autres ce seront declairez. L'anticipation
d'icelle declaracion peult porter beaucoup de dommaige au ‹N3› [roy], qu'est la principalle cause pour quoy est
expediee ceste presente poste.

**Translation.** A servant of the salt-warehouse keeper of Dieppe has been here and told several of our servants that
the duke of Albany had secretly gone to Scotland, having embarked at Honfleur, and that his master had had his ship
victualled and fitted out. As soon as we heard it we sent for him, but he had left. Monsieur du Tour spoke to another
servant of the same keeper, who told him nothing was truer, that the fitting-out and victualling had been done, and by
his own hands, and that if [the duke] had not left, he thinks he will leave shortly. We do not know whether these
things are true or invented, and if true, whether it is with the King's consent or without his knowledge. But if it
is true and the English know it, they will declare against the King, who can well consider that the harm of it would
be rather the multiplying of enemies than anything he will gain by this means. The English had not declared
themselves, and although their people are inclined enough to it, they would not dare undertake it without the King
and the Cardinal, who mean to dissemble a while longer to see what course the affairs of the King and the Emperor
take; in which time the King's business might have come to some end, by peace, truce or war. Whereas if the English
declare, there is no hope of peace or truce, but war, greater than now, both for the force of men they will land
somewhere in the kingdom and for the money they can furnish to the Emperor. We have thought well to write this so
that, if he has not left, he be stopped, and if he has, some gentleman be sent here to ‹N1› and the Cardinal to excuse
the King for this departure, if one means to make use of the said Albany; there will be time enough when the others
have declared. To anticipate that declaration may do the King much harm, which is the main reason this post is sent.

## f. 107r-v (no. 46, DECODE R3701) — [the chancellor, Duprat] to the King, Calais, autumn 1521

Apres disner le Cardinal a tenu long propoz avec le chancelier de Flandres seul a seul, et puis luy a donne conge, et
m'a retire en sa garderobe, ou avons demeure long temps tous seulz, et m'a monst[r]e gros semblant de familiarite, et
tout autre que n'avoit acoustume : fai[t] seoir aupres de luy, laver les mains ensemble. A quelle fin le fait, ne le
vous scavroie mander, mais tant y a que pour cela ne gangnera rien sur moy.

Il m'a tenu grand propoz de l'amour que son maistre et luy vous portent ; la grosse envye et malice que plusieurs
d'Angleterre ont conceu sur luy a cause de cela ; la grand peine qu'il a a entretenir le peuple d'Angleterre, qui est
superbe et dificile a domestiquer, et tout enclin a ceste maison de Flandres ; et que si ceste guerre dure, ce luy sera
une grosse peine, et a son maistre aussi, d'obvier au vouloir de leur noblesse et peuple ; mais que de sa part ne vous
failliroit jamais ; et plusieurs autres parolles, longues a escripre.

Et entre autres, que domp Prevost avoit dit au ‹N2› [Empereur] que luy aviez donne charge luy dire que vous ne
l'aimiez point et ne l'aimeriez jamais, et mectriez peine de le ruyner et destruire ; et que luy, Cardinal, estant en
Flandres, avoit fait mectre le dit domp Prevost hors le conseil du ‹N2› [Empereur], et qu'ilz le commencent fort a
congnoistre ; et que autres avoient raporte a iceluy ‹N2› [Empereur] que l'aviez apelle idiot, meseau, ignorant et peu
valant, et que moiennant ces parolles son peuple estoit enclin [a] vous faire la guerre, et tiroit de l'argent d'eulx
plus facilement que n'eust fait ; et que les Alemans, qui ne vivent que de proie de la guerre, estoient tousiours a ses
oreilles pour l'enflamber contre vous ; et qu'il le failloit envoier en Espaigne et l'oster d'icy, et que les
Espagnolz ne l'aimeroient jamais, et auroit tant affaire avec eulx que cela luy divertiroit son entendement ; et que si
vous faisiez une tresve avec luy, et ses gens estoient une fois separez, jamais ne les rassembleroit.

Sur quoy luy ay fait la response telle que autrefois vous ay escript, et l'ay persuade par tous les moiens dont me suis
peu adviser de encliner son maistre de vous assister. Il m'a dit que cela ne se pourroit encores faire.

**Translation.** After dinner the Cardinal talked a long while alone with the chancellor of Flanders, then dismissed
him and took me into his wardrobe, where we stayed a long time alone; he showed me great familiarity, quite unlike his
custom: made me sit beside him and wash hands together. To what end he does it I cannot tell you, but he will gain
nothing on me by it. He spoke at length of the love his master and he bear you; of the envy and malice many in England
have conceived against him on that account; of the trouble he has keeping the people of England in hand, who are proud
and hard to tame and wholly inclined to this house of Flanders; that if this war lasts it will be hard for him, and for
his master too, to withstand the will of their nobility and people; but that for his part he would never fail you; and
many other words, long to write. Among others: that Dom Prevost had told the Emperor that you had charged him to say
you did not love him and never would, and would take pains to ruin and destroy him; and that he, the Cardinal, while in
Flanders, had had the said Dom Prevost put out of the Emperor's council, and that they are beginning to know him well;
and that others had reported to the Emperor that you had called him an idiot, a leper, ignorant and worthless, and
that through these words his people were inclined to make war on you, and he drew money from them more easily than he
would have; and that the Germans, who live only by the spoils of war, were always at his ears to inflame him against
you; and that he should be sent to Spain and got away from here, for the Spaniards would never love him, and he would
have so much to do with them that it would turn his mind elsewhere; and that if you made a truce with him and his men
were once dispersed, he would never gather them again. To which I gave him the answer I wrote to you before, and urged
him by every means I could think of to incline his master to assist you. He told me that could not yet be done.
