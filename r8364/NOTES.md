# R8364: Burghley to Walsingham, February 1572/3 (BL Harley MS 260 f. 419; DECODE R8364; catalogue 122)

Status: read in part (22 Sept 2026). The six-sign place name is read as **(St) Valery** (Saint-Valery-sur-Somme):
five copies of the same word turn up in four letters of the 1572-73 correspondence, and a French letter of Dec 1572
among Walsingham's papers asks for a ship to be kept waiting at St Valery. The nine-sign run and the name codes stay
unread. The key is SP 106/2 f. 141A (TNA), and the sent original is in Cotton MS Vespasian F VI. Neither is digitised.

## What the record is

DECODE R8364 ("Unknown sender to ...", Feb 1572, 2 pp.) is two pages of **Walsingham's embassy letter-book**
(Harley MS 260, a later fair copy). It is not an original letter.

- f. 419r top: the end of Walsingham's own letter of "the vjth of February 1572" (Digges p. 326-327: his "miserable
  case", no money or credit). Below it is the address of the next letter, "To the right honorable Mr Fraunces
  Walsingham Esq, the Queenes Maiesties Amba[ssador] in Fraunce".
- f. 419r "Sir I have received yor lres of the xxth of this month": the letter that holds the cipher. It runs on to
  f. 419v, and the copy ends "doe knowe" with a struck-out "finis". Digges prints it from another copy (p. 327,
  running head "The Lord Burleigh to Fr. Walsingham") with more text after that point: Worcester, the French libel,
  and the letters of the 24th.

**Proposed DECODE metadata:**
- Sender: William Cecil, Lord Burghley.
- Receiver: Sir Francis Walsingham, ambassador in France.
- Place: London/court.
- Date: February 1572 in the letter-book's Old Style, i.e. **late January/February 1573** in modern reckoning. It
  answers Walsingham's letters of 20 Jan (the ticket "concerning [9]", i.e. R8363) and of the 24th. The dishonour done
  to Lord Worcester "on the seas before Boulogne" happened on his January 1573 journey to the royal christening.

## The cipher (`transcription.txt`)

- **[9]** (a boxed 9) occurs 5 times: "concerning [9]", "sent from [9]", "Glasgow [9]" (twice) and "the ruin of [9]".
  Digges sets it as |9| or |c|. Across the letter-book a boxed digit follows a cover-name (Florence, Rome, Lyons,
  Milan, Glasgow, all standing for persons; see `r8362/`, `r8361/`). Where it stands alone ("sent from [9]", "ruin of
  [9]") the copyist has put the marker in place of a name he did not copy. It carries no cipher.
- **3** occurs after the names Glasgow and Hall ("Glasgow 3", and "Hall 3" twice; the copyist once writes "Hall
  three"), and alone in "if 3 should so do". After a name it is the same kind of marker. Alone it is a name code,
  unread.
- **tt** (Digges ℵ): "a ship sent to Valery **tt** ymagined that Hall had a meaning…" This is a separate sign after
  the place name, standing for the person who imagined it. Unread.
- **II** (Digges |—|): "that II had gotten such knowledge". A name code, unread.
- A letter run of 6 signs, "a ship sent to **2 0 ‡ H 6 1**" (Digges "20 ǂ H Ꮾ )"), read **VALERY** (below).
- A letter run of 9 signs, "to enquire **^ 4 d H m 11 11 I J**" (Digges "x. 7. D. H. m. 11. ⊏. 𝔗. ᘓ."). Unread.

## Reading of the place name: (St) Valery

The earlier pass counted about 25 cipher signs in three runs and closed the letter as too short. This time the whole
of Digges 1655 was searched. Two page-by-page scans of all 457 page images found cipher on about 45 passages in the
1570-73 letters (`digges_corpus.tsv`). Among them the same six-sign word turns up in four letters, all about the ship
for the Archbishop of Glasgow's party:

| letter | context | Digges | Harley 260 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burghley, Westminster 11 Nov 1572 (p. 301) | "provide a passenger to attend at Harwich. Le the Port of" | 2 0 ǂ 6 ɔ Δ | |
| Walsingham, Paris Nov 1572 (p. 303) | "he desireth that the ship may stay at … 9 or 10 days" | 2 ɔ ǂ 6 r y | |
| Walsingham, Paris 20 Jan 1573 (p. 313, R8363) | "departed hence to … with intention to imbarque there" | 2 ) ⌘ Ꮾ ) 4 | 2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4 |
| Burghley, Feb 1573 (p. 327, R8364) | "a ship sent to …" | 20 ǂ H Ꮾ ) | 2 0 ‡ H 6 1 |

All five witnesses have six signs, with the same first sign (2) and the same third sign (ǂ/⌘/‡), and differing signs
in the other places. That is what a homophonic alphabet gives when the same word is written several times. The place
is a port in France where a ship waits for passengers bound for England (Harwich on the English side). Walsingham's
own file of 1572-73 correspondence, Cotton MS Vespasian F VI, holds as item 111 (f. 197) "A French letter; earnestly
desiring that a ship might be kept in waiting at St. Valery. Dec. 11" [1572] (BL catalogue, after Planta 1802). That
is the ship of the Walsingham letter at p. 303. **VALERY** has six letters, and Digges's Walsingham copy even ends
"… r y". Letter by letter, V = 2, A = 0/1/ɔ, L = ǂ/⌘/‡, E = 6/Ꮾ/ε/H, R = ɔ/)/9/r/6, Y = Δ/4/y/)/1. Digges's founts
and the letter-book copyist blur some signs (0/ɔ/), 1/9), so a few signs appear under two letters. The reading does not
depend on them.

Grade **M** (probable, not certain): the alphabet is not otherwise known, but the length, the repeated V and L, the
"ry" ending and the St Valery ship in Walsingham's file agree. Harwich, Dover, Calais and Dieppe are all written in
clear elsewhere in the letters. Dieppe (DIEPPE) also has six letters but needs different signs for its two Ps and two
Es in every copy and has no support in the sources. The earlier note in `walsingham1572/` (R8363, "2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4")
reads the same way.

With the place read, the passage runs: "by Davison's writing of a ship sent to [St] Valery, [tt] imagined that Hall
had a meaning to provoke Glasgow [9] to come hither; whereupon this last gentleman came to enquire of Hall some
assurance, if [3] should so do, to enquire [nine signs]." Glasgow is James Beaton, Archbishop of Glasgow, Mary Queen of
Scots' ambassador in Paris. The affair is the one in the letters of Nov 1572 to Feb 1573 (Steward, Davison, the
ship at the port, "Marcellis le Davison").

## Why the rest cannot be read here

- **The key survives but is not reachable.** Hsuan-Ying Tu's thesis (York 2012, p. 90 n. 99) cites *TNA SP 106/2
  f. 141A, "Sir Francis Walsingham Cipher, 1571"*, and uses the Harley 260 letters (ff. 55, 113, 170, 399) and the
  Cotton Vespasian originals as examples of it. DECODE has eight keys from SP 106/2 (R341-R354) but not f. 141A. The
  one closest in period, R343 (f. 50: England A, Dover I, Deepe, Calles, Earl of Murray, Cardinal of Châtillon), is
  the 1568-70 Norris-period key. Its homophones (e = ǂ 0 3 4 5, i = 2 3 4 5 Δ …) do not produce VALERY or any sense
  in the Digges runs. SP 106 is online only in State Papers Online (subscription).
- **The sent letter survives but is not digitised.** Cotton MS Vespasian F VI (BL; catalogue record
  040-001103461, "partly in cipher", no digitised content) holds Burghley's originals to Walsingham: f. 163, 14 Oct
  1572, "with many cyphers"; ff. 181-182, 3 and 7 Nov 1572; f. 245, "various intelligence and instructions",
  Somerset House 1572-3, probably this letter. It also holds Walsingham's drafts of his Nov 1572-Feb 1573 letters
  (ff. 172, 184, 198, 237, 247), which are in plain text. His secretaries may have interlined the originals, and the
  drafts give the plain text of his own cipher passages. Either would give the key.
- **Too short on its own.** The Burghley–Walsingham runs of 1572-73 in Digges give about 80 signs (the York run twice,
  the port five times, n279, n298, R8364). The pairs of Digges and Harley copies show that the printed signs are
  loose renderings of the manuscript ones. The nine-sign run shares only H (= E) with the port. Nine signs with one
  known letter cannot be fixed. Cribs tried: passport, safe conduct, "the Queene", "Lord Seton", "the Regent". None fit
  the one fixed letter and the run's shape.
- CSP Foreign x does not calendar this letter. Walsingham kept the letters he received, so Burghley's side is not in
  SP 70. Walsingham's own SP 70 letters of the same weeks (nos. 725, 728-729) are calendared without the cipher
  passages.

## Remaining gaps
- nine-sign run "^ 4 d H m 11 11 I J" (f. 419v l.2) - blocker: no-key-material; key SP 106/2 f. 141A (TNA) is not on DECODE or in print, the sent original (Cotton Vespasian F VI) is not digitised, and 9 signs with one known letter are too few
- name codes 3 (alone, f. 419v l.1), II (f. 419v l.7), tt (f. 419r l.8) - blocker: no-key-material; single occurrences of Burghley's person codes, no key or gloss in any copy
- [9] markers ×5 and 3 after names ×3 - blocker: no-key-material; cover-name markers, the copyist left out the names they stand for
