The letters
Four letters from Lord Cobham to Walsingham, May–June 1588, written while he was an English commissioner treating with Parma’s deputies at Ostend and then Bourbourg. They are clear English with passages in Greek-like signs.
Where
British Library, Harley MS 287 ff. 80–81, 88, 92–93 and 96–97 (DECODE R8490, R8492, R8495, R8496), catalogued with no decipherment. The key record the catalogue suggested (Bodley’s 1590 cipher) does not fit.
The cipher
A polyphonic sign cipher: one sign stands for s, t and a, another for r and y. Code numbers include 7 the Queen, 10 the Lord Admiral, 15 Parma and 16 the King of Spain.
How it was read
The key comes from Cobham’s own April letters in the same volume, deciphered between the lines at the time. About sixty glossed words aligned at full scan resolution give a sign inventory (signs.tsv). A solver (solve.py) matched cipher words against a word list from the Calendar of State Papers Foreign for 1588.
What it says
Cobham reports on the talks, a captain gone into Scotland, Italians arriving and the haven of Rain, in fragments such as “within France”. The graded decipherment is in targets/cobham1588/READING.md.
Still open
About a third of the cipher words are deciphered. The rest could not be resolved from the scans: the L-shape and K-shape signs on f. 88, the square on f. 80 and the codes 4, 22 and 27 are unglossed.

01 The letters

William Brooke, Lord Cobham, was one of Elizabeth’s commissioners sent to treat with Parma’s deputies in the spring of 1588. The talks moved from Ostend to Bourbourg while the Armada was fitting out. The Calendar of State Papers Foreign (vol. 21 pt 4) calendars his letters in SP 77, but not these Harley copies. No printed decipherment of them was found.

02 The key, from the glossed siblings

On f. 75 (Ostend, 9 April 1588; R8488) the decipherer wrote the plain text over each cipher word: “cardinal is coming”, “upon our arrival”, “willing to harken”, “by the clergy they made means”, “their great desir of”, “the Duke of Parma his bastard son”. 03 The sign inventory, and what is deciphered

A third pass worked from the scans at full resolution (7,200 pixels wide), aligning about sixty glossed words on ff. 75, 79 and 89 sign by sign. The result is targets/cobham1588/signs.tsv. The families that look alike at normal size separate cleanly. p is a loop with its tail swept down-left (“upon”, “popes”, “perswade”). l is a circle crossed by a straight stem (“arrival”, “holding”). h is a stem with a bowl to the right (“harken”, “thought”). m is a horizontal S with a loop (“made”, “commission”), and w a dot and stroke (“willing”, “sworne”). The glosses also show that several signs are polyphonic. Λ is s and t (and probably a), γ is r and y, c is o and once a, and the l sign once stands for t. The code numbers are glossed too: 7 = her Majesty, 10 = the Lord Admiral, 15 = the Duke of Parma, 16 = the King of Spain.

  • f. 80r: the word first taken for ARMADA (“as usuall in all armadas”) is treaties: its third sign is the 7-form e, not the m sign (re-checked 22 October 2026), so the passage reads “as usuall yn all treaties in [?numbers], that at the treaty of [?Breda]”. Also “hoping theym in the [?ex]pances”; a run with vertical word dividers ending “to these termes”; and “[22] is so forwarde yn the action that he promised Q[ueen]”. (M)
  • f. 88r: “15:” is the Duke of Parma. The first cipher run probably begins “[?captain] …” before a name, “with 40 men of […] have promised the haven of […]”. The rest turns on signs no gloss shows. (M)
  • f. 92r: “4 sent by […]” and “of words of [?letters]” (M); the September guess “the Portugals” is withdrawn.

04 What stays open

The sign inventory is now fixed, so the images are no longer the obstacle. Several signs are polyphonic and a few (an L-shape and a K-shape on f. 88, the square on f. 80, the codes 4, 22 and 27) are unglossed. The next step is to transcribe the runs sign by sign from the full-resolution crops, give each sign its set of possible letters from signs.tsv, enumerate each cipher word’s possible decryptions against a word list of the 1580s, and let the clear context choose.

05 The partial readings, letter by letter

On 22 October 2026 the clear text of ff. 80r–80v, 92r and 96v was transcribed as running English and every cipher run re-checked on the 7,200-pixel images (texts/R8490_reading.md and the others, with modern English). One sign was misread in September: the third sign of the f. 80r word taken for armada is the 7-form e, so the word is treaties, and the hostages passage now holds. The September guesses graded I are withdrawn. The cipher runs (in italics in the reading files) still give scattered words, so all four letters remain decoded, not read; […] marks an unread run, [?word] a word only partly carried.

  • f. 80r (5 May): “Touching hostages, yt ys honorable for [7] to […] their surety to these termes, as usuall yn all treaties in [?numbers], that at the treaty of [?Breda], which was the 20”; “[22] is so forwarde yn the action that he promised Q[ueen] to send to […] [27]”; f. 80v “[22] hath a desire to retourne from theym”.

  • f. 92r (27 May): the clear news of the Armada still at Lisbon and the Hamburg mariners at Sluys, then “4 sent by […] are […] […]”, “imprisoned in [?Bruges]”, “of words of [?letters]”.
  • f. 96v (9 June): “[?Within] [?France] […] […]”, “his [?servants] […] [?priests]”; f. 96r, wholly in clear, is not transcribed yet. f. 97r is blank.
