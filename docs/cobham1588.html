British Library, Harley MS 287 ff. 80–81, 88, 92–93 and 96–97 (DECODE R8490, R8492, R8495, R8496). Four letters from the English commissioner at the Bourbourg peace talks. The catalogue entry pointed to the wrong key record. The right key sits in Cobham’s own earlier letters in the same volume, where the cipher was deciphered between the lines at the time. Most of the alphabet was recovered from those glosses. Several signs are polyphonic, and scattered cipher words of the four letters decode; the four letters stay classed as decoded, not read. A second reader, Brice Dudout, transcribed every cipher word of the four letters sign by sign in November 2026: counting sure words only, 42 of their 166 cipher words come out (25%), far under the 95% bar. No decipherment of these four letters was found on the leaves or in print.

The letters
Four letters from Lord Cobham to Walsingham, May–June 1588, written while he was an English commissioner treating with Parma’s deputies at Ostend and then Bourbourg. They are clear English with passages in Greek-like signs.
Where
British Library, Harley MS 287 ff. 80–81, 88, 92–93 and 96–97 (DECODE R8490, R8492, R8495, R8496), catalogued with no decipherment. The key record the catalogue suggested (Bodley’s 1590 cipher) does not fit.
The cipher
A polyphonic sign cipher: one sign stands for s, t and a, another for r and y. Code numbers include 7 the Queen, 10 the Lord Admiral, 15 Parma and 16 the King of Spain; the gloss of f. 78r (DECODE R8489) adds 22 Sir James Croft, 26 Dr John Rogers, 27 Dr Valentine Dale and the square for the States (Brice Dudout, 9 November 2026).
How it was read
The key comes from Cobham’s own April letters in the same volume, deciphered between the lines at the time. About sixty glossed words aligned at full scan resolution give a sign inventory (signs.tsv). A solver (solve.py) matched cipher words against a word list from the Calendar of State Papers Foreign for 1588. On 9 November 2026 Brice Dudout, with an AI model, learnt an alphabet on the glossed words of f. 78r alone and tested it once on every glossed cipher word of f. 78v (349 letters): 0.917 of characters agree, against at best 0.284 for 200 shuffled keys.
What it says
Cobham reports on the talks, a captain gone into Scotland, Italians [?arriving] and the haven of […] (the September guess “Rain” is withdrawn), in fragments such as “within France”. In R8490, Sir James Croft (22) promised the Queen to send Dr Dale (27) to Bruges, and wishes to return. The November pass on the other three letters adds probable words: men imprisoned at Bruges, an ensign of Nieuport (“ancient”, a restored word), a servant priest called John, someone who has “written into England”. The graded decipherment is in targets/cobham1588/READING.md.
Still open
Counting sure words only, 21 of 43 cipher words come out in R8490, 7 of 55 in R8492, 7 of 38 in R8495 and 7 of 30 in R8496 (42 of 166, 25%); Brice Dudout’s own figures, which add the probable words, are 81%, 36%, 32% and 60%. The values of his derived key were not measured against a control. The K-shape and some other signs on f. 88, the code 4 and a dotted V seen once on f. 88 are unglossed; codes 22 and 27 and the square are glossed on f. 78r (the L-shape was fixed as e from “haven” in solver loop 1).

01 The letters

William Brooke, Lord Cobham, was one of Elizabeth’s commissioners sent to treat with Parma’s deputies in the spring of 1588. The talks moved from Ostend to Bourbourg while the Armada was fitting out. The Calendar of State Papers Foreign (vol. 21 pt 4) calendars his letters in SP 77, but not these Harley copies. No printed decipherment of them was found.

03 The sign inventory, and what is deciphered

A third pass worked from the scans at full resolution (7,200 pixels wide), aligning about sixty glossed words on ff. 75, 79 and 89 sign by sign. The result is targets/cobham1588/signs.tsv. The families that look alike at normal size separate cleanly. p is a loop with its tail swept down-left (“upon”, “popes”, “perswade”). l is a circle crossed by a straight stem (“arrival”, “holding”). h is a stem with a bowl to the right (“harken”, “thought”). m is a horizontal S with a loop (“made”, “commission”), and w a dot and stroke (“willing”, “sworne”). The glosses also show that several signs are polyphonic. Λ is s and t (and probably a), γ is r and y, c is o and once a, and the l sign once stands for t. The code numbers are glossed too: 7 = her Majesty, 10 = the Lord Admiral, 15 = the Duke of Parma, 16 = the King of Spain.

  • f. 80r: the word first taken for ARMADA (“as usuall in all armadas”) is treaties: its third sign is the 7-form e, not the m sign (re-checked 22 October 2026), so the passage reads “as usuall yn all treaties in [?numbers], that at the treaty of [?Breda]”. Also “hoping theym in the [?ex]pances”; a run with vertical word dividers ending “to these termes”; and “[22] is so forwarde yn the action that he promised Q[ueen]”. (M)
  • f. 88r: “15:” is the Duke of Parma. The first cipher run probably begins “[?captain] …” before a name, “with 40 men of […] have promised the haven of […]”. The rest turns on signs no gloss shows. (M)
  • f. 92r: “4 sent by […]” and “of words of [?letters]” (M); the September guess “the Portugals” is withdrawn.

04 What stays open

The sign inventory is now fixed, so the images are no longer the obstacle. Several signs are polyphonic and a few (a K-shape and other signs on f. 88, the code 4) are unglossed; the square on f. 80 and the codes 22 and 27, unglossed in the siblings used until then, are glossed on f. 78r of R8489 (the States, Sir James Croft, Dr Dale; Brice Dudout, 9 November 2026); the L-shape was fixed as e from “haven” in solver loop 1. Every cipher word of the four letters has now been transcribed sign by sign from the full-resolution images (R8490 on 9 November, the other three on 13 November 2026, by Brice Dudout). What remains is the signs that no gloss shows and the choice among the values of the polyphonic signs, which the clear context settles only for some words. In R8492 the two readers also read four places of the clear text differently (lines 3, 7, 8 and 21); the DECODE images need a login and could not be re-opened to settle them, so both readings are kept (texts/R8492_reading.md, last section).

05 The partial readings, letter by letter

On 22 October 2026 the clear text of ff. 80r–80v, 92r and 96v was transcribed as running English and every cipher run re-checked on the 7,200-pixel images (texts/R8490_reading.md and the others, with modern English). One sign was misread in September: the third sign of the f. 80r word taken for armada is the 7-form e, so the word is treaties, and the hostages passage now holds. The September guesses graded I are withdrawn. The cipher runs (in italics in the reading files) still give scattered words, so all four letters remain decoded, not read; […] marks an unread run, [?word] a word only partly carried.

  • f. 80r (5 May): “Touching hostages, yt ys honorable for [7] to […] their surety to these termes, as usuall yn all treaties in [?numbers], that at the treaty of [?Breda], which was the 20”; “[22] is so forwarde yn the action that he promised Q[ueen] to send to […] [27]”; f. 80v “[22] hath a desire to retourne from theym”.
  • f. 88r: “There ys captayne Mongomery of latt gone into Schotland. [?captain] […] with 40 men of […] have promised the haven of […] […] yn […] there to [15]”; “Italians [?arriving] […] [?Naples] Antonio”.
  • f. 92r (27 May): the clear news of the Armada still at Lisbon and the Hamburg mariners at Sluys, then “4 sent by […] are […] […]”, “imprisoned in [?Bruges]”, “of words of [?letters]”.
  • f. 96v (9 June): “[?Within] [?France] […] […]”, “his [?servants] […] [?priests]”; f. 96r, wholly in clear, is not transcribed yet. f. 97r is blank.
  • Key test and second pass on R8490, 9 November 2026 (Brice Dudout, with an AI model): an alphabet learnt only on the glossed cipher words of f. 78r (DECODE R8489) was tested once on every glossed cipher word of f. 78v (76 words, 349 letters), with the criterion fixed in advance: 0.917 of characters against at best 0.284 for 200 shuffled keys. The errors are all on the shared signs (caret s/t, gamma r/y) and d = o. One declared deviation: the f. 78v crops showed the glosses while the held-out cipher was transcribed (the key was already frozen). The f. 78r gloss names 22 = Sir James Croft, 27 = Dr Valentine Dale, 26 = Dr John Rogers and the square = “ye States”. Every cipher word of R8490 ff. 80r–80v was then transcribed and hashed before decoding, and decoded with the frozen f. 78r key and a declared derived key: 21 of the 43 cipher words come out sure, 14 more probable and 8 stay unresolved, under the 95% bar. New: Croft promised the Queen “to send to Bruges [27: Dr Dale]”; “yt will not be kept”; “treat of Breda”; “subiect”. The derived values were not measured against a control, and the class of the four letters is unchanged. See dudout/reading_R8490.md.
  • Third pass on R8492, R8495 and R8496, 13 November 2026 (Brice Dudout, with an AI model): every cipher word of f. 88r, f. 92r and f. 96v was transcribed sign by sign twice, the second pass made without looking at the first, arbitrated on the images and hashed before decoding, then decoded with the same frozen f. 78r key plus declared derived values (dudout/reading_R8492.md, reading_R8495.md, reading_R8496.md). By the contributor’s record the protocol was fixed before any transcription; the hashes match the files, but there is no outside timestamp. Sure words: 7 of 55 (R8492), 7 of 38 (R8495), 7 of 30 (R8496); the rest are probable, restored or unresolved. The probable words include, on f. 88r, “40 men of account”, “Italian starving”, “intelligence”, “in France” and, with a restored verb, a promise to deliver “the town” to Parma (15) (line 7, one of the four places of the clear text the two readers read differently); on f. 92r, men “imprisoned at Bridges” (Bruges, twice) and an “ancient” (restored) of Newport; on f. 96v, “his servant Ihon preist”, a “treater” and “written into England”. “The Earle of [Westmorland]” on f. 96v is a restoration with three signs changed and is weak. Where the two passes meet the October readings (the haven, Italians, Antonio, Bruges, Newport, France, servant, priest), the agreement is only partly independent: the contributor had seen the project’s notes and its sign transcriptions of the runs of ff. 80–92 (runs.txt) before transcribing (by his own declaration in the protocol). The class of the four letters is unchanged. The raw decodes were reproduced with a second decoder; the transcriptions could not be compared with the images, which need a DECODE login.

4. Codes: 22 = Sir James Croft; 27 = Dr Valentine Dale; 15 = the Duke of Parma; ◻ = the States (glosses on R8489 f. 78r); 7 = the Queen (gloss on f. 79, Daniel).
5. Classes: *sure* = every sign has a frozen value or a strength-A derived value and the word makes sense; *probable* {x} = one sign of strength B/C, unexplained or missing, sense clear; *restored* [x] = word supplied by sense (none); *unresolved* ‹…› = no word that makes sense.

Polyvalent signs, decided word by word: Λ (A) = t in *the, not, kept, to, ther, these, treat(s), subiect, action, brother, retorn*, = s in *is, hostages, surti, send, Bruges, desire, restrain*; one broad caret = a (*treats*, C). γ (Y) = r in *ther* ("their" in Cobham's spelling). d = o everywhere (*of, to, openning, brother*; strength A). The letter d is written ∞ or a small closed o (O: *forwarde, send, Breda*), a sign distinct from d and c at 1:1.
