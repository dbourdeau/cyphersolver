R4306: passports, rival envoys and an agent’s watch

Go to the full text ↓

An alphabetical number cipher yields a Dutch report about passports, rival envoys and surveillance, with four unidentified codes and several uncertain readings retained.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 18 October 2026 · updated 20 October 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: partial (99% of the cipher)

Contributed by George Lasry (solution) and Brett Burford (verification)

01 Cryptanalysis and visual key

No interlinear or marginal decipherment is visible on the supplied page. The key is a monoalphabetic substitution with nulls and an unresolved nomenclator. Values 30–52 form a 23-position alphabet: 21 occur, while 45=q and 51=x are predictions. There is one value per letter, with no homophones. Fifteen null classes occur; null 13 is listed but unobserved. The source key’s “24-letter” header is a counting error.

The 35 CTTS rows contain 463 selections: 422 letter occurrences, 34 nulls, five unresolved-code occurrences, and two date numerals. Thus there are 461 cipher elements and 40 observed cipher types. The CTTS date labels 285 and d13 represent 23 and 13; they are excluded from the cipher count. Assignment coverage is 422/427 non-null cipher occurrences (98.83%), which does not establish semantic accuracy.

02 The Dutch reading and translation

The following two texts are preserved as supplied, including their uncertainty marks. The Dutch is an edited, normalized reading, and the English includes interpretive expansions.

Decrypted text (edited, normalized)

Alles wt den uwen verstaen. Ic sal mondelinge [gekl…men?] antwoorden. Als o[o]c des saecken kan seer grooten gewichte syn ende metbrengen, antwoort op U.L. [v]ersoeck met paspoorten hier. Begint ialousye tussen [106] en [114] [105] gesanten; syn beyde van hier vertrocken. Ic weet [113] syn gantsen voornemen, wat hy inden sin heeft. Wel, den heb ic ooc ga[d]e gegeuen. Hy is naden [114]. Ic rust nacht noch dacht, sy dat ic sieck ben oft niet, en schouw geen peryckel. U.L. twee breuen ontfangen vanden 23 Febr. en 13 Mar[t]y, om een noodige saecke. Blyf ic noch wat, die importeert.

Translation

I have understood everything in yours. I shall answer [it] orally [when I come?]. Since these matters may be of very great weight and bring great consequences: [here is my] answer to Your Honour’s request concerning passports here. Jealousy is beginning between the [106] and the [114] [105] envoys; both have left here. I know [113]’s whole intention, what he has in mind. Well, I have also kept a close watch on him. He has gone to the [114]. I rest neither night nor day, whether I am ill or not, and shrink from no danger. I have received Your Honour’s two letters of 23 February and 13 March, about a necessary matter. I am staying on here a while longer, which matters.

Contributor’s translation notes

  • U.L.: Uwe Liefde, explained by the contributor as a conventional address, roughly “Your Honour” or “dear Sir”.
  • gade gegeven: interpreted as “kept watch on” or “observed”.
  • Codes: 106, 114 and 105 suggest rulers, states or persons; “114 105 gesanten” and movement to 114 may point to a sovereign or court. These are contextual possibilities, not identified key values. Code 113 is also unresolved.
  • Last sentence: “Blyf ic noch wat” is the contributor’s least certain reading.

04 Historical context and research

Contributor’s interpretation: the writer appears to be an agent or informant reporting to a superior. Passports, rivalry between envoys and their departure fit diplomatic activity, possibly a congress or mediation. The Swedish collection suggests a possible Swedish connection, and the references to 23 February and 13 March suggest writing later than the cited March letter. Late March or April is a working suggestion, not a recovered date.

What was checked: the public DECODE record gives only the broad range 1600–1699, leaves the correspondents and languages blank, and still carries an unresolved catalogue status. Its general collection note concerns Oxenstierna correspondence, not an attribution of this specific letter. The supplied scan visibly carries a pencilled “1632?”. Neither this annotation nor archive custody proves the year or recipient. The earlier survey reports an Amsterdam address on another view; that view was checked on 18 October 2026 and does read Amsterdam (section 05).

The earlier group survey records unsuccessful attacks on R4306 and tests of keys R4296/R4310. Note added 18 October 2026: that attempt (21 September 2026: simple and homophonic annealing over quadgram models in ten languages, Dutch among them, on two transcriptions; box keys R4310 and R4296 rejected) had its own entry from 14 October, which is now folded into this one; its transcriptions are kept in targets/sweden4306/project_attempt/. No complete earlier plaintext was located in a limited opening-phrase search. No exhaustive publication search or first-publication claim is made. The identities of the four codes and the correspondents remain open.

05 Method, verification and remaining gaps

The contributor confirms the workflow: CTTS transcription, then Opus 5.5 solution. The supplied key describes crib dragging and an ordered number alphabet. Codex’s digit-box merging preceded the final snapshot; the source key records subsequent corrections. The CTTS position-label histogram matches all 463 transcription selections exactly. The five-line excerpt was recomputed directly from the submitted key and checked against the image.

The full edited reading remains distinct from mechanical output. Four codes, doubtful words, the final sentence, historical identities and dating remain unresolved. No new code identities or a full mechanical decryption are supplied.

Escalation, 18 October 2026 (proposals for George Lasry’s review)

Claude worked the open pieces after publication, on DECODE images. George’s key, reading and translation are unchanged.

  • The other two views. The address leaf reads Aen Mr. ?ilick (Wilick?) … Amsterdam, which confirms the Amsterdam address; the wrapper is II:104, “1600– Chifferbref till … i Amsterdam”.
  • A cipher note on the address leaf, three lines in the same numerals, partly torn: it contains code 113 again and a fifth code, 101. Under the letter’s key it gives ler., … hlden / [113] naeldan / kn [101] na..mk, which is not Dutch; too short and damaged to fit a variant key.
  • Every key in the box. The archivists’ wrappers of the Chifferklaver keys give each key’s a values; none of the 43 labelled keys has a = 30, and the unlabelled keys and the never-fetched R4319–R4321, opened page by page, have no alphabet a=30 … y=52. A faint list on R4306’s wrapper (Tz Rex Polon., Vz Victoria …) is a ghost of the neighbouring Brandenstein key R4305, not this letter’s nomenclator.
  • The template. R4319 (Jacob du Moulin, 1650) is built the same way at another offset: a sequential alphabet (a = 20), then an alphabetical nomenclator from 101 (101 Archangel … 112 Ambassadeur, 113 Amsterdam). If R4306’s key followed it, codes 101–114 would be words beginning with A or B. A proposal only; five occurrences cannot test it.
  • Doubtful words. The scribe writes 0 as a tailed o, so 30/38 and 40/43 are ambiguous on the only image (ekll / men, Mar…y).

Remaining gaps and their outside blockers: the codes (no key material on DECODE), the doubtful words (illegible digit forms), the address-leaf note (too short), year and sender (only Riksarkivet provenance records could settle them).

# R4306 transcription (Riksarkivet, "1632?"; archive folder cover reads "1600- Chifferbref ... i Amsterdam", II:104)
# Digit notes (this hand): 3 = open "z"-like with top hook, often with long lead-in stroke (30/33 start like a "Z");
#  4 = closed "q"/"4" with short tail staying on line; 9 = "g"-shaped with long descender below line (49, 39);

#  2 = "z" with long trailing horizontal (42, 32, 52); 6 = tall looped ascender; 5 = "S/ʃ"-shaped.

# R4306, the cipher note on the address leaf (image I25833): this project's finding and Brett Burford's reading

Not part of George Lasry's reading (`reading.txt`), which covers the one cipher page. Copied verbatim from NOTES.md,
"Findings of 18 October 2026 (proposals for George Lasry's review)" (22 Oct 2026). Both readings are proposals.

- **A cipher note on the address leaf, not counted before.** Three lines in the right margin, in the same numerals,
  part of line 1 torn away: `40:34:46. , [torn] 37:40:33:34:42` / `113:42:30:34:40:33.30:11:42:` /
  `39:42: 101. 42:30:27:11:41:39`. With the letter's key: *ler., … hlden / [113] naeldan / kn [101] na..mk*. That is not
  Dutch. Caesar offsets of the alphabet and the R4287 sequential key fail too. It adds a fifth code value, **101**,
  and a second occurrence of **113**.
  **21 Oct 2026, Brett Burford (issue #47):** read the scribe's tailed *o* as 8, as this page's own doubtful words
  already allow (30/38, 40/43), and the note turns Dutch: line 1 `40:34:46` *ter* (40 -> 48), line 2
  `42:30:34:40:33.30:11:42` *niet dan* (42 38 34 48 33 30, 11 a null), line 3 `39:42: 101. 42:30` *in [101] na...*
  (39 -> 38). A hole loses two or three groups at the start. It continues the letter after *importeert*. Proposal: each
  digit still to be checked on image I25833 (the DECODE cookie had expired when this was written).
