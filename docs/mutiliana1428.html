An Italian letter with runs of cipher, deciphered with a key taken from the faint decipherment written over it at the time.

The letter
“Famulus et servulus Ricc. de Mutiliana” (Modigliana) to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, lord of Mantua, Forlì, 6 November 1428. Italian, clear text with runs of cipher.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Mantova, Archivio Gonzaga. DECODE R1854, image 3; the record was catalogued as one letter of unknown sender.
The cipher
A homophonic substitution in graphic signs with nulls and three name signs: the lord Malatesta, the marquess’s sons, the pope.
How it was deciphered
A contemporary hand wrote the plaintext letter above each graphic sign; about two-thirds of that gloss is legible. Thirty values of signs taken from it, and a few rare signs from context, decipher every run from its signs.
What it says
The governor of Forlì secretly favours the marquess’s sons; with the pope, the lord Malatesta and the count of Urbino, the lord of Mantua could become “una grande signore in pocho tempo”.
Still open
Two unglossed signs and about 25 signs in doubtful words.

This letter is image 3 of DECODE R1854, which holds four letters to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga in four ciphers. The others have their own pages: a Gonzaga agent in Rome, 9 August 1428, Francesco de’ Cattanei, February 1430 and Pandolfo Malatesta, 22 April 1428 (not solved). They were written up together until 14 October 2026.

01 Forlì, 6 November 1428: the Malatesta, Urbino and the pope

This letter is in Italian, clear text interrupted by cipher runs. Over every sign a contemporary hand has written the plaintext letter in small faint script, and three word signs are glossed as names: el signor Malatesta, li fioli del signor marchese and el papa. About two-thirds of the gloss is legible. Thirty values of signs attested in it (each two to fifty-six times), plus a handful of rare signs fixed from context, decipher all 894 signs:

… a ciascuno pare che questo acto sia molto dannoso a le signor Malatesta et cusì, perché [el] dà ad intendare a molti che el papa fia cum loro. Se la S.V. havesse da papa fare questa impresa insieme cum lo signor Malatesta [e] conte d’Urbino, la S.V. se faria una grande signore in pocho tempo. Bene che el signor Malatesta è riputato così povaro, molti se copriano per lui …

The informant reports that the governor secretly favours the marquess’s sons, and that Guidantonio da Montefeltro, count of Urbino, is in secret contact with Giovanni de Ramberto, which has aroused suspicion. Full text: reading_p3.md.

02 Method

One pass transcribed the gloss (p3_gloss.md), a second the signs (p3_signs.txt). The key comes from the gloss, and every run was deciphered from its signs rather than from the faint gloss, so the legible gloss checks the reading. A test against chance and overfitting with an Italian letter model (docs/_validate.py) passes.

03 What remains uncertain

  • 869 of 894 signs read as sense, re-counted 5 October 2026.
  • Two unglossed signs (one of them twice) and about 25 signs in doubtful words (“de Ianus(?) ubi(?)”, “dice cavad(?)”).
  • The writer’s first name is abbreviated, “Ricc.”.
