London → the Carlist Secretary of State · letter substitution with a few digit groups · 8 September 1837 · tables recovered from the cipher by a contributor, deciphered in part

Manuel Azuares to the Carlist Secretary of State, London, 8 September 1837

Go to the full text ↓

A Carlist agent in London hides parts of a report on Portugal in letter cipher; a contributor recovered the tables from the cipher, validated one of them, and deciphered about four fifths of the words.

Under rules Brice Dudout fixed in advance, his AI agents recovered letter tables from the cipher by annealing on Spanish. Five passage tables were measured on held-out lines: one, the table T6 of pages 6 and 7, passed (0.882 against 0.508 for the best of 1,000 shuffled tables) and four did not; an earlier test of the tables of pages 3 to 5 was negative. 01 The letter

The letter is in the Archivo Histórico Nacional in Madrid, DIVERSOS-COLECCIONES,161,N.2 (PARES 3735838): no. 780, marked “Duplicado”, written from London on 8 September 1837 by Manuel Azuares, a Carlist agent, to the First Secretary of State of the pretender Carlos V. It has 12 images. Most of it is clear Spanish. On views 03 to 12 runs of cipher written in letters stand inside the clear sentences, with a few groups of digits. PARES describes the letter as partly in cipher and gives no decipherment, and none is written on the leaves.

The letter entered this project’s catalogue as entry 2421 on 9 November 2026, from a sweep of PARES. The contributor, Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée, with Claude Opus 5.5 agents), worked on it the same day. He checked this repository for earlier work and found none; the catalogue’s vetting had found no edition, and no DECODE record or key for Carlist agents in the repository’s index (the live DECODE database needs a login and was not queried).

No image is shown on this page. The images are online on PARES without login, but the contributor did not find the reproduction licence on the record and included no image, crop or thumbnail; the project has not reproduced any. A line of the cipher is printed as letters in the strip under the title.

02 The cipher and its tables

The cipher replaces each letter of the Spanish by another letter, in the same cursive hand as the clear text. The transcription keeps apart a long s (ſ), a round r shaped like z, and ll and ñ as single units. One unit (written e or c) stands for u or b. The transcription file holds 1,741 cipher units, counted here, and the six lines of passage P112 (11.04–09) are only in the frozen transcription of the test; the profile’s figure of about 1,500 letters was not measured.

On the contributor’s model the table changes inside the letter, with one table for each group of pages, not one for each run: K3 for page 3, K5 for pages 4 and 5, T6 for pages 6 and 7, T8 for page 8 and T9 for pages 9 to 12. Each change is announced by a group that starts with x followed by l, z, g or h (xgzl, xzlg, xlgz, xlzh, xlzgh). He takes these for indicators, “probably” in his notes; they are not resolved. Five groups of digits (4607229, 5071574925, 5344727605, 507, 403710131) are not resolved either, and no nomenclator was found.

The contributor ran two pre-registered protocols, with the transcription, the tables and the measuring script frozen by hash before any held-out line was decoded.

  • First protocol: negative. Tables K3 and K5 were learnt on views 03 and 05 and measured once on the cipher of view 12, set aside beforehand: 0.198 against 0.198 for the best of 200 shuffled tables. The contributor concludes that view 12 is under another table.
  • Amendment 01. New passages of views 04 and 06 to 11 were transcribed before any decoding. Five passages of at least 100 units were each cut in two: a table was learnt on the first half only (annealing on Spanish 4-grams, with hand edits on that half) and frozen, then measured once on the second half. The measure is the share of held-out letters covered by words of a Spanish lexicon. The reference is the best of all 1,000 shuffled tables (200 for each passage), 0.508, to correct for making five comparisons. A table counts as validated at 0.60 or more with a margin of 0.30 or more.

So only the P063 table, called T6, is verified. Before decoding, the amendment itself noted that identical cipher strings recur from one passage to another, so that the model of several tables may be wrong (a single table with homophones or reading errors was not excluded); the protocol was applied all the same. Applied unchanged, it also gives Spanish on the rest of pages 6 and 7 (the passage P062, line 06.05 and view 07). The contributor counts the words it gives letter for letter as certain, and declares that this is not a further measurement. The four negative passages have short held-out halves, 51 to 71 units, against a high corrected reference; their readings stay probable. Views 03 and 05 were wholly decoded during the first attempt and cannot be measured. The profile records the validation as weak overall: a pass for T6 and a fail for the four other passages.

These are the contributor’s tests, re-run by him on 9 November 2026 with identical figures. The project did not repeat the tests and did not check the transcription or the reading against the images; its catalogue vetting had viewed four of the twelve images, only to confirm the cipher.

Certain and probable words together are 285 of 361, or 78.9%, with the restored words left out; with them the share is 352 of 361 (97.5%). The five indicator groups and the five digit groups are not counted as words. Because 78.9% is under the 95% bar and four tables are not verified, the letter is deciphered in part.

The passages under the verified table, in the contributor’s reading. Clear words of the letter are in angle brackets, restored letters in square brackets, [x…] is an indicator group and # marks a group of digits.

⟨y siendo⟩ [x…] Globet [q]uien dirige al príncipe [F]ernando ⟨que es un joven lleno de carácter … no dudo que él sabrá⟩ establecer en a[q]uel reyno un gobierno adaptado [a las] circunst[an]cias, monárquico y aumentado con las institu[cio]nes [a]ntiguas gratas al pueblo portugués.

⟨La Inglaterra conoce esta necesidad … el Gobierno de Lisboa⟩ se apresure a tratar con el rey don Carlos bajo [bases] mutuas de seguridad y de conveniencia política #5344727605 la [ll]egada de su Magestad a Madrid dará sin duda prin[c]ipio a este paso [q]ue la corte de Lisboa desea.

In the contributor’s translation, with the marks left out here: “and as it is Globet who guides Prince Ferdinand, who is a young man full of character, I do not doubt that he will know how to establish in that kingdom a government adapted to the circumstances, monarchical and enlarged with the ancient institutions dear to the Portuguese people. Britain knows this need … the Lisbon government should hasten to treat with King Don Carlos on mutual bases of security and political convenience #5344727605; the arrival of His Majesty in Madrid will no doubt begin this step, which the court of Lisbon desires.”

The rest, under the unverified tables, is probable. On page 3 Azuares names “el encargado de negocios de Austria en esta capital”, whom he saw the day before. On page 5 the Austrian’s language makes him suppose that a change is being prepared in the Portuguese system of government, from which the Powers hope to secure order in that country “aunque sea sacrificando los derechos de S.M. Fidelísima el rey don Miguel primero”. On pages 7 and 8 he gives his own view: to recognise Doña María de la Gloria would be to recognise the usurpation, and the Portuguese people are in general attached to Dom Miguel; the lines then speak of the consolidation of her throne and of guarantees of a lasting peace, which the contributor’s summary takes as a denial that such a settlement would secure one (the negation is not in the lines deciphered). The plan he reports on pages 9 and 10 is to declare her absolute queen, summon the ancient Portuguese Cortes and negotiate with Don Carlos’s government on conventions of mutual security. Britain, he writes on page 11, undertook to prepare what has taken place in Lisbon. The general is written “Globet” throughout; the contributor identifies him as the Belgian general Goblet d’Alviella, sent to Lisbon in 1837. (This identification is the contributor’s and was not checked here.) The whole reading and its English translation are in the full texts below.

Before publication the contributor reviewed his first draft on 9 November 2026. He removed words that are not in the cipher (“de Lisboa”, “del Norte”, “se”), noted that “se trata de” and “existe en” are clear text, moved doubtful words to unresolved, and limited the certain class to the verified table.

04 What remains uncertain

The contributor grades words as certain, probable, restored or unresolved; the grades are his.

  • Four tables are not verified (K3, K5, T8, T9): their 215 words are probable only. The passages of T8 and T9 failed on held-out halves of 51 to 71 units, short against the corrected reference. K3 and K5 were never measured on their own pages: views 03 and 05 were wholly decoded in the first attempt, and the one measurement, on view 12, was negative. The contributor’s notes say that fresh cipher under the same tables (other Azuares letters in volume 161) or a second human reader would settle them.
  • Five indicator groups and five digit groups (4607229, 5071574925, 5344727605, 507, 403710131) are open: each occurs once and no nomenclator was found.
  • Line 04.02 (18 units) and nine unresolved words, among them {piensa?} on 07.03, {ocasionar?} on 08.05, {desean?} on 09.07, {unanim…?} on 10.16 and {movimiento?} on 11.04–09: the notes ask for a human reading of those lines at full resolution.
  • 67 restored words rest on sense, not on the table.
  • The word counts were made by hand and are approximate. The six cipher lines of passage P112 (11.04–09) are in the frozen test transcription only, not in the transcription and decryption files shown below, so that part of the reading cannot be checked from them.
  • One AI transcription and one AI reader. No human palaeographer has checked the transcription or the reading, and the project did not check them against the images.

What the contributor’s notes record of the other routes: N.5 of the same volume (Villafranca, catalogue 2422) uses another system, and no sibling in this letter cipher was found; the 12 leaves carry no clear minute or decipherment; no key for Carlist agents was found in the repository or its index of DECODE; no edition was found. Every line was decoded again with the table of its group at the review of 9 November 2026 and regraded, and the open words stayed open.

## Notes on the reading

- Spelling of the time kept where the cipher gives it (reyno, Magestad, ynglesa). The unit e/c stands for u or b; in this
  reading u/b is read by sense and not counted as a restoration.
- The general is written "Globet" (Goblet d'Alviella, Belgian general sent to Lisbon in 1837).
- Corrections made at the review of 9 Nov 2026, before publication: words that are not in the cipher removed ("de Lisboa"
  in 07.03, "del Norte" in 09.07, "se" in 10.09; "se trata de" in 05.b and "existe en" in 10.06 are clear text); doubtful
  words marked {?} moved to unresolved; "la ?egadades" read "la [ll]egada de" (restored); the certain class limited to the
  verified table.

Summary: Azuares reports talks with the Austrian chargé d'affaires in London. Austria and Britain contemplate changing the
Portuguese regime (making Maria II an absolute queen and reviving the old Cortes, with General Goblet guiding Prince
Ferdinand), even at the cost of Dom Miguel's rights; Lisbon would seek a treaty with Don Carlos on mutual security once he
reached Madrid. Azuares objects that recognising Maria would mean recognising the usurpation, that the Portuguese people
remain loyal to Dom Miguel, and that such a settlement would not secure a lasting peace.
