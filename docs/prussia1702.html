Blenheim Papers · to an unknown teacher · plain French · date unknown (volume 1702–1723) · not a cipher

A Lesson in Cipher

A leaf catalogued as a numerical cipher of a Prussian king. There is no cipher on it: it is a short French thank-you note for lessons in writing in cipher.

Daniel Bourdeau · resolved 21 September 2026 · updated 22 September 2026

At a glanceNot a cipher

The document
Seven lines of French in a large, careful hand, spelled by ear and set out syllable by syllable. Writer, recipient and date are not known.
Where
British Library, Add MS 61575 f. 95, the Blenheim volume of the Marlboroughs’ cipher material. DECODE R8774, listed as a non-decrypted numerical cipher of “Frederic, Re di Prussia”, 1702–1723. Unsolved Catalogue item 98.
What was done
The page image was read. It shows no numbers and no signs. Read aloud, the French makes sense as it stands.
What it says
The writer thanks someone for the trouble they took in showing how to write in cipher, and wishes them a good journey. That subject explains why it was filed with the ciphers.
Still open
Who wrote it and to whom. No signature survives under the ink blot, and DECODE’s attribution to a Prussian king cannot be checked from the leaf.

01 The page

One page, written in brown ink across the top half of the leaf, with a long rule below and an ink blot at the left where a signature could have been. The folio number 95 is at top right. The words are broken into syllables and spaced out in rough columns, as in a writing exercise. Spelling follows sound: eun for une, Payne for peine, d'es cria re for d'écrire, sif fer for chiffre, as seu re for assurer. The s for ch is what a German speaker would write, which fits a Prussian writer, but proves nothing about which one.

02 Transcription and reading

Line for line, as written:

par cel le cy je vient vous re
mer cier en cor eun ne foy de
la Pay ne que vous aves eue avec
moy en me mon trant d'es cria re
en sif fer et vous as seu re
que je se roys tous jours re cog naissent
vous sou haytant eun bon voya ge.

In standard French:

Par celle-ci je viens vous remercier encore une fois de la peine que vous avez eue avec moi en me montrant d'écrire en chiffre, et vous assurer que je serai toujours reconnaissant, vous souhaitant un bon voyage.

"By this I come to thank you once more for the trouble you took with me in showing me how to write in cipher, and to assure you that I shall always be grateful, wishing you a good journey."

Every word reads; the only doubt is the letter shapes of eun in the last line, which could be un with a flourish.

03 Why it was catalogued as a cipher

The note is about cipher. Someone who kept the Marlboroughs' keys filed it with them, and DECODE catalogued each leaf of the volume as a ciphertext or a key, with the volume's date span and a generic "numerical" symbol set. No one had looked at the image against the metadata. The correction has been queued for DECODE: not a cipher, cleartext French, no numbers.

04 What stays open

  • Writer. "Frederic, Re di Prussia" comes from DECODE and is unverified. The hand and spelling suggest a young German-speaking writer still learning French, which fits a Prussian prince, but no name appears on the leaf.
  • Recipient. Someone who taught ciphers and was about to travel. Not identified.
  • Date. Unknown; 1702–1723 is the volume's span.

05 Sources
