The letters
Six cipher letters (docs 322, 307, 309, 310, 311, 333) of Francesco Pandolfini from the French court, October 1514 to March 1515, to Rome, to Lorenzo de’ Medici and to the Otto di Pratica.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Firenze, Mediceo avanti il Principato, filza LXXII; lead 2389 (found by Daniel Bourdeau).
The cipher
Consonant signs carry the vowel as a superscript figure, with bare vowel signs, homophones, word signs and a nomenclator between points.
How it was deciphered
Brice Dudout learnt the key from the cipher and decipherment pairs 345/346 and 340/341 of the same filza and tested it on three held-out parts: two under the 0.85 bar (0.788 and 0.691, not validated) and a third (0.613) that is partial only under a lenient threshold; overall partial. Later keys are declared and not measured. The project repeated none and did not see the images.
What it says
Letter 322 (in the probable reading): an ambassador will be sent to Rome once Rocciafort arrives, and the King asks that Venice be approached to give back Monselice, Este and Montagnana to the Este.
Still open
Unresolved codes and groups in every letter except 333; transcriptions of 311 and part of 310 not blind; the key is partial.

01 The letters

Francesco Pandolfini was the Florentine orator at the French court. Filza LXXII of the Mediceo avanti il Principato in the Archivio di Stato di Firenze holds six of his letters from October 1514 to March 1515 whose cipher is bare (no decipherment beside it), next to about fifteen cipher and decipherment pairs of the same system. The lead (catalogue 2389) was found by Daniel Bourdeau; Brice Dudout (L’Europe chiffrée, with Claude Opus 5.5 agents) rebuilt the key and read them. No image or derivative is reproduced here.

02 The key and its tests

Consonant signs carry the vowel as a superscript figure (1–5 for u, o, i, e, a), with bare vowel signs, homophones, word signs (F for et, M for non, T for senza) and a nomenclator between points (.2. papa, .4. Cristianissimo, .5. Cattolico, .6. Inghilterra, .20. Svizzeri, .24. Milano, .26. Arciduca). The only measured key, v3, was learnt from the pairs of the time 345/346 and 340/341 in the same filza and frozen. Three held-out tests with protocols hashed first: 346/345 gives 0.788 and 340/341 gives 0.691 (both under the 0.85 needed for validated), and 342 c. 433r, by an Italian n-gram criterion calibrated in advance, gives N = 0.613, which is partial: the partial threshold is 0.004 and the validated one 0.765, a criterion the contributor calls more lenient than character agreement, so it is not comparable with the first two figures. Keys v4 and v6, which add values derived afterwards and merge two agents’ derivations by a declared arbitration, are declared and not measured; the key stays partial. The contributor says the ceiling is the resolution of the images. The project did not repeat any test and did not see the images.

03 What the letters say

In the contributor’s reading of 322 (about 77% by sense), once Rocciafort arrives the King will send an ambassador to Rome, believed to be the bishop of “Lancres” (a see not identified) and empowered to negotiate everything with the Pope (in the probable reading; some tokens in that block are unresolved); the informant (code .51., unresolved) expects the same quarrel as the Pope’s with the bishop of Marseille; and after Alfonso Ariosti’s visit for the Duke of Ferrara the King asked that the Venetian ambassador be approached so that Venice gives back Monselice, Este and Montagnana (the reading is broken here; the contributor’s summary has the Este holding them as imperial fiefs). The mention of the peace and marriage made (“pace et parentado fatto”) suggests 1514 (the contributor reads them as the Anglo-French peace and Louis XII’s marriage to Mary Tudor, October 1514). 309 reports Louise of Savoy’s offer of the daughter of the Duke of Valentinois (identified by the contributor as Louise Borgia) for Lorenzo, which the contributor notes is still to be checked in the printed sources. “Ghiscia” in 310 is identified by the contributor as Pierre de La Guiche, on Letters and Papers, Henry VIII, vol. 2, and The National Archives E 30/754 and E 30/927. No human has checked any transcription. The readings and translations are in targets/pandolfini1514.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Unresolved words and groups: 29 in 307, 8 in 309, 85 in 310, 65 in 311 and about 39 in 322, mostly codes missing from the key (.22., .16., .17., .48., .51. and others) and shifted superscripts; high-resolution images would settle some.
  • The transcriptions of 311 and of position 759 of 310 were not blind (shapes were settled while reading); lines B15–B16 of 382v are nearly illegible.
  • The key is partial and the later keys were not measured.
  • Desjardins and Canestrini, Négociations diplomatiques de la France avec la Toscane, vol. II, has a gap from 16 August 1514 to 25 May 1515; no printed text of these letters was found.

## Notes on the uncertain words

- ‹.51.›: a code absent from the key; a person at the French court who informs the Florentines ("ha detto", "crede"): unresolved.
- {ti} (Z^3): "ti" by the key, though "ci" would suit "noi crediamo" better; flagged, nothing restored.
- {ambasciadore}: seven signs a-n-ba-?-?-do-re, structure only: weak probable.
- {vescovo} di {Lancres}: ve-po-co-vo (shifted superscript) and la-n-c-re-s, letters read; the see (Langres?) is not identified.
- {havere} {chiarito}: need bare 5 = h (a v4 alternative); sense clear.
- {Santità} (.64.): "T^n .64." (to negotiate with the Pope) and "sua .64. et monsignore di Marsilia"; a v4 value.
- {monsignore} {Marsilia}: shifted superscripts; supported by the clear "mons. di Marsilia" just before.
- {significare}, {commissione}: d = g and 2^5 = ca (v4 alternatives); la-?-mm-i-?-?-a-ne.
- {Monselice} {Este} {Montagnana}: m-n-se-li-ce, e-s-ti, m-n-t-g-na-na (one superscript missing, d = g): three towns of the Padovano held by Venice and claimed by the Este, consistent with Alfonso Ariosti's mission for Ferrara and with "investiti dello imperadore".
- {imperadore} (.3.): a v4 value, attested by the pair 342/343.
- Unresolved tokens are given as transcribed; no word was restored against certain letters.

France; the King, sure of England, will man his frontiers and sends the governors to their provinces. (E, 2 March) The King told Pandolfini he

# Translation (English): ASF MAP LXXII 333, 27 Dec 1514

From the reading by sense (`reading_333.md`). Every word read; the content is also given in clear in 332.

Pandolfini to Lorenzo de' Medici, Paris, 27 December 1514: he writes only to send the copy of his letter to the Otto. In cipher: on
this side (the French court) there is little of weight to report for now; everything hangs on the decisions to be taken "there" (in
Rome), which are awaited here with very great suspicion.
Full translation of the cipher block: "On this side there is at present little of moment to report, everything depending on the resolutions that will be taken there [in Rome], about which we are here in very great suspicion."
