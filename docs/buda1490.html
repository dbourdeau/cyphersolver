At a glanceKey from external plaintext · partial (90% of the cipher)

The letter
Maffeo da Treviglio, the Milanese agent who had negotiated the Sforza–Corvinus match since 1489, writes from Milan on 30 November 1490 to Tamás Bakócz, bishop of Győr and chancellor of the King of Hungary. The salutation, closing, date, signature and address are in clear.
Where
Hungarian National Archives (MNL OL), Diplomatikai Levéltár, DL 46150 (Hungaricana charter 228061). Four paper pages in cipher. Catalogue item 1235.
The cipher
A homophonic Latin cipher of letter-like signs, figures and marks: 76 labels over 3,199 tokens, about 60 of them carrying the text. It is not Maffeo’s 1489 key.
How it was deciphered
The key was recovered from the clear minute of the same letter kept in Milan, then extended in a retry pass. Doubtful transcriptions were checked on the image. The statistical validation passed.
What it says
After King Matthias’s death the planned marriage of Bianca Maria Sforza to John Corvinus had lapsed, and the new king, Vladislas II, took up the “affinity” himself. Maffeo reports the Duke’s warm welcome of the offer and says that, having come down to 150,000 ducats, he now offers 200,000, in jewels or money at the King’s choice, with 10,000 for the bride’s trousseau. Bakócz is to send the courier back, with envoys or the King’s decision.
Still open
63 words are not read as sense: 8 on ambiguous signs, 15 on letter signs standing once for a title or phrase, and others on lost or single-use signs. The images were not seen here.
Credit
Harrison Bornstein (PR #199) transcribed the letter from the Hungaricana images, broke it ciphertext-only to 79% of words, rebuilt the key from the Milan minute, and read it.

01 The letter

Maffeo reports his audience in Buda with the king, Bianca Maria’s part in the affair and the Chancellor of Bohemia, and the Duke’s reception of the offer. He presents the alliance as the continuation of the bond with King Matthias. On the dowry he says the King’s demand had seemed to exceed all measure and made the Duke hesitate, since no king had ever received such a sum. He asks Bakócz to send the courier back at once. The Latin and an English translation are under Full texts.

02 The key

The key has several homophones for the common letters: five signs for e, four for i, four for a and five for s. Some signs have two or three values by context (for example qA is s, i or t). The letter minute in Milan gave the plain text against which the key was rebuilt. That makes the minute a partial check, not an independent one: the notes set out how far it can count as evidence. A second Maffeo key, “Cum Mafeo Triviliensi” (DECODE R5407), may be the key of this cipher; that was checked only as far as one session could.

03 The reading

561 of 624 words (89.9%) are read as sense, up from 544 (87.2%) before the retry pass. That is under the 95% bar, so this is a partial reading. The 39 transcription suspects were checked on the image: 11 fixed, 19 confirmed as the writer’s own departures, 9 left ambiguous.

04 What remains uncertain

  • 8 words rest on signs the image check left ambiguous (dominationem, cepit, semper, adduxit, secundum, reuerendissime, descendit, responso); only the original would settle them.
  • 15 words rest on letter signs that stand once for a title or phrase (such as Bianca and the Chancellor of Bohemia), known from the minute only.
  • 4 words rest on signs with no value at that place, and 9 more are the writer’s own departures (grade I).
  • 15 words have letters left out or multi-sign departures; the shape check on the page images is still to do.
  • 12 words have a lost sign, mostly on page 3 lines 15–21, filled from the minute.

## Summary

Maffeo reports to Bakócz that he reached the Duke of Milan (Gian Galeazzo Sforza, "my Most Illustrious Prince") and
set out what the bishop had done for the marriage alliance ("affinity") planned between the King of Hungary (the new
king, Vladislas II) and the Duke, through the Duke's sister Bianca Maria Sforza, who had earlier been betrothed to John
Corvinus, son of King Matthias. The Duke welcomes the alliance, now counts the bishop among his foremost friends, and sees it
as the continuation of the bond made with King Matthias. The dowry asked by the King, said to exceed all measure, has
given the Duke pause; still he will add to what he promised King Matthias: having once come down to 150,000 ducats,
he now goes to 200,000, in jewels or money at the King's choice, with 10,000 of it for the bride's trousseau if paid
in money, and the King to provide the jewels honourably. Bakócz is to send the courier back at once with the answer,
and either have envoys sent with a mandate or have Milanese envoys come to conclude; otherwise he must report the new
king's will and decision.
