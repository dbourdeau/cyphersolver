Printed novel, no correspondents · simple substitution in an invented alphabet, and symbolic dates · Strassburg 1616

The Inscriptions of the Chymische Hochzeit

Embassy of the Free Mind (Ritman Library), Amsterdam: Chymische Hochzeit: Christiani Rosencreutz. Anno 1459, Strassburg, Lazarus Zetzner's heirs, 1616, pp. 73, 95, 97, 116 · DECODE R4502, catalogued as a letter of Andreae with no decipherment

DECODE's four cipher pages are the printed inscriptions of the Rosicrucian novel. The novel gives the clear text of its two long inscriptions a paragraph later, and English editions have printed all four in clear since 1690. The symbol table was recovered here by aligning the images with those glosses, and it decrypts both German texts in full. The two date lines fit the published years as wholes.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 22 September 2026 · updated 24 September 2026

Summary. Catalogue item 210 listed DECODE R4502 as a ciphered letter from Johann Valentin Andreae to an unknown recipient, 1616, with a note that no decipherment was known. The DECODE images show a printed book: the first edition of the Chymische Hochzeit Christiani Rosencreutz, the third Rosicrucian text, attributed to Andreae. Its four cipher pages are inscriptions that the narrator copies in the story. Two are simple substitutions in an invented alphabet cut as type. The page boy gives the text of both in the next paragraph: "here lies buried Venus, the fair lady who has brought so many high men to lose fortune, honour, blessing and welfare"; and "when the fruit of my tree has quite melted, I shall awake and be the mother of a king". The other two lines are strings of alchemical and numeral signs that stand for dates. The crcsite.org edition of Foxcroft's 1690 translation gives them as 1378 and 1459. The 19-symbol alphabet was recovered here and decrypts both German texts in full. The date lines fit the published years as wholes, but not every sign in them is identified.

01 The book, not a letter

DECODE R4502 was uploaded by the Embassy of the Free Mind with the title "Johann Valentin Andreae, 'Chymische Hochzeit Christiani Rosenkreutz'", eight images and a note: "This rosicrucianism book in German has four pages with ciphertext. As far as is known, this has never been deciphered." The owner adds that the library holds three copies and only one was photographed. When DECODE's catalogue was imported here, the import script classified the record as a "letter to an unknown recipient". The images show the title page (Strassburg, "In Verlägung Lazari Zetzners S. Erben", 1616) and five openings, pp. 72–73, 94–95, 96–97, 98–99 and 116–117, and the colophon (Conrad Scher, 1616). The four with cipher are:

  • p. 73, Day IV: a line of signs under the fountain inscription Hermes Princeps … Bibite fratres, et vivite;
  • p. 95, Day V: the words in copper letters on the iron door to Venus's vault;
  • p. 97, Day V: the tablet behind the bed where Venus sleeps;
  • p. 116, Day VI: the line after Anno at the foot of the urn inscription.

02 The door to Venus's vault, p. 95

HIE LIGT BEGRABEN VENUS DIE SCHON[E] FRAW SO MANCHEN HOHEN MANN UMB GLUCK EHR SEGEN UND WOLFART GEBRACHT HATT

"Here lies buried Venus, the fair lady who has brought so many high men to lose fortune, honour, blessing and welfare." The narrator copies it "into my writing tablet". On the next page, when he asks what the tomb means, the boy answers: "hie ligt begraben (sagt er) Venus die schöne Fraw / so manchen hohen Mann umb Glück / Ehr / Segen und Wolfart gebracht hatt" (p. 96).

03 The tablet behind the bed, p. 97

WAN DIE FRUCHT MEINES BAUMS WIRT VOLLENDS VERSCHMELZEN WERDE ICH AUFWACHEN UND EIN MUTER SEIN EINES KONIGS

"When the fruit of my tree has quite melted, I shall awake and be the mother of a king." On p. 98 the boy repeats what he heard Atlas tell the King: "wann der Baum … wirdt vollends verschmeltzen / so wirdt Fraw Venus wider erwachen / vnd sein ein Mutter eines Königs".

04 The alphabet

Both inscriptions use one simple substitution. There is one sign per letter, words are divided, and there are no nulls or code words. The 156 letters use 19 signs, with one capital form (the initial H). U and V share a sign. M, N and O are three dotted variants of one shape. The pairs are fixed by words the boy's glosses supply: B-E-G-R-A-B-E-N gives b, e, g, r, a, n; F-R-A-W gives f, w; G-L-U-C-K gives l, u, c, k; F-R-U-C-H-T gives h, t; and so on. Every sign that occurs has a value, and the table applied to both texts gives no contradiction. The alphabet is the author's own: only 20 letters of German occur, and there is nothing to compare it with.

05 The two sign lines: dates

These two lines are not letter ciphers. They mix Roman numerals with alchemical and planetary signs, and they are dates. The English edition on crcsite.org, which follows Foxcroft's translation of 1690, gives 1378 for the fountain line (footnote 12) and 1459 for the urn (footnote 21). It also interprets the ligature at the right end of the urn line as P.H.M.D., "Paracelsus Hohenheimensis Medicinae Doctor". 1378 is the birth year the Rosicrucian manifestos give Christian Rosencreutz. 1459 is the year on the title page.

Checked against the signs: the urn line has four rings (CCCC), an IX and an M, which fit M CCCC L IX. The fountain line opens with a lemniscate carrying three dots, ∞ being an old sign for 1000, and it has XX and a V with dots, which fit M CCC LXX VIII. Both interpretations are graded C (probable): the structure agrees with the published years, but the signs for L and for the smaller units are not identified one by one, and each line is too short to fix them.

06 What stays open

  • The individual numeral signs of the two date lines (grade C as wholes). They are one-off symbolic dates of about ten signs each, too short to fix every value.
  • The three dotted signs for M, N and O were separated with the plaintext in view: at DECODE's resolution, about 1,450 px per opening, the dots are too small to tell them apart blind. A better image would settle them. The other 16 signs are unambiguous and fix the text by themselves.
  • The other two Ritman copies were not photographed. The 1616 edition was printed from standing type, so they should carry the same inscriptions.

DECODE should list R4502 as decrypted: a printed book, not a letter, with a simple substitution plus two symbolic dates. The correction is queued.
