London → the Hereditary Prince · 6×6 digit square with word signs · 7 May 1795

Prince Frederick of Orange to his brother, London, 7 May 1795

→

Go to the full text ↓

Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, Koning Willem I, inv. XVIII-3 (DECODE R2242), four pages: a short cipher with clear Dutch under it, a clear letter dated London 7 May 1795, and two pages of cipher. The digits are the same vertical pairs as in R1892, whose key was recovered from the ciphertext alone in this project; the R1892 key was applied to R2242 unchanged. The contemporary clear Dutch under page 1 confirms it; pages 3–4, written in late May or June 1795, had not been deciphered anywhere and are now deciphered except twenty word signs seen once each. Their reading builds coherent sentences but reads at 88.0% of its words, under the 95% a complete reading needs.

At a glanceExternal key matched · partial (96% of the cipher)

The letter
Prince Frederick of Orange, in exile in London, to his elder brother the Hereditary Prince: a ciphered note with clear Dutch under it, a clear letter of 7 May 1795, and two pages of cipher written in late May or June 1795.
Where
Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, Koning Willem I, inv. XVIII-3; DECODE R2242, listed as a numerical substitution with no decryption.
The cipher
Each letter a pair of digits 1–6 written one above the other, with graphic signs for frequent words and names.
How it was deciphered
The square recovered from the ciphertext alone for R1892 fits unchanged; the clear Dutch under page 1 confirms it. On 2 October 2026 the two doubtful lines were re-transcribed at full resolution, twelve more word signs were given values from context, and Colenbrander’s Gedenkstukken identified the envoys and ministers named in the text. On 3 October one more pair was corrected on the image and the measure was made stricter (section 04).
What it says
The émigré troops are scattered, “geheel verstrooyd en [zonder] hooft”; the ratification of the treaty with France was published before it arrived, from letters of the ministers Meyer and Blauw in Paris, and De Sitter and Van Grasveld are sent to carry it over; England persists in not recognising the new government.
Still open
Twenty word signs seen once or twice, with no key sheet and no other occurrence to value them; four more whose values rest on one context each; two tokens under ink blots; and the identity of the “Dankaert” of the postscript.

01 The document and the key

The record has four images. Page 1 holds four cipher lines and the clear text beneath. Page 2 is a clear Dutch letter headed “Den 7 Mey 1795”, beginning “Weest zoo goed en zegt aan mijn Broer dat hij een brief van mij onder couvert van mijn Banquier … ontfangen moet hebben”. It speaks of money drawn on the banker, a bill of exchange, and letters from Hamburg that never came. One word in it is ciphered: 24 43 25 55 15 15 65 = famille. Pages 3 (17 lines) and 4 (8 lines and a postscript) are all cipher.

The key is R1892’s (see that write-up): 11 t, 12 w, 13 ij, 15 l, 21 z, 22 o, 23 k, 24 f, 25 m, 31 d, 32 n, 33 v, 34 h, 35 b, 41 g, 42 u, 43 a, 44 s, 45 p, 51 c, 52 r, 55 i, 65 e; the circle with a dot is de.

02 Page 1, with its clear text

als deezen brief geleesen hebt, dan […] genoomen en gebraaden. Zo het niet verandert, moeten wy niet in de Zee. Als de Erfprins iets begeert, zoo zulks maar weet: ben tot zijn dienst, dood of zoo te leeven, is het zelfde.

“When you have read this letter, [it is] taken and roasted. If it does not change, we must not [go] to sea. If the Hereditary Prince wants anything, let him only know it: I am at his service; dead or alive, it is all the same.” Word signs fixed by this page: >⊙▱ als deezen brief; δ genoomen; H-bar zo/zoo; long S moeten; ⊙Y de Zee; >⊙♁ als de Erfprins; ÷ zoo; ɣ zulks; § maar; v tot; crossed 8 zijn; ʃ zelfde.

“What do people say about our alliance with France? Does that change anything too? The officers and troops have been so scattered that so little reliance can be placed on many of the dismissed officers that one cannot expect much from them; there is not the least cohesion among them, they are wholly dispersed and without a head … The ratification of ours with France has been published, though the ratification itself had not yet come. It rested on letters from Paris of our ministers Meyer and Blauw, and now De Sitter and Van Grasveld are sent to France as ambassadors to carry over the ratification of ours. If England only persists in not recognising the present government, then they will not dare to introduce Robespierism…”

The date and the names. The ratification is that of the Treaty of The Hague between the Batavian Republic and France (16 May 1795). Colenbrander’s Gedenkstukken (vol. I pp. 655 and 678) names the Dutch negotiators in Paris, Blauw and Meyer, and the extraordinary envoys who carried the ratification, De Sitter and Van Grasveld, received by the Convention on 23 June 1795. Pages 3–4 were therefore written in late May or June 1795, later than the clear letter of 7 May on page 2. The letter itself is not printed in the Gedenkstukken (vols. I–II searched in full text, and the May–July 1795 letters of vol. II pp. 834–841 read).

04 What remains uncertain

Measured with the folder’s scripts over every digit pair and word sign of pages 1, 3 and 4. A token reads as sense when a Dutch model scores its 13-letter window above a threshold calibrated on page 1, or when it lies inside an attested Dutch word of six letters or more. 994 of 1,037 tokens read as sense (95.9%): p. 1 100%, p. 3 95.7%, p. 4 94.5%. By tokens the letter passes the threshold for a complete text (95% of the cipher gives sense). Counted by words on the written-out reading of pages 3–4 (24 October 2026), with the open word signs and doubtful words as holes, it reads at 88.0%: coherent sentences, but under 95%, so the extent is partial. What stays open is single word signs with no key material and two ink blots.

The measure changed on 3 October 2026, and the change should be stated. The 2 October figure (981, 94.6%) counted every value given to a word sign, including those resting on one context. The 3 October measure is stricter in that respect: a value inferred from one context where another word would also fit (grade I) now counts as unread. It is fairer in two others. The writer’s y is scored as ij, since the modern Dutch corpus spells zijn where he writes zyn. A word also passes when it is attested in Dutch of the period, from the project’s Dutch corpus and Colenbrander’s Gedenkstukken for 1789–1798, because the small modern corpus failed plain period spellings such as alliansie, gedimitteerde and persisteeren. Seven values of graphic word signs were upgraded where the sentence admits only one word (below). Applied to the state of 30 September, the new measure gives 90.7%; on the 2 October readings, with no word check, 90.6%. The forced word signs added 26 tokens, scoring y as ij 15 and the word check 13. As a control, letter-shuffled decrypts pass 8.4% of tokens (at most 12.2% over five seeds) with or without the word check, so the word list adds no false passes. Counting the four context-only values as well gives 1,000 of 1,037 (96.4%). The model checks only that the letters read as Dutch, so a wrong word value that is still Dutch passes.

# R2242 pp. 3-4, Prince Frederick of Orange to the Hereditary Prince, 1795: the reading

Written 23 Oct 2026 (verification 3) from the decrypts `decrypt_p3.txt` and `decrypt_p4.txt` (R1892 square key,
`apply.py`, transcription state of 3 Oct 2026) and the reading in NOTES.md ("What the cipher says"), divided into
words. Spelling is the writer's (y for ij, *persisteern*, *offeciers*). Word signs with an H or M value are written as

# R2242 pp. 3-4, Prince Frederick of Orange to the Hereditary Prince, 1795: English translation

The letter [?braaden]. What do people say of our alliance with France? Does that make any change either? The officers
and the troops have been so thoroughly separated from each other, and so [?little] reliance is to be placed on many
of the dismissed officers, that one cannot […] much […] of […] [?imagine] there; nor is there the least cohesion
among them; they are entirely scattered and without a head.

[…] who […] naturally would […] and that would have shown themselves [?for] […], did not […] themselves brilliantly. I
fear that the distress […] the politicians too […] that slackening […] […] there where one […] trusts […] as to
o[…], if only one did not open oneself too much, that the point of the coalition is handled with all [?possible] […]ness.

The ratification of ours with France has been published, although the ratification itself had not yet come. It was
on […] genuine letter from Paris from our ministers Meier and Blaauw, and now De Sitter and Van Grasveld have also
been sent to France as ambassadors to carry over the ratification of our […].

If only [?England] goes on persisting in not recognising the present government, then they will not dare to
introduce Robespierrism, for which there is a very great disposition […]. My respects where they are due; is the
whole company still there? […] here […] detailed answer. Greet my brother.

P.S. Dankaert is also abroad […] […] […] already […] with them.

Page 4 (8 cipher lines and P.S.) read in part

If England persists in not recognising the present government, they will not dare introduce Robespierrism, for which there is much disposition; respects to the whole company; a detailed answer is awaited; greetings to his brother. P.S.: Dankaert is also abroad.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (88% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

`apply.py`, transcription state of 3 Oct 2026) and the reading in NOTES.md ("What the cipher says"), divided into
words. Spelling is the writer's (y for ij, *persisteern*, *offeciers*). Word signs with an H or M value are written as
words; the four I-grade values (X *weinig*, & *Engeland*, + *mogelyk*, crossed diamond *voor*) are `[?word]`; `[…]` is

(`wainut_proposals.md`) are not used. Page 3 runs to *om de ra-*, page 4 begins *-tificatie*.
