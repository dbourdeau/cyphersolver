London → the Hereditary Prince · 6×6 digit square with word signs · 7 May 1795 · read in part

Prince Frederick to his brother, 7 May 1795: the “non-decrypted” R2242 reads with the R1892 key

Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, Koning Willem I, inv. XVIII-3 (DECODE R2242), four pages: a short cipher with clear Dutch under it, a clear letter dated London 7 May 1795, and two pages of cipher. The digits are the same vertical pairs as in R1892, and that key reads them unchanged.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 21 September 2026

Summary. DECODE lists R2242 as a non-decrypted numerical substitution. Its own note guesses that the writer is Prince Frederick of Orange (1774–1799) writing to his elder brother, and that the cipher is linked to the “Rassemblement” letter R1892. It is. Each letter is again a pair of digits 1–6 written top over bottom, and the square recovered ciphertext-only for R1892 reads all three cipher pages without change. Page 1 checks the key independently: a contemporary hand wrote the clear Dutch under its four cipher lines, and it agrees word for word, giving the values of a dozen word signs as well. Pages 3 and 4 are one continuous Dutch text. It reports that the officers and troops are scattered with “geen de minste saamenhang”, that the ratification (of the Prussian Peace of Basel) was published before it had arrived, and that ministers and ambassadors were sent to Paris with it. It also says that “they” persist in not recognising the present government unless no “Robespierismus” is introduced. About twenty word signs on those pages stay unread, and the transcription from photographs of photocopies has doubtful pairs.

01 The document and the key

The record has four images. Page 1 holds four cipher lines and the clear text beneath. Page 2 is a clear Dutch letter headed “Den 7 Mey 1795”, beginning “Weest zoo goed en zegt aan mijn Broer dat hij een brief van mij onder couvert van mijn Banquier … ontfangen moet hebben”. It speaks of money drawn on the banker, a bill of exchange, and letters from Hamburg that never came. One word in it is ciphered: 24 43 25 55 15 15 65 = famille. Pages 3 (17 lines) and 4 (8 lines and a postscript) are all cipher.

The key is R1892’s (see that write-up): 11 t, 12 w, 13 ij, 15 l, 21 z, 22 o, 23 k, 24 f, 25 m, 31 d, 32 n, 33 v, 34 h, 35 b, 41 g, 42 u, 43 a, 44 s, 45 p, 51 c, 52 r, 55 i, 65 e; the circle with a dot is de.

02 Page 1, with its clear text

als deezen brief geleesen hebt, dan […] genoomen en gebraaden. Zo het niet verandert, moeten wy niet in de Zee. Als de Erfprins iets begeert, zoo zulks maar weet: ben tot zijn dienst, dood of zoo te leeven, is het zelfde.

“When you have read this letter, [it is] taken and roasted. If it does not change, we must not [go] to sea. If the Hereditary Prince wants anything, let him only know it: I am at his service; dead or alive, it is all the same.” Word signs fixed by this page: >⊙▱ als deezen brief; δ genoomen; H-bar zo/zoo; long S moeten; ⊙Y de Zee; >⊙♁ als de Erfprins; ÷ zoo; ɣ zulks; § maar; v tot; crossed 8 zijn; ʃ zelfde.

03 Pages 3 and 4

Read with the key; | marks an unread word sign, [ ] a restoration.

… | onse alliansie met | geeft | ook verandering. Men heeft de officier[s] en de trouppes | wel | malkander gelegen … is | staat op veele der gedimitteerde offeciers te maaken; men zi[et] | niet veel | stellen. Ook is er geen de minste saamenhang tussen | [z]elve, zij [zijn] geheel verstrooijd … hooft … natuurlijk zoude | zig | zouden toonen hebben … de politiken ook | verslappen … op vertrouwen … men zig | niet te veel op[laten] … het point der coalitie met alle … men heeft de ratificatie ons met | gepubliceert, of schoon de ratificatie zelver nog niet gekoomen was … | Parijs | onse ministers … zijn nog | ambassadeurs gesonden om [de] ratificatie van onse | over te brengen … maar blijft persisteeren het tegenwoordig gouvernement niet te erkennen, dan zoo zij geen Robespierismus durven te introduceeren … zeer veel dispositie … is mijn respect … het geheele geselschap … groet mijn broe[der].

The news fits London in late April and early May 1795. Prussia had made peace with France at Basel on 5 April and ratified it that month; the Batavian Republic was negotiating the Treaty of The Hague (16 May); and the Orange émigré troops, dismissed or scattered after the flight of January, were the subject of R1892.

04 What remains uncertain

  • About twenty word signs on pages 3–4 (Λ, ∞, Δ, φ, script L, ⊥ and others) are unread; context proposes none of them with confidence.
  • The transcription was made by a language model from photographs of photocopies. Several pairs are doubtful: p3 line 1 (“wat ze id men”), p4 line 8, and single letters in saamenhang, ministers and coalitie (grade C). The overall sense of pages 3–4 is H; individual clauses between signs are M.
  • “Robespierismus” is clear (H); whose recognition of “the present government” is meant depends on an unread sign.
