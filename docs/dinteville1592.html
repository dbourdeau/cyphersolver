Langres → Nevers · letters, digits and signs, apparently with code signs · 3 July 1592 · not read

Dinteville to the Duke of Nevers, 1592

BnF Français 3621, no. 116, f. 130 (DECODE R9451; Gallica btv1b52524472n, view 269). A clear letter from the royalist lieutenant in Champagne with two cipher passages, about ten lines in all.

The catalogue listed it as a transcription job, because DECODE says the leaf has a decipherment. It has none. The one crib, on a sibling letter at f. 128, did not give a key this session.

Summary. DECODE describes R9451 as a “letter, with cipher and decryption”, and the catalogue therefore marked it as read at the time. The image shows no decipherment. f. 130r has a clear letter dated at Langres on 3 July 1592 with two passages in cipher (about 3 and 7 lines), then an autograph postscript in clear French. The volume turns out to be on Gallica in full resolution, and two leaves earlier, f. 128r is another Dinteville letter of July 1592 in the same signs, with a contemporary decipherment written above each cipher line: m’a dict avoir veu d’ascendre a Geneve deux millions d’or d’Espaigne … quarante cinq + mulets chargez … prendre le chemin de Besançon. That is a crib of about 150 signs. A test for a one-sign-one-letter key (homophones allowed, up to three null types) finds none: the same sign sits under o in auoir and under d a few signs later. The decipherer also left some signs as crosses, which suggests code signs for words. Status: not read. What would finish it is a careful sign-by-sign transcription of f. 128 against its interlinear, then of f. 130.

01 The leaf

The letter is in clear French, and the cipher breaks into it twice. The signs are a mixed set: digits (1 3 4 9 0), letters (o v w m z c a) and marks (# ψ α Δ □ ¢ ∇ π ƒ +), some barred or crossed, many followed by a dot. There are roughly 400 to 450 signs. The paragraph after the date, beginning Monseigneur ce matin j’ay sceu … l’armée lorraine, is Dinteville’s own clear postscript, not a decipherment. The verso carries the address. Nothing on the leaf glosses the cipher.

02 The crib on f. 128

This leaf is probably no. 114, which the BnF notice lists as a Dinteville letter “avec chiffre et déchiffrement”. It uses the same signs as f. 130. The decipherment is written above the cipher, roughly word by word, and runs: … m’a dict / avoir veu d’ascendre a Geneve deux millions d’or d’Espaigne / … quarante cinq + mulets chargez qui doibt aussi passer dans trois / jours et prendre le chemin de Besançon …, news of Spanish money moving through Geneva and Franche-Comté towards Flanders.

03 What was tried

I transcribed the second cipher line of f. 128 as 50 signs against its 48-letter gloss. A search for a consistent key (each sign one letter, several signs per letter, up to three sign types as nulls) finds no solution. The first five signs fit auoir cleanly, but α then has to be both o and d. Either the cipher is not a plain homophonic alphabet (code signs for words such as Geneve and Espaigne, or signs worth more than one letter), or my reading of the glyphs by eye is wrong in places. With about 150 aligned signs and no second crib, the two cannot be separated from here.

04 What remains

  • Both cipher passages of f. 130: unread.
  • The f. 128 system: undetermined. The crib exists, but no key has been rebuilt from it.
  • Next steps: a verified hand transcription of f. 128’s three and a half lines; a sweep of the rest of fr. 3621 (and fr. 4716 no. 31, Dinteville to Nevers, 1590) for more Dinteville letters with decipherments; then the nomenclator-plus-language-model method used for Lorraine 1592 and Pélissier 1592.
