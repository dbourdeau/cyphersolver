Pozsony → Ferrara · homophonic substitution with three code signs for names · 1 June 1482

Matthias Corvinus to Ercole I d’Este, 1 June 1482

→

Modena, Archivio di Stato, Carteggio principi esteri 1622, b. 1/3, nos. 2, 2a · one page, Latin, with about fifteen runs in cipher · DECODE R1156, “partially decrypted”

The King of Hungary tells his brother-in-law, whom Venice had just attacked, what help he can send and why it cannot yet get through. The letter was printed with its decipherment in 1877 and again in 1895. Here the cipher runs were transcribed from the image and a working key was rebuilt by aligning them with the print; with it, four places where the printed text went wrong are corrected.

Where
Modena original; marked on DECODE as only partly decrypted.
The cipher
Invented signs for letters, two to four for common vowels, dots for doubled letters, three name signs (Venice, King Ferdinand, Matthias).
How it was read
The decipherment was already printed (Magyar diplomácziai emlékek III, 1877; Fraknói 1895, no. 131). Aligning it with the runs rebuilds most of the key.
What was added
Four corrections to the edition, including valent for “valoris (sic)”, gentibus for servants, and the closing sentence Speramus cito nos res nostras ita disposituros….
Still open
One run after faciet and the verb before it, and two name signs after regina.

01 The letter

Venice declared war on Ferrara in May 1482. Duke Ercole I d’Este was married to Eleonora of Aragon, daughter of King Ferdinand of Naples and sister of Beatrix, Queen of Hungary since 1476. Eleonora wrote to her sister and brother-in-law for help. Matthias answered from Pozsony (Pressburg, Bratislava) on the first of June. He was himself at war with the Emperor Frederick III, whose lands lay between Hungary and Italy.

The letter is one page of Latin in a chancery hand, with the address and a red seal on the verso. The courtesies and the reasoning are in clear. The numbers, the troops, the route, the names and the plan are in cipher, set into the clear lines as short runs. DECODE holds two images (I5815, I5816). The archive keeps the rights, and the images are not reproduced here.

02 The cipher

Each letter of the Latin is replaced by an invented sign. Many signs look like numerals or letters turned about (5, 4, 9, 2, 3, 7, 8, a reversed C, L, T and its inversion ⊥, Ŧ, Π, Λ, ⊏, ⊐, ∃, ∅), which makes the runs look like a string of figures at first sight. Vowels and the commonest consonants have two to four signs each. A doubled letter is one sign between dots, ·ıı· for tt in mittere and mitteremus. There are no word breaks inside a run, but the runs themselves are mostly single words or short phrases, spaced like the clear text.

Name signs: a looped ampersand for Veneti (and Venetorum), a crossed square for regi Ferdinando, and a q with a looped tail for Rex Hungarie, the King referring to himself. Some signs seem to serve two letters (+ for o, t and e; 2 for i and d). With about 330 signs of cipher in all, the sample is too short to tell whether these are true homophones shared between letters or two similar shapes that a transcription from the image cannot separate.

03 The decrypted text

Clear text in roman, the cipher runs in brackets, corrections to the printed text in bold. The first lines of the letter, all in clear, are as Fraknói prints them.

… statim cum domina regina consorte nostra statuimus [mittere] in [subsidium] [vestrum] [quingentos] [equites], inter [quos] essent [centum] [usarones], idest [homines] levis [armature], qui plus [ceteris] [valent]. Sed cum examinaremus cum domina regina ‹two signs› et [aliis] nostris, per [quam] [viam] possemus [eos] [mittere], non invenimus aliquam [tutam]. …

In crastinum hinc recedet [Mager] [Balas] [capitaneus] [noster] cum [pluribus] [gentibus], iturus in [Croatiam] versus [confinia] [Venetorum], ubi [plures] etiam [congregabit]; et quotienscunque inventa erit [tuta] [transeundi] [via], statim significet [Regi Hungarie] et illic faciet [?] [venient].

Speramus cito nos [res] [nostras] ita [disposituros], ut meliori postea modo [vos] [iuvare] possemus. [Rex Hungarie] [scripsit] [regi Ferdinando] quomodo possent [diverti] [Veneti] et [confundere] [eos] et cum paucis [pecuniis].

Dat. Posonii, primo Junii 1482.

“… at once, with our lady the queen, we decided to send five hundred horse to your aid, among them a hundred hussars, that is light horse, who are worth more than the rest. But when we considered with the queen and our other counsellors by what road we could send them, we found none safe. … Tomorrow our captain Balázs Magyar leaves here with a large force for Croatia, towards the Venetian border, where he will gather more; and whenever a safe way through is found, he is to tell the King of Hungary at once, and they will come there. We hope soon to have our own affairs so arranged that afterwards we can help you better. The King of Hungary has written to King Ferdinand how the Venetians might be drawn off and thrown into confusion, and at small cost.”

04 What the printed text got wrong

  1. “qui plus ceteris valoris (így)”. The run is v-a-l-e-n-t. The hussars are the ones “who are worth more than the others”. The editor printed a non-word and marked it sic.
  2. “cum pluribus servis”. The run is g-e-n-t-i-b-u-s, with the same hooked-L g as congregabit. Balázs leaves with troops, which suits a captain sent to gather more on the Venetian border.
  3. “Speramus nos eos nostris contra dispositiones ut meliori postea modo possemus (így)”. The edition skipped the clear word cito, took r-e-s for eos and n-o-s-t-r-a-s for nostris, turned d-i-s-p-o-s-i-t-u-r-o-s into “contra dispositiones”, and left out v-o-s i-u-v-a-r-e, the verb the sentence needs. Decrypted from the signs, the sentence is sound Latin: Speramus cito nos res nostras ita disposituros, ut meliori postea modo vos iuvare possemus.
  4. The “regest”. Fraknói ends the letter with the date and gives, as a regest written “at the end of the letter in a contemporary hand”, the words Rex Hungarie scripsit regi Ferdinando, quomodo possent diverti Veneti et confundere eos et cum paucis pecuniis. That is the letter’s own last sentence, in the same hand, before the date, with the three name signs and s-c-r-i-p-s-i-t, d-i-v-e-r-t-i, c-o-n-f-u-n-d-e-r-e, e-o-s and p-e-c-u-n-i-i-s in cipher. His words are right, but he placed the sentence outside the letter. The verso carries only the address and the seal, so if a separate decipherment exists it is on leaf 2a, which DECODE did not image.

05 What remains uncertain

  • statim significet [Regi Hungarie] et illic faciet [?] [venient]. The printed text has convenire. The run after faciet is the looped-q name sign followed by v-e-n-i-e-n-t (grade C). The same sign may stand here for quod or for the syllable con-. The clear verb before the first q-sign looks more like sufficietur than significet on the image (grade M). The sense is not in doubt.
  • Two name signs after cum domina regina are not in the printed text and are not deciphered (grade I).
  • Signs that serve two letters (+, 2) and the single f are grade C. The rest of the key is grade H, confirmed on two or more words.
  • The two lines Fraknói gives about the letter to King Ferdinand (“Scripsimus regi Ferdinando, ut rex ipse vel dux Ferrarie possent aliquem excogitare modum…”) were checked only where they are in cipher (modum, tuto, mitteremus). His “libenter mitteremus (így)” stands as printed: the run does spell m-i-tt-e-r-e-m-u-s.

Uncertain reading (copied verbatim from NOTES.md):

Uncertain: "illic faciet ꝗ venient". The printed text has "convenire". The glyphs read ꝗ + v-e-n-i-e-n-t, so ꝗ may
here stand for *quod* or *con-*. The clear verb before the first ꝗ looks like *sufficietur*, which the printed text
gives as *significet*. Neither point changes the sense: Balázs is to tell the king as soon as a safe road is found,
and the troops are to meet there.

# R1156, Matthias Corvinus to Ercole I d'Este, Pozsony, 1 June 1482: English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `texts/R1156_reading.md`: the clear letter (Fraknói's print) with the
cipher runs read from the glyphs in ⟨ ⟩. Same marks: `[2 signs]` unread, `[ꝗ]` a code sign of uncertain value (here
rendered by the sense of the sentence, kept), code words given in English. Latin.

## Translation

Matthias, by the grace of God King of Hungary, Bohemia etc., to the most illustrious prince, Lord Hercules, Duke of
Ferrara and Modena etc., greeting and success according to his wishes. From the letters of the most illustrious Lady
Duchess, your consort, our dearest sister and sister-in-law, we have learned the state of your affairs and the
preparations of the Venetians against you, which we did not hear gladly; [and] that the most serene lord, our king
and our dearest father, and your other lords and confederates are to help you, and that they have already in fact
begun; we too, out of our singular goodwill towards you, shall not fail to do those things which we know will be of
use to you. For, having read the letters, we at once resolved with the Lady Queen our consort ⟨to send to your aid
five hundred horse, among whom there would be a hundred hussars, that is, men⟩ of light ⟨armour, who are worth more
than the others⟩. But when we examined with the Lady Queen [2 signs] and ⟨others⟩ of ours by ⟨what road we could send
them⟩, we found none ⟨safe⟩. In our judgement they would have to pass through the lands of the Emperor, with whom we
are at present in conflict; but of this we would not care much, provided we found a safe way of passing; but since
they have their passage through the lands of the Venetians, we fear that they could in no way pass. We have written
to King Ferdinand that the King himself or the Duke of Ferrara might devise some ⟨way⟩ by which they could [come]
⟨safely⟩; we would gladly ⟨send them⟩. We do not doubt that they would serve faithfully and well. Tomorrow ⟨Magyar
Balázs, our captain⟩, will leave here with ⟨many troops⟩, to go into ⟨Croatia⟩ towards the ⟨borders of [the
Venetians]⟩, where he will also ⟨gather⟩ ⟨more⟩; and as soon as a ⟨safe road for passing⟩ is found, he is to make it
known at once [ꝗ], and will make [ꝗ] ⟨them come⟩ there. We hope soon to ⟨arrange our affairs so⟩ that afterwards we
may be able ⟨to help you⟩ in a better manner.

⟨[The King of Hungary] has written [to King Ferdinand]⟩ how ⟨[the Venetians] might be diverted and confounded⟩, and
with little money. Given at Pozsony, the first of June 1482.
