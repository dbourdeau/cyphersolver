Edmondes to Burghley, 1592–1594 — “Lord Threr?” and the King’s secrets

→

BL Stowe MS 166 ff. 50–61, 109–112 (DECODE R7770–R7775), catalogue item 140. The DECODE record names “Lord Threr?” as the author, but the Lord Treasurer is the addressee. The letters are reports from the King’s camp in Edmondes’s papers.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved in part 21 September 2026

solved in part
On this pageThe lettersThe keyThe readingsWhat stays openSources
Summary. The catalogue had five DECODE records of “Lord Threr? to unknown recipient”, 1592–94, marked Non-decrypted. There is a sixth, R7771, in the same run. They are letters to the Lord Treasurer, Burghley, among the papers of Thomas Edmondes, Elizabeth’s agent with Henri IV. The writer reports what the King told him in person, and dates from Tours, Blois and the camp before Laon. In each letter a few lines are written in invented signs inside the clear English. The signs are a simple substitution of English, keeping word divisions, with a few homophones and single signs for persons. The key was broken from the words the, of and to, and the same key serves all six letters, in two hands. Every passage reads except four lines on f. 59. Among them: the King breaking off his sister’s marriage to [Soissons?], Montmorency’s demand for custody of the young Prince of Condé, a would-be purchaser told “that his faith is not vendible”, and [Guise?]’s designs on Champagne and Reims in 1594. Butler’s Edmondes Papers (1913) has not been checked and may print some of these letters.

The DECODE “sender” fields (John le Rios, John Symmons, “Permell of Dieppo”, “One of Dieppe”) come from endorsements. The letters themselves are written by someone at the King’s side, in Edmondes’s papers.

02 The key

A = δ, b = +, c = 2, d = ш, e = ʋ, f = 8, g = б/6, h = 4, i = ǂ, k = 7, l = 3, m = □, n = ȝ, o = α, p = 9, r = f, s = &, t = ω, u/v = ш or a small o, w = π, x = 5, y = λ. ш serves for d as well as u/v, and б for a as well as g. Person signs: 9 standing alone (probably the Count of Soissons, 1592); n (a suitor for Catherine de Bourbon, unidentified); £, £2 (probably the Duke of Guise, 1594).

03 The readings

f. 50 (R7770). “…the K. continueth his resolution to goe to Saumur to see his sister, not so much for that cause (as it pleaseth himself to tell me) as especiallye to breake of the marriage betwene her and [9] wch he tould me he is resolued not to permitt but to geive her vnto [n] of whom he said he hath great hope that he will render himself of the religion. [9] is herewth enraged and extreemelie discontented.”

f. 57 (R7771). “…by the Pope, will appeare vnto yor L. by the enclosed. [9]: is glad to here of, hoping to be no further troubled wth the clamor of the Catholikes for the seekinge of him.”

f. 58v (R7772). “I doe secretelie learne that Monsr Montmorancy hath made great instance, as a condition whereof he required the performance before he would come, to haue the yong prince of Condé deliuered into the handes of Madame d’Angolesme his sister in law to be vnder his custodie.” The four cipher lines that continue on f. 59 are not read: only “papistes” and a few other words are certain.

f. 60v (R7773). “…but the K. thereuppon saide vnto me that his faith is not vendible.”

f. 109v (R7774). “…choise expreslie to negotiate therein, for the difficulties of empeachement wch he knoweth he will geive to the same in respect of his owne interest against [£] pretending for the gouernment of Champaigne, [?] hauing no desire it should nowe take place, the rather for that he hath renewed his intelligences in Amiens and Rheins by the meanes of the duke; the said townes…”

f. 112 (R7775). “[£2]: execute an enterprise vppon Rheins wch would be the ca[…]inge of the place and him. The King hath a disposition to geve him the Gouernment of Provence to embarque him against the D. Espernon…”

04 What stays open

  • f. 59, four cipher lines (I).
  • Who the person signs stand for. 9 = Soissons and £ = Guise fit the context (M); n is unknown.
  • Single words: “ca[…]inge” (f. 112), the sign before “hauing” (f. 109v), “hope” and “marriage” under blots (f. 50) (C).
  • Whether G. G. Butler, The Edmondes Papers (Roxburghe Club, 1913), prints any of these letters.
