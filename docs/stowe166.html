Henri IV’s court → Lord Treasurer Burghley · simple substitution in invented symbols, inline with clear English · 1592–1594

Thomas Edmondes to Burghley, 1592–1594

→

BL Stowe MS 166 ff. 50–61, 109–112 (DECODE R7770–R7775), catalogue item 140. The DECODE record names “Lord Threr?” as the author, but the Lord Treasurer is the addressee. The letters are reports from the King’s camp in Edmondes’s papers. The key was recovered here from the ciphertext alone, and every cipher passage is deciphered, 1,023 of 1,035 signs (98.8%); the last four lines, on f. 59, on 2 October 2026. An earlier decipherment exists: G. G. Butler printed five of the six letters, with the cipher passages deciphered, in The Edmondes Papers (1913). It was found on 30 September 2026, after the key had been recovered here.

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · complete

The letters
Six despatches to the Lord Treasurer, Burghley, from Henri IV’s court, January 1593 to July 1594, among the papers of Thomas Edmondes, Elizabeth’s agent in France; a few lines of each in cipher inside the clear English.
Where
British Library, Stowe MS 166 ff. 50–61 and 109–112; DECODE R7770–R7775, catalogued as “Lord Threr?” to an unknown recipient, not deciphered.
The cipher
A simple substitution of English in invented symbols, keeping the word divisions, with a few homophones and single symbols for persons.
How it was deciphered
Key recovered from the ciphertext alone, taking frequent cipher words as the, of and to; one key for all six letters. Every passage was transcribed letter for letter and measured on 3 October 2026, after f. 109v and f. 112 were checked again on the images.
What it says
The King breaking off his sister’s marriage to [Soissons?]; Montmorency’s demand for the custody of the young Prince of Condé; “that his faith is not vendible”; [Guise?] and Champagne in 1594.
Credit
G. G. Butler, The Edmondes Papers (1913), prints five of the six letters deciphered; found after the key was recovered here.
Still open
Who the eleven person symbols stand for (no key with names survives), and one unexplained sign on f. 109v.
Summary. The catalogue had five DECODE records of “Lord Threr? to unknown recipient”, 1592–94, marked Non-decrypted (no decipherment known). There is a sixth, R7771, in the same run. They are letters to the Lord Treasurer, Burghley, among the papers of Thomas Edmondes, Elizabeth’s agent with Henri IV. The writer reports what the King told him in person, and dates from Tours, Blois and the camp before Laon. In each letter a few lines are written in invented symbols inside the clear English. The cipher is a simple substitution of English, keeping word divisions, with a few homophones and single symbols for persons. The key was recovered from the ciphertext alone by taking frequent cipher words as the, of and to, and the same key serves all six letters, in two hands. Every passage is deciphered, 1,023 of the 1,035 cipher signs (98.8%); the last, four lines on f. 59, on 2 October 2026, with one new sign. The twelve signs not read are the person symbols and one unexplained sign. The passages include the King breaking off his sister’s marriage to [Soissons?], Montmorency’s demand for custody of the young Prince of Condé, a would-be purchaser told “that his faith is not vendible”, and [Guise?]’s designs on Champagne and Reims in 1594. Butler’s Edmondes Papers (1913) prints five of the six letters with the cipher deciphered. That was found only after the key had been recovered here, so this break is an independent re-solution. The letter on ff. 109–110 is the one Butler leaves out.

Edmondes wrote the English year, which began on 25 March, so the letters dated January to March 1592 belong to 1593 in modern reckoning. Butler prints them under 1593, and the bracketed dates are his.

The DECODE “sender” fields (John le Rios, John Symmons, “Permell of Dieppo”, “One of Dieppe”) come from endorsements. The letters themselves are written by someone at the King’s side, in Edmondes’s papers.

02 The key

A = δ, b = +, c = 2, d = ш, e = ʋ, f = 8, g = б/6, h = 4, i = ǂ, k = 7, l = 3, m = □, n = ȝ, o = α, p = 9, r = f, s = &, t = ω, u/v = ш or a small o, w = π, x = 5, y = λ. ш serves for d as well as u/v, and б for a as well as g. Code symbols for persons: 9 standing alone (probably the Count of Soissons, 1593); n (a suitor for Catherine de Bourbon; Love (2001), quoting the same sentence from the letter as Burghley received it, glosses him as the Duke of Montpensier (M)); £, £2 (probably the Duke of Guise, 1594); o, a small circle, twice on f. 109v (by context the King, I). Eleven occurrences in all.

03 The decipherments

f. 50 (R7770). “…the K. continueth his resolution to goe to Saumur to see his sister, not so much for that cause (as it pleaseth himself to tell me) as especiallye to breake of the marriage betwene her and [9] wch he tould me he is resolued not to permitt but to geive her vnto [n] of whom he said he hath great hope that he will render himself of the religion. [9] is herewth enraged and extreemelie discontented.”

f. 57 (R7771). “…by the Pope, will appeare vnto yor L. by the enclosed. [9]: is glad to here of, hoping to be no further troubled wth the clamor of the Catholikes for the seekinge of him.”

f. 58v (R7772). “I doe secretelie learne that Monsr Montmorancy hath made great instance, as a condition whereof he required the performance before he would come, to haue the yong prince of Condé deliuered into the handes of Madame d’Angolesme his sister in law to be vnder his custodie.”

f. 59 (R7772), deciphered 2 October 2026. The four cipher lines at the head of f. 59 continue that sentence: “and by consequence popis[h]lie nourished, pretending so to appertaine to the place of the Constable, to haue the gouer[n]ment of the younge heire of the crowne, but wherein he hath nothing effected, the K. being resolued not to assent thereunto.” The young heir of the crown is the Prince of Condé of f. 58v. They were transcribed from the full-resolution DECODE image and read with the key unchanged, 126 signs for 126 letters, except for one sign that occurs only here, a vertical stroke for q in consequence. The fragments recorded before, “papistes” and an “effected” in cipher, were misreadings: the cipher word is popislie, and effected is in the clear ending. All twenty distinct words of the decipherment stand, in Butler’s modern spelling (popishly, appertain, government), on his p. 56 in the HathiTrust word data, against ten on the next best page of the volume; his page text itself could not be opened.

f. 60v (R7773). “…but the K. thereuppon saide vnto me that his faith is not vendible.”

f. 109v (R7774), lines 1–2 checked again on the image on 3 October 2026. After the clear “and whereas,”: “[9]: of whome especiallie [o] hath made choise expreslie to negotiate therein, for the difficulties of empeachement wch he knoweth he will geive to the same in respect of his owne interest against [£] pretending for the gouernment of Champaigne, [o] hauing no desire it should nowe take place, the rather for that he hath renewed his intelligences in Amiens and Rheins by the meanes of the duke [3:] the said townes…” The earlier reading began at choise; the symbol before hauing, left as [?], is the same small circle as in line 1, a person symbol.

f. 112 (R7775). “[£2]: execute an enterprise vppon Rheins wch would be the carryinge of the place and him (the r and y written as one ligature, re-read on the image on 3 October 2026). The King hath a disposition to geve him the Gouernment of Provence to embarque him against the D. Espernon…”

04 What stays open

  • Who the person symbols stand for. 9 = Soissons and £ = Guise fit the context (M); n is the Duke of Montpensier in Love’s gloss (M). o, twice on f. 109v, is by context the King (I). No key with names survives: Tomokiyo’s Elizabethan tables have no Edmondes cipher, Unton’s cypher of 1591 is a different system, and DECODE has no Edmondes key record (checked 2 October 2026); HMC Salisbury IV and Birch (1749) print no decipherment naming them, and the Calendar of State Papers Foreign for 1592–94 is not online. F. 59 has no person sign. These eleven signs and the “3:” of f. 109v are the twelve of 1,035 not read.
  • The new sign on f. 59, a vertical stroke for q, occurs once (C).
  • The sign “3:” before the said townes on f. 109v (after the clear “by the meanes of the duke”) is not explained. “Hope” and “marriage” on f. 50 are under blots (C). Measured on 3 October 2026 over a letter-for-letter transcription of every passage (reading.txt, measure.py): 1,023 of 1,035 signs read, 98.8%, and 99.9% leaving out the person symbols. The earlier figure of 95% was an estimate.
  • Butler’s wording, pp. 46–59 and 148–151, to be compared with the decipherments here. HathiTrust refuses this project’s automated requests, so the check so far rests on HathiTrust’s page-by-page word counts. The words of every passage deciphered here stand on the matching Butler page (all 17 for f. 58v; a few others are garbled by the OCR of his italics), next to a note on the cypher.
