Rome, Venice, Lisbon → the French court · symbol substitution · 1551–1558 · read in part

La Guiche from Rome, 1551, with Noailles and Seure, 1558

BnF français 3138 no. 22 and français 3151 nos. 33 and 39–44: three ambassadors’ letters with ciphered passages, catalogued together as item 10. The La Guiche passage had no decipherment. It was transcribed and broken here.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved in part 21 September 2026

solved in part

Summary. The catalogue grouped three letters with cipher that had no decipherment noted. Each was checked for a court decipherment on the leaf or nearby. Claude de La Guiche, bishop of Mirepoix, wrote to Montmorency from Rome on 22 November 1551 with about thirteen lines of cipher inside the clear text, and nothing deciphers them. The 406 signs were transcribed. There are 22 distinct signs, one for each letter of the period’s 22-letter alphabet, and a permutation annealer with a French language model of the 1530–1600 despatches broke the alphabet. The passage reads in substance. Dom Diego [Hurtado de Mendoza] has had a Sienese imprisoned, and La Guiche has seen the trial papers. The Sienese envoy has left Rome for Siena with Dom Diego. The Count of Santa Fiora « est mal satisfaict » and « facilement se reduiroit au service du roy », on the conditions he would ask. One name and the two struck-through opening lines remain uncertain. François de Noailles’s Venice letter of 13 November 1558 has the court’s decipherment in the inner margin, partly lost in the binding. The chevalier de Seure’s letters from Lisbon, December 1558, are not one letter but six, including duplicates. They hold about 2,000 signs of a homophonic cipher with numeral codes and no decipherment, and are left open as their own target.

01 The three letters

  • La Guiche → the duc de Montmorency, Rome, 22 November 1551, fr. 3138 no. 22, f. 60v–61r. The clear text is about cardinals and the Pope’s intentions during the Parma war. It is interrupted by six cipher runs, 406 signs in all. There is no decipherment on the leaf, in the margin or on the following leaf.
  • Noailles → the Cardinal de Lorraine, Venice, 13 November 1558, fr. 3151 no. 33, f. 59–60. The letter is in clear (galleys, Candia, Corfu), with two cipher blocks of three lines on f. 60r. Each has its decipherment written in the inner margin.
  • Seure → de Fresne and Henri II, Lisbon, 12 and 27 December 1558, fr. 3151 nos. 39–44. The catalogue listed one letter, no. 39, but the volume holds six, and nos. 40/41 and 43/44 are duplicates. The cipher runs over some eight pages.

02 La Guiche: the reading

Normalised spelling. Clear words from the letter are in italics, [?] marks an uncertain reading, and … marks signs left unread.

[struck through: Dom Diego … constituer prisonnier en ce … un Siennois … croy que …] le procès duquel j’ay trouvé moien de veoir, affin de informer led. Sr cardinal du contenu en icelluy pour selon cela mettre tel fondement qu’il advisera. … de ceux de Sienes qui estoit icy, lequel est party pour s’en retorner a Sienes avec Dom Diego. … Le conte [de] Sainte Fiore est mal satisfaict, et que facilement se reduiroit au service du roy … Car ledict personnage qui estoit icy tractant avec moy la faire du sieur +arna+e … vous informer du … que le roy pourroit avoir du dict sieur +arna+e, et aux [?] … des conditions qu’il demanderoit.

“Dom Diego” is Diego Hurtado de Mendoza, Charles V’s ambassador in Rome and the power behind Siena until the Sienese rising of July 1552. The count of Santa Fiora is the Sforza lord, whose family were the pope’s relations. The letter shows La Guiche sounding him out for the French side eight months before Siena turned to France.

03 Method

The cipher was transcribed sign by sign from full-resolution crops of each line (guiche_ct.txt). The counts pointed to a simple substitution: 22 distinct signs, with one sign at 16%, the rate of French e. A free many-to-one annealer collapsed onto a degenerate i/x solution. A permutation annealer with swap moves, using the fr-1530-despatches 5-gram model without spaces, read le conte sainctefiore est mal satisfaict on its best restart. Four signs were then set from context (qu, p, y, v), and the rest re-annealed. Key: 3=e, t=r, #=i, θ=u, the stemmed 4=t, barred p=s, hook=a, f=d, d=n, open 4=o, curled d=o, p=c, Ⅲ=l, ff=qu, overbarred d=m, φ=y, the circle=v, hooked q=f, ∞=p.

04 Noailles, 1558: deciphered at the time

The glosses can be read only in part, because the start of each line runs into the binding: « … mauluaise … gouvernement … affaires », « … grand seigneur … ne luy … de la fortune … ». The letter was read when it arrived. A complete text would need the volume opened flat.

05 Seure, 1558: open

Seure’s six letters mix clear French (the Indies fleet, twenty-two ships at Havana, the queen of Portugal’s confessor) with long blocks in about sixty invented signs and numeral groups such as 12, 23 and 100: a homophonic cipher with a small nomenclator. None has a decipherment. There are roughly 2,000 signs under what looks like one key, so a solve is realistic, but the transcription alone is a job of several sessions. The letters stay in the catalogue as their own item.

06 What remains uncertain

  • La Guiche: the name « sieur +arna+e » (signs t r t d r t 3; the barred-cross sign read r elsewhere may stand for a second letter here); two rare signs (1–2 occurrences) unassigned; the struck-through first two lines read only in fragments; the Ⅲ sign is l everywhere but gives g in « Diego ». Key grade C; the text is grade M where marked.
  • Noailles: the margin decipherment is incomplete; the cipher was not attacked.
  • Seure: not transcribed.
