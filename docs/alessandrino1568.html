Cardinal Alessandrino to the nuncio in Spain, 1568–69

→

Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Spagna 6/I items 1–10 and 6/II item 13 · DECODE R93–R102, R115, eleven ciphertexts, fifteen pages · catalogued as partially decrypted

George Lasry’s reconstructed key, applied with a decoder that picks one of each digit’s two letters · all eleven read end to end

Summary. Michele Bonelli, Cardinal Alessandrino, was the nephew of Pius V and ran his correspondence. His letters of 1568 to the nuncio at Madrid, Giovan Battista Castagna, sent their sensitive passages in figures. Ten of these ciphers (Spagna 6/I) and one of 1569 (6/II) are on DECODE, transcribed digit by digit, with a key George Lasry reconstructed in 2020 but no plaintext; the catalogue here listed them as open. The system is polyphonic: every digit from 1 to 9 stands for two letters (1 is u or t, 4 is a or l), 0 is a null, and a small nomenclator is written in four-digit groups with zeros in second and fourth place. A digit therefore does not say which letter it is; the reader has to choose. A beam search that makes that choice under an Italian model of the period gives running Italian at once, on all eleven. What they say: an approach, through friends of the French crown, to marry Philip II to one of the French King’s daughters; the Pope’s complaints about the King’s ministers and ecclesiastical jurisdiction; the Bolognese exiles sheltered in the King’s lands; the Turk at Adrianople courted by the German Protestants; Cardinal Borromeo’s quarrel with the senate of Milan over the Corpus Domini procession. What stays open is a few dotted groups outside Lasry’s list, single-letter choices, and the comparison with the printed correspondence (Serrano 1914), which could not be reached from here.

01 The documents and the key

Spagna 6/I is described by the archive as “Lettere orig. e cifre del cardinale Alessandrino dal 16 gennaio 1568”. DECODE has ten ciphertexts from it (items 1–10: ff. 6r, 42r, 55r, 81r, 88r–89v, 136r, 169r–v, 291r–v, 306r; item 8 has no folio on DECODE), 9,842 digits in all, and one from 6/II (item 13, f. 641r, 1569; 769 digits). None is dated on DECODE; the dossier’s folio order places 6/I in 1568. The transcriptions were made for DECODE in 2016–2020 and are used here as they stand.

Lasry’s key, attached to every record:

  • Letters: 1 u|t · 2 i|m · 3 f|g · 4 a|l · 5 o|s · 6 e|n · 7 d|r · 8 b|c · 9 p|z · 0 null. There is no sign for h or q.
  • Nomenclator: 1010 Nostro Signore · 1020, 4010, 9010 Sua Santità · 1050 Francia · 1090 V. S. · 2060 non · 2090 che · 3010 Re · 4030 S. M.tà · 4060 questo · 4090, 9030 Germania · 5020 per · 5090 qui · 6090 quel · 7090 quello · 8010 Re di Francia · 9020 quelle · 9060 questa; dotted 0̇0 comma, 3 0̇ 9 qua; and a few tentative (1040 Regina di Francia?, 3040 Fiandra?, 8090 sapere?).

02 A French bride for the King

The first cipher (f. 6r), set out from the decoder’s unspaced output with the words divided:

… è ricercato qui da alcuni amorevoli della corona di Francia a fare officio … [che S. M.tà] contenti di ricevere una delle sue figliole per moglie … la pratica N. S. non ha voluto movere non sapendo l’animo di [S. M.tà] in quel particolare … scoprirà qui [V. S.] in quella maniera che le parerà più opportuna e conveniente, assicurandola che [N. S.] non è per farne parola mai se prima non sa [di S. M.tà] l’intentione sua espressa …

“Some well-wishers of the crown of France have asked here that [the Pope] act … that His Majesty be content to take one of [the King of France’s] daughters to wife. Our Lord has not wished to move the matter without knowing His Majesty’s mind … you will discover it in whatever way seems best, assuring him that Our Lord will never speak of it before he knows his intention.” Elisabeth of Valois died on 3 October 1568; Philip’s eventual fourth wife was Anna of Austria, and the French candidate pressed at Rome was Marguerite of Valois. The fourth cipher (f. 81r) is still “waiting for the answer from Your Lordship on the particular of the marriage”, with the argument that the union of “quei tre principi principali della cristianità” would serve the Church.

03 All eleven, in brief

  • 6/I/1, f. 6r (R93). The French marriage proposal, above.
  • 6/I/2, f. 42r (R94). Jurisdiction: the Pope “non intende di revocare i suoi privilegi … ma sibene di levare li abusi cresciuti” through the ministers’ fault; they have sought to widen the privileges “in detrimento di quella autorità” of the Holy See.
  • 6/I/3, f. 55r (R95). Answer to the ministers’ claims; the crusade alms and indulgence; “che il Re si saria alienato da quella santa sede” must not be said; the Pope cares for the King’s soul above any human respect.
  • 6/I/4, f. 81r (R96). The marriage again; a man prosecuted as a heretic; an order to be carried out for the Hermits of St Jerome.
  • 6/I/5, ff. 88r–89v (R97), four pages. Exiles of the state of Bologna received in the King’s name, the Cavaliere Gualengo; the King, “feudatario” of the Holy See, bound by his investiture to return them; then the Turk: a French embassy “in Andrinopoli”, the Protestant princes promising the Sultan that once all is ruined they will be at one with him — “la povera cristianità”.
  • 6/I/6, f. 136r (R98). The King’s orders about the clergy lose their effect while ministers wait for commissions.
  • 6/I/7, f. 169r–v (R99). The religion of St John and its Grand Master; Don John of Austria; the senate of Milan, the archbishop’s vicar and the Corpus Domini procession; the Pope “pensa di procedere” with “armi spirituali”.
  • 6/I/8 (R100). The King’s ministers’ proceedings in a Church matter; a brief to be executed.
  • 6/I/9, f. 291r–v (R101). The St Peter’s-day dues of the Neapolitan fief and the investiture; a schism stirred by the ministers; “alla fine” the King’s subjects will be disobedient if he is disobedient to God’s vicar.
  • 6/I/10, f. 306r (R102). A prelate who fails in his office in the kingdom; the Pope “le parerà convenire” to leave it to the King.
  • 6/II/13, f. 641r (R115), 1569. Pensions to be transferred on a see; a commission; the Council.

04 Method

Each digit 1–9 gives two letters and 0 nothing, but a group X0Y0 may be a nomenclator word or two letters with their nulls. The decoder (alessandrino1568/decrypt.py) runs a beam search over the page, at each digit trying both letters, the null, and any nomenclator group that starts there, and scores the resulting Italian with the repository’s shared five-gram model (it-cinquecento, spaces removed). The same code, with a different key table, read the Morosini ciphers of 1588–89. Every page decodes at about −1.75 nats a letter, the level of ordinary text for this model, and ends in a few letters of clerk’s padding.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Letter choice is the model’s (grade M for single letters); words forced by context are H. Proper names are the weakest part.
  • Dotted groups not in Lasry’s list: 01, 02, 03, 04, 08, 09, 1, 40, 43, 44, 51, 60, 70, 80, 90. 04 and 60 recur where a comma or et fits. Not resolved.
  • Lasry’s tentative glosses (1040, 3040, 8090, 2020) are kept as tentative.
  • No comparison yet with the contemporary decifrati or with Serrano’s edition, which prints Alessandrino–Castagna letters; some of these letters may already be in print there in clear.
  • Transcriptions are DECODE’s and were not re-checked against the images.
