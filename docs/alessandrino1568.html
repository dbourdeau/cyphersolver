Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Spagna 6/I items 1–10 and 6/II item 13 · DECODE R93–R102, R115, eleven ciphertexts, fifteen pages · catalogued as partially decrypted

George Lasry’s key (2020, on the DECODE records) was applied. Because it is polyphonic, the decryption is ambiguous: for every digit one of two letters has to be chosen, and the work here is making those choices well, with a search scored by a language model of 16th-century Italian. In October 2026 the contemporary decipherments of ten letters were found among the DECODE images and checked against the figures; they extended the key, which deciphered the eleventh · all eleven give Italian sentences; nine reach the 95% bar, while R99 (94.7% of its words read as sense) and R115 (88.5%) build coherent sentences under it · Lasry posted no plaintext with the key

The letters
Cardinal Alessandrino (Michele Bonelli, nephew of Pius V) to the Madrid nuncio Castagna, 1568: ten ciphers, plus one of 1569.
Where
Spagna 6/I and 6/II. DECODE has the transcriptions, 9,842 digits in 6/I, with a key but no plaintext; the catalogue here listed them as open.
The cipher
Polyphonic: each digit 1–9 stands for two letters, 0 is a null, and a small nomenclator uses four-digit groups with zeros in second and fourth place.
How it was read
Lasry’s 2020 key, with a search choosing between the two letters of each digit, scored by a model of 16th-century Italian. It gives continuous Italian on all eleven.
What it says
An approach to marry Philip II to a French princess, the Pope’s complaints about the King’s ministers, the Bolognese exiles, and Borromeo’s quarrel with the Milan senate.
Still open
A few dotted groups outside Lasry’s list, single-letter choices, and the comparison with Serrano’s 1914 print, which could not be reached.

01 The documents and the key

Spagna 6/I is described by the archive as “Lettere orig. e cifre del cardinale Alessandrino dal 16 gennaio 1568”. DECODE has ten ciphertexts from it (items 1–10: ff. 6r, 42r, 55r, 81r, 88r–89v, 136r, 169r–v, 291r–v, 306r; item 8 has no folio on DECODE), 9,842 digits in all, and one from 6/II (item 13, f. 641r, 1569; 769 digits). None is dated on DECODE; the dossier’s folio order places 6/I in 1568, but R94’s covering letter (f. 44r, Rome 7 November 1567, “Con questa un poco di cifra”) dates that one to 1567, and R96’s covering letter is of 19 May 1568. The transcriptions were made for DECODE in 2016–2020 and are used here as they stand.

Lasry’s key, attached to every record:

  • Letters: 1 u|t · 2 i|m · 3 f|g · 4 a|l · 5 o|s · 6 e|n · 7 d|r · 8 b|c · 9 p|z · 0 null. There is no sign for h or q.
  • Nomenclator: 1010 Nostro Signore · 1020, 4010, 9010 Sua Santità · 1050 Francia · 1090 V. S. · 2060 non · 2090 che · 3010 Re · 4030 S. M.tà · 4060 questo · 4090, 9030 Germania · 5020 per · 5090 qui · 6090 quel · 7090 quello · 8010 Re di Francia · 9020 quelle · 9060 questa; dotted 0̇0 comma, 3 0̇ 9 qua; and a few tentative (1040 Regina di Francia?, 3040 Fiandra?, 8090 sapere?).

The contemporary decipherments correct and extend it: 9 also stands for con, dotted 0 for z, 0̇0 for et; 1040 is lettere (not Regina di Francia), 8090 havere, 4010 Re di Francia (not Sua Santità), 2010 Imperatore, and 1020 esser, 3020 quando, 3060 -mente, 4020 come, 4040 Milano, 4080 et, 5070, 6050 and 7070 Imbasciatore, 6060 Inquisitione, 7030 Cardinale, 7040 galere, 8020 avviso, 8070 Ugonotti, 3050 and 6030 Italia, 9090 havendo, 6020 causa. The extended key is in key.md.

02 A marriage for the King of France

The first cipher (f. 6r), as deciphered from the figures and the contemporary decipherment on f. 8:

N.S. è ricercato qui da alcuni amorevoli della corona di Francia a fare officio con Imperatore perché si contenti dare una delle sue figliole per moglie al Re di Francia; la qual pratica N.S. non ha voluta movere, non sapendo l'animo di Re in questa parte. Però V.S. potrà scoprirlo con quella maniera che le parerà più opportuna et conveniente, asecurandola che N.S. non è per farne parola mai se prima non saperà qual sia l'intentione sua espressamente; et saputa che […] l'habbia, mi ne darà avviso con la prima commodità.

“His Holiness is asked here by some friends of the crown of France to use his good offices with the Emperor so that he be pleased to give one of his daughters as wife to the King of France; His Holiness has not wished to set this negotiation moving without knowing the King’s mind in this matter. Your Lordship will therefore find it out … assuring him that His Holiness will never say a word of it before he knows what his intention is …” The September decryption had taken the codes 2010 and 4010 with Lasry’s tentative values and made this a French bride for Philip II; the decipherment gives the Emperor and the King of France. Charles IX married the Emperor Maximilian II’s daughter Elisabeth of Austria in 1570. The fourth cipher (f. 81r, R96) is still waiting for an answer “sopra il particolare del matrimonio”, judging that it might bring “qualche bona unione o lega tra questi tre principi principali della Christianità”.

03 All eleven, in brief

Each letter’s full Italian text and English translation are in the Full texts section below; the quotations are from those readings.

  • 6/I/1, f. 6r (R93). The French marriage request, above.
  • 6/I/2, f. 42r (R94), November 1567. Jurisdiction: the Pope “non intende di revocare i suoi privilegii antichi, ma sì bene di levare li abusi cresciuti in essi per colpa delli suoi ministri”; if the King strips the Holy See of its authority, the Pope will resist “non con forze temporali” but with spiritual ones, “usque ad effusionem sanguinis”.
  • 6/I/3, f. 55r (R95). The French ambassador’s claim about raising troops in the state of Milan; the crusade alms and indulgence; the man who said “che il Re si saria alienato da questa santa Sede”; the Pope grieves more for the King’s soul than for any human respect.
  • 6/I/4, f. 81r (R96). The marriage again; Scipione [?Cuttinardo], who aspires to be Regent of the Collateral, indicted by the Holy Office as a heretic; the order against the Hermits of St Jerome.
  • 6/I/5, ff. 88r–89v (R97), four pages. The Duke of Ferrara, offended over exiles of the contado of Bologna, may complain to the King through Count Ercole Tassone; the Duke is the Holy See’s “feudatario”, bound to return its exiles. The French ambassador at Constantinople offers to join the Huguenots and the Protestants with the Turks, and at Adrianople urges Piali Pasha to come with a great fleet against Rome: “la povera Christianità”.
  • 6/I/6, f. 136r (R98). The King’s orders to his ministers in Italy against a Huguenot move lose their effect while the ministers wait for new commissions; the nuncio is to ask for express orders.
  • 6/I/7, f. 169r–v (R99). The Order of St Lazarus, its Grand Master and two galleys from Gian Andrea Doria; Don John of Austria’s opposition; in Milan the senate’s threats to the archbishop’s vicar before the Corpus Domini procession: the Pope “pensa di procedere in questa causa contra di loro con quelle armi spirituali che il signor Dio gli ha date”. The letter builds coherent sentences but reads at 94.7%, under the 95% bar.
  • 6/I/8 (R100). The King’s ministers obstruct the Inquisition of Catalonia; the nuncio is to execute the brief.
  • 6/I/9, f. 291r–v (R101). The chinea and the St Peter’s-day dues accepted under the fisc’s protest; devolution under the investitures of Julius II and Julius III; “un manifesto scisma in quello regno”, bishops’ notaries and executors imprisoned; “chi è disobediente a Dio et al suo vicario non sarà meraviglia se perderà la obedientia de' suoi sudditi”.
  • 6/I/10, f. 306r (R102). Cardinal [Espinosa] fails in his office with the King in the service of the Holy See, and the Pope has judged him “indegno di quella dignità che tiene”; the nuncio may let him know, if amendment can be hoped for.
  • 6/II/13, f. 641r (R115), 1569. The nomination of Don [?Francesco] de Reinoso to the see of Monreale, and the transfer to it of pensions on the archdeaconry of Toledo. It builds coherent sentences but reads at 88.5%, under the 95% bar.

04 Method

Each digit 1–9 gives two letters and 0 nothing, but a group X0Y0 may be a nomenclator word or two letters with their nulls. The decoder (targets/alessandrino1568/decrypt.py) works through the page keeping the best few partial decryptions at each step; at each digit it tries both letters, the null, and any nomenclator group that starts there, and scores the resulting Italian with the repository’s shared five-gram model (it-cinquecento, spaces removed). The same code, with a different key table, decrypted the Morosini ciphers of 1588–89. Every page decodes at about −1.75 nats a letter, the level of ordinary text for this model, and ends in a few letters of clerk’s padding.

That score did not make the text a reading. Divided into words, the beam output had corrupted runs (“liberagemptume”, “unmannifestsobismain”) and many single letters chosen by the model, and the reading audit of 22–23 October 2026 classed the letters decoded, not read until each was checked. The records’ DECODE “cleartext” images were then opened one by one: R94 (f. 45r), R95 (f. 58r) and R100 (under the cipher, f. 280r) on 22 October, and R93 (f. 8), R96 (f. 80), R97 (f. 90r–v), R98 (f. 139), R99 (a leaf after f. 169), R102 (f. 307) and R115 (a half-sheet with f. 641) on 23 October each hold the contemporary decipherment. Each was transcribed and aligned word by word with the figures (reread_aless_align.py); where the decipherer and the figures differ, the figures decide. R101 has no decipherment; it was decoded again with the extended key and deciphered word by word on the figures.

05 What remains uncertain

  • R94: two short gaps the contemporary decipherer also left (the group 9 0 4 0 2 0 before S.M., and a verb after “comincia”).
  • R95: the dotted groups [08.] (the Crusade?) and [3.] (a person), and two words where the decipherment is illegible.
  • R101: two places under a burn and a hole in the paper, one illegible group, the place-name [?Sora] and code 6020.
  • R93, R96–R99, R102, R115: short runs and codes the decipherers left blank or gave words the figures do not carry: one group in R93; the heretic’s surname in R96; four runs in R97 (“Duca di Ferrara” and “Principe di Condé” rest on the decipherer’s word); [?possa] and [?et] in R98; the stretch after d’Oria and the head of the Milan paragraph in R99 (code 2020 open); three words in R102; codes 4050, 2010 (where the decipherer has N.S.), 9070 and four runs in R115, whose last sentence is only partly carried.
  • Dotted groups [01.], [03.], [4.]8 and others are partly or not resolved.
  • No comparison yet with Serrano’s edition, which prints Alessandrino–Castagna letters (not reachable from here).
  • Transcriptions are DECODE’s; where the decipherer and the figures differ the place was checked on the image.

## Reading

N.S. è ricercato qui da alcuni amorevoli della corona di Francia a fare officio con Imperatore perché si contenti
dare una delle sue figliole per moglie al Re di Francia; la qual pratica N.S. non ha voluta movere, non sapendo

conveniente, asecurandola che N.S. non è per farne parola mai se prima non saperà qual sia l'intentione sua

## Notes on the figures

- "è ricercato": 1010 6 0 7 2 8 6 7 8 4 1 5; "qui" 5 0 9 0; "Francia" 1 0 5 0, then 4 = "a".
- "con Imperatore": 0 9 (con) 0 2 0 1 0 — 2010 is the Emperor (the decipherer "l'Imper.re"; R96's "l'Imp.re").
- "perché": dotted 4 + 8, as in R95. The decipherer's "S.M." after it has no figures.
- "al Re di Francia": figures 4 0 4 0 1 0 between "moglie" (2 5 3 4 2 6) and "la qual" (4 4 0 3 0 9 4): 4 = a,
  then 4010 = Re di Francia, as in R96 ("tra Imperatore et Re di Francia"), not Lasry's "Sua Santità".
- "l'animo di Re in questa": 4 6 2 2 5 0 7 2 0 3 0 1 2 6 0 6090 — 301 is 3010 Re written X0Y.
- "espressamente": 6 5 9 7 6 5 4 3 0 6 0 (3060 = -mente); the decipherer split it "espres|sam.te".
- "che […] l'habbia": 2 0 9 | 6 5 4 0 4 4 8 2 4; the decipherer wrote "che l'habbia"; 6 5 4 not read.
- "avviso": 8 0 2 0 (8020, new; also in R101 and R99).
- End: 0 9 3 7 9 3 is the clerk's filler.

# R97 — English (Cardinal Alessandrino to the nuncio, cipher, Rome 1568)

Same holes as R97_reading.md. Modern spelling; codes as names.

It is known for certain that the Duke of Ferrara, out of the dissatisfaction he has perhaps felt with His Holiness in
a matter of the exiles of the county of Bologna, dealt with here in his name by the Cavaliere Gualengo, intends to
make some complaint of it to the King; and if Count Ercole Tassone comes to your court he will probably have a
particular commission to speak to him on this matter. That office ought not to be done by this ambassador,
considering the fatherly affection His Holiness bears him and the little respect shown on his side to this Holy See,
of which he is a feudatary, not observing the terms of his investiture, and in particular one that obliges him to
hand over the exiles of this state whenever they are asked of him. […] I wanted Your Lordship to know, so that if you
hear it spoken of you can tell the King to be pleased to keep an ear for His Holiness, who will justify himself in
such a way that he does not doubt he can satisfy all the princes, if it comes to hearing their reasons; but if you do
not hear it spoken of, there is no need to take any step, and you will be […] watchful while the said man stays at
court, and keep me informed of what happens from time to time.

It would be well to see, through His Holiness, that that bad ambassador who is there in the name of the King of France
be removed, who does so many ill offices for Christendom that if he is tolerated he puts it in great danger, as he
offers to unite the Huguenots with the Protestants in a league with the Turks to attack […] and besiege poor
Christendom from every side; and to establish that league he affirms that ambassadors of the Prince of Condé and of the
Protestants will soon be in Constantinople, promising that after everything has been ruined and destroyed they will
all unite and be all of one faith with the Turks. This talk, which at another time would have been laughable, is now
much to be feared for afflicted Christendom; so that by removing so bad an instrument from there, besides the King of
France showing that these offices are not done with his consent, no small benefit would be done to the affairs of
Christendom.

And by another route it is likewise understood that in Adrianople the ambassador [of] the King of France has done many
ill offices with Piali Pasha, general of the sea, against His Holiness, against the Emperor and against Italy, urging
him to procure from the Sultan to come with a very great fleet to seize Rome and His Holiness; and he has done this
not only as ambassador [of] France but also on commission from the Protestant princes, promising that ambassadors on
their part will soon be there […], and offering that in Italy countless Huguenots would easily and quickly be sent to
corrupt the minds of the Italians.

R98 (ASV, Segreteria di Stato, Spagna 6/I, item 6, f. 136r (DECODE R98), unknown)

The King's orders to his ministers in Italy against a Huguenot move into Italy are good, but ministers delay waiting for new commissions; the nuncio is to press for express orders now, so that the Pope can call on them in time for the security of Italy.

# R115 — Cardinal Alessandrino to the nuncio Castagna, cipher of [Rome, January 1569] (Spagna 6/II/13, f. 641r): reading

Rests on: the contemporary decipherment on a half-sheet bound with the cipher (`R115_decifrato_f641bis.txt`, DECODE
cleartext image DOC_R115_D1340), found 23 Oct 2026 (r2g), and on the figures, read word by word against it
(`python reread_aless_align.py decode/DOC_R115_D1685_1685.txt 641r.jpg texts/R115_reading.md`). The decipherer's draft
is fast and loose; where it and the figures differ the figures are followed: "ricercare" (he "ricercai"), "et il
consenso ancora" (he "il [assenso] anchora"), "che haveva imposte" (he "et gravezza imposte"), "avisandomi [?ancora] del
vero [?…]", "non manchi di parlarne con Re", "et avisarmi poi", "la tratta di costà", "per quelli mezi che pareranno
migliori a Re". Code values: 6020 causa / negocio (as in R99), 2010 here where the decipherer wrote "N.S." (the Pope's
scalco; elsewhere 2010 is the Emperor: noted, not resolved), 4050 not identified, 9070 where he wrote "risposta".
The person is Francisco de Reinoso, Pius V's scalco segreto, named to the see of Monreale (Sicily, a royal
nomination) and later bishop of Córdoba. Date: the clear letters on the record are of January 1572, but the content
(the request made "two months ago, the last of November") fits the Monreale nomination of 1568-69; the record's own
dating (Spagna 6/II, item 13, 1569) is kept.
Marks: [?word] partly carried, […] unread, [word] supplied, [NNNN] an unidentified code.
