St Petersburg → Warsaw · one-part code, table 3865 · 12 September 1786 · key learnt from paired clear texts, read in part

Deboli to Stanisław August, St Petersburg, 12 September 1786

Go to the full text ↓

The Polish envoy in St Petersburg tells the King he has handed the Vice-Chancellor his observations on Prince Czartoryski’s memorandum, in a code whose key was rebuilt here from other despatches and their clear texts.

Method: key recovered based on plaintext from external sources · Extent: partial (90% of the cipher) · On the map

At a glanceKey from external plaintext · partial (90% of the cipher)

The letter
Antoni Augustyn Deboli, Polish envoy at St Petersburg, to King Stanisław August, 12 September 1786: the first page (16 lines, 227 groups) of a fair-copy despatch, in cipher from end to end.
Where
AGAD Warsaw, Zbiór Popielów (zesp. 12), sygn. 2, scan 146 right. Catalogue entry 415. The cabinet’s clear text of the time is in Archiwum Królestwa Polskiego 269, scans 253 right – 254.
The cipher
Table 3865, a one-part code of 1–4 digit groups for letters, syllables, endings, words and names, with nulls and suffixed variants (1504z differs from 1504). No key of the time was found.
How it was read
A key (K3, 1,287 entries) was learnt by script on 16 other cipher pieces paired with their clears, frozen and hashed before this page was transcribed. On this page it reads 0.645 of the words of the clear, against at most 0.020 for 200 shuffled keys: partial, under the 0.85 set in advance.
What it says
Deboli has given Vice-Chancellor Ostermann his written observations on Prince Czartoryski’s memorandum and sends them to the King, with a passage added for Ambassador Stackelberg; Sedler presented the memorandum only on the Sunday.
Still open
6 unresolved groups and 17 restored from the clear; the key is not validated; the rest of the despatch (scan 147) is not read.
Credit
Brice Dudout (L’Europe chiffrée) learnt the key, transcribed and read the page, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents. The Deboli entries and the leads to AKP 269 are Daniel Bourdeau’s.

01 The page

Volume 2 of the Zbiór Popielów, “Deboli 1786”, holds the envoy’s cipher despatches of that year. Scan 146 right is the first page of the despatch of 12 September 1786, a fair copy without corrections, opening “3865 9” and in table 3865 throughout: 16 lines, 227 groups. The despatch continues on scan 147, which was not transcribed. The images on szukajwarchiwach were used for private research and are not reproduced here, so this page shows the cipher as numbers only.

The cabinet’s clear text of this despatch survives in AKP 269 (header “St Petersbourg ce 12. 7bre 1786. reçue le 22.”). It was used to measure the reading and to restore groups the key cannot give, and is not published here. The content is therefore not new; what is new is the system, table 3865, used by Deboli in 1786–87, and a reusable key for it.

02 The key and its tests

Table 3865 is a one-part code of 1–4 digit groups, with nulls (3865 and 9 as openers, 7, 12 and others) and suffixed variants (a, b, x, z). Its alphabet has no j, v or y (i = j = y, u = v). No key of the time was found among the key collections of AGAD (Zbiór Popielów 444, 451, 445/452, 453/454).

K3 was learnt by script on 16 cipher pieces paired with their clears in AKP 269, with variants and nulls learnt without feedback; its predicted coverage was 0.918. Four rounds were pre-registered, one measurement each, with 200 shuffled-key controls:

  • R1 (key K1): 0.591, against the 0.60 then required: failed.
  • R2 (key K2): 0.598: failed.
  • R3 (key K3): 0.519: failed; one held-out piece turned out to be a corrected draft whose cipher follows the struck-out first state of the text.
  • R4 (K3 unchanged, this page): 0.645 against at most 0.020, a margin of 0.625: partial (the margin passes, 0.85 is not reached).

On clean copies K3 levels off at 0.63–0.65. The misses are mostly truncated values (reso, exe, uau) and spelling variants (auoit/avois) that the measure does not join, rather than wrong values, which is why the reading by sense is higher than the measure.

03 The reading

The page was re-read group by group. A group is certain when its K3 value equals the aligned letters of the clear, probable when it is read by sense only, restored when K3 has no value or the clear contradicts it. Of 219 groups (without the nulls 3865, 9 and 7): 163 certain, 33 probable, 17 restored, 6 unresolved. Read by sense without the restored groups: 196 of 219, 89.5%, under the 95% bar. In the text, {braces} mark probable words, [brackets] restored ones and ‹…› unresolved ones.

Moi je n’ai fait que l’eplucher, eu [egard] [à la] necessité qu’il [y] a eu d’[ob]vier à ce qu’{on} ne ‹pri›t l’e‹change› sur {le} dit memoire. Ce ne fut que [dimanche] [que] Mr de Sedler l’[a] presenté au [ViceChancelier], et comme j’{avois} guetté …

In short: as announced in his last despatch, Deboli has handed the Vice-Chancellor his written observations on Prince Czartoryski’s memorandum and sends the King the full text. He added a passage aimed at Ambassador Stackelberg, so that Stackelberg sees the matter can be well received in St Petersburg only if their ideas agree, or with the corrections Stackelberg makes. Sedler presented the memorandum to the Vice-Chancellor only on the Sunday. The page ends at “guetté”.

04 What remains open

  • Six unresolved groups: 774 (K3 “exist|texiste”, clear “j’”), 3527 (clear perhaps “très”), 77x and 1039 “interuen” (no counterpart in the clear), 2036a (about “pri”), 279 “belle” (clear “change”).
  • Seventeen groups restored from the clear: 1647 ViceChancelier (twice), 1602 dite, 1165 le, 2174 remis, 2255 so, 3551a uniformité, 1162 l’, 429 conçue, 682 egard, 1411 à la, 1593b ob, 631 dimanche, 106 a, 2064 (K3 “put”, clear “que”) and 3778 (a null in K3, standing where the clear has “y”, twice). These suggested values were not added to K3.
  • K3 is not validated (0.645 against 0.85); a better key needs new pairs and a new held-out piece.
  • The rest of the despatch (scan 147) and the other 3865 pieces of Zbiór Popielów 2 and 3 are not read; they are kept unopened as held-out material for the next key.

# Translation (English summary): Deboli to King Stanisław August, St Petersburg, 12 Sep 1786, first page in table 3865

Deboli, Polish envoy at St Petersburg, tells the King that, as announced in his last despatch, he has handed Vice-Chancellor
Ostermann his written observations on Prince Czartoryski's memorandum (the Branicki-Czartoryski affair, and the threat to take it to
the Diet), and sends the King the full text as he handed it in. He deliberately added a passage aimed at Ambassador Stackelberg, so
that Stackelberg sees that the matter can be well received in St Petersburg only if Deboli's ideas agree with his own, or with the
corrections he will make to them. Deboli only "picked the memorandum apart", so that nobody would be misled by it. Sedler presented
it to the Vice-Chancellor only on the Sunday, and Deboli had been watching for him ... (the page ends here).

This is a summary of what is read, not a full translation; restored and unresolved words are marked in `reading/z2_146.md`.
