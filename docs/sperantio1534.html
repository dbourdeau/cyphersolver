King John of Hungary and an agent at Buda to the dukes of Bavaria, 1533–1534

→

Three cipher letters to the dukes of Bavaria in one key, two of them filed under their addressee, Cornelio Sperantio: rebuilt from glosses of the time, the key deciphers all three, 95.5% of the cipher words measured; written out as readings, R9411 and R9412 build coherent sentences but read at 92.2% and 89.1% of their words, under the 95% a complete reading needs.

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · partial (96% of the cipher)

The letters
R9415: King John’s side to the dukes, 18 April 1533, in Latin, addressed to their agent Aurelio Augurelio. R9411: King John Zápolya of Hungary to the dukes of Bavaria, Buda, 6 February 1534, in Latin. R9412: a German newsletter from an agent at Buda to a duke of Bavaria, 8 February 1534. The cover is addressed to Cornelio Sperantio, the dukes’ man at Buda; DECODE lists him as the sender.
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591, ff. 248, 250–251 and 260–261; DECODE R9411 and R9412 (catalogue 163), “partially decrypted”, and R9415 (catalogue 162).
The cipher
A homophonic substitution on graphic signs, without word division, with a few signs for pairs and words and clear Latin passages left standing: system L of the volume.
How it was deciphered
R9411 carries a contemporary interlinear decipherment over every line; the key was fixed from it by a solver held to the gloss values, then refitted for German and applied to R9412, which has none. The same key had already been recovered from a one-word gloss on R9415 with a solver; the two agree sign for sign.
What it says
R9415: Ferdinand opened a treaty with King John while secretly suing the Sultan for peace. King John on the Hungarian gold and silver mines and his servant Nicolaus Freiberger; the newsletter on Werbőczy’s everlasting peace with the Turk, Peterwardein, Titel and Salankamen, plans against Transylvania, and the mines.
Measured
Cipher words only, every supplied word counted unread (measure.py, 5 October 2026; R9415 r9415/count.py): R9415 388 of 405 (95.8%), R9411 340 of 356 (95.5%), R9412 546 of 573 (95.3%), together 1,274 of 1,334 (95.5%). Counted word by word on the written-out readings, with doubtful and supplied words as holes (24 October 2026): R9411 92.2%, R9412 89.1%, under the 95% a complete reading needs.
Still open
Words at blotted signs: 16 in R9411, including the captain’s name; 27 in R9412, including the second Danube fortress, the bishop, the Nuremberg factor and the signature; 17 scattered words in R9415.

The cipher is a homophonic substitution on the graphic sign set of the volume (↓ ω π 4 8 ÿ …), written without word division, with clear Latin passages left standing in the cipher (“Ceterum”, “Misimus nunc”, “Et primo” … “Quarto”). Main values: w/4 e, q n, v r, x/L s, 5 a, 3 u, y t, 7 m, m i, c c, 9 o, d l, 6 d, 8 n, D f, A b, K rr, Y ll. A few signs are polyphonic (π b/w/o, 4 e/i, ∃ d/ch, a double stem for u/v/k/g), and a few stand for pairs or words: 2o = et in Latin and und in German, oo = qu, r+ = sch, a+ = der. A ‡ before a double colon is a paragraph null.

Checked against the key rebuilt for R9415 (18 April 1533) a day earlier, the common signs all agree (w e, x s, 3 u, v r, y t, q n, 5 a, 7 m, 9 o, 8 n, d l, 6 d, D f, c c). One session found the key from R9411’s gloss; the other applied the R9415 key unchanged, without the gloss, and reached 91% on R9411. The two routes confirm each other.

02 R9415: King John’s side to the dukes, 18 April 1533

This letter was filed until 15 October 2026 with two letters in other ciphers (Łaski’s and the Wardein report, each with its own page); it is in this key and is shown here. The signs look like the System A′ set of the dukes’ agents, but the A′ key does not fit, and neither does R9369, Augurelio’s own key of 1531. A gloss over part of line 3 (“incrementum”) gave the first values; a solver (simulated annealing) with a Latin language model and a search over the candidate values gave the rest (r9415/). Six signs after the clear passages are nulls marking the join.

… hostis noster una parte tractatum cum […] nobiscum inceperit, ex alia parte Turcarum Caesari pro pace clam supplicaverit, credimus D. vestras illud iam compertum habere.

Georgius, the dukes’ servant, came back with their three reasons for urging concord at the diet of Pressburg. Ferdinand, “our enemy”, opened a treaty with King John while secretly suing the Sultan for peace; the Sultan’s own letters revealed it, so John recalled his orators and, at the Sultan’s request, sent his governor to him. Ferdinand boasts of peace but does not know its terms, and the dukes should not believe the rumour. The Turkish envoy in Vienna has made Ferdinand’s orator in Turkey “his” orator. The address on the fourth page, in clear, is to Aurelio Augurelio. Open: 17 scattered words (r9415/count.txt).

03 R9411: King John to the dukes, Buda, 6 February 1534

From the decipherment (targets/sperantio1534/r9411/reading_r9411.txt), in the original Latin, with gaps as […]:

Salutem et omne bonum. Illustrissimi principes domini ac fratres carissimi. Accepimus literas Vestre et ea quae […] etiam intelleximus quae […] secretarium suum scripserunt. Vestre ita sibi persuadere velint, quidquid nos in rem commodum et dignitatem earum et fratrum atque amicorum suorum id facere possumus, id semper libentissimo animo facturi sumus. […] videtur tamen esse res communicanda cum illustri domino regni nostri gubernatore, qui propediem hic apud nos futurus est. Videtur enim nobis esse satius de hac re capere consilium ipsius quam si ex abrupto apud potentissimum Turcarum imperatorem rem tentaremus. … Ceterum Vestre medio magnifici […] capitanei et consiliarii nostri … ex parte minerarum auri, argenti et aliorum metallorum, quae res nobis vehementissime fuit cordi, … cum Dei auxilio rem aggredi. … Misimus nunc hunc servitorem nostrum Nicolaum Freeberger ad Vestras … Bude sexta Februarii, anno etc. xxxiiii.

In English: King John will do whatever he can for the dukes, whom he addresses as brothers. Their request must first go to the governor of his realm (Lodovico Gritti), who is expected shortly: better to take his counsel than to try the matter abruptly with the Turkish emperor. The news his adversaries spread is false. Through his captain and councillor he has begun on the gold, silver and other mines, a matter very close to his heart. He sends his servant Nicolaus Freiberger to the dukes, invites them to take on the working of such mines, and asks for masters skilled in them. A postscript asks for the Bavarian mining ordinance (“ordinacionem rerum montanarum”) by a sure messenger.

04 R9412: a newsletter from Buda, 8 February 1534

German, no gloss. It opens by repeating what the writer sent on 22 December by an unsure messenger. That date, in line 1, belongs to the earlier letter; this one is dated at its foot, “Datum Ofen, des achten Februarii”. From the decipherment (targets/sperantio1534/r9411/reading_r9412.txt; […] not deciphered, braces supplied from sense, capitals in clear on the page):

wie erstlich, was der canczler herr Stephan Werbeczi bei dem Turcken hab ausgericht. ET PRIMO: so hat er dem Ungerland mit dem Turcken einen ewigen frid gemacht und angestossen. SECUNDO: das alle stett und flecken … als nemlich Peterwardein und […], mit Titil, Salmaulack {Salankamen} … TERTIO: … die zwitrachten, als von wegen des titels der kunig … QUARTO: … SED TAMEN PER MAXIMOS SUOS CONATUS NIHIL ALIUD IMPETRARE POTUIT QUAM ILLUD QUOD …

The chancellor Stephan Werbőczy has obtained at the Porte an everlasting peace between Hungary and the Turk. The Danube places are named: Peterwardein (Petrovaradin), Titel, Salankamen. The dispute over the kings’ titles and the frontier towns is to be settled, and the writer has sent a copy of the Sultan’s letter. Then come plans against Transylvania and the mines. Fridrich Schmalcz has made himself head of the mining business and sends men skilled in mining to Pest; his factor at Nuremberg is named, and “herr Caesar” at Vienna. A bishop sends the duke his greetings in clear Latin. The b/w and d/t pairs share signs, so “beczi” is Werbeczi.

05 Method

  • Both letters transcribed sign by sign from full-resolution line crops of the DECODE images (R9411 2,040 signs, R9412 2,725), in four passes on R9411 and three on R9412.
  • R9411: a solver (simulated annealing) with a Latin language model (la), started from and held to the values of the interlinear gloss (r9411/solve.py, run7.txt); checked by hand against the gloss.
  • R9412: the R9411 key refitted with a language model of sixteenth-century German (de-1500s), with pair and digraph signs set from context; then a manual pass on the sense (84% → 89%), a check of every gap, and on 5 October 2026 a further pass on the remaining gaps.
  • Measure (measure.py, 5 October 2026): cipher words only, leaving out the clear Latin and German passages and the clear date, with every word supplied from sense, whole or in part, counted unread. R9411 340/356 (95.5%), R9412 546/573 (95.3%), together 886/929 (95.4%). The earlier figure of 92% counted clear text and could not be reproduced.
  • Rejected, because each raised its own score while making the German worse: two automated look-alike repairs (confuse.py) and two key optimisers driven by a lexicon measure (lexopt.py, lexopt2.py).
  • Control: a second, independent transcription of all five pages (4,745 signs) with the R9415 key and a word-level fit (fit.py) gives R9411 at 91% without the gloss.

06 What remains uncertain

  • R9412: 27 cipher words unread, supplies included (grade C supplies are counted unread). The open places include the second Danube fortress (l. 10), “Caiianer” (l. 44), the bishop (l. 56, perhaps Varad, grade I), Schmalcz’s Nuremberg factor (l. 51), the signature (l. 65) and about twenty single words at blotted signs. The independent transcription does not resolve them either. Blocker: illegible.
  • R9415: 17 words open or doubtful, scattered single signs (r9415/count.txt). Blocker: open codes.
  • R9411: 16 words, thirteen short gaps at blotted signs and the supplies, including the captain’s name on l. 19 (“Casparis?”, grade I), where the gloss is also faint. Doubtful words such as cumis, composuit and decretam are grade M. Blocker: illegible.
