c. 1550 · Philadelphia manuscript

Latin conjuration in MS 10a 132

Go to the full text ↓

A one-letter shift reveals a Latin love-binding conjuration and a cancelled opening.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 26 November 2026

01 What the text says

The enciphered text is a Latin conjuration, a spoken spell, addressed to a rota. The writer calls on the Trinity, Mary, the apostles, the saints, the angels, the sun and moon and the words of the Mass, and then commands the rota to bind the person the writer touches: that person is not to walk, stand, drink, eat or sleep, or to take pleasure with anyone else, and is to love only the writer. The spell ends with an instruction to say it a number of times. It is a love-binding spell, and the cipher hides ordinary devotional Latin, not a letter or a secret of state.

By holy Mary… by the twelve apostles; by the four evangelists… by the sun and moon; by the stars; by all the saints of paradise… you, rota, do not let that person go, nor stand, nor drink, nor eat, nor sleep, nor [enjoy] carnal pleasure with any lover except me… and let that person love only me.

An extract from the translation below; square brackets mark words supplied or uncertain. What rota means here is not settled: Latin rota is a wheel, and the spelling may also stand for ruta, rue. The page does not choose. Why the scribe enciphered the text, and what else the manuscript holds, were not checked for this page.

02 The cipher and key

The cipher is a Caesar shift of one place: each written letter stands for the letter one place before it in the alphabet. The scribe used a 23-letter Latin alphabet, with no j, v or w (i and u do both jobs), so the shift runs through 23 letters and A stands for z.

Cipher letterABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUXYZ
Plain letterZABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUXY

Worked example, the first word of f. 76r: Dpon kxsp is D→c, p→o, o→n, n→m and k→i, x→u, s→r, p→o, which gives Conm iuro, that is coniuro, “I conjure”, with one extra letter that is not yet explained. The decryption is literal: it keeps the scribe’s spelling (santam, mondi, airem), so it is not always correct Latin. The initials after in nomine are enciphered like everything else (q . f . g . f . t gives p e f e s, for patris et filii et spiritus sancti). The Roman numerals (12, iiij, xxviij) are written in clear, not enciphered, and are left out of the letter counts.

Who found it and how it was checked. Larry Beck, Mark Addis and Panos Archondakis, working with ChatGPT, recovered the shift. Jacob Houtrouw supplied a second, independent decipherment and transcription, made with a model; no person checked that transcription, and it agrees with the first on key and text. The key was then re-run over all 39 transcribed lines, including the cancelled opening on f. 75r, and gave the filed text on every line. Earlier searches for prior readings are recorded in the submission; one possible source, Hirsch 1983, could not be consulted, so an earlier reading is not ruled out.

03 What is deciphered and what remains open

Two measures are easy to confuse. The key gives a value to 1,128 of the 1,138 cipher letters (99%); the other 10 signs are marked ? where the gutter, the cancellation or damage hides them. The words are a stricter test: a word counts as sure only when the whole word reads without repair. By that count 185 of 217 words are sure on ff. 76r–76v and 14 of 20 on the cancelled opening on f. 75r, 199 of 237 (84%), short of the 95% of words the project requires. That is why the extent is partial, although every letter of the cipher is deciphered.

What is open is Latin and damage, not the cipher:

  • a clause of about ten words on f. 76v (ut quanqunque de te rota personam tangam…) that the page cannot yet construe;
  • whether rota is meant, or ruta;
  • word endings lost in the gutter (person?, bibe?, qu?) and the signs under the cancellation on f. 75r;
  • the closing numeral and the last word (vij or iij, uices);
  • repairs the reading makes to the scribe’s spelling, such as dignatus, ascensionem, resurrectionem, and the opening conm; each is bracketed in the reading and listed in the hole ledger.

Reading

reading.md

This is an editorial reading, separate from the literal decryption below. Ordinary spelling is normalized; brackets mark uncertain readings, supplied text, or an unresolved clause. Irregular grammar has not been completely recast into classical Latin. The repeated word rota is left untranslated: literal Latin suggests “wheel”, but the actual object or material is not established. A spelling related to ruta, rue, is another possibility to investigate, not an identification asserted here.

### 76r

> [Coniuro] te, rota, in nomine p[atris] e[t] f[ilii] e[t] s[piri]tui sancti. Amen. Per sanctam Mariam, mater[em] D[omini] N[ostri] I[esu] X[risti]; per 12 apostolos; per 4 evangelistas; per [3?] patriarchas; per [28?] prophetas; per 10 choros angelorum et archangelorum; per cherubin et seraphin; per sol et lunam; per stellis; per omnes sanctos paradisi; per omnes caelos; per aerem et terram; per incarnationem filii Dei; per nativitatem eius; per baptismum eius; per sanctum ieiunium eius; per sanctam passionem eius; per sanctam crucem eius, pro qua [dignatus] est mori; per sanctam [ascensionem] eius; per sanctam [resurrectionem] eius; per omnia verba quae dicuntur in sancta missa; per omnia bona mundi, qua tu, rota, habes potestatem et fortitudinem posse facere et adimplere omnia quae voluero. Amen.
>
> Item [coniuro] te, rota, per spiritus malignos, per omnes quae habet ...

### 76v, continuing the sentence

> ... potentiam adimplere meam voluntatem, qua colligam te, ut quam tangam person[am], ut faciam nomen [clausula incerta: ut quanqunque de te rota personam tangam et tangere faciam], tu, rota, non dimittas ipsam ire, nec stare, nec bibe[re], nec comedere, nec dormire, nec delectatione carnali cum omni amico praeter qu[am] me, nec aliquam personam diligere praeter [me], et me solum diligat, ut [?quocunque] te, rota, portavero super me. Et hoc facias propter [tu]am magnam virtutem. Quando factum es[t] [coniuramentum], colige ipsas rota in [no]mine illius personae a qua vis amari.
>
> Et dicatur per [vij/iij?] [uices?].

The uncertain naming/touching clause is retained in deciphered form. “Colige ipsas rota” is also retained; its grammatical repair and precise ritual meaning remain open.

### 75r, cancelled opening

> [Coniuro] te, rota, in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritui Sancti. Amen. Per sanctam [Matrem]; per [12?] [choros?] angelorum et archangelorum.

Cancellation obscures parts of the third line. The word after sanctam and the number in the last line are provisional.
Translation

translation.md

### 76r

> [I conjure] you, rota, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. By holy Mary, [mother] of our Lord Jesus Christ; by the twelve apostles; by the four evangelists; by the [three?] patriarchs; by the [twenty-eight?] prophets; by the ten choirs of angels and archangels; by the cherubim and seraphim; by the sun and moon; by the stars; by all the saints of paradise; by all the heavens; by the air and earth; by the incarnation of the Son of God; by his birth; by his baptism; by his holy fasting; by his holy Passion; by his holy cross, for which he [deigned] to die; by his holy [Ascension]; by his holy [Resurrection]; by all the words spoken in the holy Mass; by all the good things of the world: through these, you, rota, have the power and strength to do and fulfil everything I shall wish. Amen.
>
> Again [I conjure] you, rota, by the evil spirits, by all those who have ...

The literal reading is “digntus est mori”; [dignatus] / [deigned] is an editorial repair supplying the missing a, not a silent change to the ciphertext.

### 76v

> ... the power to fulfil my will, by which I bind you, so that the person whom I touch [ending supplied], so that I make a name [the naming/touching clause is uncertain: approximately, “whatever person I touch with you, rota, or cause to be touched”], you, rota, do not let that person go, nor stand, nor drin[k], nor eat, nor sleep, nor [enjoy] carnal pleasure with any lover except me, nor love any person except [me]; and let that person love only me, [?wherever] I carry you, rota, upon me. And do this through [your] great power. When the [conjuration] is completed [final letter supplied], “colige ipsas rota” [collect/bind the rota; number and syntax unresolved] in the [na]me of the person by whom you wish to be loved.
>
> And let it be said [seven or three?] times.

The feminine grammatical forms follow persona, a feminine noun; they do not by themselves establish the sex of the intended beloved. [Enjoy] supplies a verb missing before the ablative phrase about carnal pleasure.

### 75r

> [I conjure] you, rota, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. By the holy [Mother]; by [twelve?] [choirs?] of angels and archangels.
