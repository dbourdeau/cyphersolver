The Cipher Atlas

Five centuries of secret letters, from a besieged pope at Avignon in 1399 to a police radio net in 1942. Follow the story, or open any line on the map to see what the letter says.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 5 November 2026

About the data

Each letter comes from a target's profile.json, the record kept for every cipher on this site. It is placed at the city where it was written and the city it was sent to, as the notes state them. A court or a person is placed where they were at that date. Letters whose sender or recipient cannot be located from the evidence are left off rather than guessed. So are the cryptograms with no route at all, such as Voynich, Scorpion and the gold bars.

A line stands for one route in one direction, and it gets thicker with each letter sent along it. Moving dots show the direction of travel on the busiest routes on screen. A route's colour is the commonest outcome among the letters showing: key recovered here, an existing key applied, an existing decipherment, or not solved. The glossary gives the exact categories.

Hollow rings are letters from the catalogue with no known decipherment, at the one place the catalogue names, where that place can be located. English excerpts appear only where a reader has passed the reading, and never for texts withheld at their owner's request; each write-up has the complete text. The coastlines are modern (Natural Earth). The map draws no frontiers, because they moved too often over five centuries.
