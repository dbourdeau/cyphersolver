Champigny sends Maurepas a cipher copy of Commodore Knowles's notice to Dominica, 1744

The ministry's inventory says part of the letter was not decrypted; its 154 of 155 words are now deciphered: Knowles offered Dominica's inhabitants his protection if they stayed neutral and four louis d'or for every deserter returned.

The document
A copy, signed "Pour copie Champigny", of the notice that Commodore Charles Knowles circulated on Dominica in the summer of 1744. Jacques-Charles Bochart de Champigny, governor general of the Windward Islands, enclosed it with his letter of 17 August 1744 to Maurepas, the Navy minister.
Where
Archives nationales d'outre-mer, Aix-en-Provence, COL C8 A 56 fols. 57–57v (images FRANOM23_C8A_056_0103–0104). The inventory says of the letter that part of it was not decrypted. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
The Navy code of the Windward Islands: about 500 numbers for syllables, letters and a few words, with several numbers for common syllables, in no alphabetical order.
How it was deciphered
Brice Dudout aligned the code against the interlinear glosses of six letters of Champigny of 1744, froze it, and tested it on a seventh, which it reproduced at 0.894 of characters, against 0.578 at most for shuffled keys. Applied to the enclosure, it gives sentences.
What it says
Knowles promises protection to the inhabitants of Dominica "as if they were of his own nation" as long as they stay neutral, demands the return of his deserters at four louis d'or a head, and threatens to treat anyone who hides them as an enemy.
Still open
One word of 155 (group 320, "agissent" or "obéissent"); 5 words restored from the sense and 7 probable.

01 The notice

In the summer of 1744 the war between France and Britain reached the West Indies. Commodore Charles Knowles anchored at Dominica, an island that both powers treated as neutral, and circulated a notice to its inhabitants. Champigny's letter of 17 August, glossed between the lines in Versailles, tells of Knowles's arrival at the Grande Anse with the Superbe, two 50-gun ships and frigates, of his sailors deserting to Martinique, and of an attack on Saint Lucia that was feared. It announces a copy of the writing Knowles had circulated "to offer the inhabitants of that island a neutrality, which they did not accept".

That copy is the enclosure on fols. 57–57v: 48 manuscript lines of numbers, 381 groups in all, with a few numbers underlined for figures that are meant as figures. The letter was deciphered at the ministry; the enclosure never was, and the ANOM inventory records that part of the letter "has not been decrypted". The key sheet is not in the file.

No image of the leaf is reproduced here: the ANOM viewer states no reuse licence and could not be reached by script, so the strip at the top is set from Brice Dudout's transcription.

02 The cipher

The code is a numerical nomenclator of about 500 values (1–500), two-part, so that nothing in the order of the numbers tells you what they mean. Most values are syllables and letters, spelling the text out in pieces (276 187 = ch-ef), and a few are words: 205 is Dominique, 474 Royal, 3 trois. Frequent syllables have several numbers each. Numbers written in clear and underlined stand for themselves, as the "4 louis d'or" of the notice does.

It is not the code of 1761 rebuilt by Daniel Bourdeau (Mercier 1761), nor the d'Ennery form of 1765, nor the Guiana code of 1744–1762: the three agree with it on none, one and none of the numbers they share with it.

03 How the key was built

The key, called K5 in the notes, was built only from the contemporary interlinear glosses of six letters of Champigny of 1744 in the same volume (C8 A 56 fols. 40, 44, 54–56v, 59, 63 and 67), about 4,900 groups aligned automatically to their glosses, with four written decisions on ambiguous groups (393 = i, 296 = ro, 465 = qu, 300 = fa). It was frozen, its SHA-256 recorded, before the validation letter was opened.

The test was a seventh glossed letter never used before the freeze, fol. 79 of 17 November 1744: 566 groups and 263 words of gloss. Against the gloss, with a measure fixed in advance, the key reaches 0.894 of characters and 0.814 of words; 200 control keys, with the values shuffled between groups, reach 0.578 and 0.122 at most. The criterion (0.85 and 0.75, above every control) was met. Three earlier tests with poorer keys on other letters had stayed below it, at 0.76–0.81 and 0.65–0.74.

04 The reading

The 381 groups give 155 words of French:

M. [Knowles], chef d'escadre de Sa [Majesté] {Britannique}, fait savoir à tous les {habitans} de l'isle de la Dominique que, pendant qu'ils demeureront dans une parfaite neutralité, qu'ils peuvent être assurés de sa protection en toute manière, comme s'ils étoient de sa propre nation, ayant bien soin d'{empêcher} toute insulte et pillage qui pourroient leur [arriver] par les corsaires ou autres vaisseaux {armés} ; mais aussi de la part des habitans de l'isle de la Dominique, il demande [absolument] [d'eux] la restitution de tous les gens de son équipage qui ont déjà ou pourront déserter des vaisseaux, et pour comble de récompense il promet de donner pour chaque homme déserté du bord de son escadre 4 louis d'or ; mais si d'autre manière ils cachent aucun de ses gens pour {empêcher} leur restitution, ils peuvent être assurés qu'ils seront {traités} comme des ennemis ; mais s'ils ‹320›issent comme il {demande}, ils peuvent être assurés de sa protection.

Braces mark a probable reading, square brackets a word restored from the sense, angle quotes a group left unresolved. Mr Knowles, commodore of His Britannic Majesty, makes known to all the inhabitants of Dominica that while they stay perfectly neutral they may count on his protection in every way, as if they were of his own nation, and that he will see to it that privateers and other armed ships do them no harm. In return he demands that they give back every man of his crew who has deserted or may desert, and promises, "as a crowning reward", four louis d'or for each man deserted from his squadron. Those who hide his men to prevent their return will be treated as enemies; those who do as he asks keep his protection.

To Brice Dudout's knowledge this is the first published decipherment of the enclosure.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Unresolved, 1 word. "s'ils ‹320›issent comme il demande": agissent (act) or obéissent (obey). Group 320 occurs in none of the seven glossed letters examined. Either reading gives the same sense.
  • Restored from the sense, 5 words. Knowles: 381 251 326 110 217 301 (elsewhere the name is 381 25 326 110 217, with 25 = K, so 251 is probably a malformed 25). Majesté: number 222, absent from every glossed letter, the only word that fits "Sa … Britannique". arriver: 300 (fa) written for 303 (ve), neighbouring numbers. absolument: 470, not in the glosses. d'eux: 336 306 428 = ce eu x, which would make "il demande absolument ce eux la restitution" ungrammatical.
  • Probable, 7 words. Britannique (391 standing for -mbr- in "nombre"), habitans (253 followed by 436 instead of 435, neighbours), empêcher twice (296 = ro in 35 of 35 glossed cases, so the sense is relied on), armés (254 with a number seen once), traités (204 = tr in a September letter) and demande (433, one doubtful alignment).
  • Control. The held-out test is on a letter of the same year and volume. The enclosure has no gloss of its own, so the reading itself is checked by the sense and by the four louis d'or, the neutrality and the deserters that Champigny's glossed letter reports.

# English translation

Mr Knowles, commodore of His Britannic Majesty, makes known to all the inhabitants of the island of Dominica that, as long as they remain in perfect neutrality, they may be assured of his protection in every way, as if they were of his own nation; he will take good care to prevent any insult and plunder that privateers or other armed ships might inflict on them.

But on the part of the inhabitants of the island of Dominica, he absolutely demands of them the return of all the men of his crew who have already deserted, or may desert, from the ships; and as a crowning reward he promises to give four louis d’or for every man deserted from on board his squadron.

But if in any other way they hide any of his men to prevent their return, they may be assured that they will be treated as enemies; if they [act / obey] as he demands, they may be assured of his protection.
