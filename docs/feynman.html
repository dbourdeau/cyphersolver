Feynman ciphers 2 and 3, 1953

Go to the full text ↓

Two English ciphers attributed to Feynman, one a stanza of Housman and one the opening of his helium paper, solved by David Vierra in 2023; we re-enciphered both readings and they match.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 31 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: complete

Credits · David Vierra (key or decipherment used)

At a glanceKnown key applied · complete

The ciphers
Two short English ciphertexts from 1953, 261 letters (no. 2) and 231 letters (no. 3), with no word boundaries.
Where
The ciphertexts are the strings copied into verify.py in the repository folder; no image of the originals was used and their source is not recorded there.
The cipher
Two monoalphabetic substitution alphabets, used alternately word by word. Words of even length are written backwards after substitution, and the word boundaries are dropped.
How it was read
David Vierra published the solution in May 2023. Here the two alphabets were applied to re-encipher his plaintexts and compared with the ciphertexts, and a word-segmenting search over the same rules recovered both texts from the ciphertexts alone.
What it says
No. 2 is lines 19–32 of Housman’s “Terence, this is stupid stuff”; no. 3 is the opening of Feynman’s 1953 paper on the lambda transition in helium.
Still open
Nothing in the solution. The re-encipherment differs from the ciphertexts in three letters of no. 3 and in a stretch of no. 2 where the encipherer broke his own rule.
Credit
David Vierra found the system and both plaintexts (May 2023). This page records our check of it and adds nothing to the solution.

01 The two ciphers

Both ciphertexts are runs of capital letters with no spaces. Each uses only 26 distinct letters, as a substitution cipher does, and each plaintext is plain English of the period.

No. 2 begins XUKEXWSLZJUAXUNKIGWFSOZRAWURORKXAOSL… and no. 3 begins WURVFXGJYTHEIZXSQXOBGSVRUDOOJXAT…. The full transcriptions are in the Full texts section below.

02 The system and its key

Vierra’s two alphabets, written as the cipher letter for plaintext A, B, C… in order (a full stop stands for a letter not fixed by the published solution):

A1 = MANYREQUS.HVBCID.OLWGZX.K.
A2 = JHAZTENYXMLOCUFBQVKPSGW.D.

The first word of each line or sentence uses A1, the next A2, and so on. A word of even length is reversed after substitution. Word boundaries are then removed. A search that segments the ciphertext into words under those rules recovers both texts without the plaintext supplied.

03 What the check found

No. 3 re-enciphers to the ciphertext at 228 of 231 positions. The three mismatches are one letter in “phenomenological” and a transposed pair in “principles”, slips in the encipherment or in the copy we hold.

The behavior of liquid helium, especially below the lambda transition, is very curious. The most successful theoretical interpretations so far have been largely phenomenological. In this paper and one or two to follow, the problem will be studied entirely from first principles.

No. 2 matches exactly for the first 118 characters and at 208 of 261 overall. The mismatching stretches decode to “England brews” (both words under A2, the documented exception) and to a line 27 whose wording differs a little from Housman’s printed text, “ale is” for “ale’s”.

Why, if ’tis dancing you would be, there’s brisker pipes than poetry. Say, for what were hop-yards meant, or why was Burton built on Trent? Oh many a peer of England brews livelier liquor than the Muse, and malt does more than Milton can to justify God’s ways to man. Ale, man, ale is the stuff to drink for fellows whom it hurts to think.

04 What remains uncertain

  • The origin of the two ciphertext strings, and therefore any transcription error in them, is not recorded in the project.
  • Three letters of no. 3 and the stretches of no. 2 named above do not re-encipher exactly; they are the encipherer’s or the copyist’s slips, not gaps in the key.
  • Letters the solution does not need (the full stops in A1 and A2) are not fixed by these texts.
