Łask, Hungary, Wardein → the Dukes of Bavaria · three sign ciphers · 1531–1537 · read in part

Łaski, King John and a Bavarian at Wardein: three more ciphers in the Munich key volume

→

Three letters from King John of Hungary’s circle to the Bavarian court, catalogued as one entry with no names. Two are read in full and the third to 91%.

At a glanceRead in part · two read, the third 91.3%

The letter
Three letters to the Bavarian court: Hieronymus Łaski from Łask, 17 September 1531; King John’s side to the dukes, 18 April 1533; and a Bavarian servant at King John’s court to Duke Ludwig, Wardein, 14 March 1537.
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 226–228 and 257–261, on DECODE as R9403, R9415 and R9414. Catalogued as one entry with no sender and no recipient.
The cipher
Three sign ciphers. Łaski uses his own graphic alphabet with word signs. The other two are homophonic alphabets, with several signs for each letter.
How it was read
Łaski’s key, rebuilt from glossed letters in the same volume, reads R9403 with the signs for e and r swapped. For the other two, a short interlinear gloss gave the first values and computer search with Latin and German models gave the rest.
What it says
Łaski reports that the Sultan will give Ferdinand no peace unless he yields Hungary to King John. The 1533 letter says “our enemy” opened talks while secretly begging the Turkish emperor for peace. The 1537 report describes the writer’s journey back into Hungary and the state of Hungarian affairs.
Still open
In R9414, about ten words are word signs with no key, about fifteen are blotted and about sixty-five are unresolved. R9415 has nineteen words unread or doubtful. R9403 has two unread words and ten doubtful ones. The recipient of R9403 is inferred.

02 Łaski, 17 September 1531 (R9403)

Łaski’s graphic cipher (System B) was rebuilt from the glossed letters R9322 and R9417 in the same volume. In this letter the signs for e and r are the other way round (starred x = e, plain x = r) and some shapes differ. The sign DAG, three times, stands for King John. The docket says “anno 32”; the text is dated 1531.

… cum hac hieme Turca oratoribus regis Ferdinandi hoc dederit responsum: Item ad dominum uestrum dicite illi nunquam ipsum amiciciam nostram, numquam pacem uel inducias consequuturum nisi cedat regno Hungarie amico nostro Ioanni regi serenissimo; quod nisi fecerit, ueniam ad querendum eum …

Ruffus brought the dukes’ letters on the 15th, and Łaski thanks them. The Sultan told Ferdinand’s envoys this winter that there would be no friendship, peace or truce unless Hungary was given up to King John. Ships, guns and grain are being carried to the Danube and soldiers levied. The diet of Speyer will be short; Łaski is ill but will ride post to it, and the dukes and their friends should stop any decision against the king until he arrives. On the marriage he cannot yet write: within ten days he will settle it with the King of Poland, “for among the Poles, as among the French, the dowering of kings’ daughters is fixed by law.” Postscript: the councillor should put the king’s letters before the imperial estates.

03 King John’s side to the dukes, 18 April 1533 (R9415)

The signs look like the System A′ set used by the dukes’ agents in the 1534–35 reports, but the A′ key does not fit, and neither does R9369, Augurelio’s own key of 1531. A gloss over part of line 3 (“incrementum”) gave the first values; annealing with a Latin model and a beam search gave the rest. Six signs after the clear passages are join nulls.

… hostis noster una parte tractatum cum […] nobiscum inceperit, ex alia parte Turcarum Caesari pro pace clam supplicaverit, credimus D. vestras illud iam compertum habere.

Georgius, the dukes’ servant, came back with their three reasons for urging concord at the diet of Pressburg. Ferdinand, “our enemy”, opened a treaty with King John while secretly suing the Sultan for peace; the Sultan’s own letters revealed it, so John recalled his orators and, at the Sultan’s request, sent his governor to him. Ferdinand boasts of peace but does not know its terms, and the dukes should not believe the rumour. The Turkish envoy in Vienna has made Ferdinand’s orator in Turkey “his” orator. The address on the fourth page, in clear, is to Aurelio Augurelio.

04 From Wardein to Duke Ludwig, 14 March 1537 (R9414)

The opening, the date (“Datum Wardein … den vierzehenden tag Marcii anno siben und dreissigisten”) and the address (“Herzogen Ludwigen in Bayrn”) are in clear. The gloss over lines 6–8 gave the first values of a new homophonic alphabet; annealing and a beam with a sixteenth-century German model, three visual passes over every line and a one-edit lexicon search brought it to 91.3%.

… dan der turkisch kaiser irn herrn befolhen hab das si in aller eil, ieder mit […] pferden gerust sein …

The writer describes his journey back into Hungary and the state of Hungarian affairs; envoys of the pashas of Belgrade and Bosnia; the Turkish emperor ordering them to be ready and arming a hundred thousand men to win Hungary and then turn on Austria; Ferdinand’s party taking towns and villages. Duke Wilhelm never writes to him, so he asks Ludwig to intercede, citing his service to their father; he writes of his debts and leave, of mining and ore specimens, and of Hans Schwab of Kraków as carrier. Postscript: “der herr Camermaister hat mein ziffer”.

05 What stays open

  • R9414: about ten words are word signs with no key (Ψ at P2 lines 2, 8, 9, 27; a swash X at P2.15, P2.25, P4.2, P4.21; K at P4.33, probably “the king”). About fifteen are blotted or struck. About sixty-five are legible but unresolved: several homophones still have more than one value.
  • R9415: nineteen words unread or doubtful, scattered (listed in r9415/count.txt).
  • R9403: two words on f.228 (“[?]orator”, “[?]meus”) and a small insertion on P1 l.4; ten more words are read but doubtful (ergo, Ruffus, sequentes, contra, postas, constituar, nam, ubi, dotandis, meus). The transcription is aligned to the reading, so its sign variants are not recorded.
  • The recipient of R9403 is inferred, not named.
