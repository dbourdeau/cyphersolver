Łaski’s cipher: four letters to the Dukes of Bavaria, 1529–1531

→

Hieronymus Łaski and King John Zápolya wrote to the Bavarian court in one graphic cipher; its key was recovered from glosses written at the time over two of the letters and deciphers all four.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 22 September 2026 · updated 15 October 2026

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: partial (95% of the cipher)

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext, partial · 1,184 of 1,243 words

The letters
Hieronymus Łaski, King John Zápolya’s envoy, to a Bavarian duke (Buda, 24 November 1529), with a letter of King John to Duke Ludwig; Łaski to the dukes’ secretary (Kraków, 16 June 1530); a short note to the same secretary; and Łaski to a ducal councillor (Łask, 17 September 1531).
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 121–123, 226–228 and 264; DECODE R9322, R9323, R9417 (catalogue 161) and R9403 (catalogue 162), listed with no sender or recipient.
The cipher
One invented sign per letter, words separated, and a few signs for whole words: a large dagger for King John, a large X for Ferdinand. In the 1531 letter the signs for e and r are the other way round.
How it was deciphered
A clerk of the time wrote the plain text over parts of R9322 and R9417. The key was rebuilt from 153 of those word pairs and deciphers the unglossed lines and the other two letters.
What it says
The Sultan has left John the kingdom of Hungary without tribute and will return against Ferdinand unless there is a concord; Łaski asks the dukes to work for it in the Empire, and in 1531 reports the Sultan’s terms: no peace unless Hungary is left to King John.
Still open
Four faint lines of the short note R9323, a word sign that is probably the king’s title, and a few cramped signs.

Until 15 October 2026 these letters were split between two pages, each grouping the records of a DECODE catalogue entry. The other letters of those entries are different ciphers, each with its own page: the System A′ reports of 1534–35, the Wardein report of 1537, the King John letters in system L (with R9415 of 1533), the Fulda protest of 1576, R9367, R9416, R9424 and R9319.

The counts are words of the ciphered text; clear words are not counted. For R9322, R9417 and R9323 they are counted on the decodes made with the gloss key, where a recognisable word with a slipped letter counts as deciphered, so they are generous; R9403 was deciphered line by line.

02 The key

The glosses give 153 cipher words with their plain words. Aligned sign by sign they fix 21 letter signs: 3 = i, x = e, circled cross = s, a flattened 2 = u/v, dotted circle = a, and so on (sysB/key_from_glosses.tsv). One sign, a w-shape, serves both c and t. Three larger signs stand alone for words: the dagger DAG is King John (“non alio loco vult habere [DAG] quam loco fratris sui”, said of the Sultan), the big X is Ferdinand (“concordia inter [DAG] et [X]”), and a four-lobed flower is probably the king’s title. In R9403 the starred x is e and the plain x is r, the reverse of the glossed letters, and some shapes differ; the raw decode of R9417’s first page shows the same exchange, so it is taken as a variant of the one key. A statistical check on the glossed words alone (a Latin letter model) passes: the glosses read far better than random keys or keys fitted to shuffled text.

03 Buda, 24 November 1529 (R9322)

Łaski writes that King John followed the dukes’ counsel. The Sultan, forced home by plague in his army, left John the kingdom of Hungary without tribute, with fifty guns and a thousand hundredweight of powder, and will come back next summer against Ferdinand’s lands unless there is a concord. A copy of the same news went to Count Palatine Frederick, the imperial captain. On f. 122, “Joannes Dei gratia rex Ungarie” tells Duke Ludwig that he is sending Lazarus the Jew as his envoy.

04 Kraków, 16 June 1530 (R9417), and the note R9323

Łaski has the secretary’s letters of 12 June. The siege army lay before Buda; a concord is best made through princes of the Empire; Nicolaus Min… has been sent to the King of France; the Sultan will receive in friendship whomever King John names. He asks for envoys to meet at Coburg in Saxony in July. The short note R9323 says that King John loves the Duke of Bavaria and that Łaski wants concord between John and Ferdinand, for otherwise the Turk will consume Bohemia and Austria; four of its lines are too faint to decipher.

05 Łask, 17 September 1531 (R9403)

… cum hac hieme Turca oratoribus regis Ferdinandi hoc dederit responsum: Item ad dominum uestrum dicite illi nunquam ipsum amiciciam nostram, numquam pacem uel inducias consequuturum nisi cedat regno Hungarie amico nostro Ioanni regi serenissimo; quod nisi fecerit, ueniam ad querendum eum …

Ruffus brought the dukes’ letters on the 15th, and Łaski thanks them. The Sultan told Ferdinand’s envoys this winter that there would be no friendship, peace or truce unless Hungary was given up to King John. Ships, guns and grain are being carried to the Danube and soldiers levied. The diet of Speyer will be short; Łaski is ill but will ride post to it, and the dukes and their friends should stop any decision against the king until he arrives. On the marriage he cannot yet write: within ten days he will settle it with the King of Poland, “for among the Poles, as among the French, the dowering of kings’ daughters is fixed by law.” Postscript: the councillor should put the king’s letters before the imperial estates. The docket says “anno 32”; the text is dated 1531.

06 What stays open

  • R9323, lines 4 and 8–10: 39 of its 163 words; the signs are faint.
  • The flower word sign (R9322, R9323): probably King John’s title, not fixed by its three contexts.
  • R9403: two words on f. 228 (“[?]orator”, “[?]meus”) and a small insertion on the first page; ten more words are deciphered but doubtful (ergo, Ruffus, sequentes, contra, postas, constituar, nam, ubi, dotandis, meus). Its transcription was written from the decipherment, so its sign variants are not recorded.
  • R9322: 16 words hold a doubtful sign.
  • The recipient of R9403 is inferred, not named.
