The Hague → William V in exile · French numerical nomenclator · 18 June 1804 · attempted, not solved

Robert Fagel to William V, 18 June 1804

Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, Prins Willem V (old inv. nr. 337), DECODE R2238: three pages, the middle one a cipher passage of 128 figure groups between clear French sentences about the Orange compensation talks with the Batavian Republic.

Transcribed from the DECODE images, dated and placed in its negotiation. Every key that can be reached online, in DECODE and in the digitised Royal Collections and Fagel papers, was tested against it and ruled out. The key has to come from the archive.

Daniel Bourdeau · attempted 21 September 2026

Summary. DECODE lists Robert Fagel’s letter of 18 June 1804 to the exiled Stadholder as non-decrypted. Its cipher passage is 128 figure groups, 99 of them distinct, from 12 to 2510; dots join groups inside a word, and a few groups carry clear endings (503t, 788e, 281s), so the code is a large syllabic nomenclator for French. The clear sentences around it tie it to the final 1804 liquidation of the House of Orange’s claims, days before Fagel and d’Yvoy set out for Germany. The key was sought in every place reachable online: Euler’s 1782 Grand Chiffre of the Stadholder (DECODE R1024), direct, through its additive table from every start, and under 5,000 shifts, affine maps and digit permutations; all four KHA keys in DECODE; the eleven Fagel family keys R2842–R2852; the British Library French codes of 1798–1805; the Uppsala key R5160; and the small book cipher of the sibling letters. None can produce the letter’s groups or yields French. A text this short from a code of 2,500 values has no unique ciphertext-only solution. Status: not solved. The likeliest home of the key is KHA A31-902, “Stukken betreffende de aan het hof gebruikte geheimschriften”, a packet with no scans; then A31-2307/2308 and Nationaal Archief 1.10.29 no. 1909. A request to the archive is drafted in the repository.

01 The letter

DECODE numbers the pages back to front. The first page (P2) is in clear: a letter from the Hereditary Prince of the 6th announces the Stadholder’s departure; the courier Gaebel arrived on the 9th; the Commissaires of the House kept him so that d’Yvoy could be briefed; a note went to the Batavian side, whose answer was that he hoped to speak with Damen. The second page opens in clear, “Dès lors il n’a plus fait entendre parler de lui, et l’on ne tardera pas à connaître la déclaration, sur laquelle les instructions de V. A. S. et celles du ministre du Roi sont parfaitement d’accord”, then goes into figures. The last page closes “La Haye 18 Juin 1804 … R. Fagel”.

The context is secure from print. Colenbrander’s Gedenkstukken IV prints the surrounding papers: d’Yvoy’s appeal to Talleyrand of February 1804, his promise of 500,000 guilders for payments in Paris, Berlin and The Hague, Princess Wilhelmina’s letter of 14 June saying the arrangement was decided, and Fagel and d’Yvoy’s return with the signed convention on 7 August. It does not print this letter.

02 The cipher

128 groups, 99 distinct, two to four digits; 1531, 1731 and 2510 were checked at high resolution and are genuine four-digit groups. Commas separate words and dots join groups inside a word. The words &, car and que are in clear, and a boxed insertion adds a line between the rows. Small superscript marks (c, 3, 6, 9, fractions) follow some groups; their reading is uncertain. The full transcription is fagel1804/ciphertext.txt.

04 What would finish it

With 128 groups from a code of about 2,500 values, no statistical attack can give a unique reading; only the codebook or a deciphered copy will. The modern KHA inventory does not match DECODE’s old number 337; the subject belongs with A31-2306–2308, the 1802–1804 compensation negotiations. The best target is A31-902, a packet of court cipher papers of the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, which the Royal Collections’ catalogue backend confirms has no scans. After it come A31-2307 and A31-2308 (1804), A31-2292–2293, and Robert Fagel’s negotiation file, Nationaal Archief 1.10.29 no. 1909. A research request to the Koninklijke Verzamelingen is drafted as fagel1804/ARCHIVE_REQUEST.md.
