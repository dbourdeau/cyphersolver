Morosini’s second key: two enclosures from Tours and Moulins, spring 1589

→

Two of Cardinal Morosini’s cipher enclosures from the spring of 1589 use a second, syllabic key; George Lasry’s partial reconstruction of it gives long stretches of the first and fragments of the second.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: partial (75% of the cipher)

At a glanceKnown key applied, partial · 75% of decoded letters in Italian words (upper bound)

The letters
Two cipher enclosures of Gian Francesco Morosini, cardinal legate in France, to Rome: R53 from Tours in the spring of 1589, R54 dated in clear at Moulins, 11 May 1589.
Where
Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 22, items 22/32 and 22/33 (images 499v and 502r). DECODE R53 and R54, “partially decrypted”; part of catalogue item 243.
The cipher
Syllables and letters in groups of one or two figures, written without separators, several forms for common syllables, no nulls; three-figure names and words marked with a dot on the first figure.
How it was deciphered
George Lasry reconstructed about seventy elements of the key in 2020 and filed them on both records. The decoder written for Morosini’s other enclosures divided the figures with his values and an Italian language model.
What it says
The vice-legate of Avignon at Tours, lodged near Plessis; “a mixing at court of heretics and Catholics”; Mayenne near Le Mans. R54: heretics growing stronger at court, the writer’s own fears.
Still open
About a quarter of R53 and more of R54, which wait for more of the key; no decipherment or clear copy of either is known, and the two letters are too short to rebuild the missing values.

Until 14 October 2026 these two enclosures shared a page with Morosini’s other thirty-four cipher sheets of Francia 22, which use Meister’s key no. 40: a different key, with its own page.

01 The documents and the key

  • R53, Francia 22/32, image 499v. A short clear passage (the legate is waiting “con quella sollecitudine” for letters from Rome granting his leave) and 785 cipher figures, transcribed for DECODE by the volunteer Midas on 31 March 2017. Its place, Tours, and season come from the series.
  • R54, Francia 22/33, image 502r. 663 cipher figures (DECODE transcription by NiLan, 6 April 2017) and the clear dating line Di Molins à gli xi di Maggio 1589.

DECODE files a key for these two records under the name “ASV – F22b”: George Lasry’s reconstruction of 24 October 2020. He notes that it is variable in length and not deterministic. Its elements are one or two figures for syllables and letters (a 3 or 7, ba 86, ca 73, di 93, e 5 or 8, la 60, re 43, si 83, 85, 89 or 95, u 21 or 54), and three figures for a few syllables and words (102 mpo, 116 or 216 me, 495 or 498 chi, 496 che). Names carry a dot on the first figure: 1̇24 Umena (Mayenne), 1̇85 Navarro. There are no nulls. The other enclosures of Morosini’s legation use a different system, Meister’s key no. 40, with the dot on the middle figure and nulls.

02 R53: Tours, spring 1589

With Lasry’s values the figures of R53 divide into long stretches of Italian. The vice-legate of Avignon has arrived at Tours and lodged near Plessis with a large company of lords and gentlemen. On the same day someone goes to see him, and the morning after there is a scene at the King’s rising and at table. The decoder’s text, word breaks added, as it comes out:

… il medesimo giorno ando a vederlo […] al quale la matina siguente […] diede la camisia al leto et la saluieta ala tauola et sua maiesta la fece desinare […] onde si e fata una mescolanza ala corte di heretici et di catolici …

(The same day he went to see him … the next morning gave him his shirt in bed and his napkin at table, and His Majesty had him dine … so that there is now a mixing at court of heretics and Catholics.) Who serves whom is not clear from the decoded text and is not interpreted here. Later the letter turns to troops passed in review before the King (“far passare tutte le sue genti ala vista di sua maiesta”) and to Mayenne (Umena), who is near Le Mans.

03 R54: Moulins, 11 May 1589

R54 comes out in fragments. The heretics are growing stronger every day (“andauano ingrosando ogni giorno”) and are “mescolati gli heretici co i catolici ala corte”; the writer speaks of his own misfortune and fears, asks that something be made known, and refers to what was discussed “ala partita vostra”. Between these phrases the decoded text does not read as sense.

04 Method

The figures run on without separators, so the key cannot be applied group by group. The decoder of the sibling entry (targets/morosini1588/decrypt.py, run with --f22b) tries every way of dividing the figures into Lasry’s one-, two- and three-figure elements and keeps the division that a five-gram model of sixteenth-century Italian scores best. Where the key has a gap, the model fills it with the nearest Italian it can make from the elements it knows, so a stretch that looks like Italian is not proof that the values are right. The figures follow DECODE’s volunteer transcriptions; the scans were not checked figure by figure.

05 What remains uncertain

  • How much reads: 893 of 1,187 decoded letters (75%) fall in Italian word forms found in the project’s sixteenth-century corpora, 79% in R53 and 71% in R54. The thirty-four fully deciphered sheets of Morosini’s other key score 82% by the same count, held down by word-splitting errors and spelling, so the figure is an upper bound: by eye R53 reads in most of its length and R54 in fragments. The key has about seventy elements, and twenty-four two-figure codes have no value.
  • The readable stretches quoted above are graded C: the language model chose the division, and Lasry marks his key as incomplete.
  • Nomenclator groups outside Lasry’s list are open.
  • Rebuilding the missing values was tried on 14 October 2026: each empty code was given the syllable that best raised the language-model score, Lasry’s values kept. Fitted on R53 alone and on R54 alone, the two results agree on one code in ten, and each breaks stretches that already read in the other letter, so none was adopted. Meister (1906) prints no second key for Morosini: his index lists him only for no. 40, and his no. 32, a cipher of the Bishop of Brescia with a canon of St Peter’s, is a short list of cover names.
