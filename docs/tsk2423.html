Cairo Genizah · Probably 11th century · Partial decipherment

Two fragments of a Cairo Genizah cipher recipe book

Go to the full text ↓

A note beside a diagram appears to name three incense materials. On another fragment, a repeated cipher word preserves part of an appeal for haste.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 22 November 2026

Method: known key applied · Extent: partial

Credits · Gideon Bohak (key or decipherment used)

At a glance

Where
Cambridge University Library, T-S K24.23, and the Jewish Theological Seminary, ENA 3211.2.
How the pages relate
Gideon Bohak identified them as pieces of the same booklet. Both carry a heading for ophthalmia, an inflammation of the eyes.
Dating
Edna Engel tentatively placed the handwriting in the 11th century, according to Bohak.

An incense list beside the diagram

On the reverse of ENA 3211.2, two lines in ordinary Hebrew letters run beside a diagram. Read as Judeo-Arabic, the first appears to name sandarac and sal ammoniac. The second continues with wild costus. The last group remains uncertain.

אלבכור סנדרוס ונשאדר
וקסט אלברי …

The eye-disease heading is on the other face. No surviving instruction connects the materials to that recipe.

How the cipher can be checked

Bohak published the cipher key and identified the heading באב רמד (bāb ramad, “section on ophthalmia”) and two formulas calling for haste. On ENA the six signs of the heading have Hebrew-letter glosses. The sequence of signs repeats on the Cambridge fragment. ENA continues with אהיה (Ehyeh). At the corresponding point in Cambridge only a possible initial aleph can be made out.

Cambridge line 7 gives a second check. After al-ʿajal (“quickly”) and a separator, the first four signs recur before the edge cuts through the word. The reading אלעג[ל] supplies the final lamed from the intact word. The resulting “quickly, quickly” strengthens a particular occurrence of the formula Bohak identified.

How much of the recipe survives?

On the face with the ENA heading, surviving clauses invoke a name, ask for a request to be answered, and refer to the lids of someone’s eyes. The shared heading fixes a point of comparison. Neither strip supplies the other’s missing line endings, and a continuous reconstruction remains open.

Translation

cambridge-en.txt

Translation and commentary on the surviving groups

top horizontal line — bāb ramad, section on ophthalmia. Read right to left. The first and third signs repeat. Compare the six cipher signs under the glosses on ENA 3211.2.
top line immediately after title — The native-resolution crop preserves a crossed sign beyond the separator. Its form resembles the aleph inside the title. The next sign reaches the cut. The shared initial is compatible with ENA's אהיה, but no further letter of that word can be securely supplied from Cambridge.
second and third horizontal lines — Two different urgency formulas. The second word of the first pair is damaged in Bohak's edition.
fourth horizontal line — Probably qalbahā, her heart. The cut removes the rest of the clause. Its relation to the male target on ENA is uncertain.
fifth horizontal line — The second cluster is ʿalā, “on, upon.” The first can be read wa-Zuḥal, “and Saturn”. The third is cut after ayin and yod. Both a reference to eyes and a grammatical connection to the first line remain conjectural.
right margin beside horizontal lines 2–3 — Read with the photo rotated clockwise. The descender of final qof agrees with the qof opening line 4. Arabic root ḥ-r-q, burn. The consonants allow an active or passive verb. Joining the marginal verb to line 4 would give “may he burn her heart” or “may her heart be burned”. The words are on different baselines and no insertion mark is visible.
sixth horizontal line — Conventional anonymous target placeholder, also recorded by the FJMS MAGIC catalogue. Its presence is compatible with a formulary. It supplies no personal name for alignment.
seventh horizontal line — The same cipher group opens the second and seventh lines above the large drawn signs.
seventh line, after the separator — Cross, angular sign, loop and stem repeat the first four letters of אלעגל. The final lamed is supplied by repetition at the cut edge. Line 2 preserves less of its second word.

Translation

ena-en.txt

Translation and commentary on the surviving groups

1 — Two groups of three cipher signs, each with its Hebrew-letter gloss. Plain-script Ehyeh follows on the same baseline.
2 — Two repeated groups. The first occurrence is overwritten. Provisional consonants only.
3 — Provisional sequence. The stand-alone bet could finish something lost at the end of the preceding line.
4 — The first sequence is uncertain, especially its middle letters. No translation assigned.
5–6 — Divine address and an affirmation, “you have no partner.” Each slash marks a new manuscript line.
7–8 — “By the power of this name … [I charge you] to answer my request …” The second-person plural addresses the executors of the spell.
9–10 — “My need … and [that] you cause ophthalmia … and sever the lids of his eyes …” The causative verb independently explains the title. The object of that verb falls beyond the cut.
11–12 — A relative clause and “there be for him …”. The lost end of line 11 could have contained a negation governing line 12. The word קרא may anticipate the fully written קראר on line 14. A continuous clause would require restoration.
13–14 — “Nor in the daytime, nor … / nor rest [qarār], by the power of this …”. The final resh of קראר is present on line 14. The gap after line 13 precludes supplying its paired time expression.
15 — Three visible urgency words, with al-ʿajal between two instances of al-waḥā. The left edge cuts off any continuation.
verso, two lines beside the diagram, read after rotation — Provisional reading: “the incense: sandarac and sal ammoniac / and wild costus …”. The diagram and note are on the opposite face from the eye-disease heading. The final short group and the note's connection to any particular recipe are unresolved.
Reverse, separate cipher inscription (Bohak, p. 15) — … iron … written [on the] day … . The surviving words leave the day unspecified.
