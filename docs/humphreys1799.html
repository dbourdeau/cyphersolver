At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · partial (89% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count)

The letters
David Humphreys, American minister at Madrid, to the Secretary of State Timothy Pickering: the coded parts of despatch No. 214 (6 August 1799, 352 groups in ten passages), No. 188 (19 March 1799, 56 groups) and a 16-group sentence of a despatch of April 1799.
Where
National Archives (NARA), RG 59, Despatches from U.S. Ministers to Spain, vol. 4, microfilm M31 roll 5, frames 0414–0417, 0294 and 0364, free on the National Archives Catalog. No decipherment is on or after any of them on the film.
The cipher
A one-part code of about 1,600 numbers in scrambled order: whole words, syllables, single letters and punctuation; proper names are spelled out. The same values run from 1791 to 1801.
How it was deciphered
A table of 686 groups was rebuilt from the State Department clerks' decipherments of other Humphreys despatches and from printed decoded texts, frozen, and tested on a despatch kept out of the build: 129 of 135 groups (95.6%) came out as the clerk had them.
What it says
No. 214: Humphreys can explain Spain's wavering over the recall of its minister Yrujo only by supposing Yrujo shares in an exclusive trade, and reports his talk with Urquijo on Louisiana, ending in the assurance that France {should} {never} get it while Urquijo remained in office. No. 188: news from the Swedish chargé about [Sweden?] and France.
Still open
36 code numbers with no value in the table, found nowhere else with a decipherment; 12 groups of No. 214 with digits hidden in the binding.
Credit
Andrew Beling, with AI agents (Claude Opus 5.5), 14 November 2026 (sure words recounted 15 November): transcriptions, table, test and readings. The pairs come from the State Department's clerks and from the Papers of Thomas Jefferson and of James Madison.

A word counts as sure when every group comes from the frozen table or from a value the clerk gave the same group in the held-out despatch; context values, guesses and digits restored by sense are holes. The 16 despatches found in this code are listed in inventory.tsv, from a page-by-page search of M31 rolls 4, 5 and 6 and M43 rolls 2 and 3. No second copy of No. 188 or No. 214 is on the film.

02 The code

A one-part code: the numbers do not follow the alphabet (Spearman correlation between number and alphabetical rank 0.04). About 46% of its elements are whole words, 49% syllables and 5% single letters, with punctuation groups (1436 full stop, 1235 comma, 1312 semicolon, 1527 a stop often closing a passage). There are some homophones (of = 240 and 24; be = 641 and 108) and i and j share 502. Names are spelled out: Lo-u-i-si-a-n-a = 649.543.436.1466.925.892.925.

The editors of the Papers of Thomas Jefferson decoded Humphreys's letters of January 1791 "employing Code No. 8", Julian Boyd's name for the code Jefferson made in 1785; they describe it as partially reconstructed, and that partial key is an unpublished working file that we have not seen. Weber's United States Diplomatic Codes and Ciphers (1979) lists the code as not located. Compared with the codes of Weber's appendix, Code No. 16, King's, Carmichael's, Code No. 10 and Pinckney's, none is a relative.

03 How the table was built

  • Pairs. 3,460 code-and-word pairs, each cited by roll, frame and line: 579 where a clerk wrote the word over one group (1791), 620 from the clerks' running decipherments of 1800–01 split into elements here, and 2,261 aligned here to printed decoded texts (Papers of Thomas Jefferson 18 for 1791; Papers of James Madison, Secretary of State Series 1:50–53 for 1801). 3,368 went into the build.
  • Frozen table. 686 groups, with a recorded checksum; two groups with tied values were left out.
  • Held-out test. Planned in writing before the run. Despatch No. 262 (24 Feb 1801, M31 roll 6 frame 0235, interlined by a clerk, and printed in italics by F. L. Humphreys in 1917) was kept out and transcribed after the freeze: 129 of 135 scored groups (95.6%) decode to the clerk's element, against at most 3.0% for 200 shuffled tables. A stricter count on groups carrying one whole word gives 74 of 78. The misses are five groups absent from the table and one partial.
  • Language-model check. Underdetermined: the reading scores far above random keys, but a key annealed onto shuffled text scores as well, because there are only about two tokens per code number. The held-out test is the evidence for the table.

04 No. 214, 6 August 1799

Plain words come from the table; {braces} are context values supported elsewhere; [brackets with ?] are guesses; [numbers] are groups left unread; Humphreys's clear text is in parentheses.

(It is difficult to account for this strange fluctuating conduct respecting the recal of Mr. de Yrujo) upon any other [ground?] than by supposing he is con[ne?]ced with certain lea[d?][ing?] [men?] here in the profits of an exclusive [trade?] from the United States to the Spanish colonies and that his presence in America is necessary to com[ple?]te that operation.

... if the President should [646] to send him [away?] ... that the {reason}s for that [proceed?]ing should be explained in a letter from him {to} the King of Spain ... (I would request) to be honoured [with?] the orders of the President whether {to} remain at Lisbon or [proceed?] to the United States in ca{se} of my being obliged to leave this country.

(I told him I was not ignorant of the French intrigues on that subject and that I had good reason to believe) that the design of {re}covering Louisiana had {never} been re[197][1355]ished ... {ces}sion was [in?]serted in the [origin?]al project of the {trea}y of [Basl?]e ... Truguet had re[22]d the negociation with saave[dra?] ... he had [314]d against the ra{ti}fication of the treaty for want of that ces{sion} (which he said) would have been made but for trea[ch?]ery ... so long as he {re}mained in offi[ce?] I might rest as[sure?]d France {should} {never} get possession of Louisiana.

The belief about Louisiana is Humphreys's own, told to Urquijo, not a fact the despatch establishes. The man who "had [314]d against the ratification" is Guillemardet, the French ambassador, named in the clear text just before. Of the named details, "Truguet" is fairly strong, "Saavedra" and "treachery" medium, "Basle" weak, and "relinquished" three-quarters guessed. Passages b to d are short and mostly names, partly unread.

05 No. 188, 19 March 1799

(This Gentleman) [ga?]ve me in confidence reason to believe that the government of [Sweden?] {now} less [inclin?]d to use management with that of France than [126] [?405] of that letter and that it is even probable the Baron [935] sta[ël?] would soon retire from Paris

"This Gentleman" is the Swedish chargé at Madrid, Carl Gustaf Adlerberg, identified from the enclosures on frames 0296–0297. That makes Sweden likely but not settled: group 1097 occurs nowhere else. "de Staël" is weak. The passage fills the asterisks printed at this place in Naval Documents Related to the Quasi-War between the United States and France, vol. 2 (1935), p. 437.

06 April 1799

I have seen a letter from the {Dutch} minister at {Paris} which reports that (great clamours prevail particularly against the peculations of Scherer late Minister of War, that Rewbell went out of the Directory with thirty millions...)

"Paris" stands on 74[2?], whose last digit is cut by the binding; "Dutch" comes from a clerk's gloss on frame 0307 found after the test.

07 What remains open

  • 36 code numbers in Nos. 188 and 214 with no value in the frozen table (37 with group 1290 of the April passage), among them 1097, 864, 126, 314, 22, 197, 1355, 646, 766, 713, 983 and 426. Each was searched in every transcription and in both machine readings of every code page found; none occurs a second time with a decipherment. Some are given context values, marked as such.
  • 12 groups of No. 214 with digits hidden in the binding (frames 0415 and 0417). No second copy is on the film; the volume itself or the copies sent by other ships would restore them.
  • Doubtful readings: 827 or 927 on frame 0415 line 7 stays open; [?]405 in No. 188 has an unclear first digit.
  • Nothing has been checked by a person; the transcriptions, pairs, test and readings are AI agents' work, checked by a second AI agent. The readings were read as sentences at integration on 24 Nov 2026 (No. 214 B, No. 188 B, April 1799 A).

08 Earlier work

The clerks deciphered many Humphreys despatches on receipt; whether the Department read Nos. 188 and 214 in 1799 is not established. F. L. Humphreys's Life and Times of David Humphreys (1917) prints the 1791 letters and the coded part of No. 262, not these. Not found in: Naval Documents vol. 2 and vol. 4 (text search only), American State Papers, Foreign Relations vol. 2, the calendar of the Pickering Papers, Tomokiyo's Cryptiana (which describes the code without a key) or Lasry's GL.htm. Not searched: Naval Documents vols. 1 and 5–7, Humphreys's papers at Yale and the New Haven Museum, and the books by Whitaker, Lyon and DeConde.
