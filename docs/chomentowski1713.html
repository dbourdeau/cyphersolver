Adrianople and Constantinople → Hetman Sieniawski · three-digit syllabary in alphabetical bands · 1713–1714 · existing decipherment, partial

Stanisław Chomentowski at the Porte to Hetman Sieniawski, 1713–1714

→

Three letters of the Polish envoy to the Sultan, partly in a syllabic code that a second hand deciphered between the lines; the decipherment was transcribed and checked. Two letters of 1713 give sentences; the third, of 1714, with the faintest glosses, is decoded, not read.

At a glanceExisting decipherment · partial (27% of the cipher)

The letters
Stanisław Chomentowski, voivode of Mazovia and Polish envoy at the Porte, to Hetman Adam Mikołaj Sieniawski: Adrianople 9 May 1713, an undated letter of 1713, and (probably his) a letter from Constantinople of May–August 1714.
Where
Archiwum Narodowe w Krakowie, Zbiór Dzieduszyckich 24/11 no. 116 (DECODE R7484, R7485, catalogued “Krakow_UNK_3/4” with no shelfmark) and 26/32 nr 13 pp. 31–34 (R7476).
The cipher
Three-digit numbers for syllables, in alphabetical bands (a 6–8, b 14–21 … z 320–333), names heading their band (Cesarz 42, Moskwa 160, Turcy 271), mixed with clear Polish.
How it was deciphered
A second hand glossed the numbers syllable by syllable between the lines, and on R7485 also wrote the passage out in clear. The glosses were transcribed and checked number by number; the faint ones of R7476 were re-read at high contrast.
What it says
The Seraskier and the Khan, the road to Bender, the hospodar of Wallachia: “Seraskierowi Turcy wierzyc nie chcą i Han[o]wi”.
Still open
R7476 as a whole: about forty doubtful words under glosses unreadable on the DECODE images, so it is decoded, not read; 83 of its tokens give no word. In R7484, 18 tokens in three short passages; the faint clear body of R7485.

DECODE catalogues these records with letters in other keys, which have their own pages (a different cipher in each): Brâncoveanu and Cantacuzino (sieniawski1708), news from Bender in 1709 (turculet1709), an adviser’s letter (r7475) and a Vienna agent to Baron Schenck (schenck1706).

01 The letters

R7484 and R7485 are one archival unit, Zbiór Dzieduszyckich 24/11 no. 116 (the archive wrapper gives it; DECODE has “Krakow_UNK_3/4”). R7484 is dated Adrianople, 9 May 1713. R7485 is undated; its cipher passage is written out in clear at the head of the page by the hand that glossed it. In the writer’s spelling, as the figures carry it (restored on 23 October 2026 from a modernised version; slips in brackets): “Seraskierowi Turcy wierzyc nie chcą i Han[o]wi, i dla tego mnie prosto wo[ła]ia. Seraskier pisał do hospodara, aby mi perswadował, y namawiał, zebym na Bender iechał.”

R7476 (nr 13 pp. 31–34) was bound with the Brâncoveanu letters. Code XVIII is glossed Hospodar Multański, Brâncoveanu, who was held in Yedikule from April 1714, so the letter is of May–August 1714, from Constantinople. Its partial reading, in the writer’s spelling since 23 October 2026, has “on z tod nie ruszy, puki m[u] Porta nie da takiego konwoiu”, “z listu przeietego” and “sołtan gałga uciekł do Czerkies”, but about forty words of pp. 31–32 are doubtful, under glosses that cannot be read at three to three and a half times on the DECODE images: R7476 is decoded, not read (explicit-hole share 0.524, below the bar once the doubtful words count as holes).

02 The syllabary, and the checks

The syllabary was rebuilt from the glosses of the three letters. Its numbers run in alphabetical bands, and the names of persons and powers stand at the head of their letter’s band (Cesarz 42 among the c’s, Moskwa 160, Turcy 271), so a number whose gloss is lost can be bounded by its neighbours.

  • R7485: 96 of 96 tokens give sense; measured on the words of the reading, the letter builds coherent sentences but reads at 88.8%, under the 95% bar. The faint clear body of pp. 115–117 is transcribed only in part.
  • R7484: 495 of 513 (96.5%; three clear date figures taken out of the count); measured on the words of the reading, it builds coherent sentences but reads at 91.6%, under the 95% bar. Code 76 is ferman (two glosses, the f band, and R7476’s “dano ferman”); slips settled (191 for 181 in Hanowi; 160 is Moskwa, not 169).
  • R7476: 1,538 of 1,621 tokens have a value (94.9%), but the reading does not hold as sentences (above). Its glosses are faint. They were re-read at high contrast, the bad stretches re-transcribed digit by digit at 2.5–3 times (this hand closes its 7 like a 4), candidate words scored against a Polish model and checked against the code’s bands, and names checked against Perłakowski’s study of the embassy (Jan Spiegel, “Pan Szpigieł”; Franciszek Goltz). Codes 135 Kanclerz, 215 Porta, 280 u and 310 x were added.

03 What remains uncertain

  • R7476 is decoded, not read: about forty [?word]s on p. 31 lines 19–34 and p. 32 lines 9–36 sit under glosses unreadable at three to three and a half times. Of its 1,621 tokens, 83 give no word: 67 whose syllables are known from gloss or code but form no word after seven passes, 8 in names not identified (code 216, glossed “Pułta”), five one-off codes with no gloss (2, 224, 266, 299 twice), three illegible.
  • The readings of R7484 and R7485 were in modernised Polish until 23 October 2026; they now give each word as its figures carry it (mowio, widzoc, bedoc), with the encipherer’s slips emended in brackets. R7484: 18 of 513 tokens unread, in three short passages whose syllables are certain but whose words are not recovered (“umowi-ia t-r-ze-y s-za psuje”), and code 112, whose gloss is a flourish.
  • R7476 is ascribed to Chomentowski by its content and code, not by a signature.

p. 31
One cannot [?understand] what they are keeping us for and do not dispatch us, nor give us the freedom that was granted,
nor the meetings with the envoys, nor the visits that are due. Although I lately sent presents to the vizier and his
kehaya, the vizier received them gratefully and declared that the dispatch will come, [?that] we will not be kept
long; but I know [better]. To [?pay] visits to Their Graces the envoys residing at Galata they cannot [?allow]; they did
allow it indeed, but on condition that a memorial be handed in for each visit, which we could not accept for two
reasons. First, that they could allow one and not another, and so make a difference without it; second, that we could
by no means allow this prejudice. Better that it remain their courtesy than our condescension. [?Our detention],
moreover, [comes from this], that they fear [?] - [?lest] it come from this - that they want to [?press] Your Grace's
passage of the King of Sweden, and to keep us here as a pledge. But it seems to me that if [?] the Turks had sent to
Your Grace about the treaty, it [?cannot be known], [?without] the [?commissioners] not seeing the treaty from the Porte,
which is to be in the envoys' hands; secondly, not being secure of the friendship of the Porte, since with these
documents I do not see the envoy, and such [...] you give me, Your Grace, [...] [?quicker] for their service, haste,
and not [?having] [...] anyone [...] and dared. To be informed more fully [...] [?Istanbul] [...]; I may have [?word]
[?information] that the Porte [?is sending] to the King of Sweden, letting [?me] know that it has finished with me;
and when [he says] that he wants to move from here, this bad lord [answered] that on

R7484, Adrianople, 9 May 1713 read in part

Chomentowski, voivode of Mazovia and Polish envoy at the Porte, to Hetman Sieniawski: the Kapudan Pasha, made vizier, began to change course; a public divan on 24 April declared war sub ignoto nomine, with fear of a march on Chocim and the envoys sent to Yedikule; on the 26th the vizier was deposed and strangled, so things are in confusion.

Summary: Chomentowski, Polish envoy at the Porte, writes from Adrianople on 9 May 1713. The Porte held a public divan
on 24 April at which war was declared "sub ignoto nomine"; the envoys feared being sent to the Yedikule; the Turks
wanted to force the Swedish king to recognise Stanisław or elect a third candidate, not wishing Augustus out of fear of
the German and Muscovite alliance; someone "being with the Sultan" (wezyr ... u Cesarza) was thrown down and strangled (reading of p. 113 L1-L2 uncertain); nothing is

p. 111
Monseigneur.
I wrote to Your Grace in my last letter by way of Vienna that new changes keep being made here.
Today [?they agree] [...] spoils the resolution.
The Kapudan Pasha, having become vizier, began to change; some say that he has now been talked round, others that

For having confidence [...] of the ferman, and to me this [...] could be.

p. 112
seeing the King.

Time itself [?will show] the longed-for [...] personaliter [in person] [...] I remain Your Grace's and benefactor's
