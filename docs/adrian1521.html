Vitoria → Charles V · nomenclator of code groups and signs · 30 December 1521

Adrian of Utrecht, the Admiral and the Constable of Castile to Charles V, Vitoria, 30 December 1521

→

Go to the full text ↓

Archivo General de Simancas, Estado leg. 8 no. 150 · two leaves, “cifrada prácticamente en su totalidad” · the last letter the three governors signed together, ten days before Adrian was elected pope

Deciphered in the nineteenth century by Claudio Pérez Gredilla, keeper of Simancas, and printed by Manuel Danvila in 1899. His text was aligned here against the ciphertext, and the key was rebuilt from the alignment. The key corrects the dead king from England to Portugal and decodes or corrects about seventeen of the roughly twenty-five groups Gredilla left blank; about ten stay open.

01 The letter

In 1521 Castile was governed for the absent Charles V by three men: Adrian of Utrecht, cardinal of Tortosa and Charles’s old tutor, whom he had left as regent in 1520, and the two great nobles added to him during the revolt of the Comunidades, Fadrique Enríquez, Admiral of Castile, and Íñigo de Velasco, Constable of Castile. The letter is signed by all three: Adrian in French, Votre treshumble serviteur, el Cardenal dertusense, then the Admiral and the Constable. It is dated Vitoria, 30 December 1521. On 9 January 1522 the conclave in Rome elected Adrian pope; Danvila calls this the last letter the three governors signed together.

It covers two sheets, of which three sides are cipher. Only the address and the first two and a half lines are in clear; after La una es the scribe goes into the code and stays there until the dateline, De bitoria a xxx de deziembre de D.xxj.

02 Found in print

PARES gave the date and the place. An OCR search of the Internet Archive scan of Memorial Histórico Español 38 (memorialhistri38realuoft) for that date at Vitoria found the letter on p. 703, under the heading Carta cifrada de los Gobernadores de Castilla al Emperador, fecha en Vitoria á 30 de Diciembre de 1521. Danvila’s note (3) gives the credit: Esta traducción se debe á D. Claudio Pérez Gredilla, Jefe del Archivo general de Simancas, sin que á pesar de su especial competencia haya podido descifrar algunas palabras, que se trasladan tal como resultan en el original. Note (4) gives the reference as Arch. gen. de Sim., Estado, leg. 8.°, fol. 150, the number stamped on the leaf. Neither PARES nor the lists of ciphers without a decipherment mention the edition.

Gredilla’s text is good. Measured against the cipher, it holds through the whole letter. What it leaves out falls into three kinds. There are about twenty-five code groups printed in italics as he found them (fun, suf, ral, rip, pah xiu, top te, rol, xep, lag, fac, fap, fir, xin, dopca, nag, pij and others). There are a few groups dropped without a mark (for twice; xac in “[xac] y franceses”). And there is one confident reading that is wrong, the King of England. A full transcription of his text from the page scans is targets/adrian1521/gredilla_1880s.txt.

03 The cipher, and the key rebuilt by alignment

PARES serves these leaves at about 1,000 pixels on the long side and no larger, so the ciphertext was transcribed from upscaled line crops. The code groups, written in ordinary letters, transcribe reliably. The signs of the spelling alphabet are harder to transcribe, and several are still rough. The transcription was then cut into chunks at each xif, the group that stands for V. M. (vuestra Majestad): 19 in the cipher as transcribed, against 21 “V. M.” in Gredilla. Between these fixed points, cipher and Spanish were matched word by word. The alignment never has to force a reading, and every value below comes out of it.

The system is a mixed nomenclator:

  • Code groups of three letters, sometimes two, stand for words and also for syllables inside words. log is en both as a word and in s-en-tirá and fallecimi-en-to. lan is de in pier-de and de-udo. dop is ur- in urca.
  • Inflection by suffix. A sign after a group changes the ending: mix la, mix= las; fom cosa, fom= cosas.
  • A homophonic alphabet of letters, figures and signs spells everything else: a = α, e4, Δ; c = a, 6, σ; d = q, z; e = ℓℓ; g = C; o = +; p = R, ff; r = nt, G; s = =; t = g, d; u/v = ≠T. These values are good enough to follow every spelled word against Gredilla’s text, but at this resolution the homophones are not all separated. The table is enough to follow the text; it is not a finished key.
  • A triple-barred staff and the sign P4 open and close paragraphs.

The code is one-part in blocks. The initial letter of a group follows the plaintext alphabet: r for words in a- (agora, al, alianza, amistad, antes, allá), f for c- (con, como, cosa, Consejo, contra), l/L for d–f (de, del, dicho, el, en, Francia, franceses), n and m for h–l, p for m–n (mar, mucha, negocios, no), s for o–r (otra, para, parece, por, que), t for r–t (Rey, razón, salvo, su, tiene), d for u-, x for v–y (V. M., voluntad, ya, yo). The order inside a block cannot be recovered from one letter. The block structure matters, because it limits which words an undecoded group can be.

04 The wrong king

Gredilla’s first sentence of news reads la una es el fallecimiento del Rey de Inglaterra, de que avemos tenido la pena que es razón. Henry VIII was alive in December 1521 and lived until 1547. The letter itself says who died:

  • the loss is felt by la Reina de Portugal y hermana de V. M., that is Leonor of Austria, Charles’s sister, married to Manuel I in 1518 and widowed on 13 December 1521;
  • the dead king was tan buen amigo y deudo de V. M., and now there is este moço el Rey que sucede en aquel Reino: João III, nineteen, with people about him que no le dexan las orejas ociosas;
  • los Reyes de Portugal tienen [alianza] antigua con los de Francia, which the French will work on;
  • Charles is asked to send someone to the new king para demostrarle el sentimiento que tiene del fallecimiento del Rey su padre, and to visit and console a la Reina.

In the cipher, the dead king is Len top (del Rey) followed by one short group, which the notes read as suz. Gredilla left the same group blank at envié al dicho [suz] una persona, where the sense is the new King of Portugal. Inglaterra is spelled nowhere. On line 6, where Gredilla correctly reads la Reina de Portugal, Portugal is spelled out letter by letter after top, with the same signs (C = g, roc = al) as the other spelled Portugal on line 16. The correction does not depend on reading that one group. Everything the letter says about the king fits Manuel and Portugal, and nothing fits England.

05 The groups decoded now

Most of the new readings come from the letter itself. A group Gredilla left blank in one place is often spelled out, or deciphered by Gredilla himself, in another. The marks follow the notes: ** certain (the same group is decoded or spelled elsewhere in the letter), * probable (context, plus the initial-letter block the group must belong to), ? a guess.

06 What the letter says

Gredilla’s text, lightly normalised; bold a group now decoded or corrected, with its confidence mark; [brackets] still undecoded

La una es el fallecimiento del Rey de Portugal**, de que avemos tenido la pena que es razón, para ello así por lo que pierde y sentirá la Reina de Portugal y hermana de V. M., como** porque nos parece que el fallecimiento del dicho Rey ha acaecido en coyuntura? que no [suf] a los negocios de V. M., porque la amistad y deudo que con él tenía estava tan seguro lo que tocava a aquella parte, y él se mostrava tan buen amigo y deudo de V. M.; y agora* con este moço el Rey que sucede en aquel Reino, y con que diz que tiene cabe sí algunos que no le dexan las orejas ociosas, no sabemos la demostración que querrá hacer; porque como** V. M. debe ya tener noticia, los Reyes de Portugal tienen alianza* antigua con los de Francia, y creemos que los franceses no estarán perezosos en solicitar por aquella vía lo que haze a su propósito.

In English, the letter runs as follows. Since writing the general letter that goes with this one, a duplicate of one sent some days ago, two matters of substance have arisen.

The King of Portugal. The governors grieve at his death, for the sake of the Queen, Charles’s sister, and because it comes at a bad moment for Charles’s affairs. With Manuel as friend and kinsman, that side was secure. Now there is a young king whose advisers do not leave his ears idle, and no one knows which way he will turn. The kings of Portugal have an old alliance with France, and the French will not be slow to use it. The governors are sending a prelate or a gentleman from Spain to visit and console the Queen. They ask Charles to do the same before the King of France takes the lead: to send a person of his household to the new king to show his sorrow at his father’s death, to ask that he succeed his father in the love, kinship and alliance between the two houses, and to promise that Charles will keep it up with great goodwill*. The same envoy should visit the Queen on Charles’s behalf.

The hulks. Storms at sea have driven some urcas, the big northern cargo ships, onto the Spanish coast. One lies at San Sebastián, detained and well guarded. Two are at Laredo, and the governors are trying to hold them, but there is no force at hand to compel them. They mean to keep whatever they can seize, cargo and all, under sequestration until Charles decides. The difficulty is that the ships sail under a safe-conduct from Charles; a copy goes with this dispatch. The councillors present, and the Consejo** Real at Burgos, hold that the safe-conduct rests only on the suspension of the marcas represalias, the letters of reprisal. Charles cannot have meant that, if [xac] were at war against* him, their hulks should go safely haciendo guerra y paz a su voluntad*. If [xac] join the French, the ships could be treated as good prize. If they stay neutral, they might be won over with [tib], or Charles might use the matter for reasons of state. In either case the French would take the hulks, by fair means or foul, for their fleet if the war between [Lag] and France goes on, and that fleet is said to be growing very large. The governors ask for orders.

Postscript. After writing, they learned that the Laredo hulks had set sail, so Charles need decide only about the one at San Sebastián, which remains and will be kept under good guard until he gives other orders.

The full corrected text, with every change marked, is targets/adrian1521/plaintext_corrected.txt.

07 What remains uncertain

  • Still undecoded (about ten groups): suf (que no [suf] a los negocios; re-examined 30 Sept 2026: the line reads Log fun per suf =δ roc t= pex, so Gredilla’s “que no” stands for the single group per (m–n block), and two spelled signs, s and a δ-like sign, follow suf (o–r block). en coyuntura muy peligro-sa a los negocios would fit the blocks and the sense, but only if that sign is a; the same sign is y in pierde y sentirá (f. 1r l. 5), so the reading is a guess and is not put in the text); xub and yo after urcas; ren nod before procurar; xac, a people (que [xac] y franceses son a unirse, si [xac] están neutrales); sif; tib; xit; Lag, one of the belligerents with France; and ne (la or mala fortuna). The Scots fit xac in sense: Albany landed in Scotland with French backing in November 1521, and reprisals were a Scots–Flemish grievance. But the group sits in the x-block, which should hold words in v–z, so it is not proposed.
  • The group after Len top. In this hand r and z look much alike. The working transcription writes the group as sur, which is que, and the notes read it as suz. Que makes no sense in that place, and Gredilla printed a group suz where the new king is meant, so suz = Portugal is probable, not certain. The Portugal correction itself does not rest on this group (section 04).
  • The spelling alphabet is good enough to follow every spelled word against Gredilla, but its homophones are not all separated at the 1,000-pixel resolution PARES serves. Several sign readings in the transcription are marked rough.
  • The probable readings (*) come from sense plus the initial-letter block. They are not confirmed by a second occurrence, and the guesses (?) are only that.
  • No shared key. Adrian’s own ciphered letters with decipherments were checked, in the hope that one of them would supply the key. None does. Estado 8/112 (28 July 1520) uses a cipher of signs and figures. PTR 2/1/94, 99, 100 and 101 (October–December 1521) use Adrian’s dotted trigraph code (leb. nud. sa. cab.), in which V. M., que, de and en have other groups. The joint letter is in a third code, probably the Admiral’s or the Constable’s. The Admiral’s 1521 cipher letters survive in clear copies (PTR 1/96/5, 9, 12, 13, 44, 45), but their originals were not found on PARES. A sibling in the same code, with its decipherment, is the way to close the open groups.

The three governors of Castile (Adrian of Utrecht, the Admiral and the Constable) write to Charles V ten days before Adrian's election as pope, on the consequences of the death of Manuel I of Portugal (13 Dec 1521): the widowed queen Leonor, Charles's sister, the young João III, and Portugal's old alliance with France. plaintext_corrected.txt is the project's corrected text; gredilla_1880s.txt is Claudio Pérez Gredilla's decipherment as printed by Danvila (1899), which misreads Portugal as 'Inglaterra'.

# AGS Estado leg. 8 no. 150, the three governors of Castile (Adrian of Utrecht, the Admiral, the Constable) to Charles V, Vitoria, 30 Dec 1521: English translation

Sacred Caesarean Catholic Majesty,

After the other general letter that goes with this one was written, which is a duplicate of another that we sent to
Your Majesty some days ago, two substantial matters have arisen of which to inform you. The first is the death of the
King of Portugal, for which we have felt the grief that is due, both for what the Queen of Portugal, Your Majesty's
sister, loses and will feel, and because it seems to us that the death of the said King has happened at a
[?juncture] that does not [suf] Your Majesty's affairs; for through the friendship and kinship you had with him,
everything touching that quarter was so secure, and he showed himself so good a friend and kinsman of Your Majesty.
And now, with this young man who succeeds as King in that realm, and since it is said he has about him some who do
not leave his ears idle, we do not know what attitude he will choose to show; for, as Your Majesty must already know,
the Kings of Portugal have an ancient alliance with those of France, and we believe the French will not be slow to
pursue by that route what serves their purpose. To remedy this, and to try to continue Your Majesty's kinship and
friendship with the said King, and to visit and console the Queen as is right and as she needs, we have resolved to
send from here a prelate or a lightly equipped gentleman; and since we believe Your Majesty will be pleased to do the
same from there, we beg you, before the King of France takes the lead, to send to the said Portugal a person who is
kin to your house, to show him the sorrow you feel at the death of the King his father, so dear to you and so close a
kinsman, and to ask him to be pleased to succeed to the love, kinship and alliance that used to exist between Your
Majesty and his father, offering him that on Your Majesty's part all this will be kept up with great goodwill, and
whatever more Your Majesty may command; and the same person will visit and console on your behalf the Queen, for
which she has [?great] [?grief]. And since Your Majesty will consider and provide the other things that the case
requires, there is no need to write more about it.

The other matter is that some days ago, with the [?bad] fortune they have had at sea, hulks (urcas) [xub] [yo]as have
put in to the seas of Spain: one of them at San Sebastián, which is detained and kept very securely where they come
in from the cove of Guipúzcoa, where it now is; and the other two at Laredo, for whose detention [ren] [nod] to
procure there; but we do not know whether they will let themselves be detained, because at present there is no force
there to compel them. Those that can be embargoed, our purpose is to keep sequestered, both the ships and the goods
that come in them, until we have consulted Your Majesty. The question is this: although they come under Your
Majesty's safe-conduct, a copy of which will go with this despatch, it has seemed to those of the [?Council] who were
here, and also to those of the Royal Council who are at Burgos, whom we informed of it, that the whole basis of the
safe-conduct depends on the letters of marque and reprisal; and that, notwithstanding that it does say the fleet of
war is not understood to alter the safe-conduct, it is still understood to depend on that and to extend to the
suspension of the letters of reprisal and no further; that it cannot reasonably be believed that Your Majesty granted
the safe-conduct, or meant that, in case [xac] were at war against him, their hulks should go safely, making war and
peace at their will; and that if he were to come, as we are told he is coming, and [xac] and the French are to join
the [sif], and Your Majesty in the war being waged in [?Italy], this should be held good prize; and that if [xac] are
neutral with Your Majesty, they should be won over with [tib]... or that Your Majesty should take advantage of this
in the considerations of state that are held with them. I beg Your Majesty to have the circumstances that depend on
this looked into, and to write to us at once what you are pleased that we should do, so that it may be done as is
right. And besides the above, it seems to us well that Your Majesty should know that, in case they come to act in aid
or company of the said French and it is presumed that these hulks would go there, we believe that the French would
take them by fair means or foul to use them in their fleet, if the war between [Lag] and France is to go on; for which
the said hulks would not be worth much, nor the French fleet, which they are already beginning to make very large, as
we are told today. And since we inform Your Majesty of what we can learn, send us [xit] word of the decision on all of
it.

After the above was written we learned that the hulks at Laredo have set sail, so that Your Majesty has to decide
only about the hulk at San Sebastián, which still remains and will be kept securely until Your Majesty orders
otherwise. From Vitoria, 30 December 1521.

Your most humble servant. — The Cardinal of Tortosa. — The Admiral. — The Constable.
