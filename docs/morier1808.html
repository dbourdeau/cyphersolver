Bombay → Lord Minto, Calcutta · homophonic cipher in figures 2–99 · 28 April 1808 · key recovered from the ciphertext alone

Harford Jones to Lord Minto, Bombay, 28 April 1808

Go to the full text ↓

A letter from the British envoy to Persia to the Governor General, copied in figures by his private secretary; the key came from the figures alone and the whole letter reads as English.

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · complete

The letter
Sir Harford Jones to the Governor General, Lord Minto, Bombay, 28 April 1808: a true copy in figures signed by Jas. Morier, private secretary. It was enclosed in Jones's letter of 30 April 1808 to the Secret Committee. A second figure copy of it carries the docket "Recd 6 June 09". Also on the volume: the ninth paragraph of Minto's instructions to Malcolm, in figures, in two copies.
Where
British Library, India Office Records, Political and Secret, IOR/L/PS/9/67, items 18 to 21. The images are free on the Qatar Digital Library (links below, not copied here). Catalogue entry 2649, a lead from the Koç/Ottoman deep sweep.
The cipher
A homophonic substitution of letters by figures 2–99, two to five figures for a letter, one letter each for u/v and i/j. A stop ends a word and a comma joins figures within a word. A few 3- and 4-figure groups stand for words (8674 is Persia).
How it was read
An AI model transcribed the QDL images and solved the key by simulated annealing against an English 4-gram model, on the two copies of the letter only. The key was then tested on text it had not seen: the first test failed its bar, the second, frozen before the new text was transcribed (by the contributor's record), passed.
What it says
Jones, just arrived at Bombay, finds that General Malcolm has already gone into the Persian Gulf under Minto's authority. To avoid "the appearance of clashing authorities in Persia" he suspends his own departure until Minto's orders.
Still open
The place name in "from ... Roads"; the code words for Bombay and French and the figure signature are probable, not sure. No clear copy of the letter was found.
Credit
Lead: Daniel Bourdeau. Images fetched in Chrome, and the solution contributed in pull request #705: Brice Dudout, with the AI model's work. The contemporary decipherment in the margin of /19 was used only as a check, after the reading.

01 The documents

Four items in one British Library file, all in figures:

  • /18: Jones to Minto, Bombay, 28 April 1808, 20 images, a "True Copy" in figures signed Jas. Morier, Priv. Sec.
  • /20: a second figure copy of the same letter, 12 images. It ends "I have the honor to be &c" in clear and a signature in figures. Docket "Recd 6 June 09".
  • /19 and /21: two figure copies of the ninth paragraph of Minto's instructions to Malcolm. /19 has the clear text written in its margin; /21 has none.

The two copies of the letter served to collate each other. The figures are the project's own transcription by an AI model from QDL images that Brice Dudout fetched in Chrome. Nothing in the files records a person checking a transcription against the images.

02 The cipher

The letter is a homophonic substitution of letters by figures from 2 to 99. A letter has two to five figures: e is 2, 6, 8, 40, 74 (and 94), t is 7, 44, 50, 77, h is 5, 60. The cipher uses one letter for u and v and one for i and j. A stop ends a word and a comma joins figures within a word, but the copyists cut and join words freely.

A few groups of three or four figures stand for words: 8674 is Persia and takes suffixes (8674,90,66 is Persians). 4040 is French and 630 is Bombay, both probable from context and the heading. The heading, "630. 9,63,79,98,68. 128. 218081.", reads Bombay April 28 1808; the figures 1 and 2 are nulls in the date and in the signature.

It is not the code of Jones's page-and-word code book of 1798–1802 (targets/jones1802): there each group stands for a word at a page, here each group stands for a letter, and no value from that code applies. That is a different cipher, kept in its own folder.

03 How the key was found

The key (62 figures in key.json) was solved by simulated annealing over figure-to-letter maps, scored by an English 4-gram model with the stops kept as word boundaries. The model came from a 19th-century English diplomatic corpus. It was solved on /18 and /20 only (2,640 figures, 600 words). Every restart converged on the same readable English, beginning "the inclosed copy of a letter No. 2 addressed by me on the following day to the Hon. the Gov. here". Four values were then fixed by hand: 34 is k, 2 is e, 6 is e, and 24 is the numeral 24. Two more figures turned up after the tests: 88 is z (authori[z]ed) and 94 is e (therefor[e]); they are added in the decoder, not in the frozen key.

No sibling key was used. The key of Jones's 1802 code book was tried and does not apply (see section 02).

04 The two held-out tests

First test, failed. The enclosure /21 (316 figures, 72 words) was held out. The measure was the share of decoded words found in the corpus vocabulary: 0.729, against a best control of 0.157 over 200 within-class key shuffles. The bar was 0.85, so the test failed. No word was misread: the misses were the copyists' cuts (g en, instructi ons), names (Malcolms, Bagdad, Pashaw, Harford, Jones), u/v spellings (enuoy, euent) and two copy errors (cort, accreditted). That portion is burned.

Second test, passed. The second measure was frozen before the new text was transcribed. It held out /18 images 0012–0014 (444 figures, 94 words) with the key unchanged. It allows u/v and i/j and a fixed list of names, and counts cuts and joins separately. The score was 0.968 (86 words, 2 names, 2 splits, 1 join, 3 fails), against a best control of 0.340; the bar was the larger of 0.85 and 0.640. The strict share of plain dictionary words alone was 0.915. The second measure was written after the first failure, with its misses in view. That the text was transcribed after the measure was frozen rests on the contributor's record: the transcription's hash is not in the files.

A statistical validation of the finished key (the project's _validate.py) gives a reading score of −2.21 per letter over 3,844 tokens, against −6.69 for random keys (best of 200: −5.96) and a best of −4.43 for a key annealed onto shuffled ciphertext. Verdict: pass. An agent re-ran the decode and this validation for the maintainers and obtained the same figures. The two measuring scripts were not re-run, because they point to the contributor's local corpus and word list.

05 The reading

The whole letter reads from the heading to the signature, in the order of the pages (f. 2v–3r and ff. 8v and 9v–10r were deciphered from the second copy). Marks: {Bombay}, {French} and {Persia} are code words with values from context; [x] is a letter restored where both copies miswrite it (relin[q]uish, thin[k], o[cc]urred, [s]entiments, Tur[k]ey, a[d]vantage). The full text is under Full texts below.

I think it is to be lamented that Gen. Malcolm, as I must believe him aware that the Sapphire could not be long before she arrived at {Bombay}, had not thought it expedient to have suspended his departure hence for a few days ... for surely nothing could be more impolitic than to suffer even the appearance of clashing authorities in {Persia}, and nothing more unjustifiable than putting the public to the expence of separate missions to the same court.

Jones writes that he has received instructions from His Majesty's Ministers and the Secret Committee and that his mission is under His Majesty's immediate sanction. He says he would not relinquish his mission without the King's command, but suspends it in deference to Minto. He adds that since the French appeared at Tehran the Persians know that national ministers can only be accredited by His Majesty.

The enclosure is the ninth paragraph of Minto's instructions to Malcolm: Malcolm is to carry credentials as envoy or ambassador to the court of Persia and to the Pashaw of Bagdad, to present if he finds it practicable and expedient, and if he finds neither Sir Harford Jones nor any other person accredited by His Majesty in Persia. The "watch it decipher" strip decodes it figure by figure.

The reading was graded A (coherent, with scattered holes) for /18, /20 and /21 by an AI agent's review for the maintainers; this is a model's grade, and a person has not checked it.

06 What remains open

  • The opening "from ‹to› Roads": both copies write 44,36, which reads "to". A copying slip for a place name; the place is not read.
  • 630 is Bombay and 4040 is French: probable, from the heading, the docket and the context; no other copy gives a spelling.
  • The figure signature, "Har(f)ord J(o)nes", is probable, with nulls 1 and 2.
  • 88 is z rests on one word.
  • Strict read share over the cipher words of the letter: 646 of 654 (0.988). The enclosure has no hole.
  • The QDL images were not seen by the integration check (Cloudflare 403 to curl); the transcription's soundness there rests on the decode reading as continuous English over 3,844 tokens. The images were not compared figure by figure by a person.

07 Earlier work

No decipherment of the letter was found in the BL or QDL records or in print. For the enclosure, /19 carries in its margin the clear text of the ninth paragraph, written at the time. The reading of /21 was made from the key alone before /19 was seen, by the contributor's record, and agrees with it word for word. A later reader pencilled a few letters between the lines of /18 ff. 7–8 (for example "receiving"); that partial decipherment was not used. A clear copy of the letter itself may be among Minto's papers or the Bengal Secret enclosures; that was not checked. (Who James Morier and Harford Jones were, beyond what the letter shows, is not from the sources and was not checked here.)

# Harford Jones to Lord Minto, Bombay, 28 April 1808 (IOR/L/PS/9/67/18, with the second copy /20): the reading

English, so no translation. The cipher writes u for both u and v, and i for both i and j. Here they are given as
v and j where the word needs them (have, Majesty). The copyists' word cuts are joined. Marks used:
- {Persia} = a 3- or 4-figure code word, value from context: 8674 = Persia (sure); 630 = Bombay (probable,
  from the heading and the docket); 4040 = French (probable).
- [x] = a letter restored where both copies miswrite it: relin[q]uish, thin[k], o[cc]urred, [s]entiments, Tur[k]ey, a[d]vantage.
- ‹…› = not read.
Abbreviations are kept as written in the cipher (Excel., Gov., Gen., Brig., Rt. Hon., inst.).
Order of the pages: /18 ff. 1-10 where present; f. 2v-3r (images 0007-0009 of /18) and ff. 8v and 9v-10r
(images 0016, 0018-0019) were read from the second copy /20 (images 0004, 0008, 0009).

## Reading

{Bombay} April 28 1808.

To His Excellency the Rt. Hon. Lord Minto, Governor General.

My Lord,

I have the honor to enclose the duplicate of my address No. 2 to your Excel. from ‹to› Roads, and to inform you of
my arrival here on the 24 inst. The inclosed copy of a letter No. 2, addressed by me on the following day to the
Hon. the Gov. here, will communicate to your Excel. the resolution I formed on hearing that Brig. Gen. Malcolm had
previously to my arrival proceeded into the {Persian} Gulph under a special authority from your Excel. The Hon.
the Gov. in Council having favored me with the perusal of the papers which I requested, I lose no time in
submitting to you the ideas which occur to me relative to the situation in which I find myself placed, premising
that I wish to shape my conduct in such manner as may be most beneficial to the public service and agreeable to
your Excel.

I think it is to be lamented that Gen. Malcolm, as I must believe him aware that the Sapphire could not be long
before she arrived at {Bombay}, had not thought it expedient to have suspended his departure hence for a few days,
since, had he even ultimately thought it his duty to proceed on his mission, I presume it might have o[cc]urred to
him that, from my being in possession of the [s]entiments and wishes of His Majesty's Ministers and the Hon. the
Secret Committee on the subject of our relations with {Persia} and the eastern part of Tur[k]ey, the a[d]vantage he
would have derived from a personal communication with me would more than have compensated for the trifling delay
in his departure from {Bombay} which would have been necessary to procure it. Although it appears clear to me from
the ninth paragraph of your Excel.'s instructions to Gen. Malcolm that it is not your intention his mission should
suspend, much less supersede, a mission under the immediate sanction of His Majesty, yet for reasons which must be
obvious to your Excel. I consider it most prudent to suspend my departure for {Persia} until I am honored with
your Excel.'s commands; for surely nothing could be more impolitic than to suffer even the appearance of clashing
authorities in {Persia}, and nothing more unjustifiable than putting the public to the expence of separate
missions to the same court.

I do not perceive the smallest disadvantage to the public service that can possibly arise from the line of
conduct I have adopted; and though I should not think myself authorized under any circumstances, except those of
receiving His Majesty's commands to do so, to annihilate or relin[q]uish the mission of which he has been
graciously pleased to honor me with the charge, yet I thin[k] His Majesty's Ministers cannot blame me for
suspending it to the extent I now do, in deference to your Excel. and for the weighty and solid reasons mentioned

To His Excellency the Rt. Hon. Lord Minto, Governor General.

My Lord,

I have the honor to enclose the duplicate of my address No. 2 to your Excel. from ‹to› Roads, and to inform you of

Secret Committee on the subject of our relations with {Persia} and the eastern part of Tur[k]ey, the a[d]vantage he

# Enclosure: ninth paragraph of Minto's instructions to Malcolm (IOR/L/PS/9/67/21, copy /19): the reading

English. Same key as /18. {Persia} = code word 8674. [x] = restored. The copy /19 carries this clear text in its
margin, written at the time; the reading below was made from the key alone and was then checked against it.

## Reading

Ninth paragraph of Gen. Malcolm's instructions: You will be furnished with credentials as Envoy or Ambassador from
myself to the Co[u]rt of {Persia} and to the Pashaw of Bagdad, to be presented in the event of your finding it
practicable and expedient to repair [i]n person to those places, and in the event of your finding neither Sir
Harford Jones nor any other person accreditted by His Majesty in {Persia}.
