Pozsony → Ferrara · substitution of graphic signs, with homophones · July–November 1482

Sadoleto at Pozsony, 1482: the Venetian counter-offer

Archivio di Stato di Modena, Cancelleria, Ambasciatori, Ungheria b. 1/9 · four ciphered despatches of Nicolò Sadoleto, Ferrara’s envoy to Matthias Corvinus, catalogued in DECODE as partly decrypted (R1101, R1102, R1103, R1106)

The key was recovered by aligning two sibling letters with the contemporary clear copies filed beside them. Two of the four targets were deciphered at the time. The cipher of 16 July 1482, which has no clear copy, is deciphered here except a few words under stains and in the fold; the faded block of 17 August gives only fragments. Reading audit, 22 October 2026: R1101’s block and R1102 read as sentences; R1103 and the cipher of 27 November (R1106, collated only clause by clause with its clear copy) are decoded, not read.

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · partial (50% of the cipher)

The letters
Despatches of Nicolò Sadoleto, Este envoy to Matthias Corvinus, from Buda and Pozsony in 1482, written when Venice attacked Ferrara; never printed.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Modena, Ambasciatori, Ungheria b. 1/9; DECODE R1101–R1106, four listed as partly decrypted; images also on Vestigia.
The cipher
Graphic signs with word divisions kept and clear words mixed in: about twenty-five signs, with homophones for the vowels and n, r.
How it was read
The alphabet came from aligning two letters (15 July, 31 August) sign by sign with the chancery’s clear copies. It then read the seven cipher lines of 16 July, which have no copy.
What it says
On 16 July Sadoleto learnt in secret that Venice had offered the King Veglia, a fleet command for his natural son and a hundred thousand ducats a year; he urges the League to match that sum.
Still open
The faded block of 17 August gives fragments only; a few words of 16 July lie under stains or the binding fold; the 27 November decipherment sheets are not aligned with the cipher. The twelve 1483 letters were all deciphered at the time.

01 The documents

The file holds forty-seven reports of Sadoleto, June 1482 to November 1483, most in his own hand, with a handful of ducal minutes in reply (Mátyus 2020). Vestigia catalogues all of them with images; DECODE has eighteen of those with cipher. The ones that matter here:

DECODE’s “partially decrypted” on R1101 and R1106 reflects that the decipherments are filed as separate items and were not attached to the records; the chancery deciphered both letters at the time. For R1106 the decipherment slips survive only in photographs whose right halves are faded. On 23 October 2026 slips 26a and 26b were transcribed as far as the photographs allow and a partial reading was built on them, following the cipher where its read spans differ (R1106_reading.md, English R1106_en.md): the two paragraphs of p. 2 that 26a covers (the Queen, the King of Hungary’s moves, the League’s spending, Venice) build sentences, but the p. 3 cipher that 26b covers keeps holes in most sentences, so R1106 stays decoded, not read. The faded right halves of 26b and of p. 3 are the blocker.

02 The key, from two cribs

No Este key for this embassy is known. The archive’s Cifrario series holds a key “per corrispondere con Nicolò Sadoletti a Napoli tra 1479 e 1480”, rebuilt by nineteenth-century archivists (Cremonini 2017, n. 39), but it is not digitised. The key was therefore rebuilt from the letters.

The cipher keeps word divisions, which makes alignment straightforward. The clear copy 7a of 15 July runs “… volesse unirse e pigliare questa impresa, e non lassasse pretermittere questa opportunità laquale mai più fo, né forse sera, e che lo facesse per lo amore …”, and the first lines of the cipher on the original’s second page match it word for word. The one-page letter of 31 August and its clear 13a confirm it: “epsa me dice che è certa che la liga non … perchè Millano et Fiorenza non voriano …”. One habit shows up at once: where 13a has perchè the original writes quid in ordinary letters, which works as a code word.

03 Pozsony, 16 July 1482: what Venice offered the King

The letter’s clear part is routine news of the court. The last seven lines of p. 2 are in cipher, and the cipher runs on into p. 3. From the reading (R1102_reading.md, English R1102_en.md; [U..] an unresolved sign label):

Essendo in Buda per partire per venire a Posonia, sapi per gran secreto da dom Francesco como [Signoria di Venetia] haveva scripto ad Francesco Quirino, che e qua, fosse a [Re di Hungaria], gli facesse offerta como [Signoria di Venetia] voleva havere seco bona amicitia, et voleva dargli Vegla, et voleva lo figliolo (e bastardo) per loro capitaneo de l’armata per mare, et gli voleva dare ogni anno cento milia ducati; ma voleva potere soldare et [U20] cauare gente de questo regno. Et questo l’aveva saputo sua [U03] per subtile via, et non volse per cosa del mondo che io monstrasse saperne cosa alcuna.

“Being in Buda about to leave to come to Pozsony, I learnt in great secret from dom Francesco that [the Signoria of Venice] had written to Francesco Quirino, who is here, to go to [the King of Hungary] and make him the offer that [the Signoria] wanted to have good friendship with him, and wanted to give him Veglia, and wanted his son (the bastard) as captain-general of the fleet at sea, and would give him a hundred thousand ducats every year; but it wanted to be able to hire soldiers and draw men from this kingdom.” The reading of 19 September had [X] and gaps where the text above now stands; the second pass below supplied them.

The words that fix the decipherment could be nothing else: Buda, Posonia, Francesco twice, Quirino, Vegla, capitaneo, per mare, ogni anno, cento milia. The sense fits the moment. Veglia (Krk) had been taken by Venice from Count Giovanni Frankopan in 1480, and the count was at Matthias’s court that summer (13a: “le qui lo Conte Zohane da Vegla”). The “son, the bastard” can only be János Corvin, Matthias’s natural son and intended heir. The sum links this letter to a later one. Six weeks later, in the letter of 31 August, Sadoleto told Ercole that Matthias “per cento milia ducati ogni anno pigliaria la impresa”, that is, would enter the war for the League for a hundred thousand ducats a year. The cipher of 16 July shows where that price came from: it was the figure the other side had already put on the table.

[X] is a five-sign group that occurs twice, after como both times, as the subject of haveva scripto and of voleva havere seco bona amicitia. It does not spell a word in the letter key, so it is a nomenclator entry, and the context makes it the Venetian side, most likely “la Signoria”. The informant’s title before Francesco is another three-sign group (see the second pass below).

Second pass, 20–21 September 2026

A second pass corrected the text above and found more cipher. The informant’s title spells out in the ordinary alphabet as dom, so the name is “dom Francesco”. Group [X] turns up again in the letter of 27 November, where the contemporary decipherment 26b renders it as the Signoria of Venice. A six-sign group K is aligned with the King of Hungary’s title in the 15 July clear copy. The doubtful end of line 3 and start of line 4 give fosse a [K] / gli facesse offerta. The sign for ss is checked against facesse and imparasse in the 15 July letter, and the sign for ff against affinita. Those corrections are in the reading quoted above.

The cipher does not stop there. The sentence continues on p. 3 (quoted above): Venice also wanted the right to recruit soldiers in Hungary, and the informant made Sadoleto promise to show no knowledge of it. Further down p. 3 comes a mixed passage of clear and cipher in which Sadoleto argues his own case to Ercole:

… ⟨per ogni modo disponerse⟩ ad dare a questo [Re di Hungaria] cento milia ducati ogni anno sino a sei o septe anni, che seri[a] questa suma che li domanda; et non seria gran suma alla liga, considerata la [?indubitata] victoria che sua m[aesta] … [?prome…] ⟨est lo modo che mai fo la migliore opportunita⟩ … Non scio percio se sua maesta [?atentasse] … monstra desiderare et haver piu bisogno in questo principio. Ma [U15] alla liga [U16] tractare, et non e da perder tempo; et se [?volen]o [?spen]dere dinari a questo tempo et fare la bona spesa …

“… to make up [the League’s] mind at all costs to give this King a hundred thousand ducats a year for six or seven years, which would be the sum he asks, and it would not be a great sum for the League, considering the certain victory that His Majesty … he shows he wants it and needs it more at this beginning … there is no time to lose, and if they mean to spend money, now is the time to spend it well …” So the letter sets the Venetian counter-offer beside Sadoleto’s advice to match it: a hundred thousand ducats a year from the League. Page 4 carries one more cipher line, “se bene non la mandato a questo [U11]”. Sections A–C of the letter build sentences; the monetary passage keeps unresolved labels in its last lines.

04 Pozsony, 17 August 1482: a faded block

R1103 is the copy, sent to Ercole, of Sadoleto’s letter to King Ferrante of Naples. Four lines of cipher in the middle of p. 2 have faded almost to the colour of the paper. Contrast stretching on the blue channel brings out perhaps half the signs, and they give fragments only: “… se tener che … et … lo credo …”, “… suoi …”, “… vostro … meco … che …”. The clear text around the block concerns the King’s price for joining the League and the hundred thousand ducats, so the block probably says something about that, but no reading is offered: R1103 is decoded, not read. Re-cropped line by line on 23 October 2026, the block gave no word beyond vostro that could be accepted, and DECODE R1103 carries only the page images. It needs better imaging (ultraviolet or multispectral) of the original in Modena.

05 The 1483 letters: all deciphered at the time

DECODE lists twelve more Sadoleto letters of 1483 from Buda and Zagreb (b. 1/10, R1107–R1118), several as partly decrypted. None is open. Four have clear copies filed beside them (nos. 4a/4b, 20a, 21a, 24a on Vestigia); two of the DECODE records are those copies themselves (R1108 = 9a, R1113 = 20a, R1115 = 24a); and the three letters with no filed copy carry the chancery’s decipherment on slips pasted to the leaf: R1109 (4 April, to King Ferrante) over both cipher pages, R1116 (Zagreb, 3 October) and R1118 (30 November) at the foot, keyed to the cipher by a caret. The slips show on DECODE’s photographs; on Vestigia’s later ones they have been lifted, which is why the pages appear to have no decipherment. The 1482 key decrypts the cipher of R1118 in agreement with its slip (“ma come scripsi a V.S. non gli [s]panza questo accordo col turcho”).

06 Method

All images are 4320 × 3240 two-page spreads, fetched from DECODE with a logged-in session and from Vestigia, which needs no login. The cipher lines were cut into strips, glyphs segmented by connected components and numbered on the strip (the segmentation script is the one written for the Milanese letters of 1489, see Buda 1489), and transcribed by eye against the clear copies. The pairs of signs most easily confused were settled on high-magnification crops: the two Δ forms (p and q, which differ only in the loop at the apex), the stemmed and flat-topped sixes (e and n), and the long ſ against Γ.

07 What remains uncertain

  • 16 July, line 1 and lines 5–7 (Buda, Posonia, Veglia, the son, the fleet, the hundred thousand): H.
  • 16 July, lines 2–4 as corrected (dom, fosse a, facesse offerta): C. [X] = the Signoria of Venice, from 26b: C for the referent; the exact wording of the code group is not known. K = the King of Hungary, from 7a: C for the referent.
  • Page 3: subtile via, soldare et cauare gente, victoria, in questo principio, dinari a questo tempo, spesa: C. seri[a], indubitata, atentasse, vol[e]no spen[dere]: M.
  • Still without a reading in the 16 July letter: the word after epsa non scia che; a clear word and a cipher word (piacede?) after ma; a form of promettere(?) with an unassigned K-shaped sign; the last word of the page-4 line (effecto? M); a small cross mark after et. Each lies under a stain, in the binding fold, or turns on a sign with no value yet. Blue-channel enhancement was tried on all of them.
  • 17 August: fragments only. I
  • 27 November: the decipherment sheets 26a and 26b were located but not aligned sign by sign with the cipher.

## Direct control

The original final-page opening corresponds to the upper part of **clear
copy page 3**, not clear-copy page 2. The following readable passage is from
the clear witness, with abbreviation expansion and punctuation editorial:

> ... volesse unirse et pigliare questa impresa, et non lassasse pretermittere
> questa opportunita laquale mai piu fo, ne forse sera, et che lo facesse per
> lo amore et benivolentia, et per la affinita strectissima che l'ha cum tuta
> la liga, et per honore et gloria de sua Maesta, et abassare una volta
> Venetiani per modo che l'imparasse de stare fra li suoi fine.

This is **a transcription of the clear witness**, not a claim that every
word has independently been read from the cipher. The original visibly
retains `unirse` in ordinary writing at its first line, then enciphers the
following appeal. Several direct alphabetic matches support the existing key:
`pigliare questa impresa`, `non`, `ne forse sera`, `amore`, `benivolentia`,
`tuta la liga`, and `de stare fra li suoi fine`. The transition back to clear
writing, `Bene e vero che poi neli parlamenti...`, matches the next sentence
of 7a. These boundaries make the identification independent of a single word.

The earlier notes proposed isolated fragments such as `se tener che`,

could change this result; the cached duplicate spread does not supply such
evidence. Further work on the other Sadoleto letters remains useful, but
repeatedly enhancing this same exposure should not be reported as successive
