Champagne news letters to the duc de Nevers, June 1590

Three news-letters of June 1590 hide names and phrases in a short two-digit alphabet whose values rise with the alphabet; a few letters glossed above the figures and that order recovered it, and ten of the twelve cipher passages are deciphered in whole or in part.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 15 October 2026

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: partial (57% of the cipher)

Credits · Contemporary key or decipherment (key or decipherment used)

Summary. The catalogue item grouped six letters to Louis de Gonzague, duc de Nevers, as one Champagne correspondence. They are in different ciphers, and since 14 October 2026 each cipher has its own page. This page keeps nos. 24, 25 and 60, news-letters of June 1590 in one hand, which use a short two-digit homophonic alphabet whose values rise with the alphabet (a in the 30s, e in the 40s, n in the 60s, u in the 90s). A few letters written faintly above the figures of nos. 25 and 60 anchor it, and the order gives the rest. Ten of the twelve cipher passages are deciphered in whole or in part (“la rupture de l’edict [de] l’union”, “contre la ligue”, “du sieur Ludovic”): 63 of the 104 figures. Tomokiyo’s key no. 46, used from Nevers’s office in 1593, does not fit, nor does Laurière’s key no. 57. No. 78 (1591) has a single group of six signs in another code and stays open. The other two ciphers have their own pages: no. 23, Bienvenut’s letter from Paris of February 1590 with its own name key (Bienvenut to Nevers, 1590), and Laurière’s letters of 1593 in Nevers key no. 57 (Laurière to Nevers, 1593).

01 The documents

The BnF notice and the DECODE records give the folios: no. 23 f. 35 (key slip f. 36r), no. 24 f. 37, no. 25 f. 38, no. 60 f. 88, no. 78 f. 111 in fr. 3623; on Gallica canvas = 2 × folio + 9. The two letters bound between them, no. 26 (f. 39, from Sy, 11 January) and no. 27 (f. 41), are entirely in figures with a contemporary decipherment between the lines, but in another key. No. 23 (f. 35, with its key slip on f. 36r) is on its own page, a different cipher.

02 Nos. 24, 25, 60: an alphabetical two-digit key

The glosses give d u n i, a, n and, in no. 60, L u d o v i c. Set against the context they place a = 34/35, c = 37, d = 38, i = 53/54, l = 56/58, n = 63/64, o = 67, u = 93. The values rise with the alphabet, so every other figure has a predicted slot: e 42–45, f 46, g 47, h 48, r 74–75, s 82–86, t 92. Decryptions were accepted only when each value fell in its slot.

03 What stays open

  • The two passages of no. 24, the first half of one no. 25 passage, the name in no. 60, and the code numbers 12, 16, 17, 19, 39, 89, 99 (and 22, 44, 51, 84 in no. 24).
  • No. 78 (f. 111r, 1591): one group, “traictast de 96 □ 3 9̄ f 3”, too short to decrypt, in another code; Laurière’s key no. 57 gives no sense on it.
  • On 3 October 2026 key no. 57 and its word list were tried on the two-digit runs and give no sense (a different system), and the five letter values still unknown were bracketed by the alphabetical order and searched exhaustively with a French model (240 combinations). The best (52 = i, 62 = n, 92 = u/v, 95 = y) gives “de Launan” and “…audovic”, but no value is confirmed by a second occurrence, so none is counted. The code numbers are words or names that no model can fill.
  • Measured on this page’s letters: 63 of 104 figures of nos. 24, 25 and 60, none of the six signs of no. 78 (63 of 110).
