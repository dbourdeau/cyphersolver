Champagne news letters to the duc de Nevers, June 1590

Three news-letters of June 1590 hide names and phrases in a short two-digit alphabet whose values rise with the alphabet; a few letters glossed above the figures and that order recovered it. Michael Brackx then found the key itself among the Duke of Nevers’s cipher tables, and the letter of 5 June reads in part.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 25 October 2026

Method: read after matching with key from external sources · Extent: partial (92% of the cipher)

Credits · Michael Brackx (solution) · Satoshi Tomokiyo (key or decipherment used) · Contemporary key or decipherment (key or decipherment used)

At a glanceExternal key matched · partial (92% of the cipher)

The letters
Unsigned news-letters of June 1590 to Louis de Gonzague, duc de Nevers, from the royal army before Paris (BnF fr. 3623 nos. 24, 25, 60), in clear French with short cipher runs.
The cipher
A two-digit homophonic alphabet whose values rise with the alphabet, with nulls and barred figures for names.
The key
Nevers key no. 25 of October 1589 (BnF fr. 3995 f. 51r), matched by Michael Brackx from Tomokiyo’s catalogue. Its letters agree with the alphabet rebuilt here from the contemporary glosses.
Read
No. 25 of 5 June, whole recto, with 63 of its 64 cipher groups: 0.925 of words read as sense.
Still open
The clear text of nos. 24 and 60, no. 24’s barred names, doubtful words in no. 25, and no. 78’s single group in another code.

The catalogue item grouped six letters to Nevers as one Champagne correspondence; since 14 October 2026 each cipher has its own page. The other two: no. 23, Bienvenut’s letter from Paris of February 1590 with its own name key (Bienvenut to Nevers, 1590), and Laurière’s letters of 1593 in Nevers key no. 57 (Laurière to Nevers, 1593).

01 The documents

The BnF notice and the DECODE records give the folios: no. 23 f. 35 (key slip f. 36r), no. 24 f. 37, no. 25 f. 38, no. 60 f. 88, no. 78 f. 111 in fr. 3623; on Gallica canvas = 2 × folio + 9. The two letters bound between them, no. 26 (f. 39, from Sy, 11 January) and no. 27 (f. 41), are entirely in figures with a contemporary decipherment between the lines, but in another key. No. 23 (f. 35, with its key slip on f. 36r) is on its own page, a different cipher.

02 Nos. 24, 25, 60: an alphabetical two-digit key

The glosses give d u n i, a, n and, in no. 60, L u d o v i c. Set against the context they place a = 34/35, c = 37, d = 38, i = 53/54, l = 56/58, n = 63/64, o = 67, u = 93. The values rise with the alphabet, so every other figure has a predicted slot: e 42–45, f 46, g 47, h 48, r 74–75, s 82–86, t 92. Decryptions were accepted only when each value fell in its slot.

Chazeron is named in clear in no. 24, which supports the decryption in no. 25. Before the key was found, the figures below 30 and 39, 89, 99 looked like word or name codes.

03 The key found: Nevers key no. 25

Satoshi Tomokiyo’s catalogue of the Duke of Nevers’s cipher tables (BnF fr. 3995) describes key no. 25, endorsed October 1589, as a substitution by two-digit figures with some homophones, nulls, and figures with an overbar for names and words. Michael Brackx compared the Nevers keys on their photographs and found that no. 25 (f. 51r; f. 50r is its wrapper) carries this alphabet: c 37, d 38, g 47, i 53 54, l 56, n 63 64, o 67, u 93, every value the glosses had given here. Keys 28, 30 and 38 do not. The table also settles what our transcription had flattened: a plain 39 is a null, a barred 39 is M. de La Noue, a barred 17 the comte de Soissons, and the plain 12, 19, 89 and 99 are nulls. His transcription of the alphabet, the nulls and six barred entries is in texts/key25.md; the sheet was not re-checked here.

No. 25, 5 June 1590. Brackx transcribed the whole recto and read it. With key no. 25, 63 of its 64 cipher groups decode; one isolated figure 3 stays unread:

  • Nous treuvons bon la rupture de l’edict [de] l’union
  • la venue d’un qui faict les affaires de chaseron broille quelque chose v[ost]re seruice
  • vos troupes disent qu’elles ne combatroient point contre la ligue
  • Joynez a cela qu’on dict: comte de Soissons [?] il se defie du M[onsieu]r de la noue plus que de l’autre. [?Il] a aussi de la defiance du comte de Soissons, lequel n’ose veoir ny caresser personne

The letter around the runs is a newsletter from the royal army before Paris: Paris held by famine and division, the legate and the Spanish ambassador “at the end of their roll”, the Pope waiting for the Holy Spirit, Mayenne gathering weak forces, Villeroy working for peace in vain, Chasteaudun taken by the League. Measured on Brackx’s reading, 0.925 of words read as sense; most holes are doubtful words in the clear text. That is a partial result, under the 95% bar, and no second reader has checked it.

Nos. 24 and 60. Key no. 25 gives du faict de Ludouic in no. 24 once a figure transcribed 30 is seen as 56 on the image, matching the gloss Ludouic in no. 60, and de launan in no. 60’s second run (not identified). No. 24’s second run gives le Roy and an unchecked join. The clear text of these two letters has not been transcribed, so neither letter has a running decipherment yet.

04 What stays open

  • The clear text of nos. 24 and 60, and no. 24’s barred figures 22, 44, 51 and 84 (the rest of key no. 25’s name table would identify them).
  • In no. 25, doubtful clear words (poix and doigt, Lamour, balliages de flandre, the besieged town in Picardy) and the isolated figure 3.
  • No. 78 (f. 111r, 1591): one group, “traictast de 96 □ 3 9̄ f 3”, too short to decrypt, in another code; Laurière’s key no. 57 gives no sense on it.
  • On 3 October 2026 key no. 57 and its word list were tried on the two-digit runs and give no sense (a different system), and the five letter values still unknown were bracketed by the alphabetical order and searched exhaustively with a French model (240 combinations). The best (52 = i, 62 = n, 92 = u/v, 95 = y) gives “de Launan” and “…audovic”, but no value is confirmed by a second occurrence, so none is counted. The code numbers are words or names that no model can fill.
  • Before the key was found (to 23 October 2026): 63 of 104 figures of nos. 24, 25 and 60 had been deciphered from the glosses and the alphabetical order.

# No. 25, BnF fr. 3623 f. 38r, 5 June 1590: English

> Michael Brackx (issue #96, 24 Oct 2026), with Codex; uncertainty marks match the French.

### A

The despatch of 31 May which I sent you by a boy belonging to the s[ieur] de Vaumas will have fully informed you of the state of this army. The King went alone to Senlis and from there again to Compiegne with M[onsieu]r the M[arshal] de Biron. You will have heard what he had done at Crecy and Conflans, and [what] M[onsieu]r the M[arshal] d'Aumont had done at S[ain]t Cloud, and to what condition Paris had been reduced, held by [?weight] and [?finger], where it is hoped that famine and division will serve our purpose.

### B

Of [?Lamour], whatever the legate and the ambassador of Spain may do, who are said to be at the end of their resources. As for the latter, there is no doubt that he has done and will do what he can to sustain the League, [?ruin] a party in France which maintains his master's greatness, even advising him to spare neither [?slanders] nor flatteries, [?making] very great affairs for a poor man full of ardour and desire for great things. Spain will never find rest with what he [?] [?] treats openly.

### C

As for the other, we consider him at a loss, since we know that the Pope no longer wishes to hear of the League, still less of the Spaniard, and that when he is pressed on the matter he answers that he awaits what the H[oly] Spirit will inspire in him. [?He] has had a bull published to urge the peoples to pray to God that He may impart the graces of His H[oly] Spirit to him in these affairs.

### D

You will also have been informed of the gathering the Duke of Mayenne is making of his forces, which are weak, and of how we are assembling [ours] from every quarter. [?As] for the advice I have given you about this, if I have added words of liberty that were too explicit, please blame only the affection I bear to [your] greatness. Whatever you decide on this, hold it certain that we shall no longer hazard anything on the [?uncertain] outcome of a battle, following your judicious advice. We have exerted ourselves enough to have established ourselves and acquired among foreigners the reputation of a great and magnanimous cap[tain], and among our enemies that of a bold and valiant man.

### E

You have heard of the revocation of the passport granted to the Primate of Lyon, with [?force] and [?clamours] of deputies of the Paris law courts and of [?burghers] of the city. This refusal was borne so impatiently by the [said] primate of Lyon that he almost lost control of himself. He had asked M[onsieu]r de la Guiche to procure him a passport to retire to his house, which he would have had granted, such is his influence, if the [said] primate of Lyon had been willing to promise to abandon the League, which he would not do. [our] army from Germany is preparing for the other descent.

### F

We approve the breaking of the edict [of] union. I [?learnt] [something] clearly. What misfortune! You would do well to [?consider] breaking this move. I fear that the arrival of one who conducts the affairs of Chaseron may disturb [your] service somewhat, with what he [?demonstrated] [?little] [?well] that at the battle of Arques your troops say they would not fight against the League.

### G

As for peace, M[onsieu]r de Villeroy is working at it, but in vain. [?He] has drawn them in too far to withdraw them from it. M[onsieu]r de Montmorancy, otherwise M[onsieu]r the Constable, will not come and, as far as I can judge, will not leave. Add to this what is said: the Count of Soissons [?] distrusts M[onsieu]r de La Noue more than the other. [?He] also distrusts the Count of Soissons, who dares not see or show favour to anyone, whether he creates these suspicions for himself or there is some truth in the rumour.

### H

The son of M[onsieu]r de Montmorancy is expected with M[onsieu]r de Chastillon. Meanwhile he, the s[ieur] de la Valette and the [?two] [?brothers] are gathering at S[ain]t Esprit on the [?province] of Piedmont. It is said that l'Ecluse, an important place belonging to the Duke of Savoy, has been taken by surprise by those of Geneva, and that the Swiss have taken several [?bailiwicks] [?of] [?Flanders] from him. I report this, and it is not to help him.

### I

I am assured that the Leaguers say that the Prince of Parma is ill with the army. The [?siege] of [?Breda] at [?Mars] to [?serve]. [?Maure], an important town on the Rhine which would restore freedom of navigation on the river to the Low Countries, is besieged by Count Maurice with 22 cannon.

On the 3rd of this month a letter from the Duke of Mayenne to his sister was seen, informing her of the efforts he is making to relieve Paris, but saying that he cannot be ready before the end of this month. Moreover, the provost of the merchants has written to that person and begs that person to make a final effort; otherwise he cannot tell that person how soon they will be compelled to submit to the mercy of the most cruel and barbarous enemy they have.
