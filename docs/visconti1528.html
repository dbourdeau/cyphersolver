Campo a Landriano → the grand maître · invented-sign substitution with numbered codes · 30 August 1528

Galeazzo Visconti to Montmorency from the camp at Landriano, 30 August 1528

BnF Français 3034 f. 154r–v, no. 68 (DECODE R4223, catalogue item 177, “unknown sender”). Thirty-nine lines of cipher in Italian. George Lasry published a key for Visconti’s cipher in 2023, with no decipherment of this letter; it was applied here, extended on the letter and checked on two signed sibling letters. 93.3% of the cipher signs give sense; the numbered name codes stay open.

01 The letter and the key

Français 3034 is a volume of original letters to Montmorency. No. 68, f. 154, opens with three words in clear, Poco scritti le lre, then runs in cipher for thirty lines on the recto and nine on the verso. The close and date are in clear: pregando n[ost]ro S[ign]or Dio dona a V. S. bona e longa vitta. Da al Campo a Landriano ali XXX d’aust. Landriano, south of Milan, was the camp of the League army in the late summer of 1528, after the collapse of Lautrec’s expedition to Naples.

The cipher replaces each letter with an invented sign. The signs include a tau for A, a round d for D, a bold k for R, a psi for I, a sign like ne for S and one like me for L, and a sign written like the word far for CHE. E has three forms and A two. Lasry’s table gave most of the letters. The letter added the values for ne, me, the small y (N, with a bent-arm form for B), θ (G), plain p (M), far and fu, and fixed q as H, or F in Francesco. It also showed that the long thin diagonal strokes under the lines are later word dividers, added by someone who worked on the letter. They are not parts of signs.

In English

Visconti writes that the council held the day before was meant to bring the captains present round to the view that the army should not go to Milan. That morning the Duke of Urbino came to the lodging of [48] to decide between Milan and Pavia, with all the captains, many of them, and the officers of the Signoria of Venice. It was arranged that each should give his opinion in turn. This, he says, was a device of Francesco’s, whispered to the Venetian officers, so that the vote would go against Milan, and the Duke of Urbino, the proveditor and most of the captains came down against Milan. Visconti was usually called first, “to open the way for the others”. This time Francesco was called first, then a few others, then Visconti. [4θ] said the army should go to Pavia. When Visconti began to argue for Milan, as he had written, Francesco was the first to interrupt, then others he is ashamed to name, and in the end he was let speak only with difficulty. “If it goes on this way we shall have a stew of bad digestion.” He sees much and can do little to remedy it. He asks Montmorency to see to matters for all the captains, in the service of [peu], perhaps the King. The Duke of Urbino and the proveditor have said that the army will go to cut the road between Milan and Pavia, and he will write the rest later.

“Francesco” may be Francesco Maria Sforza, the League’s duke of Milan, who wanted Pavia. The letter does not say so.

03 Method

The two pages are Gallica views 243 and 244 of btv1b90600370. They were deskewed and cut into line strips at full resolution. The line centres drift by up to 90 pixels across the page, so each strip was placed by measurement. A first rough transcription of 1344 tokens showed at once that Lasry’s table gives Italian. Each line was then transcribed again sign by sign against the table, and every value the table lacked was fixed from words already deciphered. A second pass redid the verso and retried every open spot on the recto.

Two other Visconti letters in the same cipher were then opened: Visconti to Angelo Bolano, Alessandria, 15 October 1528 (fr. 3045 f. 28), and Visconti to Francis I, in campo in la pieve de Loccate, 15 June 1529 (fr. 3096 f. 91). The key decrypts both (“mal tratamento”, “che disperat-”). In both, the crossed sign appears in numeral pairs, 42 and 48, so it is the figure 4. The name groups of this letter are therefore numbered codes, and 48 appears in the letter to the King as well. The groups rr and qq also recur there as units. None of these codes has a value anywhere.

04 What remains

  • Re-measured 5 October 2026. 1,248 of 1,337 cipher glyphs read as sense (93.3%). Every open stretch was re-cut from the full-resolution Gallica image and re-read sign by sign; one word was gained (che havemo, w = O). Lasry’s own table was tested on the open runs (L as a third A, the m-shaped sign as L): no word results. Codes 48 (Saint-Pol?) and peu (the King?) fit every context but none forces the value, so they are not counted. 95% needs 24 more glyphs; 16 of the 89 unread are codes with no key, the rest one-offs and blotted spots.
  • Numbered codes, no key material: 48 (twice; the host of the council), 4θ (the captain who spoke for Pavia), 4r (before sapendolo dissimulare, and again in v03), rr (r21), qq (r09, before uolse), peu (twice; perhaps the King). Only a surviving nomenclator could fix them.
  • The final sign pair of so··, lo··, do·· (r10–12): the same pair each time. The sense suggests soi, loi, doi, one I value for the pair. That is a proposal only (I). It does not occur in the siblings.
  • One-off signs: [Y r] in r12, a small sign and a blot in r16, [v b] in r30, Π in v08 and v09. The lines make sense without them, so they may be nulls. That is not proved (M).
  • Illegible: only the blotted sign and the flourished ſt ligature in v01. A third pass (22 September 2026) enhanced the other spots on the Gallica native (autocontrast, unsharp mask, threshold, 2×): the middle of v03 is legible, and its ǂ-r pair is the numbered code 4r of r25, so that gap is a code; the r21 rr carries an overbar, so it is the code rr; fuo is Lombard fu, as in the letter to the King. The count is now 1248 of 1337 signs (93.3%).
  • Values decrypted, sense open (M): la mia (r03), asciilati (r07), ascicsai pui (r09), acuvante (r21), lasoreno and se non (r26–27, C), and in v03 and v07 the runs marked (?).

1. **Word dividers.** The long thin diagonal strokes are dividers, not glyph parts. This removes "p with hook = N" (contagio is spelled "comtagio"; mane is `a 7 p 7 y d`), the "crossed q" in line 1, and the "crossed y" in opinione and "in secreto".
2. **Two different bold Y signs.**

   - Ym, a short thick stubby y, is **M**. Evidence: r06 "o a Milano" `7 Ym Y M 7 y n`.
3. **+ = O.** Evidence: "consiglo" r01, "o a Pavia" r06, "erano" r06 end (`d k 7 y t`), "Urbino" r09 end. The Milano sign in r03 is a tau with only a top bar (⊤) = A, not +.
4. **Crossed psi X = O.** It is written fused with a following y (N) in "opinione" (r02 `X y` = on) and in "o nostri" (r07 `X y n S a k Y`). The first sign of the X8 code group is a different, 4-like sign.
5. **Y' = N.** This is a psi-like sign with a left tick on the arm. Evidence: "uno" (r10 `s Y' n`) and "Urbino" (r05 `k yb Y Y' n`, r09 `k yb Y Y' t`). The same sign closes the unresolved words `S n y Y'`, `M n y Y'` and `D n y Y'` (r10-12), where N gives no sense. So either there is a second value, or it is a look-alike not yet separated.
6. **yb = B.** This is a y with a bent left arm. Evidence: "deliberare" (r06), "deliberaseno" (r12), "Urbino" (r05, r09). The plain y stays N (sono, erano, signoria, opinione, ognuno).
7. **LA = A.** This is an L whose foot turns up like a "2". Evidence: "a casa" (r05 `7 l LA S 7`). This is George Lasry's third A sign. The tall L-with-foot stays C (causato, capitanei, Francesco).
8. **lY = "ci".** This is a small l joined to psi. Evidence: "aciò che se vincese" (r14 `7 lY n F S d`) and "a ciò dito" (r11).
9. **q.**
   - Plain q is H. Evidence: "heri" (r01), "chiarito" (r04), "ha" (r05, r13).
   - q is F in "Francesco" (r13 `qF k 7 y L d S L n`).
   - The recurring group `q s n`, "fuo", is read as the verb "fu": "il consiglio de heri fu per tirare" (r01) and "questa fu[o] inventione" (r12). Tentative: the group is overlined in r09, which may mark a code group.
10. **Bold k = R.** Evidence: "erano" (r02). Some other dark blobs are later ink retouches over the underlying letter: A in "duca" (r05), T in "et" (r10), and "quest[a]" (r12). Read them from context. They are not new signs.
11. **X8 (the ǂ8 group).** It occurs in r05 "a casa de [X8]" and r09 "[X8] che il duca d'Urbino tirase tuti li so[i] ...". The context fits a person: the host of the council, whose word decides how the Duke of Urbino deploys his men. Most likely that is the French commander at Landriano, the comte de Saint-Pol. This is not proven from the text.
12. **ʃu (bold)** in r07 "che [ʃu] sono": "vi" fits, not proven.
