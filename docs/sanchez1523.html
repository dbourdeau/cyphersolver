Alonso Sánchez at Venice, 1523 — already calendared, key unchanged

Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid, Colección Salazar y Castro 9/28 (A-28), ff. 18–574; DECODE R9670, R9671, R9672, R9674, R9677, R9689, R9690, R9767, R9769, R9770. The catalogue listed these as ten unread ciphertexts to an unknown recipient. They are the imperial ambassador’s despatches to Charles V from the months when the Venetian league was negotiated.

Summary. The records sit in the Salazar volume that follows the 1522 despatches (Sánchez 1522), and they use the same cipher in the same hand. DECODE flags every one of them “Inline Cleartext: Yes”, but that means passages written in the clear inside the letters; no decipherment was imaged with them. The decipherings made at court do exist, filed elsewhere in the volume. G. A. Bergenroth used them in 1866 for his Calendar of State Papers, Spain, vol. 2. Seven of the ten records are the despatches he calendared as nos. 549 (8 May), 552 (20 May), 554 (5 June) and 558 (13 June), or duplicates of them. The folio numbers match. The other three are dated 16 August 1523, the day after the league was published. They are not in the Calendar, and they are mostly in the clear. The 1522 key (Tomokiyo’s, with this project’s additions) reads the code groups of every page examined without change. Read here in part: Sánchez’s own letter of 16 August (R9767). Its clear text is transcribed in full, and in its cipher passages every code group but two resolves. The spelled letter runs inside them are only partly read. The short cipher head of R9770 stays open.

01 The ten records

Every letter is headed Sacra cesarea y catholica magestad, so the “unknown recipient” is the Emperor. The dates come from the date lines and from internal references. The matches are by folio and content against Bergenroth’s abstracts, which carry the Salazar A-28 folio numbers.

02 The key

The cipher is the one Sánchez used in 1522. Satoshi Tomokiyo reconstructed it in 2025 from a letter with its decipherment attached, and the 1522 write-up extended it. It has an alphabet of cursive signs whose shapes are not their values, three nulls opening each paragraph, and a nomenclator of consonant–vowel–consonant groups assigned in alphabetical order of the plaintext. On R9670, R9671, R9672, R9677 and R9767 the groups read coherently with that key and nothing contradicts it: xig de, vog en, ho que, nuf no, dom pud vuestra magestad, vum … lal esta [re]publica, gub rac señor infante, tum frances. The ambassador did not change his key between the two volumes.

03 Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 16 August 1523 (R9767)

Notation: […] marks a code group read from the key and ‹…› a spelled run read tentatively; … is not read.

Pues por la que en comun escrivimos el prothonotario Caraciolo e yo vera vuestra magestad todo lo que occorre, no dire en esta salvo que recebi la de vuestra magestad de xv del passado, y quanto … aunque a dezir a vuestra magestad verdad [en el … se con … que no es … frances]. Pero espero en dios que poco podra danyar, aunque es bien toda via [en …].

Vuestra magestad vera por la capitulacion [que no se ha] ‹expressa›[do] ninguna … [por que da lo de esta] ‹re›[publica el di]‹nero› a [vuestra magestad] … [no se … haui … de da]‹r› [mas] … [lo ver]‹e› [vuestra magestad] … [de vuestra magestad].

Pareciendo nos lo mismo que vuestra magestad escrive, no quisimos obligar al señor Infante a la liga defensiva, pues estos no querian obligarse contra el turco. Lo de la navegacion para los subditos del archiducado de Austria no se ha podido acabar con estos; estarse han como hasta aqui. … De Venecia a xvj de agosto de mil D xxiij.

The gist is graded M for moderate. Sánchez refers the Emperor to the joint letter and acknowledges the imperial letter of 15 July. He says something in Venice leans French but “can do little harm”. Then he turns to the financial side of the league, the Venetian payment the ambassadors had failed to raise (CSP 566, 577): the capitulation expresses nothing of it. The particulars are in the spelled runs, which are not read. The clear postscript on the defensive league and the Turk, and on shipping for the Archduke’s subjects, is new: neither point is in the Calendar abstracts.

04 What remains open

  • R9767: the spelled runs inside both cipher paragraphs of f. 563r, and the code groups pem and yol, which are not in the key. By the alphabetical order, yol falls between causa and cierto.
  • R9770: the four lines of cipher at the head of the last written page, not read.
  • R9670–R9690: not edited. Their content is in Bergenroth’s English abstracts, and a full Spanish edition would be transcription against the contemporary decipherings, not decipherment.
