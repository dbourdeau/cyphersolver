Venice → Charles V · homophonic alphabet with a three-letter nomenclator · May–August 1523

Alonso Sánchez at Venice to Charles V, May–August 1523

Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid, Colección Salazar y Castro 9/28 (A-28), ff. 18–574; DECODE R9670, R9671, R9672, R9674, R9677, R9689, R9690, R9767, R9769, R9770. The catalogue listed these as ten ciphertexts with no decipherment, to an unknown recipient. They are the imperial ambassador’s despatches to Charles V from the months when the Venetian league was negotiated. Their key is Tomokiyo’s reconstruction of Sánchez’s 1522 cipher, applied unchanged. Bergenroth calendared seven of them in 1866 from the contemporary decipherings; the two August letters not in the Calendar were transcribed here in full from contemporary decipherments found on neighbouring leaves (23 October 2026).

The letters
Ten records of Sánchez’s despatches to Charles V from Venice, May–August 1523, in the Salazar volume following the 1522 ones. DECODE flags them “Inline Cleartext: Yes”, meaning clear passages, with no decipherment imaged.
Where
Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid, Salazar 9/28 (A-28); DECODE R9670–R9690 and R9767–R9770.
The cipher
The same cipher as Sánchez 1522: cursive signs, three nulls opening each paragraph, and a nomenclator of consonant–vowel–consonant groups.
How it was read
The 1522 key (Tomokiyo’s, with this project’s additions) decodes the code groups of every page examined, unchanged. Seven records match despatches calendared by Bergenroth (1866).
What it says
The letter of 16 August (R9767) tells the Emperor the Venetian capitulation expresses nothing of the money, and says the Infante was not bound to the defensive league against the Turk.
Still open
In R9767 the spelled letter runs and two code groups (pem, yol) are undeciphered; the cipher head of R9770 is not deciphered; R9670–R9690 are not edited.

02 The key

The cipher is the one Sánchez used in 1522. Satoshi Tomokiyo reconstructed it in 2025 from a letter with its decipherment attached, and the 1522 write-up extended it. It has an alphabet of cursive signs whose shapes are not their values, three nulls opening each paragraph, and a nomenclator of consonant–vowel–consonant groups assigned in alphabetical order of the plaintext. On R9670, R9671, R9672, R9677 and R9767 the groups decode coherently with that key and nothing contradicts it: xig de, vog en, ho que, nuf no, dom pud vuestra magestad, vum … lal esta [re]publica, gub rac señor infante, tum frances. The ambassador did not change his key between the two volumes.

03 Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 16 August 1523 (R9767)

From the reading of 23 October 2026 (R9767_reading.md, English R9767_en.md): the clear text, and the two cipher passages read from the court decipherment on f. 567r (DECODE R9768), checked group by group against the figures of f. 563r (R9767_cipher_align.md):

Pues por la que en comun escrivimos el prothonotario Caraciolo e yo vera vuestra magestad todo lo que occorre, no dire en esta salvo que recebi la de vuestra magestad de xv del passado, y quanto al entretener a este nuevo Duce hare lo que pudiere, ahunque a dezir a vuestra magestad verdad en el se conoce que no es mal frances; pero espero en dios que poco podra danyar, ahunque es bien toda via entretenerle.

Vuestra magestad vera por la capitulacion que no se ha expremido ninguna causa porque dan los desta republica el dinero a vuestra magestad; antes [?diversas] vezes en el tractar con ellos les diximos que vuestra magestad le pedia porque les dava una paz perpetua y no por otra causa, mostrando siempre que ni por el Imperio ni por la casa de Austria no se les havia de dar mas drecho del que tenian, y assi lo vera vuestra magestad en todas nuestras escrituras, pareciendonos que assi sera mas servicio de vuestra magestad.

Pareciendo nos lo mismo que vuestra magestad escrive, no quisimos obligar al señor Infante a la liga defensiva, pues estos no querian obligarse contra el turquo. Lo de la navegacion para los subditos del archiducado de Austria no se ha podido acabar con estos; estarse han como hasta aqui. Nuestro señor la imperial vida y estados de vuestra magestad prosperamente guarde con cumplimiento de la monarchia. De Venecia a xvj de agosto de mil D xxiij.

Sánchez refers the Emperor to the joint letter and acknowledges the imperial letter of 15 July. The new Doge “no es mal frances”, but can do little harm and should still be humoured. The capitulation expresses no reason why the Signoria pays the Emperor money: the ambassadors always said it was for a perpetual peace, not for any right of the Empire or the house of Austria. The earlier partial reading had the gist right but not this argument. The clear postscript on the defensive league and the Turk, and on shipping for the Archduke’s subjects, is not in the Calendar abstracts. The decipherment also corrects two code readings: the group read as yol is yoh causa, and mal is spelled out, not a code.

The joint letter, R9769/R9770. Caracciolo and Sánchez’s letter of the same day is transcribed in full, with its cipher paragraph read from the decipherment on f. 574r (R9769_reading.md, English R9769_en.md): the news of the peace and league concluded with Venice, the departure of the English ambassador and of the Infante’s ambassador, and prisoners released by the Signoria.

04 What remains open

  • R9767 and R9769/R9770: closed on 23 October 2026 from the court decipherments on ff. 567r and 574r; a few uncertain words in the joint letter ([?xxix], [?possessiones]).
  • R9670–R9690: not edited. Their content is in Bergenroth’s English abstracts, and a full Spanish edition would be transcription against the contemporary decipherings, not decipherment.

CSP Spain vol. 2 no. 549
8 May. M. Re. Ac. d. Hist. Salazar. A. 28. ff. 40-44. 549. Alonso Sanchez, Imperial Ambassador in Venice, to the Emperor. His negotiations with the Republic have been somewhat interrupted by the illness of Georgio Cornaro, who is one of the commissioners and a good Imperialist. The Doge, who was ninety years of age, died on the 7th, and it was necessary to wait for the election of another Doge. That was another cause of delay. On the 8th he and the ambassador of the King of England went to the Signory, and said they knew that the Doge had died, but as in Venice affairs of state are not interrupted by such an event, they begged them to give their definite answer about the alliance (between Venice, the Emperor, and the King of England). They answered that they would send them their commissioners. Fears that ... (fn. 1) Knows very well that until a new Doge is elected nothing will be done. Fears that the Venetians, under the pretext that they must first elect a new Doge, will delay their answer until they know whether the French will invade Italy, and until the Infante has sent his answer. The Pope has exhorted the Republic to conclude peace with him, and the English ambassador has done all he can to bring the affair to a speedy conclusion. Has had letters from the Infante. Has replied to them. Does not know what decision the Infante will make. Does not think that the exiles will pay the 18,000 ducats in one instalment, as they have promised. The Venetians answered the French in general words about their aid in case a French army should invade Italy. The French were not satisfied with the answer. Cardinal of Auch, &c.—Venice, the 8th of May 1523. Addressed : "To his Sacred, Imperial, and Catholic Majesty the Emperor and King, our Lord." Indorsed : "To the King. 1523. Venice. Alonso Sanchez. The 8th of May. Answered." Spanish. Autograph in cipher. Contemporary deciphering. pp . 6.

podido acabar con estos; estarse han como hasta aqui. Nuestro señor la imperial vida y estados de vuestra magestad

together, I will say nothing in this one except that I received Your Majesty's letter of the 15th of last month; and as

By our letters of the [?29th] and 30th of last month, which we sent to Your Majesty by our own messenger, Your Majesty
will have seen the conclusion of peace and league that we reached here with the lords of this Republic. And although by
letters of the 4th of this month from Genoa we are informed that on that day our messenger had left there with good
weather, and although we had concluded the peace and league here before the Rome one was concluded, our messenger
nevertheless made poor speed, for we are informed that those who carried the news of the Rome league crossed in the
same brigantine.

After what was written to Your Majesty, the English ambassador left about fifteen days ago; he stayed more than eight
days in Padua; he has now left there on the way to Ferrara, to go to Milan and then to the Swiss. Also, five days ago
the lord Infante's ambassador left here; he is the brother of the bishop of Trent, and he has truly conducted himself so
well in Your Majesty's service that more could not be wished of him.

The lords of this Republic have freed from prison two men who were sentenced there for life: one is called Geronimo de
Zotti of Treviso, who had been there two years for some plot or other he was carrying on at Treviso; the other, called
Vital Muto, had been in prison ten years. Zotti lost all he had, for it was confiscated; Your Majesty will see whether
it please him to order him put among the others who are to have their share of the [?…] gold ducats. The lords of this
Republic have told us that they have given orders throughout their lands that the exiles be freed so that they may
return to their homes.

The lords of this Republic have wished to wait for news from Rome before publishing the peace and league; they have
already told us that on Our Lady's day the publication will be made with a solemn procession, with all solemnity and
good display. They asked us to write to the lord Infante that His Highness should order a display on the same day at
Innsbruck too, and the duke of Milan likewise; and so we shall write and beg His Highness. And I the Protonotary would
like to set off on my way, and to be present at this solemnity I shall stay until the 17th of this month, when I shall
leave for Ferrara and from there for Rome.

In Friuli the restitution of the [?possessions] has begun, according to the capitulation of Worms, and it will go on
until it is completed. The money, that is the 38,000 ducats that the lords of this Republic are to pay once the said
restitution is made: the promise has been made to us as Your Majesty's ambassadors, because Filinguer's quittances are
old and he did not have sufficient authority. We, or I Alonso Sánchez who stay here, shall take the money and give it
to Filinguer, as is Your Majesty's will and command; but I shall keep back from the said money a thousand and some
ducats that I have spent on couriers, as Your Majesty can have seen from the account I send, and I shall also take what
is owed me of my salary; all the rest will be given to the said Filinguer.

We do not send Your Majesty with this an authenticated copy of the capitulation, because it could not be made in time;
and so we beg Your Majesty to be pleased to have the ratification sent to us as soon as possible.

We send Your Majesty with this the bonds of the duke of Milan, both for the troops that Your Majesty is to keep for the
defence of the state of Milan and for the 18,000 ducats of the exiles; may Your Majesty be pleased to order how the said
18,000 ducats are to be distributed among the exiles. And let Your Majesty not be surprised that we promised there would
be five hundred men-at-arms permanently in the state of Milan, for these lords showed they wanted it very much and
would not do otherwise; and it is certain that the duke of Milan will keep them, either Your Majesty's or his own, in
time of peace. Nor let Your Majesty be surprised that the lords of this Republic are bound to keep no more than five
hundred men-at-arms in time of peace: they wished to set it so in order not to show a greater obligation than ours to
them; but they have sworn to us that they have more than nine hundred and will never have fewer, nor can they, by their
ordinances.

To a man whom the marquis of Mantua keeps here we have said that we will do everything possible, and he presses us on
behalf of the said marquis; but with the Rome league we believe the Pope will keep the said marquis, and there will be
no room for this here. We understand that the duke of Ferrara and the duke of Urbino are working as hard as they can to
obtain the captaincy of these lords; we believe they will remove Teodoro Trivulzio, who is expected here soon.

It has been confirmed to us that the lords of this Republic have written to their ambassador in France to come home.
Renzo da Ceri is still here, and the French ambassador.

Yesterday we received a letter from Your Majesty of the 15th of last month; and as for the difficulty about making the
peace perpetual, Your Majesty will have seen that we did not stop at it, knowing from good lawyers that it did not
matter.

May Our Lord prosper and guard the imperial life and estates of Your Majesty with the fulfilment of the monarchy. From
Venice, 16 August 1523. Of Your Caesarean Majesty.

These Venetian lords have made the duke of Sessa a gentleman of Venice, as the Great Captain was.

We have already told Your Majesty that the lords of this Republic wanted us to promise them specifically to work with
Your Majesty for the mutual restitution; on this they wanted an instrument to be made, of which we send Your Majesty a
copy with this, by which you will see that there is no obligation beyond our working for it with Your Majesty, who may
order the answer to be as you please; but since it is not promised them, it seems to us it will be no bad thing to give
them good words.

By letters from Milan from the duke and Prospero [Colonna] we are informed how the French and Swiss boast of coming now
to make the enterprise of Italy; but after they have learned of the conclusion of this peace and league we believe they
will think again; but for all that, provision will not fail to be made everywhere as is fitting, as Your Majesty will be
informed more fully by letters from Milan.

Your most humble servants and vassals, who kiss your imperial feet and hands. Protonotary Caracciolo. Alonso Sánchez.

# R9769 (and copy R9770): Caracciolo and Alonso Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 16 August 1523 — English

Translation of `R9769_reading.md` (clear text of the original; the cipher paragraph from the contemporary decipherment on
f. 574r, checked against the figures). Same holes.

By our letters of the [?29th] and 30th of last month, which we sent to Your Majesty by our own messenger, Your Majesty
will have seen the conclusion of peace and league that we reached here with the lords of this Republic. And although by
letters of the 4th of this month from Genoa we are informed that on that day our messenger had left there with good
weather, and although we had concluded the peace and league here before the Rome one was concluded, our messenger
nevertheless made poor speed, for we are informed that those who carried the news of the Rome league crossed in the
same brigantine.

After what was written to Your Majesty, the English ambassador left about fifteen days ago; he stayed more than eight
days in Padua; he has now left there on the way to Ferrara, to go to Milan and then to the Swiss. Also, five days ago
the lord Infante's ambassador left here; he is the brother of the bishop of Trent, and he has truly conducted himself so
well in Your Majesty's service that more could not be wished of him.

The lords of this Republic have freed from prison two men who were sentenced there for life: one is called Geronimo de
Zotti of Treviso, who had been there two years for some plot or other he was carrying on at Treviso; the other, called
Vital Muto, had been in prison ten years. Zotti lost all he had, for it was confiscated; Your Majesty will see whether
it please him to order him put among the others who are to have their share of the [?…] gold ducats. The lords of this
Republic have told us that they have given orders throughout their lands that the exiles be freed so that they may
return to their homes.

The lords of this Republic have wished to wait for news from Rome before publishing the peace and league; they have
already told us that on Our Lady's day the publication will be made with a solemn procession, with all solemnity and
good display. They asked us to write to the lord Infante that His Highness should order a display on the same day at
Innsbruck too, and the duke of Milan likewise; and so we shall write and beg His Highness. And I the Protonotary would
like to set off on my way, and to be present at this solemnity I shall stay until the 17th of this month, when I shall
leave for Ferrara and from there for Rome.

In Friuli the restitution of the [?possessions] has begun, according to the capitulation of Worms, and it will go on
until it is completed. The money, that is the 38,000 ducats that the lords of this Republic are to pay once the said
restitution is made: the promise has been made to us as Your Majesty's ambassadors, because Filinguer's quittances are
old and he did not have sufficient authority. We, or I Alonso Sánchez who stay here, shall take the money and give it
to Filinguer, as is Your Majesty's will and command; but I shall keep back from the said money a thousand and some
ducats that I have spent on couriers, as Your Majesty can have seen from the account I send, and I shall also take what
is owed me of my salary; all the rest will be given to the said Filinguer.

We do not send Your Majesty with this an authenticated copy of the capitulation, because it could not be made in time;
and so we beg Your Majesty to be pleased to have the ratification sent to us as soon as possible.

We send Your Majesty with this the bonds of the duke of Milan, both for the troops that Your Majesty is to keep for the
defence of the state of Milan and for the 18,000 ducats of the exiles; may Your Majesty be pleased to order how the said
18,000 ducats are to be distributed among the exiles. And let Your Majesty not be surprised that we promised there would
be five hundred men-at-arms permanently in the state of Milan, for these lords showed they wanted it very much and
would not do otherwise; and it is certain that the duke of Milan will keep them, either Your Majesty's or his own, in
time of peace. Nor let Your Majesty be surprised that the lords of this Republic are bound to keep no more than five
hundred men-at-arms in time of peace: they wished to set it so in order not to show a greater obligation than ours to
them; but they have sworn to us that they have more than nine hundred and will never have fewer, nor can they, by their
ordinances.

To a man whom the marquis of Mantua keeps here we have said that we will do everything possible, and he presses us on
behalf of the said marquis; but with the Rome league we believe the Pope will keep the said marquis, and there will be
no room for this here. We understand that the duke of Ferrara and the duke of Urbino are working as hard as they can to
obtain the captaincy of these lords; we believe they will remove Teodoro Trivulzio, who is expected here soon.

It has been confirmed to us that the lords of this Republic have written to their ambassador in France to come home.
Renzo da Ceri is still here, and the French ambassador.

Yesterday we received a letter from Your Majesty of the 15th of last month; and as for the difficulty about making the
peace perpetual, Your Majesty will have seen that we did not stop at it, knowing from good lawyers that it did not
matter.

May Our Lord prosper and guard the imperial life and estates of Your Majesty with the fulfilment of the monarchy. From
Venice, 16 August 1523. Of Your Caesarean Majesty.

These Venetian lords have made the duke of Sessa a gentleman of Venice, as the Great Captain was.

We have already told Your Majesty that the lords of this Republic wanted us to promise them specifically to work with
Your Majesty for the mutual restitution; on this they wanted an instrument to be made, of which we send Your Majesty a
copy with this, by which you will see that there is no obligation beyond our working for it with Your Majesty, who may
order the answer to be as you please; but since it is not promised them, it seems to us it will be no bad thing to give
them good words.

By letters from Milan from the duke and Prospero [Colonna] we are informed how the French and Swiss boast of coming now
to make the enterprise of Italy; but after they have learned of the conclusion of this peace and league we believe they
will think again; but for all that, provision will not fail to be made everywhere as is fitting, as Your Majesty will be
informed more fully by letters from Milan.

Your most humble servants and vassals, who kiss your imperial feet and hands. Protonotary Caracciolo. Alonso Sánchez.
