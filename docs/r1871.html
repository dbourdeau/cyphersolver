Paris → Doge of Venice · three-digit numerical nomenclator, Italian · 11 September 1680

Sebastiano Foscarini to the Doge, Paris 1680

A Venetian ambassador’s despatch written wholly in a three-digit code; it was transcribed and attacked here without result. Paolo Bonavoglia’s decipherment, offline at the time, was retrieved in October 2026.

At a glanceExisting decipherment · complete

The letter
Sebastiano Foscarini, Venetian ambassador in France, to the Doge, Paris, 11 September 1680: despatch no. 117, “P.S. Sola”, one page of cipher.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Venezia, IT ASVe 0045 010 Busta 30 f. 4; DECODE R1871. Catalogued as “England?”, 1555, under catalogue 264 together with R1873, a Rome despatch of 1558 in another cipher.
The cipher
Continuous digits that fall into three-digit groups: 612 groups, 211 different, from a table of about 300 codes. Not an alphabetical table.
How it was tried
Transcribed from the images, segmented, then attacked with several annealing solvers and an Italian language model. No Italian came out.
Why it resisted
One page is too short for a blind solution of a table this size, and the key is not on DECODE. Brice Dudout found the table is a syllabary in blocks of five consecutive codes with the blocks out of order, which an ordered-syllabary solver cannot find.
What it says
From Paolo Bonavoglia’s decipherment: rumoured Spanish reinforcements in the Milanese, and Louis XIV’s reported plan to raise the Pinerolo garrison to 6,000 men.
Credit
The decipherment is Paolo Bonavoglia’s. Brice Dudout reported his page back online; Satoshi Tomokiyo’s note on DECODE R1872 first pointed to him.

Until 14 October 2026 this despatch shared an entry with R1873, Alvise Mocenigo’s Rome despatch of 1558: a different cipher, with its own page.

01 The record

The two images of R1871 show one page of continuous digits under “Ser.mo Principe”, marked “#117 P.S. Sola” and signed “Sebastian Foscarini Amb.r”, dated at Paris in 1680. The dorse is addressed to the Doge and docketed “11 Sett. 1680 … n.o 117”. The record is not English and not of 1555: DECODE’s region and date seem to have been copied from the neighbouring record R1872 (Giovanni Michiel, Brussels, 1555).

The DECODE images are not in the public domain and may be published only with the archive’s permission, so none are reproduced here. The strip above is from the transcription made here.

02 The cipher

The page holds 23 lines and 1,839 digits. The rare digits 0, 7, 8 and 9 occur only in one position, and within each line that position is constant modulo 3 over long stretches: the digits are three-digit groups, and the jumps in phase are slips of the pen or of the transcription. Segmented that way the page gives 612 groups (593 of three digits), 211 of them different. The first digit runs 1–5 (6 is rare), the second 1–6 and the third 0–9, so the table holds about 300 codes between 110 and 569. The commonest groups are 156 (22 times), 517 (20), 256 (19), 152 (16) and 459 (15).

Bonavoglia says Foscarini’s cipher served at both Paris and Madrid, and calls it too regular and orderly. The table here is not ordered alphabetically, though: Sukhotin’s vowel test puts vowel-like and consonant-like codes side by side (151 and 156 vowel-like, 152–154 consonant-like).

03 How it was tried

  • Other segmentations: two-digit pairs (the gap parity is random) and self-delimiting tokens ending in 5–0 or 6–0. Annealing gave no Italian.
  • A monotone annealer for an ordered syllabary, 12 seeds, scored with a clean Italian 4-gram model. It recovers a synthetic ordered control only in part, and gives the same non-Italian text on every seed for the real page.
  • Free annealers, one letter per code and letter or syllable per code, 14 seeds: fluent-looking text that changes with every seed, the mark of overfitting.

211 free code values against 612 groups is beyond what an Italian language model can pin down for a homophonic, syllabic table. The page needs the key (in the ASVe Cifre, chiavi e scontri; the only Venetian key records on DECODE, R1788–R1790, are 16th-century tables and do not match), more ciphertext in the same key, or Bonavoglia’s decipherment.

04 The reading

Paolo Bonavoglia’s page on this despatch was offline during the attempt. Brice Dudout reported it back online on 27 October 2026, and its text is saved in the repository with an English translation. Bonavoglia gives the original as ASVe Senato, Dispacci Francia, filza 171A, and the Doge’s chancery decipherment as filza 166. He worked the table out from this and other despatches.

It is a postscript written after the despatch was signed. Foscarini passes on a report he cannot yet confirm: the Spaniards are adding troops in the Milanese, and Louis XIV means to raise the Pinerolo garrison to 6,000 men of the old regiments and spread 16,000 men over Dauphiné and Provence. The Duke of Mantua’s illness and a feared Spanish move on Casale are given as reasons, and munitions are said to be reaching Pinerolo by way of Savoy. Bonavoglia judges it a false alarm.

Brice Dudout rebuilt the table, which Bonavoglia does not publish: a syllabary in blocks of five consecutive codes, one consonant per block with the vowels in order (b 115, n 142, r 151, ch 164, f 224 …), the bare vowels on X56 (a 156, e 256, i 356, o 456, u 556), and a few words (167 con, 368 non, 200 a new paragraph). Learnt on lines 1–15 of the transcription made here and applied to lines 16–23 held out, it agrees with Bonavoglia’s text at 94.9% of the letters. The table is in the repository folder (dudout/table_blocks.json), with his decoding of this transcription.

05 What remains open

  • Three number groups that Bonavoglia leaves open (“[Francesi?] 269?”, “114?” and the closing 202.106.100).
  • The key is not rebuilt here, and Bonavoglia’s text is not aligned group by group with the transcription made here: his ciphertext viewer still returned an error on 27 October 2026. Dudout’s table points at two places in the transcription to re-read on the image: the group 114 at lines 18–19, and the middle of line 22, where 3/5 and 2/0 are the likely confusions. The group 269 (Bonavoglia’s “[Francesi?]”, once) stays open in his table too.

# R1871, Sebastiano Foscarini to the Doge, Paris, 11 September 1680, despatch no. 117, P.S. sola (Bonavoglia's decipherment)

## Reading

Sermo Principe

Dopo firmato il dispazia mi uiene mandato un auiso al quale se bene non poso prestare intiera fede per non hauere tempo di cercar nei riscontri tuttauia per l'inspetion che porta seco non deuo tralasciare di parteciparlo a Vostra Serenità

Sopra gli auisi che uengono continuamente d'Italia che li spagnoli acrescono considerabilmente di trupe nel Milanese il re che ha quantità di milizia in piedi mi uien auertito che habbia risoluto di rinforzare il presidio di Pinarolo sino a seimila huomini de uechi regimenti e di distribuire nel Delfinato e nella Prouenza sedecimila conbatenti per essere al ocasion che insorgiesero piu forte degli spagnoli stesi

Non è dificile che incontri nel uero la notizia che mi uiene scrita da un mio confidente dalla corte potendo hauere consigliate queste preuention il male del signor duca di Mantoua et il sospeto che tien uiuo l'abate Morel di qualche acidente inprouiso di sua uita e la diuulgation che nella passata di lui indisposition hauesero spagnoli meditato di spingiere in Casale cinque in seimila huomini

Mi uiene pure acenato che si continui a trasportare a Pinarolo munition da [Francesi?]269? ma queste sarano li metali che gia acenai nelle mie di 114? che si faceuano condure dal Mare per la parte di Sauoia per li quali si erano anco dimandati pasaporti

Starò atento quanto bisogna a così graue materia e procurerò di purificare non auisi per renderne miglior conto nella uentura a Vostra Serenità asicurandola che io non risparmio qualunque diligienza per l'adempimento del mio douere 202.106.100

Parigi li 11 Settre 1680  Sebastiano Foscarini Ambre

# R1871, Sebastiano Foscarini to the Doge, Paris, 11 September 1680, despatch no. 117, P.S. sola: English translation

Most Serene Prince,

After the despatch was signed, a report was sent to me. Although I cannot give it full faith, having no time to look
for confirmation, the weight it carries means I must not fail to pass it on to Your Serenity.

On the reports that keep coming from Italy that the Spaniards are considerably increasing their troops in the
Milanese, I am told that the King, who has a great number of soldiers under arms, has resolved to reinforce the
garrison of Pinerolo up to six thousand men of the old regiments, and to distribute sixteen thousand fighting men in
Dauphiné and Provence, so as to be ready, should the occasion arise, stronger than the Spaniards themselves.

It is not unlikely that the news written to me by a confidant of mine at court is true. These precautions may have
been advised by the illness of the Duke of Mantua, by the suspicion that the abbé Morel keeps alive of some sudden
accident to his life, and by the rumour that during his last illness the Spaniards had meant to push five or six
thousand men into Casale.

I am also given to understand that munitions continue to be carried to Pinerolo from [the French? 269?], but these
will be the metals I already mentioned in my letters of [114?], which were being brought from the sea by way of
Savoy, and for which passports had also been requested.

I shall watch so grave a matter as closely as it needs, and shall try to sift the reports so as to give a better
account of them to Your Serenity in the next post, assuring you that I spare no diligence in doing my duty.
202.106.100

Paris, 11 September 1680. Sebastiano Foscarini, Ambassador
