Paris → Doge of Venice · three-digit numerical nomenclator, Italian · 11 September 1680

Sebastiano Foscarini to the Doge, Paris 1680

A Venetian ambassador’s despatch written wholly in a three-digit code; it was transcribed and attacked here without result, and the one published decipherment could not be retrieved.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 14 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

Credits · Paolo Bonavoglia (pointer to a source) · Satoshi Tomokiyo (pointer to a source)

At a glanceNot solved · 612 groups transcribed

The letter
Sebastiano Foscarini, Venetian ambassador in France, to the Doge, Paris, 11 September 1680: despatch no. 117, “P.S. Sola”, one page of cipher.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Venezia, IT ASVe 0045 010 Busta 30 f. 4; DECODE R1871. Catalogued as “England?”, 1555, under catalogue 264 together with R1873, a Rome despatch of 1558 in another cipher.
The cipher
Continuous digits that fall into three-digit groups: 612 groups, 211 different, from a table of about 300 codes. Not an alphabetical table.
How it was tried
Transcribed from the images, segmented, then attacked with several annealing solvers and an Italian language model. No Italian came out.
Why it resists
One page is too short for a blind solution of a table this size. The key is not on DECODE, and Paolo Bonavoglia’s decipherment is offline.
Credit
Paolo Bonavoglia lists the despatch as deciphered; Satoshi Tomokiyo’s note on DECODE R1872 points to him.

Until 14 October 2026 this despatch shared an entry with R1873, Alvise Mocenigo’s Rome despatch of 1558: a different cipher, with its own page.

01 The record

The two images of R1871 show one page of continuous digits under “Ser.mo Principe”, marked “#117 P.S. Sola” and signed “Sebastian Foscarini Amb.r”, dated at Paris in 1680. The dorse is addressed to the Doge and docketed “11 Sett. 1680 … n.o 117”. The record is not English and not of 1555: DECODE’s region and date seem to have been copied from the neighbouring record R1872 (Giovanni Michiel, Brussels, 1555).

The DECODE images are not in the public domain and may be published only with the archive’s permission, so none are reproduced here. The strip above is from the transcription made here.

02 The cipher

The page holds 23 lines and 1,839 digits. The rare digits 0, 7, 8 and 9 occur only in one position, and within each line that position is constant modulo 3 over long stretches: the digits are three-digit groups, and the jumps in phase are slips of the pen or of the transcription. Segmented that way the page gives 612 groups (593 of three digits), 211 of them different. The first digit runs 1–5 (6 is rare), the second 1–6 and the third 0–9, so the table holds about 300 codes between 110 and 569. The commonest groups are 156 (22 times), 517 (20), 256 (19), 152 (16) and 459 (15).

Bonavoglia says Foscarini’s cipher served at both Paris and Madrid, and calls it too regular and orderly. The table here is not ordered alphabetically, though: Sukhotin’s vowel test puts vowel-like and consonant-like codes side by side (151 and 156 vowel-like, 152–154 consonant-like).

03 How it was tried

  • Other segmentations: two-digit pairs (the gap parity is random) and self-delimiting tokens ending in 5–0 or 6–0. Annealing gave no Italian.
  • A monotone annealer for an ordered syllabary, 12 seeds, scored with a clean Italian 4-gram model. It recovers a synthetic ordered control only in part, and gives the same non-Italian text on every seed for the real page.
  • Free annealers, one letter per code and letter or syllable per code, 14 seeds: fluent-looking text that changes with every seed, the mark of overfitting.

211 free code values against 612 groups is beyond what an Italian language model can pin down for a homophonic, syllabic table. The page needs the key (in the ASVe Cifre, chiavi e scontri; the only Venetian key records on DECODE, R1788–R1790, are 16th-century tables and do not match), more ciphertext in the same key, or Bonavoglia’s decipherment.

04 What remains open

  • The whole despatch, 612 groups. Blocker: no key material reachable.
  • Bonavoglia’s decipherment: his page on this despatch (storia/SebFoscarini_Parigi16800911.html) is offline and not archived, and the interactive page returned an error in September 2026.
  • Foscarini’s other ciphered despatches of 1680–84 (ASVe Senato, Dispacci Francia busta 171A, and Spagna) are not on DECODE; with them a ciphertext-only rerun would be worth trying.
