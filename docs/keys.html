The Key Web

A cipher is rarely read on its own. A key rebuilt from one letter's margin is tried on its unread siblings. A contemporary key turns up filed under the next record number. A table published by Lasry or Tomokiyo is turned on a letter they never saw. And many keys are tried and do not fit. This page follows every key behind the site's readings: who made it, which letters it read, and where it failed.

Letters sit roughly by date, left to right. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan or to move a node. Hover a key to trace it; click to pin it and open its card. Turn on tried, did not fit to add the keys that were tested and rejected.

Keys tried and ruled out

Testing a key that does not fit is half of the work. These are the named keys the notes record as tried on a letter and rejected, with the reason.

    The key web

    Pick a key

    Click a key to see where it came from, the letters it read and those it failed on; click a letter to see which keys read it. The biggest keys did the most work: made for one letter, they went on to read the rest of its series. Every key has its own link: copy it from the card.

    About the data

    Every link is taken from an explicit statement in the target's notes: that a letter was read with a named key, unchanged or adapted, that a key was rebuilt from that letter's own material, or that a named key was tested on it and did not fit. Links are not inferred from resemblance. A key counts as rebuilt here when this project recovered it, from the ciphertext, from glosses or from a decipherment; keys recovered by other people (Lasry, Tomokiyo, Somogyi, and outside contributors) are scholars' keys; keys of the period are those that survive in the archives or on DECODE. A scholar's key is placed at the date of the cipher, not of its publication, which the card gives separately. Several of George Lasry's tables for the French correspondence of the 1520s and 1530s appear as one key. Letters read straight from a contemporary decipherment, with no key rebuilt, are not shown. The file is keys.json; the site build checks it.
