Mexican embassy to the consul at Eagle Pass, 17 April 1917

Go to the full text ↓

A telegram the consul said he could not decipher; its key, a folded keyword alphabet, was recovered from the cipher text alone, and it asks for the names of journalists spreading false news.

The telegram
Mexican embassy, Washington, to José M. Arredondo, consul of Mexico at Eagle Pass, Texas, 17 April 1917. Its clear preamble says it repeats a message enciphered the day before in the "Murciélago" key, "clave treintaiuno".
Where
AHGE-SRE, Archivo de la Embajada de México en Estados Unidos, leg. 507, exp. 6 (original not seen). Worked from the halftone printed as fig. 10, p. 339, in Roberto Narváez, Criptografía diplomática, política y militar en México (1813–1926) (SRE, 2019).
The cipher
246 letters in one reciprocal alphabet: A-X, B-U, C-S, D-Q, E-P, F-M, G-L, I-Z, N-W, O-V, R-Y, and T stands for itself. It is the keyword alphabet TIRANO folded on itself.
How the key was found
Key recovered by simulated annealing on Spanish letter statistics, after controls at this length; an independent blind solve by another AI agent gave the same key and sentence. The TIRANO construction was noticed afterwards and checked by a keyword search.
What it says
For the censorship committee named by President Wilson, the consul is to send the names of journalists and information agents who spread false news, chiefly of German plots.
Still open
Eleven words carry a letter the key does not give, read from the sense: nine are sender's slips, one probable, one an overtyped letter. Only the archive original can settle them.

01 The telegram

The sheet is typed, headed "WASHINGTON D.C. ABRIL 17 de 1917." and addressed to "SR. JOSE M. ARREDONDO, CONSUL DE MEXICO, EAGLE PASS, TEXAS." The clear preamble reads "MENSAJE AYER CIFRADO CLAVE MUERCIELAGO REPITOSELO CLAVE TREINTAIUNO": the message enciphered yesterday in the Murciélago key, repeated to you, key thirty-one. Then come 246 cipher letters in groups of five.

Narváez prints it with its context (pp. 338–342). In April 1917 several consuls (Douglas, San Francisco, New Orleans, Eagle Pass, Brownsville) wrote that they could not decipher embassy telegrams. On 17 April Arredondo replied that he could not read this one with "clave Murciélago ochentaidos", word 82 of the consular key list. Narváez explains the confusion by consuls using the wrong key list, and says the message stayed impenetrable to the consul.

The cipher text was transcribed from phone photographs of a loan copy of the book. No image of the book is reproduced on this page or in the repository, so the strip at the top is typed from the transcription.

02 The cipher and its key

The index of coincidence is 0.071, flat over periods 1 to 12: one alphabet, not a keyword run through the Murciélago square named in the heading. The book's square with word 31 of the printed list was tested first, under a plan frozen beforehand, and missed.

The key is reciprocal: each pair works both ways. It is the keyword alphabet TIRANOBCDEFGHJKLMPQSUVWXYZ (26 letters, no Ñ) written round a circle, each letter paired with the one opposite it across the line through T, with T and J standing for themselves. The telegram itself fixes 11 pairs and T (12 independent facts). Cipher A, H, I, J and K never occur; H = k, K = h and J = j come from the construction alone.

TIRANO was noticed by eye in the recovered pairs before a search was planned. The search, under a frozen plan, tried about 23,000 keywords over 26- and 27-letter alphabets, folds about every axis and two-row pairings (3.7 million keys): TIRANO folded through T is the only fit. That search is not in the repository, because its files hold tables printed in the book; the key itself does not depend on it, and the integration review rebuilt the key from the rule alone and found it equal to key.tsv in all 26 rows.

TIRANO is not word 31 of the printed list (that is Escultor) and does not appear in it. How "clave treintaiuno" leads to it is not known; word 31 of a second, unprinted list is one possibility. The same folding builds the 1915 "Clave Garduño" that Narváez prints (p. 311), from the keyword GARDUÑO, on one transcription of that table.

03 How the key was found

  • Three transcriptions of the 246 letters (one by the working agent, two blind) differ only at the letters listed under section 05.
  • Controls first: three shuffles of the letters gave no Spanish; three 246-letter Spanish passages under random keys came back 99.2% to 99.6% right.
  • Simulated annealing on Spanish letter 4-grams, 40 restarts. The best scores sat above every shuffle and inside the range of the enciphered controls. The rare cipher letters D, M and N were set by the reciprocal pairing the other letters show.
  • Held-out check: the key solved on letters 1–170 reads letters 171–246 unchanged apart from the sender's slips.
  • The plan's pass mark, 90% of letters in clean words, was missed: 159 of 246 letters (64.6%). The cause is slips spread over eleven words, not unread passages.
  • A separate AI agent, before seeing this work, made its own blind transcription and recovered the key with its own code and corpus: same key, same Spanish sentence, word for word.
  • Statistical validation (docs/_validate.py, 24 Nov 2026): reading −2.752 per letter, random keys −7.254, a key annealed onto shuffled ciphertext at best −3.784. Verdict: pass.

04 The reading

Telegraphic Spanish, articles and prepositions left out as written. A word in braces carries a letter the key does not give, read from the sense.

Para {presentar} los a comité censura nombrado {por} {presidente} Wilson {necesito} mándeme mayor brevedad posible nombres {periodistas} y agentes información {que} propalan {falsas} {noticias} y agitan opinión principalmente con noticias complots {alemanes}. Debemos poder {comprobarlo} caso {necesario}.

To present them to the censorship committee appointed by President Wilson, I need you to send me as soon as possible the names of journalists and information agents who spread false news and stir up opinion, chiefly with news of German plots. We must be able to prove it if necessary.

The United States had declared war on 6 April 1917, and on 13 April Wilson created the Committee on Public Information; the "comité censura" fits that committee. The identification is the independent reviewer's.

Counts (count_words.py): 26 of 37 words clean (70.3%), 159 of 246 letters. If position 121 is N rather than M, 25 of 37. The key replay reports 27 of 37 words exact, because it charges the extra N in "falsas" to the gap beside it; the strict count charges it to the word. The reading was graded B (coherent but raw) by an AI agent's integration review for the maintainers, not by a person.

05 What remains open

  • Sender's slips, clear in all four readings of the photograph: presen{t}ar (F typed), pre{s}iden{t}e (S, possibly a seseo spelling, and P), n{e}c{e}sito (T twice), periodis{t}as (P), notic{i}as (C), al{e}manes (T), co{m}probarlo (S), and an extra N in falsas. Five are one pattern, plain e and t exchanged.
  • q{u}e (position 131): V is clear and a light slash follows it, perhaps a correction mark.
  • nec{e}sario (position 241): a probable slip; read F, with P (which would make the word clean) as the alternative. F against P is the pair this halftone confuses most.
  • p{o}r (position 40): the letter is overtyped; the sender may have corrected it.
  • Doubtful letters at the limit of the halftone (positions 7, 23, 29, 73, 82, 83, 101, 114, 121, 129, 132, 136, 141, 144, 152, 208, 220, 232, 243, 245), each listed with its evidence in the folder's NOTES. Only 121 (M/N) and 129 (W/F) would change a count.
  • Every item above needs the archive's original of leg. 507, exp. 6; a sharper photograph of the book cannot separate F from P or show what lies under the overtyping. The second key list, which might explain "clave treintaiuno", is in the same file and not printed.

No earlier reading was found in Narváez 2019, his 2025 article, the repository's catalogue and leads, or web searches for the telegram's phrases. Cryptiana and Lasry's list were not checked.

## Words clean only under a doubtful reading

- información (position 121): read M, which gives f; the photograph allows N, which gives w ("inwormación"). Two of the three
  transcriptions prefer N. If N, this word is a hole too.
- mándeme mayor (position 73): read F (two transcriptions, and the reviewer after a closer look); P would give "mándemeeayor".
- que (position 130): cipher D occurs only here; its value q rests on the reciprocal pair with cipher Q = d.
- censura (29, Y/X), información (129, W/F, possibly overtyped) and poder (220, Q/G): new doubts from a fourth blind reading of the photograph; the three earlier transcriptions agree on the reading used. With every preferred reading of the fourth blind reading (7 F, 29 X, 129 F, 220 G), 23 of 37 words (136 of 246 letters) would be clean. The other new doubts (7, 152, 208) fall in words that are holes already (presentar, noticias, alemanes).

# eaglepass1917 - English translation of reading.md

Words in {braces} are holes in the Spanish (a letter read from the sense); the translation follows the reading.

To {present} them to the censorship committee appointed {by} {President} Wilson, I {need} you to send me as soon as possible
the names of {journalists} and information agents {who} spread {false} {news} and stir up opinion, chiefly with news of
{German} plots. We must be able to {prove} it if {necessary}.

Notes. "Comité censura nombrado por presidente Wilson": the Committee on Public Information, created by Executive Order 2594
of 13 April 1917, four days before this repetition (the formal Censorship Board came only in October 1917). "Presentarlos"
looks forward to "nombres": the names are to be handed to that committee.
