Kendi ter perfidus

Transylvania → King Ferdinand I · homophonic cipher of graphic signs and digits, Latin · c. 1556

Pál Bornemisza to Ferdinand I on the Kendi treason, Transylvania, c. 1556

Go to the full text ↓

Österreichisches Staatsarchiv, HHStA, Staatskanzlei Interiora, Chiffrenschlüssel Kt. 13, Fasc. 20, ff. 38–39; DECODE R1212. Catalogued as a Latin letter from an unknown sender to “Paulo Borne?”. It is a report written in the cipher of Pál Bornemisza, Bishop of Transylvania, and in his own voice, to “Your Sacred Majesty”, King Ferdinand I: Ferenc Kendi, “three times a traitor”, has lost the kingdom of Transylvania to the king. A contemporary hand deciphered nearly every run between the lines; the key is the next DECODE record, R1211, and every run was decrypted again with it (95.8% of the cipher tokens).

01 The record and the key

R1212 is two leaves, ff. 38–39, in the Vienna collection of cipher keys, a volume whose DECODE dates (1540–1774) are the span of the whole box. Pages 1–2 and 3–4 are two reports under the same head, “D[omi]no … Thur. Prim.” and “Practica Thur. Prim.”, each opening with the enciphered words horum omnium motuum, “of all these disturbances”. The foot of p. 4 carries two rows of signs with faint pencil letters over them: an inventory of the signs in the order they first occur in that heading. The key is on a separate leaf.

The key is R1211, f. 37, the leaf filed just before the letter: A = Δ 3 5, B = L, C = 9, D = ꝛ, E = y M 2, F = o, G = B, H = #, I = Π Ꝛ ~ k, K = β, L = L 4, M = Ϙ Λ, N = P, O = φ x v, P = A, Q = Ҁ, R = Ɔ, S = σ, T = Ω, U = C Λ ɧ, X = π, and the codes 34 Transsylvania and 27 Petrovics. R1210 (f. 36), also labelled for Bornemisza, is an older numerical key of about 1540 (Rex Joannes, Frater Georgius, Verbőczy) and does not fit this letter.

The writer is loose with the key: x and v stand for u as well as o, ~ for u and o as well as i, 9 (c) for t in prodicionem, nacionum, comiciis; the barred Π is his h, and β serves for c and h as well as k. A pair of digits in a run usually stands for two letters (34 in alias, aliquando, alliciebat).

03 Method

The four DECODE images were transcribed in horizontal bands, first at half and then at full resolution, one pass per pair of pages. Each cipher run was deciphered from its signs with the R1211 key before the gloss was consulted, and every disagreement was settled on the image. The gloss is right in substance throughout; where the signs differ they give the better text (ipse for the gloss’s apparent esse, nisi for nostri, maledictum for maledicti, evenisset for evenissent, inflammavit for inflamare, intellexerunt for intellexerat, perniciosarum for perniciosam, in celo for in celis, vexillum(?) for auxilium). Apparent codes 0399, 134 and several 34s dissolved into letters. Counts: 288 cipher tokens (274 words and 14 codes); 257 words decrypted securely, 14 uncertain but in sense, 3 not decrypted; 5 codes in the key, 9 not.

04 What remains open

  • Number codes not in the key: {24} {15} {10} (“are solicited beyond the gate to come into this kingdom”), {41}·{70}·{55}·{78}·{18} (“have been killed”; five persons) and {52} (“to the son … many thousands”). The contemporary decipherer could not decipher them either: he copied them into the margin and guessed “Siculi vel Saxones” for a 44 in the margin. R1211 lists only 34 and 27; R1210’s name codes (2–20, 80, 90) belong to a different cipher of about 1540 and give no sense here.
  • Illegible: two signs on p. 1 l. 9 half covered by a struck gloss, and the run R Ɔ C on p. 3 l. 5 (gloss “-ias”?).
  • Uncertain (grade M): vexillum (p. 3 l. 12, gloss auxilium), habiturum, residerem, constasset, vestras, and about ten clear words at the damaged top of p. 1 and on p. 3 (the heading “Thur. Prim.”, “tehem…”, the closing phrase of p. 4).
  • Date: not on the letter. Kendi’s turn to Queen Isabella and Bornemisza’s loss of Alba point to 1556, before Kendi’s own murder in 1558.

# R1212, Pál Bornemisza to Ferdinand I, Transylvania [c. 1556]: English translation

### Page 1

⟨To the Lord … Thur. Prim.⟩ Of all these disturbances.

⟨I have hitherto always commended to Your Most Sacred Majesty a man in all good things, of which all […]; of signal⟩
perfidy I must deservedly accuse him. ⟨For no other⟩ is the cause of this uprising but Francis Kendi, three times
a traitor; he himself ⟨to this end⟩ induced Antal Kendi and Menyhért Balassa ⟨and⟩ a few others of the nobles, ⟨and⟩
[…]. But the treacherous man committed this treason ⟨out of⟩ his own cowardice and excessive despair. ⟨May the gods⟩
overwhelm this cursed traitor, who has lost this ⟨kingdom to Your Majesty⟩, no one else. {24} {15} ⟨and⟩ {10}
⟨beyond the gate⟩ are being solicited to come into this kingdom. ⟨I have written this to Your Most Sacred Majesty
briefly but truly; there is no doubt in the matter.⟩ {41} {70} {55} {78} {18} ⟨[?have been killed]; still, to Your Sacred
Majesty,⟩ they await help ⟨from⟩ the Sacred Majesty [?vestras], but not for long. If more troops were
here now with a good captain, ⟨the wound would therefore still⟩ be curable.

### Page 2

I will say again that Francis Kendi alone has lost the kingdom to Your Majesty, no one else. This is as true as the
Gospel of Christ. Without his counsel nothing is done in this kingdom; therefore he himself inflamed the others to
stir up these disturbances; otherwise ⟨the manifest ruin⟩ would not have come about. Although there have been
horrible complaints everywhere against the insolence of the voivodes' soldiers, about the deflowering of tender
virgins, and this in the presence of the public assembly of the three nations, many violations of married women;
besides many other plunderings, ⟨alas⟩, which no man could enumerate.

pretext he [?en]ticed me with his blandishments, saying that I ought to remain in this bishopric, but that I should
cede Alba as a residence to the son of {52}, and I should [?reside] in the castle of Gialu, and sometimes at Alba in the
Provost's house. These are the words of Francis Kendi to me at Vásárhely during the diet, in a private room, the two
of us ⟨alone⟩ being present. If Francis
