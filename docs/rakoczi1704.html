Three letters to Ferenc Rákóczi II catalogued as his, 1706–1707

Go to the full text ↓

Three French reports sent to Rákóczi from Danzig and the Polish court, catalogued as his own letters: two were decoded with the Bonac–Groffey key DECODE R639 after the key and the letters were transcribed again from the images, and now build French sentences with a few holes (R912 reads at 94.6% of its words, under the 95% a complete reading needs); the third has its own contemporary clear copy.

The documents
Catalogue item 44 grouped four DECODE records attributed to Ferenc Rákóczi II. Three (R852, R912, R922) are French reports sent to him from Danzig or “au quartier”; R483 is his own letter from Lwów, 27 June 1711.
Where
Magyar Nemzeti Levéltár: G15 Caps. C. Fasc. 39 (R852, R912, R922) and P237 Festetics 10. d. 2 (R483). DECODE holds the images; 14 page images were re-transcribed.
The cipher
R639 is a one-part numerical nomenclator: letters and endings, syllables and common words, titles, peoples and places, with nulls. R852 uses an earlier issue of the table; R483 is a syllabic code.
How it was read
The DECODE key R639 was matched to the re-transcribed groups, which corrected DECODE’s copy of the table. R912 and R922 now decipher at 97%; R852 has its own clear copy. For R483, aligning 2,531 groups with the clerk’s interlinear text rebuilt a 224-code key that reproduces 70% of his letters.
What it says
R922 concerns a sale for the Palatine of Belz and money; R912 surveys Polish and Swedish politics; R852 urges printing a weekly Mercurius hungaricus.
Still open
62 groups of R912 and R922 are not deciphered, and R852’s variant word codes are not tabulated. 68 of R483’s rebuilt codes occur once, and no edited Latin text has been made.

R639 is a one-part numerical nomenclator, headed De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei. Codes 10–120 are letters and endings, 121–460 syllables and common words, and 461–560 titles, peoples and places. The odd numbers from 97 to 119 are nulls, and so are the groups of 500 and above that open a line. Transcribing the sheet from its image corrected DECODE's copy of it. The S and T columns have 45 and 47 written over other figures, and DECODE transcribed them as 95 and 96. The Z column has 54, 55, 110. The numbering jumps from 172 (Da) to 175 (de), yet the letters use 173 and 174 constantly as “de” (“le Roy 173 Pologne”, “Palatin 174 Belz”). 561 and 562, beyond the end of the table, stand for “nt”.

The new transcriptions corrected about 40% of DECODE's lines. They restored a line DECODE had dropped in both R912 and R852, removed repeated runs, and split merged groups.

02 R852: Dantzig, 22 January 1706

Monseigneur, j’ay receu la lettre que V. A. m’a fait l’honneur de m’escrire le 26. Xbre. Je feray passer au Sr Groffey celles qui estoient pour luy … V. A. peut luy donner directement les ordres, outre que je vous respons encore une fois de sa fidelité … J’ay veu une ou deux fois un imprimé latin intitulé Mercurius hungaricus. Il seroit tres important pour le service de V. A. qu’on imprimast regulierement toutes les semaines une feuille de cet imprimé … il n’y a point de gazetier allemand ny hollandois qui ne detruise à son ayse le Royme d’Hongrie … On croioit encore à Berlin qu’il n’y avoit plus de Hongrois depuis l’action.

The ciphered page is followed by its clear copy, transcribed here from the image, and by a short clear note offering to keep a register of M. de Bonnac's letters and bills of exchange. The writer is at Danzig and speaks of Groffey in the third person. The cipher's letters and syllables agree with R639, but its word codes do not: 144 is de and 254 is la, where R639 has beaucoup and jusque. It is an earlier issue of the table.

03 R922: Dantzig, 28 October

Monseigneur, je me suis privé [?pendant] quelque temps de l’honneur d’escrire à Vostre Altesse … faite à Madame la Palatine de Belz … une copie authentique de cette vente au sieur Kray … autant qu’il a esté possible, par mon credit et par mes avances … pour joindre le Roy de Pologne … l’avantage de luy continuer mes services à la Cour de Pologne … Je suis avec tout le respect possible, Monseigneur.

From the reading of 23 October 2026 (texts/R922_reading.md, English R922_en.md): the decode with the corrected R639 table divided into words and normalised by hand, the table’s words read for their sound (161 con, 395 son, 188 dont), doubtful places checked on the image. Holes: groups 9, 873 and 574, one “?” group and one short run; 18 letters rest on a look-alike figure.

The letter concerns a sale for the Palatine of Belz (Elżbieta Sieniawska), orders for carbines, and the sieur Kray. On page 2 it turns to money and to someone who is a prisoner.

A contemporary hand deciphered only the first two lines, between the lines: the date and “je me suis privé pendant quelque temps de l’honneur d’escrire à Vostre Altesse”, 37 of the 722 groups. Everything after them was deciphered here with R639 alone.

04 R912: “au quartier”, 5 November

Monseigneur, ayant eu l’honneur de marquer à Vostre Altesse le 29 du mois dernier que le Roy Stanislas … le Palatin de Belz … charge de grand Général … la pacification … une nouvelle élection en Pologne … le Czar … la lettre qu’elle avoit escrite au Maréchal Rheinschild … Monsieur le Palatin de Russie … avec un profond respect et un zèle parfait.

From the reading of 23 October 2026 (texts/R912_reading.md, English R912_en.md). Page 1 follows the 1707 interlinear decipherment read on the image (texts/R912_interlinear_p1.txt), checked against the figures, with seven figure corrections seen there (473 is 493 les Polonois, 501 is 301 …); the rest is the R639 decode, divided and normalised by hand. Page 1 and the end of page 3 build fully. Page 2 keeps runs of holes (ll. 3–7, 14–22, 26–31), where the groups as transcribed give no French under R639 and a second look at the image is still due.

A survey of Polish and Swedish politics that includes the Rehnskiöld affair of R902. It also covers a regiment asked of Rákóczi, which King Stanisław and the Germans hold against him, and Tököly.

What was deciphered at the time. A contemporary hand wrote the plaintext between the lines of page 1 over every line except the end of one and the two below it (659 of the page’s 713 groups), over the first four lines and the start of the fifth on page 2, and over the first line and the start of the second on page 3: about 780 of the letter’s 1,630 groups. DECODE’s transcription records these interlinear lines. The opening, the Palatine of Belz, the grand général, the pacification, the new election, the Tsar and the letter to Rehnskiöld quoted above all lie under it. The rest of pages 2 and 3 carries no decipherment on the leaves and was deciphered here with R639 alone: the Palatine of Ruthenia, the regiment, Stanisław and the Germans, Tököly and the closing formula. So were the three lines of page 1 that the decipherer left.

Those three lines finish a sentence about the King of Sweden, “ce Prince s’est voulu réserver un prétexte de rester cet hiver en Pologne pour ne pas s’éloigner de l’Allemagne”, where the interlinear stops (added 30 September 2026):

… ni perdre de veue les affaires générales de l’Europe. Aussy publie-t-on comme une chose presque certaine qu’il n’entrera en Moschovie que la campagne qui vient.

‘… nor lose sight of the general affairs of Europe. So it is given out as an almost certain thing that he [Charles XII] will not enter Muscovy until the coming campaign.’ He did invade Russia only in the campaign of 1708. Of the 54 groups, 44 read directly with R639 (H), four are fillers or nulls (589, 619; 115, 97), and six need comment:

  • publie: the group is 327 = pu. Its last figure has the open top of this hand’s 7 (as in 257 and 297), not the round bowl of its 9 (349, 149). It had been transcribed 329 = pas (M).
  • une: the group is 87 = U. Its first figure closes in a loop at the foot, like the 8 of 87 in “un homme” higher on the page, not the open hooked 5. It had been transcribed 57 = Et, here and by DECODE (M).
  • veue: 427 426, both ve in the key. The key has no separate v (its alphabet runs T, U, X), and a line higher the contemporary decipherer wrote ue over 427, so the pair reads ve-ue (M).
  • presque: 343 350, présent que in the key. The writer used 343 for the pré- of prétexte two lines higher, where the decipherer also wrote pre (M).
  • campagne: 58 = ns on the image and in the key, read as a slip for 88 = M (M).
  • vient: 534 46, Vienne and T. The place code is used for its sound, as 266 leur gives the l’Eur- of l’Europe in the same line (M).

The first transcription also had 509 (France) at the end of the last line. The image has 589, a filler, as DECODE reads it, so R912 has 1,585 keyed groups, not 1,586.

05 What remains uncertain

  • The reading audit of 22 October 2026 set R912 and R922 back to decoded, not read: their image decodes were syllable-split with misreadings in most lines. Written out as French on 23 October, both read (grade B), so all three letters now build French sentences; the entry stays partial because R912 page 2 ll. 3–7, 14–22 and 26–31 and page 3 ll. 2–3 keep runs of holes, to be re-read on the images; with them R912 reads at 94.6% of its words (measured 24 October 2026), under 95%. A clear letter shows under the R922 sheet (fascicle p. 350), possibly a clear copy, but pp. 348–350 are not imaged on DECODE.
  • R912 and R922: 62 groups not deciphered. They are line-opening and line-closing fillers, R912's closing block of small figures (probably a date and a name), and values outside the table: 93 (×3), 89 (×3), 9 (×2). 173/174 = de and 561/562 = nt are inferred from use (C).
  • The year 1707 for R912 and R922 rests on content (C). R852's 1706 is written on the page.
  • Writer: unsigned. R852 and R922 come from Danzig, and R852 names Groffey in the third person (M).
  • R852's variant word codes have not been tabulated. Its clear copy gives the text.

# R922, an agent at Danzig to Ferenc Rákóczi II, 28 October [1707] (MNL OL G15 Caps. C. Fasc. 39, pp. 346-347)

## Reading

A Dantzik le 28 Octobre. Monseigneur, je me suis privé [?pendant] quelque temps de l'honneur d'escrire à Vostre Altesse,
ne voulant pas le faire que je ne pusse luy marquer que l'affaire de Jaroslau estoit finie, que la vente ou donation en
avoit esté faite à Madame la Palatine de Belz. Ce fust mardi dernier; je remettray une copie authentique de cette vente
au sieur Kray, et je me contenteray de feliciter ici Vostre Altesse de la belle acquisition qu'elle a faite.

Nous avons reglé avec le sieur Kray les comptes de ce qui a esté dépensé depuis son départ. Elle verra dans le raport
qu'il luy en fera que j'ay évité autant qu'il a esté possible, par mon credit et par mes avances, le préjudice que le
service de Vostre Altesse pouvoit recevoir du peu d'ordre avec lequel le Comte Bertoti faisoit ses traittes, malgré la
manière dont il s'est comporté en cela, dont la [?veuve] [...] ni moy ne pouvions estre guère contents.

A l'arrivée du sieur Kray, afin [...] tout cela, je luy ay communiqué les ordres de Vostre Altesse pour les nouvelles
carabines; c'est luy [à qui] on a donné les ordres pour les premières, à l'emplette desquelles je n'ay eu aucune part.
Je compte de partir au premier jour pour joindre le Roy de Pologne. Tout sera reglé avant mon départ d'une manière dont
Vostre Altesse aura lieu d'estre satisfaite, et qu'elle n'aura qu'à adresser ses ordres au sieur Kray pour ces sortes de
choses, me reservant seulement l'avantage de luy continuer mes services à la Cour de Pologne, si elle juge à propos de
m'employer en quelque chose.

Je me suis expliqué amplement avec le sieur Kray au sujet des sieurs de Chassan et du Boucard. J'ajouteray seulement
ici que le premier, manquant de tout dans sa prison et m'asseurant qu'il n'a [873] receu directement de la part de
Vostre Altesse depuis qu'il est prisonnier, comme je juge que c'est parce qu'elle manque de correspondans en Autriche,
et que je puis luy faire toucher directement de l'argent d'ici, j'espère [qu'elle] aura agréable que je le [?fasse].
Je suis avec tout le respect possible, Monseigneur, [...]

# R922, an agent at Danzig to Ferenc Rákóczi II, 28 October [1707]

## Translation

Danzig, 28 October. My Lord, I have denied myself for [?some] time the honour of writing to Your Highness, not wishing to
do so until I could tell you that the Jarosław business was finished, that the sale or donation of it had been made to
Madame the Palatine of Bełz. That was last Tuesday; I shall hand an authentic copy of the sale to Mr Kray, and I shall
content myself with congratulating Your Highness here on the fine acquisition you have made.

We have settled with Mr Kray the accounts of what has been spent since his departure. You will see from the report he
will make to you that, as far as possible, by my credit and by my advances, I have averted the harm that Your Highness's
service could have suffered from the disorder with which Count Bertoti drew his bills, despite the way he behaved in
this, with which the [?widow] [...] and I could hardly be satisfied.

On Mr Kray's arrival, so that [...] all this, I passed on to him Your Highness's orders for the new carbines; it is he
who was given the orders for the first ones, in whose purchase I had no part. I expect to leave any day now to join the
King of Poland. Everything will be settled before my departure in a way with which Your Highness will have reason to
be satisfied, and you will only have to address your orders to Mr Kray for such matters, while I keep only the privilege
of continuing my services to you at the Polish court, if you see fit to employ me in anything.

I have explained myself fully with Mr Kray about Messrs de Chassan and du Boucard. I shall only add here that, since the
first, lacking everything in his prison, assures me that he has [873] received directly from Your Highness since he
became a prisoner, and as I judge that this is because you lack correspondents in Austria, and as I can have money paid
to him directly from here, I hope [you] will approve my [?doing] it. I am, with all possible respect, My Lord, [...]

# R852, to Prince Ferenc II Rákóczi, Dantzig, 22 January 1706: English translation of the contemporary clear copy

At Dantzig, 22 January 1706.

My Lord, I have received the letter that Your Highness did me the honour of writing to me on 26 December. I shall pass
on to the sieur Groffey those that were for him. I am sure that he will make good use, for Your Highness's service, of
the orders that you are pleased to give him. I shall help him on my side in everything that depends on me. Your
Highness may give him orders directly; besides, I answer to you once more for his fidelity. He is very prudent and
will never make anything but good use of them. I am waiting impatiently to learn that the sieur Roth. has finished
with the sieurs [?], so as to come and join me. I have seen once or twice a Latin print entitled Mercurius hungaricus.
It would be very important for Your Highness's service that a sheet of this print be printed regularly every week and
that one or two copies at least be sent to me. There is no German or Dutch gazetteer who does not destroy the Kingdom
of Hungary at his ease and turn your affairs upside down as he pleases. A similar paper would serve to prevent the bad
effect that those of your enemies produce. In Berlin they still believed that there were no more Hungarians since the
action of Kubo? when the letter of M. Rada[y] was received. M. de Bertoti could easily have the news printed and send
it to me by way of Cracow.
