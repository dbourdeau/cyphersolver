Four letters “of Ferenc Rákóczi II”: three were written to him

MNL OL G15 Caps. C. Fasc. 39 pp. 115, 312, 346 and P237 Festetics 10. d. 2. 42–44 (DECODE R852, R912, R922, R483). The catalogue listed all four as ciphered despatches of Rákóczi to an unknown recipient. The three French letters are reports sent to him, and they read with a preserved key once that key and the letters are transcribed again from the images. The Latin letter really is his. Its key has been rebuilt from the decipherment the clerk wrote between its lines.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 21 September 2026

Summary. Catalogue item 44 grouped four DECODE records attributed to Ferenc Rákóczi II. Three of them address “Monseigneur” and “Votre Altesse”: they are reports sent to Rákóczi, like R902 (Groffey to Rákóczi, 1707). A first pass on DECODE's text transcriptions read them in substance with the preserved key DECODE R639. A second pass went back to the fourteen page images. It re-transcribed every cipher page and read the key sheet itself, which corrected DECODE's copy of the table (45 = S, 47 = T, the Z line, and the two unnumbered “de” codes 173 and 174). R912 and R922 now read at 97% of groups. R852 turned out to carry its own contemporary clear copy: it is dated Danzig, 22 January 1706, and its word codes come from a different issue of the table. The fourth record, R483, is Rákóczi's own letter from Lwów, signed “Franciscus Princeps” on 27 June 1711, eight weeks after the Peace of Szatmár. Aligning its 2,531 groups with the clerk's interlinear rebuilds a 224-code syllabic key that reproduces 70% of the clerk's letters and settles several of his doubtful words.

R639 is a one-part numerical nomenclator, headed De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei. Codes 10–120 are letters and endings, 121–460 syllables and common words, and 461–560 titles, peoples and places. The odd numbers from 97 to 119 are nulls, and so are the groups of 500 and above that open a line. Reading the sheet corrected DECODE's copy of it. The S and T columns have 45 and 47 written over other figures, and DECODE read them as 95 and 96. The Z column reads 54, 55, 110. The numbering jumps from 172 (Da) to 175 (de), yet the letters use 173 and 174 constantly as “de” (“le Roy 173 Pologne”, “Palatin 174 Belz”). 561 and 562, beyond the end of the table, stand for “nt”.

The new transcriptions corrected about 40% of DECODE's lines. They restored a line DECODE had dropped in both R912 and R852, removed repeated runs, and split merged groups.

02 R852: Dantzig, 22 January 1706

Monseigneur, j’ay receu la lettre que V. A. m’a fait l’honneur de m’escrire le 26. Xbre. Je feray passer au Sr Groffey celles qui estoient pour luy … V. A. peut luy donner directement les ordres, outre que je vous respons encore une fois de sa fidelité … J’ay veu une ou deux fois un imprimé latin intitulé Mercurius hungaricus. Il seroit tres important pour le service de V. A. qu’on imprimast regulierement toutes les semaines une feuille de cet imprimé … il n’y a point de gazetier allemand ny hollandois qui ne detruise à son ayse le Royme d’Hongrie … On croioit encore à Berlin qu’il n’y avoit plus de Hongrois depuis l’action.

The ciphered page is followed by its clear copy, read here from the image, and by a short clear note offering to keep a register of M. de Bonnac's letters and bills of exchange. The writer is at Danzig and speaks of Groffey in the third person. The cipher's letters and syllables agree with R639, but its word codes do not: 144 is de and 254 is la, where R639 has beaucoup and jusque. It is an earlier issue of the table.

03 R922: Dantzig, 28 October

Monseigneur, je me suis privé pendant quelque temps de l’honneur d’escrire à Vostre Altesse … fait à Madame la Palatine de Belz … une copie authentique de cette vente au sieur Kray … autant qu’il a esté possible par mon credit et par mes avances … pour joindre le Roy de Pologne … l’avantage de luy continuer mes services à la Cour de Pologne … je suis avec tout le respect possible, Monseigneur.

The letter concerns a sale for the Palatine of Belz (Elżbieta Sieniawska), orders for carbines, and the sieur Kray. On page 2 it turns to money and to someone who is a prisoner.

04 R912: “au quartier”, 5 November

J’ay eu l’honneur de marquer à Vostre Altesse … le Roy Stanislas … le Palatin de Belz … le grand Général … la pacification … une nouvelle élection en Pologne … le Czar … escrite au maréchal Rehnskiöld … Monsieur le Palatin de Russie … avec un profond respect et un zèle parfait.

A survey of Polish and Swedish politics that includes the Rehnskiöld affair of R902. It also covers a regiment asked of Rákóczi, which King Stanisław and the Germans hold against him, and Tököly.

05 R483: Rákóczi, Lwów, 27 June 1711

This letter is signed in autograph “Franciscus Princeps” and dated in clear “Leopoli die 27. Junii Anno 1711”. It is addressed to a trusted envoy on his way to Holland. Cipher and clear alternate within the lines, and the clerk wrote the decipherment above every ciphered stretch. Rákóczi sends a copy of the “Tractatus sic dictae Pacis Carolianae”, the Peace of Szatmár, which he calls a fraud worked out in Vienna. He will not recognise a king proclaimed in the counties outside a free diet, contrary to the 1687 article of Pozsony:

nos Regem extra dietam promulgatum, non deposito [juramento], agnoscere non posse sine … ruina [libertatum nostrarum].

He appeals to the Tsar and the Polish Republic to mediate. He offers to come to a free diet and recognise a lawfully crowned king if the confederates recover their estates. He calls the Allies' assurances a “diversio armorum” and asks for the war to go on. He also reports Pálffy's cannon before “arcem nostram Munkatsiensem”, the confederation in Poland and the Muscovite advance. A clear postscript concerns mathematical instruments ordered from Mangold.

The key. Each cipher line was aligned with the Latin above it by hard EM, with each code taking up to four letters. The result is a syllabic cipher with homophones. Single letters have several values (190 and 430 s; 140 and 380 n; 60 and 300 e; 20 and 260 a), open syllables have their own codes (257 re, 428 ti, 68 se, 362 de, 96 no, 457 ri, 92 ce, 152 co), and a few words and names are coded whole. Of 224 codes, 156 occur at least twice. Decoding the letter with this key reproduces 70% of the clerk's letters. Where the clerk's handwriting is doubtful, the cipher usually decides the reading.

06 What remains uncertain

  • R912 and R922: 61 groups unread. They are line-opening fillers, R912's closing block of small figures (probably a date and a name), and values outside the table: 93 (×3), 89 (×3), 9 (×2). 173/174 = de and 561/562 = nt are inferred from use (C).
  • The year 1707 for R912 and R922 rests on content (C). R852's 1706 is written on the page.
  • Writer: unsigned. R852 and R922 come from Danzig, and R852 names Groffey in the third person (M).
  • R852's variant word codes have not been tabulated. Its clear copy gives the text.
  • R483: 68 of the rebuilt codes occur only once (C). A line-by-line edited Latin text has not been made yet.
