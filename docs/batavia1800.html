Smissaert to Prediger, 20 November 1800

Go to the full text ↓

A Dutch letter to Batavia with its sensitive words in cipher lies in the archive beside its own clear minute; the key was rebuilt from that minute and passed a held-out test.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 9 November 2026

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: complete · On the map

With Brice Dudout (transcription) · Also credited · Contemporary key or decipherment (earlier solution)

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · complete

The letter
J. C. Smissaert, secretary of the Raad der Aziatische Bezittingen (the Dutch Asiatic Council), to C. J. Prediger, then at Batavia, 20 November 1800, “Geacht vriend”: 14 lines carry cipher, 60 cipher words in all.
Where
Nationaal Archief, The Hague, 2.01.27.02 (Raad der Aziatische Bezittingen en Etablissementen), inv. 209, scan 0002 (right-hand page); the clear minute is on scans 0001, 0002 (left) and 0004. Not on DECODE and not in the catalogue.
The cipher
A simple substitution of letters for Dutch. Each letter is a column of two digits, a top digit 1–7 over a bottom digit 1–4. Only the sensitive words are enciphered, and word division is kept.
How the key was found
Under a protocol Brice Dudout fixed in advance, his AI agents aligned lines 1 to 12 with the clear minute, built the key by script and froze it, then measured it once on lines 13 and 14: 0.933 of the characters against at best 0.433 for 200 shuffled keys. The project did not repeat the test and did not see the images.
What it says
Besides the long-delayed shipment of troops to the Indies, the Council has been authorised to prepare a second expedition to Batavia from another place in Europe; Prediger is to tell the Governor-General in secrecy. The content is the minute’s, not new.
Still open
The rest of the minute and the postscript are not transcribed; one AI reader, no human check of the digit columns; q is not seen and f rests on one word.
Credit
The transcription, the key and its test are Brice Dudout’s (L’Europe chiffrée, with Claude Opus 5.5 agents). The clear text is the minute of 1800.

01 The letter and its minute

Inventory number 209 of the archive of the Raad der Aziatische Bezittingen en Etablissementen (Nationaal Archief, The Hague, 2.01.27.02) has five scans. The archive’s description already names the encipherment (“overbrenging in cijfer”) together with the minute. The contributor, Brice Dudout (L’Europe chiffrée, with Claude Opus 5.5 agents), reports finding the file in his own sweep of the Nationaal Archief on 2 November 2026 and worked on it on 9 November 2026. The project’s first sweep of the Nationaal Archief (23 October 2026, research/nationaal_archief) had already listed the item from the inventory, without working on it. The inventory’s title describes a secret missive written by order of the Council to C. J. Prediger, councillor of the Indies at Batavia, by the Council’s secretary, Mr. J. C. Smissaert, with the encipherment attached and a later postscript added to a triplicate sent on 20 April 1801.

There is no cipher in the file without its clear text. The content has always been readable in the minute, and nothing here is claimed as a new text. What the contributor adds is the key as a table, and the check that the cipher page matches the underlined passages of the minute, which the inventory already files together. He found no printed edition of the letter and no study of the cipher (two web searches), and no entry for it in this project’s catalogue, leads, targets, issues or pull requests.

No image is shown on this page. The inventory record offers the scans for download but states no licence, so the contributor included none and the project has not reproduced any; the first words of the held-out passage are printed as digits in the strip under the title.

02 The key and its test

Each cipher letter is written as a column of two digits, one above the other. The top digit runs from 1 to 7 and the bottom digit from 1 to 4, so the alphabet fits a grid of seven rows and four columns. The transcription writes a column as top digit then bottom digit (61 is 6 over 1). The letter has 375 such columns.

top \ bottom1234
1hr–l
2vmi/ju
3yzcp
4tksa
5bgof
6edwx
7n–––

The contributor wrote his protocol and hashed it after the cipher was transcribed and before the clear minute was opened. The learning part is lines 1 to 12, aligned word by word with the minute. The key was built by script as the majority letter for each code: 23 codes, with no conflict. It was frozen before the clear text of the held-out lines was opened. The held-out part is lines 13 and 14, 60 letters (“Heer Gouverneur Generaal zulks in secretesse gelieft te communiceren”), hashed before learning.

One measurement was made. The frozen key gives 0.933 of the characters of the minute’s text for that passage, against at best 0.433 for 200 shuffled keys (a margin of 0.500), with a value in the key for 0.983 of the held-out columns. The thresholds, 0.85 for the score and 0.30 for the margin, were fixed in advance, so the key counts as validated by the contributor’s protocol. The differences that remain are the encipherer’s 42 (k) written for s in “secretesse” and the code 54, which the learning part never shows. 54 = f was added to the key after the measurement, from the held-out word “gelieft” only.

The contributor declared two reservations before the freeze. Two copying slips in the learning part were corrected from the raw notes taken before the clear text was opened; the held-out lines were not changed. And the crop of scan 0001 used for learning also showed the next two lines of the minute, where the beginning of the held-out clear text was seen. The test is therefore not blind on those words. It stays mechanical: the key comes by script from the learning alignments only, with no value added by hand.

This is the contributor’s test, re-run by him from the frozen files on 9 November 2026 with the same figures. The project did not repeat it and did not see the images.

03 The reading

Of the 60 cipher words the contributor counts 54 as certain, 3 as probable and 3 as restored, with none unresolved: 57 of 60 (0.95) with the restorations left out, 60 of 60 with them. All six doubtful words are confirmed by the minute. In the text below the cipher passages are in italics; {x} is a probable word and [x] a restored letter.

Geacht vriend! Behalven de U bekende en vóór uw vertrek reeds geprojecteerde verzending van een corps troepen voor Indie, welke door veele tegenspoeden tot hiertoe opgehouden is geweest, maar evenwel, zoo wij hopen, eerlang voortgang zal hebben, is de Asiatische Raad geautoriseerd en reeds bezig met de arrangementen tot eene {andere} [s]oortgelyke expeditie te vertrekken naar Batavia uit eenen anderen oort van Eur[o]pa, waardoor, indien deselve reusseerd, eene aanzienlyke ver[ste]rking der militaire magt voor Java zal worden aangebragt. Ik ben gelast U daar van deze onderhandsche en voorlopige kennis te geven, met verzoek, dat Gij den Heer Gouverneur Generaal zulks in {secretesse} {gelieft} te communiceren tot deszelfs vertrek. Mijne bezigheden laten mij niet toe meer …

In the contributor’s English translation: “Esteemed friend! Besides the sending of a corps of troops to the Indies, known to you and already planned before your departure, which through many setbacks has been held up until now but which, as we hope, will nevertheless go forward before long, the Asiatic Council is authorised, and already busy with the arrangements for another, similar expedition to leave for Batavia from another place in Europe, by which, if it succeeds, a considerable reinforcement of the military force for Java will be brought. I am instructed to give you private and preliminary notice of this, with the request that you be pleased to communicate it in secrecy to the Governor-General until his departure. My occupations do not allow me more …”

04 What remains uncertain

The contributor grades words as certain, probable or restored; the grades below are his.

  • Probable: {secretesse}, enciphered k-e-c-r-e-t-e-k-k-e, with 42 (k) written for s three times, a slip of the encipherer.
  • Probable: {gelieft}, the only occurrence of 54 = f.
  • Probable: {andere}, which is followed by a stray 44 (a).
  • Restored: Eur[o]pa, the top digit of the o is illegible; [s]oortgelyke, the first column is struck out; ver[ste]rking, where the encipherer wrote “verking” and left out three letters.
  • The key has no value for q, which was not seen in the letter; cells 13, 72, 73 and 74 of the grid are empty.
  • The clear minute is transcribed only where it pairs with the cipher and for the letter’s opening. The rest of the minute and the postscript (scans 0004 and 0005) are not transcribed; this was not attempted.
  • One reader, an AI agent, read the digit columns. No human palaeographer checked them, and the project did not see the images.
  • The held-out test is not blind on the first words of its passage, as declared above.

The contributor also looked for other cipher near this one: inv. 209 holds no other, and the 1811 Janssens despatches (Nationaal Archief 2.01.27.05 inv. 12) use another system, a numeric code in French.
