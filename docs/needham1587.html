Before Sluys → Walsingham · three-grid pigpen inside clear English · 28 July 1587

Francis Needham to Walsingham before Sluys, 28 July 1587

British Library, Harley MS 287 ff. 39–40 (DECODE R8479): Walsingham’s servant with Leicester’s relief fleet writes of the failure to relieve Sluys. The cipher is a pigpen alphabet. The long cipher passages of f. 39v had no decipherment. The key was recovered from a handful of words glossed at the time on the same pages and from the alphabetical order of the grids, and every run was deciphered; one word stays uncertain. The key is said to survive in BL Add MS 5935, which was not consulted.

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · complete

The letter
Francis Needham, Walsingham’s servant, to Walsingham before Sluys, 28 July 1587, written on Leicester’s expedition to relieve the town.
Where
British Library, Harley MS 287 ff. 39–40; DECODE R8479, catalogued as a partially decrypted letter of “Needham” to an unknown recipient in an unknown cipher of graphic signs.
The cipher
A pigpen of nine box shapes in three forms (plain, dot below, dot inside) carrying the alphabet a–i, k–s, t–z in order.
How it was read
A contemporary hand glossed a few cipher words on f. 39r. The glosses advise, enemyes, campe nor the towne and Burgrave gave half the alphabet, and the order of the grids gave the rest.
What it says
The besiegers had no intelligence of the enemy, did not know whether there was a bridge, and found the channel closed with hoys and flyboats fastened with chains.
Still open
One word of about six letters on f. 39v is unsure.
Credit
The clear text follows the same-day despatch calendared in CSP Foreign 1587 (pp. 206–8).

01 The letter

Harley MS 287 is a volume of Walsingham’s correspondence (the Cobham letters of 1588 on ff. 70–72 are deciphered in another write-up). Folios 39–40 are one letter: f. 39r opens “It may please your honour. The sudden departure of the bark … before Sluce, was the cause that I was forced to break off”, f. 39v continues, f. 40r ends it with the date 1587, and the address leaf f. 40v is endorsed “28 July 1587. From Needham”.

CSP Foreign vol. 21 pt 3 (1929) calendars Needham’s letter to Walsingham of the same day from Flushing (SP 84/16 f. 216): the council aboard Leicester’s ship before the haven of Sluys, the firework ship that could hardly be brought up to the bridge, the States’ captains who would not lend boats because “this commission must come from the States”, and the fall of the town. The Harley letter tells the same story in more words; the calendar does not list it. A note in the same volume (p. 237) says Needham wrote to Walsingham in the cipher of Thomas Wilkes, whose key is at the front of BL Add MS 5935 (p. 35 n. 4). That manuscript is not online and was not used here.

02 The cipher

Nine box shapes, each written plain, with a dot beneath, or with a dot inside its angle. Taken in order, the shapes carry the alphabet straight through three grids:

⌟akt
⊔blu, v
⌞cmw
⊏dnx
□eoy
⌜fpz
⊓gq—
⌝hr—
⊐is—

Words are divided and spelled as in the clear text (wyth, chaynes, bootes). No homophones, nulls or code names occur on f. 39v. The dots are the whole difficulty: a dot below sits under the lower left of its sign, the script slants to the right, and a dot inside ⌟ lies in its upper left, so the first transcription gave several t’s as k’s until the grid order showed the error.

03 The decipherment of f. 39v

Cipher in italics; clear words around it paraphrased where the clear text was not transcribed in full.

  • “… we had want of intelligence these five days last past … [no] advise out of the enemyes campe nor the towne” (the last four words glossed at the time).
  • “and we were so far from knowing what was meet to be done at that time [that] we knewe not whether there was any bridge or not, or whether there were any at the chanel.”
  • “… being above 100 of their files into the town walls, and that the chanel was b[…]ed with hoyes and flyboates fastened wyth chaynes, strengthned behind wyth flatt bootes.”

This is the “bridge” of the calendared despatch: Parma had closed the haven of Sluys with a boom of boats chained together, and the English and Dutch could neither see it nor learn what it was. The rest of f. 39v, in clear, covers Sir Roger Williams’s sallies, the storm, the state of the town, Count Maurice, Colonel Morgan and Sir William Russell; the three words glossed near its foot (“Mr Burley”, “the Spaniard”, “were removed hence”) were deciphered at the time.

04 Method

The four images were fetched from DECODE. The glossed words gave letters by alignment: advise = ⌟ ⊏ ⊔: ⊐ ⊐̣ □, enemyes = □ ⊏̣ □ ⌞̣ □: □ ⊐̣, the towne = ⌟: ⌝ □ ⌟: □̣ ⌞: ⊏̣ □, and Burgrave on f. 40r began ⊔ ⊔̣ ⌝̣ ⊓. The first bare line then gave want of intelligence, and once a, b, c, d, e, h, i and k–s, t–z were in hand the order was plainly alphabetical, which fixed f, g, p and q and corrected the mistranscribed dots. A solver (simulated annealing on quadgram statistics) was prepared in case the key could not be completed, and was not needed. The transcription is in targets/needham1587/cipher.txt and the key in decode.py.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Line G, the verb after “the chanel was”: ⊔ then four cramped signs (one blotted), then □ ⊏, so b ···· ed. Sense: closed or blocked. Grade M.
  • All other cipher words: grade H (every sign decrypted with the key; they spell English in the letter’s own spelling).
  • The key has not been checked against the Wilkes key in Add MS 5935.
  • The clear text of ff. 39r–40r is summarised, not transcribed.

Needham to Walsingham, 28 July 1587, f. 39v cipher runs

Francis Needham, Walsingham's servant with Leicester at the relief of Sluys, reports that they had no intelligence out of the enemy's camp or the town for five days, did not know whether there was any bridge or anything at the channel, and that the channel was closed with hoys and flyboats fastened with chains and strengthened behind with flat boats. The f. 39r and f. 40r cipher words were glossed at the time; the clear text of the letter is not transcribed here.

# Copied verbatim from NOTES.md, section 'Reading of f. 39v' (22 Oct 2026): the cipher runs in their clear context.
## Reading of f. 39v (cipher in italics; clear text paraphrased where not transcribed)

- **A**: "... we had *want of intelligence* these five days last past ..." — the gloss "advise" above the next
  cipher word: "we could have no *advise* out of the *enemyes campe nor the towne*".
- **C–D**: "and we were so far from knowing what was meet to be done at that time [that] we *knewe not whether
  there was any bridge or not, or whether there were any at the chanel*".
- **F–G–H**: "... *and that the chanel was b[…]ed with hoyes and flyboates fastened wyth chaynes, strengthned
  behind wyth flatt bootes*."

The rest of f. 39v is clear: Sir Roger Williams' sally, the storm, the enemy's hundred files at the walls, the
state of the town, Count Maurice, Colonel Morgan and Russell; the lower glosses ("Mr Burley", "the Spaniard",
"were removed hence") were read at the time.

The reading agrees with the calendared despatch of the same day, which says the fireship could hardly be brought
up to "the bridge" across the haven: Parma had closed the channel with a boom of boats.
