Rome → Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, Mantua · homophonic substitution, Latin · 22 April 1428

Pandolfo Malatesta to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, 22 April 1428

Go to the full text ↓

The archbishop of Patras hid in cipher his advice to his brother-in-law to keep his soldiers together and to make himself king of the Morea; a reader outside the project found the key, and this page checks it.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026 · updated 5 November 2026

At a glanceExternal key matched · complete

The letter
Pandolfo Malatesta, archbishop of Patras and brother of Gianfrancesco’s wife Paola, to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, lord of Mantua, Rome, 22 April 1428. Clear Latin with three cipher runs of 66, 82 and 160 signs.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Mantova, Archivio Gonzaga. DECODE R1854, image 1; the record was catalogued as one letter of unknown sender and holds four letters in four ciphers.
The cipher
A homophonic substitution of Latin letters: three signs each for a, e, i, o and u/v, two for r, one for most consonants, two null signs, no word division. The marks first taken for word separators are cipher letters.
How it was read
Mark Vladimir Prizmic, working with Claude from Satoshi Tomokiyo’s transcription, built the key and the reading and corrected five signs on the image. Here his key decodes his corrected transcription to his text, and the decode scores far above shuffled keys in Latin. This project had tried the letter in September and October and failed.
What it says
Three days after the peace of Ferrara, Pandolfo asks Gonzaga not to discharge his men-at-arms, and proposes that he be made “rex” of the Morea, “without risk and without great expense”, citing Count Riciardo, who went there with their sister Cleofe, the Despot’s wife.
Still open
One sign (23, once). The Count Riciardo is not identified.
Credit
Key and reading: Mark Vladimir Prizmic (with Claude), preprint of 5 November 2026. Transcription: Satoshi Tomokiyo.

This letter is image 1 of DECODE R1854, which holds four letters to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga in four ciphers; the other three have their own pages: a Gonzaga agent in Rome, 9 August 1428, an informant at Forlì, 6 November 1428 and Francesco de’ Cattanei, February 1430, in which Pandolfo, as archbishop of Patras, appears offering his see’s lordship to Venice.

01 The letter

The letter opens in clear with the news, just arrived in Rome, of the peace between the duke of Milan and the league (the peace of Ferrara, 19 April 1428), “licet nondum publicatam”. Pandolfo writes that he has conceived plans “valde utilia” for his brother-in-law, and the secret parts follow in three runs: after “decrevi ne”, after “quo modo valeat” and after “nolit rem ipsam negligere”. It closes with greetings to Gonzaga’s wife, Pandolfo’s sister. The scan on DECODE carries a notice that the Archivio di Stato di Mantova allows publication only with its permission, so no image of it is reproduced here; the transcription is.

02 The reading

Cipher in bold, spelling as the scribe wrote it (north-Italian x, single l); letters the scribe left out are in parentheses.

Qua de re ipsam premissa rogare ac simul decrevi ne de suis gentibus armigeris cas(s)andis vel quid aliud peragendum [·] delliberet donec sciam; alioquin non possim … Nam sibi tue prepositum(?) me sciat quo modo valeat absque dis(c)rimine et multo dispendio provincie Moree, patrie siquidem fertilis et amene, rex efici; precor itaque prefatam amplitudinem Vestram, cum se fratrem(?) paratam quoque reperiat posse sibi et natis amplitudinem tantam vendicare, nolit rem ipsam negligere. De eo autem regno magnificum c(o)mitem Riciardum bene informatum puto, quoniam ad illud cum serenissima sorore nostra domina vaxilissa tetendit, et iterum deinde etiam sui ocaxione redivit. Recommendo me prefate amplitudini Vestre …

About this, having made the request above, I decided at the same time that he should not decide about discharging his men-at-arms, or about anything else to be done, until I know … let him know, through me, how he could, without risk and without great expense, be made king of the province of the Morea, a country fertile and pleasant indeed; I therefore beg the said Highness, since he finds me, his brother, ready, and that he can claim so great a dignity for himself and his sons, not to neglect the matter. About that kingdom, I believe the magnificent Count Riciardo to be well informed, since he went there with our most serene sister, the lady basilissa, and afterwards went back there again on her account.

The “serenissima soror nostra domina vaxilissa” is, on Prizmic’s reading, Cleofe Malatesta, Pandolfo’s and Paola’s sister, who married Theodore II Palaiologos, Despot of the Morea, in 1421. The timing explains the first run: with the peace made, Gonzaga, a condottiere in the league’s service, would be expected to stand down his companies. Pandolfo also appears in Cattanei’s letters of February 1430 (R1854, images 4–5) offering his see’s lordship to Venice. The complete letter, with the clear parts and the runs together, is under Full texts below.

03 The cipher

A homophonic substitution of Latin letters: one sign for most consonants, two for r, three for each of a, e, i, o and u/v, two nulls, no word division and no code groups. The sign 49 stands for x in rex, vaxilissa and ocaxione. The signs are named by Tomokiyo’s labels; the three marks first taken for separators are letters (the double slash is i, the three dots a).

04 How it was deciphered, and the checks

How. Prizmic took Tomokiyo’s transcription and the values implied by his CTTS trial as a starting key, ran a homophonic annealing solver scored on a Latin four-gram model with a letter-frequency penalty, and read the result by hand; words such as magnificum, serenissima and basilissa fixed several values at once and showed two signs to be nulls. He then checked every value the reading needed on the DECODE image, which gave five corrections to the transcription (two signs transcribed 30 are the double-c sign, d; two H signs and one mislabelled sign are r, r and o; and the final cc of run 1 is not a cipher sign). Tomokiyo accepted them on 5 November. His preprint is doi 10.5281/zenodo.23969170; it says that Claude was used for the solving and drafting.

Why this project missed it. Our transcription had 262 signs; the corrected one has 308. The difference is the marks we had set aside as word separators, which are cipher letters. On 5 October we kept five kinds of separator as extra signs and still found nothing, and our annealers, working from the 262-sign transcription, never produced words. We did not find the answer; it reached us.

Checks here (5 November 2026), using the key and the corrected transcription he sent:

  • Re-decoding. dec.py applies the key to the corrected transcription: all 308 signs have a value except the unread 23, and the three decoded runs agree letter for letter with his reading, with 66, 82 and 160 signs.
  • A control. Under the Latin model la the decode scores −5.25 per letter, against −6.90 for the best of 300 keys of the same shape with the letters shuffled among the sign classes (mean −7.88); the language check names Latin ahead of every other language.
  • The clear text. The reading fits the Latin round each run: run 2 follows “me sciat quo modo valeat” and leads to “amplitudinem tantam vendicare … sibi et natis”; “ne … delliberet” takes “amplitudo Vestra” as its subject, in the third person like the rest of the letter. The clear text is this project’s transcription; the preprint does not say whether Prizmic used it as cribs, so the fit is evidence of sense, not an independent test.
  • Our own transcription. The beginning and the end of run 1 (cc ss Z K bz Z and cc ss g cc ss 8 vb 8 bz x n H y I in our labels) read d-e-s-u-i-s and d-e-l-l-i-b-e-r-e-t with one sign each for the repeated letters, as the key says.

These checks do not make the reading independent: the key was built together with the reading, and the control shuffles a key already fitted to this text. What carries the reading is that 308 signs of 33 kinds decode to whole Latin clauses that agree with the clear text round them, and that Prizmic checked each value on the image and Tomokiyo accepted the corrections.

05 What remains open

  • Sign 23, once, before delliberet: the sentence is complete without it, so it may be a null.
  • Count Riciardo, who went to the Morea with Cleofe and returned for her, is not identified, and whether Gonzaga was ever offered the Morea is not established.
  • The clear text round runs 1 and 2 rests on the DECODE image and this project’s transcription (“prepositum(?)”, “fratrem(?)”, “sensu(?)” stay doubtful); a better image would settle them.
  • No contemporary decipherment has been looked for in the Archivio Gonzaga. Falcioni’s 2004 note on Malatesta chancery ciphers was not seen. Pandolfo’s ciphered letter of 1 March 1438 (ASMn, AG, b. 1081, c. 159–160, with the contemporary translation in b. 840, c. 95) may use the same cipher; this key could be tried on it if its cipher is ever imaged.

# Pandolfo Malatesta to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, Rome, 22 April 1428: English translation

Magnificent and mighty lord, and most honourable dearest brother. News has just been brought here that peace has been
concluded between the illustrious ducal dominion and the lord duke [and the league(?)], though it is not yet published.
Since then I have conceived and thought out several things that are very useful for us, by which Your Highness could
advance your own position and ours not a little. About this, having made the request above, I have decided at the same
time that **[RUN 1] he should not decide about discharging his men-at-arms, or about anything else to be done, until I
know**; otherwise I could not, which by God's favour I hope will be within twenty days if the news of the peace is
confirmed. For let him know, through me, **[RUN 2] how he could, without risk and without great expense, be made king of
the province of the Morea, a country fertile and pleasant indeed.** I therefore beg the said Highness, since he finds me,
his brother, ready, and that he can claim so great a dignity for himself and his sons, not to neglect the matter.
**[RUN 3] About that kingdom, I believe the magnificent Count Riciardo to be well informed, since he went there with our
most serene sister, the lady basilissa, and afterwards went back there again on her account.** I commend myself to the
said Highness, whom I wish well from the heart, with your magnificent consort, my honoured sister, and your sons.

Pandolfo de' Malatesti, archbishop of Patras, Rome, 22 April 1428.

Notes: "ne ... delliberet" takes "amplitudo Vestra" as its subject, in the third person like the rest of the letter. The
clear transcription marks "prepositum(?)", "fratrem(?)", "sensu(?)" and the last line as doubtful, and so does this
translation. "Basilissa" is Cleofe Malatesta, Pandolfo's and Paola's sister, who married Theodore II Palaiologos, Despot
of the Morea, in 1421.
