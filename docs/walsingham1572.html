a Gentleman of [9] departed hence to  2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4  wth intention to imbarke there

Paris → London · digits and signs in a clear letter · 20 January 1572/3

Walsingham to Burghley, 20 January 1572/3

→

British Library, Harley MS 260 ff. 398–399 (DECODE R8363), a later copy in Walsingham’s Paris letter-book. DECODE lists it as “Frauncis Walsingham? to …, 21 Jan 1572, short parts encrypted only”. The images show a clear letter to the Lord Treasurer with a single ciphered place name. The group was transcribed but not deciphered: no key survives, and with the one group in Burghley’s reply it is too short for cryptanalysis. No earlier decipherment is known.

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The document
Sir Francis Walsingham to Lord Burghley, Paris, 20 January 1573 (dated 1572 old style), a fair copy in an embassy letter-book. It is clear English except one passage.
Where
British Library, Harley MS 260 ff. 398–399, DECODE R8363. Dudley Digges printed it in The Compleat Ambassador (1655, p. 313).
The cipher
Six symbols, 2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4, naming the port a gentleman left for. The bracketed [9] is only the copyist’s mark for a name he left out.
How it was tried
Digges prints the group as cipher with no decipherment. No Walsingham–Burghley key is on DECODE, and the 1568 Cecil–Norris table gives nonsense.
Why it resists
With one more group in R8364 there are only twelve symbols. Dieppe, Calais, Nantes and other ports all fit.
Still open
The port is not deciphered, and the name behind [9] was never copied into the book.

01 The record

Harley MS 260 is a fair-copy letter-book of Walsingham’s embassy in France, 1570–73, the book Digges printed. DECODE R8363 has three images:

  • f. 398r, top: the end of a letter signed Frauncis Walsingham, “At Paris the 21th of Januarie 1572”. It covers the Cardinal of Lorraine bringing a dispensation from Rome for Monsieur to marry his niece, and Marshal Tavannes’s hope of recovery. CSP Foreign x calendars it between nos. 726 and 727 as “a copy of Mr. Secretary’s letter”. DECODE’s date and its blank recipient come from this tail.
  • f. 398r–399v: “To the right hono[ra]ble and my verie good Lord the Lord Treasurer”, dated 20 January 1572/3 and signed. It concerns the Vidame’s man, who has not delivered two letters and some books; “Steward” (Colonel Stewart), gone to La Rochelle, who sent “a fond cipher” that Walsingham found “not for the purpose”; letters from Davison he cannot forward safely; and Walsingham’s reluctance to carry on as “a distributer of letters” for young and inexperienced parties.

02 The cipher

The whole of the cipher is the last two lines of f. 398r:

The matter of most importance conteyned in the same was touchinge a Gentleman of [9] departed hence to 2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4 wth intention to imbarke there yf the barke were not departed.

The copyist writes [9], a bracketed flourish, wherever the original had a cipher name he did not copy. It stands for other names in R8362 (“longeth to heare from Lyons [9]”) and R8364, and Digges prints it as “[7]” or “[s]”. The six symbols 2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4 were copied, and Digges set them as “2 ) ⌘ 6·) 4”. Burghley’s answer in the same book (f. 419r, DECODE R8364, February 1572/3) has one group in the same system: “a Shipp sent to 2 0 ⌘ H 6 1 tt ymagined that Ball there had a meaninge to provoke Glasco [9] to come hither”. Both groups start with 2 and have ⌘ third.

03 What was tried

  • Print. Digges 1655 pp. 312–313: the cipher printed as cipher. CSP Foreign x, January 1573: no. 725 (Walsingham to Burghley, 20 Jan) opens with the Vidame’s servant but goes on to a book defending the Queen of Scots and to Dr Dale, and does not include the passage. The signed original in SP 70 was not seen.
  • Keys. DECODE has no key record from Harley 260 and no Walsingham key before 1577. Tomokiyo’s survey of Elizabethan ciphers has none for Walsingham’s Paris embassy. The Cecil–Norris table of 1567–68 (see norreys1567) turns the group into “r a ? ? i p”.
  • Siblings. The nine Harley 260 records on DECODE (R8356–R8364) were fetched. The two from the same weeks were examined: R8362 has placeholders only, and R8364 has the one group above.
  • Ciphertext only. Twelve symbols of nine kinds. Under a homophonic alphabet of digits and signs, Dieppe, Calais, Nantes, Brest(e) and Rouen(s) can each be made to fit both groups. A gentleman leaving Paris to catch a ship points to a Channel port, Dieppe likeliest, but that is a guess (grade I).

04 What remains open

  • The port “2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4” is not deciphered. It is too short, and no key survives.
  • The name behind “[9]” was never copied into the book. Only Walsingham’s signed original in SP 70, if it survives, could show it.
