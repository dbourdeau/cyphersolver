Paris → London · digits and signs in a clear letter · 20 January 1572/3 · attempted, not read

Walsingham to Burghley, 20 January 1572/3

→

British Library, Harley MS 260 ff. 398–399 (DECODE R8363), a later copy in Walsingham’s Paris letter-book. DECODE lists it as “Frauncis Walsingham? to …, 21 Jan 1572, short parts encrypted only”. The images show a clear letter to the Lord Treasurer with a single ciphered place name.

Summary. The record holds the end of Walsingham’s letter of 21 January and all of a second letter, to Burghley, dated “At Paris the xxth of Januarie 1572”, that is 20 January 1573 in the new-style year. The second letter is in clear English except for one passage: “a Gentleman of [9] departed hence to 2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4 with intention to imbarke there yf the barke were not departed”. The bracketed [9] is a mark the copyist uses wherever he left a cipher name out, so it carries nothing to read. The six signs are a port. Dudley Digges printed the letter from this book in The Compleat Ambassador (1655, p. 313) and set the group in cipher type, unread. The Calendar of State Papers Foreign summarises a letter of the same day (no. 725) that opens with the same matter but has no cipher passage. No Walsingham–Burghley key of 1572–73 is on DECODE or in Tomokiyo’s survey, and the Cecil–Norris table of 1568 gives nonsense. One more group in the same system, in Burghley’s reply (R8364), gives twelve signs in all. That is too few: Dieppe, Calais, Nantes and other six-letter ports all fit. The target is closed unread.

01 The record

Harley MS 260 is a fair-copy letter-book of Walsingham’s embassy in France, 1570–73, the book Digges printed. DECODE R8363 has three images:

  • f. 398r, top: the end of a letter signed Frauncis Walsingham, “At Paris the 21th of Januarie 1572”. It covers the Cardinal of Lorraine bringing a dispensation from Rome for Monsieur to marry his niece, and Marshal Tavannes’s hope of recovery. CSP Foreign x calendars it between nos. 726 and 727 as “a copy of Mr. Secretary’s letter”. DECODE’s date and its blank recipient come from this tail.
  • f. 398r–399v: “To the right hono[ra]ble and my verie good Lord the Lord Treasurer”, dated 20 January 1572/3 and signed. It concerns the Vidame’s man, who has not delivered two letters and some books; “Steward” (Colonel Stewart), gone to La Rochelle, who sent “a fond cipher” that Walsingham found “not for the purpose”; letters from Davison he cannot forward safely; and Walsingham’s reluctance to carry on as “a distributer of letters” for young and inexperienced parties.

02 The cipher

The whole of the cipher is the last two lines of f. 398r:

The matter of most importance conteyned in the same was touchinge a Gentleman of [9] departed hence to 2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4 wth intention to imbarke there yf the barke were not departed.

The copyist writes [9], a bracketed flourish, wherever the original had a cipher name he did not copy. It stands for other names in R8362 (“longeth to heare from Lyons [9]”) and R8364, and Digges prints it as “[7]” or “[s]”. The six signs 2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4 were copied, and Digges set them as “2 ) ⌘ 6·) 4”. Burghley’s answer in the same book (f. 419r, DECODE R8364, February 1572/3) has one group in the same system: “a Shipp sent to 2 0 ⌘ H 6 1 tt ymagined that Ball there had a meaninge to provoke Glasco [9] to come hither”. Both groups start with 2 and have ⌘ third.

03 What was tried

  • Print. Digges 1655 pp. 312–313: the cipher printed as cipher. CSP Foreign x, January 1573: no. 725 (Walsingham to Burghley, 20 Jan) opens with the Vidame’s servant but goes on to a book defending the Queen of Scots and to Dr Dale, and does not include the passage. The signed original in SP 70 was not seen.
  • Keys. DECODE has no key record from Harley 260 and no Walsingham key before 1577. Tomokiyo’s survey of Elizabethan ciphers has none for Walsingham’s Paris embassy. The Cecil–Norris table of 1567–68 (see norreys1567) turns the group into “r a ? ? i p”.
  • Siblings. The nine Harley 260 records on DECODE (R8356–R8364) were fetched. The two from the same weeks were read: R8362 has placeholders only, and R8364 has the one group above.
  • Ciphertext only. Twelve signs of nine kinds. Under a homophonic alphabet of digits and signs, Dieppe, Calais, Nantes, Brest(e) and Rouen(s) can each be made to fit both groups. A gentleman leaving Paris to catch a ship points to a Channel port, Dieppe likeliest, but that is a guess (grade I), not a reading.

04 What remains open

  • The port “2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4” is not read. It is too short, and no key survives.
  • The name behind “[9]” was never copied into the book. Only Walsingham’s signed original in SP 70, if it survives, could show it.
