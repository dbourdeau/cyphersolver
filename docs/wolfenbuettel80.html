The Wolfenbüttel alchemical manuscript

Go to the full text ↓

Beneath an alchemical recipe, a reader used numeral-like signs to record a judgment in German. Other annotations in the volume help identify the word for “way.”

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 22 November 2026 · updated 26 November 2026

Method: ciphertext-only break · Extent: partial (92% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count)

On this pageThe four linesRecovering the keyThe word for “way”Full textsSources

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · partial (92% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count)

The document
Four lines on folio 103v. The host manuscript dates to the fifteenth century. The added note is undated.
Where
Herzog August Bibliothek, Wolfenbüttel, Cod. Guelf. 80.4 Aug. 8°.
The cipher
Distinctive strokes on numeral-like signs change their letter values. The last word continues on the fourth line.
How it was deciphered
A search for German letter patterns recovered part of the substitution. Repeated signs in the manuscript supplied checks.
Still open
Four sign readings are provisional, including the last two consonants in the word for “way.” Partial: 8 of 11 words come out of the key with nothing supplied. The cipher groups of the separate recipe on f. 105r stay open as catalogue 3897.

01 The four lines

Mich dunct also, da[s] dises der rechte wec[gk] ist der philo[s]ophei.

“It seems to me, then, that this is the true way of philosophy.” The strict key reproduces 8 of 11 words exactly (72.7%). The other three contain the four bracketed letters, so they count as holes. Three holes are more than the two the short-text floor allows, so the note counts as deciphered in part, not in full. The passage is too short for the statistical validation test. Capitalization and punctuation are editorial.

02 Recovering the key

The search scored four-letter sequences against word lists in five languages. The German run produced also, dises and ist. The German syntax and the repeated three-sign group helped restore der rechte. On folio 105r the same values yield the Latin words sulphurem and salem, sulphur and salt. The closing leaves were visible during the search. Folio 105r.

03 The word for “way”

The strict key gives wec??. Its first sign is a dotted circle that also gives w in weisse and worden. A word for a coin on image 00155 supplies a possible spelling pfennigck. Applying those final consonant values on 103v gives wecgk. A Hessian place-name record has weckg in 1432, with the final consonants reversed. The comparison supports the sense “way” while leaving the exact spelling open. Coin passage · Hessian register.
