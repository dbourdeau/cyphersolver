Siena no. 8: the Milan–Ferrara league slip

A cipher slip on a league against Venice and a marriage offer for Bianca Maria Visconti, bound below a clear memorandum; aligned sign by sign, the memorandum turns out to be its decipherment.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 18 October 2026 · updated 23 October 2026

At a glanceExisting decipherment · complete

The document
A slip of six lines, clear cursive mixed with cipher, under a clear memorandum on the same sheet. Unsigned and undated; c. 1440–41 from its contents.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Siena, Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2 “Lettere in cifra”, no. 8; DECODE R4797 (Concistoro_2308_51), catalogued “1500–1599” with no status.
The cipher
A simple substitution in letter-like signs: two signs for a, one for s and ss, one for u, v and b, and two name signs, for the Duke of Milan and the Venetians.
How it was deciphered
The memorandum’s last three paragraphs open with the slip’s clear words. Aligned letter for letter with them, the four cipher runs give one consistent key, which reads every sign.
What it says
The articles of a league between the Duke of Milan and the Marquis of Ferrara to make war on Venice; the Duke “aveva dato madonna Biancha a missere Leonello”, the Marquis’s son.
Still open
The memorandum’s first four paragraphs decipher a covering letter that is not on the sheet. Writer and date are not given.
Credit
The contemporary decipherer of the memorandum. George Lasry’s report of 17 October 2026 listed no. 8 as not yet deciphered and called a link with the memorandum “possible but speculative”. This page puts the alignment to him.

The other pieces of the Sienese cipher file are listed by key on the Concistoro 2308 index. Nos. 7, 19 and 21 are different ciphers, with their own pages; none fits this key.

01The sheet and the disagreement

The upper two thirds of the sheet are a clear memorandum in seven paragraphs. Each paragraph opens with an underlined catchword, and the words after it run on as plain prose: the layout of a decipherment, where the clear words of the ciphered letter are copied and the cipher after them is written out. The first four paragraphs (the Marquis of Ferrara “rimesso nel duca di Milano a fare guerra e pace”, “di papa Felice”, Niccolò Piccinino, Lucca) belong to a letter that is not on this sheet. The fifth opens “Nel polizolo dicto segue”, “in the said slip there follows”, and the last two catchwords, Anchora disse and antiveduto questo, are clear words on the slip below.

The project’s inventory of 24 September 2026 took no. 8 as deciphered at the time, from the memorandum’s contents alone. George Lasry’s report of 17 October 2026 lists it as not yet deciphered, “c. 400 symbols”, with no interlinear plaintext, a relation to the memorandum “possible but speculative”. On 18 October the slip was transcribed sign by sign and aligned with the memorandum, and the alignment settles the question.

02The alignment

The memorandum’s last three paragraphs, transcribed here (catchwords in italics):

Nel polizolo dicto segue: [modo?] leggiere e capitagli e legha fral duca di Milano el marchese di Ferrara a fare guerra a Viniziani e sua legha. Anchora disse el duca di Milano aveva dato la donna Biancha a misser Leonello figlolo del marchese. Antiveduto questo e no puo manchare che questo udrete grandissimi facti.

The slip’s four cipher runs, deciphered with the key rebuilt from that text (in the cipher’s own spelling; names in brackets are single signs):

  1. le|giere e chapitoli e legha fra [duca di Milano] el marchese di ferrara a fa|re
  2. [guerra, faint, in clear] [Viniziani] e sua legha
  3. [duca di Milano] aveva dato madonna Biancha a missere Leonello filgliuolo de marchese
  4. e non puo manchare che questo udirete grandissimi fatti

“… to read the articles of the league between the Duke of Milan and the Marquis of Ferrara, to make war on the Venetians and their league. He also said that the Duke of Milan had given Madonna Bianca to messer Leonello, the Marquis’s son. This being foreseen, it cannot fail that you will hear of very great deeds.”

The pattern words leave no room for chance: 5 9 T 9 5 is aveva (the shape ABCBA), F T W W 2 W 2 is ferrara, L T Z PHI T L L Z is leonello; the sign 7 is d in dato, madonna, di, de and udirete, and the two a signs never stand for anything else. The cipher and the memorandum differ only in spelling (chapitoli / capitagli, madonna / la donna, fatti / facti).

The key is not among the filed keys compared by eye in September; the two keys of about 1440 that name the same persons, R4748 (“Cifra cum fratre Bernardino”) and R4749, have not yet been compared with it sign by sign.

04Date and context

The Marquis whose son is Leonello is Niccolò III d’Este, who died on 26 December 1441; “papa Felice” in the memorandum is the antipope Felix V, elected in November 1439; and Bianca Maria Visconti married Francesco Sforza in October 1441, so the report that her father had given her to Leonello belongs before that. The slip therefore dates from between November 1439 and October 1441 (most likely 1440–41). The writer, perhaps a Sienese envoy at Milan, is not named. This dating is proposed here, not written on the sheet.

05What remains

  • The covering letter whose cipher passages the memorandum’s first four paragraphs decipher: not on this sheet and not among the DECODE photographs of the busta.
  • Doubtful signs: a faint group in line 3, read as the clear word guerra; the end of the s sign in sua; the missing l of de(l); the p of puo; the q and u of questo, which occur only once. None changes the reading, which the memorandum gives.
  • George Lasry’s confirmation of the alignment, and whether he counts c. 400 symbols for the whole slip with its clear words (the cipher signs alone are 166).

Siena, Concistoro 2308 fasc. 2 no. 8 (DECODE R4797), c. 1440-41

A ciphered slip on the Milan-Ferrara league: the league between the Duke of Milan and the Marquis of Ferrara against the Venetians and their league, the Duke of Milan having given 'madonna Biancha' to messer Leonello, the Marquis's son, and 'grandissimi facti' to be expected. The clear memorandum on the same sheet (paragraphs 5-7) is its contemporary decipherment; the key rebuilt from the alignment reads all 166 signs.
