Benedetto Fantini, Buda and Eger, 1517–1518

Archivio di Stato di Modena, Ambasciatori, Ungheria b. 4, “Benedetto Fantini” nos. 5 and 6 (Vestigia 1849, 1851; DECODE R1126, R1127, where the writer appears as “Benedetto Jantini”). Two letters of a Ferrarese in Cardinal Ippolito d’Este’s household in Hungary, one with two full pages of cipher.

The cipher was transcribed column by column and attacked from the ciphertext alone, and with the only sibling key there is. It is not read.

Summary. The catalogue listed two DECODE records of “Benedetto Jantini”, Ferrara, 1517–18, “partially decrypted”. The public Vestigia database has the whole file under the right name, Benedetto Fantini, nos. 1–7 (1515–1518). Only nos. 5 and 6 carry cipher, and none of the seven has a decipherment, a clear copy or a gloss. DECODE’s “partially decrypted” reflects the clear passages. The cipher belongs to the family of Giuliano Caprile’s 1519 letters from the same household: a base stroke with a small sign above it, plus bracket-shaped signs. A pairing of Caprile’s letter of 1 May 1519 with its 1882 decipherment shows that family to be syllabic, with the small sign giving the consonant, the base the vowel, and the brackets whole syllables. Fantini’s two cipher pages (1,370 columns) were transcribed and attacked with a syllabary annealer. The same annealer recovers a control text of the same length in the same structure, even with a tenth of its signs corrupted. On Fantini it produces no Italian, and Caprile’s values make the fit far worse. Status: attempted, not read. It needs a key or a decipherment from the Este chancery, or a paired letter in Fantini’s own key.

01 The documents

DECODEASMo no.Date, placeCipher
R1126Fantini no. 5 (Vestigia 1849)[spring 1518?], Buda. Vestigia and DECODE give “1517”, the file yearp. 1 after six clear lines (18 lines) and all of p. 2 (25 lines): 1,370 columns; pp. 3–6 clear
R1127Fantini no. 6 (Vestigia 1851)6 March 1518, Eger (“Agria”)five short lines, about 46 columns, in a torn and faded leaf

The letters are addressed to a lord styled “Vostra Signoria Illustrissima” and “Vostra Eccellenza”, most likely Duke Alfonso I. The clear opening of no. 5 asks for news of the restitution of Modena and Reggio and of Lorenzo de’ Medici’s journey to France. The clear pages 3–6 report the Hungarian diet and the governors appointed around the young Louis II. They continue the letter; they are not a decipherment. Fantini’s other letters (nos. 1–4 and 7, 1515–1518) are in clear.

02 The cipher

Each unit is a base stroke written on the line (shapes like 7, m, n, g, y, 4, t, a) with a small sign written above it (o, 2, 1, b, &, c, L, s, d, 9 …), or an L-shaped bracket enclosing a small sign. The transcription (t1126.txt) counts 1,370 columns: 167 distinct forms, 299 of them brackets (22%) with 22 different inner signs. The commonest columns are 2/7 (77), -/7 (63), o/7 (60), -/y (47), -/g (46) and &/m (44).

Caprile’s letters of 1519 use the same kind of script. His letter of 1 May 1519 (DECODE R1130) mixes clear sentences with short cipher runs, and the 1882 archivist’s decipherment (Vestigia 1860, “4c”) covers them. Aligned by hand, the runs read as syllables. intertenere is seven columns, y/7 9/7 [y] c/7 [y] z/m [m]: i, n, te, r, te, ne, re. The same opening recurs in intertenerlo and in che termine. oratore is o [ra] [to] [re], twice. In that key base 7 carries no vowel, m gives e and n gives o. Tested on stretches not used to build it, the values placed 16 of 18 letters in the true text, against 8.1 on average for shuffled text.

03 What was tried

  • Siblings, clear pages, print. All seven Fantini letters on Vestigia. Bonzagni’s letters of 1512–14, the other Este–Hungary cipher, use a different, simple alphabet. DECODE has no key record from Modena for 1490–1540, and nothing turned up in print.
  • Caprile’s key. Three pairings with the 1882 decipherments. R1131 is water-stained, and its alignment was no better than shuffled controls. R1134’s supposed interlinear decipherment is a second letter in clear with cipher insertions. R1130 gave the syllabic structure and about twenty values.
  • A syllabary annealer (syl_anneal.py: consonant for each upper sign, vowel for each base, a syllable for each bracket, scored with a sixteenth-century Italian 5-gram model). On a control of 2,200 letters from sixteenth-century Italian, enciphered in the same structure with two homophones per consonant, it recovers the text almost word for word once three base values are fixed. It still does so with 10% of the signs corrupted.
  • On Fantini, eight restarts of 150,000 moves each, on the raw transcription and with the frequent pairs -/7 -/y and -/7 -/g merged as ligatures. The result is Italian-looking syllable salad that differs from restart to restart, scoring −2.37 per letter against −1.77 for the control’s true key. Seeding Caprile’s values drops the score from about −140 to −1,250.

Because the method works on the control at this length and at this level of transcription noise, the failure lies in the model or the key family, not in the search. Fantini’s key is not Caprile’s, and it may use another division of labour between base and upper signs.

04 What would finish it

  • A key or decipherment in the Este chancery (ASMo, Cancelleria, Cifrari), or a letter to or from Fantini with its decipherment.
  • A second, independent transcription of R1126. The present one has had a single reading, with a second pass over three lines only, and the pairing of small signs to bases is sometimes doubtful.
  • R1127 is too short and too damaged to attack on its own.
