Edward Wotton from Scotland to Walsingham, August–September 1585

→

British Library, Add MS 32657, ff. 167–168, 173–174 and 175–176 (DECODE R4841–R4843). Three despatches catalogued as numerical ciphers with nothing decrypted. They are the ambassador’s drafts in clear English, with small code numbers standing for persons and places. Read in full; the code numbers are identified from the drafts’ own context.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 21 September 2026

solved

Summary. The Unsolved Catalogue (item 81) listed three DECODE records of Edward Wotton, ambassador to Scotland, to Sir Francis Walsingham, 25 August to 1 September 1585, as “Non-decrypted, numerical, cipher type unknown”. The images show Wotton’s own drafts, headed “To Mr Secretary the 25th August”, “… the 30th of August 1585” and “… the first of September”, written in clear English. There is no letter cipher. The only concealment is a small nomenclator of one- and two-digit numbers written in place of names (“39. came to me yesterday”, “the enlargement of 19”), which the fair copies would have carried in cipher. So nothing needed breaking: the drafts read as they stand, and the work was to identify eleven code numbers. Three are certain from context (19 the Earl of Arran, 39 the Master of Gray, 10 King James VI), four are probable (9 England or the Queen, 20 Lord Maxwell, 24 the Earl of Huntly, 3 France), and four remain unidentified (27, 32, 36, 40). The despatches carry the plot that ended in the fall of Arran at Stirling in November 1585. Whether the Calendar of State Papers, Scotland vol. 8 already resolves these numbers was not checked: the calendar was not accessible.

01 The documents

Add MS 32657 is a volume of Walsingham’s Scottish correspondence (DECODE gives the span of the volume, 1547–1590, as the records’ date). DECODE imaged four pages each of three items:

  • R4841, ff. 167–168: “To Mr Secretary the 25th August”. The top of f. 167 carries the end of an earlier draft and jotted notes (“Maxwel”, “1000”, “Sterling”).
  • R4842, ff. 173–174: “To Mr Secretary the 30th of August 1585”.
  • R4843, ff. 175–176: “To Mr Secretary the first of September”.

All three are working drafts in Wotton’s hand, with deletions and interlinear additions. The code numbers are written inline, usually followed by a point, where a name would stand. The drafts also write “the K.” and “her Mtie” in clear in places, so the code was applied unevenly.

03 What the despatches say

25 August 1585 (R4841)

The King is hunting in a land of the Duke of Lennox. Gray (39) came to Wotton “yesterday”: although he had written that he would forbear to deal further until he heard that the Queen was satisfied with his doings, he judged delay dangerous. His discourse: he and the rest have run a wrong course “in seeking to disgrace 19 with 10”, whose love toward Arran is so extreme that he will not suffer a hair of his head to fall; as long as Arran continues in favour it is impossible to bring home the banished lords, and the league “cannot be sure”. Arran will be able to divert the King from England and toward France (3). To obviate this, the Queen should furnish the lords to take Arran and seize the King’s person. There are rumours that Arran and 24 are agreed, and that 24 may carry the King to France.

30 August 1585 (R4842)

Wotton answers Walsingham’s letter of the 21st about Gray’s complaint to “his friend in England”. Gray, 40 and 27 cannot live safely at court while Arran may return; 27 “was greatly hers and is a great favourer of the Hamiltons”. Arran still remains at Kinneil under guard. Bothwell has come to court, “to the great grief of the Arranistes”. The Jesuits Hay and Dury are in the country, and the King has charged Gray to take them.

1 September 1585 (R4843)

The device of Gray and the Prior of Blantyre: the lords are to join with 20 under colour of his feud with Johnstone, and send 500 men to seize the King and Arran. Gray asks for money to keep a hundred men and hold the King’s house; the commissioners are to meet on the Borders. This is the plan that became the return of the banished lords and the fall of Arran at Stirling in November 1585.

04 Method

The twelve images were fetched from DECODE with the account cookie and read directly: there was no cipher to break, only a hand to read. Each code number was identified by collecting every sentence it occurs in across the three drafts and matching it to the persons and events of August 1585 (Arran’s warding after the Russell affair, Gray’s intrigue with England, the Maxwell–Johnstone feud, the Jesuit mission). A number counts as certain (H) where two or more independent contexts fit only one person; probable (C) where one context points to one person or to a small set.

05 What remains open

  • 27, 40: two of Gray’s associates at court. Maitland and Bellenden are candidates, not tested.
  • 32: a place of refuge for Arran. 36: a source of money.
  • 9, 20, 24, 3: probable only (grade C).
  • Prior reading, checked later the same day. CSP Scotland vol. 8 (1914) was read afterwards through the HathiTrust text view for the companion page on Walsingham’s side of this correspondence. The calendar glosses the numbers in Wotton’s sent despatches: Arran [“19”], the Master of Gray [“39”], the Justice Clerk [“40”] and Farnyhurst [“24”]. So 40 is Sir Lewis Bellenden, and 24 is Sir Thomas Kerr of Ferniehirst, not Huntly. Its no. 106 calendars the despatch of 1 September with “Morton” for 20 (Lord Maxwell, styled Earl of Morton).
