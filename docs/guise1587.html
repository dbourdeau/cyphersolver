[Guise] → the duc de Mercœur · homophonic cipher with a small nomenclator · 27 May and 20 June 1587

Guise to the Duke of Mercœur, May and June 1587

→

BnF français 15564 ff. 119 and 142 (DECODE R4162 and R4165): a page wholly in cipher and a slip with seven clear lines and sixteen of cipher. Both were decrypted with the key George Lasry recovered in 2022, which he published without a decipherment of either letter; the decryptions do not yet build sentences throughout, so both letters are decoded, not read.

Method: read with known key · Extent: none (key obtained, text not read)

At a glanceKnown key applied · none (key obtained, text not read)

The letters
Two League war letters from the Duke of Guise (probably) to Mercœur, 27 May and 20 June 1587; DECODE gave no sender.
Where
BnF fr. 15564 ff. 119 and 142 (Gallica btv1b9064027v).
The cipher
Lasry’s 2022 key, whose many near-identical sign shapes needed a French language model and about twenty passes over 2× crops.
How it was read
Every sign of both letters was transcribed; the key gained a dozen values in this hand.
What it says
Two thousand Italian or Albanian lances and four thousand reiters promised for August, and a secretary sent with twelve thousand écus to raise nine thousand Swiss; by June, “nous sommes maintenant contraincts de donner ordre a nos affaires”.
Still open
Twelve name codes from Lasry’s “unknown” row.
Credit
George Lasry recovered the key in 2022; his table is on Tomokiyo’s Cryptiana, but neither letter’s text was published.

DECODE gives no sender. Lasry’s key calls the cipher “Guise → Mercœur” and Tomokiyo writes “probably from the Duke of Guise”. The texts fit: the writer speaks for the League’s war effort, of “la cause que nous poursuivons de mon costé” and of a meeting at Meaux, and the companion letter f. 78 names “mon frere”. The same cipher is used in ff. 27 and 78 of this volume and in fr. 15565 ff. 105 and 122.

02 The letter of 27 May 1587 (f. 119)

Decoded, not read. The letter was re-read on 23 October 2026 (texts/f119_reading.md, with an English translation): the passages on the levies build sentences, but the opening lines and lines 5, 12, 25, 29–30, 33–34 and 37–38 keep unread runs, between homophones the context does not fix and name codes (24 of 43 sentences clean). In the manuscript’s spelling; […] marks unread signs, [?word] a doubtful word, [21] a name code. The passage on the levies:

Nous avrons sans doubte deux mil lances italiennes ou albanoises et quatre mil reistres, soubs la levée de ce de [21], lesquels les leve pour nous. Il a envoyé son secretaire ve[…] vers [?le] colonel Fifer avec douze mil escus pour arrester et traicter d'une levée de neuf mil suisses, auquel nous n'avons eu encor response, et l'attendons a toutes heures. … L'armée des heretiques, de la grand et [?forte], et composée d'huict mil reistres, douze mil suisses, deux regimens de lansquenets, quatre mil harquebusiers pour Chastillon, leur doibt amener de cavallerie françoise ce qu'ils pourront. [?Je] vous diray que [?je] craigs trop plus les traistres que les ennemis, et le doubte for[…] si nous ne nous resolvons a nous en defaire, ce qu'est tant utile pour la gloire de Dieu et la cause que nous poursuivons.

The Protestant army described is the German and Swiss force that the Huguenot party was raising in 1587 and which crossed into Lorraine in August; Châtillon is François de Coligny, the Admiral’s son, who led its French contingent. “Colonel Fifer” is Ludwig Pfyffer of Lucerne, the Catholic Swiss colonel who raised regiments for the League. The name codes [21], [43], [71] are not in Lasry’s key and are left as numbers.

03 The letter of 20 June 1587 (f. 142)

The clear lines give news from Germany: the duke of Lunebourg with nine thousand horse will be on the Rhine by 20 July. The cipher, joined into running text (f142_running_reading.md; UPPER is a nomenclator group, [?] one unread sign, [x] a supplied letter), is decoded, not read: the middle builds, but unread signs and supplied letters fall in nearly every sentence (2 of 12 clean, 23 October 2026). The middle:

la levee [de] mil lances et quatre mil reistres, qui est ung [i]nstable secours; et a si bien faict par des resolutions precipitees, nous sommes maintenant contrainccts [?] donner ordre a [n]os affaires, qui ne peuvent estre avec telles seurete[z] … si nos premiers desseings eussent esté effectue[z] avec ordre et mesure.

[73] is probably a person the writer is to meet with troops at Meaux; the pronoun “la trouver” suggests a woman, perhaps the Queen Mother, who was negotiating with the League princes in the summer of 1587, but the code is not in Lasry’s key and the identification is not made here.

04 Method

  1. Prior art. The DECODE records (created 2023) carry no key and no decipherment. Cryptiana’s page on Lasry’s BnF solutions lists ff. 27, 78, 119 and 142 under “Duke of Guise? in BnF fr. 15564 and fr. 15565” and gives the key table (GL_BnFfr15564.png) and the decipherment of f. 27 only.
  2. Transcription. Every sign labelled from Gallica full-resolution crops: 1,861 signs on f. 119, 623 on f. 142. Nomenclator groups (ff-ligatures for common words, digits for names) count as one sign.
  3. Decoding. Lasry’s key gives two to seven signs per letter, several of them near-identical shapes. A French language model (5-grams, fr-1600-letters) chose among each label’s candidate letters and gave a first French text (about 70% sense).
  4. Fixing letters. About twenty passes of three lines each at 2× zoom, comparing each sign with the key and fixing the letter where shape and word agree; for the last runs, a search of the French corpora for phrases that fit the candidate letters (it found arrester, cherchions, en faisant estat, jusques icy).
  5. Key extensions in this hand, beyond Lasry’s sheet: the hooked sign = D or “de”; ✝ = Q; ɤ = D, T or X; the x-shape = Y or N; 3/ʒ = C or D; 8 = M, B, N or T; the dotted Є = R; ʃ = A; “=ʃ” = P.
  6. Measurement. Each decryption file carries, per line, the number of signs and the number unresolved; the totals give 95.5% (f. 119) and 92.9% (f. 142), 95.1% of letter signs together. That counts signs given a letter, not sentences that read: the reading audit of 22–23 October 2026 found both letters decoded, not read.

05 What stays open

  • 121 letter signs of 2,477, scattered, no run longer than eight: each was retried in three or more zoomed passes and a phrase search. A few look like two-sign code groups (“98”, “89”, “z3”) rather than letters. Longest gaps: f. 142 line 1 (eight signs after “de”, including two ff-groups from Lasry’s unknown row), f. 119 line 3 (four), line 27 (“que [five] eschauffé”).
  • Name codes: [21] (twice, a duke), [43], [71] (with “son frere”), [73] (twice, f. 142), [N], [&], [9b], and the groups ffv, ffσ, ffx, ffou. None is in Lasry’s table; each occurs once or twice.
  • f. 119: unread runs in the opening lines and lines 5, 12, 25, 29–30, 33–34 and 37–38; the remaining choices are between homophones the context does not fix and nomenclator groups. f. 142: lines 0–2, 9–10 and 13 do not build, and the sign-level transcription was settled in 26 passes; no decipherment or clear copy exists.
  • Grades: words in the full files marked with ? or in brackets are C or M; the identifications of Pfyffer and Châtillon are historical, not from the cipher.

f. 119 (DECODE R4162), 27 May 1587 decoded, not read

The writer (Guise?) tells Mercœur the levy of reiters is assured: by August two thousand Italian or Albanian lances and four thousand reiters; his secretary has gone to colonel Pfyffer with twelve thousand écus to treat for nine thousand Swiss; the heretics' army (eight thousand reiters, twelve thousand Swiss, lansquenets, four thousand arquebusiers for Châtillon) must be defeated, sparing nothing for the glory of God and the cause.

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.

f. 142 (DECODE R4165), 20 June 1587 decoded, not read

After clear news of Lunebourg with nine thousand horse on the Rhine by 20 July, the cipher says the levy that was to be so far advanced is not, the thousand lances and four thousand reiters are "ung instable secours", hasty resolutions force the party to order its affairs less securely than its first designs, and within five or six days the writer hopes to settle the matter with [73], the forces going to find it at Meaux.

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.
