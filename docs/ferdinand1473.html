Ferdinand to John II, 1473 and 1474

Two cipher passages from the young Ferdinand to his father, the first on the troops he is raising to relieve Perpignan and the second on his sister Juana’s marriage at Naples, read from the notes and the clear lines on the same pages.

The letters
Two letters from Ferdinand, king of Sicily and heir of Aragon, to his father John II: Hostalric, 5 June 1473, and Zaragoza, 3 November 1474.
Where
Biblioteca Nacional de España, MSS/20211/64 (BNE Digital bdh0000186643) and MSS/20211/73 (bdh0000186628); catalogue item 857. A third cipher letter of 1470 (/56) is transcribed only.
The cipher
Two different homophonic substitutions of letters, with several signs for each vowel, one place sign (Barcelona) in 1473, and a null and a sign for qu in 1474; the cipher is set inside the clear text.
How it was deciphered
A second hand wrote words and single letters over the signs. The 1473 key was rebuilt from them and extended by sense; the 1474 key was taken from them and then aligned on the clear paragraph at the foot of the page, which is the plaintext.
What it says
1473: Ferdinand brings more than 500 horse from Aragon, Castile and Valencia, 800 in all with 100 men-at-arms, for the relief of Perpignan. 1474: he is astonished that the infanta Juana’s marriage has not been broken off and asks that Mossèn Santcliment go to Naples.
Credit
Transcription, both keys and both readings by Michael Brackx, working with Claude Code (pull request #195).
Still open
Two signs in the 1473 line 5; two look-alike signs whose values rest on sense; rompido and promendo in 1474; the 1470 letter.

01The two letters

MSS/20211/64 is a letter from Ferdinand, then king of Sicily, to his father John II, written at Hostalric on 5 June 1473. John II was besieged at Perpignan by the French, and the cipher passage, nine lines of the recto, reports the troops Ferdinand is bringing: his continos (the paid household guard of the Castilian crown) and men from Castile, more than five hundred horse from Aragon, and the Valencians, some already at Barcelona and the rest within the week, eight hundred horse in all with a hundred men-at-arms.

MSS/20211/73 was written at Zaragoza on 3 November 1474. Its cipher passage has eleven lines. Ferdinand has learned that the marriage of the infanta Juana, his half-sister, “no se es rompido” as negotiated, and he is astonished; he asks that, if anyone is sent to Naples on the matter, it be Mossèn Santcliment, so that his own affairs are handled with the others. Juana married Ferrante I of Naples in 1476–77. A third cipher letter of Ferdinand to John II, MSS/20211/56 (Dueñas, 12 November 1470), is in a different system with a faint pencil decipherment; it is transcribed and not attacked.

Galende Díaz (1994) lists /56 and /73 as ciphered letters of Ferdinand to John II, with no key; /64 is not in his list. The search made for this reading found no edition or decipherment of /64 or /73, but it missed two sources, found afterwards and not used. DECODE has marked both letters Decrypted since 2019 (R1183, R1177), with no document listed on the record, so that status may reflect only the interlinear notes. Satoshi Tomokiyo’s 2018 article reconstructs ciphers Ferdinand used in his 1470s letters to John II; it has not been read here, so whether it covers these two letters, and whether its keys agree, is open. This may not be a first reading.

02The 1473 cipher

A homophonic substitution of letters, 39 kinds of sign as transcribed and about 30 with a value, set inside the clear text. The vowels and c have several signs each; one place sign, a long s joined to an i with a cross-bar, stands for Barcelona; there is no other nomenclator sign. Signs that looked alike were separated at 12× magnification: 3 is h and 3- is y, T is r in 8 of 8 places and TT is s in 13 of 13.

The key was rebuilt from the second hand’s interlinear notes (certificar dela gente, trayo, continos, de castilla, de aragon, mas de quinientos, de cavallo, comigo and others, with single letters on ruled guidelines over the signs) and extended by sense. The decoder prints both values where a sign has two, and the raw decode is in the repository untouched; the reading below normalises it.

… e porque agora me puede mandar lo que acerca su servicio tengo de fazer, acorde de lo certificar de la gente que comigo trayo, que ahora entre los [?mios] continuos e los que de Castilla c[…]o viene, e los de Aragón, más de quinientos de cavallo. E los valencianos son ya; algunos dellos llegados en Barcelona, e todos los otros hi serán por toda esta semana que viene; en manera que todos [?juntos], estimo, fan los que comigo irán ochocientos de cavallo, en que havrá ciento hombres de armas. Vea V. S. la forma que devo tener …

03The 1474 cipher

A homophonic substitution of its own, not the 1473 alphabet: bar signs, dotted signs, a null (an undotted A) and a sign for qu. The sign 4 gives a, r and c, probably three look-alike signs. The 1473 key does not read /73 or /56.

The key was recovered in two steps. A key of 19 signs read from the interlinear letters alone decoded no res menos se, matrimoni… infanta mi hermana no se, mossen s…, and the same words begin the clear paragraph at the foot of the recto. align1474.py then aligned the 310 signs with the paragraph’s 325 letters and re-estimated the key. Because the paragraph is the plaintext, this is a key recovered from adjacent plaintext, not an independent break, and polyphony of 20% of read tokens means the validation test does not apply.

No res menos, senyor, yo he sabido como el matrimonio de la infanta mi hermana no se es [?rompido], segunt stava praticado e concertado, de que me so admirado. Y pues assi es, supplico humilmente a vuestra alteza le remede de lo que me [?promendo], que si aya hombre alguno a Napols por estos fechos, que vaya mossen Santcliment, porque yendo ell, mis fechos se facan a bueltas de los otros, e assi se seguira grant benefficio.

04What remains uncertain

  • 1473, line 5. Two signs (H C) after de Castilla: C occurs only here and the sense wants con migo or similar. Open.
  • 1473, two look-alike signs. The sigma-like 6 is a in four words but i/j in mios and juntos, and the plain p stands for c, o and once a. Thirteen values were chosen by sense. The 1085-pixel scan does not separate the forms; a better image would.
  • 1474, rompido and promendo. The first cipher sign of rompido is c elsewhere, while the clear text and the interlinear letter both give r; promendo is so written in both texts and its sense is not settled. The place sign near a Napols and the signs met once or twice are too few to fix.
  • Reading status. The reading checker finds half the sentences of the 1473 passage and 43% of the 1474 passage clean; the 1474 shortfall is probably the Catalan-Aragonese spellings (senyor, fechos, facan) against a Spanish model. A reader has gone through both readings as sentences, but neither has been checked against the image by a person.
  • Other letters. Ferdinand’s other letters to John II in the same BNE series (/57, /80, /82, /99, /105, /120, /123) have not been checked for cipher in either alphabet.

# BNE MSS/20211/64: English translation of the cipher passage

> … and so that Your Lordship may now tell me what I am to do for your service, I resolved to inform you of the
> men I bring with me: at present, among my *continos* and those who come from Castile [...], and those of
> Aragon, more than five hundred horse. And the Valencians are on their way; some of them have already arrived
> at Barcelona, and all the others will be there in the course of this coming week; so that all together, I
> reckon, those who will go with me will make eight hundred horse, among them a hundred men-at-arms. Let Your
> Lordship see what course I must take …

*Continos* (*contínuos*): the paid household guard of the Castilian crown. In June 1473 Ferdinand was raising
troops for the relief of Perpignan, where John II was besieged by the French.

Zaragoza, 3 Nov 1474 (MSS/20211/73)

Ferdinand is astonished that the infanta Juana's marriage has not been broken off as negotiated, and asks that Mossèn Santcliment be the one sent to Naples, so that his own affairs are handled with the others.

# BNE MSS/20211/73, Ferdinand to John II, Zaragoza 3 Nov 1474: transcription of the cipher

Source: BNE Digital bdh0000186628 (viewer id 647a859a-254f-4fb8-b7f9-b42629fa962b), image 1 of 3, the "high" PDF
(1114 x 1510 px), read in the pdf.js viewer at 4x and 5x (29 Oct 2026). The cipher runs from the end of the
clear line "... quanto el raxo seca ... yda en Castilla sea necessaria" to "... e aquesto senyor supplico por
merced": the tail of that line (K00) and ten full lines (K01-K10): 310 signs of 47 kinds as transcribed.

**Interlinear decipherment.** Over almost every sign there is a small letter or two in a second hand (e.g. over
K00: *n o r … e d l s … m e n o s … s e*; over K09: *s e f a c a n a b …*). The letters are small and pale
at this resolution; those read are in the second block below. The plaintext of the passage is the clear
paragraph at the foot of the recto (`../decryption/d73_clear.md`), with which the signs are aligned
(`../align1474.py`).

**System.** Not the alphabet of /64 (1473), though some shapes recur (`4`, `6`, `2`, `oo`, `+`). The signs are
largely of the "bar" type: o's and crosses on a horizontal stroke, signs with dots or colons, plus `me` (a
syllable or word sign, five times). Strokes `/` and `//` separate groups.

| `d` | ∂ / ∋ with a dot |

- K01 end: **"… el matrimonio de la in[fanta] …"** over `/. A tt 4 o- y° q lp 9. 6 ro // 2f: /. A d ro 7`. This gives

# BNE MSS/20211/73 (Ferdinand to John II, Zaragoza, 3 Nov 1474): the clear paragraph at the foot of the recto

Diplomatic transcription of the paragraph after the date line, read on BNE Digital image 1 at about 7x of the
1114-px scan. It is the plaintext of the cipher passage K00-K10 (see `d73_reading.md`). Line breaks as on the
page; `[?]` = not read; `q̃` = *que*; superscript letters in `^ ^`.

```
No res menos Senyor yo he sabido como el matrimonio dela infanta mi ermana no se es rompido
segunt stava praticado e concertado de que me so admirado y pues assi es supplico humilmente
vra altesa le remede delo q̃ ^me^ ꝓmendo que si a ye hombre alguno a napols por
estos fechos q̃ vaya moss santcliment porq̃ yendo ell mis fechos [?] mis fechos se facan abueltas
delos otros e assi se seguira grant benefficio
```

Doubtful: *rompido* (the cipher sign for the first letter is *c* elsewhere), *ꝓmendo* (also so in the cipher),
*si a ye* (the cipher has *si aya*), the repeated *mis fechos* at the line break.

# BNE MSS/20211/73: English translation of the cipher passage

> Nevertheless, my lord, I have learned that the marriage of the infanta my sister has not been [?broken off],
> as had been negotiated and agreed, at which I am astonished. And since this is so, I humbly beg Your Highness
> to remedy it [as I ?…]: that if any man is to go to Naples on these matters, Mossèn Santcliment should go,
> because if he goes my affairs will be dealt with together with the others, and so great benefit will follow.

The infanta is Juana of Aragon (1454-1517), Ferdinand's half-sister, who married Ferrante I of Naples in
1476-77.
