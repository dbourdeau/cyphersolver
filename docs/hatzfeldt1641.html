Regensburg → Hatzfeldt’s army · zodiac, aspect and planet signs · August–September 1641

Ferdinand III to Hatzfeldt, August–September 1641

Go to the full text ↓

The Emperor orders troops, money and wagons for his field marshal in the Rhineland in five cipher pieces whose key was printed in 1963 but whose texts had never been published.

The pieces
Letters of the Emperor Ferdinand III to Field Marshal Melchior von Hatzfeldt of 21 August, 7 September and 14 September 1641, an undated postscript, and a ciphered copy of the bond of the city of Aachen of 12 September, enclosed with the last letter.
Where
Hohenlohe-Zentralarchiv Neuenstein (Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg), Ni 10 (Rosenberg-Hatzfeldt) Bd 242, fols. 46–49, 90, 93, 95–105. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
Homophonic substitution with ten nulls: zodiac signs for a–m, aspect signs and planets for the rest, numerals as homophones of the vowels, no word division.
How it was deciphered
With the key of Hermann Stützel, “Chiffrierwesen im Dreißigjährigen Krieg” (1963), applied unchanged. Stützel says the letters could all be translated but prints none of their texts.
What it says
Orders, money, troops and provisions: 20,000 reichsthalers on a two-month credit and 500 cuirassiers (in both the bond and the letter), the Mandelsloh regiment, sixty Austrian ox-wagons, the Franconian Circle and the Bishop of Würzburg.
Still open
Fols. 97v–98r of the 14 September letter are missing from the copies; 49 words carry a scribe’s slip; the clear passages were transcribed by AI agents only.
Credit
The key is Hermann Stützel’s and is not claimed. Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée) ordered the copies, transcribed and read the pieces, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents.

01 The pieces

The five pieces are cipher blocks inside letters from the imperial court at Regensburg during the Reichstag of 1640–41. P1 (21 August, fols. 46r–49v) is endorsed “in Ziffern geschrieben”; P2 (7 September, fols. 90r–90v) and P3, a postscript in a lighter hand, follow; P4 (12 September, fols. 95r–96r) is the ciphered copy of the bond of the city of Aachen, signed by its delegate Caspar von Schwarzenburg; P5 (14 September, fols. 97r and 98v–105v) is endorsed “Schreiben ganz mit Ziffern”. Only the cipher pieces were reproduced. The copies are private research copies and are not shown here.

The pieces confirm each other. P4 and P5 give the same 20,000 reichsthalers, two months and 500 cuirassiers, independently; P1 and P5 share the Mandelsloh regiment, the sixty ox-wagons, the Franconian Circle and the Bishop of Würzburg; P2 and P5 share Leipzig and a half-cannon.

02 The key

Stützel broke all four systems of the Neuenstein letters to Hatzfeldt and printed their keys; group I (Ferdinand III to Hatzfeldt, 1641, Anlage 1) is the system of Bd 242. It is a homophonic substitution: the twelve zodiac signs stand for a–m in order, the aspects for o–r, the planets and the Moon for s–z, numerals are homophones of the vowels (3, 10, 25 = a; 7, 8, 65 = e; 5, 70, 81 = i; 30 = o; 4 = u), and ten signs are nulls. His table is applied here unchanged.

Two features of the 1641 hands are shapes, not changes to the key: u is mostly a single loop with a cross above and b an 8 with a cross above, and y is an 8 with a cross below and x a single loop with a cross below. Thirty-seven and ten signs were reviewed on the images and corrected. A second transcriber, blind to the first, redid one page per hand (1,576 tokens) and matched 91.5% of tokens exactly and 94.4% on the key value.

03 The reading

The count is made by script. Of 859 cipher words, 677 are certain, 131 probable, 49 restored and 2 unresolved: 94.1% read, 98.9% by letters. The letter of 14 September reads at 95.3% and is classed as complete; the 21 August letter (93.0%), 7 September (90.7%), the postscript (50.0%) and the Aachen bond (94.9%, one word short) stay partial. Restored words are mostly one slip of the scribe in an otherwise certain word and count as holes. The German and an English translation are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Fols. 97v–98r, the middle of the letter of 14 September, are not among the copies supplied. Other cipher letters of 1641 may lie in the folios not reproduced and in Bd 243, which has been ordered and not received.
  • Six letters (two words) stay unresolved after a blind review of 18 doubtful signs: one word of the postscript and two letters of a marginal insertion on fol. 103r.
  • Glyph variants outside Stützel’s list (56 letters) were assigned to his signs by shape and context and counted as probable, never certain. Nine signs not reviewed are left as Venus or Mercury.
  • The clear passages, the signatures and the pale abstract on fol. 49v were transcribed by AI agents, not by a palaeographer. One reader made the decipherment into sense while seeing the decryption; the key was frozen before and not changed.
  • The Aachen agreement may be printed in the digital edition of the Reichstag of 1640/41 now in preparation (Deutsche Reichstagsakten), to be checked when it appears.

## P1 — Regensburg, 21 August 1641 (fols. 46r–48v)

*Ferdinand the Third, by the grace of God elected Roman Emperor, at all times Augmenter of the Empire. High- and well-born, dear and faithful.
We have duly received your most obedient report from Dorsten dated the eleventh of August, and have graciously learnt from it how, as soon as
Dorsten is taken, you most obediently offer to carry out the order sent to you through Our Court War Secretary Vincentz [?] Ottman,* ‹and what
way in particular you intend to take in doing so›. *Since We* ‹entirely approve of it, We have at once informed Our beloved brother, the Archduke
Leopold Wilhelm of Austria, His Highness, and reminded him to send you urgent orders to follow you in that same enterprise as soon as you have
finished with Dorsten›. [?] *you will persevere in the enterprise against the said Dorsten (since you have already got so far with it), and will
not need to give it up even if its surrender should be delayed somewhat beyond the end of this month of August.* ‹You shall not lack the supply of
more provisions, ammunition and foot when you march up here; We mean then to send to join you the Mandelsloh regiment and whatever else can be

have already arrived up here from Austria, so that on your coming they are ready at hand and can bring the provisions to you; and We are likewise
asking the Franconian Circle Estates, by separate letters, for a contribution in provisions, to be set against their next imperial contribution,

way to the main army and have had answers, We nevertheless think it safer that, besides what you can still send to Our main army by that way —
which must always be done in well-kept cipher — you also send the same every time in duplicate to Us here … The two regiments in the land of

resolution will be taken on how to deal with them … and We remain graciously well disposed to you. Given in Our and the Holy Empire's city of
Regensburg, the twenty-first of August 1641, in the fifth year of Our Roman, the sixteenth of Our Hungarian and the fourteenth of Our Bohemian reign.
