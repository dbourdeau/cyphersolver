Urbino → the Signoria of Florence · homophonic letter cipher with word signs · early 1425

Galeotto da Ricasoli to the Dieci di Balía, Urbino, early 1425

Archivio di Stato di Firenze, Dieci di Balía, Responsive 2 no. 171 (old 168), DECODE R3754: a one-page letter of Florence’s envoy to Count Guidantonio da Montefeltro, in fast Florentine cursive with about twenty runs of cipher. The abbé Gabbrielli rebuilt the key in 1863 from a few words he glossed on this letter and three others; Meister printed his table in 1902. The letter itself was never printed.

Gabbrielli’s key was applied, and extended from his own glosses over the cipher runs of this letter and of the sibling letters nos. 21, 91 and 92 (DECODE R3755–R3757). With the extended key, 97 of the 99 cipher tokens are deciphered, 94 of them at grade H or M. No fuller key was found in Gabbrielli’s volume, in print or on DECODE. The letter is still decoded, not read: the clear Italian around the runs is a first-pass reading with gaps in nearly every sentence, so the whole could not be built into sentences and translated (reading audit, 22 October 2026). A re-read of the clear text on 23 October filled much of the middle of the letter and found its date line, but the opening, line 9 and the close still do not hold (11 of 23 sentences clean).

The letter
Galeotto Fibindacci da Ricasoli, Florence’s envoy at Urbino, to the Dieci di Balía, after the defeat at Zagonara; undated, with siblings of February 1425.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Firenze, Dieci di Balía, Responsive 2 no. 171; DECODE R3754, listed as “Non-decrypted”.
The cipher
An alphabet with two or three signs per letter, eighteen nulls and thirty word signs, plus six clear cover words.
How it was read
Gabbrielli’s 1863 key (printed by Meister, 1902) was applied and extended from the siblings’ glosses, adding six certain and fourteen probable letter values.
What it says
The Count of Urbino calls his wife “after all a woman, and suspicious”; her relatives and the citizens were to bind themselves to his wishes in secret.
Still open
Two signs before SOSPETOSA have no value and some words of the fast clear hand are uncertain.

01 The letter, the key and the siblings

The letter opens “Magnifici potenti Signori miei” and is signed “Vostro servidore Galeotto de Fibindacci”. Its foot, never transcribed before 23 October 2026, reads “[?In] Castello, a dì viij di settembre”, with no year; the dating to early 1425 below rests on the siblings and is not confirmed by it. Its siblings in the same hand, filza 3 nos. 91 and 92, are dated at Urbino on 17 and 28 February, in the Florentine year 1424, that is 1425. Guasti’s edition of the Commissioni di Rinaldo degli Albizzi shows the Dieci sending Galeotto to the Count of Urbino in September 1423, and prints a later clear letter of his from Urbino of 28 August 1425. “Madonna” is the Countess, Caterina Colonna, niece of Pope Martin V, who married Guidantonio in 1424 and reached Urbino on 4 March 1424.

The key is page 1 of Gabbrielli’s volume Chiavi delle cifre delle lettere ai Dieci di Balía dal 1424 al 1530, compiled in 1863–64. The volume is on the Ilardi microfilm at Yale, reel 58, and Meister printed page 1 in Die Anfänge der modernen diplomatischen Geheimschrift (1902), p. 50. It is headed “Cifra di Galeotto Fibindacci da Ricasoli, carteggio dei X di Balía, an. 1424”. It gives an alphabet with two or three signs for most letters, eighteen nulls, and a nomenclator of thirty word signs such as Conte d’Urbino, Duca di Milano, Malatesta, fanti and mille. It also lists six cover words written in clear: Giovanni for the Dieci, Andrea for Galeotto, trombetta for an ambassador, che for horse, con for war and tagliato for loss.

Gabbrielli marked the letters he worked from in the margin: filza 1 nos. 163 and 165, filza 2 no. 72 and filza 3 nos. 21, 91 and 92. His interlinear glosses on no. 171 cover a dozen words: paolo, di madona, cintadini, il conte, sarrem andino, a laog., ambasciatore, puro femina e sospettosa, el conte d’Urbino, cavalli, parenti and madonna. Several of these sit over runs far longer than the gloss, and two of them are misreadings.

02 The three unread runs

Each run was cut from the deskewed scan at four to five times magnification and transcribed sign by sign. The signs were then matched against Gabbrielli’s table and his gloss. Capitals are the decoded text.

“Madonna è pure femina e sospetosa”

The clear text runs: “… che […] Giovanni [i Dieci] mandasse trombetta [ambasciatore], perché dice che”. Thirty signs follow. MADONNA is seven signs, all in Gabbrielli’s table. PURE is P, U, R, E from his table with a feathered null after the P. FEMINA is six signs, all his. E is his. Two signs follow that have no value (section 05). SOSPETOSA is nine signs. The two O signs in it stand exactly five places apart, as the two O’s of s-o-spet-o-sa do, and that fixes the alignment. Gabbrielli glossed the whole phrase but never tabulated the signs in it. The sentence reads: “because he [the Count] says that Madonna is, after all, a woman, and suspicious”.

“Parenti e citadini”

The run has seven signs and then eight. Gabbrielli’s values give P, A, R, E, I, T and D; the others are values recovered from the sibling glosses (9 = N, ɣ = A) or fixed by position (two light strokes = T, ᴗ = C, # = I). The two words are PARENTI and CITADINI, one letter per sign. The sentence reads: “et segretamente MADONNA et PARENTI [e] CITADINI promettessono e obligassensi che, non obstante [ELLA?] dicesse e volesse el [?contrario], essi farebbono quello volesse [IL CONTE]”. In English: Madonna’s relatives and the citizens should secretly promise and bind themselves that, whatever she might say or want to the contrary, they would do what the Count wished.

“Acomandino a lo…”

Gabbrielli glossed this run sarrem andino and the next word a laog. The first sign is on his own list of nulls. The next ten are A C O M A N D I N O by his table and by the value +o = O, which the siblings confirm. The last three are A L O, and the clear text continues. The 18 September reading took the last sign for I (“a lui”); at three times magnification it is Gabbrielli’s O. The clause is “Ma prosetto che no lo pare che [IL PAPA?] sia [?atento], S'ACOMANDINO A LO[…]” (re-read on 23 October 2026), “that it does not seem to him that the Pope is attentive; that they commend themselves to […]”.

[?In] Castello, a dì viij di settembre.

Vostro servidore Galeotto de Fibindacci da Ricasoli.

Galeotto, held up by the weather of a great flood, reports an hour’s talk with Paolo, who came to see him, about the intentions of the Countess and the citizens; it does not seem that the Pope is attentive, and some party is to commend itself to someone. He thinks it more useful that someone open the talks as a private person than that the Dieci send an ambassador, because the Count distrusts his wife: “Madonna is after all a woman, and […] suspicious”. Foot and horse are to be engaged quietly, with the Dieci giving them work. Her relatives and the citizens are to bind themselves, in secret, to follow the Count whatever she says. He ends that the business should be kept more secret.

04 The key corrected and extended

Was a fuller key already available? No. Gabbrielli’s volume has a second key for a Fibindacci in 1424 (key no. 2, “Karolus commissarius”), but it belongs to another sender and gives other values to the shared shapes (+o = B, 6 = S, ∧ = H), which the glossed words here contradict. His keys nos. 3 and 4 are Latin syllabic nomenclators. Meister and Somogyi reproduce key no. 1 only. DECODE holds Gabbrielli’s volume as key records R5687–R5698 (the 1424 frames are R5687–R5690); none is linked to R3754–R3757 and none goes beyond key no. 1.

What the siblings add. Gabbrielli glossed most cipher runs of nos. 21, 91 and 92, but tabulated only some of the signs under them. Forty glossed runs were transcribed sign by sign and matched letter for letter with the gloss: la novella della rotta, andarono, tante e tali, non contenterà mai, venuta a Imola, Modigliana twice, d’Oriuolo, promettere, Filippo, mandato, salvo condotto, venne and the word signs for Malatesta, the Duke, Galeotto, Pandolfo, Carlo, the Count, the Dieci and fiorini. Eleven runs could not be matched (faint gloss, blots). The result, with the evidence for each value, is the file key_extended.md.

  • Six new letter values are certain, each spelled out under a gloss: ɣ = A, a 5-shape with a cross-stroke = M, 9 = N (Gabbrielli has B), +o = O, a π with a hooked leg = O, and an open 4 with a long bar = T.
  • Fourteen more are probable, among them three signs with two values (ᴗ = D and C, # = L and I, π = F and S), each resting on one letter so far.
  • Nulls. q is a null, as Gabbrielli has it; ɣ is a variant A, not a null; a heavy filled triangle is a null (in andaro▼no and madon▼na). His null “o+” (circle then cross) is a different sign from +o (cross then circle), which is O.
  • Nomenclator. A three-stroke sign is pace; β, Gabbrielli’s con, also enciphers the syllable con inside a word (SALVOβDOTTO).
  • Corrections to the tabulation of 18 September. Rereading the film at full size shows that ▽ is under E in Gabbrielli’s table, and that the cross over a small circle (T), the 8 with a ring below (S) and the ⊓ with a stem (I) are his own signs, not new ones.

Effect on no. 171. All 99 cipher tokens were transcribed again from three-times crops and deciphered with the extended key (token list cipher171.tsv). 72 values come from Gabbrielli’s table, 16 from the recovered values, 5 are nomenclator entries and 4 rest on context. The changes are small: the name is spelled PAVOLO; the Count’s word sign stands three times; the Dieci’s word sign stands on line 11; “a lui” becomes A LO…; the heavy pair on line 4 is a null and one word sign.

05 What remains uncertain

  • The word sign before “sia” (a box with a bar, after a heavy null) is not in Gabbrielli’s nomenclator. He wrote over it a small p with a looped flourish, the usual abbreviation for Papa. The Countess is the Pope’s niece, so “the Pope” fits. Grade I.
  • One single word sign, before “dicesse”, is given the value ella from context alone. Grade I.
  • Two signs before SOSPETOSA, after the E, have no value: an open 4 with a long bar (T in the siblings) and ψ (P by the key). “e t[ro]p[o] sospetosa” would fit, but one occurrence with no gloss cannot fix it. The word sospetosa is not affected.
  • PAVOLO is deciphered cleanly; who he is stays open. He may be messer Paolo da Capranica, whom a Florentine cover list of 1424 calls “Capra”.
  • The clear text: about a dozen words of the opening (lines 1–4), of “O detto …” (line 9) and of the close (lines 12–13) stay doubtful after the re-read of 23 October 2026: the mercantile cursive is fast and faded at the resolution of the DECODE image. Six cipher letters rest on look-alike readings.
  • The date: the foot gives “a dì viij di settembre” and a doubtful “[?In] Castello”, but no year.
  • The key is still partial. B, H and Q have no values beyond Gabbrielli’s, eleven sibling runs are not aligned, and about fifteen glossed runs of no. 92 are not yet transcribed. The three siblings have not been deciphered end to end. The extended table is queued for DECODE as a key derived from Gabbrielli’s (R5687–R5690), with its grades.
