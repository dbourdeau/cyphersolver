Kurz von Senftenau to Trauttmansdorff, Hamburg 1639 — the vice-chancellor’s cipher rebuilt

→

SOA Plzeň, pracoviště Klášter u Nepomuka, Rodinný archiv Trauttmansdorffů, inv. č. 200, kart. 9 · DECODE R3811–R3815, R4625, R4645, R4734, R4736 · 73 images, about 23,000 cipher signs

A contemporary reader glossed one page; from it the whole table falls out. The other eight letters had never been deciphered.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved in part 19 September 2026 · updated 21 September 2026

solved in part

Summary. Ferdinand Sigmund Kurz von Senftenau, the imperial vice-chancellor, spent the winter of 1638–39 in Hamburg, where the preliminaries of the Westphalian peace were being sounded with Sweden, France and the Danish mediator. His ciphered reports to Maximilian von Trauttmansdorff survive in the Trauttmansdorff family archive at Klášter. DECODE holds nine of them. Jakub Mírka rebuilt the key in 2012 from the interlinear decipherment on the first page of the 17 January letter and read that one letter, but he printed neither the key nor the text. Here the whole corpus was transcribed from the public DECODE images. The key was rebuilt from the same glosses, which show a regular table: numbers 41–100 stand for consonant–vowel syllables, two consonants to each ten in reverse alphabetical order, and the low numbers and signs are homophones for letters. With a German language model filling the rest, all nine letters decipher. They cover Banér’s invasion of Lower Saxony, Arnim’s bid for pardon, the Danish mediation and its collapse, and Kurz’s contempt for “universal” treaties (“ein teufflisch inventum”). The key is complete, but the reading is not: the transcription was a single pass, so 20–45 % of each letter is still garbled. A checked second transcription would close it. Status: key rebuilt; nine letters read in part.

R3812 is dated 1638 in DECODE, but it opens “Mein jüngstes sub dato 27. January”, which is R3813. It is dated Hamburg 3 Feb. 1639 in its clear postscript and reports Arnim in Hamburg after his escape in late 1638. It belongs to 1639.

The cipher runs inline with clear German. Kurz writes the harmless words in the clear and enciphers the names, numbers and judgments.

02The key

About 210 glossed signs on the first two pages of R3811 give the pattern straight away: 83 de, 82 di, 84 da; 43 re, 42 ri, 44 ra; 51 no, 52 ni; 72 hi, 74 ha; 93 be; 57 mi, 59 ma. Every ten is split into two halves of five. Each half has one consonant, and its first four places run o i e a:

The consonants run backwards through the alphabet from 41: r p n m l k h g d c b. The fifth place of each half is a separate series of -u syllables, and each was fixed from words: Reputation and Graf von Puechheim (45), illustrissimi (50), unmüglich (55), Hunger and Churbrandenburg (60), Ankunft (65), durch (70), Herzog Augustus (75), Magdeburg, Meckelburg (80), pericula (85), Haus Österreich (95), zurück, darüber (100).

The numbers 1–40 and some twenty letter and graphic signs are homophones for single letters. The commonest are 28 r, 31 n, 27 s, 26 t, 39 c, 35 h, 18/19/20 e, 17/15/16 i, 21/22/23 a, 10/11/25 u, 12/13/14 o, 36/38 g, π/α m, ψ/φ z, w/#z-tail n and 29 qu. A few stand for pairs: 9 te, 6 se, c si, n ve, d so, k ti and a looped ℓ tu. A handful of marks are nulls or flourishes. The full table, with counts, is in KEY.md. This is Mírka’s description (“homophonic substitution, bigrams, nulls, no codes”) written out as a table.

03What the letters say

The readings below are word-divided and lightly normalised from the machine decipherment. Where the text is garbled, the summaries say so.

17 January (R3811)

Nothing can be done with Duke Georg of Lüneburg: ohne Widerstand ist der Feindt über [the Elbe]. Recruits are going to Austria and money is short. France, Sweden and Holland im Werck sein, zum König eine ansehnliche Legation zu schicken [are about to send a considerable embassy to the King] of Denmark, to prevent a congress. Herzog Augustus von Sachsen-Lauenburg wolle sich in dennische Protection begeben [Duke August of Saxe-Lauenburg means to place himself under Danish protection]. Arnim, suspected over the Friedland affair, swears he plotted nothing against the Peace of Prague, and Kurz advises a Protectorium in optima forma. These are the points of Mírka’s summary, one by one.

27 January (R3813), a twenty-page memorandum

Der Feindt gehet ganz unverwehrt hieher, zieht sich gegen Waltzwedel [the enemy comes on quite unhindered, heading for Salzwedel]. Kurz writes that it is besser ein ungeschlossener, durch den Feindt zertrennter Craistag als ein geschlossener mit einer expressen Neutralitet [better a Kreistag left unconcluded and broken up by the enemy than one concluded with an express neutrality]. He advises a defensive war to keep Austria, Bohemia and Silesia. He distrusts Salvius, who is “voller Gifft contra Domum Austriacam”, and weighs a secret particular treaty through Denmark or Brandenburg. He calls the universal treaties and safe-conducts “ein teufflisch inventum et ad exclusionem omnium tractatuum”. France wants Lorraine, Alsace and das Kaiserthumb; mit Schweden ist umb ein leichters noch zu accordiren [Sweden is somewhat easier to come to terms with].

3 and 6 February (R3812, R3815)

Banér’s plundering will bring das ganze Stift of Halberstadt zu Grund. Between Denmark and Sweden there are grosse disgusti. Sweden wants half of Pomerania and an amnesty and will not come to a congress while it does not feel the war at home. Arnheim ist gestern Abent bei mir gewesen: Arnim describes Banér’s army as zehentausend Mann, ein ermattetes, armseliges, hungeriges Volck [ten thousand men, worn out, wretched and hungry]. To the Emperor, Kurz reports the King of Denmark ganz perplex and the Danish Reichsrat irresolut und pusillanimes.

10 and 13 February (R4625, R4645)

A plan to keep the Lower Saxon Circle divided and to hold Halberstadt, Magdeburg, Hildesheim and Wolfenbüttel follows. It warns that the imperial army is gar ruinirt, et venter caret auribus. The King’s conditions for his mediation have proved empty, and wir wollen uns dannoch und auch allein wehren [we will defend ourselves nonetheless, even alone]. Count Pentz says the King must drop the mediation and denounce Sweden: ergo ruptura tractatuum hic est principium coniunctionis.

8 and 14 April (R4734, R4736)

Die Interposition ist schon expirirt: the King says he has bound hands and will do nothing per force. From Glückstadt, Christian IV’s residence, Kurz writes von hinnen wolt man mich nit gern dimittiren. He reports the Danish complaint that war in Austria is paid for with peace in Lower Saxony while the Swedes sit in Mecklenburg and Pomerania: für solche Mauren geben wir unser Schweiss und Bluet [for such walls we give our sweat and blood]. A courtier ist der gescheideste in dissimuliren [is the cleverest at dissimulating].

04Method

Agents transcribed all 73 DECODE images token by token, two at a time. Clear text went in braces and the glosses were attached to their numbers. Plain homophonic annealing with a German 5-gram model (Grimmelshausen and Gutenberg German, early spelling) collapsed into enenen…, even with a letter-frequency penalty. The glosses turned the problem into a structured one: one consonant per half-ten and one value per single sign, seeded with seventeen glossed values. That solver produced fluent German on the first run (ich müesse die Kranckheit beschreiben, damit man sich den remediis darnach zu richten…). The irregular signs were then fixed by reading them in context and by ranking candidate values with the language model. Of the 22,913 transcribed cipher signs, 98.3 % then had a value.

A checked second pass followed on 21 September 2026. Agents compared every line of the 73 transcriptions with crops of the images, working from a printout of each sign’s value, and changed a sign only where the image showed it. About 360 signs were corrected. The commonest slips were a plain c written for the looped sign worth tu, a bold 9 written for the hooked sign worth w, and 6 written for φ (z). In R3813 a crossed ƒ-like sign worth ta had been merged with another sign. A script fault had also dropped 56 cipher lines that began with a graphic sign. The language-model score of the decipherment improved on eight of the nine letters. Of 23,699 cipher signs, 99.9 % now have a value.

05What remains uncertain

  • Transcription, not key. After the checked second pass, the garble that remains sits where the image itself does not decide: digits run together, blots and overwritten figures. The Latin passages are still the weakest. tr/PASS2_LOG.md lists the remaining lines file by file.
  • α was first read as m, which gave Salv- before l. The images show two signs: the small upright α is sa (Salvius, caesaris, universal-, Sachsen), and a larger looped form is m. In one hand (R3813) the two shapes are hard to tell apart.
  • 99 is uncertain (e?), 90 occurs once, and 96 does not occur. The signs 3, 4, 5, 7, m, #s-loop, #dot and p are read as nulls or are unassigned (grade M).
  • The recurring group read ml in R3812–R3814 is sometimes part of universal- and sometimes a name. It is unresolved.
  • The two Banér copies in R4645 are transcribed: to Lüneburg, 10 January 1639, and to the Lower Saxon envoys, 29 January 1639, refusing to leave the Circle. The endorsement on R4734 names the addressee as Count Schwarzenberg. Kurz’s own clear postscript on R4645 is only half legible.
