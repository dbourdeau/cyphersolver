The letters
Six letters of Arthur Lee in Paris to his brother Richard Henry Lee, from [29 March] 1779 to 29 February 1780, as copied in Lee’s letter book. Most of each letter is in clear English; the sensitive words are number groups.
Where
US National Archives, RG 360, Papers of the Continental Congress, M247 item 102 vol. 3 (Arthur Lee’s letter book), National Archives Catalog 502968528, images 0019, 0027, 0035, 0041–0042, 0052, 0122–0123 and 0183. The received copies of letters 2–4 are in the Lee Family Papers, University of Virginia (microfilm roll 6).
The cipher
A book code: each word is written as page, column and line of John Entick’s New Spelling Dictionary, for example 243,a,XVII = minister. Persons come from the dictionary’s list of Christian names, so the code gives only a first name.
How it was deciphered
The system and the book were known (Burnett 1917, Tomokiyo). The London 1776 printing and the rule for counting entries were fixed here, then every group was looked up. Two blind readers read every figure again, and the old decipherment written over the received copies of letters 2–4 agrees in 63 of the 72 groups where it can be compared.
What it says
Lee’s private charges: Gérard suspected of dealings with Deane, a new man to be sent because “Never will our affairs go well, while Benjamin is here”, and distrust of French troops in America.
Still open
4 groups unread and 14 doubtful. No received copy of letters 1, 5 and 6 was found, and three tests of the figure reading on other glossed Lee letters fell short of their mark.
Credit
Andrew Beling, with AI agents (14–17 November 2026): the letters found by paging the letter book, the edition and counting rule, the reading, the blind checks and the comparison with the received copies. No person has checked the manuscripts.

01 The letter book

The volume the National Archives Catalog titles “Transcripts of Letters from Arthur Lee, William Lee, Ralph Izard, and the Joint Commissioners … Volume 3” is in fact Arthur Lee’s book of copies of the letters he sent from Paris between March 1779 and May 1780, to Vergennes, Franklin, the Committee, his brothers and others. Paging all 227 images turned up six letters to “The Honble R. H. L.” with bare number groups and no decipherment anywhere in the volume.

02 The dictionary code

Each code word is page, column (a or b) and line, the line being the Nth headword entry down the column. Endings are added after a dash: 115b38-s = Deane’s, 322a7-sia = Rus-sia. Persons are taken from the list “The most usual Christian Names of Men” on pages 454–455, five columns lettered a to e: 455a14 Gerard, 454b14 Benjamin, 455b11 John with 454a5-s Adam-s, 455d27 Samuel, 455a9 Francis. Who is meant is never in the code.

The printing. The 1766, 1772, 1773, 1776 and 1781 editions on the Internet Archive were compared. The 1772 and 1781 are paged differently. The 1773 and 1776 share many page breaks, but on p. 108 col. a Court is the 10th entry in 1773 and the 9th in 1776, and Lee writes court as 108a9 four times. The London 1776 printing (Edward and Charles Dilly) fits; it is the one used here. Burnett and Tomokiyo name a 1777 edition; no copy of it was found, and no difference between it and the 1776 setting has been shown.

The counting rule. Count headword entries from the top of the column, leaving out the running head, the letter-section headings and the indented run-over lines of long entries. The rule was fixed on loan 229a37, I 196a28 and friend 170b15, which come out right only under it.

03 What the letters say

These are what Arthur Lee wrote to his brother, not established facts. Words from code groups are as the readings give them; doubtful ones carry a query in brackets.

  • Letter 1, [29 March 1779]. La Luzerne is named to replace Gérard. “The [vendible? venerable?] friend, is doing all he can here with his assistants to promote Deane’s [charges?].” French “faith, & friendship” count for nothing: “Interest, is the sole guide”, and the nation begins to talk against the alliance.
  • Letter 2, 22 April 1779. Gérard “is suspected here of hav[in]g been concernd with Deane & others, in merchandise, Contracts &c”; in Lee’s opinion the Doctor, the French consul, Gérard, Deane, Chaumont and Beaumarchais are all interested together.
  • Letter 3, 27 April 1779. Since Gérard’s recall people talk of his jobs with Deane and Beaumarchais; a certain person “is a mere instrument in the hands of those whom the court, set over him”; La Luzerne and Marbois will not stir up parties.
  • Letter 4, 21 May 1779. Spain has fifty ships of the line ready but has not declared. Lee does not want “2000 French [troops?]” brought over: “I am by no means satisfyd they do not wish for a footing.” “Never will our affairs go well, while Benjamin is here”, and a new man should be sent. Whether to present his piece against “the old Doctor” “will entirely depend upon the part taken by John Adams”.
  • Letter 5, 27 September 1779. On the anonymous letter of abuse received in Congress, “the wicked manoeuvre of M. Gerard”, a man (name unread) whose design is to be “alternately governor & delegate”, and plans for the family’s sons.
  • Letter 6, 29 February 1780. “Do you think it practicable to get myself, appoint[ed] from yr state, to Congress.”

Some things are inference only, because the code does not carry them: that “the former” is Gérard, that the French consul is Holker, and who is meant by “he is the servant of this court, not of his country” (the sentence before is about the new man to be sent). “Benjamin”, “the Doctor” and “the old Doctor” are presumably Franklin, and “Samuel” presumably Samuel Adams: identifications from context, not from the code. The full readings, letter by letter, are in the Full texts below.

04 How sure it is

  • Blind re-derivation. A second agent, shown none of the reading, chose the edition and rule from Tomokiyo’s groups alone and looked up 54 groups: the same word in 43, and in 29 of the 30 groups chosen by a rule written down first. The nine “other” cases were all groups already marked doubtful.
  • Controls. Page +1, page −1, the other column and the 1781 printing give 0 or 1 hit out of 71 in the two 71-group gloss tests (0 of 19 in the third, which had no 1781 control).
  • The received copies. The copies of letters 2, 3 and 4 that Richard Henry Lee received carry an old decipherment written between the lines, found only after this reading was made. Scored before any regrading, it agrees in 63 of the 72 groups where it can be compared; the nine differences are the interliner’s guesses, not dictionary words. Who wrote the interlinings, and when, is not settled.
  • Richard Henry Lee’s own letter. His coded letter of 22–23 May 1779, printed decoded by Burnett and Smith, gives the printed word in 14 of 15 groups under the same rule.
  • The weak step is reading the figures. Three held-out tests on other Lee letters glossed at the time missed their 80% mark (54 of 71, 55 of 71, 10 of 19); in each, most misses were misread figures, not wrong look-ups. On the letter book itself, two blind readers read all 166 groups again: the transcription was wrong in 1 group. A figure misread the same way by every reader would not be caught.

All image readings, ours and the checks’, were made by AI agents. No person has checked them.

05 Who read this before

Edmund C. Burnett identified the book in 1917 as “probably” Entick’s dictionary, worked from a 1782 edition, and left a typed partial key to the Lee code, filmed on roll 1 of the Lee Family Papers microfilm (1966). It gives a value for 39 of our 119 distinct groups: the same word for 33, another form for 3 and a different word for 3. Satoshi Tomokiyo (Cryptiana) identifies the system and the dictionary, gives about a dozen groups and lists the letters of 29 March and 27 April 1779; he does not decode these letters. The interlined decipherment on the received copies of letters 2–4 has not been printed, as far as was found, but it is older than this work, so no novelty is claimed for those three letters. Ralph Weber’s p. 53 (1979) and Louis Potts’s biography of Lee (1981) were not seen in full.

06 What remains uncertain

Unread (4): 331b6-d (letter 3, “almost [...]d the French”; the entry is Seemly, and the received copy writes the same group with nothing above it); 250?12 (letter 5, heavily corrected, column under a blot); 455a15 (letter 5, “I suspect [...] exceedingly”; the entry is German, which names nobody here); 293b13 (letter 5, “places of [...]”; the entry is Profferer, and profit would be 293b18).

Doubtful (14):

  • Letter 1: [Bishop?] and [former?] (the page digits are cut off at the scan’s left edge and supplied from sense); [vendible? venerable?] (written 390a15, Vendible; Venerable would need XXV); [charges?] (we read 82, both blind readers 12, which has no entry); Frenchified [French?] (XXV is clear; French is one line up); [considerableness?] (the third figure could be 8 or 0).
  • Letter 2: [exertions?] (page under a blot; 152 only on the received copy).
  • Letter 3: [chaplain? character?] (the letter book writes 82,b,XXI, the received copy 82,b,XXXI).
  • Letter 4: [troops?] twice (both copies write 384,b,XXVI-s, Trot; Troop is XVI); [you?] (both copies write 426b29, Young; You is 28); [contempt?] (the letter book reads 100, Conspicuously; the received copy 101, Contempt).
  • Letter 5: [Gerard? Thomas?] (digits and column letter under a blot); [war?] (the numeral runs off the edge after X).

No received copy of letters 1, 5 and 6 was found on the Lee Family Papers microfilm or in the catalogues searched. The 1777 printing of Entick, if it differs, was not seen.

## Reading

I have received since my last pretty full accounts from Mr Lovell of the proceedings at Philadelphia. I cannot
conceive how it coud have been agreed to receive an anonymous Letter of abuse against two of their commissioners.
Anonymous abuse may be from a notorious Enemy, an interested or an unworthy person, & therefore deserving of contempt
instead of credit. — not seeing any probability of doing good here, I, will probably return to you in the spring. I do
not know whether without any all instructions was calculated to prevent [...] from[...] but certainly it must have
that effect.

Mr S. Adams has informd me that Mr J. Adams wrote him very warmly in my favor, & he convinced me before his
departure that he had done so here. I have sent you Copies of his Letters by a former opportunity which will be the
more important after the wicked manoeuvre of M. Gerard. The Supplies I see are announced as
arrived, but no mention made of the senders. It is by these omissions that the Public at large, are unacquainted with
our Services, while they are imposed upon by the boastings of our Enemies to give all the merit to them. I hope you &
Francis being at home will enable them to counteract the machinations of their Enemies, to whom absence gives an
infinite advantage. For my part I suspect [...] exceedingly. His design is you may depend to be alternately governor
& delegate, to wch he must think you are an obstacle & therefore wish to destroy our influence. Till you make it a
point, as they do, to get places of [...], as well as influence the struggle will be in vain. [Gerard? Thomas?] will come
immediately to you, & I think his plan should be to marry as soon as possible the best fortune he can find & commence
merchandise in the manner that yr observation of things shall point out as the most proper. A little more knowledge
of the world & marriage will be of service to him. If Arthur comes, Ludwell will also, if the former should be obliged
to stay he will send the latter in the Spring, who will be of use to his Father & studying the Laws of Virginia for a
year before he begins to practise as i[t] be necessary. This winter will I think decide on peace, or a long [war?] by
the interference of other powers among wch Russia is most to be feard. Farewell.

## Summary

Lee protests that Congress received an anonymous letter of abuse against two of its commissioners, says he will
probably come home in the spring, and calls Gérard's conduct a "wicked manoeuvre". He urges his brothers, at home,
to counter their enemies in Virginia, warns of a man (the name group is unread) who wants to be "alternately governor
& delegate", and gives family plans for two of the sons (the first name is a doubtful group, Gerard or Thomas; then Ludwell). Peace or a long war
will turn on other powers, Russia above all.

The Honble Richd H. Lee,   Dear Brother,   Paris, Feby 29th 1780
I expect to embark very shortly in the Alliance, & hope soon
to have the happiness of seeing you once more. Ludwell comes
with me, & Tom goes in a private Ship from Nantes, care having
been taken to engage all the passage in the Alliance, before I was in-
formd of her destination. This comes by the Marquis de la Fayette
who returns into our service, & will I hope be followd by a force suf-
ficient to strike some decisive blow. It is safer to leave the details to him
as I presume he will write you on his arrival. We are in great expec-
tation of the spanish fleet intercepting that of Rodney in his return from
Gibralter. Should this happen & his fleet receive a blow, it will crip-
ple the Enemy in their naval operations, on which all others depend.
Should Rodney escape, so hardy an enterprize happily atchieved will
raise their credit, reanimate their efforts, & do us most essential in-
jury. In that case too, the force which [struck word] [interl.: on] his failure, they must
send to protect their Islands will be turned against us. The uncertainty of these events
gives me great inquietude, & suggests a repetition of what I have always ad-
vised that you shoud remitt of no exertion on your part, that may render
us as independent as possible of events in Europe. My love to yr family; to
London & all our friends. Do you think it practicable to get 251b12, appoint[ed]
from yr 354a36, to 99a17. It will be of great importance. Think of it, in time.
                                                   Adieu
