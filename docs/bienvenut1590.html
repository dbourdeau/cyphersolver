Bienvenut to the duc de Nevers, Paris, February 1590

A news-letter from League Paris, copied in clear except for nine names and provinces coded by small numbers, deciphered in full with the key slip bound after it.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: complete

Credits · Contemporary key or decipherment (key or decipherment used)

At a glanceKnown key applied · 26 of 26 code numbers

The letter
Bienvenut to “Vostre Altesse”, Paris, the last day of February 1590: a copy, in clear except for code numbers for persons and provinces. The style and the content suggest the duc de Nevers.
Where
BnF français 3623 no. 23, ff. 35–36 (DECODE R9453, catalogued there as a key record); catalogue item 30, with the Champagne news-letters.
The cipher
A name code of nine numbers between 1 and 53: the King of Spain, the Pope, the King, Mayenne, Madame and M. de Nemours, Saint-Sorlin, the towns of the Union, the Duke of Parma.
How it was deciphered
The key is bound with the copy on f. 36r, and every number in the letter is on it.
What it says
Madame de Nemours walks Bienvenut through the Queen’s gallery and shows him Saint-Sorlin’s letters; the Pope and the King of Spain are close allies, and the League waits for papal troops to keep the war going.
Still open
Which reading of the two dual entries (15, 17) applies in each place, and the recipient, are taken from context.

This letter was filed with three news-letters of June 1590 that use a two-digit alphabet, a different cipher with its own page (Champagne news letters, June 1590); their numbers conflict with this key (there 35 is a, here Mayenne).

01 The letter and its key

Every number in the letter is on the slip, so the letter decodes end to end (grade H). It is addressed to “Vostre Altesse” and reports the court of the League in Paris:

[Madame de Nemours] me pourmena hyer par la gallerie du logis de la Reyne plus d’une heure… elle me monstra plusieurs avis qu’elle avoit eu de [M. de Saint-Sorlin]… J’ay advis… qu’il y a une tres estroicte intelligence entre [le Pape] & [le Roy d’Espagne]… le courrier que Mr le Legat despescha a Rome… pour entretenir la guerre jusques a ce que [le Pape] puysse envoyer 8 ou 10000 hommes de guerre avec lesquelz il baste l’ame a [Mayenne]…

02 What stays open

  • Numbers 15 (the towns of the Union, or Provence and Dauphiné) and 17 (the Duke of Parma, or Picardy) each carry two readings on the slip; the one used in each place is chosen from the context.
  • The recipient, “Vostre Altesse”, is probably the duc de Nevers (the volume is his correspondence); the letter does not name him.
  • The copy has 26 code numbers (15 on the recto, 11 on the verso), all nine values on the slip.
