At a glanceKnown key applied · complete

The letter
Bienvenut to “Vostre Altesse”, Paris, the last day of February 1590: a copy of two pages, in clear except for 26 code numbers for persons and provinces. The writer is probably Bienvenu, secretary of Charles-Emmanuel I of Savoy.
Where
BnF français 3623 no. 23, ff. 35–36 (DECODE R9453, catalogued there as a key record); catalogue item 30, with the Champagne news-letters.
The cipher
A name code of nine numbers between 1 and 53: the King of Spain, the Pope, the King, Mayenne, Madame and M. de Nemours, Saint-Sorlin, the towns of the Union or Provence and Dauphiné, the Duke of Parma or Picardy.
How it was deciphered
The key is bound with the copy on f. 36r and every number in the letter is on it. Both pages were transcribed line by line from the Gallica images on 26 October 2026, the doubtful words re-read at full resolution on 27 October, and the key applied to the whole text.
What it says
Madame de Nemours wants her son in Spanish service or back in his government; the Pope, Spain and Mayenne have agreed that Spanish aid will pass through the Pope; Mendoza and Moreo keep Bienvenut out of the council, and he means to leave Paris in March.
Still open
Four doubtful words and a doubtful heading; which value of the two dual entries (15, 17) applies in two places; the places Roccas and Vier; and the recipient, probable but not proven.

This letter was filed with three news-letters of June 1590 that use a two-digit alphabet, a different cipher with its own page (Champagne news letters, June 1590); their numbers conflict with this key (there 35 is a, here Mayenne).

01 The letter and its key

Every number in the letter is on the slip, so every code decodes (grade H). The copy marks twice where its original was in cipher (“Ce qui suyt mys en chiffre”, “Et apres en chiffre”, f. 35v): the copyist wrote those passages out in clear and left only the names in numbers.

02 The reading

Both pages were transcribed line by line from the full-resolution Gallica images (canvases 79–80) on 26 October 2026: 33 lines on the recto, 35 on the verso, 1,259 words, abbreviations expanded and struck words kept (texts/n23_transcription.md). The 26 code numbers stand where the earlier list of 14 October put them. With the codes resolved, 99.6% of the words read as sense (963 words, none unread, four doubtful, after a second pass at full resolution on 27 October that settled eight doubtful words and corrected two); the full French and an English translation are under Full texts below. Four passages:

[Madame de Nemours] me pourmena hyer par la gallerie du logis de la Reyne plus d’une heure … elle me monstra plusieurs avis qu’elle avoit eu de [St Sorlin], qui contenoient principallement que pour les émotions et pratiques qui se commencent a faire en celle province l’on appelle [Monsieur de Nemours] en son gouvernement avec toute l’instance qu’on se peult.

Madame de Nemours had asked Juan Moreo to have Philip II take her son into his service; Moreo did not answer a word, and she said in anger that Nemours might do best to withdraw into his government, where he could make himself sought after by whichever party survived. She also showed Bienvenut news of the siege of Grenoble, the demolition of the castle of Gex and the siege of Bonne, “qui nous travaille grandement”.

J’ay advis … qu’il y a une tres estroicte intelligence entre [le Pape] et [le Roy d’Espaigne], et que despuys l’arrivée du Legat il a esté avisé entre luy, [Monsieur du Maine] et les Ambassadeurs de [15] que … la pluspart du secours que [le Roy d’Espaigne] fera a ceste cause se donnera par voye du [Pape], tant d’hommes que d’argent, et que soubz main [le Roy d’Espaigne] l’en remboursera avec les interestz a taux de banque, et par ce moyen la guerre se fera au nom du [Pape].

If the Pope sends forces from Italy, “the game will be played out entirely between [the King of Spain], [the Pope] and [Mayenne]” without the Altesse, unless the Altesse acts in Provence and Dauphiné. Panigarola explained to Bienvenut why Moreo and Don Bernardino de Mendoza had shut him out: they feared he wanted to “mettre le nez dans le conseil”.

[Ce qui suyt (?) mys en chiffre:] Qui me faict resouldre, voyant que je suys icy inutille a son service … de me mettre en chemin a la premiere commodité que je pourray avoir de passer et m’en retourner, qui pourra estre a mon avis dans tout le moys de mars prochain, que j’entens que Monsieur de Nemours s’en ira avec quelques trouppes en son gouvernement.
[Et apres en chiffre:] Si pour la guerre que vostre altesse a presentement sur les bras elle avoit besoin de 1000 ou 1200 harquebuziers, m’en donnant avis, je luy en ferois recouvrer des meilleurs de [17].

03 Who is “Vostre Altesse”?

The letter is in the Nevers papers and was first taken as written to him. The full text points to Charles-Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy. Bienvenut signs as the Altesse’s “subject”; Gex and Bonne, Savoyard places attacked by Geneva and Bern in 1589–90, are a siege “qui nous travaille”; the remedy he proposes lies in Provence and Dauphiné, where Savoy intervened in 1590; and the Altesse has “ministres” in League Paris whom Spain keeps out. Nevers was with Henri IV in 1590.

The writer has a name in the Savoyard record. The letters of René de Lucinge, the Duke of Savoy’s ambassador in France, mention “le sieur de Bienvenu, qui est icy” in 1585, sent on League business, and their editor Alain Dufour calls “le secrétaire Bienvenu” the side of the Duke’s policy committed to the League. A Savoyard secretary already working with the League in 1585 is the natural author of this report from League Paris in 1590. The recipient is therefore probably Charles-Emmanuel I, and the copy in fr. 3623 an intercept or a copy that reached Nevers. It is not proven until the hand or a copy in Turin is matched.

04 What stays open

  • Doubtful words (grade C), with folio and line: fer[r]oit (35r.11, ending written over), empieter (35r.29, the letter after p looks like r), baste l’ame (35v.5), me (35v.12), and the heading “Ce qui suyt mys en chiffre” (35v.20). The second pass corrected two first readings: “parmy les aff[air]es de ce Royaume” (35r.24) and “de bon lieu” (35r.26, where only “ne part” is struck).
  • Roccas and Vier (35r.22): the letters are certain, the place and its captor are not identified. Cramer and Dufour’s history of the Geneva–Savoy war of 1589–93 is the book to check; M. de Riddes (35v.22) is also unidentified.
  • The dual entries: 15 reads as Provence and Dauphiné at 35v.9 (a place where the Altesse can act) and 17 as Picardy at 35r.30 and 35v.19; at 35r.28 (“les Ambassadeurs de 15”) and 35v.33 (“des meilleurs de 17”) the context does not choose, and both values are kept.
  • The recipient: probable, not proven (section 03).

Paris, 28 Feb 1590, BnF fr. 3623 no. 23, ff. 35r-v (key f. 36r)

Bienvenut reports from Paris to 'Vostre Altesse' (probably Charles-Emmanuel I of Savoy, whose secretary Bienvenu was) that Madame de Nemours wants her son Nemours in Spanish service or back in his government; that news has come of Grenoble, Gex and the siege of Bonne; that the Pope, Spain and Mayenne have agreed Spain's aid will pass through the Pope, who is to send 8 or 10,000 men; that Mendoza and Moreo keep the Altesse's ministers out of affairs; and, in cipher, that he means to leave Paris in March with Nemours's troops, and can raise 1,000-1,200 arquebusiers.

## Doubtful readings (second pass, 27 Oct 2026, native-resolution crops of every doubtful word)

Settled at native resolution:
- 35r.04 "émotions": é-m-o-t-i-o-n-s, the final s long; certain.
- 35r.22 "Roccas", "Vier": every letter certain; the place and the captor are not identified (see NOTES).
- 35r.24 "aff[air]es": "affes" with a superscript abbreviation sign, after the struck "françoys": "parmy les affaires de
  ce Royaume". Corrects the first pass's "affect[ionné]s".
- 35r.26 "de bon~~ne part~~ lieu": the stroke starts inside "bonne" and runs through "part", so the copyist turned
  "de bonne part" into "de bon lieu". Corrects the first pass's unread word.
- 35r.31 "q[ue]": a q with a long tail, the period's repeated "que" after "d'ores en là".
- 35v.05 "a" (qu'il a / d'accabler): a plain a, as written.
- 35v.22-23 "stylé", "cogneu", "occurran[ce]s": all letters clear; the last has a superscript ending.

Still doubtful:
- 35r.11 "fer[r]oit": "fer" then an ending written over another (r + oit?); the conditional fits the sentence.
- 35r.29 "empieter": the letter after p is r-shaped ("empretes"); "empieter 17" (encroach on Picardy) is the sense.
- 35v.05 "baste l'ame": b or h, and "ame" has no visible r or s; "haste l'arme" and "baste l'asne" were tried and do
  not fit the strokes better.
- 35v.12 "po[ur] me": "p" with superscript, then m or n with a hat and e.
- 35v.20 the heading: "Ce" + q with a long stroke + "suytte"/"suyt e[st]" + "mys en chiffre".

# Bienvenut to "Vostre Altesse", Paris, 28 February 1590 (BnF fr. 3623 no. 23, ff. 35r-v)

## Reading

[f. 35r, 1] [Madame de Nemours] me pourmena hyer par la gallerie du logis de la Reyne plus d'une heure, la ou elle me
discourut plusieurs choses, entre autres elle me monstra plusieurs avis qu'elle avoit eu de [Monsieur de St Sorlin], qui
contenoient principallement que pour les émotions et pratiques qui se commencent a faire en celle province l'on
appelle [Monsieur de Nemours] en son gouvernement avec toute l'instance qu'on se peult. Que pour rien du monde elle ne
veult plus laisser retarder son partement, car elle veult que d'ores en là il assoie tout le fondement de sa fortune
sur son gouvernement.

[f. 35r, 8] Elle s'est plainte a moy que parlant au Seigneur Moreo, elle luy avoit faict entendre que se trouvant
[Monsieur de Nemours] presentement sans maistre elle desireroit qu'il pleust au [Roy d'Espaigne] de s'en vouloir servir,
que s'il luy plaisoit le retirer a son service, et a luy de le moyenner, elle feroit (?) qu'il serviroit avec tant de
fidelité que sa majesté en auroit tout contentement. Et sur ce elle dict que le Moreo ne luy avoit pas respondu une
seule parolle, dequoy elle demeura tres indignée, et me dict quasi comme en colere que ce seroit paraventure la bonne
fortune de [Monsieur de Nemours] de se retirer en son gouvernement, et là y faire comme il pourroit, puys que les moyens
luy failloient de tous costez. J'ay faict consideration la dessus et ay estimé qu'elle veult paraventure dire que par le
moyen de son gouvernement il aura tousjours en main dequoy se faire rechercher et respecter ou de l'un ou de l'autre des
partys au cas de succombance de l'un d'eux.

[f. 35r, 21] Elle me monstra encore un advis contenant le siege de Grenoble, la prise de Roccas par Vier, la ruyne
et demolition du Chasteau de Gex, et le siege de Bonne, qui nous travaille grandement.

[f. 35r, 24] Monsieur le Legat se trouve bien empesché parmy les affaires de ce Royaume, voyant la hayne mortelle
que les françoys ont contre le nom Espaignol. J'ay advis de bon lieu et de personne qui le peult scavoir qu'il y a une
tres estroicte intelligence entre [le Pape] et [le Roy d'Espaigne], et que despuys l'arrivée du Legat il a esté avisé
entre luy, [Monsieur du Maine] et les Ambassadeurs de [Provence et Daulphiné, ou les villes de l'Union], que pour
contenter la France et pour oster l'opinion que l'on a conceu que [le Roy d'Espaigne] veuille empieter (?) [Picardie]
soubz pretexte ou de protection ou de la Religion, qu'il sera mieux que d'ores en là que la pluspart du secours que
[le Roy d'Espaigne] fera a ceste cause se donnera par voye du [Pape], tant d'hommes que d'argent, et que soubz main
[le Roy d'Espaigne] l'en remboursera avec les interestz a taux de banque, et par ce moyen la guerre se fera au nom
[f. 35v, 1] du [Pape]. Et qu'il soit vray, j'ay sceu veritablement par voye de celluy de qui j'ay escrit a vostre Altesse
par une mienne precedente que le courrier que Monsieur le Legat despescha a Rome portoit charge de faire venir
promptement argent comptant pour entretenir la guerre jusques a ce que [le Pape] puysse envoyer 8 ou 10000 hommes de
guerre, avec lesquelz il baste l'ame (?) a [Monsieur du Maine] et les porte sans qu'il a d'accabler [le Roy].

[f. 35v, 6] Si nous voyons que [le Pape] se resolue a cela et qu'il mande forces d'Italie, il ne faut plus doubter que le
jeu ne se joue entierement entre [le Roy d'Espaigne], [le Pape] et [Monsieur du Maine] sans que vostre Altesse y aye
participation, sinon qu'elle y remedie en [Provence et Daulphiné], ou qu'elle se voulut accorder de l'autre party, qui
seroit a mon avis le plus mauvais conseil.

[f. 35v, 11] Je ne scay quel jugement faire d'un discours que le sieur Panigarol m'a faict des occasions que le Moreo luy
dict avoir heu pour me (?) faire la sortye que D. Bernardin et luy me feirent, laquelle il dict avoir esté parce qu'il
prevoyoit que je voulloys mettre le nez dans le conseil et entendre les affaires, et qu'il n'estoit bon que tant de gens
sceussent ce qui se traitte. Je juge qu'ilz n'ont envye sinon de tenir et moy et tous les autres Ministres de vostre
Altesse les plus reculez et esloignez des affaires qu'ilz pourront, de peur qu'elle (de qui ilz ont le grand coeur pour
suspect) n'aye le vent des entreprises et menées qu'ilz font en [Picardie].

[f. 35v, 20] [Ce qui suyt (?) mys en chiffre:] Qui me faict resouldre, voyant que je suys icy inutille a son service,

donna commencent ja fort a me manquer, de me mettre en chemin a la premiere commodité que je pourray avoir de passer et

1000 ou 1200 harquebuziers, m'en donnant avis, je luy en ferois recouvrer des meilleurs de [Picardie, ou le Duc de Parme].

[f. 35v, 34] Du dernier febvrier 1590. A Paris. De Vostre Altesse treshumble et tres affectionné subject et serviteur,

# Bienvenut to "Your Highness", Paris, 28 February 1590 (BnF fr. 3623 no. 23, ff. 35r-v): English translation

[f. 35r] Yesterday [Madame de Nemours] walked me through the gallery of the Queen's lodging for more than an hour, where
she talked to me of many things. Among others she showed me several reports she had had from [Saint-Sorlin], which said
chiefly that because of the risings and intrigues beginning in that province, [Monsieur de Nemours] is being called
to his government as urgently as can be. Not for anything in the world will she let his departure be delayed any
longer, for she wants him from now on to set the whole foundation of his fortune on his government.

She complained to me that, speaking to Señor Moreo, she had let him understand that since [Monsieur de Nemours] now
found himself without a master she would wish it to please [the King of Spain] to make use of him; that if it pleased
him to take him into his service, and him [Moreo] to arrange it, she would see to it (?) that he would serve with such
fidelity that His Majesty would be entirely satisfied. And on this she said that Moreo had not answered her a single
word, at which she was very indignant, and she told me, almost in anger, that it might well be [Monsieur de Nemours]'s
good fortune to withdraw into his government and there do as he could, since means were failing him on every side. I
have considered this, and I judge she may mean that through his government he will always have the means to make
himself sought after and respected by one party or the other, should either of them go under.

She also showed me a report covering the siege of Grenoble, the taking of Roccas by Vier (neither identified), the ruin and
demolition of the castle of Gex, and the siege of Bonne, which troubles us greatly.

The Legate finds himself very much hampered amid the affairs of this kingdom, seeing the mortal hatred the
French have for the Spanish name. I have word from a good source, and from a person in a position to know, that there is
a very close understanding between [the Pope] and [the King of Spain], and that since the Legate's arrival it has been
agreed between him, [Mayenne] and the ambassadors of [Provence and Dauphiné, or the towns of the Union] that, to satisfy
France and remove the belief that [the King of Spain] wants to encroach (?) on [Picardy] under pretext either of
protection or of religion, it will be better from now on that most of the help [the King of Spain] gives to this cause
be given through [the Pope], in men as well as money, and that [the King of Spain] will secretly repay him with interest
at the bankers' rate; and in this way the war will be waged in the name [f. 35v] of [the Pope]. And that this is true I
have learned for certain, through the man of whom I wrote to Your Highness in an earlier letter, that the courier the
Legate sent to Rome carried orders to have ready money sent promptly to keep the war going until [the Pope] can send
8 or 10,000 men of war, with whom he may put heart (?) into [Mayenne] and carry them on, without his having to crush
[the King].

If we see [the Pope] resolve on this and send forces from Italy, there is no more doubt that the game will be played out
entirely between [the King of Spain], [the Pope] and [Mayenne] without Your Highness having any part in it, unless you
take your remedy in [Provence and Dauphiné], or else choose to come to terms with the other party, which in my opinion
would be the worst counsel.

I do not know what to make of what Monsieur Panigarola told me of the reasons Moreo gave him for the affront (?) that
Don Bernardino and he dealt me: he says it was because he foresaw that I wanted to put my nose into the council and
learn its business, and that it was not good for so many people to know what is being negotiated. I judge that they want
only to keep me and all Your Highness's other ministers as far back and as far from affairs as they can, for fear that
you (whose great heart they hold suspect) should get wind of the enterprises and plots they are running in [Picardy].

[What follows (?) put in cipher:] This makes me resolve, since I see I am useless to your service here, inasmuch as the
diligence and fidelity of Monsieur de Riddes, who is very practised and very well known here, is quite enough to
keep you informed of what happens, and since I also see that the means Your Highness gave me are already beginning to
run short, to set out at the first opportunity I can have to cross and return, which in my opinion may be at any time in
the coming month of March, when I hear that Monsieur de Nemours will go with some troops to his government. Otherwise I
shall find myself so committed and cornered that I shall be here until next winter, for want of being able to cross in
safety.

[And afterwards in cipher:] If for the war Your Highness now has on your hands you needed 1,000 or 1,200 arquebusiers,
on your sending me word I would get you some of the best of [Picardy, or the Duke of Parma].

Paris, the last day of February 1590. Your Highness's most humble and most devoted subject and servant, Bienvenut.
