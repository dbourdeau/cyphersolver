Noailles to the Cardinal de Lorraine, Venice, 1558

A French ambassador’s letter with two short cipher blocks whose decipherment of the time stands in the margin, half hidden in the binding; the cipher itself has not yet been worked.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026 · updated 27 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained · On the map

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The letter
François de Noailles, French ambassador at Venice, to the Cardinal de Lorraine, 13 November 1558: news of the Levant galleys, Candia and Corfu, with three cipher blocks.
Where
Bibliothèque nationale de France, français 3151 no. 33, ff. 59–60v (Gallica btv1b9059865k, views 60–62); catalogue item 10, with two other letters.
The cipher
Invented symbols, 262 in all in three blocks (Michael Brackx’s count), in an alphabet different from La Guiche’s of 1551.
How it was tried
Checked here for a decipherment: the court’s is written beside each block, in the inner margin of f. 60r and the outer margin of f. 60v. Michael Brackx has transcribed the blocks and largely recovered the key from the glosses (issue #113); not yet checked here.
Still open
The start of every gloss line on f. 60r, lost in the gutter, and a check of Brackx’s reading.

This page was split on 14 October 2026 from the joint entry for catalogue item 10. La Guiche’s Rome letter of 1551 and Seure’s Lisbon letters of 1558 are in other ciphers, each with its own page: La Guiche to Montmorency, 1551 and Seure’s Lisbon letters, 1558.

01 The letter and its marginal decipherment

The letter is in clear (the Levant galleys, Candia, Corfu) with three cipher blocks: two of three lines each on f. 60r, and one of seven lines on f. 60v, which Michael Brackx found (issue #113) and our first notes missed. The court’s decipherment is written beside each block, so the letter was deciphered when it arrived. On f. 60r it stands in the inner margin, and only part of it is legible, because the start of each line runs into the binding: « … mauluaise … e pauur[eté] … [cond]uitte q[u’il] donne a … affaires », « [gr]and seigneur … [entre]prend sur luy et le … de la Tou[ss]aint ». (Our earlier reading of these glosses had « gouvernement », « ne luy » and « de la fortune »; Brackx corrected them.) On f. 60v the gloss stands in the outer margin and is nearly complete.

02 What remains open

  • The hidden start of each gloss line. It needs the volume opened flat, which only the BnF can do.
  • The cipher itself. 262 symbols in three blocks. Michael Brackx reports a key recovered from the glosses and a French language model, with 246 of the 262 symbols given a value; we have asked for his files and have not yet reproduced it. Where the f. 60v gloss is legible over unread groups, it gives cribs for the gaps. The printed edition of Noailles’s embassy (Vertot, 1763) has not been searched.
