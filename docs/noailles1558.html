Noailles to the Cardinal de Lorraine, Venice, 1558

A French ambassador’s letter with two short cipher blocks whose decipherment of the time stands in the margin, half hidden in the binding; the cipher itself has not yet been worked.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

At a glanceNot solved · decipherment of the time partly legible

The letter
François de Noailles, French ambassador at Venice, to the Cardinal de Lorraine, 13 November 1558: news of the Levant galleys, Candia and Corfu, with two cipher blocks of three lines each.
Where
Bibliothèque nationale de France, français 3151 no. 33, ff. 59–60 (Gallica btv1b9059865k, views 60–61); catalogue item 10, with two other letters.
The cipher
Invented symbols, about 150 in all (an estimate by eye), in an alphabet different from La Guiche’s of 1551.
How it was tried
Only checked for a decipherment: the court’s is written beside each block in the inner margin.
Still open
The start of every gloss line, lost in the gutter, and the cipher itself, not yet transcribed.

This page was split on 14 October 2026 from the joint entry for catalogue item 10. La Guiche’s Rome letter of 1551 and Seure’s Lisbon letters of 1558 are in other ciphers, each with its own page: La Guiche to Montmorency, 1551 and Seure’s Lisbon letters, 1558.

01 The letter and its marginal decipherment

The letter is in clear (the Levant galleys, Candia, Corfu) with two short cipher passages of three lines each on f. 60r. The court’s decipherment is written beside each block in the inner margin, so the letter was deciphered when it arrived. Only part of the glosses is legible, because the start of each line runs into the binding: « … mauluaise … gouvernement … affaires », « … grand seigneur … ne luy … de la fortune … ».

02 What remains open

  • The hidden start of each gloss line. It needs the volume opened flat, which only the BnF can do.
  • The cipher itself. About 150 symbols, not yet transcribed. The legible gloss words would serve as cribs for a solve, and a solved key would restore the hidden parts of the glosses. Noailles’s other letters and the printed edition of his embassy have not been searched.

03 Sources
