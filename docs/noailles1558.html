Venice → the Cardinal de Lorraine · symbol cipher with a marginal decipherment · 13 November 1558

Noailles to the Cardinal de Lorraine, Venice, 1558

Go to the full text ↓

A French ambassador’s letter with three short cipher blocks whose decipherment of the time stands in the margins, half hidden in the binding on one page. Michael Brackx recovered the key from those margins and deciphered the first block in full and the other two in part; Andrew Beling checked his transcription sign by sign on the images.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026 · updated 12 November 2026

01 The letter and its marginal decipherment

The letter is in clear (the Levant galleys, Candia, Corfu) with three cipher blocks: two of three lines each on f. 60r, and one of seven lines on f. 60v, which Michael Brackx found (issue #113) and our first notes missed. The court’s decipherment is written beside each block, so the letter was deciphered when it arrived. On f. 60r it stands in the inner margin, and only part of it is legible, because the start of each line runs into the binding: « … mauluaise … e pauur[eté] … [cond]uitte q[u’il] donne a … affaires », « [gr]and seigneur … [entre]prend sur luy et le … de la Tou[ss]aint ». (Our earlier reading of these glosses had « gouvernement », « ne luy » and « de la fortune »; Brackx corrected them.) On f. 60v the gloss stands in the outer margin and is nearly complete, though soft at the resolution Gallica gives.

02 The key and what reads

Michael Brackx transcribed the three blocks from the Gallica images, giving each sign a working label, and recovered the key in two steps: an alignment of the signs with the words of the marginal gloss, then a French 5-gram model to fill the values the gloss does not fix. He worked with an AI coding assistant, as his notes state. The key is homophonic: e, a, u, i, r, s and t each have several signs. Three signs (a slash, a thin upright and a box) are nulls, and two stand for the words qui and et.

Counted from his files: 272 sign tokens of 76 kinds; 12 are nulls; of the other 260, 244 have a value (93.8%). Of those, 232 rest on firm values, fixed by two words or by one word and the gloss, and 12 on tentative values with a single context. Sixteen signs of 15 kinds have no value. (Earlier versions of this page gave 262 and 246: 262 counted two words written in clear inside the blocks, and 246 came from another round of his work.)

What the blocks say, in his reading:

  • Block A reads as one clause: [po]ur le maulvais ordre et pauurete u peu … conduitte qu’il donne a ses affaires (one sign between peu and conduitte has only a tentative value), “for the bad order and poverty, or little conduct, that he gives to his affairs”. The subject is not in the cipher. The legible part of the gloss has the same words.
  • Block B is fragments: five large signs without a value, then entreprand, sur luy et le, the clear word Comme, and le iour [de] la Toussaint. The gloss has [gr]and seigneur before entreprend, so the opening signs probably carry the Sultan’s title; that is an inference from the gloss, not a value.
  • Block C builds a sentence with holes: mais ie doubt pour le singulier plaisir a prouffitt qu’ilz ont … es richesses ilz ne viennent … ny plus hardiz ny … ritailles qu’ilz ont … aux aultres … des passees, “but I doubt that, for the singular pleasure and profit they have … they come … any bolder or … victualled”, running into the clear tant en Hongrie qu’en Italie.

How much reads depends on the count, and all the counts are under the 95% bar. The project’s measure, which treats every tentative value, supplied letter and letter inside an unread stretch as a hole, gives 0.548 (51 words, 42 holes). Counting each unread stretch once gives 0.708 (51 words, 21 holes). Brackx’s own figures are about 0.75 of words on firm values and 0.84 with the tentative ones.

03 The checks

  • The decode. Rebuilt here with our own script from his transcription and key: all 272 sign tokens give the value in his decode file. His key search itself was not rerun; one of its two scripts needs a 5-gram table that is not among his files.
  • The transcription. Andrew Beling (pull request 423, 11 November 2026, with AI agents) located each of the 272 signs on the image with Brackx’s positions and judged its shape against his legend, without the key: 270 agree, 2 are unclear (a pale sign in block B and a sign in a blot in block C), none differs. He notes two pale marks that may be extra signs or show-through. An independent verification sampled about 80 of the signs again and found them as labelled. No decoded letter changes.
  • A statistical test. Scored with a French letter model of the period, the reading stands at −2.171 per letter; 200 random keys of the same structure reach −3.712 at best; a key refitted to shuffled copies of the text reaches −2.356 at best. The verdict is a pass. This is a test of fit: the key was built from the glosses and a French model, and no glossed text is left over for a held-out test.
  • Print. Beling searched Charrière’s Négociations de la France dans le Levant, volume II, in the Internet Archive’s OCR text: it prints Noailles’s Venice letters of 7 and 15 November 1558 but not this one of the 13th, so there is no printed plaintext. He searched the OCR only; the page images were not opened. Vertot’s edition of the Noailles embassies (1763) has not been opened; by Beling’s account it covers the embassies in England.

04 What remains open

  • The hidden start of each gloss line on f. 60r. It needs the volume opened flat, which only the BnF can do. The five large signs that open block B and the four signs after affaires in block A wait on it.
  • Three stretches of block C. Beling re-read the outer-margin gloss beside them (ilz ont d’autruy les, [vi]ennent p[ou]r a hazard, ravitailles qu’ilz) and proposes three tentative values from it: one sign for z (hazard), one for r (d’autruy; the sign may be a blot) and one for ost (ont osté aux). None is applied to the reading here. They do not close the gaps: the decode still has u l at the end of line C2, e r o where the gloss has pour a ha-, and t h where it has ravi-. This is open work, with cribs in hand; a sharper image of the margin would help, since d’autruy could not be confirmed at Gallica’s resolution.
  • Sixteen signs without a value and twelve on tentative values. Each occurs once or twice, and 272 signs offer no second context to test them.

The three cipher blocks, ff. 60r-60v read in part

Three short passages in cipher inside a clear letter of Francois de Noailles, ambassador at Venice, to the Cardinal de Lorraine. Block A blames someone's 'bad order and poverty, or little conduct, that he gives to his affairs'; the subject is not in the cipher. Block B has someone who 'undertakes on him' and All Saints' Day; its opening signs are unread. Block C doubts that, for the singular pleasure and profit they have, 'they' come any bolder or better victualled; three stretches of it are open.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (55% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

# noailles1558: English translation

Translation of `reading_original.md`, line for line, with the same marks: `[?…]` unread signs or letters, `{?…}`
tentative values, `[word]` supplied. The reading is *read in part*, so this translates a partial reading, not the

## Block A (f. 60r)

```
A1  [for] the bad order and poverty-
A2  {?} [or] little {?} conduct that he gives to his
A3  affairs [?3 signs] [?]
```

"… on account of the bad order and poverty, [or] little conduct, that he gives to his affairs …" The subject is not in
the cipher; the clear text before it is about news from the Emperor's lands (see Charrière II, 505, where Noailles
writes on 7 Nov that the Emperor "still gives bad order to his affairs").

[Correction added 12 Nov 2026, not by Brackx: the phrase is on p. 505 of Charrière II, but in the letter headed "Venise, 7 et 20 octobre 1558", not in the letter of 7 Nov (Andrew Beling, `beling/charriere_check.md`, confirmed on the OCR at the verification).]

## Block B (f. 60r)

```
B1  [?5 signs: the gloss has "…Seigneur"] undertakes, {?Monseigneur},
B2  on him, and the [?sign][?] AS [?sign] which {?is} the day
B3  [of] All Saints [?] {?are}
```
