Schöning’s secret mission to the Prussian court, 1783–84 (KHA A32-603)

Go to the full text ↓

Princess Wilhelmina’s secret agent wrote from Potsdam in a private symbol cipher; the decipherer’s own copies gave the alphabet, and the eleven letters he never copied are deciphered here.

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: complete · On the map

The letters
Lieutenant-Colonel G. von Schöning, sent to the court of Frederick II on Princess Wilhelmina’s behalf, to the advocate-fiscal J.C. van der Hoop and to the Princess, 28 July 1783 – 10 July 1784.
Where
Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, A32 Wilhelmina, inv. A32-603 (212 scans), with the sibling files A32-599, 601, 602 and 604. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
Running French in a private symbol alphabet, one sign per letter, a dot above to double it, and names and titles as numbers from a one-part alphabetical code with homophones (Berlin 106, Hertzberg 446–450, Prince 724–733, Roi 809–818, Sa Majesté 857–861).
How it was read
The alphabet was aligned on Van der Hoop’s ten clear copies (142 of 143 signs agree on a second pair); the code was built from his glosses, his “Déchiffrement” of 14 December 1783 in A32-599 and De Larrey’s key list, then applied to the eleven letters he never copied. Statistical test: pass.
What it says
“914 [Thulemeier] est un infâme. 446 [Hertzberg] sera envoyé. 818 [le Roi] hait et méprise de bon cœur 724 [le Prince], mais en revanche il aime tendrement 734 [la Princesse].” Later, a plan for a defensive and offensive alliance of the Republic with France, Spain and Prussia.
Still open
38 scattered code numbers, 62 scattered signs under blots or a later hand, and a fixed signature formula and an address panel too short to read.

01 The file

A32-603 holds 43 letters of Schöning, 28 July 1783 to 10 July 1784: 34 to Van der Hoop in French and 9 to the Princess in German, with notes of Frederick II and De Larrey’s memoranda. Twenty-four contain cipher. Van der Hoop made clear copies of ten of them, writing out the symbol text but leaving most code numbers as numbers; the other eleven he never copied. Schöning wrote on 26 January 1784 that Van der Hoop could not read his last letter for numbers missing from his alphabet.

02 The cipher

The running text is a private alphabet of hooks, strokes and loops, one sign per letter, with a dot above a sign to double it and a short tick to separate words written together. The three earliest letters use a variant in which one sign serves both o and d. Names, titles and a few words are numbers of two or three digits from a one-part code in alphabetical order, with several numbers per frequent name. The code table rests on Van der Hoop’s glosses, his “Déchiffrement de Lettres de S., communiqué à LL.AA.S.&R. le 14 Dec 1783” (A32-599), glossed copies in A32-602, and a seventeen-entry key list in De Larrey’s dossier (A32-604) marked “Byland”. 188 of the 226 numbers that occur now have a value.

03 The reading

Code values in brackets; Schöning spells by sound (“aumeur” for humeur, “hasar” for hasard), normalised here. Full text and translation in reading.md and reading_en.md.

No. 3, Potsdam, 8 August 1783. “… parler [au Roi]. [Thulemeier] est un infâme. [Hertzberg] sera envoyé. [Le Roi] hait et méprise de bon cœur [le Prince], mais en revanche il aime tendrement [la Princesse]. Dans peu un courrier exprès, et alors plus amplement. Le temps me manque, adieu.” — “… [Thulemeier] is a scoundrel. [Hertzberg] will be sent away. [The King] heartily hates and despises [the Prince], but he tenderly loves [the Princess].”

No. 1, 28 July 1783. The town is full of Dutchmen “entre lesquels il y en a des faux patriotes”; he told his contact to praise the reigning burgomasters of Amsterdam, and found him furious against Thulemeier.

24 December 1783. The privy council should take care that [the Stadholder] does not thwart Bérenger’s money negotiation, nor the intrigues he will play “pour faire un parti contre l’Empereur par ordre de [Sa Majesté]”.

January 1784, the alliance. “Il me paraît qu’une alliance pour quelques années avec [la France], l’Espagne et la Prusse serait non seulement utile mais même nécessaire à [la République]. Cette alliance faudrait être défensive et offensive, pour empêcher [l’Angleterre] et [l’Empereur] qu’ils ne profitent de la faiblesse de [la République].” The Orangists should assure the future French ambassador, de Vérac, that they are “rien moins qu’anglomanes”.

26–27 January 1784. The King is better, “ce n’est qu’une crampe aux bras”; the secret channel “n’est point découvert et ne le sera jamais”; and “rien ne me peut retenir que l’amour pour ma très chère princesse, car mon poste secret je l’abhorre depuis longtemps”.

10 July 1784. The revolt the Gazette placed at Petersburg was at Constantinople, stirred up by “notre” Gaffron, whom [the King] has recalled for Spandau.

04 Method

  • Alphabet. Subagents labelled the signs in ASCII (G01–G24) and aligned the 6 January 1784 original with its copy; the table was checked on the 25 November 1783 pair, 142 of 143 signs agreeing (letter_table.tsv, verify.md).
  • Code. Glosses, context and alphabetical bracketing (code_table.tsv), then a survey of the sibling files A32-599, 601 and 602 and the No. 4 glosses, which added about 128 values (code_values_new.tsv, applied by apply_values.py).
  • Reading. The eleven uncopied letters transcribed sign by sign (dec_*.tsv); every line checked with a French model (sense.py), and the weak lines of No. 1, Nos. 2–3 and the January letters re-read at five to six times magnification.
  • Measure. 4,088 tokens, 3,988 given a value (97.6%); 95.9% of letter signs on lines that read as French; 188 of 226 code numbers known. _validate.py: the reading scores −2.15 per letter against random keys at −5.89 (z 5.7) and a key fitted to shuffled text at −3.26: pass.

05 What remains open

  • 38 code numbers with no gloss anywhere in the five files (among them 475, 499, 500, 661, 950, 1066); 500 opens three letters, probably “J’ai” or a formula.
  • 62 scattered signs under blots or under figures a later hand wrote over No. 1.
  • Schöning’s signature formula (30–31 signs in a digit script) and the address panel of s029 (about 33 signs in a third script): one fixed short string each, no clear form in the file.
  • Readings rest on the sign tables and a model check; the decipherer’s copies confirm the tables, not the uncopied letters themselves.

# Schoning's cipher passages, 1783-84 (KHA A32-603): reading

The deciphered cipher passages letter by letter, with code numbers given their values in [brackets]
(`code_table.tsv`, `code_values_new.tsv`). Spelling is Schoning's, which is often by sound ("aumeur" = humeur,
"hasar" = hasard, "reucisoit" = reussissoit). Clear French around the cipher is marked (clear: ...). [?] = unread sign,
[500?] = code number without a value. Sign-level transcriptions: `dec_*.tsv`; measure: `measure.log`.

## No. 1, Potsdam (Brandenburg territory), 28 July 1783 (s001; dec_001)

[J'ai] l'honneur de vous mander que je suis arriver hier san(s) le moindre accident facheux [sur] le territoire
prussien, (clear: hormis que je me porte un peu mal ... Cette ville est remplie de Holl)andois, entre lesquels il y en
a des faux patriotes. [500?] parler monsieur ... (son) humeur du courrier ... lui ai dit de donner une louange bien
meritee aux bourguemestres regnant d'Amsterdam. (clear: Je suis arrive a temps pour l'empecher de faire le
contraire,) car le parti du duc avoit [?] donner le bien qu'ils viennent de faire. (clear: Il est furieusement
emporte,) cela veut dire l'humeur dont il me parle contre monsieur Thulemeier; (clear: il ne lui donne point de
pardon; mais je lui ai dit de rester tranquille a ce sujet, et d'etre bien precautionneux pour quelque temps) de peur
qu'il ne manque des agents bien intentionnes ... (clear: Je suis assure qu'il n'y manquera pas.) Il soupconna
monsieur votre frere le general d'etre du parti du [?]: [499?] de le desabuser ... avant qu'on ne soit tout a fait
sur de la bonne reussite ... [880 S.A.R.]. [475?]: Serenissime, et la malheureuse [801 Republique]. Cette idee ...
avec zele ... a la caisse du [403?] ...

## No. 2, 3 Aug 1783 (dec_003)

(j'espere etre en etat) de me presenter personnellement au roi; pour la correspondance tout est arrange. (Le 7 je
serai de bonne heure) a Potsdam, et je crois bien que [750 le Prince de Prusse] aura pris les arrangements necessaires
d'etre averti du moment de mon arrivee et de me faire avoir ses ordres.

## No. 3, Potsdam 8 Aug 1783 (dec_005)

[500?] parler [809 le Roi]. [914 Thulemeier] est un infame. [446 Hertzberg] sera envoye. [818 le Roi] hait et meprise
de bon coeur [724 le Prince], mais en revanche il aime tendrement [734 la Princesse]. Dans peu un courrier expres, et
alors plus amplement. Le temps me manque, adieu. Potsdam ce huit d'aout mil sept cent quatre-vingt-trois.

## 24 Dec 1783 (dec_129)

[Ma] mission ... [prier tout] le conseil prive d'etre bien sur leur garde, que [70 S.A.S.] ne contrecarre point
Berenger regardant la negociation d'argent pour ..., ni les intrigues qu'il jouera pour faire un parti contre
l'Empereur par ordre de [857 Sa Majeste].

## Jan 1784, the plan for an alliance (s172; dec_172_page)

Je crois que [S.A.S.] ferait tres bien s'il cherchait de persuader ses amis qu'ils [?] de Verac, futur
ambassadeur de [France] a [418 La Haye], par leurs avis, dans les [180 Etats?], qu'ils ne sont rien moins
qu'anglomanes; qu'au contraire ils sont parfaitement convaincus que l'amitie de [861 Sa Majeste] tres chretienne leur
est chere et absolument necessaire pour ... l'harmonie et la tranquillite de la [Republique]. Il faudrait se montrer
en toute occasion contraire a une alliance avec l'Angleterre, l'Autriche et la Russie. Au contraire il me parait
qu'une alliance pour quelques annees avec [350 la France], l'Espagne et la Prusse serait non seulement utile mais
meme necessaire a [801 la Republique]. Cette alliance faudrait etre defensive et offensive, pour empecher
l'[481 Angleterre] et l'[250 Empereur] qu'ils ne profitent de la faiblesse de [804 la Republique]. Par la elle aurait
le temps, les esprits etant tranquillises, ... d'ameliorer et fortifier les ports et les frontieres, redresser les
vrais abus dans la constitution, et de reformer ... les ministres. Sans une telle alliance la Republique court le
risque que l'[485 Angleterre] et l'[255 Empereur], la force des armes a la main, ... lui prescrivent ce qui leur
semblerait bon: l'[482 Angleterre] s'emparera premierement des possessions hollandaises ... et renouvellera le
traite, tant nuisible que deshonorant, ...; l'[291] ouvrira ... la navigation ..., et
s'emparera de toutes les possessions de [803 la Republique] ... La France seule [344] ... a cause du derangement de
leurs finances ... L'Espagne n'est presque en rien interessee ... Mais si ces trois puissances s'unissent par une
alliance defensive et offensive pour quelques annees avec [803 la Republique], alors les circonstances de ce temps
font que toutes ces quatre puissances y trouvent leurs interets, et, alliees ensemble, elles se donneront
prudemment les forces necessaires pour empecher les vues ambitieuses et nuisibles pour toute l'Europe de
l'[253 Empereur] et de la [Russie?]. Vous m'ecrirez a ce sujet.

## Jan 1784 (s173-s174; dec_173_pages)

... ce que les politiques de votre pays en jugent; et si on me laisse agir, je tournerai le tout au bien de [801 la
Republique] et de la maison d'Orange. Le changement du ministere en [85 Angleterre] ne changera point le projet forme
entre l'[252 Empereur] et le roi d'[82 Angleterre?] ... pour le necessaire de ma mission ... la bourse de [67 S.A.S.]

son interet et le sort de la maison d'Orange, qui me sont seuls chers, eu egard a [884 S.A.R.] (clear: le demandaient

Majeste] et de lui faire concevoir et agreer tout ce qui tend au bien de [805 la Republique] et de la maison
d'Orange ... Mais pourtant, si par hasard elle reussissait, voila les points apres lesquels je pourrais faire mon
decompte: ... eloigner le prejuge de monsieur Larrey, que je ne suis qu'un simple porteur de lettres ... (clear:
cela veut dire mon veritable secret, la necessite de) ma mission, (l'influence que j'ai sur l'esprit de [857 Sa
Majeste]) ... dans des affaires politiques ... avec tous les gens d'affaires du roi ... dans les affaires de
negociations ...

## 26-27 Jan 1784 and Feb 1784 (s175-s176; dec_175_pages)

P.S. [809 le Roi] se porte aujourd'hui beaucoup mieux, et dans le fond sa maladie n'est pas dangereuse, ce n'est

Petersbourg, l'Imperatrice, Constantinople, Gaffron, l'Imperatrice, Gaffron, Spandau ... [164 la Cabale] ... [766 la

# Schoning's cipher passages, 1783-84: English translation

Translation of `reading.md`. Code words in [brackets]; "..." marks clear text not repeated or signs unread.

**No. 1, 28 July 1783.** I have the honour to inform you that I arrived yesterday on Prussian territory without the
slightest unfortunate accident, except that I am a little unwell ... This town is full of Dutchmen, among whom there
are some false patriots ... [I] told him to give well-deserved praise to the reigning burgomasters of Amsterdam. I
arrived in time to stop him doing the contrary, for the duke's party had [tried to undo] the good they have just
done. He is furiously worked up, that is, the ill humour he speaks of against Monsieur Thulemeier; he gives him no
pardon; but I told him to keep calm on this subject and to be very cautious for some time, for fear that he might
lack well-intentioned agents ... He suspected Monsieur your brother the general of belonging to the party of the
[...]; [...] to undeceive him ... before one is quite sure of success ... [Her Royal Highness] ... Serene, and the
unhappy [Republic]. This idea ... with zeal ... the treasury of [...].

**No. 2, 3 Aug 1783.** (I hope to be able) to present myself in person to the King; for the correspondence everything
is arranged. (On the 7th I shall be early) at Potsdam, and I believe [the Prince of Prussia] will have made the
arrangements needed to be told the moment I arrive and to have his orders sent to me.

**No. 3, Potsdam 8 Aug 1783.** [...] speak [to the King]. [Thulemeier] is a scoundrel. [Hertzberg] will be sent away.
[The King] hates and heartily despises [the Prince], but on the other hand he loves [the Princess] tenderly. Shortly
an express courier, and then more fully. Time is short, adieu. Potsdam, the eighth of August 1783.

**24 Dec 1783.** [My] mission ... [to ask the whole] privy council to be well on their guard, so that [His Serene
Highness] does not thwart Berenger over the money negotiation for ..., nor the intrigues he will play to form a
party against the Emperor on the orders of [His Majesty].

**Jan 1784, the plan for an alliance.** I believe [His Serene Highness] would do very well to try to persuade his
friends that they should [show] de Verac, the future French ambassador at [The Hague], by their advice in the
[States], that they are anything but Anglomaniacs; that on the contrary they are perfectly convinced that the
friendship of [His] Most Christian [Majesty] is dear to them and absolutely necessary for ... the harmony and
tranquillity of the [Republic]. They should show themselves on every occasion opposed to an alliance with England,
Austria and Russia. On the contrary it seems to me that an alliance for some years with [France], Spain and Prussia
would be not only useful but even necessary to [the Republic]. This alliance should be defensive and offensive, to
prevent [England] and the [Emperor] from profiting by the weakness of [the Republic]. In that way it would have time,
once minds are calmed, ... to improve and fortify the ports and frontiers, to redress the true abuses in the
constitution, and to reform ... the ministers. Without such an alliance the Republic runs the risk that [England]
and the [Emperor], arms in hand, ... dictate to it what they please: [England] will first seize the Dutch possessions
... and renew the treaty, as harmful as it is dishonourable ...; [...] will open ... navigation ..., and seize all
the possessions of [the Republic] ... [France] alone ... because of the disorder of their finances ... Spain has almost
no interest ... But if these three powers unite in a defensive and offensive alliance for some years with [the
Republic], then the circumstances of the time make all four powers find their interest in it, and, allied together,
they will prudently give themselves the forces needed to stop the ambitious views, harmful to all Europe, of the
[Emperor] and of [Russia]. Write to me on this subject.

**Jan 1784 (s173-s174).** ... what the politicians of your country think of it; and if I am left to act, I shall turn
the whole to the good of [the Republic] and of the House of Orange. The change of ministry in [England] will not
change the plan formed between the [Emperor] and the King of [England] ... for the needs of my mission ... the purse of
[His Serene Highness] in a secret correspondence ... known to [His Serene Highness] ... that [His Majesty] be interested
in my mission, and that his interest and the fate of the House of Orange, which alone are dear to me, with regard to
[Her Royal Highness], absolutely required it, I would never have revealed myself ... out of love for [Her Royal
Highness] ... to watch over the mind of [His Majesty] and make him conceive and accept all that tends to the good of
[the Republic] and of the House of Orange ... But if by chance it succeeded, these are the points on which I could
make my reckoning: ... to remove Monsieur Larrey's prejudice that I am only a simple carrier of letters ... that is,
my true secret, the necessity of my mission, the influence I have over the mind of [His Majesty] ... in political
affairs ... with all the King's men of business ... in matters of negotiation ...

**26-27 Jan and Feb 1784.** P.S. [The King] is much better today, and at bottom his illness is not dangerous; it is
only a cramp in the arms. If the news that reached Holland was not written directly by Madame la Marquise de
Chatelet or by Count G. von Goertz, it still comes from that source ... will have told it to Monsieur [...] and
Schula will have written it to [Heiden]. As regards the correspondence, the channel is not discovered and never will
be ... of [the Prince]; here there must also be a regular exchange of letters ... there is no one of [the Cabal] who
knows me ... the confidence of [His Serene Highness] ... informed of much of what passes at foreign courts ... for the
interests of the House of Orange and of the Republic ... nothing can hold me but love for my very dear Princess, for I
have long loathed my secret post ... underlined letters of [His Serene Highness], [Her Royal Highness], [Van der
Hoop], [His Majesty] and [Schoning] ... that he be at [Berlin] ... to the new [...] of [France] ...

**10 July 1784.** The revolt the Gazette placed at Petersburg was really at Constantinople, stirred up by "our"
Gaffron; [His Majesty] recalled him, and he will be put in Spandau ([the Cabal], [Prussia], [the Stadholder], [the
King]).
