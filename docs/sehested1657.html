Christoffer Sehested in Vienna, 1657: the cipher letters of a secret mission

Go to the full text ↓

Denmark’s incognito envoy to the Emperor wrote home in numbers; a clerk’s gloss on one page gives the alphabet, and with it 27 letters now give German text, with the signs for persons and places only partly resolved.

One number per letter (2 to 96), colons between numbers and a cross between words, in German. Persons, places and set phrases are written as signs: capitals, Greek letters and doubled letters.

01 The letters

Christoffer Sehested left Copenhagen in secret on 12 December 1656 and spent ten weeks in Vienna incognito, as “baron van Hasselt”, negotiating an alliance between Denmark and the Emperor against Sweden with the prince of Auersperg and with the Spanish ambassador La Fuente. The unit in the Rigsarkivet holds his letters to Frederik III (“Sire”) and to the chancellor Christen Thomesen Sehested (“Monseigneur”), copies of his proposition to the Emperor and of a memorial handed to Auersperg, and second cipher copies of five letters. Some letters are wholly in cipher; from March on they are in clear German with long cipher passages.

The Danish chancery deciphered part of it at the time. Three letters have a clear copy on separate leaves (images 112–113 for L01, 157–158 for L18, 169 for L25), and five pages carry words or letters written between the lines (images 132, 136, 138, 140 and 171). Two more leaves were not used here. A further clear leaf of the time on images 114–115, a letter to the king, was not matched to a cipher letter. And the 19th-century slips on images 124–125 give German text of L04, with the signs left as signs (both according to the project’s sweep note); they were not compared with the decode. Further slips of the same kind lie at images 137 and 138. The remaining letters have no plaintext with them.

Two historians used the letters in the 19th century. Edvard Gigas (Grev Bernardino de Rebolledo, 1883) and J. A. Fridericia (Adelsvældens sidste Dage, 1894) summarise the mission in Danish, and Fridericia thanks Gigas for a decipherment of Sehested’s letters passed to him privately. That decipherment was never printed and was not seen here; Gigas quotes one German phrase of the cipher (“die Gemüther alhie nicht wenig ermunteret”, in L03). The German text of the cipher passages was not found in print, on DECODE, or in Tomokiyo’s or Lasry’s lists (searched 11 November 2026).

02 The key

The alphabet is a simple substitution: 2 l, 4 b, 5 e, 6 a, 7 h, 8 k, 9 d, 12 z, 13 o, 15 i/j, 16 p, 17 c, 20 q, 24 y, 25 m, 26 u/v, 30 f, 32 w, 36 x, 40 g, 48 t, 72 n, 80 r, 96 s, and 90 for s once. The same alphabet enciphers the French of the Prague letter (L27).

These values were not recovered by analysis. They stand on image 132, where the clerk wrote each letter over its number. The project’s own sweep of the unit on 11 November 2026 had already noted 19 of them and decoded the opening line of L03, and judged that the cipher “reads on sight”. Brice Dudout took 8 k, 16 p, 36 x and 90 s from the same gloss, found 20 q and 24 y later in the text (bequemliche, kayserliche), and checked the alphabet on the clear copy of L01: the first five lines of image 110 decode to the text of image 112 word for word. The key was therefore extended here, not recovered.

The signs are the harder part. Thirteen have a value given by a gloss or a clear copy of the time: Λ Eure Excellenz (the chancellor), Φ Königliche Majestät, Σ Kaiserliche Majestät, XX alliance, ZZ defensiv, YY offensiv, X Auersperg, aa Plenipotenz, gg Befehl, E the king of Poland, G the king of Sweden, W Bremen, m Pommern. About 25 more (the rows marked probable in the key table) were given a value by Brice Dudout from the sense of the sentence or from one attestation (Ψ allergnädigst, Ω unterthänigst, ii Schreiben, Y La Fuente, L die Staaten, xx Instruction and others). 26 sign forms, as marked in the reading, are open (b, H, bb, cc, Γ, EE, O, R, M, N, Q, F, J and rarer ones). No table of the signs is in the unit.

The sign X was first given here as the vice-chancellor Kurz, from a misreading of one clear copy. The images settle it. The clear copy of L01 writes “Fürst von Aursberg” at the three places where the cipher has X (image 112, line 5; image 113, lines 4 and 9–10), and the clear copy of L18 opens “hatt mihr der F: von Auersp: eine weitlaufftige deduction” where the cipher has “hat mir der [X] eine weitlauftige deduction” (image 157, right page, line 2). The gloss over X on image 132 is a ducal title, “Hertzog von S…”, whose last word was not read with certainty. No “Kurtz” was found on the clear leaves (images 112–113, 157–158, 169).

03 The count

The 27 letters hold 6,013 words spelled in numbers and 561 signs, 6,574 tokens in all. Counted strictly, a token is certain only when its letters decode without correction into a word that fits, or when it is a sign whose value a gloss or clear copy of the time gives. That is 5,903 words and 365 signs: 6,268 of 6,574, or 95.3%. The rest are 90 words where one figure had to be read otherwise (grssem for grossem), 19 words restored across a blot or the binding, 1 word unresolved, 152 signs whose value rests on the sense or on a single attestation, and 44 open signs.

Letter by letter, 15 letters reach 95% and 12 do not; the lowest is L05 at 90.1%. This is why the group is classed as deciphered in part and its key as partial. Brice Dudout’s own figure was 99.7%: it counts the spelled words only (certain and probable, 5,993 of 6,013) and leaves the signs out. The German of the spelled words is not in doubt; the difference is whether the value of a sign counts when it was guessed from the sense or rests on one attestation.

04 The glossed page

The ten lines in the picture above decode as follows; square brackets mark the signs and three words restored where the transcription could not read one figure; braces mark a word emended by one figure:

wiwol ich nicht unterlassen mit dem [X: Auersperg] bis anher {uber} allerhand uns betreffende sachen
insonderheit aber das [w: Bremen] fleissig zu conferiren so habe ich dennoch [befunden] das
er disesfals sich [nimahln] recht uertrewlich eusseren wollen furgebend das dergleichen
sachen erst nach [uolnzihung] der [XX: alliance] furzunehmen und alsdan desto leichtlicher
ein expedient darinnen getroffen werden konte

In English: “Although I have not failed to confer diligently with Auersperg until now on all sorts of matters that concern us, and on Bremen in particular, I have nevertheless found that he would never quite open himself in confidence on it, giving as his reason that such matters were to be taken up only after the alliance was concluded, and that an expedient could then be found the more easily.”

05 The checks

Brice Dudout set one test in advance. By his record, the protocol, the scorer and the key were hashed before twelve lines of image 145, which carry no gloss, were transcribed (318 numbers, 51 words). One run then compared the decode with 900 controls (permuted keys, random keys, shuffled numbers): a letter-model score of −2.023 per character against −3.693 for the best control, 0.569 of the words in a period word list against 0.078, and 1 number outside the key. It passed by the criteria written beforehand. Two reservations apply: the agent that transcribed those lines knew the key, as the protocol says, and the order of events rests on the contributor’s record alone (one commit, no outside timestamp).

The project verified the pull request independently on 12 November 2026. The hashes of the five frozen files match. A second decoder gives the same string for the held-out lines and the same text for all 27 letters and the 5 copies. The two exact scores could not be run again, because the model file and its corpus were not sent; with the repository’s own German model the held-out lines score −2.615 against −6.861 for the best of 500 permuted keys. Since the key comes from a gloss of the time, this test confirms the key and does not establish it.

Other evidence that the decode is right: the clear copies of the time for L01, L18 and L25 agree with it; the three cipher passages of the French letter L27 carry a letter-by-letter gloss that agrees with it; and the five letters that exist twice in cipher, transcribed separately, agree with their copies at 76% to 98% of the words (76% for L06, whose copy is cut by the binding). For these four letters with a clear copy or full gloss the text is, in effect, the contemporary decipherment checked against the decode. About 300 numbers of images 116 and 145 were compared with the film: no number differed, and one sign was corrected (Φ, not Ψ, on line 3 of image 145).

06 What the letters say

What follows comes from Brice Dudout’s summaries, one per letter; they are summaries, not translations. Only L03 and L27 were translated in full, at integration.

  • January. Auersperg is surprised that Sehested has come without full powers, then takes the matter up after conferring with La Fuente. The Emperor is not averse to the defensive alliance the king proposes but expects the draft from Denmark (L01, L02).
  • February. Rebolledo’s report on Danish armaments raises spirits in Vienna (L03). Sehested writes that ciphering has worn out his eyes (L04). The Emperor consents orally to Bremen passing to Denmark; Sehested asks whether to press for a written promise (L05, L06). On 18/28 February he puts his proposition to Ferdinand III in audience (L07, L08).
  • March. The Emperor wants a general offensive and defensive alliance and waits for the Danish full powers (L09 to L12). Sehested hands Auersperg a memorial: if Sweden made peace in Poland it would recoup itself “mit den spoliis des armen teutschlandes” (L13). In the letter of 21/31 March Auersperg explains that the Emperor has promised troops to Poland and calls that a sufficient diversion; Sehested answers “praeuenire melius als das praeueniri”, and Auersperg: “multa fiunt et cogitantur quae non dicuntur” (L18).
  • April and May. The Emperor dies and Sehested’s powers lapse (L19, L20). Hatzfeldt is ordered into Poland; an imperial councillor, Cridel or Lützow, may be sent secretly to Denmark (L22). Sehested’s merchants betray his name and errand to the Swedish resident (L24). The ministers want to carry on with the alliance begun under the late Emperor, and in May Vienna wishes to conclude a defensive one (L25, L26).
  • October, from Prague, in French. “cette cour se resoudra a la fin ou a une entiere cooperation ou bien a une puissante [diversion] en [Pommern] par les polonois” (L27).

Fridericia, from the Vienna protocols, gives the talk of 17 March to Auersperg. The clear copy of the time of L18 agrees: it names “der F: von Auersp:” (section 02).

07 What remains open

  • The signs. 152 tokens of about 25 signs have a value from the sense or a single attestation (grade I), and 44 tokens of about 26 sign forms have none. One by-sense value is doubtful on its face: in L03 the sign L, given as “Staaten”, does not fit a sentence where the Swiss are to submit to something France would set them. The sign X is Auersperg when masculine and “diversion” when feminine, perhaps two similar signs. The clear copies of L01, L18 and L25 were not yet compared with the decode sign by sign; they write out several values that are counted here as probable.
  • The archive’s key file. The Rigsarkivet lists “Christoffer Sehested keys 1650–70”. It is not scanned and was not seen; it may hold the table of the signs.
  • Emended and restored words. 90 words read with one figure changed, 19 restored, 1 unresolved (copeilig, in L14). Three of the emended words of L01 were looked at on image 110 and stand on the page as transcribed, so they are the encipherer’s slips; the others were not rechecked.
  • Two leaves not used. The clear leaf of the time on images 114–115 is not matched to a cipher letter, and the 19th-century slips on images 124–125 were not compared with L04.
  • The date of L03, read 28/7 on the page and probably 7/17 February.
  • The translation. There is no full English translation of 25 of the 27 letters.
  • The transcription was made by AI agents from the film, number by number. No person has checked it against the images beyond the sample above.
  • The test scores of the contributor cannot be re-run until the model file and corpus are supplied.
