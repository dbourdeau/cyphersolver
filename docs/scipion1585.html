Earlier reading of the Français 4698 letters. Paolo Rosson’s folder gonzaga-1590 covers the same cipher. By his own record, dated 3–4 October 2026, before George Lasry’s report of 10 October, he linked Français 4698 ff. 30r–31r (21 January 1590) and f. 104r (the sheet sent with the letter of 16 February 1590) to the decipherments made for the Duke of Nevers in 1590 in BnF Français 4702 (ff. 94r–95r and 97r–v), edited and translated them, named the sender as Cardinal Scipione Gonzaga, and rebuilt the cipher as a word code of 448 values in 14 lists. This project listed his page in a harvest on 14 October 2026 without matching it to this item, and recognised it on 16 November 2026 after Brice Dudout’s report (issue #708). By his own account the plain text of the two letters is the clerk’s, not a new decipherment; what he adds is the link, the editions, the rebuilt code, a test on Français 4696 and readings of two letters (24 and 31 March 1590) that have no period decipherment. This page claims no reading of its own and nothing here is withdrawn; George Lasry’s report does not cite Rosson’s work, and whether either knew of the other is not known. Details and limits under Other contributions.

The letters
Italian letters in a graphical cipher: from Scipion de Gonzague to the Duke of Nevers (Français 4696, 1585–1589), and a second letter in Français 4698 (1590). George Lasry leaves its sender and recipient not established; Paolo Rosson names the sender as Cardinal Scipione Gonzaga, writing to the Duke of Nevers (his reading, not checked here).
Where
BnF Français 4696 / 4698 (ff. 5r, 62r–v, 30r…), via Gallica.
The cipher
A key of 66 symbols and codes: 41 letter signs, 1 digraph and 24 nomenclature items.
How it was read
George Lasry recovered the key from adjacent plaintext: the interlinear plaintext on Français 4696 f. 5r and worksheets in 4698. This project checked one sample against Gallica and partly confirmed it.
What it says
The excerpt on f. 62v reports that a third person (“egli”) offers himself ready to serve the recipient, especially in defending or advancing the recipient’s position.
Earlier work
Paolo Rosson linked the Français 4698 pages to the 1590 decipherments in BnF Français 4702, edited them and rebuilt a 448-value code, 3–4 October 2026 by his record, before the report. This project did not re-measure either reading.
Credit
The result presented here is George Lasry’s, from his BnF cipher research report of 10 October 2026 (p. 51). Paolo Rosson (earlier reading), Nevers’s clerk of 1590 (the decipherments) and Brice Dudout (who pointed to them) are credited above.
Still open

Earlier reading found after the page was written (16 November 2026)

Brice Dudout pointed to Paolo Rosson’s gonzaga-1590 (text CC BY 4.0; made with a model, no palaeographer has checked it, by his own README). What it holds, by his record of 3–4 October 2026:

  • the decipherments of Nevers’s clerk of 1590 in BnF Français 4702 for Français 4698 ff. 30r–31r (ff. 94r–95r) and f. 104r (f. 97r–v), transcribed, edited and translated, and for f. 34r (f. 108r), aligned; and a decipherment of f. 17r–v on Français 4698 f. 105r itself, whose author his folder does not name;
  • a word code of two-digit figures in 14 lists (448 glossed values) and a symbol alphabet of about 45 signs;
  • a test on Français 4696 with an earlier table of 378 values, built before that volume was seen: of 587 units transcribed, 463 have their word in the table, and for 452 of those (97.6%) the gloss on the page is the table’s word (word and mark both agree for 408, 88.1%); a shuffled table gives 2 of 572 (the 448-value table came after);
  • readings, with gaps, of the letters of 24 and 31 March 1590 (fr. 4698 ff. 26v–27r, 36r–37r), which have no period decipherment;
  • the sender of the 1590 letters as Cardinal Scipione Gonzaga.

What this project checked on 16 November 2026, by a model and not by a person: the table has 448 values in 14 lists; the 452 of 463 figure (with the earlier table) adds up; Gallica Français 4702 f. 94r is a clear Italian decipherment in a clerk’s hand and its first lines match his transcription; his edition of the letter of 21 January 1590 contains the run George Lasry reads on f. 30v (“richiedermi … prieghi”). Not checked: the unit-by-unit alignments, the symbol alphabet, the shuffle test, whether George Lasry’s 24 nomenclature values are part of his lists, and which of George Lasry’s unread groups the clerk’s text fills. His editions (Italian text of the clerk with an English translation) are 21 January 1590 (f. 30r–31r) and 16 February 1590 (f. 104r); they are kept in his repository, not copied here, and this project has not graded them as a reading. His commit dates are his own; the dates he pushed them could not be read. This project’s own harvest of 14 October 2026 already listed his page without matching it. George Lasry’s report does not cite it; it does compare f. 17v with f. 105, the same page Rosson starts from.
