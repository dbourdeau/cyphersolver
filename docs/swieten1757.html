At a glanceExisting decipherment · complete

The letters
Three letters from Gottfried van Swieten at Bonn to Count Cobenzl, minister plenipotentiary in the Austrian Netherlands, reporting on the French armies in the Seven Years’ War. One is undated (R955); the others are of 14 January and 4 February 1759.
Where
DECODE R955, R957 and R956, with no decipherment recorded for them.
The cipher
An alphabetical list of letters and syllables with a few homophones. The 1759 table uses numbers 100–442; no key for it survives.
How it was read
R955 has the decipherment written under the numbers and shows the system. The 1759 table was rebuilt from the ciphertext alone by simulated annealing over alphabetical-order assignments scored by a French language model (one start in eight came out as French), then corrected by hand against the surrounding cleartext.
What it says
The convention leaving the revenues of Prussian Gueldre to the French, the principality of Moers handed to a prince without asking the Empress, and a sharp judgement of Soubise’s army.
Still open
About 21 of 553 groups in the 1759 letters, nearly all proper names.

01 The letter

R955, f. 141, undated, written while the French held Hanau (1757–58): 89 dotted groups, 202–586, with the decipherment written below them. DECODE holds two later letters of van Swieten to Cobenzl in the same file (R956, R957, 1759); they use another table, and are on their own page.

02 The 1757 table, from its own decipherment

The clear lines on f. 141 repeat the cipher word for word: “on a délibéré ici dans une députation de Magistrat sur le parti à prendre au cas que les François fussent obligés d’abandonner Hanau, on a osé opiner pour la neutralité et conclure même; on garde cependant un profond secret là-dessus comme de raison.” Aligning the two gives values that rise with the alphabet: a 202/203, ab 205, an 216, ar 220, as 223, at 224, au 228, be 241, c 250, d 279, da 280, de 282/290, en 308, er 313, et 315, fu 337, gi 343, ha 355, i 364, l 387, le 388, les 389, li 390, ma 396, ne 411, o 422, on 435, p 447, po 453, pr 455, que 465, r 469, ra 471, re 472, s 480, su 488, t 494, ta 496, ti 500, tr 503, u 511, ur 512. Each letter heads its own section and is followed by its syllables. The common units have a second number.

03 What remains uncertain

  • The last numbers, 400, 297 and 586, stand inside “et conclure même”; the decipherment gives the words, but each number occurs once and the split between them is not fixed.

# R955, Gottfried van Swieten to Count Cobenzl, f. 141 (undated, 1757-58): English translation

«In a deputation of the Magistrate here they have deliberated on the course to take in case the French should be obliged

about it, as is right.
