Bonn → Brussels · numerical syllabary · 1757–58 · read from existing decipherment

Gottfried van Swieten to Count Cobenzl, Bonn 1757–58

→

ARA Brussels, Secrétairerie d’État et de Guerre, inv. 1236, f. 141 · DECODE R955, no decipherment recorded · a letter in French with one sentence in three-digit numbers

The ciphered sentence has its decipherment written below it on the same page; it was transcribed and aligned with the numbers, which gives the 1757 table. Van Swieten’s two letters of 1759 use a different table and have their own page.

Method: read from existing decipherment · Extent: complete

Summary. Gottfried van Swieten, later Mozart’s and Haydn’s patron in Vienna, began as a diplomat under Count Cobenzl, the Empress’s minister plenipotentiary in the Austrian Netherlands. From Bonn, the court of the Elector of Cologne, he reported to Cobenzl during the Seven Years’ War, putting the sensitive sentences into numbers. This undated letter (R955) has the decipherment written under the numbers: the magistrates of Hanau debated neutrality in case the French left. Aligned with the 89 numbers, it shows the system, an alphabetical list of letters and syllables with a few homophones, numbered 202–586. The two letters of January and February 1759 use a new table in the same style, numbered 100–442, whose key was recovered from the ciphertext alone; they are a different cipher with their own page, Van Swieten to Cobenzl, 1759. Extent: complete; the whole sentence stands in its decipherment, and three numbers inside “conclure même” are not split.

01 The letter

R955, f. 141, undated, written while the French held Hanau (1757–58): 89 dotted groups, 202–586, with the decipherment written below them. DECODE holds two later letters of van Swieten to Cobenzl in the same file (R956, R957, 1759); they use another table, and are on their own page.

02 The 1757 table, from its own decipherment

The clear lines on f. 141 repeat the cipher word for word: “on a délibéré ici dans une députation de Magistrat sur le parti à prendre au cas que les François fussent obligés d’abandonner Hanau, on a osé opiner pour la neutralité et conclure même; on garde cependant un profond secret là-dessus comme de raison.” Aligning the two gives values that rise with the alphabet: a 202/203, ab 205, an 216, ar 220, as 223, at 224, au 228, be 241, c 250, d 279, da 280, de 282/290, en 308, er 313, et 315, fu 337, gi 343, ha 355, i 364, l 387, le 388, les 389, li 390, ma 396, ne 411, o 422, on 435, p 447, po 453, pr 455, que 465, r 469, ra 471, re 472, s 480, su 488, t 494, ta 496, ti 500, tr 503, u 511, ur 512. Each letter heads its own section and is followed by its syllables. The common units have a second number.

03 What remains uncertain

  • The last numbers, 400, 297 and 586, stand inside “et conclure même”; the decipherment gives the words, but each number occurs once and the split between them is not fixed.

04 Sources

  • Archives générales du Royaume, Brussels, Secrétairerie d’État et de Guerre, inv. 1236; images and transcriptions on DECODE, record R955 (transcriber SofPe 2019).
  • No printed edition or earlier decipherment of these letters was found.
