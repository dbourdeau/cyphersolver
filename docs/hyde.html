Brussels · Restoration correspondence · superscriptions in figures · 1659–60

Hyde's ciphered superscriptions to Barwick

Sir Edward Hyde to Dr John Barwick, Brussels, 29 Sept and 17 Oct 1659, 14 and 16 Jan 1660 · printed in Peter Barwick, Vita Johannis Barwick (1721), pp. 358, 360, 389, 396

The figures are nulls, numbers "only to puzzle the Enemy", as the 1724 editor, who had the papers, states. Applying the full key confirms it. There is no address to decipher.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 September 2026 · updated 26 October 2026

At a glanceNot a cipher

The letters
Four letters from Hyde in Brussels to John Barwick, the royalist agent in London, in the months before the Restoration. Where an address should be, each has a line of figures.
Where
Tomokiyo lists them as undeciphered, meaning no decipherment is known. They were printed from the Barwick papers in 1721 together with the Hyde–Barwick key.
Why they have none
The figures are nulls. The 1724 English edition says several letters “were superscribed with Numbers signifying nothing”, and cites these pages.
How it was checked
The full Hyde–Barwick key, transcribed here from the 1721 plate: one superscription decrypts to nonsense and the other three use numbers that do not exist in the key.
What was added
The item should be removed from Tomokiyo’s list as not a cipher.

01 The documents

Tomokiyo's page references (313, 335, 339, 441, 442 for the clear addresses; 358, 360, 389, 396 for the ciphered ones) are to the Latin Vita Johannis Barwick (London, 1721), whose appendix prints Hyde's letters to Barwick in English, with an engraved "Tabula Cryptographica" of their cipher facing p. 316. The four superscriptions, verified against the page scans (every group is followed by a full stop, so the grouping is certain):

Clear superscriptions in the same series are cover names for Barwick: "For Mr. Burden", "For Mr. Brookes", "For Mr. B.", "For Mr. Burges, these"; the editor notes that "B. or any Name beginning with B. seems to have signified Barwick".

02 The Hyde–Barwick key

The 1721 plate gives the complete cipher: numbers 1–692 in eleven columns. Column 1 is the alphabet, three figures per letter (two for k, w, y; none for x, z); 64–69 are skipped; columns 2–11 (70–131, 132–193, 194–254, 255–317, 318–379, 380–440, 441–503, 504–566, 567–628, 629–692) each run A to Y down the rows, three rows per letter, so syllables and words are grouped by initial letter across the columns rather than in one alphabetical list. Several slots are deliberately empty. Entries relevant here: the = 370, for = 210, from = 211, Mr. = 536, Master = 475, Barwick = 572, Hyde, Lord Chancellor = 589; there is no entry for these, Sir or London.

The template "For / Mr / [name] / these" cannot be rescued by a shift either: for is 210 and Mr. 536, so 729 and 549 would need offsets of 519 and 13 respectively. The block 729 549 856 … 245 831 recurring across three letters is a stock dummy line that Hyde's clerk copied out.

04 The editor's statement

The unabridged English Life of Dr. John Barwick (1724, tr. Hilkiah Bedford), working from the Barwick family papers, appends this note to the letter of 12 June 1659 that enclosed the cipher (pp. 408–409):

But to render the Cypher more difficult to be found out, as each Letter in the Alphabet is represented by three several Numbers, except K, W, and Y only by two, and X and Z by none: So several of the Numbers have no Letter, Syllable, or Word over against them, because they stand for nothing, and were only us'd to perplex such as should attempt to read what was written hereby, without the Help of the Cypher. So several of the Letters were superscribed with Numbers signifying nothing, some with two or three Lines of them, only to puzzle the Enemy, if they should fall into their Hands, which I the rather mention here, because some Persons, for want of examining those Superscriptions, as printed in the Appendix to the Latin Life (p. 358, 360, 389, 396, and 417.) with the Cypher also published there, have wondered what was the meaning of them: and it was for the same Reason, that several of the Chancellor's Letters, besides the Number denoting his Name, were subscribed with other Numbers, that either had nothing in the Cypher to answer them, or nothing to the Purpose. As to the Superscriptions, B. or any Name beginning with B. seems to have signified Barwick.

The pages he names are the four in question. The question had already been raised and answered in 1724.

05 Method

  1. Identified the printed source of Tomokiyo's page numbers, the Latin Vita Barwick (Internet Archive bim_eighteenth-century_vita-johannis-barwick-s_barwick-peter_1721); located the four superscription pages and the key plate; verified every figure on the scans.
  2. Transcribed the 643 entries of the plate into a table (targets/hyde/barwick_key.py) and decoded all four superscriptions.
  3. Checked the shift hypothesis for the "For Mr … these" template; rejected.
  4. Searched the 1724 English edition (lifeofjohnbarwic00barw) for editorial matter on the cipher; found the note above.
