Henriette de Clèves and Renato Birago to Nevers, 1574

Two letters from Lyon to the Duke of Nevers in substitution ciphers of graphic signs, each with its own contemporary decipherment. Birago’s cipher flap covers a complete clear copy, transcribed in full; the duchess’s interlinear decipherment is faded and was transcribed only in part. No key was applied: the similar 1584 Nevers alphabet does not fit.

  2. [illegible: needs a new source] about a dozen single groups on f. 15v (ll. 7, 10, 12-16, 20-23) and f. 16r (l. 1 `21 CF(99F`, l. 14 `C824F2bTF`) - blocker: illegible; neither the signs nor the faded gloss settle them

ALREADY TRIED (from NOTES.md, "## Escalation")
  [x] siblings: checked the neighbouring fr. 3315 leaves and Tomokiyo's description; ff. 12-13 have a different 1574 key, while the f. 21 cipher flap carries its own Italian interlinear reading.
  [x] clear-pages: found the clear ending of the duchess's letter on f. 16r and Birago's complete clear copy on f. 21r beneath the flap; f. 21 was read in full.
  [x] known-keys: inspected the 1584 Nevers no. 2 alphabet in fr. 3995 f. 16 and Tomokiyo's comparison; it differs at F=e and 9=q and is not an exact decoder.
  [x] print: searched exact wording and historical editions; rejected the superficially similar Henri III appeal printed by Lavisse because it predates this letter and has a different sender. No published edition of f. 15 or 21 was found.
  [x] key-rebuild: tested alignment of the enlarged f. 21 signs with its interlinear Italian gloss; crowded glyphs and uncertain word boundaries prevented a reliable complete table. No uncertain mapping was used to fill f. 15.
  [x] retry: revisited f. 15r-v in overlapping native-resolution crops after the clear ending and f. 21 parallel were found; the opening remained secure, but the ciphered middle still could not be transcribed continuously.
  [x] retry: (22 Oct 2026): gloss and cipher signs transcribed line by line, alphabet rebuilt from their alignment (`f15_key.md`); f. 15 and its f. 16r continuation read, only f. 15v ll. 2-5 and single groups open.
  [x] key-rebuild: (22 Oct 2026): the f. 15 alphabet rebuilt from gloss-sign alignment, every value attested in several words.

STEPS
0. Set up. If you can run a shell, fork and clone the repository and work inside it:
     git clone https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver.git
   Then read, in this order: CONTRIBUTING.md (how a contribution is sent and credited); README.md, sections
   "Repository layout" and "Conventions" (how readings are measured and named); targets/nevers1574/NOTES.md in full, and the
   key, transcription and reading files it names; lang/README.md (shared language models).
   Work in targets/nevers1574/. Add your findings to NOTES.md under a dated heading without rewriting what is there, and
   update the "## Remaining gaps" line of every gap you close. Keep WORKLOG.md there: one dated line per step
   (hypothesis, value tried, solver run, crib, control), failures included. Do not edit docs/ (the site is generated).
   If you cannot run a shell, fetch the files from the links above and keep your work in files you send with the issue.
1. Pick a gap. Start with the ones marked "doable now" (open-codes, not-attempted, left-open): the images and the key
   are online, so they need only work. A gap marked "needs a new source" (no-key-material, too-short, illegible,
   needs-physical-access) can be closed only by something new: a key
   or a deciphered sibling found elsewhere, a better image, a printed edition or calendar, a photograph from the archive.
   For those, search for that material first, and say where you looked even when you find nothing.
2. Start from the existing key and transcription in the folder. Do not rebuild the key from scratch, and do not
   re-read parts that are already read unless you find an error in them.
3. For a code group or sign value: list every place the group occurs, show how each reads with the value you propose,
   and say what still does not fit. For a passage: re-read it at the image and give the folio and line. For a missing
   letter or leaf: say where it is held and how it can be reached.
4. Measure again. Apply the key to the whole text, write the reading file, and count the share of cipher tokens that
   give sense (not an estimate). The reading is "complete" only when (1) at least 95% of its cipher tokens give sense;
   (2) no gap is left untried; (3) every letter in it is covered; (4) what stays open is scattered code groups or names,
   or pieces blocked from outside. Otherwise it stays "partial": say which gaps are still open and why.
5. Be honest. Mark every uncertain word with [?], leave unread groups as their numbers or signs, and never fill a gap
   with a plausible guess. A gap retried and still open, with what was ruled out, is worth reporting.
