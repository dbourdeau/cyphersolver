Pacheco to Granvelle, April–June 1554

Five letters of the viceroy of Naples to the bishop of Arras on money, troops and the war of Siena. Their cipher passages carry a decipherment written at the time, and Michael Brackx rebuilt the chancery syllabary from it: 887 signs transcribed, 98% valued.

ALREADY TRIED (from NOTES.md, "## Escalation")
  [x] siblings: all six letters of MSS/20214/61 viewed; five carry cipher, all with decipherments of the time. Pacheco's thirteen letters of 1554-1555 in the Real Biblioteca, Madrid (II/2251; H15Corpus GRANVELA-H-0032 to 0044) viewed: four carry cipher in the same key, with decipherments of the time, and are transcribed in `siblings/rb2251/` (37 lines, 626 signs). The volume also holds a letter of the viceroy of Sicily almost wholly in cipher (f. 182r), another correspondence, not worked. Pacheco's other letters to Granvelle in the Real Biblioteca (II/2322, II/2325, II/2258; Rome 1552-1553) are not checked
  [x] clear-pages: the decipherments of the time are on the same pages (interlinear and margin); 61/3 f. 1r has a second photograph with the margin legible
  [x] known-keys: Tomokiyo's list of Spanish ciphers (cached upstream in `targets/vasto1527/prior/spanish2C_now.htm`) searched: no Pacheco or Sigüenza key (its "Cardinal Pacheco" is Francisco Pacheco, a 1566 cipher). Pich-Ponce's PP1-PP3 (Granvelle-Renard, Saint-Mauris) are other ciphers. The structure (base sign plus vowel marks, letter-word codes such as `o-o`) is that of Charles V's syllabic ciphers, as is the Imperial Cipher of 1544-1554 of `vargas1552`, whose short-word codes (`s-o` *el*, `x-o` *del*, `n-o` *al*) have the same shape. No code group is shared among the few known, and that key's full table (Meister 1906, Bertomeu Masiá 2009) is not in the repository, so the identity is not tested
  [x] print: web search (2 Nov 2026): no edition or study of these letters' cipher. Martín Aizpuru, *Onomázein* (2025), studies forty letters of Pacheco and Bolea to Granvelle (1551-1557), but those are in the Real Biblioteca (Granvela collection, H15Corpus), not the BNE, and her study is linguistic. Bucuré, *Estudios Románicos* 34 (2025), studies the Naples cipher of 1547 (Pedro de Toledo), not compared. Van Durme 1956, cited by the BNE record, not seen
  [x] key-rebuild: syllabary rebuilt from the alignment with the decipherments
  [x] retry: letter 1 re-read at full size after the key was built from letters 2-4; look-alikes compared side by side

STEPS
0. Set up. If you can run a shell, fork and clone the repository and work inside it:
     git clone https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver.git
   Then read, in this order: CONTRIBUTING.md (how a contribution is sent and credited); README.md, sections
   "Repository layout" and "Conventions" (how readings are measured and named); targets/siguenza1554/NOTES.md in full, and the
   key, transcription and reading files it names; lang/README.md (shared language models).
   Work in targets/siguenza1554/. Add your findings to NOTES.md under a dated heading without rewriting what is there, and
   update the "## Remaining gaps" line of every gap you close. Keep WORKLOG.md there: one dated line per step
   (hypothesis, value tried, solver run, crib, control), failures included. Do not edit docs/ (the site is generated).
   If you cannot run a shell, fetch the files from the links above and keep your work in files you send with the issue.
1. Pick a gap. Start with the ones marked "doable now" (open-codes, not-attempted, left-open): the images and the key
   are online, so they need only work. A gap marked "needs a new source" (no-key-material, too-short, illegible,
   needs-physical-access) can be closed only by something new: a key
   or a deciphered sibling found elsewhere, a better image, a printed edition or calendar, a photograph from the archive.
   For those, search for that material first, and say where you looked even when you find nothing.
2. Start from the existing key and transcription in the folder. Do not rebuild the key from scratch, and do not
   re-read parts that are already read unless you find an error in them.
3. For a code group or sign value: list every place the group occurs, show how each reads with the value you propose,
   and say what still does not fit. For a passage: re-read it at the image and give the folio and line. For a missing
   letter or leaf: say where it is held and how it can be reached.
4. Measure again. Apply the key to the whole text, write the reading file, and count the share of cipher tokens that
   give sense (not an estimate). The reading is "complete" only when (1) at least 95% of its cipher tokens give sense;
   (2) no gap is left untried; (3) every letter in it is covered; (4) what stays open is scattered code groups or names,
   or pieces blocked from outside. Otherwise it stays "partial": say which gaps are still open and why.
5. Be honest. Mark every uncertain word with [?], leave unread groups as their numbers or signs, and never fill a gap
   with a plausible guess. A gap retried and still open, with what was ruled out, is worth reporting.
