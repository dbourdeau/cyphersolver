Germany → Russia · two columnar transpositions of German · December 1919 – February 1920 · keys recovered by a contributor

Seven courier letters from communists in Germany to Soviet officials in Russia, 1919–20

Go to the full text ↓

Typed copies, made at Riga, of seven German letters taken from couriers in 1920 were enciphered in two columnar transpositions; both keys were recovered from the ciphertext alone, under George Lasry’s direction, and six parts decrypt to German sentences apart from a few blotted or missing letters.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 8 November 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: partial (99% of the cipher)

Contributed by George Lasry (new cipher with solution) · Also credited · Contemporary key or decipherment (earlier solution)

The letters
Seven reports of the communist secretariat in Germany to Soviet officials in Russia, 23 December 1919 to 19 February 1920, typed copies, made at Riga, of letters carried by couriers on an aeroplane forced down in Latvia on 6 April 1920.
Where
US National Archives, RG 59, Central Decimal File 1910–29, file 862.20261, microfilm M336 roll 75, frames 0456–0470 (naId 574886726); the cipher text is on frames 0462–0464.
The cipher
A single columnar transposition of German with no nulls and no word division, in two keys: 19 columns for indicator 27001 and 25 columns for 27003.
How it was deciphered
both keys were recovered, by Claude agents under George Lasry’s direction, by annealing over column orders with a German 4-gram score, using no plaintext; the 1920 State Department translations of five parts, found in the same file, were used afterwards and agree.
What it says
Couriers, money and jewels, addresses in Berlin, a secretariat in need of funds, a branch in Vienna, a planned congress, and the failed conference in Holland with its arrests.
Still open
About 34 letters in Fortsetzung 2, one letter of a name in Zusatz, 15 letters in Fortsetzung 3 supplied from the sense and the 1920 translation, and some blotted places in the copy; no human check beyond the contributor’s own comparison with the images.
Credit
The keys, the corrected ciphertext and the readings are George Lasry’s, made with Claude agents under his direction (contributed through the repository). The State Department’s 1920 translations are the earlier reading of five parts.

01 The letters

Typed copies, made at Riga, of letters sent by communists in Germany to Soviet officials in Russia, which NOTES records as sewn into the clothes of couriers on a German aeroplane forced down in Latvia on 6 April 1920 (from the despatch of 21 April 1920, not seen here). The US Commissioner at Riga sent the copies to the Secretary of State with despatch No. 155 of 21 April 1920. The cipher text is on frames 0462–0464 of the microfilm: seven parts, each opening with an indicator (27001 or 27003) and its letter count.

The State Department read the letters in 1920. Its instruction No. 16 to Riga (5 August 1920, frame 0456) says the code material “has proven of great interest” and encloses translations, and a confidential memorandum (frames 0468–0470) gives English translations of Anfang and of Fortsetzung 3, 4 and 5 and Schlussmitteilungen. The file holds no German plaintext and no key, and Fortsetzung 2 and Zusatz have no translation in it.

02 The two keys

Both indicators are a single columnar transposition of German: no nulls, no word division, Ä, Ö and Ü as single letters. The plaintext is written in rows under a numbered key and the columns are taken top to bottom in the order of the key numbers; when the last row is short, the long columns are the leftmost ones.

Reading each row across gives “…auf eigene Faust nach Schweiz Moskau gereist Bericht Italien wahrscheinlich do…”, the first three rows of the part.

03 How the keys were found

  1. Letter counts: all seven parts have a German profile (E high, or R high where the copy types R for E) and no dependence between adjacent letters, so the text is a transposition.
  2. Simulated annealing over column orders, scored with German 4-grams, recovered the 19-column key from Fortsetzung 4 and Schlussmitteilungen (indicator 27001) and the 25-column key from Zusatz (27003, 940 letters). No plaintext was used. The 25-column key decrypts Fortsetzung 2 and 5 as well.
  3. Fortsetzung 3 reads with the 27001 key once the three groups missing from the copy (15 letters) are located; Anfang reads with it once a struck-out group of five letters after TSUWI is removed, and the remaining letters are exactly the 296 of the indicator line.
  4. The 1920 English translation was used afterwards, to check the readings and to settle some names.

The method is a recovery of the key from the ciphertext alone. The 1920 translations were not used to find either key.

04 The reading

The German is built into sentences and translated for every part (reading.md). George Lasry graded the German of all seven parts A: coherent, with scattered holes. In brief:

  • Anfang. The air consignment arrived; the writer asks for letters in the hand of Lenin, Trotsky and Zinoviev for Italy and France, rejects Osipoff as unreliable and asks for Koriniez to be recalled. “Flugsendung gut angekommen. Material nicht wiederholen, neues senden.” (“Air consignment arrived safely. Do not repeat material; send new.”)
  • Fortsetzung 2. A man travelled on his own to Switzerland and Moscow and is highly suspicious; the couriers Else, Olga and Hilde came from Riga with old material; courier addresses in Berlin; the Commissariat demands material.
  • Fortsetzung 3. Pochitonoff is said to be in Switzerland; negotiations with Sylvia, money given, more handwritten English wanted from her; Litvinov answers neither couriers nor letters; Dzerzhinsky’s confidential man is wanted.
  • Fortsetzung 4. The secretariat urgently needs money and material, names its newly elected members, and reports contact with Holland and its own publications (Russische Korrespondenz).
  • Fortsetzung 5. A branch is set up in Vienna; contact with all countries; a congress in January or February; Radek or Bukharin is needed; signed Thomas (James).
  • Schlussmitteilungen. Fedoroff’s stones handed over for France; a plea not to fund individual countries but mainly the party press; a commission for Balabanoff.
  • Zusatz. The failed conference in Holland and the arrests that followed, police pressure on the secretariat, a radio station ready, journalists exchanged with the Frankfurter Zeitung, and an urgent need of money for Bulgaria, Serbia and France.

05 The typed copy

The copy on the microfilm is a blurred carbon: many letters are blotted (E, R, B, H, K, M and N fill in) and the 1920 typist made slips. transcription.txt gives the letters as they stand on the page. For each part the corrected plaintext was re-enciphered and compared letter by letter with that transcription, and the differences were checked on the NARA images under George Lasry’s direction (he compared the places listed by eye); ciphertext_corrected.txt is the ciphertext as it fits the decryption, with ? only where a letter is lost.

The reading notes record how many letters of the typed copy are blotted or mistyped in each part: 33, 89, 35, 3, 107, 36 and 115 in the order of the table, Fortsetzung 5 (107 of 340 letters) being the worst; the confusions are mostly visual (R for E, P for F, N and M). The project did not re-measure these counts and did not see the images. The contributor’s decrypt.py only decrypts the corrected ciphertext and compares it with the plaintext file written from it, so it checks the files against each other and does not test the reading.

06 What remains uncertain

  • Fortsetzung 2: about 34 letters decrypt but do not read (the opening GALIEIE, OE after “verdächtig”, the names PEL…ZIERSKI and RELCW, VILARDOVRRHAFEET before “jetzt frei”); the part has no 1920 translation.
  • Zusatz: one letter of the name O?galski is illegible.
  • Fortsetzung 3: the 15 letters of three groups missing from the copy are supplied from the sense and the 1920 translation.
  • Fortsetzung 5: “Es geht schön vorwärts” rests on two letters corrected against the copy; it matches the 1920 translation (wording not checked here).
  • Schlussmitteilungen: “Auftrag auf Zürich [?]”; the copy has ZURUCK and the 1920 translation “Order on Zuruch”.
  • Two more blotted places are illegible in the reading notes: the fourth letter of DDN?Ä in Fortsetzung 4 and the two blotted letters of PL??F in Schlussmitteilungen.
  • No statistical validation is recorded; for a transposition there is no substitution key to score against shuffled keys. The images were not seen by the project, and no printed edition of the German text is known.

# German text (readable, normalized)

## r75-0462-1 — Anfang, 23 December 1919

Flugsendung gut angekommen. Material nicht wiederholen, neues senden. Erwünscht wiederum Handschreiben von Lenin, Trotzki, Zinowiew für Italien, Frankreich. Osipoff bestimmt nicht mehr senden: unzuverlässig, unkonspirativ. Sendet statt ihm einen absolut sicheren Deutschen. Koriniez: Adresse ungültig; er selbst muss von euch abberufen werden, unfähig, schädlich.

## r75-0462-2 — Fortsetzung 2, 23 December 1919

[…] auf eigene Faust nach Schweiz, Moskau gereist. Bericht Italien wahrscheinlich dort erhalten. Er höchst verdächtig. […] Neben Else Kuriere Olga, Hilde aus Riga angekommen, altes Material gebracht. Eine Kurieradresse Berlin: […]zierski, Regensburger Str. 9, Pension von […], absolut sichere. An Kopp, Fasanenstr. 27. Kommissariat verlangt Material; unmöglich. Doppelt senden. Trifft Arrangement Lewin–Feodoroff. Provokation […] jetzt frei.

## r75-0462-3 — Fortsetzung 3, 23 December 1919

Reisen laut Bestimmung. Pochitonoff angeblich Schweiz. Erwarte Mitteilung Ranson. Unbekannt, wem Geld übergeben. Habe mit Sylvia hier verhandelt, Geld gegeben. Brauchen mehr Handschriftliches englisch von ihr. Litwinoff reagiert nicht, weder Kuriere noch Briefe. Sehr wünschenswert Dzierzinskis Vertrauensmann hier. Große Möglichkeiten.

## r75-0462-4 — Fortsetzung 4, 23 December 1919

Sendet Geld! Italien, Frankreich braucht dringend. Gut gehen große Perlen, in England Saphiren. Sekretariat bittet dringend Material. Geld zentralistisch zusenden vor Konferenz. November Sekretariat gewählt: Paul Levi, Bronski, Zetkin, ich Sekretär. Kontakt mit Holland, dort einverstanden. „Kommunistische“ erscheint hier, auch gebe heraus „Russische Korrespondenz“.

## r75-0463-1 — Fortsetzung 5, 23 December 1919

In Wien Filiale geschaffen. Von Carlo Moment keine Nachricht; er arbeitet vorzüglich. Abramowitsch, Paris, war dort. Bereits von hier Verbindung mit allen Ländern. Es geht schön vorwärts. Januar, Februar bestimmt Kongress. Radek wird ausführlich euch berichten. Absolut nötig hier Radek oder Bucharin. Bitte größere Summe, sobald fertig. Kopp momentan kann tatsächlich nicht. Ich heiße jetzt Thomas. Gruß, James.

## r75-0463-2 — Schlussmitteilungen, 25 December 1919

Fedoroffs neun Steine habe ich für Frankreich Abramowitsch gegeben. Fedoroff geht dorthin zur Verfügung unseres dortigen Kollegiums. Alle Zeitungen für euch gründlich nachschauen: absichtliche Unordnung, Material darin. Nochmals: In Anbetracht der Verluste durch Korruption bitten wir, einzelnen Ländern keine Gelder zu senden. Wir ersuchen um eine größere Summe für uns, somit für andere. Bei Brillanten entstehen Verluste, wenn kleine Kohlenstücke darin sind. Hauptsächlich Unterstützung der Parteipresse aller Länder, weniger der Organisationen. Salatt, Tscheche, beruft sich auf Balabanoff. Auftrag auf Zürich [?]. Bitte Antwort. Gruß. Jetzt Thomas.

## r75-0464 — Zusatz, 19 February 1920

Konferenz Holland dank Unvorsichtigkeit Dortiger reingefallen. Klara und Sylvia verhaftet. Holländische Presse spricht von Brillantenverkauf durch Unsere und von zwanzig Millionen. Beschluss Exekutive hier auch. Merkliche vermehrte Tätigkeit Polizei gegen Sekretariat. Bronskis Photos unaufgeklärterweise in ihren Händen. Vermutung: durch Polen bei euch. Unlängst wurden verhaftet Lewi und Alexander. Berichtet, ob bei euch Stenographisten, System Stolze-Schrey oder Gabelsberger, sind; würde Stenogramme senden. Habe Aussichten, als Begleiter einer ärztlichen Mission oder Delegation Auswanderer zu euch zu kommen. Bitte um Meinung, ob euch erwünscht. Radiostation endlich ausgebaut, Fachmann engagiert, beginnen nächste Woche aufzunehmen. Wir benötigen Gelder dringend für Bulgarien, Serbien, Frankreich. Delegierten hier warten. Triestiner mit Geld Italien angekommen, er in Wien verhaftet. Geld hier, geht demnächst nach Italien. O[?]galski auf der Reise nach Amerika mit Geld hier angekommen. Deutsche Regierung bewilligt Tausch Journalisten, von hier Frankfurter Zeitung. Benützt Gelegenheit. Dringend nötig: tüchtige Kraft.

# English translation

## r75-0462-1 — Anfang, 23 December 1919

Air consignment arrived safely. Do not repeat material; send new. Again wanted: letters in the hand of Lenin, Trotsky and Zinoviev for Italy and France. Definitely do not send Osipoff any more: unreliable, not conspiratorial. Instead of him send an absolutely reliable German. Koriniez: address void; he himself must be recalled by you; incapable, harmful.

## r75-0462-2 — Fortsetzung 2, 23 December 1919

[…] travelled on his own initiative to Switzerland and Moscow. Report on Italy probably received there. He is highly suspicious. […] Besides Else, the couriers Olga and Hilde have arrived from Riga and brought old material. A courier address in Berlin: […]zierski, Regensburger Strasse 9, boarding house of […], absolutely safe. To Kopp, Fasanenstrasse 27. The Commissariat demands material; impossible. Send in duplicate. The Lewin–Feodoroff arrangement applies. Provocation […] now free.

## r75-0462-3 — Fortsetzung 3, 23 December 1919

Journeys as arranged. Pochitonoff allegedly in Switzerland. Awaiting word from Ranson. Not known to whom the money was handed over. Have negotiated here with Sylvia; money given. We need more handwritten material in English from her. Litvinov does not respond, neither to couriers nor to letters. Dzerzhinsky’s confidential agent here is highly desirable. Great possibilities.

## r75-0462-4 — Fortsetzung 4, 23 December 1919

Send money! Italy and France are in urgent need. Large pearls sell well; in England, sapphires. The Secretariat urgently asks for material. Send money centrally before the conference. In November the Secretariat was elected: Paul Levi, Bronski, Zetkin, myself as secretary. Contact with Holland; agreed there. The Communist appears here; I am also publishing the Russian Correspondence.

## r75-0463-1 — Fortsetzung 5, 23 December 1919

Branch set up in Vienna. No news from Carlo at the moment; he works excellently. Abramowitsch, Paris, was there. Contact already established from here with all countries. Things are going forward well. Congress certainly in January or February. Radek will report to you in detail. Radek or Bukharin absolutely needed here. Please send a larger sum as soon as it is ready. Kopp really cannot at present. My name is now Thomas. Regards, James.

## r75-0463-2 — Schlussmitteilungen, 25 December 1919

I have given Fedoroff’s nine stones to Abramowitsch for France. Fedoroff is going there to be at the disposal of our local council. Examine all newspapers intended for you thoroughly: the disorder is intentional, and material is inside. Once again, in view of losses through corruption, please do not send funds to individual countries. We request a larger sum for ourselves and thus for others. Diamonds lose value when they contain small carbon particles. Support mainly the party press in all countries, and the organizations to a lesser extent. Salatt, a Czech, invokes Balabanoff. Order on Zurich [?]. Please reply. Regards. Now Thomas.

## r75-0464 — Zusatz, 19 February 1920

The conference in Holland failed through the carelessness of people there. Klara and Sylvia arrested. The Dutch press speaks of the sale of diamonds by our people and of twenty million. Executive decision here too. Noticeably increased police activity against the Secretariat. Bronski’s photos inexplicably in their hands. Suspicion: through Poles with you. Lewi and Alexander were arrested recently. Report whether you have stenographers with you, system Stolze-Schrey or Gabelsberger; I would send shorthand notes. I have prospects of coming to you as escort of a medical mission or of a delegation of emigrants. Please give your opinion whether this is welcome to you. Radio station finally built, specialist engaged; we begin receiving next week. We urgently need funds for Bulgaria, Serbia and France. Delegates are waiting here. The man from Trieste with money for Italy has arrived; he has been arrested in Vienna. The money is here and goes to Italy shortly. O[?]galski, on his way to America, has arrived here with money. The German government approves an exchange of journalists, from here the Frankfurter Zeitung. Use the opportunity. Urgently needed: a capable man.
