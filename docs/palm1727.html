London → Starhemberg / Visconti · homophonic cipher with nomenclator · March 1727

Palm to Starhemberg and Visconti, 1727

British Library, Add MS 32270 ff. 16–18 and 41 (DECODE R7927–R7930). The two records behind the catalogue entry hold one German letter with ciphered passages and one decipherer’s worksheet for another letter that is not imaged.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 26 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The document
Palm, the Emperor’s resident in London, to Starhemberg, 1727: a German letter fragment, mostly in clear, with 14 ciphered passages.
Where
British Library, Add MS 32270, ff. 16–17; DECODE R7927. Related records R7928, R7929 and R7930 do not supply a usable key.
The cipher
Numbers 2–79 for letters, about two per letter, and three-figure groups for words and syllables.
How it was tried
The R7928 key contradicts the glosses. Annealing with a German 5-gram model on 162 letter tokens did not converge, with or without the gloss values fixed.
Why it resists
A synthetic control of the same shape recovered only 44–50% of tokens, so a text this short cannot be deciphered without the key.
Still open
All 14 passages. Needed: Palm’s key, the Deciphering Branch’s clear copy, or images of R7929.

01 The four records

RecordFoliosWhat it is
R7927ff. 16–17Docket “Palm & Staremberg / German”. A letter fragment in German with 14 ciphered passages. Images 1 and 4 show only the other side of the leaf through the paper.
R7928f. 18“Count Windischgrätz & Ct Staremberg”: a letter key 1–50 with about 30 values filled in (2 b, 5 n, 8 u, 9 c, 10 i, 20 o, 40 r …).
R7929ff. 19–40The Emperor to Starhemberg, 42 pp., marked “Decrypted”. No images on DECODE.
R7930f. 41Cover “Palm to Visconti 1727 … Visconti to Palm”. On the verso is a decipherer’s tally grid, 1–500 (Addresse 21, Hollande 109, Italie 120, Traité 215, syllables in the 300s and 400s, several nulls). This is a different system, and no ciphertext is imaged.

02 The cipher of R7927

162 letter tokens across 47 values (2–79) and 23 code tokens across 10 values: 124, 138, 170 (five times), 210 (three), 230 = der (six), 380, 423, 432, 474 and 799. The glosses, in German Kurrent script, give 33 = e, 23 = n, 10 = d, 15 = t, 40 = ſ, and probably 9 = u and 4 = z. The clear text concerns how England saw the war then coming over Gibraltar and the Hanover alliance, and the rumours that a rupture would bring (“dass im Fall es zu einer Ruptur kommen solte, man …”). One passage ends in … d ſ ? e t z e n zu bringen.

03 What was tried

  • The R7928 key applied to the passages: it gives 40 = r and 23 = u where the glosses have ſ and n. Ruled out.
  • Simulated annealing on the letter values (the project’s de-modern 5-gram model), with the codes drawn from a list of German words and syllables and the clear context kept for scoring. With and without the gloss values fixed, the output was not German.
  • A control on synthetic German of the same shape (162 letter tokens over 47 values), with the true values of the six commonest symbols fixed, recovered only 44–50% of the tokens. At that rate the text cannot be made out, so a text of this length cannot be deciphered without the key.
  • A web search for a printed decipherment of Palm’s 1727 intercepts found nothing.

04 What remains

None of the fourteen passages was deciphered. With about two homophones per letter and only 162 letter tokens, a ciphertext-only attack has too little to work on. What is needed: Palm’s Imperial cipher (Vienna, HHStA, England, Berichte 1727), the Deciphering Branch’s clear copy (other Willes volumes, SP 107), or images of R7929.
