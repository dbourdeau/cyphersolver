Loeschner's Cipher Memorandum

ÖStA, Haus-, Hof- und Staatsarchiv, Staatskanzlei Interiora, Chiffrenschlüssel Kt. 20, alt. Fasc. ad 27, ff. 30–40 · DECODE R2227, catalogued as a non-decrypted letter "with short encrypted parts" from "W. Loeschnen?" to an unknown recipient

It is not a ciphered letter. It is a memorandum about how the ministry's cipher keys should be used, and every figure in it is a worked example with its plaintext written above it.

Daniel Bourdeau · resolved 21 September 2026

Summary. DECODE R2227 (20 pages, "Non-decrypted", numerical, "Letter with short encrypted parts") entered the Unsolved Catalogue as a ciphered letter of "W. Loeschnen?" to an unknown recipient, dated 1 July 1816. All eleven openings were viewed here. The document is written in clear German Kurrent. Its heading reads Zusätze und Abänderungen, welche in der Instruktion zum richtigen und zweckmäßigen Gebrauch der in der offiziellen Korrespondenz zwischen den k.k. Missionen und der geheimen Hof- und Staatskanzlei üblichen Ziffernschlüssel gemacht werden dürften. It is signed "Wien am 1ten Julius 1816. W. Loeschner". It comments on a cipher instruction page by page and line by line. The number groups in it are demonstrations, and each one has the French plaintext written over it, for example the announcement that the King of Spain has named the Duke of San Carlos ambassador. Nothing is hidden, so there is nothing to decipher. The target is closed as explained, and a DECODE correction (name, record type and status) is queued.

01 The document

The eleven openings contain one continuous memorandum in a single clerical hand. After the heading, each paragraph cites a place in the instruction it amends: "Seite 2, Spalte 2, Zeile 3 von Unten", "Seite 9, Spalte 2, Zeile 5", "Seite 10, Spalte 2", "Seite 23, nach der Zahl 1918", "Seite 32", "Seite 33, Linie 2 von Unten". It closes with an appendix, Beilage: Ueber den Gebrauch der Doppel- (Supplementar-) Zahlen. The last page carries the date and the signature, W. Loeschner. DECODE's "Loeschnen?" misreads the final letter. Among the topics:

  • the misleading name "Ziffernkabinett", which the author wants dropped from an instruction meant for Austria's diplomats;
  • entering new words in the Déchiffrant (the deciphering half of the key) as well as the Chiffrant. The author cites a despatch of Prince Kaunitz that added supplements 16–17 to a key;
  • the range of numbers from 3 to 6000, and the supplementary figures (Ergänzungszahlen);
  • how to cipher proper names, the envelope of a ciphered despatch, and the Parenthesis and Claudatur signs.

02 Why there is nothing to decipher

The figures occur only inside examples. On opening 9 the plaintext le Roi d'Espagne vient de nommer (Parenthesis) M. le Duc de San Carlos (Claudatur) son Amb.r &c is written directly above its code groups, 388 1949 600 477 2395 1800 983 1440 692 1369 267 2506 1801 1949 335 1954 709 1212 958 477 1211 307 2415, with ⊖ marks for the word divisions. Opening 5 has a short table of the same kind (A, B and syllables against four-digit numbers). Opening 3 shows syllable splits such as "Com-pa-ra-tion". Every one of these examples comes with its reading. The memorandum describes a key but does not contain one, and it hides no message.

03 What stays open

Nothing on the cipher side. The memorandum was not transcribed in full. It is a useful source for Austrian cipher-office practice around 1816, and the numeric examples could be checked against the Staatskanzlei keys of that period on DECODE. For DECODE, the queued correction asks for the author to be changed to W. Loeschner, the record to be described as an instruction memorandum rather than a ciphertext, and the status to be changed from "Non-decrypted".
