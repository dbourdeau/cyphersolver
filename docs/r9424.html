A letter to Duke Wilhelm V of Bavaria, 12 June 1583

A German letter from the year of the Cologne war with long runs in a cipher of Latin letters; transcribed and attacked, it gave no text.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

not solved
On this pageThe letterWhat was triedWhat remainsSources

At a glanceNot solved · transcribed, 913 signs

The letter
German, to Duke Wilhelm V of Bavaria, dated 12 June 1583; it opens in clear and turns to cipher from the eighth line. The writer is not named in the clear text.
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 274–277; DECODE R9424, part of catalogue entry 161 (still open in the catalogue).
The cipher
Words of Latin minuscules mixed with a few invented signs (a Greek-looking e, a triangle, a tailed z, a slash), separated by spaces; a few single groups carry names written over them at the time (Hildesheim, Würzburg, Eichstätt, Freising).
How it was tried
The volume’s keys of the period (R9420, R9421 and R9422, the last cut off at the left of the photograph) do not read it. Its letter statistics look like a simple substitution, but a solver gives no German, Latin or Italian.
Why it resists
The glosses over single groups suggest code words for names among the letters, and there are too few glosses to use as cribs.

Until 15 October 2026 this letter was filed on one page with thirteen other ciphertexts of the same key volume. They are different ciphers, each with its own page; the largest group is System A′ of the dukes’ agents in Hungary. The other piece of 1583, a set of grilles, is a different device.

01 The letter

The first page (f. 274r) opens in clear German and changes to cipher at line 8; clear words stay in the cipher (“Zwisch Ho. Fe.”, “und Ni. sein”). The second page is clear German only. On the facing leaf, f. 273v, part of a table shows at the edge of the photograph: names with signs beside them (Cöln, Wirtemberg, Landgraf), perhaps the nomenclator of this cipher. The letter was transcribed sign by sign (913 cipher signs, 29 different).

02 What was tried

  • The volume’s key of 1582–83 (R9422), whose alphabet is cut off at a and b in the photograph: the letters do not read with the part that survives. R9420 (keys of 1531, a shifted alphabet) and R9421 (a tabula recta) were checked too.
  • The index of coincidence is 0.068 and flat over periods 1–8, as for a simple substitution.
  • A solver (simulated annealing) against sixteenth-century German, Latin and Italian models, with and without the slash and nulls: no text.

03 What remains

  • The whole cipher. To try next: a full re-transcription at zoom, and the f. 273v table transcribed in full and applied as the key.

04 Sources

  • Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 274–277, via DECODE R9424 (login; publication of the images needs the archive’s permission, so this page shows none).

The transcription is in the repository folder targets/r9424/.
