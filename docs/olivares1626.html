Madrid → Naples · royal cipher A of Philip IV · 22 August 1626

Olivares to the Duke of Alba, 22 August 1626

The all-powerful minister of Philip IV tells the viceroy of Naples of the deaths in his family and names his son-in-law heir of his house, in a cipher the key for which was rebuilt six years later.

The letter
Gaspar de Guzmán, Count-Duke of Olivares, to Antonio Álvarez de Toledo, 5th Duke of Alba, then viceroy of Naples; Madrid, 22 August 1626. Wholly in cipher except the closing, the dateline and the signature. A covering letter in clear of the same day (f. 11r) refers to it.
Where
Biblioteca Nacional de España, MSS/10249 (“Papeles varios”, Osuna; BDH bdh0000042230), ff. 13r–15r. DECODE R1190 lists the letter as “Decrypted”, but no decipherment or key is attached there.
The cipher
Philip IV’s general cipher A: a homophonic syllabary of letter groups, numbers for letters and syllables, special signs and a nomenclator (fro = Dios, 199 = hija).
How it was deciphered
The key K6A, rebuilt from the Santa Cruz pairs of 1630–32 (Santa Cruz 1630), reads the first part. Twenty values learnt there only were added, the key was frozen, and then it was measured once on the held-out part.
What it says
The deaths of a young Guzmán nephew and of his daughter María after a stillbirth; he keeps her widower Ramiro Núñez de Guzmán, Marquis of Toral, as son and heir, with the dukedom of Medina de las Torres and the office of sumiller de corps.
Still open
The key is partial by the project’s protocol (0.506 against 0.144, under the 0.60 threshold). Fifteen codes have no value, and about 54 line ends are lost in the gutter.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée), with Claude Opus 5.5 agents, dated and identified the letter, extended the key, transcribed and read it. DECODE R1190 is credited as prior work, without a decipherment.

01 The letter

The letter belongs to the summer of 1626, when Olivares lost a nephew meant for the Church and then his only daughter, María, who died after a stillbirth. He writes to the Duke of Alba that he keeps her widower, Ramiro Núñez de Guzmán, Marquis of Toral, as son and heir of his house: the dukedom of Medina de las Torres on the commandery of Caravaca, granted by the King at the marriage, and the office of sumiller de corps, which the King gave Ramiro of his own motion in Barcelona. He offers his life to the King’s service and hopes the Duke his son will behave wisely. The facts agree with the known chronology.

The lead (no. 1273) was catalogued as undated; the covering letter in clear fixes it at 22 August 1626. DECODE R1190 has the same letter with the status “Decrypted”. We checked the record: it holds three images, an empty documents list, no transcription, no key and no decipherer. Where the status comes from is unknown. If an earlier reading turns up, the credit here becomes verification rather than solution.

02 The key and its test

K6A is a homophonic syllabary (fer = de, xer = me, fan = que, pon = co) with numbers and a nomenclator. The first part of the letter (ff. 13r–14v, line 17) was transcribed and read, 20 values were added from it alone (for example hija for 199, ll for c‡), and the key was frozen by SHA-256 before the rest was transcribed.

The held-out part (f. 14v.21–27 and f. 15r, 542 tokens, 20.7%) was measured once. With no contemporary decipherment, the measure is the share of decoded letters covered by Spanish words of five letters or more: 0.506, against 0.144 for the best of 200 keys shuffled within length classes. The criterion fixed in advance was at least 0.60 and a margin of 0.30. The margin is met (0.362), the threshold is not, so the key is partial. The ceiling is about 0.64: a perfect contemporary plaintext of the same series scores 0.636. Nine code tokens of the held-out part have no value and each cuts a segment.

03 The reading

The reading was made after the measurement and counted by script on the marked text. Of 1,001 words, 906 are certain, 47 probable, 13 restored and 35 unresolved: 953 read without restorations, 95.2%. A blind proof-reading of 13 sign forms, answered by Brice Dudout and fixed in advance, settled hol = Su Majestad, gli = buena, pr = for and the two forms of “t”. The three “hol” of the held-out part were then read “Su Majestad” by the value established outside it; this is a reading decision only, and without them the count is 94.9%. The Spanish and an English translation are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • About 54 line ends are lost in the gutter of ff. 13v and 14v and are not counted.
  • Codes with no value: 22‡, 197, bir, B_, dol, yil, 229, 102, Qar, per, hen, pes; bil is probably “V.E.”. Each occurs once, so a value from the sense would be a guess.
  • The square sign is “mi” in the key and “merced” by the sense on f. 14r.26; the conflict is left open.
  • The two forms of “t” (p and g) cannot be told apart at 120 dpi except where the proof-reading settled them.
  • No edition was found: Elliott and de la Peña, Memoriales y cartas del Conde Duque de Olivares, has no letter to the Duke of Alba in 1626 by its table of contents (the text was not seen).

Olivares to the Duke of Alba, Madrid, 22 August 1626 (BNE MSS/10249 ff. 13r-15r)

Olivares tells the Duke of Alba of his griefs of summer 1626: the death of a young Guzmán nephew meant for the Church; his daughter María's stillbirth, her recovery, then her death; her virtue. He keeps her widower Ramiro, Marquis of Toral, as son and heir of his house: the dukedom of Medina de las Torres on the commandery of Caravaca, the office of sumiller de corps given by the King of his own motion; he puts his life in the King's service and hopes the Duke his son will behave wisely.

So I wish it, and (in clear) may God keep Your Excellency as I desire. Madrid, 22 August 1626. Don Gaspar de Guzmán.
