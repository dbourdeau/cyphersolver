Spanish despatches of 1593: Miranda, Sessa, Ibarra

BnF Français 3983 nos. 45, 79 and Français 3984 nos. 47, 68 · Spain’s management of the Estates of 1593 and the Infanta’s candidature · catalogued as undeciphered

Two ciphers, each with a key Tomokiyo published as an image · and the office’s own key, with the nomenclature he lacks, found in another volume through DECODE

Arya Sanketbhai Patel · solved in part 19 September 2026 · updated 21 September 2026

solved in part

Summary. Four Spanish despatches of 1593 — the Conde de Miranda to the duque de Feria, the duque de Sessa to Diego de Ibarra, Sessa to Philip II, and Ibarra from Paris — are catalogued as undeciphered, cited only by piece number. The BnF dépouillement gives their folios, and the leaves were then found on the images: ff. 98, 162, 108 and 145. They are in two ciphers, not one, and Satoshi Tomokiyo had already reconstructed a key for each — published, like the Maisse keys, as image files that a text dump of his page silently drops. But his table gives only the syllables; the nomenclature is missing, and on these leaves the code words are frequent enough to leave every line full of holes. The catalogue’s own instruction was “verify first … DECODE for Sessa”, and that is where the answer was: DECODE’s only two hits for these correspondents are not letters but keys, both pointing into the Nevers cipher book BnF fr. 3995. Folio 97 of that volume is the Spanish office’s own key, complete — alphabet, syllable table (identical to Tomokiyo’s, which it independently confirms) and the full nomenclature. With it the leaves read. Status: the cipher system is solved. nos. 45 and 79 are read across every cipher line on their leaves; no. 47 across nine of thirteen bands, corroborated by its own 1593 gloss; no. 68 decodes but will not run continuously, because its cipher’s nomenclature is unpublished. A scatter of groups remains open in each.

01 What the four pieces are

The catalogue cites piece numbers, which are not folios. BnF fr. 3983 and fr. 3984 are two volumes of the Collection Mémoires de la Ligue, and their catalogue notice carries a piece-by-piece dépouillement that gives both. Taken from it:

piecefoliothe entry
fr. 3983 no. 45f. 98“Lettre, avec chiffre, d’«el conde DE MIRANDA,… al duque de Feria,… De Napoles, a 25 de hebrero 1593». En espagnol.”
fr. 3983 no. 79f. 162“Lettre, avec chiffre, d’«el duque DE SESSO,… a don Diego de Ibarra,… De Roma, 11 de março 1593».”
fr. 3984 no. 47f. 108“Lettre, avec chiffre et commencement de déchiffrement, d’«el duque DE SESSA» à Philippe II. «De Roma, 30 de junio 1593». Copie.”
fr. 3984 no. 68f. 145“Lettre, avec chiffre, de «D. DIEGO DE IBARRA,… De Paris, a 10 de julio 1593».”

Neither volume’s canvases are labelled, and neither is foliated at a constant rate — some pieces are shot page by page, others as openings, and the leaves carry piece numbers and folio numbers in different corners. The map was built by reading folio numbers off a full-width top strip at intervals: for fr. 3983, c178 = f.98, c200 = f.111, c220 = f.122, c276 = f.156; for fr. 3984, c188 = f.103, c228 = f.123, c266 = f.144. That places the four targets at canvases 178, 286, 198 and 268, and each was confirmed on the image.

02 Two keys, published as images

Tomokiyo’s page for Philip II’s reign carries 32 images, two of which are the keys that matter here:

  • phelippes2.png, “Spanish Cipher (1592–1593)” — the cipher used among Philip II, Parma, Feria, Sessa, Acuña, Olivares, Fuentes, Tassis, Ibarra, Doria and Miranda, which he cites at fr. 3983 ff. 98, 158 and 162. That is nos. 45 and 79.
  • spanish3mansfeld.png, the syllabic cipher for the duke of Feria and the count of Mansfeld of July 1593, cited at fr. 3984 ff. 130 and 145. That is no. 68, and the leaf bears it out: its values run far higher (97, 98, 800, 7243) and it carries three-letter word codes — pra, rom, mon, xen, vul, cil — exactly as he describes.

As with the Maisse ciphers, the local copy of that page in this repository is a text dump, so the tables are absent from it and the leaves look keyless. He reconstructed the keys. He lists the four letters as undeciphered, which on cryptiana means no contemporary decipherment, not unsolved; no reading of them was published.

Applied, they work: the syllables are two-digit numbers for consonant + vowel, so 38 94 68. 31 73 40 89 23 91: reads yo · su · pon · go · que · ha · va · de · ser. But every line also carries code groups the table does not have.

03 The office key, and its nomenclature

The catalogue entry ends “Verify first. Tomokiyo’s Spanish keys of 1589–93; DECODE for Sessa.” DECODE’s record search returns exactly two hits for these correspondents, and neither is a letter:

DECODEnamesendertype
4076BNF_Français_3995_094Don Diego de IbarraKey — homophonic substitution, nomenclatures
4077BNF_Français_3995_096Don Diego de Ibarra, Duc de SessaKey — homophonic substitution, nomenclatures

Both point into BnF fr. 3995, the Nevers cipher key book, a volume this repository already fetches for the Lorraine cipher. Folio numbers there run about canvas − 91 in this stretch (c188 = f.97, c190 = f.98, c194 = f.101). Folio 97 is the key itself, and it is complete.

The sheet gives the letter alphabet with its symbol equivalents (a = 4 / ʒ, e = n / f / ʃ, g = 3, i = y / 6, n = x / ·, o = 7₂, r = y / :, s = ∆, u = o, y = q, z = ∂), the syllable table, a Nulles row — and, beneath, the thing Tomokiyo’s table does not have.

These are precisely the groups that stood unresolved on the leaves. The diacritic system resolves with them, and two of the marks are now confirmed on plaintext: the ring is −h−, giving 1̊9 = cha in achaque, and the dot above is final −l, giving 13 with a dot = bol in bolsa. A trailing dot is final −n, a colon final −r, a circumflex final −s, a bar means the number itself, an umlaut doubles the consonant, and ⊤ marks a nomenclature group.

04 The reading

With the office key all four leaves decode. Each was fetched at native resolution, deskewed — the search finds the row-profile variance maximum within ±3° — and cut into centred line strips.

no. 45, f. 98 — the Conde de Miranda to the duque de Feria, Naples, 25 February 1593

Every cipher line on the leaf is read — the one band that carries no reading is not cipher but a blank gap with show-through from the verso:

… el [alla] con este achaque, que siendo confidente … se po[drá] procurar … a costa de la bolsa … yo supongo que hava de ser … residencia … sobre esto … a que hava dado ya alguna [Roma] … no [se] verá lo que en esto convernà hazer, que yo me remito a su prudencia. Solo le suplico que … tuviere [lettera] me mande [causa]r … la persona que se eligirá … donde le po[drá] escribir a dar algun recado, si fue[re] … dar de manera que no se entienda, por que la hora que … le tengan por … perderá mucho[s] …

no. 79, f. 162 — the duque de Sessa to Diego de Ibarra, Rome, 11 March 1593

Every cipher line on the leaf is read. There is no clear text at all: the only non-cipher bands are the folio number 162 and the docket 11 de Março 93:

… de ver los testemonios que a todos levantan … a [legato] … de feria … constantes, si bien los que bivimos en [Roma] no desemos de … por [causa] que no dura 4 dias, ni pasa a los sucesores. Se hazen tan estas ordinarias … difi[cul]tades en la [election] … con todo eso es [negocio] tan importante … que espero que le a de favorecer … de [manera] que … conviene … que entre los … que siguen la … [di]versas pretensiones … en cada uno … si no me espanto … no puedo dexar de hazerlo, a lo menos, de que no estén [ministro]s conformes … ganados, de que no se pue[den] denfiar …

no. 47, f. 108 — Sessa to Philip II, Rome, 30 June 1593

This leaf carries the contemporary decipherer’s words above the cipher, and where both survive the decode agrees with the gloss — an independent check of the office key on a third letter of the group:

Read on: a ve esperando a ver si venia … de los … de que no tiene … de [legato] [mucho] … — against the gloss “si ver[n]ia alguna nueva cierta” and “fres[ca] carta”.

no. 68, f. 145 — Don Diego de Ibarra, Paris, 10 July 1593

A different cipher. This is the Feria–Mansfeld key, whose numbering is unrelated — ba 24, ca 25, da 10, la 59, ma 49, que 66, and de the single figure 8. The letter opens in clear and drops into cipher mid-sentence:

“… a la venida del mensagero que despache, porque espero que me dara V.S.a mas claridad de las cossas, porque en la primera me dise V.S.a” — then que aguarda cavallería y … y no continua … que aya recivido su …

05 What is open

All four letters now decode and are read in part. What is still outstanding:

  • nos. 45 and 79 are read across every cipher line on their leaves. What is open in them is a scatter of individual groups, marked · in the reading files — mostly rarer nomenclature and a few diacritics ambiguous at this resolution.
  • no. 47 is read across nine of its thirteen bands; the rest of the leaf is not transcribed.
  • The Feria–Mansfeld cipher has no published nomenclature, and that is the one real gap left. Its three-letter groups — pra, dur, hes, gar, pun, rom, mon, xen, vul, cil — carry much of no. 68’s sense, which is why its lines decode to clean syllables without becoming continuous Spanish. Tomokiyo tabulates none of them, noting only vas = segnor in passing. The obvious candidate was checked and excluded: fr. 3995 f. 101 (canvas 194), the large multi-column nomenclature on the leaves beside the Spanish office key, is Italian, not Spanish — Austria, Ambasciatore, Assemblea, Battaglia, Bisogno, Borbone, Borgo o Sobborgo, Cardinale di, Castello, Cattolici, Concilio generale, Corte di — and belongs to another correspondence.
  • The other leaves of fr. 3995 near the key — ff. 94, 96, 98 — hold further Spanish keys of the same office, including the one already identified here as “1592 / Chifre d’entre le Duc de Parme et le Roy Cath[olique]” at f. 98. They have not been worked.
