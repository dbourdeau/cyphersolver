Desmarets to an unnamed correspondent, Marly, 4 June 1710

A letter from Louis XIV’s Controller General of Finances during the 1710 peace talks, partly in a numerical code. The French between the figure lines is not their decipherment; the code is unread.

At a glanceAttempted · not read

The letter
Nicolas Desmarets, Controller General of Finances, to an unnamed correspondent, dated “a Marly ce 4 juin 1710”. Three pages.
Where
DECODE R10198 (“Desmaretz_1”), marked partially decrypted. The holding library is not named on the record.
Credit
The record was contributed to DECODE by Alexander Pillon.
The cipher
A numerical code: 471 groups, 183 of them distinct, running from 1 to 568, with a repeated six-group sequence. The first page and a half alternates figure lines with lines of French.
How it was tried
The French looks like an interlinear decipherment, but four independent alignment models found no consistent key between it and the figures. The same group stands under unrelated words, so the secret text is most likely in the numbers alone.
What it says
The clear French is about the Geertruidenberg peace talks. The King wants “la paix, mais seure”, and the Allies’ demands must not reach “des demembrements entiers”. The coded part is not read.
Still open
The whole code. No French ministerial key of 1700–1715 is on DECODE and no printed text was found. A clear copy in the Affaires étrangères Hollande volumes for 1710, or the recipient’s key, would open it.

01 The document

The images on DECODE are only 590 pixels wide, and some digits are uncertain (538 or 558; 115). Pages 2 (lower half) and 3 have ruled lines drawn between the figure lines, as if prepared for a decipherment that was never written. On page 3 the last group, 111, is followed on the same line by “Je suis tres parfaitement Monsieur Votre tres humble et tres obeissant serviteur”, in the same hand as the French lines above. The recipient is not named.

02 What the French lines say

Read on its own, the prose is one continuous text. It opens: “On a voulu tenter un evenement, dans l’esperance qu’il pourroit estre heureux, qu’il seroit changer la face des affaires presentes. Soit prudence soit impossibilité on n’a rien fait, mais il y a grande apparence que ce qu’on n’a pas tenté volontairement deviendra forcé”. It goes on to the courier held back and the powers now sent, “assez estendu”, though “on ne s’y rendra pas absolument”. It asks for guarantees and explanations about the further demands: “on veut la paix, ou la veut seure, mais on veut encore menager l’honneur de son gouvernement”. It then states the condition. The demands must not go “a des demembrements entiers” and must be held to “peu de chose au dela des preliminaires”. It closes: this is “un relachement qui peut produire la paix, ce qui n’auroit pas esté selon toutes les apparences si l’on estoit demeuré fermes dans les premieres resolutions.” The subject is the French negotiation at Geertruidenberg, March to July 1710.

03 Why the French is not the decipherment

If the French were written over the figures that encode it, each group would stand for a fixed word, syllable or letter run, apart from homophones and nulls. Four models were fitted to the 34 glossed lines:

  • per-line EM with each group emitting 0–9 letters;
  • one global alignment of all the text against all the groups, with a length prior;
  • per-line soft EM with bonuses for word boundaries;
  • word-level EM, with each group emitting zero, one or two whole words.

None converges on a key. 111 falls under paix, seure, selon, on and apparence, and 231 under toutes, fermes and la. The run 209 92 85 sits under both “vostre courier” and “inevitable, peu”. “Demandes ulterieures” occurs twice, over 128 78 65 343 147 and over 209 64 321 161 27. A line-offset test finds only a weak excess of shared groups between lines that share French words (0.77 against 0.49 for any pair, on 22 pairs).

The numbers themselves are not decoration. There are 183 distinct groups in 471, where a random fill of the range would give about 320. The commonest are 111 (13 times), 224 (11), 347 and 56 (10 each). The six-group run 119 101 402 49 197 424 occurs twice, first under “dans les premieres resolutions” and again in the unglossed tail. That is the one hint that the coded text may deal with the same matter.

04 What would finish it

  • A clear copy or minute: Archives des Affaires étrangères, Correspondance politique Hollande for 1710 (the Torcy, Huxelles and Polignac correspondence), or Desmarets’s papers. Boislisle’s Correspondance des contrôleurs généraux III (1897) was not searched here.
  • The key held on the recipient’s side, if the recipient can be identified.
  • Better images: the 590-pixel scans leave a few digits in doubt.

A ciphertext-only attack on a homophonic code of about 570 values from 471 groups is not realistic.
