Marly → unnamed correspondent · numerical code · 4 June 1710

Desmarets to an unnamed correspondent, Marly, 4 June 1710

DECODE R10198 (“Desmaretz_1”, contributed by Alexander Pillon): three pages, signed by Nicolas Desmarets, Controller General of Finances. The holding library is not named on the record.

Deciphered with the table Satoshi Tomokiyo published on Cryptiana, as suggested in issue #28 by yuanyi-350: read as one continuous stream, 456 of the 471 groups (96.8%) are in the table and the letter reads as French. Twelve group types are open.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 9 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: complete

Summary. The letter is dated “a Marly ce 4 juin 1710” and signed Desmaretz. Its first pages alternate a line of French prose with a line of dotted numbers, 34 pairs; then come 22 lines of numbers with nothing between them. The first version of this page, posted on 21 September 2026, said the French was not the decipherment of the figures and that no key existed. That was wrong. Issue #28 on the repository (by yuanyi-350, 9 October 2026) pointed to the table that Satoshi Tomokiyo published on Cryptiana (section 11B), built from the letter itself with Alexandre Pillon’s alignment, and noted what the earlier tests had missed: the 471 groups have to be read as one stream, and the line breaks are only layout. I checked it. Applied to the whole stream, the table has a value for 456 of the 471 groups (96.8%), and the letter, tail included, reads as French from “on a voulu tenter un evenement” to “si l’on estoit demeure fermes dans les premieres resolutions”. Twelve kinds of group (15 tokens) are not in the table. The table was reconstructed from this letter, so this is a check against the sheet’s own decipherment, not an independent break. Status: deciphered with Tomokiyo’s key; 96.8% of groups attested, twelve group types open.

01 The document

DECODE R10198 (“Desmaretz_1”) is three pages from Nicolas Desmarets, Controller General of Finances, to an unnamed “Monsieur”. The images on DECODE are only 590 pixels wide, so a few digits are uncertain (538 or 558; 115). Pages 2 (lower half) and 3 have ruled lines between the figure lines, as if prepared for a decipherment. On page 3 the last group is followed on the same line by the clear closing “Je suis tres parfaitement Monsieur Votre tres humble et tres obeissant serviteur”. The code is a table of about 570 values: single letters with homophones, syllables and a few short words (“vous”, “paix”, “dans”).

02 The reading

Tomokiyo’s table (Cryptiana, Cipher Used in Louis XIV’s Reign, 11B) is an image. I transcribed it into targets/desmarets1710/tomokiyo_decode.py and decoded the transcription of the 471 groups as one run, without line breaks. The result, with the words divided by hand (reading.txt):

On a voulu tenter un evenement dans [l’esper]ance qu’il pourroit estre heureux, qu’il feroit changer la face des affaires presentes. Soit prudence soit impossibilite, on n’a rien fait, mais il y a grande apparence que ce qu’on n’a pas tente volontairement deviendra force … Peu de semaines feront connoistre ce qui en arrivera. C’est cependant la raison qui a fait differer de renvoyer vostre courier. …

Le pouvoir qu’on vous envoie est assez estendu et on ne s’y tiendra pas absolument. Si vous pouvez venir a bout d’avoir des suretes et des eclaircissements sur les demandes ulterieures: on veut la paix, on la veut sure, mais on veut encores menager l’honneur du gouvernement. Enfin pourvu que les demandes ulterieures n’aillent pas a des demembrements entiers et qu’on les borne a peu de chose au dela des preliminaires, on s’y portera. … Il ne se [con]clura pas sans bien des explications et des conferences, mais voila au moins un relachement qui peut produire la paix, ce qui n’aur[oit] pas este selon toutes les apparences si l’on estoit demeure fermes dans les premieres resolutions.

The first part is the text already written between the figure lines; the last 22 lines of figures decipher to the second part, which has no French over it on the sheet. Tomokiyo notes the same: the secretary deciphered on a separate sheet and wrote the clean text on the original out of step with the figures, and the letter was printed in L’intermédiaire des curieux 50 (1904), which remarked that the last lines carry no plaintext. The sense is the French position at Geertruidenberg: the King wants peace, a safe peace, but means to keep “l’honneur du gouvernement”, and will agree if the Allies’ further demands stop short of “demembrements entiers”.

What is not clean. The decoded stream has local misfits that come from the table or from the transcription of it: for example “heureux” comes out with V and X for the last two letters, “presentes” as pre followed by letters that do not spell it, and “le p…n qu’on vous… envoie” has an unread word. A few cells are queried in Tomokiyo’s own table (463 “temps?”, 25 “S?”), and the 590-pixel images leave some digits doubtful. Twelve group types are not in the table at all: 94 (“ce”, three places), 539 (“l’esper”) and 565 (“oit”) are fixed by the context; 179+534 (in “differer”), 133 (“aplanir”), 65 and 147 (“renvoyer vostre courier”), 189, 229, 23 and 559 are plausible readings only, and 559 may be a null.

03 Why the first attempt failed

On 21 September I aligned each French line with the figure line under it, with four models (per-line EM, one global alignment, per-line soft EM with word-boundary bonuses, word-level EM). None converged: 111 fell under paix, seure and on, and “demandes ulterieures” sat over different figures in its two places. Those failures were real but the conclusion drawn from them was not. The French is a decipherment, but it is not written line against line with the figures, so a per-line alignment has nothing consistent to learn. Read as a single stream the groups fall out in order. The numbers are a genuine code (183 types in 471 tokens), and the repeated six-group run 119 101 402 49 197 424 is “les premieres resolutions”, which occurs in the glossed lines and again in the tail.

04 What remains

  • The twelve group types above, which need the original table or another letter in the same code.
  • An independent check. The table was built from this letter, so it cannot confirm itself; another letter of the 1710 series in the same code would.
  • Better images of the three pages: the 590-pixel scans leave a few digits in doubt.
  • L’intermédiaire des curieux 50 (1904) was not seen here, and Boislisle’s Correspondance des contrôleurs généraux III (1897) was not searched.
