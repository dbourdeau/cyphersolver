Siruela to Philip IV, 19 May 1638

A Spanish ambassador at Genoa tells the king in cipher that a marquis is delaying money for munitions, read with a key rebuilt on neighbouring letters that survive with clear copies.

The letter
Juan de Velasco y la Cueva, Count of Siruela, Spanish ambassador at Genoa, to Philip IV, 19 May 1638, a signed original.
Where
Archivo General de Simancas, Estado, legajo 3594, no. 156 (PARES 3586918). It is one of 20 cipher letters of catalogue entry 755.
The cipher
The numerical cipher of the Genoa embassy in 1638: vowels 16–20, syllables in series of five numbers (25 ba, 30 ca, 35 sa, 40 da, 45 la), disguised syllables, single letters and code signs such as 85/86 = que.
How it was deciphered
Other 1638 letters of the same bundle survive with a contemporary clear copy next to them. The key was rebuilt on three of those pairs, frozen, and tested on a held-out pair (3594,160 against 159): 0.896 of characters and 0.784 of words, against a 99th percentile of 0.343 and 0.204 for 200 permuted keys.
What it says
The king had ordered the Marquis Pablo Jerónimo Pallavicino to advance the money for munitions. The marquis was stalling, and Velasco warns that if he fails his suppliers they will fail him in turn.
Still open
One code sign (197, probably a name) is unresolved, and 205 is restored as haciendo. The images were not seen here.
Credit
Brice Dudout rebuilt the key, ran the held-out tests and read the letter, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents.

01 The letter

In the spring of 1638 the Spanish ambassador at Genoa had to buy munitions on the spot. The king had ordered the Marquis Pallavicino (“Palavesín”) to advance the money, and the marquis was stalling. The bundle keeps, as nos. 154 and 155, a letter of Pallavicino to Velasco dated 25 May 1638, presumably the sequel. Most of Velasco’s other 1638 cipher letters in the bundle have their clear copy beside them (143/142, 146/145, 147/144, 158/157, 160/159, 169/168). No. 156 has none among items 110–242, so it is the reading added here. The letters of 1643–1651 in entry 755 are in other ciphers and were not attempted.

02 The key and its tests

Key K3f was built step by step on the pairs 146/145, 143/142 and 169/168 and frozen with its SHA-256 before each test. The earlier held-out tests (169 later pages and 158/157) gave 0.75–0.84 of characters, below the criterion fixed in advance (0.85 of characters and 0.75 of words). The causes were code words still missing from the key and confusion of two signs of the second hand (a tall long-s for s, a small loop for a). The final pre-registered test used 3594,160, whose transcription was frozen before its clear copy was opened: 0.896 of characters and 0.784 of words, so the criterion was met. Applied unchanged to 3594,147, the previous key version recovered its clear copy at 0.923 of characters and 0.850 of words.

03 The reading

Of the 94 words of the cipher part, 89 are certain, three are probable (marqués, me, faltando), one is restored from the sense and one is unresolved: 93 of 94, or 98.9%, read as sense. Code sign 250 stands for “marqués de Leganés” in an April letter of the same bundle, but the singular verbs here point to Pallavicino, so we read “el marqués” and mark it probable. The full Spanish text and an English translation are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Code 197 occurs only here. It names the person in whose absence Pallavicino was to advance the money, probably the governor of Milan or another minister. We leave it unresolved.
  • Code 205, followed by a stroke, is restored as haciendo from “las instancias que le estoy haciendo”.
  • me before acudiese is taken for the cipheris read for the cipher’srsquo;s mo, probably a copying slip.
  • The transcription was made by AI from the PARES images and not checked by a person against them here.

In the spring of 1638 the Spanish ambassador at Genoa had to lay in munitions on the spot. The King had ordered the Marquis Pablo Jerónimo Pallavicino (“Palavesín”) to advance him the money; the Marquis was stalling. Velasco told the King in cipher: if he failed his suppliers, they would fail him in turn.

Translation

translation.md

# English translation

[On the 29th of last month I reported to Your Majesty on the delays and doubts] that the Marquis Pablo Jerónimo Palavesín was raising over carrying out the royal order in which Your Majesty was pleased to command him, in the absence of ‹…›, to supply me with the money needed to lay in whatever munitions could be had here.

And now I refer to the copy of the paper with which he has answered the many requests I keep making to him to carry out Your Majesty’s royal order; for if I fail the persons with whom I had made contracts, neither will they be able to keep their engagements to me,

[in plain text:] of which it seemed right to inform Your Majesty, whose Catholic and royal person God keep as Christendom has need. Genoa, 19 May 1638. [Signature of the Count of Siruela.]
