Arend Willem van Reede to the Hereditary Prince, 1796–1807 (KHA A35-1232, A32-549)

Nine code passages from the Orange family’s envoy in Berlin were left undeciphered; his own key to one letter shows a book code whose book is not named, so they stay unread.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 26 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The letters
Arend Willem baron van Reede, the Orangist envoy in Berlin, to the Hereditary Prince Willem Frederik (1796–1807) and to Princess Wilhelmina (1797–1801), in French with runs of numbers; several are written over a letter in invisible ink.
Where
Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, A35-1232 (scans 143592, 143603, 144339, 144352, 144361, 144448–49, 144507) and A32-549 (171667, 171744). Not on DECODE.
The cipher
In 1796 a “Chiffre Littéral” in which a short run of numbers stands for a whole sentence, with dotted groups such as 39.97.16: a book code keyed to a shared printed text, beside a short list of numbers for names. From 1797 a larger numbered list with prefixes and superscripts.
How it was tried
All nine passages and four glossed letters were transcribed at full size. Van Reede’s 1789–92 code and the Euler word list of 1795–96 were tested on the glossed runs; neither fits (3 of 13 and 3 of 32 decoded words in the run’s own gloss).
Why it resists
The book of the 1796 code is not named anywhere in the file, and the later list has only about fifteen glossed values.

02 Van Reede’s Explication

The letter of 15 August 1796 carries its own key. Its run (1.), seven groups, is explained as “envoyé à Ivoy un mémoire pour établir & justifier les principes d’une nouvelle coalition basant sur la reconnoissance de Louis XVIII comme seul moyen d’inspirer de la confiance à la Prusse”; run (3.) as “qu’il n’y avoit rien à faire dès que l’Angleterre n’adoptoit pas en plein la marche proposée”. Seven groups for some two hundred letters cannot be a letter cipher or a word code; with the dotted groups in the text (“39.97.16”, “29.17.19.37”) and Van Reede’s words, “Je me sers, Monseigneur, du Chiffre Littéral usité pour ma correspondance particulière avec Elle”, the system reads as a book code, each group pointing into a printed text that both men held. The text is not named in the file.

The Prince’s own drafts in the same folder (Berlin 11 June, 2 July 1796 and others) are the clear texts of letters he sent in cipher to Van Reede; the enciphered versions are not here.

03 What was tried

  • Transcription. The nine passages and the glossed letters of 15 August 1796, 27 September 1796 (the Prince’s draft, meanings in parentheses), 19 May and October–November 1798 were transcribed at full size with every mark (runs_a.txt, runs_b.txt).
  • Known keys. test_keys.py decoded every run with Van Reede’s 1789–92 code to Wilhelmina (targets/reede1789) and with the Euler word list of 1795–96 (targets/wilhelmina1795), and scored both on the fifteen glossed runs: 3 of 13 and 3 of 32 decoded words stand in the run’s own gloss, against 2 and 0 in another run’s. The glossed values contradict both (13 is Athlone here, “difficile” in the Euler list; 6/52 is Haugwitz in 1798, where the Euler list has 52 = une).
  • Print. Colenbrander’s Gedenkstukken II and III, searched in full text, print no letter of Van Reede to the Prince.

04 What remains open

  • All nine passages, about 580 groups: unread for lack of key material. A reading of the 1796 passages needs the book; the Explication gives plaintext for one letter only, too little to identify a book by itself.
  • The 1797–1801 list (groups to 1951) has about fifteen glossed values in the file.
