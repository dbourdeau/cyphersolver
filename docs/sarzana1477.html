Sarzana → the Sforza court, Milan · homophonic cipher of about 35 signs · c. 1477–1479

A Sforza informer at Sarzana, seven cipher letters (c. 1477–1479)

Go to the full text ↓

Seven undated cipher letters about messer Ludovico, Tomasino and Corsica; a key annealed here from one letter alone read the other six without a re-solve, and was then refined across all seven.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 November 2026

Method: ciphertext-only break · Extent: partial (90% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count)

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · partial (90% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count)

The letters
Seven short letters wholly in cipher. Five are dated at Sarzana, one probably at Sarzana (its place name is torn) and one at Florence, each with day and month but no year. The writer reports to the Sforza court on messer Ludovico, Tomasino and Corsica.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Milano, Carteggio Visconteo-Sforzesco, cart. 1598 nos. 88, 98, 99, 101, 102, 103 and 112. Online as DECODE R5562, R5572, R5573, R5575, R5576, R5577 and R5586, with status N/A.
The cipher
A homophonic substitution of about 35 signs: letters, digits, crossed ligatures, and o and q with a cross. Each plaintext letter has one to three signs. An o-with-cross and the digit-like 6 and 10 are nulls set between doubled letters. No code words were seen.
How it was read
The key was annealed from the 686 signs of R5562 alone, then read the six other letters without a re-solve. It was then refined across all seven: look-alike signs flagged by a language model were checked on the images, and the nulls were identified.
What it says
“meser Ludovico e Tomasino sono in molte diferentie, et ogni volta piu cresce l’odio e la difidentia fra loro” (R5577).
Still open
A few words per letter, damaged lines in R5586, and the date signs at its foot. The year is inferred, not written.

01 The letters

The cipher letters of cartella 1598 in the Sforza archive fall into many sign families, each with its own key. Catalogue 1311, worked in targets/sforza1598, was split into those families. These seven letters are family B (catalogue 2629). They share one hand and one key:

  • R5562 (no. 88), 5 December, 686 signs
  • R5572 (no. 98), 13 March, Sarzana
  • R5573 (no. 99), 23 March, Sarzana
  • R5575 (no. 101), 20 February, Sarzana
  • R5576 (no. 102), 7 June, Sarzana
  • R5577 (no. 103), 17 July, Sarzana
  • R5586 (no. 112), 8 February, Florence

Every letter is wholly in cipher, with no clear text and no contemporary decipherment on the leaf. The DECODE scans of the Milan leaves are not republished here, so the page prints the cipher as transcribed signs.

02 What they say

The writer sits at Sarzana, close to messer Ludovico, and reports to the court in Milan. He asks favours of “Madona et el Signore”, so the letters are not addressed to them directly. Three examples:

De qua non acade altro al presente, se non che meser Ludovico e Tomasino sono in molte diferentie, et ogni volta piu cresce l’odio e la difidentia fra loro … Io andero questo septembre a Volterra per observare la fede … Io bisogno de ducati quaranta per espedire certi mei strecti bisogni. (R5577, 17 July)

Nothing else happens here at present, except that messer Ludovico and Tomasino are at great odds, and their hatred and distrust grow every time … I shall go to Volterra this September to keep faith … I need forty ducats to settle certain pressing needs of mine.

Meser Ludovico ha mandato a Seno uno Bartolomeo Borella, senese, per intendere e praticare … Tomasino manda uno Marcelino in Corsica per sulevare li Corsi. (R5572, 13 March)

Messer Ludovico has sent to Siena one Bartolomeo Borella, a Sienese, to find out and negotiate … Tomasino is sending one Marcelino to Corsica to raise the Corsicans.

El messo de Tomasino e tornato de Corsica; diceva haver trovato e lasato quele cose in pacifico … sero a Milano de poi Pasqua. (R5573, 23 March)

Tomasino’s messenger has come back from Corsica; he said he had found and left things there at peace … I shall be in Milan after Easter.

The full Italian reading and an English translation of all seven letters are under Full texts below.

03 The setting

The names fit the exile of Ludovico Sforza at Pisa, from 1477 to September 1479. In those years Bona of Savoy governed for her son Gian Galeazzo, and the letters’ “Madona” and “el Signore” would be them. The other names fit as well:

  • Tommasino Campofregoso was lord of Sarzana.
  • Milan held Corsica from 1464 to 1481.
  • A Nicodemo handles the writer’s reports. Nicodemo Tranchedini was writing from Genoa in 1478.

The working date is therefore 1477–1479. The writer arranged to be given the chancellorship of Volterra and accepted it as cover (R5575). (This background is not from the letters and was not checked in the archival registers.)

04 How the key was found

The letters of cartella 1598 were first sorted by sign hand. R5562 was transcribed by hand: 686 signs, 31 distinct (about 35 across the seven letters). Lasry’s homophonic solver, run headless with an Italian language model, then annealed a key from R5562 alone.

  • Control: the real text scored 24.09 M against 10.73 M for the same signs shuffled, and two seeds agreed.
  • The key held: applied unchanged to the six other letters, it put 54–67% of their letters inside words of four or more letters, against 10–17% for shuffled signs.
  • Review: a 5-gram model flagged 130 positions where another sign would read better. Two look-alike merges in the transcription explained most of them: the underlined “rr4” (o) against the plain “r4” (m), and the S-shaped h against the 5-shaped v. Of 43 proposed corrections, 38 were confirmed on crops of the images and 5 were dropped.
  • Nulls: last, the o-with-cross and the digit-like 6 and 10 turned out to be nulls between doubled letters (“mes·so”, “Volter·ra”, “dis·se”, “podes·se”).

The read share is 0.897, measured with a strict segmenter that counts chancery spellings as unread. By letter, it runs from 0.850 (R5586) to 0.932 (R5576).

05 What remains open

  • R5586: the ends of lines 9, 11 and 12 are lost in creases and holes, and a word in line 13 is struck out.
  • R5562: the left edge of the date line is torn, so the place name is incomplete. It is probably Sarsane.
  • R5586: the date line at the foot is in numeral-like signs outside the letter key.
  • Single words that do not read: “mamiglio” (probably famiglio) and “si s’gain” (R5562); “mascano” (R5575); “canteera” and “vala” (R5573); “promqso” (R5577); “Miser Andremto” (R5572). Settling them needs a box-level transcription of those lines.
  • The readings were checked as sentences by the model only. A second reader’s review has been requested.

# R5575 (cart. 1598 no. 101), 20 February

Respondo a la vostra ricevuta heri. Nicodemo ha sempre confortato meser Ludovico circa la specialita del signore, ma ben gli ha scripto e mi ha mandato a dire che certi mesani chi sono tra el signore e lui sono casone che le sue cose non {mas}cano bene. Meser Ludovico per tali avisi e stato molti di in suspecto de li soi, e spesso mi mostrava la letera de Nicodemo; il perche, vedendolo io in tal fantasia, ordinai che mi fusse mandata la electione di la cancelaria di Volterra, la quale acceptai non per voglia che io ne havesse, ma per estinguere de mi ogni sinistro umbramento; e cosi e succeduto. El tempo non comincia fino a octobre, ne ancho anora gli andero, se me ne potero valere; tratanto seguiro l'ordine nostro cum Nicodemo. Non mi pare habiate a fare altra provisione, perche mi pare haver tolta via ogni rugine et a meser Ludovico; e mi incresce che io habia acceptato dicto oficio; nondimeno fate como vi pare meglio. Iovan Galeaso mi disse questi di a Firensa haver havuto letera da Iovan Coscia che volese mandare uno de li soi al duca Iovani per bona casone, e che gli voleva mandare uno Piero del Cerreto, famiglio di l'arcivescovo, {coqesum}, intrinsico e pratico di la lingua e del paese. Sarsane, die vigesimo februarii, etcetera.

# R5575 (cart. 1598 no. 101), 20 February (English)

I reply to yours received yesterday. Nicodemo has always encouraged messer Ludovico concerning the lord's favour, but he has {written} to him, and sent word to me, that certain go-betweens who stand between the lord and him are the reason his affairs do not go well. # R5577 (cart. 1598 no. 103), 17 July (English)

Nothing else happens here at present, except that messer Ludovico and Tomasino are at great odds, and every time the hatred and distrust between them grows. The podestà of Ponsano, whose messenger I used to send the letters to the commissioner, will not send them any more, so if I have not written to you, do not be surprised. I shall go to Volterra this September to keep faith, but when the office is done I shall come back; for so I have promised messer Ludovico. I need forty ducats to settle certain pressing needs of mine; please ask Madonna and the Lord that I be granted it, and if it cannot be, in any case I mean to be a good servant of your most illustrious lordships. Sarzana, 17 July, etc.
