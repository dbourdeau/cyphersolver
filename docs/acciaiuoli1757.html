Lisbon → Rome · homophonic cipher of two-digit figures with a four-digit nomenclator · September 1758 – 1760

Nuncio Acciaiuoli from Lisbon to the Secretariat of State, 1758–60

Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Portogallo 117, items 1–8 · DECODE R25, R195–R201, eight cipher leaves, ten pages · catalogued as partially decrypted (catalogue item 240)

George Lasry recovered the key in 2020 and posted a letter-by-letter decryption with the code groups left as numbers. Here that decryption was divided into Italian words and its code groups were fixed from the three leaves deciphered at the time. The Secretariat of State’s clear decipherments of the other five, filed on DECODE as images, were transcribed and aligned with the figures; all eight letters now give Italian sentences.

At a glanceKnown key applied · complete

The letters
Letters of Filippo Acciaiuoli, nuncio in Lisbon, and of the abbé Testa, 1757–61; eight cipher leaves.
Where
Portogallo 117. DECODE has the eight leaves, transcribed digit by digit in 2016–17, and kept them “partially decrypted”.
The cipher
Two-digit homophones, the digit 6 a null, and four-digit code words that all begin with 2.
How it was read
Lasry’s 2020 key and code-group decryption gave no Italian. Three leaves (10, 24, 31 October 1758) carry the Secretariat’s clear decipherment; lined up with the cipher it fixes 25 code words, and the other five leaves then read as running Italian.
What it says
On 12 September 1758 the nuncio reported that the king, coming back by night from a lady, had been fired on in his calesse and wounded in the right arm. Later letters follow the wound, the Jesuits and the 1760 marriage of the Princess of Brazil.
Still open
About eighty code groups that occur only once or twice, and a few passages in the two long leaves where the transcription has slipped.
Credit
George Lasry reconstructed the key in 2020.

01 The documents and the key

The volume is “Lettere di mons. Acciaiuoli, nunzio in Portugallo, e dell’abbate Testa, 1757–1761”, ff. 2–585. DECODE dates all eight records 18 January 1757, which is the first date in the volume. The cipher leaves themselves run from 12 September 1758 to 1760:

“Clear on the leaf” means the decipherment written above the figures. The Secretariat’s separate clear decipherments of R25 (ff. 90r–91r), R195 (f. 94r), R196 (f. 97r), R200 (ff. 108r–109r, second copy ff. 110r–111r) and R201 (f. 161, second text f. 162) are among the DECODE attachments of the records, with working drafts; they also give the dates of R195, R200 and R201.

The key is Lasry’s reconstruction (DECODE, 24 October 2020). Letters and common syllables have fixed two-digit homophones: a 04 38 55, e 84, i 08 53, o 81, s 88, il 43 91, che 39, non 14, de 71 78, qu 70 87 90. The digit 6 is a null, scattered freely, and the code words are four digits beginning with 2. No two-digit element begins with 2 or contains a 6, so the figures cut up only one way.

03 The attempt on the king, 12 September 1758

The first leaf (117/1, R25) is the longest. The reading follows the Secretariat’s clear decipherment (“Decif. li 11 Ottobre”), aligned word by word with the key decode of the figures; [?word] marks a word the decipherer wrote that the figures as transcribed do not confirm.

… e si racconta che, venendo il Re da una visita di una Dama (della quale già molto per questo da anni vi si parlava) in calesse con Pietro Texeira, [?Vilasso], domestico favorito (perciò Cavaliere dell'Ordine di Cristo e sotto cavallerizzo), fossero sparate contro il calesse tre schioppettate, per le quali restò ferito malamente il cavalcante e la mula del bilancino che egli montava; il Texeira ferito a morte (essendosi detto domenica che lo avevano sagramentato), ed il Re con molte ferite nel braccio destro. … La Dama è delle primarie famiglie, sorella del Marchese di Tavora che fu Vice Re all'Indie, maritata al di lui figlio primogenito. Si parla che venga il colpo da di lei parenti, o che venga da nemici del Texeira, creduto si governatore nella sedia. Il fatto non si mette in dubbio.

“…it is told that the King, coming back from a visit to a Lady (about whom, for this reason, people have been talking for years) in a calash with Pedro Teixeira, [?Vilasso], his favourite valet (for that reason Knight of the Order of Christ and under-equerry), three musket shots were fired at the calash, by which the postilion and the mule of the leader he was riding were badly wounded; Teixeira was wounded to death (it was said on Sunday that he had received the sacraments), and the King had several wounds in the right arm. … The Lady is of the first families, sister of the Marquis of Távora who was Viceroy of the Indies, married to his eldest son. It is said that the blow comes from her relatives, or from enemies of Teixeira, who is believed to be the governor [of the King] in the chair. The fact is not doubted.” The Marquis of Angeja, the King’s confidant, then took the King to the Real Barraca. This is the attempt of the night of 3 September 1758, which Carvalho turned two months later into the arrest of the Távoras and the Duke of Aveiro and, in 1759, their execution at Belém.

04 The wound, September–October 1758

117/2 (R195, 19 Sept). The illness is the public story; the king is now said to have come not from the Marchioness but from “altra donna non nobile”. The arm is said to be broken. The Queen “non muterà cosa alcuna, poiché non ha da durare”, and the fleets held back for a year by the Secretary’s negligence are made to sail, “con danno del Re, e de' mercanti, e de' poveri capitani”. 117/3 (R196, 26 Sept). “Continua la pubblica voce sulla causa del male del Re, che da nessuno più si dubita, e ciascuno se ne persuade dal sentire, che ancora Sua Maestà resta per tutto il giorno a letto: Molti certo lo vedono, ma nessuno vede il braccio, che è fasciato.”

117/4 (R197, 10 Oct). Deciphered at the time: “Il Rè si è levato, ma mai [esce] tutto il giorno. Vogliono i più, che sia molto guasto nel Braccio, e che almeno resterà perpetuamente impedito.”

117/6 (R199, 24 Oct), deciphered at the time: “Benché si dica il Re assai meglio si vuole, che assolutamente il Braccio resterà impedito: parlando un suo confidente col Re sulla morte della Regina il Re gli disse: è stata buona Sorella, e che sono sicuro, che il Re sposerà la mia Figlia Maria Francisca, e che lo ha promesso alla Regina avanti, che ella morisse: ciò, che il Ministro di Spagna mostra è tutto il contrario … di tutto il resto non posso io compromettermi.” Barbara of Braganza, Queen of Spain and José’s sister, had died on 27 August 1758. The widowed Ferdinand VI did not marry Maria Francisca.

117/5 (R198, 31 Oct), deciphered at the time: the king has not had erysipelas, “ma un sacco di materia, che ha fatto la piaga”, with fever as a result. The king is very apprehensive; “il credere il male maggiore di quello, che si dice” is universal.

05 Carvalho, November 1758, and the marriage question, 1760

117/7 (R200, 28 Nov 1758) is the longest leaf; its cipher is crowded and much corrected, and the reading follows the Secretariat’s decipherment of 28 December, checked against a second copy and the draft. The Jesuits’ affair: the city is for them, but nobody dares speak. “La passione è tutta del Segretario de Carvalho, che veramente è dispotico, né ha chi gli resista, e pubblicamente si dice che il Re di Portogallo ne ha paura.” The Cardinal Patriarch trembles before him; the expulsion of Father Torres, the nuncio’s confessor, is “un altro motivo di diffidenza meco”. A lay brother “cacciato dalla Compagnia” by Torres “sta tutto il giorno da Carvalho, ed è quello che dà notizie, e con esso accende tutto il fuoco.”

117/8 (R201, 12 Feb 1760) is about the succession. The British ambassador is said to have no other errand than to thwart a marriage of the Princess of Brazil to the Infante Don Luis of Spain, and the Conde d’Oeiras (Carvalho) confers often with the Spanish ambassador; others think the king means her for Don Antonio, eldest of his legitimised brothers. The court of London “è molto allarmata” and fears for its trade from “uno della Casa di Spagna e di Borbone sul trono di Portogallo”. The letter ends: “Il paese freme, che vorrebbe la Principessa unita coll'Infante Don Pietro.” She married her uncle Pedro on 6 June 1760. Acciaiuoli was expelled from Portugal nine days later.

06 Method

The DECODE records were fetched with a logged-in session: images, the NiLan/Midas digit transcriptions, Lasry’s key and his decryption, which is one file attached to every record. The decryption was flattened into one letter string per leaf and split into Italian words by hand. Code words were fixed by aligning the three clear decipherments with the cipher below them, and then checked for consistency wherever the same code recurs in the other five leaves (2759 Re 11 times, 2300 tutto 12). The September working text, leaf by leaf, is targets/acciaiuoli1757/READING.md.

The reading audit of 22 October 2026 asked whether each letter builds sentences that can be translated. Four (R25, R195, R200, R201) did not: an open code broke almost every sentence. The same day the png attachments on their DECODE records, filed as “key and work images”, turned out to be the Secretariat’s clear decipherments and drafts. They were transcribed and aligned word by word with the key decode (texts/R*_align.tsv): in R25, 205 of 288 clear words are carried letter by letter by the figures and 53 by code groups whose values hold across the letters; the rest fall where DECODE’s digit transcription is garbled, and none is contradicted by the figures. R196’s decipherment (f. 97r) was transcribed on 23 October and agrees with the decode clause by clause. The readings and English translations are texts/R*_reading.md and texts/R*_en.md.

07 What remains open

  • Code 2339 in R200: the decipherer left “l’Ambasciatore di . . .” blank; the draft has “di Spagna”, read as [?Spagna].
  • Words the decipherer wrote that the figures as transcribed do not confirm, marked [?] in the readings: “Vilasso” in R25, “solamente assicurato” and “della gente di quei paesi” in R195; DECODE’s digit transcription is garbled at those places.
  • Code groups of R196 outside its decipherment’s wording.
  • Grades: codes from the decipherments on the leaves and from the Secretariat’s separate decipherments are H; the word division of the letters follows those decipherments.
  • The rest of Portogallo 117 (ff. 2–585) has more cipher than these eight leaves. It is not on DECODE and was not seen.

# R25, Acciaiuoli to the Secretariat of State, Lisbon, 12 Sept 1758

mule, the said Marquis took the King to the Real Barraca, not without much noise.

in the letter in clear; nor can I report more for now.

# R201, Cardinal Acciaiuoli to the Secretariat of State, Lisbon, 12 Feb 1760 (f. 280r)

Si va facendo generale il discorso per la Corte e Città che questo Ambasciatore d'Inghilterra non abbia altra
commissione che quella di frastornare il matrimonio, di cui si vuole che sia molto avanzato il trattato, tra questa
Principessa del Brasile e l'Infante Don Luigi di Spagna. Si vedono frequenti conferenze tra 'l Conte di Oeyras e Conte di
Masseda, Ambasciatore di Spagna, con attenzioni per parte del primo al secondo che a tutti paiono affettate.

Vogliono al contrario alcuni che non pensi il Re di Portogallo con detto Conte d'Oeyras a tal matrimonio, ma bensì a dare
la Principessa al Signor Don Antonio, primogenito de' tre fratelli di Sua Maestà legittimati, il quale infatti solamente
in questo anno, sotto pretesto di caccia, si trattiene in Salvaterra contro il costume, non essendovi l'Inquisitore suo
fratello neppure stato. Questo fa ora a molti parlare, specialmente tra li Ministri de' Principi, che fanno tutte le
diligenze per iscoprire cosa veramente sia.

L'Ambasciatore d'Inghilterra parlerà in apparenza per il commercio pregiudicato, ma in sostanza la Corte di Londra è
molto allarmata, perché teme il suo danno, particolarmente nel commercio, da uno della Casa di Spagna e di Borbone sul
trono di Portogallo, e per i tempi passati colla Spagna, e per i passati e presenti colla Francia. Il paese freme, che
vorrebbe la Principessa unita coll'Infante Don Pietro.

# R201, Cardinal Acciaiuoli to the Secretariat of State, Lisbon, 12 Feb 1760

The talk is becoming general at court and in the city that this English Ambassador has no other commission than to
upset the marriage, the treaty for which is said to be well advanced, between this Princess of Brazil and the Infante Don
Luis of Spain. Frequent conferences are seen between the Count of Oeiras and the Count of Masseda, Ambassador of Spain,
with attentions from the first to the second that seem affected to everyone.

Some on the contrary will have it that the King of Portugal, with the said Count of Oeiras, is not thinking of such a
marriage, but rather of giving the Princess to Don Antonio, the eldest of His Majesty's three legitimised brothers, who
in fact only this year, on the pretext of hunting, is staying at Salvaterra against custom, his brother the Inquisitor
not even having been there. This now makes many talk, especially among the ministers of the princes, who are doing
everything to find out what it really is.

The English Ambassador will speak in appearance for the commerce that is harmed, but in substance the court of London is
much alarmed, because it fears its own loss, particularly in commerce, from one of the House of Spain and of Bourbon on
the throne of Portugal, given past times with Spain and past and present times with France. The country is seething; it
would like the Princess united with the Infante Don Pedro.
