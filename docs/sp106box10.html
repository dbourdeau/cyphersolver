“della loro sicurezza” · “et … quelle de nuovo senza”

London, intercepted Italian letters · three-figure syllabic code · c. 1623–24 · code rebuilt from the codebreaker’s own grid; decoded, not read

The “Venetian Cifer” of a Jacobean codebreaker’s file, TNA SP 106/10

Go to the transcriptions ↓

Two intercepted Italian letters in a three-figure syllabic code, rebuilt from the English codebreaker’s own glosses and grid; phrases are deciphered, but the letters are not yet decoded through, so nothing reads as text yet.

At a glanceKnown key applied · none (key obtained, text not read)

The letters
Two Italian letters, sender and recipient unknown, written half in clear and half in code, intercepted in London. They sit in a file dated 1623–24.
Where
The National Archives, Kew, SP 106/10 ff. 167–169 and 139–140 (DECODE R664, R660); the codebreaker’s grid f. 125 (R658) and glossed strings f. 182 (R702 p. 2). DECODE lists them as anonymous ciphertexts, “1558/1625”.
The cipher
One three-figure group per syllable. Each consonant takes five groups in a row, one per vowel a e i o u; a few groups are whole words, with a superscript letter for the ending.
How it was deciphered
About 45 values were aligned from the codebreaker’s syllables written above the groups. His own grid of the code then gave 117 values and corrected four of them.
What it says
Fragments: della loro sicurezza, et hoggi terzo giorno quelle de nuovo senza, and on his sheet i colonelli, che possiamo prometter, di 24 di maggio.
Still open
Neither letter has been transcribed group by group and decoded through with the table; the writers are not identified. Checked on 23 October 2026: R664’s file holds two decoded phrases and one clear line, not the letter, so R664 is decoded, not read; R658 (the codebreaker’s grid), R702 p. 2 (his worksheet of glossed strings) and R701 p. 1 (his heading and word list) are working papers, not prose. No document of this entry reads yet. The way on is to transcribe R664 and R660 group by group and decode them with the rebuilt code.
Credit
The values are the contemporary codebreaker’s.

These letters were one part of a single codebreaker’s working file, which this site first treated as one entry. The file holds several different ciphers, and each now has its own page: the two-figure nomenclator of his key sheet R725 (sp106r725, which also dates the file), a second three-figure code used in a Venetian secretary’s letter of 1625 (sp106r722), a letter-and-figure syllabary (sp106r704), and a 1645 royalist letter that only shares the box (sp106r931).

DECODE lists each as an undated, anonymous, partly decrypted ciphertext. The other pages of R701 and R702 belong to the 1625 code (sp106r722), apart from a five-line letter cipher on R701 p. 2 (Esg frp rohxog x rygo fa ombhpeip …, about 120 letters), which a substitution solver run against five languages did not break and which is too short to go further.

02Values from the interlinear glosses

R660 and R664 are letters written half in cleartext and half in code. The writer enciphered the words that mattered and left the Italian connecting phrases in clear, which gave the English codebreaker his cribs, and gives them now. One whole line of R664 stands in the clear, and it is about the cipher itself:

“potrete continuare nel modo cominciato, osservando però il [mutamento … alle volte … col … numeri suppositi]”

The codebreaker wrote his solutions above the groups, syllable by syllable. Aligning those syllables against the figures gives the code back. About forty-five values were fixed this way, each by a word that admits no other division (260, 262, 263 and 459 as corrected by his grid, section 04):

03Method, and the check that the alignment is sound

All of this depends on matching the codebreaker’s interlinear syllables to the right groups, and a one-group drift would produce plausible nonsense. The alignment produced one apparent contradiction, and its resolution supports the rest: group 252 was glossed sen in one line and se in another, and 359 za in one line and n in two others. Both resolve if the word senza runs over the end of line 41 (252 se + 359 n closing the line, 560 za opening line 42), which is what the manuscript shows. After that correction no value conflicts with any other across lines 34–45. The images came from DECODE with the project’s login and had to be rotated before transcription. The codebreaker did the cryptanalysis four hundred years ago; the work here was to find the sheets on which he wrote it down, transcribe them and apply them.

04The codebreaker’s own table of “the Venetian Cifer”

Seven more records of the box, which DECODE lists as another set of anonymous ciphertexts (catalogue item 62), are his worksheets. On R701 he heads a list of word groups “Wordes marked with letters in the Venetian Cifer”: 369° nostro, 467° questo, 430a passata. The same words stand in the side column of R658, and R658 is his table of the code of R660 and R664: every group from 136 to 565, with a count of its occurrences below it and his value above it.

The grid shows how the code was made. Each consonant takes five consecutive rows in the order a e i o u, and each hundreds column a different consonant: 151–155 ra…ru, 251–255 sa…su, 351–355 sca…scu, 551–555 sta…stu; 260–264 ta…tu, 360–364 tra…tru, 460–463 va…vo, 560–564 za…zu; 433–437 la…lu, 142–146 na…nu, 242–246 pa…pu, 442–446 pra…pru, 542–545 qua…quo; the row x56 holds the bare vowels, x57–x59 single consonants (357 l, 359 n, 459 r), and a handful of groups are whole words (339 liga, 340 lettere, 439 per, 165 che, 465 quel), their ending given by a superscript letter. With the table, the code has 117 values, 102 of them written by the codebreaker and 15 predicted by the pattern.

The check is R702, where he wrote out group strings with his own glosses. Every one of its 46 groups is in the table, and every string decodes to his gloss:

356 318 436 143 435 → i·co·lo·ne·li “i colonelli” · 341 433 436 154 → del·la·lo·ro
252 359 518 253 → se·n·do·si “sendosi” · 167 252 459 253 → con·se·r·si “conservarsi”
165 245 556 253 156 526 445 534 261 459 → che·po·s·si·a·mo·pro·me·te·r “che possiamo prometter”

The same sheet glosses di 24 di maggio, del campo, per nome suo, sono capitate and efficacia: the letter he was working on was written in late May and spoke of a camp and of colonels. The table also corrects four values first aligned on R664 alone: 260 is ta, 262 ti, 263 to and 459 r (they had been taken as mento, to, ti and v), and 316 ta does not fit the ca…cu run it stands in.

The 1625 letter of the same file (sp106r722) is in a code built the same way but with another table (R701 p. 3: 244 ra, 245 re, 366–370 la…lu; here 244 is pi, 245 po, 433–437 la…lu). The words R701 lists under “the Venetian Cifer” are the words of R658’s side column, so on the sheets’ own evidence the name belongs to this code.

05Date

No date has been found in these two letters. The file they sit in is dated to 1623–24 by the vocabulary of the codebreaker’s other key, R725 (Marques Inichiosa, the Spanish ambassador of 1623–24; C. Barberino, cardinal from October 1623; Palatinato; see sp106r725), and R702 adds a 24 May and colonels, which would fit 1624. The date is taken as c. 1623–24, not proved for these letters.

06What is uncertain, and what remains

  • The values aligned from the glosses are C where a whole word fixes them (della loro sicurezza, senza, quelle de nuovo, che, et, volte, lettere, vostre) and M for single-syllable values seen once (245, 316, 344, 356, 357, 544).
  • The grid table is H for the 102 values the codebreaker wrote and M for the 15 predicted from the pattern; 440 Spagn… and 556 (s on R702, unclear on the grid) are M.
  • Groups seen but not valued on R664 f. 1: 233, 312, 367, 439, 477, 534, 551, 556, 566. Several dozen more stand on ff. 2–3.
  • Not done: a group-by-group transcription of R664 and R660, decoded through with the table; a search of the Venetian and Domestic calendars for the letters; the writers.
