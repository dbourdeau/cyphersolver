This letter was filed on this site until 14 October 2026 with the rest of the Sienese bundle of cipher letters, in which each letter has its own key. The other letters whose keys were found are different ciphers, each with its own page: no. 25, no. 18, no. 14 and nos. 6 and 24 (deciphered by George Lasry); the unread pieces and the filed keys are on the bundle’s page.

01The letter and its key

A letter in clear Italian with fifteen runs of cipher (144 signs) and nine Latin code words, from the jurist Galgano Borghesi, on embassy to King Alfonso, to his colleague Leonardo Benvoglienti; Senatore (2009) cites it as “una lettera in cifra di Borghesi a Benvoglienti … da Napoli, 12 maggio”. The recipient glossed some runs between the lines (“duca”, “papa”, “per parte del duca”, “al re”): this is the partial decipherment behind the 1952 inventory’s “solo parzialmente decifrate”. None of the six 1450s keys of the bundle fits, though one of them (R4750, the Milan ambassadors’ key of 1454) shares the code word storps. Below the text, torn away on the left, an alphabet is written out over its cipher signs, i to u; with the glosses it gives the key. 114 of the 144 signs read securely, 11 more from context, 19 remain unread.

02What it says

The reading audit of 22 October 2026 classed the letter decoded, not read: the first cipher runs complete their clear sentences, but the second paragraph is cipher runs, code words and unread signs between doubtful clear words. On 23 October the glosses and lines 17–24 were re-read on the DECODE image. The code word pedesi is glossed “Iacopino” (Iacopo Piccinino, whose war on Siena was settled in 1456 under King Alfonso’s arbitration), cras is glossed “a pace” and “pace” (not “parte”), and toffe “al papa”. A partial reading of the whole letter was built (transcripts/no04_reading.md). The first paragraph of news holds:

Apresso. La Maestà del re ebbe a le strette gli adversarij e […] per mia instigatione volse includere de le […] Et tandem hec fuit […] che dicevamo che quando Lucha era assediata da Fiorentini, che le […] promesse al capitano […] se no[…]. E con questo […] stavamo […] e di mandato per [?papa] de le scritture disse erano rimasti a Milano, e che non le potemmo mostrare. Tunc rex parvi fecisse […] est, e chosì rimasero quasi beffati; tutto però buono per mettere aqua al mulino nostro.

“His Majesty the King had his adversaries in a tight corner and […] at my instigation wished to include some of the […] that we said that when Lucca was besieged by the Florentines, the […] promises to the captain […] the writings, he said they had been left at Milan and that we could not show them. Then the King made light of it, and so they were left almost mocked; all of it good, though, to bring water to our mill.” The second paragraph builds only its first sentence, “Antonio d'Arezzo qua era detto el duca essere bene disposto et a Lucca et a pace” (the Duke well disposed towards Lucca and towards peace); after it come the peace with Iacopino, the Florentine clergy, “ne al papa ne a cardinali” and “contra lo Turcho”, between holes. The rapid clear hand of lines 20–23 does not resolve at two or three times on the only image, so the letter stays decoded, not read.

03What remains

  • 19 signs that occur once or twice (1, //, 8, F, the slashed x, the long T, the hooked 4, +, R, δ, B and others) and the code words toffe and vapor beyond their glosses (“al papa”, “e cardinali”); the fragment is torn and no filed key supplies them. pedesi (Iacopino) and cras (pace) are now given by the glosses.
  • Lines 18–23: the rapid clear hand around the cipher runs (abbreviations such as “miss. p. ve.”, “ad quas instituit”) does not resolve at two or three times on the DECODE image (2,248 pixels wide), the only one.

Naples, 12 May 1456, ASSi Concistoro 2308 fasc. 2 no. 4 (DECODE R4793) decoded, not read

Galgano Borghesi, orator at Naples, writes to Leonardo Benvoglienti that at his urging King Alfonso wanted the settlement to include the promises made when Lucca was besieged by the Florentines, and that the Pope, the Florentine clergy and the cardinals are to contribute against the Turk. The sense is tentative: 114 of the 144 cipher signs are given a value.

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.

# no. 4 (DECODE R4793): Galgano Borghesi, Naples, to Leonardo Benvoglienti, 12 May 1456 — English (partial)

Translation of `no04_reading.md`, same holes. The letter is not read to the bar (second paragraph fragmentary).

## Translation

Worshipful and magnificent sir, honoured as a father, after commendation etc. Since you left us here we have had no news from you, which is not without some surprise. We do not think there is in you […] any negligence, which is not your way, but rather some difficulty that we think [?has arisen]. Which, besides surprising us, also displeases and troubles us, and in [?frequent] […] that we sometimes use your term, and we say that someone, and by the road too, […] does not get here. So we ask you to let [?this] be advised quickly, and whether it is a thing to be said […] and to my instruction well etc. If there is anything else, if need be use the cipher and say we do it, and we here will keep this [?order] […].

Next. His Majesty the King had his adversaries in a tight corner and […] at my instigation wished to include some of the […] And in the end this was […] that we said that when Lucca was besieged by the Florentines, the […] promises to the captain […]. And with this […] we stayed […] and being sent for [?the Pope?] the writings, he said they had been left at Milan and that we could not show them. Then the King made light of it […], and so they were left almost mocked; all of it good, though, to bring water to our mill. But let us know about […] troops.

Antonio d'Arezzo here was saying that the Duke was well disposed both towards Lucca and towards peace. And that he had satisfied [the] Pope that, for the peace with Iacopino [Piccinino], he should take some of the […] ready […] of the clergy of the Florentines […] who had not yet allowed either the Pope or the cardinals to take any […] one day in another way […]. […] and then, as needed, against the Turk […]. This same, if it is true, you must have likewise […]. […] and so [?he said?] all of it, on behalf of the Duke, to [?report] to the King […].

At Siena we think the same, by the letter left with this one […] that we do not know whether it is true, to inform them of everything, and that we have news of us […] when it happens there is need of another expense to send it […] to Siena […] could do more, that we have been kept without a penny […] so that we have news of you […] Nothing else […] greet. Given at Naples, 12 May.
