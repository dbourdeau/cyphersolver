Open investigation · Egypt · 2nd century CE

Five line endings on the Michigan–Oslo papyrus

A name in the Isis spell on the front fits two tentative letters on the back. A third would have to be beta where the editor reads tau.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 22 November 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

not solved

Two fragments of one roll

A Greek ritual handbook from second-century Egypt survives in Oslo and Ann Arbor. On the front, the scribe used a cipher alphabet to write a spell invoking Isis. Anastasia Maravela’s edition identifies the ends of five heavily abraded lines on the back of the Oslo fragment, P.Oslo inv. 365. Their contents are uncertain. The editor tentatively interprets them as ritual utterances.

A name from the front

Line 60 on the front contains the name βωβελιξ (Bōbelix). The last seven positions on verso line 5 offer a way to test whether the name was repeated. A dot below a Greek letter marks an uncertain reading. Each dash below stands for one unidentified or missing position at the edition’s estimated spacing.

Omega and xi fit. The first letter presents the difficulty. The editor tentatively reads tau, where the name requires beta. In the published cipher table, beta looks like a shallow cup and tau has an upright with a short side stroke. The candidate also predicts a second beta immediately after omega.

How much is the spacing worth?

In selected recitations on the front, Bōbelix was the only one of 257 seven-letter sequences to agree with two of the three proposed letters. Across 26,492 sentences from the Perseus and PROIEL Ancient Greek training collections, however, the pattern τω + four letters + ξ occurred 24 times in running text. None was a complete word. Those counts show how easily spacing alone can suggest a reading. A separate search using the proposed letters of verso lines 2–4 produced no confirming parallel in the selected recto passages.

What would decide it?

A photograph of the back of the Oslo fragment could show whether the first sign preserves the side stroke of tau and whether the third position preserves the cup of beta. A clear tau would rule out an exact repetition of the name. Until the traces can be checked, all five lines remain without a new reading.
