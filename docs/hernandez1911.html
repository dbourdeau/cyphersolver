VRAALIINLA OLADIL AANVLEDS CLESE
VILLARREAL SALDRA LLEVANDO CANON

El Paso ↔ Presidio · five swapped letter pairs · 27–28 March 1911 · read with the recipient’s key card

A telegram from González Garza to Braulio Hernández, 27 March 1911

Go to the full text ↓

Four coded words in a Spanish telegram, read with the key card the same fonds holds for the recipient, say that Villarreal will leave Wednesday or Thursday taking a cannon. Hernández’s coded telegram to El Paso of the next day, in the same key, was deciphered at the time in pencil on the form: 160 men in all, and is the reinforcement coming, with the cannon?

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 9 November 2026 · updated 24 November 2026

The telegrams
A telegram of 27 March 1911 from Federico González Garza at El Paso, Texas, to Braulio Hernández at Presidio, Texas; clear Spanish with four coded words. And a telegram of 28 March 1911 from Hernández (“E H Leroy”) at Presidio to González Garza at El Paso, ten coded words.
Where
CEHM-Carso, Mexico City, Fondo CMXV (Archivo Federico González Garza), CMXV.15.1420.3-6 and CMXV.15.1430.3-3; ledger entry 1462, which listed both from the CEHM records (“texto en clave”, “en clave”), with no decipherment.
The cipher
Five pairs of letters swap in place, in both directions: L–A, N–E, R–I, S–O, T–U.
How it was deciphered
The 27 March telegram: with the five pairs of the Hernández key card of the same fonds (CMXV.16.1557.1, scan 16), found and applied by William Echols; the key was not recovered from the ciphertext, and the project re-ran the four decryptions. The 28 March telegram: deciphered at the time, in pencil at the head of the form; Daniel Jalkut applied the same card, which gives the same text, and found the pencil line. The PR #831 review compared the card with its scan and the 28 March groups with their image.
What it says
Did you receive the pills? You should stay there. Villarreal will leave Wednesday or Thursday, taking a cannon. Write back. And, the next day: a total of one hundred and sixty men; say whether reinforcements and the cannon are coming.
Still open
No person has compared the transcriptions or the card with the images, and the image of the 27 March telegram has not been compared at all; whether the telegrams are in print was not searched here.

01 The telegram

A telegram of 27 March 1911 from Federico González Garza at El Paso, Texas, to Braulio Hernández at Presidio, Texas, in the Archivo Federico González Garza (Fondo CMXV) of the CEHM-Carso in Mexico City, CMXV.15.1420.3-6 (two images, front and reverse). It is in clear Spanish with four coded words. The CEHM record calls it “texto en clave” and gives no decipherment, and catalogue 1462, the ledger’s entry for this fonds, listed it with no known decipherment on that record. William Echols read it with GPT-6.1 Sol in Codex and sent the transcription, the key and the reading; the project could not reach the images and worked from his submission.

The second telegram, CMXV.15.1430.3-3 (CEHM record 12729), was sent from Presidio on 28 March 1911 at 4:37 pm, signed “E H Leroy”, a cover name of Hernández in this traffic, to “Lic Fred G Garza” at the Alberta Hotel, El Paso. All ten of its groups are coded. A pencil line at the head of the form gives the text, written at the time: “Total 160 h - Diga si viene refuerzo y”, then a faint last word under the CEHM watermark that could not be made out. Daniel Jalkut transcribed it from the CEHM image with Claude Code, applied the Hernández card and found that the card gives the same text (pull request #831). The telegram is not in the typed list of telegrams CMXV.18.1795.1 of the same fonds, which he searched; catalogue entry 1462 had listed it with no decipherment. His files, which also hold other telegrams of the entry, are in targets/duron1911/siblings.

02 The key

Five pairs of letters swap in place, in both directions: L–A, N–E, R–I, S–O and T–U; every other letter stays as written. The contributor takes the pairs from the Hernández key card of the same fonds, CMXV.16.1557.1, scan 16. The review of pull request #831 compared the card with that scan: it is typed “Hernández : l n r s t / a e i o u”, the same five pairs. The same box holds the card headed “Herrera. Moreno.” (scan 7), with which another telegram of the fonds was deciphered (its own page); that is a different key. The key was not recovered from the ciphertext.

Coded wordRead with the pairs
VRAALIINLAVILLARREAL
OLADILSALDRA
AANVLEDSLLEVANDO
CLESECANON

Checked here, with an own script: the four coded words (29 letters) decrypt with the five pairs to the words above, as the contributor gives them, and the words fit the clear text around them. The transcription of this telegram was not compared with its image.

In the telegram of 28 March the same pairs give total cieto sesenta hombres digas iviene reouerzo y canon from the ten groups Usula crnu sonon eulhs mbino drglo rvrne ninst nizsy clese (49 letters; an own decoder gives the same letters as Daniel Jalkut’s decryption). Two letters are off: the n of ciento is missing, and refuerzo has s (plain o) where f is wanted. The pencil decipherment on the form gives both (“160” and “refuerzo”).

03 The reading

¿Recibió píldoras? Conviene permanezca esa. VILLARREAL SALDRA miércoles ó jueves, LLEVANDO CANON. Escriba. — González Garza

In English: Did you receive the pills? You should stay there. Villarreal will leave Wednesday or Thursday, taking a cannon. Write back. The coded CANON is written without the tilde, and is read as cañón. The contributor gives no background about the people or the pills, and none is added here.

The telegram of 28 March, as the pencil decipherment of the time gives it and the card confirms:

Total cie{n}to sesenta hombres. Diga si viene re{f}uerzo y cañón.

In English: A total of one hundred and sixty men. Say whether reinforcements and the cannon are coming. The braces mark the two letters the card does not give; the pencil line gives these two words, so all ten coded words are deciphered. The card alone gives eight of them, among them the last, cañón (CLESE, as in the telegram of 27 March), which cannot be made out in the pencil line.

Four more telegrams from Presidio of 27–31 March 1911 decode with the same card (pull request #831). One of them (CMXV.15.1420.5-6) builds sentences with 10 sure words of 19; the other three give single words and no sentence. They are kept with the contributor’s notes in targets/duron1911/siblings and are not counted on this page.

04 What remains uncertain

  • The transcription of the 27 March telegram was not compared with its CEHM image. The card (scan 16) and the 28 March telegram were compared with the images by the review of pull request #831, an AI agent; no person has checked either.
  • That the card is the one filed for this correspondence is the contributor’s identification; that five telegrams from Presidio of 27–31 March give Spanish words with it supports it.
  • The last word of the pencil line on the 28 March telegram, under the CEHM watermark, could not be made out.
  • Whether the telegrams are already in print (Narváez’s books, the recipients’ papers) was not searched here; the contributors report none found.
  • No statistical validation is recorded: four words are too few for a language-model test, and the 28 March text rests on the decipherment of the time.

# Readings of the sibling telegrams of catalogue 1462 (20 Nov 2026; revised after review, PR #831)

Marks: `[?word]` uncertain; `[...]` one unread word; `[... n]` n unread words; `{x}` a letter supplied against the
key's output (counted as a hole); `*` a clear word of the telegram, not coded. Translations follow each reading.
Decryption: `decryption.txt` (unedited output of the key, `decode.py`). Counts are by words, only sure words count
(README read bar). Classes: "read", "read in part" (coherent sentences under 95 %), "decoded, not read" (words but no
sentence).

## CMXV.15.1430.3-3: Hernández ("E H Leroy"), Presidio, 28 Mar 1911 (Hernández swap card). Read

> Total cie{n}to sesenta hombres. Diga si viene re{f}uerzo y cañón.
>
> Total one hundred and sixty men. Say whether reinforcements and the cannon are coming.

- The key's output: `total cieto sesenta hombres digas iviene reouerzo y canon`; two slips: "crnu" lacks the n of
  ciento, and "ninst" has s (plain o) where f is wanted (re-f-uerzo).
- **Confirmed by the period pencil gloss** at the head of the form ("Total 160 h - Diga si viene refuerzo y", the end
  under the CEHM watermark). So it was deciphered at the time, though it is not in the typed list 1795.1 (see below).
- 8 of 10 words sure from the key; ciento and refuerzo each carry one corrected letter, and the period gloss gives
  both words (160; refuerzo). Read.

## CMXV.15.1420.5-6: B. Hernández, Presidio, 27 Mar 1911, 9:35 am (Hernández swap card). Read in part

> [?Sánchez] [?tiene] [?cien] ochenta hom{b}res. Venga Villarreal [...] y tenga listo [...] [?cañón]. Salgo por él
> mañana [?a] [?mediodía].
>
> [?Sánchez] [?has] [?a hundred] eighty men. Let Villarreal come [...] and have the [?cannon] ready [...]. I leave to
> fetch it tomorrow [?at] [?noon].

- Output: `fanch eztie aedie noche ntaho mdres benga billa realb oespj entey tenga listo dan danon salgo porel manan
  aieedi odia`. The pencil strokes on the form fall after "fanchez" and "tieae", so the words are `fanchez tieae dien
  ochenta`; "dien" is perhaps cien, with d for c as in "danon" (reviewer's observation).
- Sure from the key (10): ochenta, venga (written "benga"), Villarreal ("billareal"), y, tenga, listo, salgo, por,
  él, mañana.
- Corrected or uncertain (holes): Sánchez (F for S), tiene (`tieae`), cien (`dien`), hombres (`homdres`, d for b),
  cañón (`danon`, d for c; cf. CLESE = canon in 1420.3-6), a mediodía (`aieediodia`, "ie" where "am" is wanted).
- Unread: `boespj` before `-ente y` (one word, extent estimated), `dan` before cañón.
- 19 words, 10 sure: **53 %**. It answers 1420.3-6 of the same day (Villarreal to leave Wednesday or Thursday with a cannon).

## CMXV.15.1420.6-6: "B. H. Leroy", Presidio, 27 Mar 1911, 3:08 pm (Hernández swap card). Decoded, not read

- Output: `nfsal gohoy man eand eande carta luque urdoe ctres isuae ctrar arreg loscapitul`.
- Words: salgo, hoy, carta, Luque, arreglos (5 of at least 10); `mle nled nledn` looks like a garbled "mande"
  repeated (struck through in pencil on the form); `urdoe ctres isuae ctrar` and `capitul` do not give a sentence.

## CMXV.15.1432.3-4: "E.H. Leroy", Presidio, 29 Mar 1911, 7:45 pm (Hernández card on the coded words). Decoded, not read

- Coded words with the card: usula = total, atqtn = Luque, neuin vroul = entre vista (3 of 13). The other groups
  (`osasnom aiamodon eloru irtan dedile dscse umons dtmen ouna anes`) do not read with the card; some may be clear words
  mangled in transmission. The clear words: tenemos, dícese, gobierno (`coverino`), concedió, [?mueocado], evítalo.

## CMXV.15.1447.1-3: Braulio Hernández, Presidio, 31 Mar 1911, 7:18 pm (mostly clear). Decoded, not read

- Coded words with the card: `revru Lorur Lichrh` = `invit asiti archih`, which reads **"invita sitiar Chih."**
  (reviewer's reading, PR #831): "[?Jacinto] Rubio invites [us] to besiege Chihuahua"; `Orgtn` = sigue. So 4 of 10
  coded groups read; `Kttns`, `chule edsase`, `esaan elltc`, `Mrule` do not.
- The clear part: "Presidente aliviado. Fecha 23 [?advs] correas de [?Jacinto] Rubio ... Orozco 1300 hombres ...
  Gobernador viene de Guerrero con 400. Franco sigue. Auxilio capitán Escudero ... Mándame de aquí auxilio ... Escaso
  [?Alanís vive]."

## CMXV.17.1695.1-2: J. González, Marfa, 5 May 1911 (Clave G.A. card). Existing decipherment

- The catalogue lists it as partly read, and the CEHM record prints "José Pineda". **The typed list of period
  decipherments CMXV.18.1795.1 has the whole telegram** (scan 21, entry 113): "Nemi informa mercancías enviadas capitán
  Lewis decomisadas. Envíen facturas José Pineda para reclamarlas. Conviene llamen Lewis. Esperen carta." So the clear
  words "Luis", "Etamen" of the form are Lewis and llamen.
- What this adds: the pencil gloss "José Pineda" covers all four coded groups (`kopjg Ftskf Tlkap Lvnme`), and the
  Clave G.A. card read in pairs gives the same (ko pj gF ts kf Tl ka pL vn me = j o s e p i n f d a; pL = f is a slip
  for e).

# Readings of the sibling telegrams of catalogue 1462 (20 Nov 2026; revised after review, PR #831)

Marks: `[?word]` uncertain; `[...]` one unread word; `[... n]` n unread words; `{x}` a letter supplied against the
key's output (counted as a hole); `*` a clear word of the telegram, not coded. Translations follow each reading.
Decryption: `decryption.txt` (unedited output of the key, `decode.py`). Counts are by words, only sure words count
(README read bar). Classes: "read", "read in part" (coherent sentences under 95 %), "decoded, not read" (words but no
sentence).

## CMXV.15.1430.3-3: Hernández ("E H Leroy"), Presidio, 28 Mar 1911 (Hernández swap card). Read

> Total cie{n}to sesenta hombres. Diga si viene re{f}uerzo y cañón.
>
> Total one hundred and sixty men. Say whether reinforcements and the cannon are coming.

- The key's output: `total cieto sesenta hombres digas iviene reouerzo y canon`; two slips: "crnu" lacks the n of
  ciento, and "ninst" has s (plain o) where f is wanted (re-f-uerzo).
- **Confirmed by the period pencil gloss** at the head of the form ("Total 160 h - Diga si viene refuerzo y", the end
  under the CEHM watermark). So it was deciphered at the time, though it is not in the typed list 1795.1 (see below).
- 8 of 10 words sure from the key; ciento and refuerzo each carry one corrected letter, and the period gloss gives
  both words (160; refuerzo). Read.

## CMXV.15.1420.5-6: B. Hernández, Presidio, 27 Mar 1911, 9:35 am (Hernández swap card). Read in part

> [?Sánchez] [?tiene] [?cien] ochenta hom{b}res. Venga Villarreal [...] y tenga listo [...] [?cañón]. Salgo por él
> mañana [?a] [?mediodía].
>
> [?Sánchez] [?has] [?a hundred] eighty men. Let Villarreal come [...] and have the [?cannon] ready [...]. I leave to
> fetch it tomorrow [?at] [?noon].

- Output: `fanch eztie aedie noche ntaho mdres benga billa realb oespj entey tenga listo dan danon salgo porel manan
  aieedi odia`. The pencil strokes on the form fall after "fanchez" and "tieae", so the words are `fanchez tieae dien
  ochenta`; "dien" is perhaps cien, with d for c as in "danon" (reviewer's observation).
- Sure from the key (10): ochenta, venga (written "benga"), Villarreal ("billareal"), y, tenga, listo, salgo, por,
  él, mañana.
- Corrected or uncertain (holes): Sánchez (F for S), tiene (`tieae`), cien (`dien`), hombres (`homdres`, d for b),
  cañón (`danon`, d for c; cf. CLESE = canon in 1420.3-6), a mediodía (`aieediodia`, "ie" where "am" is wanted).
- Unread: `boespj` before `-ente y` (one word, extent estimated), `dan` before cañón.
- 19 words, 10 sure: **53 %**. It answers 1420.3-6 of the same day (Villarreal to leave Wednesday or Thursday with a cannon).

## CMXV.15.1420.6-6: "B. H. Leroy", Presidio, 27 Mar 1911, 3:08 pm (Hernández swap card). Decoded, not read

- Output: `nfsal gohoy man eand eande carta luque urdoe ctres isuae ctrar arreg loscapitul`.
- Words: salgo, hoy, carta, Luque, arreglos (5 of at least 10); `mle nled nledn` looks like a garbled "mande"
  repeated (struck through in pencil on the form); `urdoe ctres isuae ctrar` and `capitul` do not give a sentence.

## CMXV.15.1432.3-4: "E.H. Leroy", Presidio, 29 Mar 1911, 7:45 pm (Hernández card on the coded words). Decoded, not read

- Coded words with the card: usula = total, atqtn = Luque, neuin vroul = entre vista (3 of 13). The other groups
  (`osasnom aiamodon eloru irtan dedile dscse umons dtmen ouna anes`) do not read with the card; some may be clear words
  mangled in transmission. The clear words: tenemos, dícese, gobierno (`coverino`), concedió, [?mueocado], evítalo.

## CMXV.15.1447.1-3: Braulio Hernández, Presidio, 31 Mar 1911, 7:18 pm (mostly clear). Decoded, not read

- Coded words with the card: `revru Lorur Lichrh` = `invit asiti archih`, which reads **"invita sitiar Chih."**
  (reviewer's reading, PR #831): "[?Jacinto] Rubio invites [us] to besiege Chihuahua"; `Orgtn` = sigue. So 4 of 10
  coded groups read; `Kttns`, `chule edsase`, `esaan elltc`, `Mrule` do not.
- The clear part: "Presidente aliviado. Fecha 23 [?advs] correas de [?Jacinto] Rubio ... Orozco 1300 hombres ...
  Gobernador viene de Guerrero con 400. Franco sigue. Auxilio capitán Escudero ... Mándame de aquí auxilio ... Escaso
  [?Alanís vive]."

## CMXV.17.1695.1-2: J. González, Marfa, 5 May 1911 (Clave G.A. card). Existing decipherment

- The catalogue lists it as partly read, and the CEHM record prints "José Pineda". **The typed list of period
  decipherments CMXV.18.1795.1 has the whole telegram** (scan 21, entry 113): "Nemi informa mercancías enviadas capitán
  Lewis decomisadas. Envíen facturas José Pineda para reclamarlas. Conviene llamen Lewis. Esperen carta." So the clear
  words "Luis", "Etamen" of the form are Lewis and llamen.
- What this adds: the pencil gloss "José Pineda" covers all four coded groups (`kopjg Ftskf Tlkap Lvnme`), and the
  Clave G.A. card read in pairs gives the same (ko pj gF ts kf Tl ka pL vn me = j o s e p i n f d a; pL = f is a slip
  for e).
