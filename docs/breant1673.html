Bréant to M. de Réveillon, Cologne, 1673–1674

Two letters on whether the French garrison of Kaiserswerth can hold if the Elector of Cologne changes sides, deciphered between the lines at the time; the numbers are now transcribed and the key rebuilt.

Method: existing decipherment · Extent: complete · On the map

At a glanceExisting decipherment · complete

The letters
Bréant, a French agent at the Cologne peace congress, to M. de Réveillon, who commands the French garrison of Kaiserswerth, 26 December 1673 and 8 January 1674. Each mixes clear French with passages in numbers.
Where
Médiathèque Georges-Delaw, Sedan, fonds Gourjault, carton 170, piece 3 (ff. 26–27) and piece 4 (ff. 28–29). Not in the project’s catalogue, and no DECODE record was found. The photographs were sent for research use and are not shown.
The cipher
A nomenclator in numbers from 6 to 319: letters, syllables and whole words in roughly alphabetical order (a = 55, z = 316), a few homophones and three name codes.
What was done
No new reading is claimed: a clerk of the time wrote the clear words between the lines of both letters. What is new is the transcription of the 394 numbers (by an AI model, from photographs), the decipherment aligned on them word by word, and a reusable key of 90 groups rebuilt from the pairs.
What it says
The French plenipotentiaries want to know whether Réveillon can hold the place with his French and Swiss troops. On 8 January the Elector has given his word to hear no proposal from the Emperor until the King answers his letter.
Still open
Two probable words, both in the one passage the decipherer struck through. A test of the key on a third letter was pre-registered and run, and could not be measured: the rule asked for at least 300 letters of decipherment in the groups counted, and there were 282.
Credit
The decipherment is a clerk’s of 1673–1674. The transcription, the alignment, the key and the counts are Brice Dudout’s (L’Europe chiffrée), working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents.

01 The two letters

Both letters are signed Bréant and dated from Cologne, where the peace congress of the Dutch War was sitting. They are addressed to M. de Réveillon at Kaiserswerth, a place on the Rhine that belonged to the Elector of Cologne and was held by a French garrison. The carton holds six pieces on 33 folios.

02 The cipher and the key

The two letters use the same table: the same five numbers spell “mpereur” in both, 296 is vous and 316 is z in both. The key rebuilt from the decipherment has 90 groups. Of these, 61 occur twice or more with the same value, 27 occur once, and 2 are inferred. The letters u and v, i and j, and s and z share their numbers.

Apart from the names and a few homophones (56, 93, 102, 106, 167, 257), the table runs in approximately alphabetical order from a = 55 to z = 316, with whole words filed under their first letter. The order holds by first letter and breaks inside a letter: 103 ent comes before 104 en, 159 j before 163 il, and 257 is a second qui out of place. Group 319 shows that the table went on beyond 316.

Four values rest on that order and on the context rather than on the decipherer alone: 208 = o (in propositions, between 196 ni and 210 ont), 293 = vo (in envoyera, where the decipherer wrote “no”), and the two inferred values, 74 = ca (between 73 c and 75 ce) and 319 = sa or za (in casaque, beyond the last known group 316). In six places the decipherer’s letters disagree with the key, and the word settles each one: 153 is i (informé, written “s”), 159 is j (jusques), 99 is e and 293 is vo (envoyera), 221 is po (point, written “pa”) and 228 is par (par un gentilhomme, written “pas”).

03 What the letters say

The text below is the decipherment of the time, in the spelling of the letters. Words in italics are in cipher on the page, the rest is the writer’s clear text. Braces mark the two probable words, and “(…)” a stretch of clear text that was not transcribed; a bare “…” is a cut made for this page. The complete texts and an English translation are under Full texts.

Nos ambassadeurs ont besoin de sçavoir si avec les trouppes que vous avez à Keyserswert vous estes en estat de soustenir une aproche des Ennemys … et si avec ce que vous avez vous pouvez estre le maistre de la place, prenez la peine de m’en informer, affin que si M. de Cologne {avoit} à tourner {casaque} (…) car il est important que nous soyons tousjours le maistre de ses meilleures places.

On 26 December 1673 the French ambassadors at Cologne ask what Réveillon has “soit de François soit de Suisses”, with Germans inside the place as well. They fear that the Elector, “M. de Cologne”, may turn his coat and listen to the proposals made to him on behalf of the Emperor. Bréant asks that the matter stay between M. de Chamilly, Réveillon and himself. The ciphered postscript adds that if the Elector came to terms with the enemy Réveillon would be warned, and asks him to make no stir about it.

… qui m’ont chargé de vous mander d’estre asseuré que vous serez informé de tout ce qui se passera, et qu’ils ont parolle de M. de Cologne qu’il ne s’escoutera aucunes propositions de la part de l’Empereur jusques à ce qu’il ayt response à une Lettre qu’il a escrite au Roi, et qu’il envoyera demain par un gentilhomme de sa chambre. Ainsy, Monsieur, soyez en repos et soyez persuadé que vous ne serez point pris faute d’estre adverti.

On 8 January 1674 the answer is calmer. The ambassadors have the Elector’s word that he will hear no proposal from the Emperor until he has the King’s answer to a letter he is sending the next day by a gentleman of his chamber. Réveillon is told to be at rest: he will not be caught for want of a warning.

Only the words written in cipher are counted, and a name code counts as one word. A word is sure when the decipherer of the time, the key and the context agree. That is agreement with the old decipherment plus sense. It is not an independent test of the key, because the key was rebuilt from that same decipherment. No word is restored and none is left without a value.

The encipherer made a few slips that leave the word certain: one i too many in veuille, one e too many in sçaurez, and the i of faict and the u of juste and jusques not enciphered. An AI agent reviewing the contribution for the maintainers wrote its own decoder from the key file; it reproduces both decryption files passage by passage. The grade of the reading, A, is that agent’s. No person has reviewed it.

05 The test on a third letter

A key rebuilt from a decipherment needs a text that was not used to build it. Carton 170.1 holds one: Louvois to Réveillon, Versailles, 19 March 1674, marked “Duplicata”, the only ciphered letter among the seventeen of that carton. It has 201 groups in the same table, with a decipherment of the time written group by group. By that decipherment, Réveillon is to leave Kaiserswerth with all the King’s troops on the order of Courtin and Barrillon, and to leave the munitions and the artillery against a receipt from the Elector’s officer. The table is therefore Louvois’s table with Réveillon, which Bréant used as well.

The contributor wrote the test down before transcribing the letter, and the rules for merging the two transcriptions after them but before any decoding; the two files are kept in the folder as frozen, in French. The rule: two transcriptions made without the key, a blind arbitration of the doubtful places, then one measurement with the key unchanged. The measure is the share of groups whose key value equals the old decipherment. The key passes at 0.85 or more and at least 0.30 above the best of 200 keys with their values shuffled, provided the decipherment counted holds at least 300 letters.

The test was run on 19 November 2026 and the result is not measurable: the decipherment counted holds 282 letters, under the 300 required. The figures are therefore descriptive only: 145 of 165 groups agree (0.879), against 0.127 for the best shuffled key. The contributor reports that the 20 disagreements are all misreadings of the letter shapes of the old decipherment (v taken for n in vous six times, for example), and that none calls for another key value.

  • 208 = o is confirmed on this letter (munitions). 293 and 74 do not occur in it.
  • Nine values new to the key fall between their neighbours in the table: 62 ba, 94 di, 111 elle, 125 fi, 131 g, 172 lo, 190 mu, 209 ordre, 218 pi. They were not added to the key.
  • With the key alone, 62 of the 78 cipher words of that letter come out sure (79.5%).

No image and no transcription of the Louvois letter is in the repository, so these figures are the contributor’s and were not recomputed. What was checked here on 24 November 2026: the two frozen files hash to the values recorded for them, and the frozen key is the key file as first committed, with the same 90 values as today. By the contributor’s own note, the hash was not published before the transcription, so the order of events rests on the contributor’s log. The formal test stays open until another letter in this table with at least 300 deciphered letters is found.

06 What remains uncertain

  • Two probable words, avoit and casaque, in the passage of 26 December that the decipherer struck through (“M. de Cologne avoit à tourner caza que”). avoit is written a-o-i-t with no v, and casaque rests on 74 = ca and 319 = sa or za, both inferred from the order of the table.
  • Two digits in the letter of 8 January differ between the contributor’s first word list and the aligned transcription: 167 or 164 in qu’il (both are l) and 243 or 247 in adverti. The words are the same either way. The digits have not been looked at again on the image.
  • In the postscript of 26 December a group struck through by the encipherer before avec, perhaps 57, is left out of the transcription.
  • The writer’s clear text is not transcribed in full. It holds no cipher and is outside the counts.
  • The key has not passed an independent test (section 05).
  • Who Bréant and Réveillon were is given here as the contributor’s notes give it, from the letters; it was not checked in other sources.

# Bréant to M. de Réveillon, Cologne, 26 December 1673 and 8 January 1674: reading

The text is the contemporary decipherment written between the lines, checked word by word against the key rebuilt from it (`key.tsv`). The ciphered passages are in italics; the rest is the writer's own clear text. `{x}` marks a probable word (rule #728). "(…)" marks a stretch of the writer's clear text that is not transcribed here; it holds no cipher and does not enter the counts. Spelling as written; u/v and i/j as in the clear.

## 26 December 1673 (carton 170, piece 3, ff. 26-27)

Nos ambassadeurs ont besoin de sçavoir si avec *les trouppes que vous avez à Keyserswert* vous estes *en estat de soustenir une aproche des Ennemys et si vous estes* (…) *les Allemands qui sont dedans ce n'est pas sans* raison *qu'ils vous demandent* cela. Ils m'ont prié de vous en escrire affin de sçavoir *au juste* ce que vous *avez soit de François soit de Suisses*, et si avec ce que vous avez vous *pouvez estre le maistre de la place*, prenez la peine de m'en informer, affin que si *M. de Cologne {avoit} à tourner {casaque}* (…) car il est important que nous soyons tousjours *le maistre de ses meilleures places*. (…) *qu'il veuille escouter les propositions* que l'on *luy fait de la part de l'Empereur*. Je vous prie que cela soit entre *M. de Chamilly & vous* et moy (…). Bréant.

P.-S. Si *par hazard M. de Cologne s'accomodoit avec les Ennemys vous en* seriez *advertis* et je vous *prie de ne point faire d'esclat de ceci ; vous sçaurez tout ce qui se passera ici*.

Notes. "une" (before "aproche") and "à" (before "tourner") are the decipherer's words with no cipher group under them; they are not cipher words. The passage "M. de Cologne avoit à tourner casaque" is the one the decipherer struck through ("tourner caza que"); his clear agrees with the key for "tourner", and {avoit} (a-o-i-t, the v not enciphered) and {casaque} (74 ca, 319 sa/za?, 236 que; 74 and 319 inferred from the approximately alphabetical order) are counted probable.

## 8 January 1674 (carton 170, piece 4, ff. 28-29)

J'ay receu la lettre que vous m'avez fait l'honneur de m'escrire du 30 du mois passé et 2 du courant. J'ay monstré vostre lettre du 2 à nos Ambassadeurs, qui m'ont chargé de vous mander *d'estre asseuré que vous serez informé de tout ce qui se passera, et qu'ils ont parolle de M. de Cologne qu'il ne s'escoutera aucunes* propositions *de la part de l'Empereur jusques à ce qu'il ayt* response *à une Lettre qu'il a escrite au Roi, et qu'il envoyera* demain *par un gentilhomme de sa chambre*. Ainsy, Monsieur, *soyez en repos et soyez* persuadé *que vous ne* serez *point pris faute d'estre adverti, et que vous le serez* aussytost (…). Bréant.

# Bréant to M. de Réveillon, Cologne, 26 December 1673 and 8 January 1674: English translation

Translation of `reading.md`. The ciphered passages are in italics; `{x}` marks the same probable words; "(…)" marks clear text not transcribed. "M. de Cologne" is the Elector of Cologne (Maximilian Henry of Bavaria); "our ambassadors" are the French plenipotentiaries at the Cologne peace congress; Kaiserswerth is the Rhine fortress of the Elector held by Réveillon.

## 26 December 1673

Our ambassadors need to know whether, with *the troops you have at Kaiserswerth*, you are *in a state to withstand an approach of the enemy, and whether you are* (…) *the Germans who are inside; it is not without* reason *that they ask you* this. They have asked me to write to you so as to know *exactly* what you *have, whether of French or of Swiss*, and whether with what you have you *can be master of the place*; please take the trouble to let me know, so that if *M. de Cologne {were} to turn {his coat}* (…) for it is important that we should always be *masters of his best places*. (…) *that he may be willing to listen to the proposals* that are *made to him on behalf of the Emperor*. I beg you that this remain between *M. de Chamilly and you* and me (…). Bréant.

P.S. If *by chance M. de Cologne came to terms with the enemy, you* would be *warned of it*, and I *beg you to make no stir about this; you will know everything that happens here*.

## 8 January 1674

I have received the letter you did me the honour of writing to me of the 30th of last month and the 2nd of this. I showed your letter of the 2nd to our Ambassadors, who have charged me to tell you *to be assured that you will be informed of everything that happens, and that they have the word of M. de Cologne that he will listen to no* proposals *on behalf of the Emperor until he has* an answer *to a Letter he has written to the King, which he will send* tomorrow *by a gentleman of his chamber*. So, Sir, *be at rest and be* persuaded *that you will not* be *caught for want of being warned, and that you will be warned* at once (…). Bréant.

Bréant to Réveillon, Cologne, 8 Jan 1674

The ambassadors assure Réveillon he will be informed of everything: the Elector of Cologne has promised to listen to no proposal from the Emperor until he has the King's answer to a letter he is sending by a gentleman of his chamber.

# Bréant to M. de Réveillon, Cologne, 26 December 1673 and 8 January 1674: reading

The text is the contemporary decipherment written between the lines, checked word by word against the key rebuilt from it (`key.tsv`). The ciphered passages are in italics; the rest is the writer's own clear text. `{x}` marks a probable word (rule #728). "(…)" marks a stretch of the writer's clear text that is not transcribed here; it holds no cipher and does not enter the counts. Spelling as written; u/v and i/j as in the clear.

## 26 December 1673 (carton 170, piece 3, ff. 26-27)

Nos ambassadeurs ont besoin de sçavoir si avec *les trouppes que vous avez à Keyserswert* vous estes *en estat de soustenir une aproche des Ennemys et si vous estes* (…) *les Allemands qui sont dedans ce n'est pas sans* raison *qu'ils vous demandent* cela. Ils m'ont prié de vous en escrire affin de sçavoir *au juste* ce que vous *avez soit de François soit de Suisses*, et si avec ce que vous avez vous *pouvez estre le maistre de la place*, prenez la peine de m'en informer, affin que si *M. de Cologne {avoit} à tourner {casaque}* (…) car il est important que nous soyons tousjours *le maistre de ses meilleures places*. (…) *qu'il veuille escouter les propositions* que l'on *luy fait de la part de l'Empereur*. Je vous prie que cela soit entre *M. de Chamilly & vous* et moy (…). Bréant.

P.-S. Si *par hazard M. de Cologne s'accomodoit avec les Ennemys vous en* seriez *advertis* et je vous *prie de ne point faire d'esclat de ceci ; vous sçaurez tout ce qui se passera ici*.

Notes. "une" (before "aproche") and "à" (before "tourner") are the decipherer's words with no cipher group under them; they are not cipher words. The passage "M. de Cologne avoit à tourner casaque" is the one the decipherer struck through ("tourner caza que"); his clear agrees with the key for "tourner", and {avoit} (a-o-i-t, the v not enciphered) and {casaque} (74 ca, 319 sa/za?, 236 que; 74 and 319 inferred from the approximately alphabetical order) are counted probable.

## 8 January 1674 (carton 170, piece 4, ff. 28-29)

J'ay receu la lettre que vous m'avez fait l'honneur de m'escrire du 30 du mois passé et 2 du courant. J'ay monstré vostre lettre du 2 à nos Ambassadeurs, qui m'ont chargé de vous mander *d'estre asseuré que vous serez informé de tout ce qui se passera, et qu'ils ont parolle de M. de Cologne qu'il ne s'escoutera aucunes* propositions *de la part de l'Empereur jusques à ce qu'il ayt* response *à une Lettre qu'il a escrite au Roi, et qu'il envoyera* demain *par un gentilhomme de sa chambre*. Ainsy, Monsieur, *soyez en repos et soyez* persuadé *que vous ne* serez *point pris faute d'estre adverti, et que vous le serez* aussytost (…). Bréant.

# Bréant to M. de Réveillon, Cologne, 26 December 1673 and 8 January 1674: English translation

Translation of `reading.md`. The ciphered passages are in italics; `{x}` marks the same probable words; "(…)" marks clear text not transcribed. "M. de Cologne" is the Elector of Cologne (Maximilian Henry of Bavaria); "our ambassadors" are the French plenipotentiaries at the Cologne peace congress; Kaiserswerth is the Rhine fortress of the Elector held by Réveillon.

## 26 December 1673

Our ambassadors need to know whether, with *the troops you have at Kaiserswerth*, you are *in a state to withstand an approach of the enemy, and whether you are* (…) *the Germans who are inside; it is not without* reason *that they ask you* this. They have asked me to write to you so as to know *exactly* what you *have, whether of French or of Swiss*, and whether with what you have you *can be master of the place*; please take the trouble to let me know, so that if *M. de Cologne {were} to turn {his coat}* (…) for it is important that we should always be *masters of his best places*. (…) *that he may be willing to listen to the proposals* that are *made to him on behalf of the Emperor*. I beg you that this remain between *M. de Chamilly and you* and me (…). Bréant.

P.S. If *by chance M. de Cologne came to terms with the enemy, you* would be *warned of it*, and I *beg you to make no stir about this; you will know everything that happens here*.

## 8 January 1674

I have received the letter you did me the honour of writing to me of the 30th of last month and the 2nd of this. I showed your letter of the 2nd to our Ambassadors, who have charged me to tell you *to be assured that you will be informed of everything that happens, and that they have the word of M. de Cologne that he will listen to no* proposals *on behalf of the Emperor until he has* an answer *to a Letter he has written to the King, which he will send* tomorrow *by a gentleman of his chamber*. So, Sir, *be at rest and be* persuaded *that you will not* be *caught for want of being warned, and that you will be warned* at once (…). Bréant.
