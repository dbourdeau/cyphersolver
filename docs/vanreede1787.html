R1024 · Grand Chiffre of William V · 1782 · 1–5000

Berlin → The Hague · one-part numerical code (William V’s Grand Chiffre) · 1787–1788

Van Reede from Berlin: the Prussian alliance and the possessions overseas

Nationaal Archief 3.01.26 (L. P. van de Spiegel) inv. 190 · DECODE R1026 (29 Dec. 1787, two pages) and R1027 (4 Mar. 1788, one page).

The two French despatches are in the 1782 Grand Chiffre of Stadholder William V (DECODE R1024), as DECODE’s description suggests. A control letter with its plaintext on DECODE confirms the key. The sign the DECODE transcription could not place is the writer’s 1 with a hooked foot, and the blocks of the key that DECODE left out are nulls; with that, both letters are deciphered complete (23 October 2026). No earlier decoding of these two letters was found. Van Reede’s 1793 letter to the Secret Committee is in another code, with its own page.

At a glanceKnown key applied · complete

The letters
Despatches from Arend Willem van Reede, the Dutch Republic’s envoy in Berlin, to the Grand Pensionary Van de Spiegel: R1026 (29 December 1787), R1027 (4 March 1788), and a 1793 letter to the Secret Committee (R1893).
Where
DECODE R1026, R1027 and R1893, with no decipherment. The key is DECODE R1024, William V’s 1782 “Grand Chiffre” (Koninklijk Huisarchief A29, inv. 301).
The cipher
A one-part French code, 4,732 entries from 1 to 5000 in alphabetical order, with whole words, names, syllables and letters. DECODE’s J+ sign does not stand for one digit.
How it was read
R1024 decodes word for word an undated Van Reede letter (R1028) whose plaintext DECODE gives. Each J+ group was tried with all ten digits and chosen by a French character-level language model; 105 groups were settled that way and 452 of 472 groups now have a value.
What it says
The treaty of alliance Prussia signed with the Republic in April 1788: the King “could not guarantee our possessions overseas”, and the article was reworded to cover only an attack by a European power.
Still open
The 1793 letter (R1893) is in marked three-digit groups (696 groups, 513 distinct forms) for which no key has been found.

01 The documents and the key

R1026 (29 December 1787) and R1027 (4 March 1788) are Van Reede’s own despatches to Laurens Pieter van de Spiegel, in dot-separated groups of one to four digits with clear French at the head and foot. R1026 carries an interlinear gloss over its first groups, dites … avant hier, which is the first check on any key. The two letters share 49 groups. Van Reede’s letter of 24 March 1793 to the Secret Committee (DECODE R1893) is in another system, three-digit groups with marks over single digits, and is written up on its own page.

The key is DECODE R1024, the “Grand Chiffre” of William V (Koninklijk Huisarchief A29, inv. 301, dated 1782): a one-part French code in which the numbers run with the alphabet, up to 5000. Whole words, names (le Comte de Finkenstein, Monsieur de Herzberg), syllables and letters all have values, so rare words are spelt out: ul·er, simple·ment.

02 The control and the transcription

R1028, an undated Van Reede letter headed Chiffre de Mr de Reede, has its plaintext written out on DECODE. Its groups, decoded with R1024, give la reponse A donner au [Roi] à la communication que Sa Majesté vient de faire A leurs haut Puissance les voici le Ministre d’Angleterre A ajouter dans son rapport qu’on esperer·oit ic[i] qu’on approuver·oit A Londres…, matching the clear version word for word. The key is right and complete enough.

The same key on R1026 decoded cleanly in some lines (il doit y avoir ce matin … une conference à ce sujet) and made nonsense in others. Every nonsense word came from a group containing DECODE’s J+, which the transcriber defined as “a sign that is not represented by other signs”. The first pass took it as 3, the second as any digit chosen by a language model. Both were wrong: it is the writer’s hooked 1 (JJ8J is 1181 le Roi). 275J, read 2750 dites after the gloss, is 2751, in a null block: the gloss writer was guessing too.

03 Method

Superseded (23 October 2026): the per-group digit choice described here was withdrawn once J+ was seen to be a hooked 1; the readings below are built on the figures as re-read on the images (R1026-R1027_reread_log.md). The first method: resolve_j.py reads the DECODE transcription, keeps plain digits as written and treats each J+ as unknown. For each group it lists every digit filling whose number is in the key, and a search that keeps the 40 best partial texts chooses among them, scored by a six-character French language model built from eighteenth-century diplomatic correspondence (the Politische Correspondenz text already in the repository). Groups without J+ are never changed. 79 groups in R1026 and 26 in R1027 were resolved this way. The model can choose a wrong but grammatical word, and it cannot repair a plain digit the transcriber misread; both kinds of error remain in the passages marked below.

04 29 December 1787 (R1026)

The reading of 23 October 2026 (R1026_reading.md, English R1026_en.md), cipher and clear together, in the writer’s spelling; [?word] marks a word the groups carry only in part:

Berlyn le 29 Decem. 1787. Monsieur,

Avant hier les deux Ministres ont proposé au Roi le projet de l'alliance tel que vous l'avés, Monsieur. Comme ils persistoyent à differer d'opinion, j'ai prié Monsieur de Herzberg de detailler separement les sentiments dans le rapport et de motiver le sien, et il l'a fait. La reponse est arrivée hier au soir et elle me paroit bonne. Il doit y avoir ce matin à dix heures une conference à ce sujet encore entre le Roi et ses deux Ministres. J'espère qu'il n'y aura pas de changement. Le seul jusqu'ici dont il [?soit] question, c'est que le Roi a dit qu'il ne pouvoit pas garantir nos possessions d'outre Mer. Je ne l'ai pas demandé, mais bien qu'au cas d'elles fussent attaquées par une Puissance Européenne on [?fournit] en argent le subside. Peutêtre Sa Majesté entend elle par garantir fournir le subside, mais quand cela seroit, je ne desespere pas encore d'obtenir ce point. L'article sept que j'ai eu l'honneur de vous envoyer separement est supprimé comme desagreable pour l'Angleterre, à la requisition du Comtte de Finkenstein. Monsieur de Herzberg a rendu le Traité temporaire et l'a fixé à vingt ans. Voila jusqu'a present la seule difference entre ce qui a êté arreté hier et ce que j'ai eu l'honneur de vous envoyer.

Depuis que ceci a été chiffré, Monsieur, j'ai été au sortir d'une Conference que les deux Ministres ont eu avec le Roi, une visite à leurs Excellences, qui m'ont dit que mon audience ne pouvoit avoir lieu que Mercredi. Le Ceremonial a été reglé d'une maniere convenable, et quoique peu versé dans les formes, je [?vois] le plus [?estimé] d'[?ici] de reconnoitre l'attention de L.H.P. par les plus grandes politesses. Le Roi a bien voulu y mettre le comble en decidant de lui même, et quoique cela ne se fut pas pratiqué du temps de Mr de Tessin, Ambassadeur de Suede, que j'aurois le jour [?la] l'honneur de diner à sa table.

J'apprens qu'il a êté decidé dans la conference de ce matin que sans articuler le mot de possessions d'outre Mer on diroit simplement que si la Republique etoit attaquée par une Puissance Européenne le Roi lui fourniroit les secours stipulés, laissant ainsi la chose comme indecise. Cependant si leurs hautes Puissances m'ordonnent d'insister pour obtenir qu'on [?énonçoit] l'article plus clairement, je crois que je pourai l'obtenir, d'après la manière dont ceci m'a êté dit.

Si j'ai bien compris je n'ai pas à m'attendre à d'autres changements. J'ai l'honneur d'être avec un très profond respect, Monsieur, votre très humble et très obeissant serviteur, A. W. van Reede.

In English. Two days ago the two ministers, Finckenstein and Hertzberg, put the draft alliance to the King as Van de Spiegel had sent it; a further conference between the King and the ministers is set for ten this morning. The only point raised so far is that the King said he could not guarantee the Republic’s possessions overseas. Van Reede had not asked for that, only that if they were attacked in Europe the King would help with money or supplies. Hertzberg wrote his own view into the report; article seven was struck out as disagreeable to England at Finckenstein’s request; and the treaty was made temporary, for twenty years. After the conference he adds that the article is to drop the words possessions d’outre Mer and say simply that if the Republic is attacked by a European power the King will furnish the aid stipulated, leaving the point undecided; he expects no further change. This is the defensive treaty signed at Berlin on 15 April 1788, which guaranteed the stadholderate and the Republic’s territory in Europe; the Anglo-Dutch treaty of the same day covered the possessions overseas.

05 4 March 1788 (R1027)

Berlin le 4 de Mars 1788. Monsieur,

Les nouvelles de Russie entrées ce matin portent que Messieurs de Segur et de Cobentzel sont fort capots de ce que l'Imperatrice doit avoir [?decidement] refusé la triple alliance. Monsieur de Keller n'a point cependant encore de reponse, et ne croit la recevoir qu'après que l'Imperatrice aura celle qu'elle attend de Vienne à la demande qu'elle a faite à l'Empereur s'il etoit resolu ou non à une attaque vigoureuse et soutenue contre les Turcs; demande qui, à ce qu'on croit, a decidé l'Empereur à faire la declaration connue à la Turquie. Monsieur de Keller croit encore que l'Imperatrice voudra attendre le retour du premier Courier expedié à Londres, qui a êté suivi de deux autres, avant de s'expliquer sur les propositions du Roi.

N'y ayant rien de plus, Monsieur, qui puisse meriter votre attention, j'ai l'honeur de vous presenter mes respects, et d'être, Monsieur, votre très humble et très obeissant serviteur, A. W. van Reede.

In English. News from Russia: Ségur and Cobenzl, the French and Austrian envoys at St Petersburg, are put out because the Empress is said to have refused the triple alliance of Britain, Prussia and the Republic. Keller, the Prussian envoy, has no answer yet and expects none until the Empress has Vienna’s reply to her question to the Emperor; Joseph II has made his declaration of war on Turkey (9 February 1788). A courier has gone to London, followed by two more, before anyone will declare on the proposals.

06 What remains

  • Four words the groups spell as slips: R1026 [?soit], [?fournit], [?énonçoit] and R1027 [?decidement] (spelt de-ci-demi-ent). The bars above and below groups are the code’s inflection signs (a bar above cuts letters, one below adds the ending), but the key gives no instruction for them, so these readings rest on sense.
  • Three faint words in the clear passage of R1026 (“je [?vois] le plus [?estimé] d’[?ici]”, “le jour [?la]”), on the 1,240-px DECODE image; a better image of the Nationaal Archief original would settle them.
  • The groups once listed as open are closed: 2637 is cependant (mis-numbered 3637 in the key transcription), and 1250, 331, 2800, 3160, 3660, 3153, 2751, 50 and 1201 fall in the key’s null blocks.
  • Grades. The decoded groups are not graded one by one. The structural sentences quoted in English (the conference, the refusal to guarantee the overseas possessions, the reworded article, Joseph II’s declaration) rest on runs of groups without J+ and are certain; words chosen by the model are probable.
  • R1893, 24 March 1793. A different code, not the Grand Chiffre: see its own page.

R1026, Berlin, 29 Dec 1787, Van Reede to Van de Spiegel

Van Reede to Van de Spiegel, Berlin, 29 Dec 1787: the two ministers put the alliance project to the King; Hertzberg motivated his own view; a conference this morning; the King will not guarantee the Dutch possessions overseas; article seven struck out as disagreeable to England at Finckenstein's request; Hertzberg made the treaty temporary, twenty years. Clear passage: his audience on Wednesday, the King invites him to dine. Second cipher passage: the conference decided to say only that if the Republic is attacked by a European power the King will furnish the stipulated aid; he could get it stated more clearly if ordered.

## Reading

Berlyn le 29 Decem. 1787. Monsieur,

Avant hier les deux Ministres ont proposé au Roi le projet de l'alliance tel que vous l'avés, Monsieur. Comme ils
persistoyent à differer d'opinion, j'ai prié Monsieur de Herzberg de detailler separement les sentiments dans le
rapport et de motiver le sien, et il l'a fait. La reponse est arrivée hier au soir et elle me paroit bonne. Il doit y
avoir ce matin à dix heures une conference à ce sujet encore entre le Roi et ses deux Ministres. J'espère qu'il n'y
aura pas de changement. Le seul jusqu'ici dont il [?soit] question, c'est que le Roi a dit qu'il ne pouvoit pas
garantir nos possessions d'outre Mer. Je ne l'ai pas demandé, mais bien qu'au cas d'elles fussent attaquées par une
Puissance Européenne on [?fournit] en argent le subside. Peutêtre Sa Majesté entend elle par garantir fournir le
subside, mais quand cela seroit, je ne desespere pas encore d'obtenir ce point. L'article sept que j'ai eu l'honneur
de vous envoyer separement est supprimé comme desagreable pour l'Angleterre, à la requisition du Comtte de
Finkenstein. Monsieur de Herzberg a rendu le Traité temporaire et l'a fixé à vingt ans. Voila jusqu'a present la
seule difference entre ce qui a êté arreté hier et ce que j'ai eu l'honneur de vous envoyer.

Depuis que ceci a été chiffré, Monsieur, j'ai été au sortir d'une Conference que les deux Ministres ont eu avec le
Roi, une visite à leurs Excellences, qui m'ont dit que mon audience ne pouvoit avoir lieu que Mercredi. Le Ceremonial
a été reglé d'une maniere convenable, et quoique peu versé dans les formes, je [?vois] le plus [?estimé] d'[?ici] de
reconnoitre l'attention de L.H.P. par les plus grandes politesses. Le Roi a bien voulu y mettre le comble en decidant
de lui même, et quoique cela ne se fut pas pratiqué du temps de Mr de Tessin, Ambassadeur de Suede, que j'aurois le
jour [?la] l'honneur de diner à sa table.

J'apprens qu'il a êté decidé dans la conference de ce matin que sans articuler le mot de possessions d'outre Mer on
diroit simplement que si la Republique etoit attaquée par une Puissance Européenne le Roi lui fourniroit les secours
stipulés, laissant ainsi la chose comme indecise. Cependant si leurs hautes Puissances m'ordonnent d'insister pour
obtenir qu'on [?énonçoit] l'article plus clairement, je crois que je pourai l'obtenir, d'après la manière dont ceci
m'a êté dit.

Si j'ai bien compris je n'ai pas à m'attendre à d'autres changements. J'ai l'honneur d'être avec un très profond
respect, Monsieur, votre très humble et très obeissant serviteur, A. W. van Reede.

## Translation

Berlin, 29 December 1787. Sir,

The day before yesterday the two Ministers put to the King the project of the alliance as you have it, Sir. As they
went on differing in opinion, I asked Monsieur de Hertzberg to set out the views separately in the report and to give
the reasons for his own, and he did so. The answer arrived yesterday evening and it seems good to me. There is to be a
conference on this subject again this morning at ten, between the King and his two Ministers. I hope there will be no
change. The only point [?raised] so far is that the King said he could not guarantee our possessions overseas. I did
not ask for that, but only that, should they be attacked by a European Power, the subsidy [?be furnished] in money.
Perhaps His Majesty understands by "guarantee" furnishing the subsidy; but even if that were so, I do not yet despair
of obtaining this point. Article seven, which I had the honour of sending you separately, is suppressed as
disagreeable to England, at the request of Count Finckenstein. Monsieur de Hertzberg has made the Treaty temporary and
fixed it at twenty years. That is, so far, the only difference between what was settled yesterday and what I had the
honour of sending you.

Since this was put into cipher, Sir, I have been, on coming out of a Conference that the two Ministers had with the
King, on a visit to their Excellencies, who told me that my audience could not take place before Wednesday. The
ceremonial has been settled in a fitting manner, and although I am little versed in forms, I [?consider it] the most
[?esteemed] [?here] to acknowledge the attention of Their High Mightinesses with the greatest courtesies. The King
was pleased to crown it by deciding of his own accord, although this was not done in the time of Monsieur de Tessin,
Ambassador of Sweden, that I should have [?that] day the honour of dining at his table.

I learn that it was decided at this morning's conference that, without spelling out the words "possessions overseas",
it would simply be said that if the Republic were attacked by a European Power the King would furnish it the
stipulated aid, thus leaving the matter as it were undecided. However, if Their High Mightinesses order me to insist
on having the article [?stated] more clearly, I believe I shall be able to obtain it, from the way this was said to me.

If I have understood rightly, I need not expect other changes. I have the honour to be with a very profound respect,
Sir, your very humble and very obedient servant, A. W. van Reede.

Van Reede to Van de Spiegel, Berlin, 4 Mar 1788: news from Russia that Ségur and Cobenzl are dismayed that the Empress has refused the triple alliance; Keller (the Prussian envoy) has no answer and expects none until the Empress hears from Vienna whether the Emperor is resolved on a vigorous attack on the Turks, which decided the Emperor's declaration; the Empress will wait for the courier sent to London before declaring herself on the King's proposals.

## Reading

Berlin le 4 de Mars 1788. Monsieur,

Les nouvelles de Russie entrées ce matin portent que Messieurs de Segur et de Cobentzel sont fort capots de ce que
l'Imperatrice doit avoir [?decidement] refusé la triple alliance. Monsieur de Keller n'a point cependant encore de
reponse, et ne croit la recevoir qu'après que l'Imperatrice aura celle qu'elle attend de Vienne à la demande qu'elle a
faite à l'Empereur s'il etoit resolu ou non à une attaque vigoureuse et soutenue contre les Turcs; demande qui, à ce
qu'on croit, a decidé l'Empereur à faire la declaration connue à la Turquie. Monsieur de Keller croit encore que
l'Imperatrice voudra attendre le retour du premier Courier expedié à Londres, qui a êté suivi de deux autres, avant de
s'expliquer sur les propositions du Roi.

N'y ayant rien de plus, Monsieur, qui puisse meriter votre attention, j'ai l'honeur de vous presenter mes respects, et
d'être, Monsieur, votre très humble et très obeissant serviteur, A. W. van Reede.

## Notes

- `de ci demi ent` (groups 37-40) spells "decidemient"; "decidement" is the sense, hence `[?decidement]`.
- `soutenir` + two bars, `U` + bar under (groups 92-93): "souten" + "u" + ending = soutenue.
- Monsieur de Keller is the Prussian envoy at St Petersburg; Segur and Cobenzl the French and Austrian envoys there;
  the Emperor's declaration of war on the Turks was made in February 1788.

# R1027 — Van Reede to Van de Spiegel, Berlin, 4 March 1788: English

Translation of `R1027_reading.md` (same holes).

## Translation

Berlin, 4 March 1788. Sir,

The news from Russia that came in this morning says that Messieurs de Ségur and Cobenzl are quite dumbfounded that the
Empress is supposed to have [?decidedly] refused the triple alliance. Monsieur de Keller, however, has still had no
answer, and does not expect to receive it until the Empress has the one she is awaiting from Vienna to the request she
made to the Emperor, whether he was resolved or not on a vigorous and sustained attack against the Turks; a request
which, it is believed, decided the Emperor to make the declaration to Turkey that is now known. Monsieur de Keller
further believes that the Empress will want to await the return of the first courier sent to London, which was
followed by two others, before declaring herself on the King's proposals.

There being nothing more, Sir, that could deserve your attention, I have the honour to present my respects to you and
to be, Sir, your very humble and very obedient servant, A. W. van Reede.
