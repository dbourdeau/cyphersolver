A Bavarian at Wardein to Duke Ludwig, 14 March 1537

A servant of the Dukes of Bavaria at King John Zápolya’s court reports the Turks arming to take Hungary; a three-line gloss of the time gave the key, and 92% of the words are deciphered; the lines that read as sentences hold 65% of the words, and six fragmentary bands are decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026). Counted by words, the written-out reading builds coherent sentences but reads at 89.2%, under the 95% a complete reading needs.

The letter
An unnamed Bavarian servant at King John’s court to Duke Ludwig X of Bavaria, Wardein (Nagyvárad), 14 March 1537. German; the opening, the date and the address are in clear.
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 257–259; DECODE R9414, part of catalogue entry 162 (“to unknown recipient, 3 ciphertexts”).
The cipher
A homophonic alphabet of 42 invented signs, several for each common letter, with a few signs for whole words; it matches no other key in the volume.
How it was deciphered
A clerk of the time wrote the plain text over lines 6–8 of the first page. Those values seeded a solver (simulated annealing and a search over candidate values with a sixteenth-century German letter model); four passes against the images followed.
What it says
The Sultan has ordered his pashas to be ready and is arming a hundred thousand men to win Hungary and then turn on Austria; Ferdinand’s party is taking towns; the writer asks the duke to intercede with his brother Wilhelm. Postscript: “der herr Camermaister hat mein ziffer”.
Still open
The reading stands at 89.2% of its words (measured 24 October 2026). Six bands (P1.12–32, P2.15–19, P3.01–09, P3.39–P4.01, P4.21–35 and the postscript P5.05–07) are decoded, not read: they hold most of the about ten word signs with no key, fifteen blotted words, and about fifty-five legible words whose homophones do not settle. Re-read on 23 October 2026: DECODE R9414 has six images and no attachment, its neighbouring records are other letters in other systems, and no edition is known, so nothing outside the letter can settle them. The lines that read were written out the same day (r9414/R9414_reading.md, English R9414_en.md).

Until 15 October 2026 this letter shared a page with two others of the same catalogue entry. They are different ciphers, each with its own page: Łaski’s letter of 1531, in Łaski’s own key, and the Latin letter of 18 April 1533, in the key of the Sperantio file.

01 The letter

… dan der turkisch kaiser irn herrn befolhen hab das si in aller eil, ieder mit […] pferden gerust sein …

The writer describes his journey back into Hungary (“got weis wie mich die rais mit groser sorg ist ankummen”) and the state of Hungarian affairs; envoys of the pashas of Belgrade and Bosnia; the Turkish emperor ordering them to be ready and arming a hundred thousand men to win Hungary and then turn on Austria; Ferdinand’s party taking towns and villages; a copy of the truce “von Lunden”, from the Archbishop of Lund, Charles V’s negotiator with King John. Duke Wilhelm never writes to him, so he asks Ludwig to intercede, citing his service to their father; he writes of his debts and leave, of mining and ore specimens, and of Hans Schwab of Kraków as carrier. The date is in clear: “Datum Wardein … den vierzehenden tag Marcii anno siben und dreissigisten”; the address reads “Herzogen Ludwigen in Bayrn”.

02 How the key was recovered

The gloss over lines 6–8 gave the first values of a new homophonic alphabet. A solver (simulated annealing) and a search over candidate values with a language model of sixteenth-century German printed texts brought it to about half; three passes over every line against the image and a search for dictionary words one letter away brought it to 91.3%. A fourth pass on full-resolution crops of every gap (5 October 2026) brought it to 92.2%. A statistical check passes: the reading scores far above random keys and above keys fitted to shuffled text. Łaski’s key, the System A′ key, Augurelio’s key of 1531 and the key of the 1533 letter were tried on it and do not fit.

03 What stays open

  • About ten words are word signs with no key: Ψ at P2 lines 2, 8, 9, 27 (it fits Mt., the King’s Majesty, in four of six places, not counted), a swash X at P2.15, P2.25, P4.2, P4.21, and K at P4.33, probably “the king”.
  • A barred ∂ at five places fits no letter or short word better than leaving it out: probably a null or a mark.
  • About fifteen words are blotted or struck.
  • About fifty-five words are legible but unresolved: several homophones still have more than one value.
