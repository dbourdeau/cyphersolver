The letters
Seven letters of Henri IV to Maurice the Learned, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel, 22 November 1602 to 24 January 1609, with ciphered passages.
Where
Printed in 1840 by Christoph von Rommel from the Marburg originals, with the ciphered passages as two-digit figures he could not decipher.
The cipher
Two-digit figures. Rommel had the key six years later from Groen van Prinsterer and van der Kemp and printed it in a German journal with one sample letter, going no further.
How it was read
The 1846 key, applied to the 1840 page images, deciphers all seven passages (about 4,100 groups). No earlier application to the other letters was found.
What it says
On 22 December 1605 the King ties the Mérargues treason to the Gunpowder Plot and promises the Dutch two million livres again for 1606. On 20 May 1606 he warns that the Spaniards mean to have a King of the Romans elected at their devotion.
Still open
A prince’s name on p. 213, seven groups on p. 307, one unlisted name-group and a line of signs the 1840 typesetter could not set. The Marburg originals were not seen.

01 The documents

Maurice of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632) was the most learned and the most francophile of the Calvinist princes of the Empire, and Henri IV’s regular correspondent on German affairs from the Bongars mission of 1599 to the Jülich crisis of 1610. Rommel, the Hessian archivist and historian, printed 118 pieces of the exchange from the Marburg archive in 1840 (Paris, Renouard). The Landgrave’s letters are in clear; several of the King’s carry passages in figures, which Rommel set as he found them, and some of the King’s letters he printed from a contemporary decipherment with the figures omitted. The list below is what the volume prints in figures from the King’s side; nothing else on it was ciphered by him.

02 The key, and what the texts corrected in it

The cipher is the one Tomokiyo found as an original sheet in BnF Clairambault 360 f. 168, dated October 1602, and it served the whole correspondence to 1610. Bare two-digit figures are letters, up to four homophones each; a comma after the figure sends it to a list of small words and syllables (le, la, leur, que, qui, qu’il, pour, pro, pre, ma, me, mon, ne, ny, nous, on, où, sa, se, roy), two dots above it to a second (con, ca, car, don, da, di, do, de, en, eulx, faire, faict, point, elle, forces, troupes, guerre, cour impériale, diète de l’Empire), an overbar to names and titles (Bouillon, Comte, Conseil, Pape, Empereur, Roi d’Espagne, Roi d’Angleterre, l’Archiduc, Brandebourg, Brunswick, provinces unies, Protestant, Allemagne, Angleterre, argent, armée, alliés, ambassadeur, au, aux, afin). A dozen drawn symbols stand for te, tion, tant, tous, tout, tre, grand, va, ville, votre, Hongrie; one sign doubles the symbol before it, one cancels it, and a few flourishes are nulls.

Rommel’s print of the key is right in every letter value, but its lists are set with fewer words than figures and its fourth row of homophones is misaligned, so the texts had to fix the rest: 70 is a fourth p missing from the table (presse, perdra, principalement); 85 is m and 83 is o (maison, ensemble, favorisé); 17, is la and 18, 19, le; 24, lettre, 25, leur; 65, pro (profiter, prospèrent), 66, pre; 83, rompre (cor-rompre); 70 with dots is faict, 84 with dots forces; 26 with overbar is Comte (Rommel’s own glossary: le comte de Solms), which shifts the names one place so that 31 is Pape, 33 Empereur, 35 Roi d’Espagne and 37 Roi d’Angleterre, as the 24 January 1609 line “les ministres du Pape et du Roi d’Espagne” and the 1604 control both require. The group Rommel set under l’administrateur de Strasbourg as 59 is le Turc (“la paix avec [59]”, December 1605); the tall 4 is te or tion and the print does not separate them; the typesetter’s “9 +” is the doubling sign 94; and the ♀-, Ψ- and tt-like flourishes are nulls (“ayant ♀ tt sceu que le duc de Féria…”). Every one of these is recorded in key.py with the passage that fixed it.

Controls. Rommel printed Groen van Prinsterer’s decipherment of the 3 April 1604 letter beside the key; the first lines of p. 171 decode here word for word to his text (“…ainsy demander la paix à leurs ennemis en leur pays, que la nécessité qui les presse est très grande…”). His footnotes to the Landgrave’s letter of 10 February 1603 gloss five groups (diète de l’Empire, l’Archiduc, Protestants, Empereur, cour impériale) and the key returns each; his 1840 glossary “Passages déchiffrés” agrees throughout. The main check is internal: every passage decrypts to continuous early-seventeenth-century French that joins the clear text it interrupts.

03 What the King wrote

22 November 1602. He holds his despatch for the advice Maurice promised on the Diet: whether it will meet, what will be treated, what proposal he should make “pour le public et le particulier de mes alliés”, and whether to send his ambassador on to Saxony and Denmark, “avec votre bon conseil sur le [mémoire] et instruction que je luy doibs donner”.

12 January 1603. Since the Emperor will not appear at the Diet nothing of weight will be treated, “et principallement de l’élection d’un roy des Romains”. Bouillon has not written a word about the Administrator of Strasbourg’s coming to France nor about the bishopric: “je ne sçay comme il aura pris ce voyage, ny de quelque sorte il embrasse la cause du dit Administrateur”.

27 December 1604. Why he has not sent the embassy to the German princes that Maurice advised: “la mauvaise intelligence que j’ay recogneue entre les [princes] protestants”, and “le mescontentement et dégoust qu’aucuns d’eulx m’ont donné … depuis qu’ils ont adjousté foy aux discours du duc de Bouillon et favorisé sa cause avec moins de respect et considération à ma personne que je n’espérois d’eulx”. The Hungarian revolt is out but “la suite et conséquence en sera grande”. James I’s courtship of Spain: “il fault qu’il en face les preuves devant qu’il le croye”. The Dutch have as much courage as ever and are provided for; a newly-visited prince has given them hope of arming for them next year, and Maurice should encourage him. The Spaniards forge new plots against him daily; the comte d’Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister are in justice “d’avoir traité avec eulx plus avant qu’ils ne devoyent”.

28 April 1605. The lines Rommel regretted: the aid the United Provinces and Geneva continue to receive from him “justifient assez la vérité de ma conduite et volonté en tels cas, et le soin que j’ay de maintenir et soubstenir ceulx de la dite religion, pour les notables intérests que j’ay à leur conservation et de la cause publicque”.

22 December 1605. Mérargues’ treason and the attempt on the King “me sont arrivés quasi au mesme temps de la conspiration d’Angleterre. Il semble aussy qu’ils ayent esté forgés sur mesme enclume”; the Spanish ambassador’s complaint is what Spanish ministers do everywhere, “seduire et corrompre les subjects des princes auprès desquels ils résident”; James I has as much cause to complain “toutesfois il le couvre et dissimule mieulx”. He has recommended the United Provinces to James “au besoin extrême qu’elles ont à présent d’estre assistées et renforcées par leurs alliés pour relever l’année prochaine la réputation de leurs armes”, and asks Maurice, who has “notable intérest à leur conservation avec les autres princes d’Allemagne”, to see them helped “tant d’argent que de troupes” as some princes have promised. He will continue “la mesme assistance d’argent et de troupes que je leur ay départie ceste-cy; elle est très notable et non moindre de deux millions de livres; mais si avec cela ils ne sont point secourus d’ailleurs, il leur sera impossible de résister”, the enemy massing money and men “pour les assaillir plus vivement et puissamment qu’ils n’ont point faict”. Get the help to them by spring, “car celuy qui pourra prévenir sa partie en cela en sera grandement avantagé”. And what does the Emperor mean by the Diet he has summoned; the Turkish peace; the Hungarian accord; the siege of Brunswick.

20 May 1606. The Emperor “ne change point de conduite” and is ill served, above all in Hungary. “Les Espagnols feront ce qu’ils pourront pour faire eslire un roy des Romains de la maison d’Austriche qui soit entièrement à leur dévotion”, and Feria goes to the Diet to promote it and to propose the pacification of the Netherlands, failing which the Empire is to make war on the States: “deux propositions de grande conséquence et auxquelles toute la chrestienté n’a moins d’intérest que l’Allemagne mesme. C’est pourquoy j’estime estre nécessaire que tous les roys et princes qui doivent avoir jalousie de l’agrandissement et prospérité de la puissance espagnole doibvent d’heure aviser et prendre conseil ensemble de ce qu’il convient faire pour empescher que les dicts Espagnols ne fassent cadrer les affaires et résolutions de la dite diète à leur souhait. Pour mon regard, j’y contribueray tousjours très volontiers ce qui dépendra de moy et me sera bienséant de faire, principalement quand je recognoistray que les autres pourront faire le semblable … pour la conservation de la liberté germanique, sans toutesfois en attendre autre advantage que d’advancer le bien de la cause commune et la seureté publique”; Maurice is to tell those he judges worthy and to advise the King what he “puis et doibs faire maintenant”. James I will not break with Spain over Owen and Baldwin, “tant il désire vivre en neutralité et en paix”, and the Spaniards ply him with “offres et ouvertures très spécieuses”; their aim is the Netherlands, “un grand effort ceste année, principallement du costé du Rhin et de Frise”, waiting only for Spinola. The States, weaker, will be forced onto the defensive, “misérable condition … en un estat composé et gouverné par plusieurs testes”; all whose interest is joined to theirs must act “devant que leur débilité et inanition soyent plus grandes, autrement les remèdes y seront trop tardifs”. His earlier request that Maurice press the German princes has had “fort peu d’effect”. The Dutch prosper in the East Indies and off Portugal, but “je doubte que ceste bonne fortune leur dure si celle de la terre leur manque”. The Spaniards will exploit the quarrel of the Pope and Venice; he will try to compose it.

24 January 1609. “Les ministres du Pape et du Roi d’Espagne” will do everything to reconcile Matthias with the Emperor.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Nine groups on p. 213 give the name of the prince who visited the States (“d’un nouveau …”) and do not resolve; seven groups at the top of p. 307, the unlisted name-group 98 with overbar on p. 211 (a prince whose friendship Lorraine mediated: Bavaria is a guess*), and a line of letters and signs on p. 311 that the 1840 typesetter could not set in figures. The Marburg originals would settle them.
  • The 1840 print is a transcription, and single figures are wrong in it (a 36 for 26, an 18 for 15); the decipherments mark supplied words in brackets.
  • The tall-4 symbol is te or tion by context; 55, and 56, are both used where paix or pays is wanted.
  • Whether the Landgrave’s chancery decipherments survive at Marburg is not checked; the Lettres missives reprinted the figures without deciphering them.

05 Method and reproduction

The Internet Archive’s hOCR of the volume located the pages with dense figures. The figures were then transcribed by eye from 900-pixel strips of the full-resolution page images, because the OCR loses exactly what matters: the comma, the two dots, the overbar and the drawn symbols. The transcriptions in targets/hesse1603/ct/ keep Rommel’s line breaks and the clear text; key.py holds the key with each correction and its evidence; run.py decodes a transcription; the hand decipherments are in letters/READINGS.md. No solver was used. With the 1846 key, the work was transcription and decryption. About 4,100 groups.

# Readings of the ciphered passages of Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice, Rommel 1840

Hand readings from the decodes in `*_decode.txt` (key in `../key.py`, transcriptions in `../ct/`). Spelling is the
clerk's as the figures give it, lightly normalised (u/v, i/j, apostrophes, punctuation). Square brackets mark words

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… Aussi, je n'en diffère plus la dépesche que pour attendre le **advis que vous m'avez promis est[re] sur icelle, et
spécialement sur le faict de la diette, et sçavoir si elle doibt avoir lieu, ce qui s'y doibt traicter, quelle
proposition j'y doibs faire pour le public et le particulier de mes alliés et a[utres], [en]suivant les discours que
nous avons [eu] ensemble, et pour sçavoir aussi si je ferois passer en Saxe et Danemarch mon dit ambassadeur, avec votre
bon conseil sur le [19, overbar: mémoire?] et instruction que je luy doibs donner.** Car j'ay pris telle asseurance et
confiance de votre amitié et prudence, que je veux cy après …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[ce qui se] peut sans préjudicier à nos alliés,** sur quoy je seray donques attendant en bonne dévotion votre bon
advis. Puisque l'Empereur ne comparoistra en la diette, j'estime qu'il ne s'y traitera chose d'importance **et
principallement de l'élection d'un roy des Romains,** dont je m'attends que vous m'advertirez, comme de ce qui se
passera en l'assemblée des Estats de Polongne. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **Mais le duc [de Bouillon] ne m'a
escript par sa lettre un seul mot de la venue par deçà de l'Administrateur [de Strasbourg], ny du faict de l'évesché de
Stras[bourg], tellement que je ne sçay co[m]me il aura pris ce voyage, ny de quelque sorte il embrasse la cause du dit
Administrateur;** je vous prie me faire sçavoir ce que vous en aurez apris.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211–214)

… ce que vous avez voulu m'en déférer. **[Le duc de … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] fait toute démonstration
de me porter de l'affection. Je vous assure aussy que je fais grande estime de leur amitié; mon frère le duc de Lorraine
en est l'entremetteur.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] Mais deux raisons m'en ont empesché: l'une est **la mauvaise intelligence
que j'ay recogneue entre les [princes?] protestants, laquelle j'ay eu crainte estre cause de rendre la dite visitation
peu fructueuse;** et l'autre **le mescontentement et dégoust qu'aucuns d'eulx m'ont donné de leurs actions,**
principallement depuis **qu'ils ont adjousté foy aux discours du duc de Bouillon et favorisé sa cause avec moins de
respect et considération à ma personne que je n'espérois d'eulx,** comme je vous ay quelques fois escrit …

Le feu de la révolte de la haulte Hongrie a esté bientost esteint, mais **la suite et conséquence en sera grande, et
pourra s'estendre avec le temps au voisinage.** Le roy d'Angleterre envoye vers moy le duc de Lenox, et en Espagne son
admiral, pour s'entretenir en bonne amitié de toutes parts: **conseil duquel je n'ay pas ouï qu'il se trouve [bien]**
avec le temps, mais il fault **qu'il en face les preuves devant qu'il le croye.**

Je suis adverty que **les Provinces Unies ont autant de courage de se deffendre et continuer la guerre que jamais,** et
veulx croire, cela estant, que **les moyens ne leur en manqueront point, estans pourveus comme ils sont; ils envoyent
des députés en Angleterre; ils espèrent qu'ils seront mieulx receus et favorisés que les Espagnols ne se sont promis,
car le d[it] roy ne veult perdre leur amitié,** ainsy que l'on m'a mandé. **Les dites provinces** ont esté naguères
visiter **d'un nouveau [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, des bonnes quallités duquel ils sont demeurés bien édifiés. Aussi
est-il vertueux. J'entends **qu'il leur a donné quelque espérance d'armer en leur faveur l'année prochaine.** En quoy je
vous prie **de le conforter et assister,** sy vous en rencontrez l'occasion.

**Je suis avec les Espagnols comme de coustume, découvrant journellement quelque nouvelle menée forgée de leur part à mon
désavantage; mais** Dieu m'en préservera. J'ay mis en justice le comte d'Auvergne, son beau-père et sa sœur, qui **sont
chargés et accusés d'avoir traité avec eulx plus avant qu'ils ne devoyent,** et serez adverty de ce qui en succèdera …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269–272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … et mesmes de ma part, **il fault considérer que tous ces
accidens me sont arrivés quasi au mesme temps de la conspiration d'Angleterre. Il semble aussy qu'ils ayent esté forgés
sur mesme enclume.** L'ambassadeur d'Espagne vouloit me faire accroire que j'avois faict injure à son maître d'avoir
retenu son secrétaire, ayant esté surpris sur la tissure de sa trahison, **tant les ministres de ceste nation ont
accoustumé se[d]uire et corrompre les subjects des princes auprès desquels [ils] résident.** Je suis asseuré que le roy
de la Grande Bretagne, mon bon frère, a autant d'argument de s'en plaindre que moy, **toutesfois il le couvre et
dissimule mieulx.** Mais je ne puis croire que pour ce faict **ses affaires en prospèrent** davantage; ce qui seroit
besoin **que tous ceulx qui l'ayment luy remonstrassent,** comme **j'ay fait par un ambassadeur que** j'ay envoyé vers
luy exprès pour cest effect … pareillement je luy ay recommandé **les Provinces Unies au besoin extrême qu'elles ont** à
présent d'estre **assistées et renforcées par leurs alliés pour relever** l'année prochaine **la réputation de leurs
armes.** Mais, mon cousin, comme vous avez **notable intérest à leur conservation avec les autres princes d'Allemagne,**
je vous prie vous emploier à ce qu'ils soient pareillement **secourus de votre costé tant d'argent que de troupes,**
suivant l'espérance qu'aucuns **des dits princes leur ont donnée,** et vous ferez un très bon œuvre, **très utile à la
cause commune et qui [me] sera** très agréable, vous priant me mander ce que j'en doibs espérer; pour mon regard j'ay
délibéré **leur continuer l'année prochaine la mesme assistance d'argent et de troupes que je leur ay départie
ceste-cy; elle est très notable et non moindre de deux millions de livres; mais si avec cela ils ne sont point secourus
d'ailleurs, il leur sera impossible de résister à leurs ennemis, car ils font amas d'argent et de forces de toutes parts
pour les assaillir plus vivement et puissamment qu'ils n'ont point [faict].** Soyez donc **autheur,** mon cousin, **de
leur faire recevoir ledict secours, et que ce soit le plus promptement que faire se pourra, afin qu'ils puissent se
mettre aux champs au printemps des premiers, car celuy qui pourra prévenir sa partie en cela en sera grandement
avantagé.** Au demeurant, **mandez-moy ce que l'Empereur prétend traiter en la diète de l'Empire qu'il a intimée, et ce
que** vous espérez **des négotiations de la paix avec [le Turc] et de l'accord des Hongrois avec l'Empereur, comme de
la poursuite du siège de la ville de Brunswick, où j'ay regret de voir [le duc de Brunswick] engagé.** Je prie Dieu …
Escript à Paris le xxiie jour de décembre 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307–314)

… d'en faire part aux princes de la Germanie de ceste condition là, **[7 groups unresolved] que les choses sont tousjours
en grande confusion,** parce que **l'Empereur ne change point de conduite, et qu'il est très mal assisté et [servi] en
ses affaires, principa[l]lement du costé de** Hongrie, où l'on ne fait la paix ny la guerre comme il convient …
[p. 308 in clear: the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … de toutes
parts. **Les Espagnols feront ce qu'ils pourront pour faire eslire un roy des Romains de la maison d'Austriche qui soit
entièrement à leur dévotion, ayant** sceu que le duc de Féria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the
election, to propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war
on them] … **qui sont deux propositions de grande conséquence et auxquelles toute la chrestienté n'a moins d'intérest
[que] l'Allemagne mesme. C'est pourquoy j'estime estre nécessaire que tous les roys et princes qui doivent avoir
jalousie de l'agrandissement et prospérité de la puissance espagnole doibvent d'heure aviser et prendre conseil
ensemble de ce qu'il convient faire pour empescher que les dicts Espagnols ne fassent cadrer les affaires et résolutions
de la dite diète à leur souhait. Pour mon regard, j'y contribueray tousjours très volontiers ce qui dépendra de moy et me
sera bienséant de faire, principalement quand je recognoistray que les autres pourront faire le semblable, et par dessus
tous les princes de la [Hollande: Germanie?], n'estant moins désireux que ont esté les roys mes prédécesseurs de
favoriser et seconder les bonnes intentions des anciens alliés et amis de ceste couronne en semblables occasions comme

# Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Kassel, 1602-1609 (Rommel 1840): English translation of the readings

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `letters/READINGS.md`, sections 1-7: the readings of the ciphered
passages, from the decodes of Rommel's 1840 printed transcription, with the clear text round them as Rommel prints it.
As in the reading, the ciphered passages are **bold**; `…` marks where the quoted text breaks off in clear; `[word]` a
word supplied where the print is faulty or a table entry is unlisted; `[?word]` a doubtful word; `[?]` and the
bracketed notes (unresolved groups, clear passages summarised) are kept. The "controls" read alongside (the
Landgrave's own letters and a letter deciphered in 1846) are not translated here.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… So I delay the despatch no longer except to await the **advice which you have promised me to be on it, and
especially on the matter of the diet, and to know whether it is to take place, what is to be treated there, what
proposal I am to make there for the public and the particular interest of my allies and o[thers], [fol]lowing the
discussions we [had] together; and also to know whether I should have my said ambassador go on into Saxony and
Denmark, with your good counsel on the [19, overbar: ?memorandum] and instruction I am to give him.** For I have taken
such assurance of and confidence in your friendship and prudence that I wish hereafter …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[what] can [be done] without prejudice to our allies,** on which I shall then be awaiting your good advice with
good devotion. Since the Emperor will not appear at the diet, I consider that nothing of importance will be treated
there, **and principally not the election of a King of the Romans,** of which I expect you will inform me, as of what
will happen in the assembly of the Estates of Poland. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **But the duke [de
Bouillon] has not written me a single word in his letter about the coming here of the Administrator [of Strasbourg],
nor about the matter of the bishopric of Stras[bourg], so that I do not know how he will have taken this journey, nor
in what way he embraces the cause of the said Administrator;** I pray you to let me know what you have learned of it.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211-214)

… what you have been pleased to refer to me about it. **[The duke of … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] makes
every show of bearing me affection. I assure you also that I hold their friendship in great esteem; my brother the
Duke of Lorraine is the go-between in it.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] But two reasons have prevented me: one is **the bad understanding I have
observed among the Protestant [?princes], which I feared would make the said visit unfruitful;** and the other **the
discontent and distaste which some of them have given me by their actions,** principally since **they gave credit to
the talk of the Duke of Bouillon and favoured his cause with less respect and consideration for my person than I
hoped from them,** as I have sometimes written to you …

The fire of the revolt of Upper Hungary was soon put out, but **its sequel and consequence will be great, and may
spread with time to the neighbourhood.** The King of England is sending the Duke of Lennox to me, and his admiral to
Spain, to maintain himself in good friendship on all sides: **a counsel which I have not heard that he finds [good]**
with time, but **he must put it to the proof before he believes it.**

I am informed that **the United Provinces have as much courage to defend themselves and continue the war as ever,**
and I will believe, this being so, that **the means will not fail them, provided as they are; they are sending
deputies to England; they hope they will be better received and favoured than the Spaniards have promised
themselves, for the s[aid] King does not wish to lose their friendship,** as I have been told. **The said provinces**
were lately visited **by a new [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, by whose good qualities they were much edified. And he
is indeed virtuous. I hear **that he gave them some hope of arming in their favour next year.** In which I pray you
**to encourage and assist him,** if you find the occasion.

**I am with the Spaniards as usual, discovering daily some new intrigue forged on their part to my disadvantage;
but** God will preserve me from it. I have brought to justice the Count of Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister,
who **are charged and accused of having dealt with them further than they ought,** and you shall be informed of what
follows …

## 4. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 28 April 1605 (Rommel p. 230)

The passage Rommel footnoted "It is to be regretted that these lines are not yet deciphered":

… and although my actions towards those who profess it, inside and outside my kingdom, **and even the [?favours] and
aid which the United Provinces and [the] city of Geneva continue to receive from me, sufficiently justify the truth of
my conduct and will in such cases, and the care I take to maintain and uphold those of the said religion, for the
notable interests I have in their preservation and in that of the public cause, nevertheless** … I reco[gnise] …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269-272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … and even on my part, **it must be considered that all
these accidents befell me almost at the same time as the conspiracy in England. It seems too that they were forged on
the same anvil.** The ambassador of Spain wanted to make me believe that I had done his master an injury in detaining
his secretary, who had been caught in the weaving of his treason, **so accustomed are the ministers of that nation to
se[d]uce and corrupt the subjects of the princes with whom [they] reside.** I am assured that the King of Great
Britain, my good brother, has as much reason to complain of it as I, **yet he covers and dissimulates it better.** But
I cannot believe that for this **his affairs prosper** the more; which it would be needful **that all who love him
should point out to him,** as **I have done by an ambassador whom** I sent to him expressly for this purpose … likewise
I have commended to him **the United Provinces in the extreme need they have** at present of being **assisted and
reinforced by their allies, to restore** next year **the reputation of their arms.** But, my cousin, as you have **a
notable interest in their preservation, together with the other princes of Germany,** I pray you to work so that
they are likewise **succoured from your side, with both money and troops,** following the hope that some **of the said
princes have given them,** and you will do a very good work, **very useful to the common cause, and which will be**
very agreeable [to me]; praying you to let me know what I am to hope for from it. For my part, I have resolved **to
continue to them next year the same assistance in money and troops that I have given them this year; it is very
considerable and not less than two million livres; but if with that they are not succoured from elsewhere, it will
be impossible for them to resist their enemies, for these are gathering money and forces from all sides to attack
them more vigorously and powerfully than they have ever [done].** Be then **the author,** my cousin, **of having them
receive the said succour, and let it be as promptly as can be done, so that they may take the field among the first
in the spring, for he who can forestall his opponent in that will be greatly advantaged by it.** For the rest, **let
me know what the Emperor intends to treat in the diet of the Empire which he has summoned, and what** you hope **from
the negotiations for peace with [the Turk] and from the agreement of the Hungarians with the Emperor, as also from
the continuation of the siege of the city of Brunswick, in which I regret to see [the Duke of Brunswick] engaged.** I
pray God … Written at Paris, the 22nd day of December 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307-314)

… to share it with the princes of Germany of that condition, **[7 groups unresolved] that things are still in great
confusion,** because **the Emperor does not change his conduct, and he is very badly assisted and [served] in his
affairs, principally on the side of** Hungary, where neither peace nor war is made as it should be … [p. 308 in clear:
the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … on all sides. **The Spaniards
will do what they can to have a King of the Romans elected from the House of Austria who is entirely at their
devotion, having** learned that the Duke of Feria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the election, to
propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war on them] …
**which are two proposals of great consequence, and in which all Christendom has no less interest [than] Germany
itself. That is why I consider it necessary that all the kings and princes who must be jealous of the growth and
prosperity of the Spanish power should advise in good time and take counsel together on what is fitting to do to
prevent the said Spaniards from making the affairs and resolutions of the said diet square with their wishes. For my
part, I shall always contribute to it very willingly what depends on me and is becoming for me to do, principally
when I recognise that the others can do the like, and above all the princes of [Holland: ?Germany], being no less
desirous than the kings my predecessors have been to favour and second the good intentions of the ancient allies and
friends of this crown on similar occasions, as on all others that may arise, for [the] preservation of German
liberty, without however expecting any other advantage from it than to advance the good of the common cause and the
public security; for which I shall be very glad not only that you inform those whom you judge worthy of it, but also
that you give me advice and counsel on what I can and must [do] now, both to assure them of my good will and to
advance its effects.**

I have been informed that the King of England is with good right very ill satisfied with the last refusal [of Owen
and Baldwin, in clear] … **[a run of letters and signs the 1840 typesetter could not render] I have at least … I have
not heard that the King of England breaks with the said Spaniards for this just offence, so much does he desire to
live in neutrality and peace; besides which I know that the said Spaniards omit nothing to do towards him and towards
his servants to compensate for and make him forget this discontent, having had several very specious offers and
overtures made to him anew expressly for this purpose. Nevertheless, as he is prudent and well counselled, I hope also
that he will take good care not to let himself be drawn by their inducements and persuasions, which have no other aim
than to abuse and circumvent those [to whom] they address them, so as to be able to [do] their business the better in
the Low Countries, where they intend to make a great effort this year, principally on the side of the Rhine and of
Friesland, having for this purpose doubled their army and awaiting only** the return to Brussels of the Marquis
Spinola … Which it will be very difficult for the said States of the United Provinces to resist, **for as they are
much weaker than the others, they will also be forced to put themselves on the defensive,** which will be **a
wretched condition for them, and one whose outcomes are too [?greatly] to be feared in a state composed of and governed
by several heads,** as is **the republic of the said provinces,** than in another. That is why **all who are joined in
interest with them must think well on it,** so as **to set their hand to it** before **their weakness and exhaustion**
become greater; **otherwise the remedies will come too late. You have [?seen] what I** wrote to you before on this
subject, praying you **to favour their affairs and suits with the princes of Germany,** of which I understand very
little effect has come. But the affairs and enterprises of the gentlemen of the said States, both on the side of the
East Indies and on the coast of Portugal, are prospering greatly, to the great prejudice of those of the said
Spaniards; **yet I doubt that this good fortune will last for them if that on land [?fails] them** and **their peoples
grow weary of bearing the burden of the war.** I shall write you nothing of the dispute between the Pope and the
Venetians, except that **I know the Spaniards will do what they can to take advantage of it,** as **they have always
done** on similar occasions. That is why, on my side, I shall do all I can to settle it as soon as possible … Written
at Fontainebleau, the 20th day of May 1606.

## 7. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 24 January 1609 (Rommel p. 391)

… **the ministers of the Pope and of the King of Spain** will make every kind of effort and diligence to reconcile
the Archduke Matthias with the Emperor …

12 Jan 1603, Rommel p. 99

With the Emperor absent from the diet, the King expects nothing important there, notably no election of a King of the Romans; he complains that the duc de Bouillon wrote nothing of the Administrator of Strasbourg's visit.

… Aussi, je n'en diffère plus la dépesche que pour attendre le **advis que vous m'avez promis est[re] sur icelle, et
spécialement sur le faict de la diette, et sçavoir si elle doibt avoir lieu, ce qui s'y doibt traicter, quelle
proposition j'y doibs faire pour le public et le particulier de mes alliés et a[utres], [en]suivant les discours que

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[ce qui se] peut sans préjudicier à nos alliés,** sur quoy je seray donques attendant en bonne dévotion votre bon
advis. Puisque l'Empereur ne comparoistra en la diette, j'estime qu'il ne s'y traitera chose d'importance **et
principallement de l'élection d'un roy des Romains,** dont je m'attends que vous m'advertirez, comme de ce qui se
passera en l'assemblée des Estats de Polongne. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **Mais le duc [de Bouillon] ne m'a
escript par sa lettre un seul mot de la venue par deçà de l'Administrateur [de Strasbourg], ny du faict de l'évesché de
Stras[bourg], tellement que je ne sçay co[m]me il aura pris ce voyage, ny de quelque sorte il embrasse la cause du dit
Administrateur;** je vous prie me faire sçavoir ce que vous en aurez apris.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211–214)

… ce que vous avez voulu m'en déférer. **[Le duc de … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] fait toute démonstration
de me porter de l'affection. Je vous assure aussy que je fais grande estime de leur amitié; mon frère le duc de Lorraine
en est l'entremetteur.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] Mais deux raisons m'en ont empesché: l'une est **la mauvaise intelligence
que j'ay recogneue entre les [princes?] protestants, laquelle j'ay eu crainte estre cause de rendre la dite visitation
peu fructueuse;** et l'autre **le mescontentement et dégoust qu'aucuns d'eulx m'ont donné de leurs actions,**
principallement depuis **qu'ils ont adjousté foy aux discours du duc de Bouillon et favorisé sa cause avec moins de
respect et considération à ma personne que je n'espérois d'eulx,** comme je vous ay quelques fois escrit …

Le feu de la révolte de la haulte Hongrie a esté bientost esteint, mais **la suite et conséquence en sera grande, et
pourra s'estendre avec le temps au voisinage.** Le roy d'Angleterre envoye vers moy le duc de Lenox, et en Espagne son
admiral, pour s'entretenir en bonne amitié de toutes parts: **conseil duquel je n'ay pas ouï qu'il se trouve [bien]**
avec le temps, mais il fault **qu'il en face les preuves devant qu'il le croye.**

Je suis adverty que **les Provinces Unies ont autant de courage de se deffendre et continuer la guerre que jamais,** et
veulx croire, cela estant, que **les moyens ne leur en manqueront point, estans pourveus comme ils sont; ils envoyent
des députés en Angleterre; ils espèrent qu'ils seront mieulx receus et favorisés que les Espagnols ne se sont promis,
car le d[it] roy ne veult perdre leur amitié,** ainsy que l'on m'a mandé. **Les dites provinces** ont esté naguères
visiter **d'un nouveau [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, des bonnes quallités duquel ils sont demeurés bien édifiés. Aussi
est-il vertueux. J'entends **qu'il leur a donné quelque espérance d'armer en leur faveur l'année prochaine.** En quoy je

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269–272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … et mesmes de ma part, **il fault considérer que tous ces
accidens me sont arrivés quasi au mesme temps de la conspiration d'Angleterre. Il semble aussy qu'ils ayent esté forgés
sur mesme enclume.** L'ambassadeur d'Espagne vouloit me faire accroire que j'avois faict injure à son maître d'avoir
retenu son secrétaire, ayant esté surpris sur la tissure de sa trahison, **tant les ministres de ceste nation ont
accoustumé se[d]uire et corrompre les subjects des princes auprès desquels [ils] résident.** Je suis asseuré que le roy
de la Grande Bretagne, mon bon frère, a autant d'argument de s'en plaindre que moy, **toutesfois il le couvre et
dissimule mieulx.** Mais je ne puis croire que pour ce faict **ses affaires en prospèrent** davantage; ce qui seroit
besoin **que tous ceulx qui l'ayment luy remonstrassent,** comme **j'ay fait par un ambassadeur que** j'ay envoyé vers
luy exprès pour cest effect … pareillement je luy ay recommandé **les Provinces Unies au besoin extrême qu'elles ont** à
présent d'estre **assistées et renforcées par leurs alliés pour relever** l'année prochaine **la réputation de leurs
armes.** Mais, mon cousin, comme vous avez **notable intérest à leur conservation avec les autres princes d'Allemagne,**
je vous prie vous emploier à ce qu'ils soient pareillement **secourus de votre costé tant d'argent que de troupes,**
suivant l'espérance qu'aucuns **des dits princes leur ont donnée,** et vous ferez un très bon œuvre, **très utile à la
cause commune et qui [me] sera** très agréable, vous priant me mander ce que j'en doibs espérer; pour mon regard j'ay
délibéré **leur continuer l'année prochaine la mesme assistance d'argent et de troupes que je leur ay départie
ceste-cy; elle est très notable et non moindre de deux millions de livres; mais si avec cela ils ne sont point secourus

# Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Kassel, 1602-1609 (Rommel 1840): English translation of the readings

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `letters/READINGS.md`, sections 1-7: the readings of the ciphered
passages, from the decodes of Rommel's 1840 printed transcription, with the clear text round them as Rommel prints it.
As in the reading, the ciphered passages are **bold**; `…` marks where the quoted text breaks off in clear; `[word]` a
word supplied where the print is faulty or a table entry is unlisted; `[?word]` a doubtful word; `[?]` and the
bracketed notes (unresolved groups, clear passages summarised) are kept. The "controls" read alongside (the
Landgrave's own letters and a letter deciphered in 1846) are not translated here.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… So I delay the despatch no longer except to await the **advice which you have promised me to be on it, and
especially on the matter of the diet, and to know whether it is to take place, what is to be treated there, what
proposal I am to make there for the public and the particular interest of my allies and o[thers], [fol]lowing the
discussions we [had] together; and also to know whether I should have my said ambassador go on into Saxony and
Denmark, with your good counsel on the [19, overbar: ?memorandum] and instruction I am to give him.** For I have taken
such assurance of and confidence in your friendship and prudence that I wish hereafter …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[what] can [be done] without prejudice to our allies,** on which I shall then be awaiting your good advice with
good devotion. Since the Emperor will not appear at the diet, I consider that nothing of importance will be treated
there, **and principally not the election of a King of the Romans,** of which I expect you will inform me, as of what
will happen in the assembly of the Estates of Poland. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **But the duke [de
Bouillon] has not written me a single word in his letter about the coming here of the Administrator [of Strasbourg],
nor about the matter of the bishopric of Stras[bourg], so that I do not know how he will have taken this journey, nor
in what way he embraces the cause of the said Administrator;** I pray you to let me know what you have learned of it.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211-214)

… what you have been pleased to refer to me about it. **[The duke of … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] makes
every show of bearing me affection. I assure you also that I hold their friendship in great esteem; my brother the
Duke of Lorraine is the go-between in it.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] But two reasons have prevented me: one is **the bad understanding I have
observed among the Protestant [?princes], which I feared would make the said visit unfruitful;** and the other **the
discontent and distaste which some of them have given me by their actions,** principally since **they gave credit to
the talk of the Duke of Bouillon and favoured his cause with less respect and consideration for my person than I
hoped from them,** as I have sometimes written to you …

The fire of the revolt of Upper Hungary was soon put out, but **its sequel and consequence will be great, and may
spread with time to the neighbourhood.** The King of England is sending the Duke of Lennox to me, and his admiral to
Spain, to maintain himself in good friendship on all sides: **a counsel which I have not heard that he finds [good]**
with time, but **he must put it to the proof before he believes it.**

I am informed that **the United Provinces have as much courage to defend themselves and continue the war as ever,**
and I will believe, this being so, that **the means will not fail them, provided as they are; they are sending
deputies to England; they hope they will be better received and favoured than the Spaniards have promised
themselves, for the s[aid] King does not wish to lose their friendship,** as I have been told. **The said provinces**
were lately visited **by a new [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, by whose good qualities they were much edified. And he
is indeed virtuous. I hear **that he gave them some hope of arming in their favour next year.** In which I pray you
**to encourage and assist him,** if you find the occasion.

**I am with the Spaniards as usual, discovering daily some new intrigue forged on their part to my disadvantage;
but** God will preserve me from it. I have brought to justice the Count of Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister,
who **are charged and accused of having dealt with them further than they ought,** and you shall be informed of what
follows …

## 4. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 28 April 1605 (Rommel p. 230)

The passage Rommel footnoted "It is to be regretted that these lines are not yet deciphered":

… and although my actions towards those who profess it, inside and outside my kingdom, **and even the [?favours] and
aid which the United Provinces and [the] city of Geneva continue to receive from me, sufficiently justify the truth of
my conduct and will in such cases, and the care I take to maintain and uphold those of the said religion, for the
notable interests I have in their preservation and in that of the public cause, nevertheless** … I reco[gnise] …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269-272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … and even on my part, **it must be considered that all
these accidents befell me almost at the same time as the conspiracy in England. It seems too that they were forged on
the same anvil.** The ambassador of Spain wanted to make me believe that I had done his master an injury in detaining
his secretary, who had been caught in the weaving of his treason, **so accustomed are the ministers of that nation to
se[d]uce and corrupt the subjects of the princes with whom [they] reside.** I am assured that the King of Great
Britain, my good brother, has as much reason to complain of it as I, **yet he covers and dissimulates it better.** But
I cannot believe that for this **his affairs prosper** the more; which it would be needful **that all who love him
should point out to him,** as **I have done by an ambassador whom** I sent to him expressly for this purpose … likewise
I have commended to him **the United Provinces in the extreme need they have** at present of being **assisted and
reinforced by their allies, to restore** next year **the reputation of their arms.** But, my cousin, as you have **a
notable interest in their preservation, together with the other princes of Germany,** I pray you to work so that
they are likewise **succoured from your side, with both money and troops,** following the hope that some **of the said
princes have given them,** and you will do a very good work, **very useful to the common cause, and which will be**
very agreeable [to me]; praying you to let me know what I am to hope for from it. For my part, I have resolved **to
continue to them next year the same assistance in money and troops that I have given them this year; it is very
considerable and not less than two million livres; but if with that they are not succoured from elsewhere, it will
be impossible for them to resist their enemies, for these are gathering money and forces from all sides to attack
them more vigorously and powerfully than they have ever [done].** Be then **the author,** my cousin, **of having them
receive the said succour, and let it be as promptly as can be done, so that they may take the field among the first
in the spring, for he who can forestall his opponent in that will be greatly advantaged by it.** For the rest, **let
me know what the Emperor intends to treat in the diet of the Empire which he has summoned, and what** you hope **from
the negotiations for peace with [the Turk] and from the agreement of the Hungarians with the Emperor, as also from
the continuation of the siege of the city of Brunswick, in which I regret to see [the Duke of Brunswick] engaged.** I
pray God … Written at Paris, the 22nd day of December 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307-314)

… to share it with the princes of Germany of that condition, **[7 groups unresolved] that things are still in great
confusion,** because **the Emperor does not change his conduct, and he is very badly assisted and [served] in his
affairs, principally on the side of** Hungary, where neither peace nor war is made as it should be … [p. 308 in clear:
the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … on all sides. **The Spaniards
will do what they can to have a King of the Romans elected from the House of Austria who is entirely at their
devotion, having** learned that the Duke of Feria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the election, to
propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war on them] …
**which are two proposals of great consequence, and in which all Christendom has no less interest [than] Germany
itself. That is why I consider it necessary that all the kings and princes who must be jealous of the growth and
prosperity of the Spanish power should advise in good time and take counsel together on what is fitting to do to
prevent the said Spaniards from making the affairs and resolutions of the said diet square with their wishes. For my
part, I shall always contribute to it very willingly what depends on me and is becoming for me to do, principally
when I recognise that the others can do the like, and above all the princes of [Holland: ?Germany], being no less
desirous than the kings my predecessors have been to favour and second the good intentions of the ancient allies and
friends of this crown on similar occasions, as on all others that may arise, for [the] preservation of German
liberty, without however expecting any other advantage from it than to advance the good of the common cause and the
public security; for which I shall be very glad not only that you inform those whom you judge worthy of it, but also
that you give me advice and counsel on what I can and must [do] now, both to assure them of my good will and to
advance its effects.**

I have been informed that the King of England is with good right very ill satisfied with the last refusal [of Owen
and Baldwin, in clear] … **[a run of letters and signs the 1840 typesetter could not render] I have at least … I have
not heard that the King of England breaks with the said Spaniards for this just offence, so much does he desire to
live in neutrality and peace; besides which I know that the said Spaniards omit nothing to do towards him and towards
his servants to compensate for and make him forget this discontent, having had several very specious offers and
overtures made to him anew expressly for this purpose. Nevertheless, as he is prudent and well counselled, I hope also
that he will take good care not to let himself be drawn by their inducements and persuasions, which have no other aim
than to abuse and circumvent those [to whom] they address them, so as to be able to [do] their business the better in
the Low Countries, where they intend to make a great effort this year, principally on the side of the Rhine and of
Friesland, having for this purpose doubled their army and awaiting only** the return to Brussels of the Marquis
Spinola … Which it will be very difficult for the said States of the United Provinces to resist, **for as they are
much weaker than the others, they will also be forced to put themselves on the defensive,** which will be **a
wretched condition for them, and one whose outcomes are too [?greatly] to be feared in a state composed of and governed
by several heads,** as is **the republic of the said provinces,** than in another. That is why **all who are joined in
interest with them must think well on it,** so as **to set their hand to it** before **their weakness and exhaustion**
become greater; **otherwise the remedies will come too late. You have [?seen] what I** wrote to you before on this
subject, praying you **to favour their affairs and suits with the princes of Germany,** of which I understand very
little effect has come. But the affairs and enterprises of the gentlemen of the said States, both on the side of the
East Indies and on the coast of Portugal, are prospering greatly, to the great prejudice of those of the said
Spaniards; **yet I doubt that this good fortune will last for them if that on land [?fails] them** and **their peoples
grow weary of bearing the burden of the war.** I shall write you nothing of the dispute between the Pope and the
Venetians, except that **I know the Spaniards will do what they can to take advantage of it,** as **they have always
done** on similar occasions. That is why, on my side, I shall do all I can to settle it as soon as possible … Written
at Fontainebleau, the 20th day of May 1606.

## 7. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 24 January 1609 (Rommel p. 391)

… **the ministers of the Pope and of the King of Spain** will make every kind of effort and diligence to reconcile
the Archduke Matthias with the Emperor …

27 Dec 1604, Rommel pp. 211-214

The King explains why he has not sent an embassy to the German princes (the Protestants' disunity and their support for Bouillon), reports the United Provinces' resolve to fight on, and his trouble with Spanish intrigues and the comte d'Auvergne.

# Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Kassel, 1602-1609 (Rommel 1840): English translation of the readings

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `letters/READINGS.md`, sections 1-7: the readings of the ciphered
passages, from the decodes of Rommel's 1840 printed transcription, with the clear text round them as Rommel prints it.
As in the reading, the ciphered passages are **bold**; `…` marks where the quoted text breaks off in clear; `[word]` a
word supplied where the print is faulty or a table entry is unlisted; `[?word]` a doubtful word; `[?]` and the
bracketed notes (unresolved groups, clear passages summarised) are kept. The "controls" read alongside (the
Landgrave's own letters and a letter deciphered in 1846) are not translated here.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… So I delay the despatch no longer except to await the **advice which you have promised me to be on it, and
especially on the matter of the diet, and to know whether it is to take place, what is to be treated there, what
proposal I am to make there for the public and the particular interest of my allies and o[thers], [fol]lowing the
discussions we [had] together; and also to know whether I should have my said ambassador go on into Saxony and
Denmark, with your good counsel on the [19, overbar: ?memorandum] and instruction I am to give him.** For I have taken
such assurance of and confidence in your friendship and prudence that I wish hereafter …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[what] can [be done] without prejudice to our allies,** on which I shall then be awaiting your good advice with
good devotion. Since the Emperor will not appear at the diet, I consider that nothing of importance will be treated
there, **and principally not the election of a King of the Romans,** of which I expect you will inform me, as of what
will happen in the assembly of the Estates of Poland. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **But the duke [de
Bouillon] has not written me a single word in his letter about the coming here of the Administrator [of Strasbourg],
nor about the matter of the bishopric of Stras[bourg], so that I do not know how he will have taken this journey, nor
in what way he embraces the cause of the said Administrator;** I pray you to let me know what you have learned of it.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211-214)

… what you have been pleased to refer to me about it. **[The duke of … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] makes
every show of bearing me affection. I assure you also that I hold their friendship in great esteem; my brother the
Duke of Lorraine is the go-between in it.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] But two reasons have prevented me: one is **the bad understanding I have
observed among the Protestant [?princes], which I feared would make the said visit unfruitful;** and the other **the
discontent and distaste which some of them have given me by their actions,** principally since **they gave credit to
the talk of the Duke of Bouillon and favoured his cause with less respect and consideration for my person than I
hoped from them,** as I have sometimes written to you …

The fire of the revolt of Upper Hungary was soon put out, but **its sequel and consequence will be great, and may
spread with time to the neighbourhood.** The King of England is sending the Duke of Lennox to me, and his admiral to
Spain, to maintain himself in good friendship on all sides: **a counsel which I have not heard that he finds [good]**
with time, but **he must put it to the proof before he believes it.**

I am informed that **the United Provinces have as much courage to defend themselves and continue the war as ever,**
and I will believe, this being so, that **the means will not fail them, provided as they are; they are sending
deputies to England; they hope they will be better received and favoured than the Spaniards have promised
themselves, for the s[aid] King does not wish to lose their friendship,** as I have been told. **The said provinces**
were lately visited **by a new [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, by whose good qualities they were much edified. And he
is indeed virtuous. I hear **that he gave them some hope of arming in their favour next year.** In which I pray you
**to encourage and assist him,** if you find the occasion.

**I am with the Spaniards as usual, discovering daily some new intrigue forged on their part to my disadvantage;
but** God will preserve me from it. I have brought to justice the Count of Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister,
who **are charged and accused of having dealt with them further than they ought,** and you shall be informed of what
follows …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269-272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … and even on my part, **it must be considered that all
these accidents befell me almost at the same time as the conspiracy in England. It seems too that they were forged on
the same anvil.** The ambassador of Spain wanted to make me believe that I had done his master an injury in detaining
his secretary, who had been caught in the weaving of his treason, **so accustomed are the ministers of that nation to
se[d]uce and corrupt the subjects of the princes with whom [they] reside.** I am assured that the King of Great
Britain, my good brother, has as much reason to complain of it as I, **yet he covers and dissimulates it better.** But
I cannot believe that for this **his affairs prosper** the more; which it would be needful **that all who love him
should point out to him,** as **I have done by an ambassador whom** I sent to him expressly for this purpose … likewise
I have commended to him **the United Provinces in the extreme need they have** at present of being **assisted and
reinforced by their allies, to restore** next year **the reputation of their arms.** But, my cousin, as you have **a
notable interest in their preservation, together with the other princes of Germany,** I pray you to work so that
they are likewise **succoured from your side, with both money and troops,** following the hope that some **of the said
princes have given them,** and you will do a very good work, **very useful to the common cause, and which will be**
very agreeable [to me]; praying you to let me know what I am to hope for from it. For my part, I have resolved **to
continue to them next year the same assistance in money and troops that I have given them this year; it is very
considerable and not less than two million livres; but if with that they are not succoured from elsewhere, it will
be impossible for them to resist their enemies, for these are gathering money and forces from all sides to attack
them more vigorously and powerfully than they have ever [done].** Be then **the author,** my cousin, **of having them
receive the said succour, and let it be as promptly as can be done, so that they may take the field among the first
in the spring, for he who can forestall his opponent in that will be greatly advantaged by it.** For the rest, **let
me know what the Emperor intends to treat in the diet of the Empire which he has summoned, and what** you hope **from
the negotiations for peace with [the Turk] and from the agreement of the Hungarians with the Emperor, as also from
the continuation of the siege of the city of Brunswick, in which I regret to see [the Duke of Brunswick] engaged.** I
pray God … Written at Paris, the 22nd day of December 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307-314)

… to share it with the princes of Germany of that condition, **[7 groups unresolved] that things are still in great
confusion,** because **the Emperor does not change his conduct, and he is very badly assisted and [served] in his
affairs, principally on the side of** Hungary, where neither peace nor war is made as it should be … [p. 308 in clear:
the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … on all sides. **The Spaniards
will do what they can to have a King of the Romans elected from the House of Austria who is entirely at their
devotion, having** learned that the Duke of Feria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the election, to
propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war on them] …
**which are two proposals of great consequence, and in which all Christendom has no less interest [than] Germany
itself. That is why I consider it necessary that all the kings and princes who must be jealous of the growth and
prosperity of the Spanish power should advise in good time and take counsel together on what is fitting to do to
prevent the said Spaniards from making the affairs and resolutions of the said diet square with their wishes. For my
part, I shall always contribute to it very willingly what depends on me and is becoming for me to do, principally
when I recognise that the others can do the like, and above all the princes of [Holland: ?Germany], being no less
desirous than the kings my predecessors have been to favour and second the good intentions of the ancient allies and
friends of this crown on similar occasions, as on all others that may arise, for [the] preservation of German

wretched condition for them, and one whose outcomes are too [?greatly] to be feared in a state composed of and governed

interest with them must think well on it,** so as **to set their hand to it** before **their weakness and exhaustion**

grow weary of bearing the burden of the war.** I shall write you nothing of the dispute between the Pope and the
Venetians, except that **I know the Spaniards will do what they can to take advantage of it,** as **they have always
done** on similar occasions. That is why, on my side, I shall do all I can to settle it as soon as possible … Written
at Fontainebleau, the 20th day of May 1606.

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[ce qui se] peut sans préjudicier à nos alliés,** sur quoy je seray donques attendant en bonne dévotion votre bon

passera en l'assemblée des Estats de Polongne. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **Mais le duc [de Bouillon] ne m'a

Administrateur;** je vous prie me faire sçavoir ce que vous en aurez apris.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211–214)

… ce que vous avez voulu m'en déférer. **[Le duc de … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] fait toute démonstration

en est l'entremetteur.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] Mais deux raisons m'en ont empesché: l'une est **la mauvaise intelligence
que j'ay recogneue entre les [princes?] protestants, laquelle j'ay eu crainte estre cause de rendre la dite visitation
peu fructueuse;** et l'autre **le mescontentement et dégoust qu'aucuns d'eulx m'ont donné de leurs actions,**
principallement depuis **qu'ils ont adjousté foy aux discours du duc de Bouillon et favorisé sa cause avec moins de
respect et considération à ma personne que je n'espérois d'eulx,** comme je vous ay quelques fois escrit …

Le feu de la révolte de la haulte Hongrie a esté bientost esteint, mais **la suite et conséquence en sera grande, et
pourra s'estendre avec le temps au voisinage.** Le roy d'Angleterre envoye vers moy le duc de Lenox, et en Espagne son
admiral, pour s'entretenir en bonne amitié de toutes parts: **conseil duquel je n'ay pas ouï qu'il se trouve [bien]**
avec le temps, mais il fault **qu'il en face les preuves devant qu'il le croye.**

Je suis adverty que **les Provinces Unies ont autant de courage de se deffendre et continuer la guerre que jamais,** et
veulx croire, cela estant, que **les moyens ne leur en manqueront point, estans pourveus comme ils sont; ils envoyent
des députés en Angleterre; ils espèrent qu'ils seront mieulx receus et favorisés que les Espagnols ne se sont promis,
car le d[it] roy ne veult perdre leur amitié,** ainsy que l'on m'a mandé. **Les dites provinces** ont esté naguères
visiter **d'un nouveau [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, des bonnes quallités duquel ils sont demeurés bien édifiés. Aussi
est-il vertueux. J'entends **qu'il leur a donné quelque espérance d'armer en leur faveur l'année prochaine.** En quoy je
vous prie **de le conforter et assister,** sy vous en rencontrez l'occasion.

**Je suis avec les Espagnols comme de coustume, découvrant journellement quelque nouvelle menée forgée de leur part à mon

chargés et accusés d'avoir traité avec eulx plus avant qu'ils ne devoyent,** et serez adverty de ce qui en succèdera …

# Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Kassel, 1602-1609 (Rommel 1840): English translation of the readings

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `letters/READINGS.md`, sections 1-7: the readings of the ciphered
passages, from the decodes of Rommel's 1840 printed transcription, with the clear text round them as Rommel prints it.
As in the reading, the ciphered passages are **bold**; `…` marks where the quoted text breaks off in clear; `[word]` a
word supplied where the print is faulty or a table entry is unlisted; `[?word]` a doubtful word; `[?]` and the
bracketed notes (unresolved groups, clear passages summarised) are kept. The "controls" read alongside (the
Landgrave's own letters and a letter deciphered in 1846) are not translated here.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… So I delay the despatch no longer except to await the **advice which you have promised me to be on it, and
especially on the matter of the diet, and to know whether it is to take place, what is to be treated there, what
proposal I am to make there for the public and the particular interest of my allies and o[thers], [fol]lowing the
discussions we [had] together; and also to know whether I should have my said ambassador go on into Saxony and
Denmark, with your good counsel on the [19, overbar: ?memorandum] and instruction I am to give him.** For I have taken
such assurance of and confidence in your friendship and prudence that I wish hereafter …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[what] can [be done] without prejudice to our allies,** on which I shall then be awaiting your good advice with
good devotion. Since the Emperor will not appear at the diet, I consider that nothing of importance will be treated
there, **and principally not the election of a King of the Romans,** of which I expect you will inform me, as of what
will happen in the assembly of the Estates of Poland. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **But the duke [de
Bouillon] has not written me a single word in his letter about the coming here of the Administrator [of Strasbourg],
nor about the matter of the bishopric of Stras[bourg], so that I do not know how he will have taken this journey, nor
in what way he embraces the cause of the said Administrator;** I pray you to let me know what you have learned of it.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211-214)

… what you have been pleased to refer to me about it. **[The duke of … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] makes
every show of bearing me affection. I assure you also that I hold their friendship in great esteem; my brother the
Duke of Lorraine is the go-between in it.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] But two reasons have prevented me: one is **the bad understanding I have
observed among the Protestant [?princes], which I feared would make the said visit unfruitful;** and the other **the
discontent and distaste which some of them have given me by their actions,** principally since **they gave credit to
the talk of the Duke of Bouillon and favoured his cause with less respect and consideration for my person than I
hoped from them,** as I have sometimes written to you …

The fire of the revolt of Upper Hungary was soon put out, but **its sequel and consequence will be great, and may
spread with time to the neighbourhood.** The King of England is sending the Duke of Lennox to me, and his admiral to
Spain, to maintain himself in good friendship on all sides: **a counsel which I have not heard that he finds [good]**
with time, but **he must put it to the proof before he believes it.**

I am informed that **the United Provinces have as much courage to defend themselves and continue the war as ever,**
and I will believe, this being so, that **the means will not fail them, provided as they are; they are sending
deputies to England; they hope they will be better received and favoured than the Spaniards have promised
themselves, for the s[aid] King does not wish to lose their friendship,** as I have been told. **The said provinces**
were lately visited **by a new [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, by whose good qualities they were much edified. And he
is indeed virtuous. I hear **that he gave them some hope of arming in their favour next year.** In which I pray you
**to encourage and assist him,** if you find the occasion.

**I am with the Spaniards as usual, discovering daily some new intrigue forged on their part to my disadvantage;
but** God will preserve me from it. I have brought to justice the Count of Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister,
who **are charged and accused of having dealt with them further than they ought,** and you shall be informed of what
follows …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269-272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … and even on my part, **it must be considered that all
these accidents befell me almost at the same time as the conspiracy in England. It seems too that they were forged on
the same anvil.** The ambassador of Spain wanted to make me believe that I had done his master an injury in detaining
his secretary, who had been caught in the weaving of his treason, **so accustomed are the ministers of that nation to
se[d]uce and corrupt the subjects of the princes with whom [they] reside.** I am assured that the King of Great
Britain, my good brother, has as much reason to complain of it as I, **yet he covers and dissimulates it better.** But
I cannot believe that for this **his affairs prosper** the more; which it would be needful **that all who love him
should point out to him,** as **I have done by an ambassador whom** I sent to him expressly for this purpose … likewise
I have commended to him **the United Provinces in the extreme need they have** at present of being **assisted and
reinforced by their allies, to restore** next year **the reputation of their arms.** But, my cousin, as you have **a
notable interest in their preservation, together with the other princes of Germany,** I pray you to work so that
they are likewise **succoured from your side, with both money and troops,** following the hope that some **of the said
princes have given them,** and you will do a very good work, **very useful to the common cause, and which will be**
very agreeable [to me]; praying you to let me know what I am to hope for from it. For my part, I have resolved **to
continue to them next year the same assistance in money and troops that I have given them this year; it is very

# Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Kassel, 1602-1609 (Rommel 1840): English translation of the readings

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `letters/READINGS.md`, sections 1-7: the readings of the ciphered
passages, from the decodes of Rommel's 1840 printed transcription, with the clear text round them as Rommel prints it.
As in the reading, the ciphered passages are **bold**; `…` marks where the quoted text breaks off in clear; `[word]` a
word supplied where the print is faulty or a table entry is unlisted; `[?word]` a doubtful word; `[?]` and the
bracketed notes (unresolved groups, clear passages summarised) are kept. The "controls" read alongside (the
Landgrave's own letters and a letter deciphered in 1846) are not translated here.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… So I delay the despatch no longer except to await the **advice which you have promised me to be on it, and
especially on the matter of the diet, and to know whether it is to take place, what is to be treated there, what
proposal I am to make there for the public and the particular interest of my allies and o[thers], [fol]lowing the
discussions we [had] together; and also to know whether I should have my said ambassador go on into Saxony and
Denmark, with your good counsel on the [19, overbar: ?memorandum] and instruction I am to give him.** For I have taken
such assurance of and confidence in your friendship and prudence that I wish hereafter …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[what] can [be done] without prejudice to our allies,** on which I shall then be awaiting your good advice with
good devotion. Since the Emperor will not appear at the diet, I consider that nothing of importance will be treated
there, **and principally not the election of a King of the Romans,** of which I expect you will inform me, as of what
will happen in the assembly of the Estates of Poland. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **But the duke [de
Bouillon] has not written me a single word in his letter about the coming here of the Administrator [of Strasbourg],
nor about the matter of the bishopric of Stras[bourg], so that I do not know how he will have taken this journey, nor
in what way he embraces the cause of the said Administrator;** I pray you to let me know what you have learned of it.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211-214)

… what you have been pleased to refer to me about it. **[The duke of … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] makes
every show of bearing me affection. I assure you also that I hold their friendship in great esteem; my brother the
Duke of Lorraine is the go-between in it.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] But two reasons have prevented me: one is **the bad understanding I have
observed among the Protestant [?princes], which I feared would make the said visit unfruitful;** and the other **the
discontent and distaste which some of them have given me by their actions,** principally since **they gave credit to
the talk of the Duke of Bouillon and favoured his cause with less respect and consideration for my person than I
hoped from them,** as I have sometimes written to you …

The fire of the revolt of Upper Hungary was soon put out, but **its sequel and consequence will be great, and may
spread with time to the neighbourhood.** The King of England is sending the Duke of Lennox to me, and his admiral to
Spain, to maintain himself in good friendship on all sides: **a counsel which I have not heard that he finds [good]**
with time, but **he must put it to the proof before he believes it.**

I am informed that **the United Provinces have as much courage to defend themselves and continue the war as ever,**
and I will believe, this being so, that **the means will not fail them, provided as they are; they are sending
deputies to England; they hope they will be better received and favoured than the Spaniards have promised
themselves, for the s[aid] King does not wish to lose their friendship,** as I have been told. **The said provinces**
were lately visited **by a new [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, by whose good qualities they were much edified. And he
is indeed virtuous. I hear **that he gave them some hope of arming in their favour next year.** In which I pray you
**to encourage and assist him,** if you find the occasion.

**I am with the Spaniards as usual, discovering daily some new intrigue forged on their part to my disadvantage;
but** God will preserve me from it. I have brought to justice the Count of Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister,
who **are charged and accused of having dealt with them further than they ought,** and you shall be informed of what
follows …

## 4. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 28 April 1605 (Rommel p. 230)

The passage Rommel footnoted "It is to be regretted that these lines are not yet deciphered":

… and although my actions towards those who profess it, inside and outside my kingdom, **and even the [?favours] and

notable interests I have in their preservation and in that of the public cause, nevertheless** … I reco[gnise] …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269-272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … and even on my part, **it must be considered that all
these accidents befell me almost at the same time as the conspiracy in England. It seems too that they were forged on
the same anvil.** The ambassador of Spain wanted to make me believe that I had done his master an injury in detaining
his secretary, who had been caught in the weaving of his treason, **so accustomed are the ministers of that nation to
se[d]uce and corrupt the subjects of the princes with whom [they] reside.** I am assured that the King of Great
Britain, my good brother, has as much reason to complain of it as I, **yet he covers and dissimulates it better.** But
I cannot believe that for this **his affairs prosper** the more; which it would be needful **that all who love him
should point out to him,** as **I have done by an ambassador whom** I sent to him expressly for this purpose … likewise
I have commended to him **the United Provinces in the extreme need they have** at present of being **assisted and
reinforced by their allies, to restore** next year **the reputation of their arms.** But, my cousin, as you have **a
notable interest in their preservation, together with the other princes of Germany,** I pray you to work so that
they are likewise **succoured from your side, with both money and troops,** following the hope that some **of the said
princes have given them,** and you will do a very good work, **very useful to the common cause, and which will be**
very agreeable [to me]; praying you to let me know what I am to hope for from it. For my part, I have resolved **to
continue to them next year the same assistance in money and troops that I have given them this year; it is very
considerable and not less than two million livres; but if with that they are not succoured from elsewhere, it will
be impossible for them to resist their enemies, for these are gathering money and forces from all sides to attack
them more vigorously and powerfully than they have ever [done].** Be then **the author,** my cousin, **of having them
receive the said succour, and let it be as promptly as can be done, so that they may take the field among the first
in the spring, for he who can forestall his opponent in that will be greatly advantaged by it.** For the rest, **let
me know what the Emperor intends to treat in the diet of the Empire which he has summoned, and what** you hope **from
the negotiations for peace with [the Turk] and from the agreement of the Hungarians with the Emperor, as also from
the continuation of the siege of the city of Brunswick, in which I regret to see [the Duke of Brunswick] engaged.** I
pray God … Written at Paris, the 22nd day of December 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307-314)

… to share it with the princes of Germany of that condition, **[7 groups unresolved] that things are still in great
confusion,** because **the Emperor does not change his conduct, and he is very badly assisted and [served] in his
affairs, principally on the side of** Hungary, where neither peace nor war is made as it should be … [p. 308 in clear:
the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … on all sides. **The Spaniards
will do what they can to have a King of the Romans elected from the House of Austria who is entirely at their
devotion, having** learned that the Duke of Feria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the election, to
propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war on them] …
**which are two proposals of great consequence, and in which all Christendom has no less interest [than] Germany
itself. That is why I consider it necessary that all the kings and princes who must be jealous of the growth and
prosperity of the Spanish power should advise in good time and take counsel together on what is fitting to do to
prevent the said Spaniards from making the affairs and resolutions of the said diet square with their wishes. For my
part, I shall always contribute to it very willingly what depends on me and is becoming for me to do, principally
when I recognise that the others can do the like, and above all the princes of [Holland: ?Germany], being no less
desirous than the kings my predecessors have been to favour and second the good intentions of the ancient allies and
friends of this crown on similar occasions, as on all others that may arise, for [the] preservation of German
liberty, without however expecting any other advantage from it than to advance the good of the common cause and the
public security; for which I shall be very glad not only that you inform those whom you judge worthy of it, but also
that you give me advice and counsel on what I can and must [do] now, both to assure them of my good will and to
advance its effects.**

I have been informed that the King of England is with good right very ill satisfied with the last refusal [of Owen
and Baldwin, in clear] … **[a run of letters and signs the 1840 typesetter could not render] I have at least … I have
not heard that the King of England breaks with the said Spaniards for this just offence, so much does he desire to
live in neutrality and peace; besides which I know that the said Spaniards omit nothing to do towards him and towards
his servants to compensate for and make him forget this discontent, having had several very specious offers and
overtures made to him anew expressly for this purpose. Nevertheless, as he is prudent and well counselled, I hope also
that he will take good care not to let himself be drawn by their inducements and persuasions, which have no other aim
than to abuse and circumvent those [to whom] they address them, so as to be able to [do] their business the better in
the Low Countries, where they intend to make a great effort this year, principally on the side of the Rhine and of
Friesland, having for this purpose doubled their army and awaiting only** the return to Brussels of the Marquis
Spinola … Which it will be very difficult for the said States of the United Provinces to resist, **for as they are
much weaker than the others, they will also be forced to put themselves on the defensive,** which will be **a
wretched condition for them, and one whose outcomes are too [?greatly] to be feared in a state composed of and governed
by several heads,** as is **the republic of the said provinces,** than in another. That is why **all who are joined in
interest with them must think well on it,** so as **to set their hand to it** before **their weakness and exhaustion**
become greater; **otherwise the remedies will come too late. You have [?seen] what I** wrote to you before on this
subject, praying you **to favour their affairs and suits with the princes of Germany,** of which I understand very
little effect has come. But the affairs and enterprises of the gentlemen of the said States, both on the side of the
East Indies and on the coast of Portugal, are prospering greatly, to the great prejudice of those of the said
Spaniards; **yet I doubt that this good fortune will last for them if that on land [?fails] them** and **their peoples
grow weary of bearing the burden of the war.** I shall write you nothing of the dispute between the Pope and the
Venetians, except that **I know the Spaniards will do what they can to take advantage of it,** as **they have always
done** on similar occasions. That is why, on my side, I shall do all I can to settle it as soon as possible … Written
at Fontainebleau, the 20th day of May 1606.

## 7. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 24 January 1609 (Rommel p. 391)

… **the ministers of the Pope and of the King of Spain** will make every kind of effort and diligence to reconcile
the Archduke Matthias with the Emperor …

Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606, Rommel pp. 307-314

The King's programme against the Spanish-Habsburg design on the Imperial diet: the kings and princes jealous of Spain should concert in defence of 'la liberté germanique'; the King of England's neutrality, Spinola's coming campaign and the weakness of the States.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[ce qui se] peut sans préjudicier à nos alliés,** sur quoy je seray donques attendant en bonne dévotion votre bon
advis. Puisque l'Empereur ne comparoistra en la diette, j'estime qu'il ne s'y traitera chose d'importance **et
principallement de l'élection d'un roy des Romains,** dont je m'attends que vous m'advertirez, comme de ce qui se
passera en l'assemblée des Estats de Polongne. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **Mais le duc [de Bouillon] ne m'a
escript par sa lettre un seul mot de la venue par deçà de l'Administrateur [de Strasbourg], ny du faict de l'évesché de
Stras[bourg], tellement que je ne sçay co[m]me il aura pris ce voyage, ny de quelque sorte il embrasse la cause du dit
Administrateur;** je vous prie me faire sçavoir ce que vous en aurez apris.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211–214)

… ce que vous avez voulu m'en déférer. **[Le duc de … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] fait toute démonstration
de me porter de l'affection. Je vous assure aussy que je fais grande estime de leur amitié; mon frère le duc de Lorraine
en est l'entremetteur.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] Mais deux raisons m'en ont empesché: l'une est **la mauvaise intelligence
que j'ay recogneue entre les [princes?] protestants, laquelle j'ay eu crainte estre cause de rendre la dite visitation
peu fructueuse;** et l'autre **le mescontentement et dégoust qu'aucuns d'eulx m'ont donné de leurs actions,**
principallement depuis **qu'ils ont adjousté foy aux discours du duc de Bouillon et favorisé sa cause avec moins de
respect et considération à ma personne que je n'espérois d'eulx,** comme je vous ay quelques fois escrit …

Le feu de la révolte de la haulte Hongrie a esté bientost esteint, mais **la suite et conséquence en sera grande, et
pourra s'estendre avec le temps au voisinage.** Le roy d'Angleterre envoye vers moy le duc de Lenox, et en Espagne son
admiral, pour s'entretenir en bonne amitié de toutes parts: **conseil duquel je n'ay pas ouï qu'il se trouve [bien]**
avec le temps, mais il fault **qu'il en face les preuves devant qu'il le croye.**

Je suis adverty que **les Provinces Unies ont autant de courage de se deffendre et continuer la guerre que jamais,** et
veulx croire, cela estant, que **les moyens ne leur en manqueront point, estans pourveus comme ils sont; ils envoyent
des députés en Angleterre; ils espèrent qu'ils seront mieulx receus et favorisés que les Espagnols ne se sont promis,
car le d[it] roy ne veult perdre leur amitié,** ainsy que l'on m'a mandé. **Les dites provinces** ont esté naguères
visiter **d'un nouveau [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, des bonnes quallités duquel ils sont demeurés bien édifiés. Aussi
est-il vertueux. J'entends **qu'il leur a donné quelque espérance d'armer en leur faveur l'année prochaine.** En quoy je
vous prie **de le conforter et assister,** sy vous en rencontrez l'occasion.

**Je suis avec les Espagnols comme de coustume, découvrant journellement quelque nouvelle menée forgée de leur part à mon
désavantage; mais** Dieu m'en préservera. J'ay mis en justice le comte d'Auvergne, son beau-père et sa sœur, qui **sont
chargés et accusés d'avoir traité avec eulx plus avant qu'ils ne devoyent,** et serez adverty de ce qui en succèdera …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269–272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … et mesmes de ma part, **il fault considérer que tous ces
accidens me sont arrivés quasi au mesme temps de la conspiration d'Angleterre. Il semble aussy qu'ils ayent esté forgés
sur mesme enclume.** L'ambassadeur d'Espagne vouloit me faire accroire que j'avois faict injure à son maître d'avoir
retenu son secrétaire, ayant esté surpris sur la tissure de sa trahison, **tant les ministres de ceste nation ont
accoustumé se[d]uire et corrompre les subjects des princes auprès desquels [ils] résident.** Je suis asseuré que le roy
de la Grande Bretagne, mon bon frère, a autant d'argument de s'en plaindre que moy, **toutesfois il le couvre et
dissimule mieulx.** Mais je ne puis croire que pour ce faict **ses affaires en prospèrent** davantage; ce qui seroit
besoin **que tous ceulx qui l'ayment luy remonstrassent,** comme **j'ay fait par un ambassadeur que** j'ay envoyé vers
luy exprès pour cest effect … pareillement je luy ay recommandé **les Provinces Unies au besoin extrême qu'elles ont** à
présent d'estre **assistées et renforcées par leurs alliés pour relever** l'année prochaine **la réputation de leurs
armes.** Mais, mon cousin, comme vous avez **notable intérest à leur conservation avec les autres princes d'Allemagne,**
je vous prie vous emploier à ce qu'ils soient pareillement **secourus de votre costé tant d'argent que de troupes,**
suivant l'espérance qu'aucuns **des dits princes leur ont donnée,** et vous ferez un très bon œuvre, **très utile à la
cause commune et qui [me] sera** très agréable, vous priant me mander ce que j'en doibs espérer; pour mon regard j'ay
délibéré **leur continuer l'année prochaine la mesme assistance d'argent et de troupes que je leur ay départie
ceste-cy; elle est très notable et non moindre de deux millions de livres; mais si avec cela ils ne sont point secourus
d'ailleurs, il leur sera impossible de résister à leurs ennemis, car ils font amas d'argent et de forces de toutes parts
pour les assaillir plus vivement et puissamment qu'ils n'ont point [faict].** Soyez donc **autheur,** mon cousin, **de
leur faire recevoir ledict secours, et que ce soit le plus promptement que faire se pourra, afin qu'ils puissent se
mettre aux champs au printemps des premiers, car celuy qui pourra prévenir sa partie en cela en sera grandement
avantagé.** Au demeurant, **mandez-moy ce que l'Empereur prétend traiter en la diète de l'Empire qu'il a intimée, et ce
que** vous espérez **des négotiations de la paix avec [le Turc] et de l'accord des Hongrois avec l'Empereur, comme de
la poursuite du siège de la ville de Brunswick, où j'ay regret de voir [le duc de Brunswick] engagé.** Je prie Dieu …
Escript à Paris le xxiie jour de décembre 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307–314)

… d'en faire part aux princes de la Germanie de ceste condition là, **[7 groups unresolved] que les choses sont tousjours
en grande confusion,** parce que **l'Empereur ne change point de conduite, et qu'il est très mal assisté et [servi] en
ses affaires, principa[l]lement du costé de** Hongrie, où l'on ne fait la paix ny la guerre comme il convient …
[p. 308 in clear: the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … de toutes
parts. **Les Espagnols feront ce qu'ils pourront pour faire eslire un roy des Romains de la maison d'Austriche qui soit
entièrement à leur dévotion, ayant** sceu que le duc de Féria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the
election, to propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war
on them] … **qui sont deux propositions de grande conséquence et auxquelles toute la chrestienté n'a moins d'intérest
[que] l'Allemagne mesme. C'est pourquoy j'estime estre nécessaire que tous les roys et princes qui doivent avoir
jalousie de l'agrandissement et prospérité de la puissance espagnole doibvent d'heure aviser et prendre conseil
ensemble de ce qu'il convient faire pour empescher que les dicts Espagnols ne fassent cadrer les affaires et résolutions
de la dite diète à leur souhait. Pour mon regard, j'y contribueray tousjours très volontiers ce qui dépendra de moy et me
sera bienséant de faire, principalement quand je recognoistray que les autres pourront faire le semblable, et par dessus
tous les princes de la [Hollande: Germanie?], n'estant moins désireux que ont esté les roys mes prédécesseurs de
favoriser et seconder les bonnes intentions des anciens alliés et amis de ceste couronne en semblables occasions comme
en toutes autres qui se présenteront pour [la] conservation de la liberté germanique, sans toutesfois en attendre autre
advantage que d'advancer le bien de la cause commune et la seureté publique; dont j'auray bien agréable non seulement
que vous advertissiez ceulx que vous en jugerez dignes, mais aussy que vous me donniez advis et conseil de ce que je
puis et doibs [faire] maintenant, tant pour les asseurer de ma bonne volonté que pour en advancer les effects.**

J'ay esté adverty que le roy d'Angleterre est à bon droit très mal satisfait du dernier refus [of Owen and Baldwin, in
clear] … **[a run of letters and signs the 1840 typesetter could not render] j'ay au moins … je n'ay pas ouï que le Roi
d'Angleterre pour ceste juste offence rompe avec les dits Espagnols, tant il désire vivre en neutralité et en paix;
joint que je sçay que les dits Espagnols n'obmettent rien à faire en son endroit et envers ses serviteurs pour
compenser et faire oublier ce mescontentement, luy ayant de nouveau fait faire plusieurs offres et ouvertures très
spécieuses exprès pour cest effet. Toutesfois, comme il est prudent et bien conseillé, j'espère aussi qu'il se gardera
bien de se laisser aller à leurs inductions et persuasions, qui n'ont autre but que d'abuser et circonvenir ceulx
[auxquels] ils les adressent, pour pouvoir mieux [faire] leur affaire aux Pays-Bas, où ils prétendent faire un grand
effort ceste année, principa[l]lement du costé du Rhin et de Frise, ayans pour cest effect redoublé leur armée et
n'attendans que** le retour à Brusselles du marquis Spinola … A quoy il sera très difficile que les dits Estats des
Provinces Unies résistent, **car comme ils sont foibles beaucoup plus que les autres, ils seront contraints aussy de se
mettre sur la défensive,** qui sera une **misérable condition pour eulx, et les événements de laquelle sont trop
[fort?] à craindre en un estat composé et gouverné par plusieurs testes,** comme est la **république des dites
provinces,** qu'en un autre. C'est pourquoy **tous ceulx qui sont conjoints d'intérest avec eulx doivent bien y penser,**
affin de **y porter la main** devant que **leur débilité et inanition** soyent plus grandes, **autrement les remèdes y
seront trop tardifs. Vous m'avez [veu?] ce que je** vous ay ci devant escrit sur ce subject, en vous priant de
**favoriser leurs affaires et poursuites envers les princes d'Allemagne,** de quoy j'entends estre réussy fort peu
d'effect. Mais les affaires et entreprises par messieurs des dits Etats, tant du costé des Indes Orientalles, qu'en la
coste du Portugal, vont prospérant grandement au grand préjudice de celles des dits Espagnols; **touttefois je doubte
que ceste bonne fortune leur dure si celle de la terre leur [manque?]** et que **leurs peuples se lassent de soustenir
le faix de la guerre.** Je ne vous escriray rien du différent, qui est entre le Pape et les Vénitiens, sinon que **je

semblables occasions. C'est pourquoy, de mon costé, je feray tout ce qu'il me sera possible pour le composer au plus
tost … Escrit à Fontainebleau, le 20e jour de may 1606.

# Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Kassel, 1602-1609 (Rommel 1840): English translation of the readings

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `letters/READINGS.md`, sections 1-7: the readings of the ciphered
passages, from the decodes of Rommel's 1840 printed transcription, with the clear text round them as Rommel prints it.
As in the reading, the ciphered passages are **bold**; `…` marks where the quoted text breaks off in clear; `[word]` a
word supplied where the print is faulty or a table entry is unlisted; `[?word]` a doubtful word; `[?]` and the
bracketed notes (unresolved groups, clear passages summarised) are kept. The "controls" read alongside (the
Landgrave's own letters and a letter deciphered in 1846) are not translated here.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… So I delay the despatch no longer except to await the **advice which you have promised me to be on it, and
especially on the matter of the diet, and to know whether it is to take place, what is to be treated there, what
proposal I am to make there for the public and the particular interest of my allies and o[thers], [fol]lowing the
discussions we [had] together; and also to know whether I should have my said ambassador go on into Saxony and
Denmark, with your good counsel on the [19, overbar: ?memorandum] and instruction I am to give him.** For I have taken
such assurance of and confidence in your friendship and prudence that I wish hereafter …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[what] can [be done] without prejudice to our allies,** on which I shall then be awaiting your good advice with
good devotion. Since the Emperor will not appear at the diet, I consider that nothing of importance will be treated
there, **and principally not the election of a King of the Romans,** of which I expect you will inform me, as of what
will happen in the assembly of the Estates of Poland. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **But the duke [de
Bouillon] has not written me a single word in his letter about the coming here of the Administrator [of Strasbourg],
nor about the matter of the bishopric of Stras[bourg], so that I do not know how he will have taken this journey, nor
in what way he embraces the cause of the said Administrator;** I pray you to let me know what you have learned of it.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211-214)

… what you have been pleased to refer to me about it. **[The duke of … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] makes
every show of bearing me affection. I assure you also that I hold their friendship in great esteem; my brother the
Duke of Lorraine is the go-between in it.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] But two reasons have prevented me: one is **the bad understanding I have
observed among the Protestant [?princes], which I feared would make the said visit unfruitful;** and the other **the
discontent and distaste which some of them have given me by their actions,** principally since **they gave credit to
the talk of the Duke of Bouillon and favoured his cause with less respect and consideration for my person than I
hoped from them,** as I have sometimes written to you …

The fire of the revolt of Upper Hungary was soon put out, but **its sequel and consequence will be great, and may
spread with time to the neighbourhood.** The King of England is sending the Duke of Lennox to me, and his admiral to
Spain, to maintain himself in good friendship on all sides: **a counsel which I have not heard that he finds [good]**
with time, but **he must put it to the proof before he believes it.**

I am informed that **the United Provinces have as much courage to defend themselves and continue the war as ever,**
and I will believe, this being so, that **the means will not fail them, provided as they are; they are sending
deputies to England; they hope they will be better received and favoured than the Spaniards have promised
themselves, for the s[aid] King does not wish to lose their friendship,** as I have been told. **The said provinces**
were lately visited **by a new [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, by whose good qualities they were much edified. And he
is indeed virtuous. I hear **that he gave them some hope of arming in their favour next year.** In which I pray you
**to encourage and assist him,** if you find the occasion.

**I am with the Spaniards as usual, discovering daily some new intrigue forged on their part to my disadvantage;
but** God will preserve me from it. I have brought to justice the Count of Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister,
who **are charged and accused of having dealt with them further than they ought,** and you shall be informed of what
follows …

## 4. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 28 April 1605 (Rommel p. 230)

The passage Rommel footnoted "It is to be regretted that these lines are not yet deciphered":

… and although my actions towards those who profess it, inside and outside my kingdom, **and even the [?favours] and
aid which the United Provinces and [the] city of Geneva continue to receive from me, sufficiently justify the truth of
my conduct and will in such cases, and the care I take to maintain and uphold those of the said religion, for the
notable interests I have in their preservation and in that of the public cause, nevertheless** … I reco[gnise] …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269-272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … and even on my part, **it must be considered that all
these accidents befell me almost at the same time as the conspiracy in England. It seems too that they were forged on
the same anvil.** The ambassador of Spain wanted to make me believe that I had done his master an injury in detaining
his secretary, who had been caught in the weaving of his treason, **so accustomed are the ministers of that nation to
se[d]uce and corrupt the subjects of the princes with whom [they] reside.** I am assured that the King of Great
Britain, my good brother, has as much reason to complain of it as I, **yet he covers and dissimulates it better.** But
I cannot believe that for this **his affairs prosper** the more; which it would be needful **that all who love him
should point out to him,** as **I have done by an ambassador whom** I sent to him expressly for this purpose … likewise
I have commended to him **the United Provinces in the extreme need they have** at present of being **assisted and
reinforced by their allies, to restore** next year **the reputation of their arms.** But, my cousin, as you have **a
notable interest in their preservation, together with the other princes of Germany,** I pray you to work so that
they are likewise **succoured from your side, with both money and troops,** following the hope that some **of the said
princes have given them,** and you will do a very good work, **very useful to the common cause, and which will be**
very agreeable [to me]; praying you to let me know what I am to hope for from it. For my part, I have resolved **to
continue to them next year the same assistance in money and troops that I have given them this year; it is very
considerable and not less than two million livres; but if with that they are not succoured from elsewhere, it will
be impossible for them to resist their enemies, for these are gathering money and forces from all sides to attack
them more vigorously and powerfully than they have ever [done].** Be then **the author,** my cousin, **of having them
receive the said succour, and let it be as promptly as can be done, so that they may take the field among the first
in the spring, for he who can forestall his opponent in that will be greatly advantaged by it.** For the rest, **let
me know what the Emperor intends to treat in the diet of the Empire which he has summoned, and what** you hope **from
the negotiations for peace with [the Turk] and from the agreement of the Hungarians with the Emperor, as also from
the continuation of the siege of the city of Brunswick, in which I regret to see [the Duke of Brunswick] engaged.** I
pray God … Written at Paris, the 22nd day of December 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307-314)

… to share it with the princes of Germany of that condition, **[7 groups unresolved] that things are still in great
confusion,** because **the Emperor does not change his conduct, and he is very badly assisted and [served] in his
affairs, principally on the side of** Hungary, where neither peace nor war is made as it should be … [p. 308 in clear:
the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … on all sides. **The Spaniards
will do what they can to have a King of the Romans elected from the House of Austria who is entirely at their
devotion, having** learned that the Duke of Feria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the election, to
propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war on them] …
**which are two proposals of great consequence, and in which all Christendom has no less interest [than] Germany
itself. That is why I consider it necessary that all the kings and princes who must be jealous of the growth and
prosperity of the Spanish power should advise in good time and take counsel together on what is fitting to do to
prevent the said Spaniards from making the affairs and resolutions of the said diet square with their wishes. For my
part, I shall always contribute to it very willingly what depends on me and is becoming for me to do, principally
when I recognise that the others can do the like, and above all the princes of [Holland: ?Germany], being no less
desirous than the kings my predecessors have been to favour and second the good intentions of the ancient allies and
friends of this crown on similar occasions, as on all others that may arise, for [the] preservation of German
liberty, without however expecting any other advantage from it than to advance the good of the common cause and the
public security; for which I shall be very glad not only that you inform those whom you judge worthy of it, but also
that you give me advice and counsel on what I can and must [do] now, both to assure them of my good will and to
advance its effects.**

I have been informed that the King of England is with good right very ill satisfied with the last refusal [of Owen
and Baldwin, in clear] … **[a run of letters and signs the 1840 typesetter could not render] I have at least … I have
not heard that the King of England breaks with the said Spaniards for this just offence, so much does he desire to
live in neutrality and peace; besides which I know that the said Spaniards omit nothing to do towards him and towards
his servants to compensate for and make him forget this discontent, having had several very specious offers and
overtures made to him anew expressly for this purpose. Nevertheless, as he is prudent and well counselled, I hope also
that he will take good care not to let himself be drawn by their inducements and persuasions, which have no other aim
than to abuse and circumvent those [to whom] they address them, so as to be able to [do] their business the better in
the Low Countries, where they intend to make a great effort this year, principally on the side of the Rhine and of
Friesland, having for this purpose doubled their army and awaiting only** the return to Brussels of the Marquis
Spinola … Which it will be very difficult for the said States of the United Provinces to resist, **for as they are
much weaker than the others, they will also be forced to put themselves on the defensive,** which will be **a
wretched condition for them, and one whose outcomes are too [?greatly] to be feared in a state composed of and governed
by several heads,** as is **the republic of the said provinces,** than in another. That is why **all who are joined in
interest with them must think well on it,** so as **to set their hand to it** before **their weakness and exhaustion**
become greater; **otherwise the remedies will come too late. You have [?seen] what I** wrote to you before on this
subject, praying you **to favour their affairs and suits with the princes of Germany,** of which I understand very
little effect has come. But the affairs and enterprises of the gentlemen of the said States, both on the side of the
East Indies and on the coast of Portugal, are prospering greatly, to the great prejudice of those of the said
Spaniards; **yet I doubt that this good fortune will last for them if that on land [?fails] them** and **their peoples
grow weary of bearing the burden of the war.** I shall write you nothing of the dispute between the Pope and the
Venetians, except that **I know the Spaniards will do what they can to take advantage of it,** as **they have always
done** on similar occasions. That is why, on my side, I shall do all I can to settle it as soon as possible … Written
at Fontainebleau, the 20th day of May 1606.

# Readings of the ciphered passages of Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice, Rommel 1840

Hand readings from the decodes in `*_decode.txt` (key in `../key.py`, transcriptions in `../ct/`). Spelling is the
clerk's as the figures give it, lightly normalised (u/v, i/j, apostrophes, punctuation). Square brackets mark words

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… Aussi, je n'en diffère plus la dépesche que pour attendre le **advis que vous m'avez promis est[re] sur icelle, et
spécialement sur le faict de la diette, et sçavoir si elle doibt avoir lieu, ce qui s'y doibt traicter, quelle
proposition j'y doibs faire pour le public et le particulier de mes alliés et a[utres], [en]suivant les discours que
nous avons [eu] ensemble, et pour sçavoir aussi si je ferois passer en Saxe et Danemarch mon dit ambassadeur, avec votre
bon conseil sur le [19, overbar: mémoire?] et instruction que je luy doibs donner.** Car j'ay pris telle asseurance et
confiance de votre amitié et prudence, que je veux cy après …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[ce qui se] peut sans préjudicier à nos alliés,** sur quoy je seray donques attendant en bonne dévotion votre bon
advis. Puisque l'Empereur ne comparoistra en la diette, j'estime qu'il ne s'y traitera chose d'importance **et
principallement de l'élection d'un roy des Romains,** dont je m'attends que vous m'advertirez, comme de ce qui se
passera en l'assemblée des Estats de Polongne. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **Mais le duc [de Bouillon] ne m'a
escript par sa lettre un seul mot de la venue par deçà de l'Administrateur [de Strasbourg], ny du faict de l'évesché de
Stras[bourg], tellement que je ne sçay co[m]me il aura pris ce voyage, ny de quelque sorte il embrasse la cause du dit
Administrateur;** je vous prie me faire sçavoir ce que vous en aurez apris.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211–214)

… ce que vous avez voulu m'en déférer. **[Le duc de … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] fait toute démonstration
de me porter de l'affection. Je vous assure aussy que je fais grande estime de leur amitié; mon frère le duc de Lorraine
en est l'entremetteur.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] Mais deux raisons m'en ont empesché: l'une est **la mauvaise intelligence
que j'ay recogneue entre les [princes?] protestants, laquelle j'ay eu crainte estre cause de rendre la dite visitation
peu fructueuse;** et l'autre **le mescontentement et dégoust qu'aucuns d'eulx m'ont donné de leurs actions,**
principallement depuis **qu'ils ont adjousté foy aux discours du duc de Bouillon et favorisé sa cause avec moins de
respect et considération à ma personne que je n'espérois d'eulx,** comme je vous ay quelques fois escrit …

Le feu de la révolte de la haulte Hongrie a esté bientost esteint, mais **la suite et conséquence en sera grande, et
pourra s'estendre avec le temps au voisinage.** Le roy d'Angleterre envoye vers moy le duc de Lenox, et en Espagne son
admiral, pour s'entretenir en bonne amitié de toutes parts: **conseil duquel je n'ay pas ouï qu'il se trouve [bien]**
avec le temps, mais il fault **qu'il en face les preuves devant qu'il le croye.**

Je suis adverty que **les Provinces Unies ont autant de courage de se deffendre et continuer la guerre que jamais,** et
veulx croire, cela estant, que **les moyens ne leur en manqueront point, estans pourveus comme ils sont; ils envoyent
des députés en Angleterre; ils espèrent qu'ils seront mieulx receus et favorisés que les Espagnols ne se sont promis,
car le d[it] roy ne veult perdre leur amitié,** ainsy que l'on m'a mandé. **Les dites provinces** ont esté naguères
visiter **d'un nouveau [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, des bonnes quallités duquel ils sont demeurés bien édifiés. Aussi
est-il vertueux. J'entends **qu'il leur a donné quelque espérance d'armer en leur faveur l'année prochaine.** En quoy je
vous prie **de le conforter et assister,** sy vous en rencontrez l'occasion.

**Je suis avec les Espagnols comme de coustume, découvrant journellement quelque nouvelle menée forgée de leur part à mon
désavantage; mais** Dieu m'en préservera. J'ay mis en justice le comte d'Auvergne, son beau-père et sa sœur, qui **sont
chargés et accusés d'avoir traité avec eulx plus avant qu'ils ne devoyent,** et serez adverty de ce qui en succèdera …

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269–272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … et mesmes de ma part, **il fault considérer que tous ces
accidens me sont arrivés quasi au mesme temps de la conspiration d'Angleterre. Il semble aussy qu'ils ayent esté forgés
sur mesme enclume.** L'ambassadeur d'Espagne vouloit me faire accroire que j'avois faict injure à son maître d'avoir
retenu son secrétaire, ayant esté surpris sur la tissure de sa trahison, **tant les ministres de ceste nation ont
accoustumé se[d]uire et corrompre les subjects des princes auprès desquels [ils] résident.** Je suis asseuré que le roy
de la Grande Bretagne, mon bon frère, a autant d'argument de s'en plaindre que moy, **toutesfois il le couvre et
dissimule mieulx.** Mais je ne puis croire que pour ce faict **ses affaires en prospèrent** davantage; ce qui seroit
besoin **que tous ceulx qui l'ayment luy remonstrassent,** comme **j'ay fait par un ambassadeur que** j'ay envoyé vers
luy exprès pour cest effect … pareillement je luy ay recommandé **les Provinces Unies au besoin extrême qu'elles ont** à
présent d'estre **assistées et renforcées par leurs alliés pour relever** l'année prochaine **la réputation de leurs
armes.** Mais, mon cousin, comme vous avez **notable intérest à leur conservation avec les autres princes d'Allemagne,**
je vous prie vous emploier à ce qu'ils soient pareillement **secourus de votre costé tant d'argent que de troupes,**
suivant l'espérance qu'aucuns **des dits princes leur ont donnée,** et vous ferez un très bon œuvre, **très utile à la
cause commune et qui [me] sera** très agréable, vous priant me mander ce que j'en doibs espérer; pour mon regard j'ay
délibéré **leur continuer l'année prochaine la mesme assistance d'argent et de troupes que je leur ay départie
ceste-cy; elle est très notable et non moindre de deux millions de livres; mais si avec cela ils ne sont point secourus
d'ailleurs, il leur sera impossible de résister à leurs ennemis, car ils font amas d'argent et de forces de toutes parts
pour les assaillir plus vivement et puissamment qu'ils n'ont point [faict].** Soyez donc **autheur,** mon cousin, **de
leur faire recevoir ledict secours, et que ce soit le plus promptement que faire se pourra, afin qu'ils puissent se
mettre aux champs au printemps des premiers, car celuy qui pourra prévenir sa partie en cela en sera grandement
avantagé.** Au demeurant, **mandez-moy ce que l'Empereur prétend traiter en la diète de l'Empire qu'il a intimée, et ce
que** vous espérez **des négotiations de la paix avec [le Turc] et de l'accord des Hongrois avec l'Empereur, comme de
la poursuite du siège de la ville de Brunswick, où j'ay regret de voir [le duc de Brunswick] engagé.** Je prie Dieu …
Escript à Paris le xxiie jour de décembre 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307–314)

… d'en faire part aux princes de la Germanie de ceste condition là, **[7 groups unresolved] que les choses sont tousjours
en grande confusion,** parce que **l'Empereur ne change point de conduite, et qu'il est très mal assisté et [servi] en
ses affaires, principa[l]lement du costé de** Hongrie, où l'on ne fait la paix ny la guerre comme il convient …
[p. 308 in clear: the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … de toutes
parts. **Les Espagnols feront ce qu'ils pourront pour faire eslire un roy des Romains de la maison d'Austriche qui soit
entièrement à leur dévotion, ayant** sceu que le duc de Féria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the
election, to propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war
on them] … **qui sont deux propositions de grande conséquence et auxquelles toute la chrestienté n'a moins d'intérest
[que] l'Allemagne mesme. C'est pourquoy j'estime estre nécessaire que tous les roys et princes qui doivent avoir
jalousie de l'agrandissement et prospérité de la puissance espagnole doibvent d'heure aviser et prendre conseil
ensemble de ce qu'il convient faire pour empescher que les dicts Espagnols ne fassent cadrer les affaires et résolutions
de la dite diète à leur souhait. Pour mon regard, j'y contribueray tousjours très volontiers ce qui dépendra de moy et me
sera bienséant de faire, principalement quand je recognoistray que les autres pourront faire le semblable, et par dessus
tous les princes de la [Hollande: Germanie?], n'estant moins désireux que ont esté les roys mes prédécesseurs de
favoriser et seconder les bonnes intentions des anciens alliés et amis de ceste couronne en semblables occasions comme
en toutes autres qui se présenteront pour [la] conservation de la liberté germanique, sans toutesfois en attendre autre
advantage que d'advancer le bien de la cause commune et la seureté publique; dont j'auray bien agréable non seulement
que vous advertissiez ceulx que vous en jugerez dignes, mais aussy que vous me donniez advis et conseil de ce que je
puis et doibs [faire] maintenant, tant pour les asseurer de ma bonne volonté que pour en advancer les effects.**

J'ay esté adverty que le roy d'Angleterre est à bon droit très mal satisfait du dernier refus [of Owen and Baldwin, in
clear] … **[a run of letters and signs the 1840 typesetter could not render] j'ay au moins … je n'ay pas ouï que le Roi
d'Angleterre pour ceste juste offence rompe avec les dits Espagnols, tant il désire vivre en neutralité et en paix;
joint que je sçay que les dits Espagnols n'obmettent rien à faire en son endroit et envers ses serviteurs pour
compenser et faire oublier ce mescontentement, luy ayant de nouveau fait faire plusieurs offres et ouvertures très
spécieuses exprès pour cest effet. Toutesfois, comme il est prudent et bien conseillé, j'espère aussi qu'il se gardera
bien de se laisser aller à leurs inductions et persuasions, qui n'ont autre but que d'abuser et circonvenir ceulx
[auxquels] ils les adressent, pour pouvoir mieux [faire] leur affaire aux Pays-Bas, où ils prétendent faire un grand
effort ceste année, principa[l]lement du costé du Rhin et de Frise, ayans pour cest effect redoublé leur armée et
n'attendans que** le retour à Brusselles du marquis Spinola … A quoy il sera très difficile que les dits Estats des
Provinces Unies résistent, **car comme ils sont foibles beaucoup plus que les autres, ils seront contraints aussy de se
mettre sur la défensive,** qui sera une **misérable condition pour eulx, et les événements de laquelle sont trop
[fort?] à craindre en un estat composé et gouverné par plusieurs testes,** comme est la **république des dites
provinces,** qu'en un autre. C'est pourquoy **tous ceulx qui sont conjoints d'intérest avec eulx doivent bien y penser,**
affin de **y porter la main** devant que **leur débilité et inanition** soyent plus grandes, **autrement les remèdes y
seront trop tardifs. Vous m'avez [veu?] ce que je** vous ay ci devant escrit sur ce subject, en vous priant de
**favoriser leurs affaires et poursuites envers les princes d'Allemagne,** de quoy j'entends estre réussy fort peu
d'effect. Mais les affaires et entreprises par messieurs des dits Etats, tant du costé des Indes Orientalles, qu'en la
coste du Portugal, vont prospérant grandement au grand préjudice de celles des dits Espagnols; **touttefois je doubte
que ceste bonne fortune leur dure si celle de la terre leur [manque?]** et que **leurs peuples se lassent de soustenir
le faix de la guerre.** Je ne vous escriray rien du différent, qui est entre le Pape et les Vénitiens, sinon que **je
sçay que les Espagnols feront ce qu'ils pourront pour s'en advantager,** aussy que **ils ont tousjours faict** de
semblables occasions. C'est pourquoy, de mon costé, je feray tout ce qu'il me sera possible pour le composer au plus
tost … Escrit à Fontainebleau, le 20e jour de may 1606.
