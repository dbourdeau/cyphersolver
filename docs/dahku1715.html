Cracow · sign substitution of Polish · September 1715 · key recovered from the ciphertext

“Dahka”, Cracow, 1715

Go to the full text ↓

A cipher passage in a Polish letter, catalogued with no decipherment, turns out to report the Sandomierz deputies’ request that the Crown army back their levy in the autumn of 1715.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 6 November 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: partial (84% of the cipher)

Contributed by Brice Dudout (solution) · Also credited · Daniel Bourdeau (pointer to a source)

ciphertext-only

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · partial (84% of the cipher)

The letter
A Polish letter from Cracow signed “Dahka” (catalogued as Dahku), probably of 21 September 1715, to an unnamed member of the Branicki circle; 17 lines of graphic signs on pp. 2–3, with clear words among them.
Where
AGAD Warsaw, fonds 338 (Zbiór Anny z Potockich Branickiej), unit 1/338/0/1/682, scan 3. The archive card says “część listu pisana szyfrem, nierozwiązanym”. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A letter-for-letter substitution of Polish with 24 signs for 20 letters and a few homophones. A framed sign and a tied sign stand where names are expected.
How it was deciphered
Broken on the cipher alone, with no crib, by annealing with a Polish model learnt from the Deboli clear texts of this repository; eight restarts gave the same text. Measured once on 72 held-out signs: 0.444 against 0.083 for shuffled keys, under the 0.60 threshold, so the key is partial.
What it says
The Sandomierz deputies asked that the Crown army support the levy of their voivodeship, set for 9 October, and not be moved away from Sandomierz; the answer was that nothing could be promised before the Primate’s resolution was known.
Still open
Seven words that do not construe, two signs that are probably names, and a word at the edge of the page.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée) transcribed the signs, recovered the key and read the passage, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents. The letter came from Daniel Bourdeau’s catalogue (no. 1581).

01 The letter

The letter is in the papers of Anna Branicka, née Potocka, at the Central Archives of Historical Records in Warsaw. Page 1, the address and the end are in clear Polish; only scan 3 (pp. 2–3) carries cipher. No gloss, key or clear copy is on the five scans. The unit card gives “1715, IX, 21”, and the cipher speaks of 9 October as still to come, so the letter is probably of 21 September 1715. That autumn the nobility of the Crown was arming against the Saxon troops of Augustus II, the movement that became the Confederation of Tarnogród in November.

02 The key and its test

The signs were named with ASCII letters in a table fixed before the line-by-line transcription, and the file was frozen before any attempt at the key. Lines d01–e02 (278 signs) were the training part: simulated annealing with a Polish four-letter model, eight restarts, the same text each time. The raw key was frozen before the held-out lines e03–e06, chosen before any attack, were decoded.

The measure is the share of decoded signs that fall in Polish words of five letters or more: 0.444 on the held-out block, against 0.083 for the best of 200 shuffled keys. The margin is met, the 0.60 threshold is not; the block is short and there is no clear text of the time to compare with. After the measurement a blind shape question settled the barred sign V as W in two places, for the reading only.

03 The reading

63 cipher words: 39 certain, 14 probable, none restored and 10 unresolved, 84.1% read by sense. The deputies of Sandomierz asked for the Crown army (wojsko koronne) to support the levy en masse of the voivodeship, set “pro die nona octobris”, and not to be moved away from Sandomierz. The answer was that no commitment could be made before the Primate’s resolution was known; it was only declared that the army would not be withdrawn but taken across the Vistula. The Polish and an English translation are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Seven words do not construe as letters: zto, e, kostalacio (d02), kodzarzyc (e05, where a place name is expected), nadra, zo, a (e05–e06).
  • The framed sign (twice) and the tied sign (once) are probably names, such as the subject of “chciał assistere” and a title before “prymas”; no nomenclator for this cipher is known.
  • The last word of e05 was transcribed from a reduced view at the edge of the page.
  • The key cannot be tested again on this letter; another text in the same signs is needed.
  • The writer and the addressee are not identified.

scan 3, pp. 2-3: the cipher passage of the letter (17 lines) read in part

The Sandomierz deputies asked that the Crown army support the levée en masse of the Sandomierz voivodeship, set for 9 October, and not be moved away from Sandomierz; the answer was that no commitment could be made before the Primate's resolution to the deputies was known; it was only declared that the army would not be withdrawn but taken across the Vistula. The run-up to the Confederation of Tarnogród (Nov 1715).

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (84% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

# dahku1715 - reading of the cipher passage (Polish, spelling of the letter)

Marks: plain = certain (every letter given by the key); {x} = probable (one or two signs off, or a letter the key does
not give, settled by the sense); [x] = restored letters inside a clear word (no cipher word is restored); ‹x› = unresolved
(the decryption as it stands). Clear words of the letter are kept as written. Line references as in transcription.txt.

The key is frozen and partial by our protocol (see validation.txt). After the measurement Brice Dudout answered a blind
shape plate on the barred sign `V` (planche V, 5 Nov 2026, 23:41): at d05 and e05 it has the shape of `W` (= z), so it is
read z at those two places, for the reading only (the frozen key and the measurement are unchanged; d07 was not asked and
keeps V = g).

Notes on single words:
- `N` (a framed sign, twice, d03 and d09) decodes z, but in the reading it is the subject of "chciał assistere" and of
  "deklarować nie może": most likely a nomenclator name (the hetman? the king?). Counted unresolved.
- `C` (e01, ".cш." tied, with a г-like superscript) decodes w; it stands where a name or title is expected ("da ‹C› im
  prymas"). Counted unresolved.
- d05 {sendomirskiego}: with V = z the cipher spells "sendomirskiezo"; the sense (wdztwa sendomirskiego) is clear, and the
  same ending is spelled with V in d07 "koronnego". Counted probable, not certain, because one cipher letter disagrees.
- e03-e04 {deklarował}: the cipher has "da klarowal" (a for e). Counted probable.
- e05 ‹kodzarzyc› (V = z; with V = g "kodgarzyc"): a place name is expected after "przeprawić z"; not identified.
- e05-e06 ‹nadra› ‹zo› … stronę: perhaps "na drugą stronę" (to the other side), but the letters do not give it; unresolved.
- e06 ‹a›: after the measurement we noticed that this isolated `+` is probably the clear word "to" (+o); not corrected.
- "posł[a]nym": the letter a is restored inside a clear word (not counted, clear words are outside the count).

## Count (Daniel's rule: certain + probable over all cipher words; restored words would not count)

Cipher words, cut as in the reading above (a cipher token like "zwoiskim" counts as two words, z + woiskim):
**63 words: 39 certain, 14 probable, 0 restored, 10 unresolved -> 53/63 = 84.1% read by sense**, under 95%:
**read in part**.
(Counted on the cipher tokens as written, without splitting z + noun or nie + verb, the same text gives 49 of 59 = 83%.)
The planche V did not change the share: d05 moves from certain to probable, e05 stays unresolved.
