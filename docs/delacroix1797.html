Delacroix to Létombe, Paris, 25 Messidor an V (13 July 1797): despatch No. 5 in the ministry’s code

Go to the full text ↓

A despatch of the French foreign minister to the consul acting as consul general in Philadelphia survives in American hands as 653 numbers. The ministry’s own clear draft, found in Paris, gives a partial table of the code and most of the text.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 24 November 2026 · updated 25 November 2026

Method · Despatch No. 5, 653 groups: partial table and reading from the ministry's clear draft (Andrew Beling, PR #884, 21-22 Nov 2026): key from external plaintext (partial) · Ciphertext-only attempt of 2 Nov 2026 (the project): not solved (none) · On the map

Contributed by Andrew Beling (solution) and Michael Brackx (transcription)

At a glanceKey from external plaintext · partial · 0.939 of the cipher words, 0.667 on values confirmed twice

The letter
Charles Delacroix, minister of external relations, to Létombe, consul at Philadelphia acting as consul general, Paris, 25 Messidor an V (13 July 1797): despatch No. 5, wholly in numbers between a heading and a close in clear. The copy is a clerk’s duplicate that Rufus King, the American minister in London, enclosed in his No. 53 of 12 November 1797: “I have no means of decyphering it.”
Where
US National Archives, RG 59, Despatches from US Ministers to Great Britain, vol. 5, microfilm M30 roll 4, frames 0244 and 0245. Not on DECODE. Catalogue 649 until this write-up.
The cipher
A code of numbers from 2 to 1198 for words, syllables, single letters and names, with several numbers for common units; full stops have numbers of their own. 653 groups, 297 different.
How it was deciphered
The ministry’s clear draft of the despatch is in the Archives diplomatiques in Paris (Correspondance politique, États-Unis, vol. 48, ff. 41r–42r), marked “à chiffrer”. Andrew Beling, with AI agents, found it, showed by a test on groups kept aside that it is the text behind the numbers, and laid it on them to build a partial table.
What it says
Delacroix has President Adams’s speech from the consuls at New York and Boston and hopes the President “ne veut pas sérieusement en venir aux extrémités”. He orders the consuls to keep politics out of despatches written in clear, or to use the cipher.
How much
27 of the 444 cipher words carry a mark, a share of 0.939. On a strict count the share is 0.667 (296 words). The two differ because 121 words rest on a number used only once in this letter, which the first count takes as read and the second as probable.
Still open
29 groups: eight where a digit on the film is in doubt, 17 with an unsure value, four where the table’s value differs from the draft’s letters. The 148 values used once are untested, since no second text in this code is known. One lead is open: volume 47 of the Paris series (938 images online), only spot checked; the other volumes have not been looked at. Michael Brackx transcribed the groups from the film; the draft has been transcribed only by AI agents, and no person has compared the reading with the film.
Credit
Finding the draft, the test, the alignment, the table and the reading are Andrew Beling’s, with AI agents (pull request 884). The 653 groups were transcribed by Michael Brackx (issue 122). The strict count, the key replay and the checks of 24 Nov 2026 were made for the maintainers by AI agents.

01 The copy in Washington

The text is a clerk’s copy on three pages of a volume of despatches from the American legation in London. It is marked “Dup.”, “(Copy)” and “No. 5 Duplicata”, and dated “Paris 25 Messidor an 5 de la République”. The heading is in clear: “Le Ministre des relations extérieures au citoyen Letombe consul à Philadelphie, exerçant provisoirement le consulat général de la République Française aux États Unis”. So is the close: “Salut et fraternité”, “Ch. Delacroix”. Everything between is numbers separated by full stops.

Rufus King enclosed the copy in his despatch No. 53 to the Secretary of State, London, 12 November 1797, and wrote of it: “I have no means of decyphering it.” The frames around it (0236 to 0252) carry no decipherment.

Michael Brackx transcribed the whole text from the film on 27 October 2026: 653 groups, 297 different, values from 2 to 1198. His transcription is used here unchanged. He also pointed to the one printed text that touches the despatch. Létombe’s reply No. 42, of 22 Brumaire an VI, is printed in Turner’s Correspondence of the French Ministers (1904), pp. 1076–77, from the ministry’s decipherment. In it he promises more attention to “ce qui m’a été prescrit par la dépêche du 25 Messidor dernier”. Turner does not print No. 5 itself.

02 The draft in Paris

The plaintext survives. The ministry’s own draft of the despatch is in the Archives diplomatiques, Correspondance politique, États-Unis, vol. 48 (39CP/48), ff. 41r–42r. It is dated Paris, 25 messidor an V, addressed to Létombe, and marked in the margin “à chiffrer. F.” and “Politique N° 5”. Andrew Beling, working with AI agents, found it in the archive’s online viewer on 21 November 2026. Two AI readers transcribed it without seeing each other’s work and the two transcriptions were merged; one disputed word (“aurez” or “avez”) went into the test text as reader A’s “aurez” by the plan’s rule, and a third AI agent of the contributor’s read “aurez” on the image afterwards.

The draft is the minute, not the text that was put into code, and the two differ in a few places. The last sentence of the draft reads “Le Citoyen Adet est arrivé à Paris, il y a quinze jours: Il a eu son audience du Directoire exécutif”; the American copy has no groups for “est arrivé à Paris, il y a quinze jours: Il” (and spells the name a-de, without its t), and where in copying the clause was dropped cannot be told from the film. Of the two places where the draft has “M. Adams”, the earlier has no group for “M.”. One word of the draft is written over another and reads “facilité”; the copy has there the number, 1126, that also stands for “utiles” a few words later.

The project has not seen the draft on the archive’s images. Its viewer asks each reader to accept the archive’s conditions of reuse first, and that step was left to the maintainer. Every reader of the draft so far is an AI agent.

03 The test

A draft that fits is not proof by itself, because a table can be made to fit any text of the right length. The contributor therefore wrote a test plan with its pass marks, and recorded the hashes of the plan, the scripts, the ciphertext and the test text, before the tests were run.

Test 2a is reported as a miss. Eleven of its 18 pairs are repeats of one run, the word “citoyen”, so its controls score almost as high and it could separate nothing. The plan had fixed beforehand that the verdict rests on tests 1 and 2b.

Test 3 is the one that matters most: a table made from the first 568 groups predicts the draft’s letters for groups it never saw. A second scorer for it, written from scratch by another of the contributor’s AI agents, was run again at the project’s review of the pull request and gave the same 36 of 43, against 12.3 on average and 18 at most for 1,000 shuffled keys. At that review the table was also rebuilt from the alignment and came out identical, and a decode of Brackx’s groups with it gave the filed decryption.

Two limits. Tests 1, 2a and 2b were not run again by the project. And the order of events rests on the contributor’s own files: the draft was first opened at 00:52 on 21 November 2026 (US Eastern time), the two readers transcribed it from 01:07 to 01:13, and the plan was written and hashed at 01:31 and the test text fixed at 01:32, by an agent that, by the session’s record, had seen only the two readers’ short reports (line counts and a few hard spots such as “[facilité?]” and “Boston”), not the draft’s wording. Those are local file times and SHA-256 sums, with no outside timestamp.

04 The code

With the test passed, the draft was laid on the 653 groups by hand, group by group. The table that follows from the pair has 297 numbers: 136 confirmed by two or more uses that agree, 148 that rest on a single use, and 13 unsure.

The numbers stand for whole words, syllables, single letters and names: 887 gouvernement, 368 dépêche or dépêches, 705 ci, 806 to, 150 m, 921 Boston, 767 Adams. The run 705 806 711 405, or 705 806 400 405, comes seven times and is ci-to-y-en, Citoyen. Adet, Rozier and Mozard are spelled out. Full stops have numbers (412, 413, 416, 417), and 469 appears to be a null.

The table is in no single alphabetical order: over all of it the order of the numbers and the order of the values do not agree (rank correlation 0.08). Yet neighbouring numbers are in alphabetical order far more often than chance, 76% of adjacent pairs against 50% for shuffled values: 2 to 10 are ce, celui, cer, ces; 253 to 257 ainsi, ajouter, al; 493 to 500 point, politique, port, possible. So the code is made of short alphabetical runs set down in an order that is not alphabetical. Several numbers for one value often sit side by side: 81 to 83 are a, 972 and 973 de, 265 to 269 le or les, 799 and 800 vous.

The decryption is the table applied to Brackx’s digits with nothing edited, and the reading is the draft’s words with every letter that the table does not give put in square brackets. Both are in the full texts.

05 What it says

Square brackets mark letters the table does not give for the groups as transcribed. They are the holes.

Delacroix opens with the news from America. The consuls at New York and Boston have sent him the speech made by Adams at the opening of the extraordinary session, and he waits for Létombe’s own despatches:

Malgré le ton du discours de Monsieur Adams, et celui du projet d’adresse [de] [la] Chambre des représentants, j’espère que le président des Etats-unis ne veut pas sérieusement en venir aux extrémités.

(Despite the tone of Mr Adams’s speech, and that of the draft address of the House of Representatives, I hope that the President of the United States does not seriously mean to go to extremes.) The Directory’s position is in his despatch of 29 prairial, to which he has nothing to add. He approves the language Létombe will have held on the instructions Adet left him, asks for prompt detail on everything that passes in Congress, and says that Hauterive, formerly consul at New York, will have helped him keep up relations with “nos amis dans la Chambre des représentants, sans les compromettre”.

Then comes the order on secrecy. The despatches of the citizens Rozier and Mozard hold political details that it is dangerous to trust to the hazards of the sea:

Ces dépêches écrites en clair nous compromettraient gravement, si elles étaient interceptées. Ce qui est déja arrivé doit nous servir de le[çon].

(Those despatches, written in clear, would compromise us gravely if they were intercepted. What has already happened must serve us as a lesson.) The consuls are to confine their correspondence, as far as possible, to consular matters, political economy, the sciences and the arts. When an opportunity to send to France arises in their districts, they should tell the agents charged with politics, so that these can use it. Where they have news of internal politics that must go without delay, “Il faut au moins [qu]’[ils] [ay]ent recours au chiffre”: they must at least use the cipher. Delacroix adds that he has opened a separate series of despatches for what does not concern Létombe’s work as consul general, and supposes Létombe has done the same for politics. He ends with Adet’s audience of the Executive Directory “le dix de ce mois”.

This agrees with Létombe’s printed reply, which answers what the despatch of 25 Messidor prescribed.

06 Two shares

The reading has 444 cipher words, counted by the contributor’s script. 27 of them carry a mark, so 417 are given by the table: a share of 0.939. That is under the project’s bar of 0.95, so the extent is partial.

This share has a weakness that the contributor states himself. The table was made by laying the draft on the groups, so a table that gives back the draft is what one expects; the share says how consistently it does so. And 148 of its numbers rest on a single use in this one letter. The project’s owner therefore decided on 23 November 2026 that a second, strict count stands beside the first. In it a word is sure only when every one of its groups has a value confirmed by two or more agreeing uses. 296 of the 444 words are sure, a share of 0.667; 121 words touch at least one single-use value and count as probable; the same 27 are holes.

The key was applied again to the transcription with the project’s replay tool on 24 November 2026. It finds 414 of 441 words identical to the reading (0.939) and the other 27 marked in the reading; it counts 441 words because it does not divide New-yorck and Etats-unis at the hyphen. No word differs from the key’s output without a mark.

The project’s statistical check does not confirm the table and does not refute it. The reading scores −1.938 per letter on a model of French, against −2.514 on average for 200 random keys of the same shape. But a key of the same shape fitted to the shuffled groups reaches −1.935 on average, as good as the reading. With about two uses per number the letter model cannot tell this table from a fitted one, and the verdict is “underdetermined”. The evidence that the draft is the text behind the numbers is the test on the groups kept aside (section 03).

An AI agent read the reading through as sentences for the maintainers on 24 November 2026 and found coherent French in every sentence, with each hole a short word or part of a word. The grade is B, because the words are the ministry’s draft laid on the groups and 121 more words rest on single-use values; no person has checked them.

07 What remains uncertain

Every value in the table comes from the draft, a known plaintext (grade C in the project’s grades); none comes from a key of the time. The unsure values and the places where a digit is in doubt are grade M. The 29 marked groups fall in three kinds.

  • Eight places where a digit on the film is in doubt. The contributor had three AI readers read each place on the film without context. Three changes are supported by two or three of them: 808 read as 898 with “80” written above (898 is con four times; le[çon]), 1024 as 1034 (pri[e]) and 327 as 527 ([d]’envoyer). Five are not: 257 at three places where the text has ai (n’[ai], n’[ayiez], consul[ai]re[s]), 108 where it has [sur], and 381 where it has [dél]ai. None is applied. They wait for a person to look at the film.
  • Seventeen groups with an unsure value. 383 (extraor[d]inaire; [de] [la] Chambre), 496 (m’ap[porte]ront, ra[pports], [porteur]s, [pui]ssent), 526 (Mozar[d], consul[ai]re[s], [d]’[en]), 571 (se[rait], Consul [général]), 1122 (de[vr]ait, de[vr]aient, [porteur]s), 1126 ([facilité], [utiles]) and 347 ([qu]’[ils]). Most stand where the draft has different letters at different places (496 is port or porte three times and pui once); 347 comes once and may be a slip for 247. With one letter in this code nothing decides.
  • Four groups where the table and the draft differ by a letter or two. 175 is se where the draft has [ses]sion, 855 pre for pro[pres], 355 es (once ez) where the draft has iez (n’[ayiez]), 20 ai for [ay]ent. The text that was put into code is not held.

Beyond the marked groups:

  • The 148 single-use values, on which 121 words rest, are untested outside this letter. No second text in this code is known: there is none on the neighbouring frames of the film or among the project’s other targets and leads, and the contributor paged volume 48 in full in the archive’s online viewer and found the ministry’s decipherments of Létombe’s despatches without their numbers. Volume 47 (January to June 1797, 938 images online) was only spot checked by him and has not been paged in full. It stays an open lead. It was not paged here because the archive’s viewer first asks the reader to accept its conditions of reuse, which is left to the maintainer. The other volumes of the series have not been looked at.
  • Two single-use numbers are doubtful on the film: 300 may be 308, and 1105 may be 1185 or 1195. A look at the enlarged frames on 24 November 2026 did not settle either.
  • Two groups stand where the draft has punctuation or nothing (411 at a colon, 802 after “je compte”) and have no value.
  • The draft was not seen by the project on the archive’s images, and no person has read it: its two transcriptions and their merging are AI agents’ work. On the film, the 653 groups are Michael Brackx’s transcription, and he listed his doubtful digits; since then the doubtful places have been looked at only by the contributor’s AI readers, and no person has compared the reading with the film.
  • The copy that Létombe received, or a deciphered copy, if it was kept, would be among the papers of the Philadelphia consulate at the Centre des archives diplomatiques de Nantes (519PO/1, article 1, letters received from the ministry, March 1793 to February 1798), which is not digitised as far as found.

08 Before the draft

The project first tried the numbers alone, on 2 November 2026, and established no value. Two measured findings of that attempt hold up against the table. Neighbouring numbers cluster (a score of 162 against 98 on average for 3,000 random reassignments, none as high), which the table explains: several numbers for one value sit side by side. And a test for one alphabetical order over the whole code, calibrated on synthetic letters of the same size, found none. The search programs themselves failed that calibration: on a synthetic letter of 653 groups with a known key they could not reach the key unless the code was in one alphabetical order, so their output on the real text carried no information.

Seven French numeric keys held in the repository were scored against the text that day and all fell within chance. With the table the comparison can be made number by number, and was on 24 November 2026: those seven keys agree with the table on none of the 39 to 209 numbers each shares with this letter, and of 174 key files compared none agrees on more than three.

# Reading: Delacroix to Létombe, Paris, 25 messidor an V (13 July 1797), despatch Politique No. 5 (653 groups)

How this text was made. The words are those of the French foreign ministry's clear draft of the despatch
(Archives diplomatiques, Correspondance politique, États-Unis, vol. 48, ff. 41r-42r; `../transcriptions/clear_draft_ff41r-42r.md`),
laid on Brackx's 653 groups by the hand alignment `../key/alignment_by_group.txt` and checked against the partial
key `../key/code_table_F_partial.tsv` (built on Brackx's digits as transcribed) by `../scripts/make_reading.py`,
with the proposals file given so that the eight places where a digit change is proposed are always marked. It is a reading from the period's own clear text,
not a solution of the code from the numbers alone.

Conventions. Square brackets `[...]` mark letters that the key does not give for the groups as Brackx transcribed
them (a digit change is proposed, the value is unsure, or the group stands for something else in the key); they count
as holes. `<<not in the groups: ...>>` marks words of the draft for which the copy has no group (not cipher words,
not counted). Spelling and accents are the draft's; "M." is written "Monsieur", "29" as "vingt neuf" and
"gouv.t" as "gouvernement", as in the test text. Paragraph breaks are editorial.

Measured by `make_reading.py`: 444 cipher words, 27 with a mark, read share 0.939 on Brackx's digits as transcribed
(under the 95% bar: read in part). The eight proposal places (`../proposals/transcription_corrections.tsv`) are marked
whether or not a change is accepted; no proposed change is applied. Apostrophe words count as one word (`[d]'[en]`); split at
apostrophes it is 29 of 465, 0.938. Caution: 148 values of the key rest on a single use in this one letter, so the
share says how consistently the key gives the draft, not that the key was found independently of it; the independent
evidence is the held-out test (`../README.md`).

# Translation: Delacroix to Létombe, Paris, 25 messidor an V (13 July 1797), Politique No. 5

English translation of `../readings/reading_1797-07-13.md`, made by an AI agent (Claude Opus 5.5) on 22 Nov 2026 and
not checked by a person. Square brackets mark the same words as in the reading: words whose letters the key does not
give for the groups as transcribed. Words of the draft that have no groups in the copy are in `<<...>>`.

Heading in clear on the copy: The Minister of External Relations to Citizen Létombe, Consul at Philadelphia, acting
provisionally as Consul General of the French Republic in the United States.

The Consuls at New York and Boston, Citizen, have sent me the speech made by <<Mr>> Adams at the opening of the
extraordinary [session]. I await with much impatience your own despatches, which will no doubt bring me the decision
of the federal government. Despite the tone of Mr Adams's speech, and that of the draft address of the House of
Representatives, I hope that the President of the United States does not seriously mean to go to extremes. As for
the Directory's sentiments, they are set out in my despatch of 29 prairial, and I have [nothing] to add to it. The
instructions that Citizen Adet left you before his departure being exactly in line with that despatch, I can only
approve the language you will have held to the American government on the strength of those instructions.

The circumstances are serious enough, Citizen, that you will [have] neglected nothing to let me know as quickly as
possible, in detail, [about] everything that will have happened in Congress: I count on your zeal in this.

Citizen Hauterive, formerly Consul of the Republic at New York, whom Citizen Adet authorised to act in concert with you,
and whose talents are known, will have been of great [use] to you in keeping up [useful] [relations] with our friends
in the House of Representatives without compromising them.

The despatches of Citizens Rozier and [Mozard] contain political details that it is dangerous to entrust to the
hazards of the sea and often to the bad faith of the [bearers]. Those despatches, written in clear, would compromise
us gravely if they were intercepted. What has already happened must serve us as a [lesson]. I [ask] you to make them,
and the other Consuls, feel that their correspondence [should], as far as possible, be confined to [consular]
matters, matters of political economy, of the sciences and arts, and others of that kind which cannot give rise to
any dispute between government and government.

When opportunities [for sending] to France arise in their districts, they [should] inform the agents in charge of
political affairs of them, so that [they] [can] make use of them. This course [would be] in keeping with prudence and
with the good of the service in cases where they have to send, on internal politics, information [that] must be sent
without [delay]: they must at least [have] recourse to the cipher.

You will see from my correspondence with you that I have opened a series of despatches for what does not concern the
matters you have to deal with as Consul [General]. I suppose you will have taken care [to do] the same for everything
that concerns politics.

Citizen Ade<<t arrived in Paris a fortnight ago: he>> has had his audience of the Executive Directory, on the tenth of
this month.

(Close in clear on the copy: Salut et fraternité. Ch. Delacroix.)

Notes on the translation: "départemens" is rendered "districts" (the consuls' areas); the bracket on "for sending"
stands for the marked "[part]" of "départemens" in the reading, which an English sentence cannot keep in place, and
is approximate. Brackets that fall inside a French word are put on the nearest English word.
