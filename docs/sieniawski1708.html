Wallachia → Hetman Sieniawski · numerical substitution, the Wallachian chancellery key · 1709–1714 · already in print

Brâncoveanu and Cantacuzino to Hetman Sieniawski, 1709–1714

→

Twelve cipher letters from the Prince of Wallachia and his uncle to the Polish hetman, listed on DECODE with no decipherment, were printed from these originals with their key in 2012.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 9 October 2026 · updated 15 October 2026

Method: read from existing decipherment · Extent: complete

The letters
Constantin Brâncoveanu, Prince of Wallachia, and his uncle Constantin Cantacuzino to Adam Mikołaj Sieniawski, Grand Hetman of the Crown, June 1709 to September 1714, in Polish.
Where
Archiwum Narodowe w Krakowie, Zbiór Dzieduszyckich 26/32 nr 13 and nr 5 (DECODE R7468–R7474, R7477–R7480, R7483), status N/A, no decipherment attached.
The cipher
Two-digit numbers for letters (a = 19, e = 9, i = 29, n = 72 …), dot-separated or run together, with a few code numbers (900 = August).
How it was deciphered
M. W. Kaczka printed all twelve from these originals in 2012, with the key. Ten lines of R7473, decoded with his key here, agree with the decipherment written between the lines and with his text.
Still open
Nothing in these letters; they were not transcribed here beyond the check.
Credit
Kaczka’s edition and key (after A. Mareș 1987); the contemporary interlinear decipherments.

DECODE catalogues these records in one range (R7468–R7500, catalogue entry 333) with letters in four other keys, which have their own pages (a different cipher in each): fifteen German letters from a Vienna agent to Baron Schenck (schenck1706), news from Bender in 1709 (turculet1709), an adviser’s letter of about 1707 (r7475) and Chomentowski’s letters from the Porte (chomentowski1713).

01 The letters and the edition

Mariusz Kaczka printed the Polish letters of Constantin Brâncoveanu and Constantin Cantacuzino to Sieniawski from these originals in Danubius XXX (2012), with the Wallachian chancellery’s key in his note 21, after Andrei Mareș (1987). The DECODE records match his numbers as follows:

Kaczka names the collection Zbiór Dzieduszyckich; DECODE’s short names read “ADzied”.

02 The key, and the check

R7473 is written with a dot after each number and deciphered letter by letter between the lines: Jaśnie wielmożny M. P. Kasztelanie krakowski hetmanie w. k. … with J/i = 29, a = 19, s = 73, n = 72, e = 9, w = 65, l = 74, m = 76, o = 39, z = 69, y = 59, P = 62, k = 67, t = 77. The same numbers, run together without dots, fill R7468–R7472, with the clear words “WM Pana” between them. Ten lines of p. 17, decoded with Kaczka’s key, give the interlinear text and his printed text, with two slips of the encipherer (69 for 67 in krakowski, prszez).

The letters were not transcribed in full here: their text is Kaczka’s. What this entry adds is the identification of twelve DECODE records, listed with no decipherment, with his edition.
