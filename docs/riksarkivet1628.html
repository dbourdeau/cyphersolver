Bremen → Johan Adler Salvius, Hamburg · homophonic cipher of two-digit figures inside clear German · November–December 1631

The archbishop of Bremen to Johan Adler Salvius, 1631

Go to the full text ↓

Two appeals for Swedish help in the winter after Breitenfeld, half in figures; a clerk’s glosses between the lines gave the key, and every figure group now has a value. A check on 22 October 2026 found the decryption not built into sentences; on 23 October both letters were written out as German sentences (bremen1631_reading.md), the clear text re-read on the photographs and each cipher word checked against its gloss. They build coherent sentences but read at 93.1% of their words, under the 95% a complete reading needs.

The letters
Johann Friedrich of Holstein-Gottorp, archbishop of Bremen, to Johan Adler Salvius, Swedish resident at Hamburg, 27 November and 2 December 1631. Clear German with the sensitive words in figures.
Where
Riksarkivet, Stockholm; DECODE R4330 and R4331 (“Riksarkivet_Bremen_Tott_1631”: the addressee is Salvius, not Åke Tott), filed under catalogue item 207.
The cipher
Two-digit figures, four consecutive numbers for each letter; the bands follow the alphabet written in a six-column grid and read down the columns. A few one- and two-digit numbers stand for names.
How the key was found
A clerk deciphered about four fifths of the groups between the lines. Matching those glosses to the numbers gave the bands, and the grid order placed the letters no gloss covers.
What it says
The archbishop begs for Swedish foot and dragoons against Gronsfeld’s and Bönninghausen’s troops, fearing “die euserste gefahr”.
Still open
Part of the reading. Of 132 cipher words, 118 equal their decode, 10 carry a clerk’s slip that the gloss of the time corrects, and 4 are holes; five name numbers rest on glosses read with doubt and six are left as numbers, and the clear German has doubtful words from the phone photographs. The two letters read at 93.1% of their words (measured 24 October 2026), under 95%. The key itself is complete.

Catalogue item 207 filed these two letters with twelve other Riksarkivet records in unrelated ciphers. Each of those is now a different cipher, with its own page: R4282 (deciphered by George Lasry), R4284, R4306, Rusdorff’s letters of 1628 and the E 708 letters of 1633.

Update, 17 October 2026: R4306, belonging to Riksarkivet key-box and Oxenstierna ciphers, now has a separate contributed Dutch decipherment, translation and visual key by George Lasry: transcribed with CTTS, then solved with Opus 5.5. Four codes remain unresolved. The project’s own unsuccessful attempt of 21 September 2026 on R4306 is recorded on that page.

01The letters

DECODE R4330 and R4331 are two letters of Johann Friedrich, archbishop of Bremen, to Salvius at Hamburg, of 27 November and 2 December 1631, in German. The figure runs sit inside the clear text, and a clerk of the time wrote his decipherment above most of them. A few small numbers stand for words: 4 = wir/uns, 5 = Herzog Christian, 7 = Herzog Georg, 32 = the Stift Minden, 59 = Salvius.

02The Bremen key

Thirty-five glossed words were set against their numbers one letter to one number. Twenty-three matched in length, and the numbers for each letter came out as runs of four: 8–11 for a, 16–19 for n, 72–75 for e, 96–99 for s. The runs are not in alphabetical order. The alphabet is written in six columns (a b c d e f / g h i k l m / n o p q r s / t u w x y z) and read downwards, so the bands go a g n t, b h o u, c i p w, d k q x, e l r y, f m s z:

8 a · 12 g · 16 n · 20 t · 24 b · 28 h · 32 o · 36 u · 40 c · 44 i · 48 p · 52 w · 56 d · 60 k · 64 q · 68 x · 72 e · 76 l · 80 r · 84 y · 88 f · 92 m · 96 s · 100 z

No gloss covers k, z, m, w or y. The unglossed groups fix them: sincken, schantze, kommen and dem accord mitbegriffen, etwas and wir, weyter and Reuterey. Once they are in place, the grid predicts q and x without a single example. Small numbers stand for words: 4 = wir/uns, 5 = Herzog Christian, 7 = Herzog Georg, 32 = the Stift Minden, 59 = Salvius.

03What the letters say

The archbishop, Johann Friedrich of Holstein-Gottorp, wants Swedish help, and quickly. In the letter of 27 November he fears that the enemy will be in the Stift before relief comes, “und in die euserste gefahr kommen würden”, and asks that “mit schleunigem hulf und succurs ausgeholffen werden möchte”. His people have taken die Burg and are attacking die schantze. They cannot hold without more men.

The letter of 2 December is more urgent. The Reuterey ranges up and down the country, laying waste one place after another, and he fears an einfal by Bönninghausen und Gronsfeldischen troupen from the Westphalian border. He hopes to be “mit etlichen tausend secundiret”, or at least to get etwas fusvolckes und theils dragoner. A postscript asks that, if the garrison of Newenburg marches out on terms, his ertzstift be in dem accord mitbegriffen.

The full decipherment, line by line with the glosses, is in bremen_decoded.txt. The remaining errors are transcription slips from the phone photographs (occupireh, feldlsihen); no group is left undecrypted.

R4330 (27 Nov 1631) and R4331 (2? Dec 1631) read in part

Two German letters of Johann Friedrich, archbishop of Bremen, to Johan Adler Salvius, Swedish resident at Hamburg, pressing for Swedish succour (foot, dragoons, 'etliche tausend') against Gronsfeld's and Bönninghausen's imperial/League troops in Westphalia; he fears the Lüneburg country will be ravaged and asks that the Stift be included in the accord if the Neuenburg garrison marches out. Both letters are in one file each (R4330 and R4331 image sections).

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (93% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

hand, and do not let our hope sink greatly; also that Tilly's [men] in [...] be not made bolder. And we remain
graciously disposed to you with all grace and especially well-inclined goodwill. Given in our town of Bremen, 2 Dec.
Anno 1631. Friedrich.

### R4331, postscript

Could Salvius also, since he [...] anyway, [...] [?go] to Celle, [and] so dispose and direct the work with Duke Christian
and Duke Georg that we [?therefore] in that quarter not only may expect all good assistance and have cause to rejoice,
but also may surely obtain that the enemy, where [he would come] [?down] from Neuenburg and otherwise over the Aller,
through the land of Lüneburg, [be] granted passage of war, [but rather be] [?blocked], [lest] from there harm of war
[82] and danger befall and meet us and also Minden, [?all the less] if the garrison of Neuenburg should march out on
accord, [?it be] not allowed to come into our archbishopric, but rather that we be included under the accord on that
account, we would graciously [?like it]. And it would be especially dear and agreeable to us, and we have therefore
graciously [?to desire] in this, as we also do not doubt that you will take it especially to heart to promote it.
Otherwise it is also our [?gracious] wish that Salvius come as soon as possible nearer to [?Stade] and to us in person.
That will be done to our [?gracious] pleasure.
