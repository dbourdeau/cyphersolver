Rome → the League council in Paris · symbol cipher inside a clear letter · 23 August 1589 · read in part

A letter from Rome to the abbé d’Orbais, 23 August 1589

BnF fr. 3413 no. 62 · a secretary of Cardinal Pellevé writes to Jean de Piles, of the League’s council in Paris, as the news of Henri III’s murder reaches Rome · catalogued with three cipher keys bound in the same volume

None of the three keys fits · the cipher is the Nevers–Piles alphabet, filled out from a deciphered letter of 1586 · most of the ciphered words now read, about thirty signs are still open

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 18 September 2026 · updated 21 September 2026

Summary. The catalogue entry supposed that one of three keys bound in BnF fr. 3413, nos. 53, 61 and 69, might read the letter at no. 62. None of them does. No. 53 is a numerical key and the letter has no figures. No. 61 is an Italian key with a different set of signs. No. 69 is a League key in which the square sign is e, where the letter needs n. The letter is almost all in clear. It comes from a secretary of Cardinal Pellevé in Rome, dated 23 August [1589], and carries the news of Henri III’s death. About ninety signs are in cipher, in short groups. Satoshi Tomokiyo identified the cipher as the Nevers–Piles alphabet and read one name in it. His table comes from BnF fr. 3612, which is not digitised, and it is partial. It was filled out here from BnF fr. 4715 f. 2, a letter of 1586 in the same cipher whose decipherer wrote the plaintext above each line. The readings: Mons. Cassin, la protection, vostre regne, depeschera, mon maitre a esté retiré, and the signature, probably Baron. Status: read in part. The bound keys were tested and none fits. About thirty signs, most of them code signs, are still open.

01 The letter

Fr. 3413 is a Nevers-collection volume of League papers, on Gallica as btv1b52510705j. No. 62 is ff. 126r–127r (views 259–261), and the address is on f. 127v: A Monsieur, Monsieur l’Abbé d’Orbais, Coner au Conseil estably a Paris pour la Ste Union, A Paris. The abbé d’Orbais is Jean de Piles. He was the League’s envoy to Rome earlier in 1589 and later a deputy of the clergy at the Estates of 1593.

The letter is dated only “du lieu que vous sçavez ce xxiije d’Aoust”, but its content fixes the year. It reports how the news of the King’s death came to Rome. A courier from Spain had passed through Lyon on the 12th. The Grand Duke of Tuscany sent an express, which reached Rome on the 20th. It carried the Duke of Lorraine’s copy of a letter of 8 August from his agent in Paris, the sr Bardin:

le Roy a esté tué, et c’est le Roy de Navarre qui l’a faict faire, l’on a proclamé Roy a Paris Mons. le Cardinal de Bourbon, et au mesme temps le Roy de Navarre s’est aussi faict appeller Roy par ceux de sa secte

The writer apologises that his senior colleague, “son aisné de mesme charge”, has been kept from writing by a fever for a fortnight. He asks Piles to keep him in mind, and he signs in cipher.

03 The cipher, and a key from 1586

Tomokiyo, cataloguing the Nevers ciphers, noted that no. 62 “appears to employ the Nevers–Piles Cipher” and read Mons. Cassin in it. Cassin appears as Pellevé’s secretary in the proceedings of the Estates of 1593. His table for that cipher was rebuilt from Piles’s own letters to Nevers of 1586 in BnF fr. 3612. That volume is not on Gallica, and the table has gaps. The same cipher is used in BnF fr. 4715 f. 2 (btv1b52509819x, view 17), the Cardinal de Guise to Nevers from Soissons, 6 October 1586. That letter is a full page of cipher, and the decipherer wrote the plaintext loosely above each line.

The signature is the writer’s own name. In the key, 2 is b, and protection fixes the tailed m as o, so the name reads b a r o and a last sign. That sign is n if the name is Baron. A “Sr Baron” writes in the same League dossier of 1593 as Piles (Gallica btv1b9059406b, piece u, “Du Sr Baron, du 7 aoust”). That is circumstantial, and nothing in this letter confirms it.

The decorated square ⌘, and Ƒ, τ and ʃ, look like word or title signs. They do not occur in fr. 4715. “Depuis que […] mon maitre a esté retiré” suggests something like Monseigneur le Cardinal mon maitre. If Ƒ is le, the group after dedans deux jours would read le doyen. That is a guess and is not counted as a reading.

05 What remains open

  • The code signs ⌘, Ƒ, τ and ʃ, and what exactly will be “despatched” within two days.
  • “Il ne vous fault rien dire pour ce qui touche φ tt o V æ ʃ ÷”: vo…a…ts, perhaps vos faits, but the signs do not support that.
  • The two groups after a esté retiré. A pattern search over period French finds no reading consistent with the values above.
  • Some sign values differ between the two sources. The plain m is r in fr. 4715, but the tailed m is o here, and two squares (n and u) are told apart only by small differences of form.
  • The route to the rest is Tomokiyo’s source, BnF fr. 3612, which holds Piles’s deciphered letters of 1586. It has to be seen at the BnF.
