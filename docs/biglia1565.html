Biglia, nuncio in Vienna, 1565

Go to the full text ↓

A papal nuncio defends his handling of the Emperor’s demand for priests’ marriage in a letter that survives only in cipher; its key was rebuilt from the ciphertext alone.

The letter
Melchiorre Biglia, apostolic nuncio to Maximilian II, to a recipient in Rome who is not named (“V.S.”, probably a cardinal of the Curia), Vienna, 5 December 1565 (day restored). It is the second of two letters entirely in cipher in the register of his letters.
Where
Veneranda Biblioteca Ambrosiana, Milan, Trotti 22, fol. 7r line 33 to fol. 7v line 23. Scans by the Università Cattolica digital library, which carries no reuse licence; no image is reproduced here.
The cipher
Digits written without spaces, words separated by 00: one digit is a vowel or bare consonant, two digits are a row consonant and a column vowel, with codes (59 qua, 97 che, 19 per) and a small nomenclator (11 S.M., 21 Imperatore, 87 connubio).
How it was read
Key K5d rebuilt from the ciphertext alone, with no contemporary key or decipherment, and frozen. Held out: 252 words of letter 1 never decoded before; lexical agreement with an Italian word list 0.761, against at most 0.500 (mean 0.352) for 200 control keys, threshold 0.65 fixed in advance.
What it says
Biglia answers reproaches from Rome for a bad start to the negotiation, discusses the offer of 4,000 foot or 2,000 horse and advises that the Emperor send a person of his blood at their head, and reports a dinner with Doctors Zasius and Weber of the privy council about the connubio.
Still open
19 words of letter 2. Letter 1 (729 words, 94.5% read by the contributor) is not published here, so it is not counted.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée, with Claude): transcription, key, validation, reading and translation. Contributed by email on 29 October 2026 (branch dudout/bundle-spain-italy).

01 The letter

In late 1565 Pius IV had sent Biglia to the Emperor Maximilian II, who asked that priests in his lands be allowed to marry (the connubio). Rome refused and offered military help against the Turk. The Ambrosiana register of Biglia’s letters holds two letters entirely in cipher; H. Dengel, who edited the nunciature in 1926, prints neither. The neighbouring letters of the register (to Altemps, 15 November 1565; to the Emperor, 16 January 1566) are in plain text. Letter 2 is published here.

02 The cipher

A single digit is a vowel or a bare consonant (2 e/s, 3 u/l, 4 i/t, 5 a/n, 7 o/r); two digits are a row consonant (0 m/g/v, 1 c/p, 2 s, 3 l, 4 t, 5 n, 6 d/f, 7 r, 8 soft c/b, 9 gn) and a column vowel (1, 2, 8 e; 3 u; 4, 6 i; 5 a; 7, 9 o). Values taken from context (bare 1 = c/p, 6 = f, 8 = b) count as probable at best.

03 The reading

Letter 2 has 381 words: 313 certain (the exact output of the frozen key), 42 probable, 7 restored and 19 unresolved. Read by sense that is 362, 95.0%, exactly the bar; strictly certain plus probable it is 93.2%. The date is restored from the year: “di decembre del sesanta” is read, the day is not. The sentences hold with holes, so the text is coherent but raw. A nomenclator value counts as certain only with three independent contexts; S.M. (11) designates the Emperor.

04 What remains uncertain

  • 19 unresolved words in 7r ll. 38–41 and 7v ll. 10–23.
  • The recipient, restored as V.S. (code 45), and the day of the date.
  • Letter 1 (fols 6v–7r l. 27): 729 words, 604 certain, 68 probable, 17 restored, 40 unresolved by the contributor’s count; its text is not published, so the target stays partial.

Letter 2, Vienna, [5] December 1565 (fol. 7r l. 33 - 7v l. 23)

Biglia answers reproaches for a bad start to the negotiation that offended the Emperor; the offer of 4,000 foot or 2,000 horse; a person of the Emperor's blood as their head; Zasius and Weber of the privy council dine with him and speak of the connubio.
