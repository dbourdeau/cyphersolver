Rome → Charles V and Gattinara · three-letter code with a homophonic alphabet of cursive signs · May 1523 – March 1524

Lope Hurtado de Mendoza at Rome to Charles V and Gattinara, 1523–24

Go to the full text ↓

Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid, Colección Salazar y Castro 9/28 (A-28) and 9/30 (A-30); DECODE R9667, R9683, R9695, R9846, R9866, R9867, R9868, R9869. The catalogue listed them as eight ciphertexts of June 1523 to an unknown recipient, with no decipherment. They are six letters to the Emperor and two to the Grand Chancellor Gattinara, written from Rome between May 1523 and March 1524, in the system of Hurtado’s 1522 letters with a changed key. The key was recovered from adjacent plaintext, the clerk’s clear version of part of one letter; the part of that letter it covers is taken from the clear. After a sign-by-sign re-reading on the images, three letters of 1524 build sentences with it; the other four decode only into word pieces with unknown code groups in most clauses: they are decoded, not read.

The letters
Eight letters of Lope Hurtado de Mendoza, Charles V’s agent at the papal court, 1523–24.
Where
Salazar volume, DECODE R9667 and R9846 among eight records; Bergenroth calendared two of them (CSP Spain ii nos. 548, 617).
The cipher
The 1522 system: three-letter code groups and an alphabet of cursive signs, with the key changed between 1522 and 1524.
How it was read
The clerk’s clear version of the first third of the February 1524 letter (f. 133r), aligned against 38 cipher lines, gives 105 values, 65 confirmed.
What it says
Beaurain’s mission to Clement VII, the Pope’s wish to see France ruined or a truce made, the Emperor’s want of money, Hurtado’s unpaid salary.
Still open
The six uncalendared letters are one quarter to two thirds deciphered; about thirty code groups and look-alike signs remain.

02 The key, from the clear of 5 February 1524

The clear on f. 133r covers the cipher of ff. 130r–130v from “para lo de contribucion” to “el buen animo”, and every clause has its cipher counterpart. Most of the letter alphabet came out of spelled runs whose length matches the plaintext word exactly, with known code groups on both sides: contribucion, ayuda, menester, salida, pensar, determinacion. The alignment gave 105 values, 65 of them confirmed (grade C). It also corrected six words of the first transcription of the clear: señorear, poco daño, concierte, deudas, seguir, conservar.

The 1522 key is the starting point, and 25 of its code groups and the signs for n, e, a and o hold in 1524. Some values changed. tef is paz in 1522 and nec- (necesidad, necesario) three times in 1524. The sign L is i in 1522 and r here. So the 1522 key cannot be applied unchanged.

5 February 1524, to the Emperor (R9846, CSP 617)

The clerk’s clear, which the cipher of ff. 130r–130v matches clause for clause (grade C), with spelling and punctuation normalised from the line-by-line transcription r9846_clear.txt:

El papa ha holgado con la nueva de la venida de mossior de Beuvre; spera le con desseo, sera provechosa su venida aqui en esta coyuntura generalmente para todos los negocios y particular para lo de contribucion, que segun hay pocos dineros toda ayuda es menester, fasta ver si la salida del visorey haze retirar los franceses, que donde agora estan haze pensar lo que ha de ser, como quiera que la determinacion del papa es que bayan fuera de Italia. […] Dize me su Sd. que querria una de dos cosas: teniendo v. mad. aparejo, lo que mas dessea es que el Rey de Francia se destruya […]; no pudiendo ser esto, dessea que se tomasse assiento de tregua o paz antes que las cosas de v. mad. veniessen a necessidad.

The Pope is glad of Beaurain’s coming, since money is short and every help is needed until the Viceroy’s march makes the French withdraw; he is resolved that they leave Italy. If the Emperor has the means, what he most wants is for the King of France to be destroyed; if not, a truce or peace before the Emperor’s affairs come to necessity. The rest of the cipher (ff. 131r–132v) is not deciphered here; Bergenroth’s abstract no. 617 gives its content.

4 January 1524, to the Emperor (R9867; decoded, not read)

Wholly in cipher, with no clear. The date and signature are spelled at the foot, “de Roma a quatro de enero — Lope Hurtado”, eleven signs for l-o-p-e-h-u-r-t-a-d-o, all but one already in the key. About half its sentences hold; the rest break on open code groups (partial reading R9867_reading.md):

Mussiur de Beure [?dexa] al papa contento de lo que le ha dicho de parte de V. Mt. … No deve mostrar necessidad, porque […]emos […] que aqui son poco[s] para sacar dineros; y que estar sin [?dezir] aprovechara […] para que los buscasen los que […] dizen que no los tienen. … De Roma a quatro de enero. Lope Hurtado.

On 22 October 2026 the three letters below were transcribed again sign by sign on the full-resolution DECODE images (look-alike signs resolved word by word, corrections in <record>_corrections.tsv) and decoded with the key; their clear text with the cipher runs (in italics) now builds Spanish sentences, with holes at the open code groups.

28 February 1524, to Gattinara (R9866)

The letter builds coherent sentences but reads at 91.4% of its words, under the 95% bar.

Los franceses desean por maña o por fuerça quedar desta parte de los montes, segun el credito que [?an] [?allanado], en no los aver ofendido por esas partes los exercitos de su V. Mt., y el que sea perdido en no poder hazer nada.

Beaurain’s coming was timely, and Hurtado has briefed him. The French want to stay this side of the Alps; the Emperor must not grant peace or truce while they hold a palm of ground, and must drive them out: “que si no son vencidos, quedan vencedores, y lo de aca esta perdido”. Naples should be made to pay the army. Hurtado asks Gattinara to read a chapter he wrote to Soria and to help him get paid what he is owed.

20 March 1524, to Gattinara (R9868)

A copy of Hurtado’s letter of 11 March to the Emperor goes with it. The archbishop of Capua has left, and in his absence all business hangs on the Datary, Giberti:

A Su Mt. tengo escrito por otras la necesidad que ay de [?gratificarle], porque de lo passado era mal contento, segun el lugar que tiene. Tengolo por inconveniente, y cada dia me parece mas necessario [?cunplir] con el.

Naturalisations would help two or three of those closest to the Pope; the protectorship would be better with Colonna, whom Vespasiano “obedecele como al Prospero su padre”, and who could complain with cause if forgotten.

28 March 1524, to the Emperor (R9869)

The letter builds coherent sentences but reads at 92.7% of its words, under the 95% bar.

The Pope has sent Beaurain money proposals and told Hurtado he would fulfil the rest, pleading his need; Florence has not yet decided; Fuenterrabía is held lost by the French. If the archbishop of Capua finds the army ready to sustain the war, his report will match the Pope’s offer. “La voluntad y palabras del papa no pare[ce] que hera[n] mejores […] […]. Quiera Dios que tales sean las obras.” Easter news, Hungary and the Turk are in the clear.

June and July 1523 (R9683, R9695)

Both letters are mostly in the clear, and little of their cipher lines is deciphered. R9683 says the Pope has been well disposed since the arrest of Cardinal Soderini of Volterra, and asks the Emperor to restore the benefices of the Pope’s chamberlain and of Francisco and to pay what is owed to the Pope. R9695, an autograph draft of 8 July, reports that the rumoured poisoning could not be verified, that the Duke of Camerino’s men are still in prison and that the Pope eats apart, served by old servants; it asks naturalisations for the Pope’s intimates.

04 What remains open

  • About thirty code groups, among them rub, ʃug, zid, tol, tid, zib, xer, xir, xal, ʃal, yab, tas, toc, yic: they leave the holes in R9866, R9868 and R9869 and most of R9867 unread. There is no crib for them.
  • R9667, R9683 and R9695 (1523): decoded, not read. The 1523 hand uses letter signs of the 1522 notation that are not in the 1524 key, and there is no 1523 crib; R9667’s contemporary decipherment, from which Bergenroth worked, is not imaged.
  • Look-alike signs: ∂ and barred ∂, two- and three-bar crosses, and a barred m that is f in some places and c in others. Most were resolved word by word on the full-resolution crops on 22 October 2026; a few stay ambiguous.
  • R9846 ff. 131r–132v: not deciphered here. The rest of the clerk’s clear is probably on f. 133v, which is not imaged; the RAH Biblioteca Digital copy of Salazar A-30 would give more of the key.
  • R9667: no crib. Its content is known only through CSP 548.
  • R9683: the docket’s sender reading and the date line (“[x]vi de junio” against the docket’s “diez de junio”) are not settled.
