Rome → Charles V and Gattinara · three-letter code with a homophonic alphabet of cursive signs · May 1523 – March 1524 · read in part

Lope Hurtado de Mendoza at Rome, 1523–24 — the key rebuilt, six letters read in part

Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid, Colección Salazar y Castro 9/28 (A-28) and 9/30 (A-30); DECODE R9667, R9683, R9695, R9846, R9866, R9867, R9868, R9869. The catalogue listed these as eight unread ciphertexts of June 1523 to an unknown recipient. They are five letters to the Emperor and two to the Grand Chancellor Gattinara, written from Rome between May 1523 and March 1524, in the cipher of Hurtado’s 1522 letters with a changed key.

Summary. Lope Hurtado de Mendoza was Charles V’s agent at the court of Adrian VI and then of Clement VII. His 1522 letters, bound in the Salazar volume before these, were broken on this site from clear versions bound beside them (Lope Hurtado 1522). The eight records of 1523–24 use the same system: three-letter code groups (ton que, zar de, rab vuestra magestad, taf papa) and an alphabet of cursive signs with several signs per letter. Two of them are not new: Bergenroth calendared R9667 (5 May 1523) as CSP Spain ii no. 548 and R9846 (3 and 5 February 1524) as no. 617, from contemporary decipherings. The clerk’s clear version of the first third of the February letter is bound with it on f. 133r. Aligned clause by clause against 38 cipher lines, it gives 105 values, 65 of them confirmed, and shows that the key changed between 1522 and 1524. With that key the six uncalendared letters are read in part: from about a quarter of a letter to about two thirds. They concern Beaurain’s mission to Clement VII, the Pope’s wish to see France ruined or a truce made, the Emperor’s want of money, the departure of the archbishop of Capua, the Datary Giberti, and Hurtado’s unpaid salary. About thirty code groups and a set of look-alike signs stay open.

The Calendar check went through Bergenroth’s CSP Spain vol. 2, pp. 547–709, by the Salazar folio numbers he prints. Only ff. 6–9 of A-28 (no. 548) and ff. 130–133 of A-30 (no. 617) are calendared. The other six letters are not.

R9667 has a leaf headed “Claro” bound beside it, which looks like a crib. It is not one. The leaf is imaged mirrored, and its docket reads “Del abbad de Najera a dos de mayo”: it is the clear of the Abbot of Nájera’s letter of 2 May 1523, not of Hurtado’s of 5 May. For R9667 the content comes from Bergenroth’s abstract, which he made from a contemporary deciphering that is not among the images.

02 The key, from the clear of 5 February 1524

The clear on f. 133r covers the cipher of ff. 130r–130v from “para lo de contribucion” to “el buen animo”, and every clause has its cipher counterpart. Most of the letter alphabet came out of spelled runs whose length matches the plaintext word exactly, with known code groups on both sides: contribucion, ayuda, menester, salida, pensar, determinacion. The alignment gave 105 values, 65 of them confirmed (grade C). It also corrected six words of the first reading of the clear: señorear, poco daño, concierte, deudas, seguir, conservar.

The 1522 key is the starting point, and 25 of its code groups and the signs for n, e, a and o hold in 1524. Some values changed. tef is paz in 1522 and nec- (necesidad, necesario) three times in 1524. The sign L is i in 1522 and r here. So the 1522 key cannot be applied unchanged.

5 February 1524, to the Emperor (R9846, CSP 617)

The clerk’s clear, which the cipher of ff. 130r–130v matches clause for clause (grade C):

El papa ha holgado con la nueva de la venida de mossior de Beuvre; spera le con desseo, sera provechosa su venida aqui en esta coyuntura generalmente para todos los negocios y particular para lo de contribucion, que segun hay pocos dineros toda ayuda es menester, fasta ver si la salida del visorey haze retirar los franceses, que donde agora estan haze pensar lo que ha de ser, como quiera que la determinacion del papa es que bayan fuera de Italia. […] Dize me su Sd. que querria una de dos cosas: teniendo v. mad. aparejo, lo que mas dessea es que el Rey de Francia se destruya […]; no pudiendo ser esto, dessea que se tomasse assiento de tregua o paz antes que las cosas de v. mad. veniessen a necessidad.

The Pope is glad of Beaurain’s coming, since money is short and every help is needed until the Viceroy’s march makes the French withdraw; he is resolved that they leave Italy. If the Emperor has the means, what he most wants is for the King of France to be destroyed; if not, a truce or peace before the Emperor’s affairs come to necessity. The rest of the cipher (ff. 131r–132v) is not read here; Bergenroth’s abstract no. 617 gives its content.

4 January 1524, to the Emperor (R9867)

Wholly in cipher, with no clear. The date and signature are spelled at the foot, “de Roma a quatro de enero — Lope Hurtado”, eleven signs for l-o-p-e-h-u-r-t-a-d-o, all but one already in the key.

Mussiur de Beure […] al papa contento de lo que le ha dicho de parte de V. Md. […] el arçobispo con el […]. V. Md. no deve mostrar necesidad, porque […] poco para sacar dineros […] para que los buscasen los que […] no los tienen. […] suplico mande proveer […] de lo que se me deve […]. De Roma a quatro de enero. Lope Hurtado.

28 February 1524, to Gattinara (R9866)

Beaurain’s coming was timely, and Hurtado has briefed him. The French want, by craft or force, to stay on this side of the Alps; the cipher runs read “en no los aver ofendido por esas partes”, “los exercitos de su Mt.”, “en no poder hazer nada”. The Emperor must not lose the world’s respect now that the French are leaving, since he spends so much on the war. Hurtado asks Gattinara to read a chapter he wrote to Soria and to help him get paid what he is owed.

20 March 1524, to Gattinara (R9868)

A copy of Hurtado’s letter of 11 March to the Emperor. The archbishop of Capua has left, and in his absence all business hangs on the Datary, Giberti, who stands as close to the Pope as anyone can. The cipher reads “porque de lo passado era mal contento, segun el lugar que tiene”, “tengolo por inconveniente” and “cada dia me parece mas necesario cunplir con el”: the Datary must be satisfied. Benefices have been shared among the cardinals, as the King of France did with his. The long cipher passage on f. 430v about a valiant man with many sons and nephews not in the Emperor’s pay is not read.

28 March 1524, to the Emperor (R9869)

The Pope has sent Beaurain money proposals, told Hurtado he would fulfil the rest, and pleaded his need; Florence has not yet decided. If the archbishop of Capua finds the army ready to sustain the war, his report will match the Pope’s offer, and the Emperor should see that he is well received. “The Pope’s goodwill and words could not be better; God grant that the deeds be the same.” Easter news, Hungary and the Turk, the Legate in Moravia and the Infante are in the clear.

June and July 1523 (R9683, R9695)

Both letters are mostly in the clear, and their cipher lines are hardly read. R9683 says the Pope has been well disposed since the arrest of Cardinal Soderini of Volterra, and asks the Emperor to restore the benefices of the Pope’s chamberlain and of Francisco and to pay what is owed to the Pope. R9695, an autograph draft of 8 July, reports that the rumoured poisoning could not be verified, that the Duke of Camerino’s men are still in prison and that the Pope eats apart, served by old servants; it asks naturalisations for the Pope’s intimates.

04 What remains open

  • About thirty code groups, among them xun, xor, zad (a person absent from Rome in R9868), ʃal, ʃub, and rub against yub.
  • Look-alike signs: ∂ and barred ∂, two- and three-bar crosses, and a barred m that is f in some places and perhaps c in others. DECODE’s images are not sharp enough to separate them everywhere.
  • R9846 ff. 131r–132v: not read here. The rest of the clerk’s clear is probably on f. 133v, which is not imaged; the RAH Biblioteca Digital copy of Salazar A-30 would give more of the key.
  • R9667: no crib. Its content is known only through CSP 548.
  • R9683: the docket’s sender reading and the date line (“[x]vi de junio” against the docket’s “diez de junio”) are not settled.
