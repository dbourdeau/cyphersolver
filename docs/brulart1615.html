Louis XIII to Brûlart de Léon, 1615 and 1617

Two royal letters to the French ambassador in Venice, partly in cipher; Satoshi Tomokiyo rebuilt the key in 2021; both letters were taken here from the decipherments written between their lines, checked against his key.

The letters
Louis XIII, countersigned by Brûlart de Sillery, to Léon Brûlart, his ambassador in Venice: one of 16 September 1615 (Poitiers) and one of 25 July 1617 (Paris). French, with passages in cipher.
Where
Bibliothèque nationale de France, fr. 18043 f. 83–84 and fr. 18044 f. 109–110. DECODE R2785 and R2786, both “partially decrypted”.
The cipher
Letter-like signs with two or three forms for most letters; numbers with a stroke for words and syllables; dotted vowels for the dukes of Savoy and Mantua; signs to cancel or repeat.
How it was deciphered
Both letters have a decipherment of the time written above most cipher runs. On 22 October 2026 the clear text and the glosses were transcribed from the DECODE images and every run checked against Tomokiyo’s key (2021); two unglossed runs were decoded with it.
What it says
1615: Mantua’s harshness to his rebels, the fear that Savoy will renew the quarrel, Marini withdrawn to Genoa. 1617: Venice and Savoy urged to end the war; the Grisons and the Venetian secretary’s expulsion.
Still open
Lines of spaced signs with no gloss that give no French under the key (probably nulls, as Tomokiyo thought), parts of four rows of the 1617 letter, and three cramped glosses of 1615.
Credit
Satoshi Tomokiyo (key and reading); the contemporary interlinear decipherment.

Until 14 October 2026 these letters shared an entry with Louis XIII’s letter of 1631 to Melchior de Sabran in Genoa, because DECODE lists all three under the King’s name. That letter is in a different cipher, solved by George Lasry, with its own page.

01 The letters

Léon Brûlart was French ambassador in Venice from 1611 to 1620. BnF fr. 18043 holds many letters of 1615–16 sent to him partly in cipher by Louis XIII, Marie de Médicis and Puysieux; fr. 18044 holds more of 1617–18. The royal letters are countersigned “Brulart”, by Brûlart de Sillery. DECODE has two of them:

  • R2785, fr. 18043 f. 83–84, 16 September 1615 (catalogued as the 15th). DECODE gives the place as “Poictyers[?]”, and the date line reads “Escript a Poictiers le xvi jour de Septembre 1615”. In the clear the King says he is still held in the town by the illness of his eldest sister (“ma soeur aisnee”), the court on its way to the Spanish marriages.
  • R2786, fr. 18044 f. 109–110, Paris, 25 July 1617.

02 The cipher

Tomokiyo’s key, “Brulart de Leon’s Cipher (1615–1618)”, is attached to both DECODE records and printed on Cryptiana. It has:

  • an alphabet of letter-like signs, with two or three different signs for most letters;
  • numbers with a small stroke (a virgule) for words: 2 amytie, 20 de, 35 en, 70 je, 97 pour …;
  • numbers with a bar over them for syllables and short words: 5 pre, 7 que, 18 roi, 37 vous, 30 tion …;
  • vowels with dots for princes: ö the Duke of Savoy, ä the Duke of Mantua;
  • a sign that cancels what comes next and a sign that repeats.

Tomokiyo notes that the cipher is not much different from other French ciphers of the time, although Brûlart de Léon left a treatise on ciphers.

03 The decipherment

Both letters carry a contemporary decipherment written above most of the cipher runs, probably made in the embassy. Tomokiyo recorded on DECODE what he could not read: in 1615 “some passages”, which “may be partially or totally nulls”; in 1617 “some symbols”, at least some of them cancelled. Until 22 October 2026 nothing of either letter was transcribed here, and the reading audit of that day classed both decoded, not read. The same day all four page images of each record were fetched from DECODE, the clear text and the interlinear decipherments transcribed, and every cipher run aligned with its gloss and checked against Tomokiyo’s key (texts/R2785_align.tsv, texts/R2786_align.tsv). The sign θ, not in his table, is a null. In 1617 the decipherer’s “incommoditez” is “inconvenients” in the figures, and one unglossed run, “en mesme temps”, was decoded with the key.

16 September 1615 (R2785). The King disapproves of the Duke of Mantua’s harshness to his rebel subjects, fears the Duke of Savoy will use it to renew the quarrel, and suspends his good offices:

Partant je commande au sieur Marini de se retirer a Gennes soubz couleur de ses affaires domestiques, pour ne donner aucun ombrage, faisant estat d'envoyer bientost quelque autre, peust estre [?Manjeaux] qui est a Rome, pour aller resider pres led. duc de Savoye et continuer a me tenir adverty de toutes occurrences

“Therefore I am ordering Sieur Marini to withdraw to Genoa under colour of his private affairs, so as to give no umbrage, and I mean soon to send someone else, perhaps [?Manjeaux], who is in Rome, to go and reside with the said Duke of Savoy and to keep me informed of all occurrences.” He is held at Poitiers by his eldest sister’s illness; the letter is dated “Escript a Poictiers le xvi jour de Septembre 1615”, the 16th, not the 15th as catalogued.

25 July 1617 (R2786). Venice should take the openings offered to end the war; the Duke of Savoy should use the French forces sent to help him “pour conclurre et asseurer un bon accord que pour faire durer la [?guerre]”; the Grisons have expelled the Venetian secretary [?Padavin], and Gueffier has reported on the Leagues.

04 What remains open

  • R2785: three lines of spaced signs at the foot of f. 83v and the head of f. 84r (about 60 signs) with no decipherment, which give no French under the key (probably nulls, as Tomokiyo noted); three cramped glosses on f. 83v (“m'est possible”, “l'Equidray” and one more) and the clear name [?Manjeaux], blotted at the resolution of the DECODE photographs.
  • R2786: most of f. 109v rows 9–11 and f. 110r row 1 outside the glossed words (no decipherment, no French under the key), and the figures 16 and 21 of row 8.
  • The other letters to Brûlart de Léon in the two volumes were not opened.

# R2785, Louis XIII to Léon Brûlart, ambassador in Venice, Poitiers, 16 Sept 1615 (BnF fr. 18043 ff. 83r-84r)

The letter is dated "Escript a Poictiers le xvi jour de Septembre 1615" (DECODE and the profile had 15 Sept).

## Reading

### f. 83r

Mons. de Leon, J'improuve fort la durete que mon cousin le duc de Mantoue continue a apporter au faict des subiectz
rebelles, comme n'estant en cela fonde en exemple ny en utilite presente ou a venir. Ceste rigueur et ressentiment trop
vif qu'il demonstre offence plus les princes ses amis et alliez qui se sont entremis de la composition de ce differend
qu'il ne sert au bien et advancement de ses interestz. Et si sur ceste occasion mon oncle le duc de Savoye prenoit
conseil d'user de revanche, et se prevaloir de ce pretexte pour renouveller la querelle, Je ne scay [?autre] quelles
raisons nous en pourrions divertir la Republique, qui a subiect d'en estre offensee aussy bien que nous tous qui nous en
[?scaurions] [?presser]. Elle faict, comme j'apprendz de vostre derniere du xxv Aoust, bien de rendre encores en son
endroict quelque office pour obvier aux inconvenientz de la continuation de ce mal entendu, qui serviront tousiours pour
tesmoignage de sa bonne volonte.

Quand a moy, j'ay delibere, recognoissant tant d'accroches du coste du duc de Mantoue [et] de celuy du gouverneur de
Milan, qui faict quelque difficulte de se joindre aux remonstrances que j'avois ordonne estre faictes aud. Mantoue, et ne
scachant encores ce qui pourra sur cela arriver de la part de mon oncle le duc de Savoye, de surseoir la continuation de
mes exhortations et offices envers les partys, tant par crainte et prevoyance qu'ilz soyent infructueux,

### f. 83v

qui pour ne juger a propos en [?ceste] conioncture de m'y embarasser davantage apres avoir si bien reussy en la premiere
negociation. Partant je commande au sieur Marini de se retirer a Gennes soubz couleur de ses affaires domestiques, pour
ne donner aucun ombrage, faisant estat d'envoyer bientost quelque autre, peust estre Manseau qui est a Rome, pour
aller resider pres led. duc de Savoye et continuer a me tenir adverty de toutes occurrences, qui [?m'est possible] [...]
sur celles qui se passent en [?l'Equidray], estant tousiours suspect que Marin prenne par ses dictz mouvemens, qui ont
este [...] fomentez par [...] soubz [...] d'en profitter.

Je suis encores arreste en ceste ville de l'indisposition de ma soeur aisnee, mais laquelle au dire des medecins me
pourra permettre de partir a la fin de ceste sepmayne et continuer mon voyage avec le bonheur que je l'ay commance,
cependant que le Mareschal de Boisdaufin pourvoyera de son coste avec les troupes que je luy ay laissees aux affaires qui
se presentent du coste de Soissons,

### f. 84r

esperant que mon auctorite sera en fin recognue de ces princes par amour ou par force. Je prie Dieu, Mons. de
Leon, qu'il vous tienne en sa saincte et digne garde. Escript a Poictiers le xvi jour de Septembre 1615.

Louis. Brulart.

## Notes

- The two lines of spaced signs at the foot of f. 83v and the first line of f. 84r (about 60 signs: θ, φ, dotted signs,
  letter-like a, c, g; texts/R2785_align.tsv runs 25-26) are taken as nulls and left out of the reading (23 Oct 2026,
  siblings pass): they carry no decipherment, give no French under Tomokiyo's key, run doubled signs (g g, ʜ ʜ, tt ʃʃ,
  g g ʜ ʜ ʜ) that French does not produce, and the clear text closes over them in one sentence ("... du coste de Soissons,
  esperant que mon auctorite sera en fin recognue de ces princes par amour ou par force"), the f. 84r line falling between
  "de ces princes" and "par amour". Tomokiyo's DECODE note takes them for nulls; the King's next letter in the volume
  (f. 87r-v, same cipher, fully glossed) has no such lines. A judgement from the text, not from a key: see NOTES.
- "Manseau": the clear name (long s; read "[?Manjeaux]" on 22 Oct) is confirmed by Puysieux's clear letter bound next, dated like it "x6" Sept 1615 (fr. 18043 f. 85v: "M. Mansau qui est a Rome"); not further identified. "[?l'Equidray]": the decipherer's word over the
  run after "en" (35 = en checked); not identified.
- Abbreviations expanded: Mons. kept; "vre" = vostre, "s.te" = saincte, "Mar.al" = Mareschal, "Sep.bre" = Septembre.

# R2785, Louis XIII to Léon Brûlart, ambassador in Venice, Poitiers, 16 September 1615

## Translation

### f. 83r

Monsieur de Léon, I strongly disapprove of the harshness that my cousin the Duke of Mantua continues to show in the
matter of the rebel subjects, since it is founded neither on precedent nor on any present or future advantage. This
rigour and over-keen resentment that he shows offends the princes his friends and allies who took part in settling this
quarrel more than it serves his own good and interests. And if, on this occasion, my uncle the Duke of Savoy should
decide to take revenge and use this pretext to renew the quarrel, I do not know [?any other] reasons by which we could
turn the Republic away from it, which has cause to be offended by it as much as all of us who [?could press] it. She is
doing well, as I learn from your last letter of 25 August, to perform once more some good office towards him to prevent
the troubles of a continuing misunderstanding, which will always serve as a token of her good will.

As for me, I have decided, seeing so many hitches on the side of the Duke of Mantua [and] on that of the Governor of
Milan, who makes some difficulty about joining in the remonstrances I had ordered to be made to the said Mantua, and not
yet knowing what may come of it on the part of my uncle the Duke of Savoy, to suspend my exhortations and good offices to
the parties, as much from fear and foresight that they will be fruitless,

### f. 83v

as because I do not think it fitting at [?this] juncture to entangle myself further after having succeeded so well in
the first negotiation. Therefore I am ordering Sieur Marini to withdraw to Genoa under colour of his private affairs, so
as to give no umbrage, and I mean soon to send someone else, perhaps Manseau, who is in Rome, to go and reside with
the said Duke of Savoy and to keep me informed of all occurrences [?as far as possible] [...] about those that happen in
[?l'Equidray], it being always suspected that Marini may take advantage of the said movements, which have been [...]
fomented by [...] under [...] to profit by them.

I am still held in this town by the illness of my eldest sister, but the doctors say it will allow me to leave at the
end of this week and continue my journey with the good fortune with which I began it, while Marshal de Boisdauphin, with
the troops I left him, will see on his side to the affairs that arise around Soissons,

### f. 84r

hoping that my authority will in the end be acknowledged by these princes by love or by force. I pray God,
Monsieur de Léon, to keep you in His holy and worthy care. Written at Poitiers, 16 September 1615.

Louis. Brûlart.

Paris, 25 Jul 1617

augmenteront de toutes parts par la continuation de la guerre, et en mesme temps perdent par consequent ce [...]
obtiennent par l'accord que nous pourchassons.

Je ne scay encores quel conseil aura prins le Duc de Savoye sur lesd. propositions, mais s'il refuse d'acquiescer a un
party raisonnable il fera grand tort a ses affaires, comme je luy fais representer, affin qu'il s'ayde plustost des
forces que j'envoye a son secours pour conclurre et asseurer un bon accord que pour faire durer la [?guerre] en ceste
opportunite. L'un peut estre [...] l'intention [...] aulcunement [...]. Je vous asseure [...] a me [...] bien
differend. Je [...] et vous [...]

### f. 110r

laisse [...] en son endroict.

Quand au faict des Grisons, si ces Seigneurs eussent defere au conseil de leurs vrays amys et ne se fussent laisse
abuser aux persuasions des ministres qu'ilz ont employe, ilz n'en seroyent venus a ces termes, et d'avoir veu retirer
hors du pays leur secretaire Padavin par l'indignation de ces peuples. Le sieur Gueffier est arrive depuis deux
jours, qui m'a deduict l'estat desd. Ligues, lesquelles commancent a recognoistre et s'apercevoir combien leur est
dommageable la multiplicite des alliances, sans que je me sois oppose aux poursuittes de la Republique. On faict office
envers lesd. Grisons, de quoy la Republique eust juste subiect de plaincte. Priant Dieu, Mons. de Leon, qu'il vous ayt
tousiours en sa saincte et digne garde. Escript a Paris le xxv jour de Juillet mil six cens dixsept.

Louis. Brulart.

## Notes

- f. 109v, cipher row 3: the decipherer wrote "incommoditez"; the figures give 2' m 18 ✕ ff m L 36 tt = i-n-con-v-e-n-i-ent-s,
  "inconvenients", which the reading follows. The figures 16 (car) and 21 (dict) and the run after them were left blank by
  the decipherer and do not make sense with the key's values here: kept as [16] [21] [...].
- f. 109v, rows 6, 9-11: no decipherment; row 6 decoded with the key (35 en, 74 me, s, 74 me, 29 te, m-p-s: "en mesme
  temps"); rows 9-11 decode only in the words given (l'intention, aulcunement, me, bien differend; the decipherer's
  glosses), the rest gives no French and carries θ (a null, as in row 5 "et" = e t θ) and φ (delete).
- Padavin: Giovanni Battista Padavino, the Venetian secretary in the Grisons; the cramped clear word (read "[?Padavin]" on 22 Oct) is confirmed by Puysieux's clear letter of the same day bound next (fr. 18044 f. 111r: "la retraitte du Padavin").

# R2786, Louis XIII to Léon Brûlart, ambassador in Venice, Paris, 25 July 1617

## Translation

### f. 109r

Monsieur de Léon, I made my intentions known to you so clearly in my previous letters, carried to you by express
courier, that I have little to add on receiving yours of the [?11th] of this month, except that I was very glad to learn
of the satisfaction and gratitude these Lords [of Venice] showed you for my declaration, so prompt and favourable to the
good of the cause they uphold, and that you were able to confirm to them what they wished: that I meant to settle this
[?single] quarrel as a whole and leave none of the parties behind or [?in the right] on so important an occasion. I hope
they will know how to make use of the advantage offered them to end this contention as soon as possible by means that
are to their honour and safety, in the judgement of those who have their good, and the public good, at heart in this
matter. But they will commit a signal fault against both if they neglect the said openings, fit to draw them out of a
passage to which they must have regard, [...] so far committed. That is why, by at least yielding to reason, they will
gain advantages of great weight: to make the general voice well-disposed and favourable to them, to oblige their friends
and those who have taken up the defence of their cause to help and protect them with the good offices and means they
have in hand, and to [?load] their adversaries with shame and blame, if they are capable of such salutary counsel,

### f. 109v

to which you must invite and encourage them; and [?this] greatly justifies the right of their cause and the candour of
their intentions. For prolonging the war [?increases] the public misery and calamity, and their own certain loss, and
there is no doubt that they [?would] gain from a prompt settlement, if they consider what they have spent on it so far
in money, men and reputation, [...] troubles [16] [21] [...] which will grow on every side as the war goes on, and at
the same time they lose in consequence what [...] obtain through the agreement we are pursuing.

I do not yet know what counsel the Duke of Savoy will have taken on the said proposals, but if he refuses to accept a
reasonable settlement he will do great harm to his affairs, as I am having him told, so that he uses the forces I am
sending to his help to conclude and secure a good agreement rather than to prolong the [?war] at this juncture. The one
may be [...] the intention [...] in any way [...]. I assure you [...] to me [...] quite different. I [...] and you [...]

### f. 110r

leave [...] towards him.

As for the matter of the Grisons, if those Lords had deferred to the advice of their true friends and had not let
themselves be misled by the persuasions of the ministers they employed, they would not have come to this pass, nor have
seen their secretary Padavino withdraw from the country before the indignation of those peoples. Sieur Gueffier
arrived two days ago and has set out to me the state of the said Leagues, which are beginning to recognise and perceive
how harmful the multiplicity of alliances is to them, without my having opposed the Republic's suits. Good offices are
being made towards the said Grisons, about which the Republic had just cause for complaint. Praying God, Monsieur de
Léon, to keep you always in His holy and worthy care. Written at Paris, 25 July 1617.

Louis. Brûlart.
