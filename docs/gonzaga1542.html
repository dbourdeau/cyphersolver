The letter
A letter of Emperor Charles V, countersigned Idiáquez, written at Montón on 11 August 1542 to Ferrante Gonzaga, his viceroy of Sicily, on the money for the island’s infantry and galleys and for La Goleta, and on the French threat to Perpignan and Navarre.
Where

The cipher
Each base sign is a consonant and a small digit above it adds the vowel; the digits 1 to 5 and 6 to 0 each run a, e, i, o, u; a sign with no digit is a single letter. A different system from the sign letters of 1549 (gonzaga1549), which has its own page.

01 The letter

In the summer of 1542 France declared war on the Emperor. Charles V, at Montón, wrote to the viceroy of Sicily, Ferrante Gonzaga, who had asked for money for the infantry, the galleys and the fort of La Goleta at Tunis. The answer is that none can be sent: the Emperor’s other kingdoms are as short or worse, and the viceroy must help himself by every means he can. The letter has a clear heading, a clear opening and clear links, with long stretches in cipher between them.

02 The key and its test

A base sign stands for a consonant and a small digit above it for the vowel, in two digit ranges that both run a, e, i, o, u; a sign with no digit is a single letter, and one more sign marks a doubled or palatal consonant, as in señaladamente and Perpiñan. The contributor’s solver reaches the same key from six starts and scores it −2.21 per letter under a Spanish model against −3.33 for shuffled signs; a key fitted on one half of the cipher agrees with the full key on 89.7% of the tokens of the other half, though each half has only about 350.

Checks made here, with the scans out of reach: the 40 rows of his key applied to his transcription give his decryption on every one of its 31 cipher lines, with no manual edit; and a list of about 85 common Spanish words covers 0.635 of the 1,114 decrypted letters against 0.300 at most for any of 300 keys with the same signs and shuffled output letters. The control is weaker than for a free substitution, because the vowels stay in place. A Spanish language model could not be built from this environment, so the project’s n-gram validation is not applicable.

03 The reading

246 words of the letter are in cipher; 6 are marked (a letter or a syllable the writer left out, or a wrong sign), so 240 are deciphered, 97.6%. One value, g with the digit 6 giving ha, rests on its four uses, all in haver; without it 95.9% read. In English: “From the spies and other reports that we have, it is understood, as you were [written] last, that the main effort of the French [is coming] to the frontier of Perpignan, and they also threatened on the side of Navarre.” The cipher passages join the clear text around them: after “you give us a particular account” comes “of the patrimony and its state, of the needs of that kingdom … the infantry, the galleys and La Goleta”. The contributor checked the content against Capasso’s account of Gonzaga’s government of Sicily (1906): the Emperor presses Gonzaga to pay the Count of Anguillara (Virginio Orsini), Cicala threatens to leave imperial service, the treasury is exhausted. The Spanish and English texts are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • No transcription was compared with a scan by the project. The contributor’s two passes agree on 94.4% of signs, each difference was settled on the scan, and a check of every line at full size on 7 November 2026 added two signs at line ends (707 signs).
  • Six words are marked: [g]randes, [?alli], t[a]n, e[x]pedientes, [?screbir] and amena[ça]van, where the writer left out a letter or a syllable or wrote a wrong sign; the day of the duplicate is written over a blot.
  • The places where the reading leaves the key are listed with what the scan shows in doubtful_places.md; the letter model used there is built from Don Quijote and knows nothing of grammar.
  • The chancery’s clear draft may survive in a Spanish archive and was not searched. The context (the war of 1542, the siege of Perpignan, Gonzaga’s offices) is from memory and was not checked here.

# Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga, Monzón, 11 August 1542: lectura

Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Modena, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera K, Karl V, sfasc. IV, cc. 109-110.

**Bold** text is cipher, and plain text is clear in the letter. Word division, accents and punctuation are mine. The long sentences are the letter's own. `[?word]` is a doubtful reading, and a letter in square brackets is supplied where the writer left it out or wrote a wrong sign.

El Rey

Ill.e Príncipe, primo, nuestro Visorey y Capitán General. Después de haveros scripto a los xiij del passado recebimos vuestra carta de xx de Junio en que nos days particular cuenta **del patrimonio y estado dél, y de las necesidades desse reyno, y de los [g]randes pesos que ay demás de lo ordinario, specialmente en el entretenimiento de la infantería, galeras y de la Goleta**, declarándonos que **no se pueden sostener sin remedio de otra parte**.

Lo qual todo havemos visto y entendido, y en avisar dello y tener lo assí particularmente mostrays bien el cuydado que teneys de las cosas de nuestro servicio y del bien desse Reyno. Y querríamos cierto **tener forma de poder remediar y proveer las necesidades de [?allí], como las conoscemos. Pero las de los otros nuestros reynos son de la misma manera o mayores. Y es menester que, como quiera que se pueda haver, se cumplan las cosas necesarias para la defensión, conservación y seguridad desse reyno, y señaladamente, entre las otras cosas, lo de la Goleta, lo de las galeras y entretenimiento dessa infantería, que es t[a]n necessaria, como sabeys**, rogándos y encomendándos quanto podemos que, como hasta aquí lo haveys hecho, os esforçeys a ello, y ayudándos de **todos los medios y e[x]pedientes que allá os pudiéredes ayudar, nos [?screbir]** lo que más os occurrirá y parescerá que se podrá hazer, presuponiendo que es menester y que no se puede dexar de cumplir con las dichas cosas **y que no se puede haver de presente ninguna ayuda de otra parte, por las necesidades que en todas ay**.

Y señaladamente os encargamos mucho la provisión **de la Goleta, por consideración del armada del Turco**, y que tengays muy grand cuydado della y de proveer todo lo que para la seguridad **della fuere menester**, como conosceys que importa.

En lo que embiastes a comunicar al Visorey de Nápoles con el Gallego, no ay que dezir sino que fue todo muy bien pensado, segund lo que a la sazón se entendía. Después haureys entendido lo que más se ha seguido, y del Gallego lo que el Visorey le respondió. Y estamos confiados que, segund las cosas sucedieren, cada uno hará por su parte lo que pudiere, como se os ha scripto, para **ayudar adonde mayor necesidad podrá haver**.

En lo de las fortificaciones de las plaças y tierras importantes desse Reyno, pues os tenemos remitido que hagays todo lo que os paresciere convenir a la seguridad dél, no tenemos que añadir más de encargaros que, dando en ello la mejor orden que se pueda, procureys que sea con el menos gasto de nuestra hazienda con que huviere lugar.

**Por las spías y otros avisos que se tiene se entiende, como se os scribió últimamente, que el mayor esfuerço de los franceses viene a la frontera de Perpiñán,** y también **amena[ça]van por la parte de Navarra**, y para hazer la defensión en la una y en la otra parte se hazen las provisiones que convienen, y sereys avisado de lo que succediere.

La paga del Conde **la paga del sueldo del Conde de la Anguillara** os lo tornamos a encargar **por las consideraciones que están ya scriptas**, y que en el cumplimiento della no aya falta en ninguna manera.

También os encomendamos mucho la paga de lo que se deve al Vizconde Cigala del sueldo de sus galeras, y tanto más causa ay para ello **porque se entiende que es solicitado para haver otro assiento, lo qual no querríamos que sucediesse por falta de ser pagado de lo que se le debe**.

De Monçón, a xj de agosto de M.D.xlij años.

Duplicada a [?xj] de agosto.

Yo el Rey

Idiáquez

# Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga, Monzón, 11 August 1542: English translation

This follows the Spanish reading, with the same holes. The long sentences follow the letter's own.

**Bold** text is cipher, and plain text is clear in the letter. A word in `[?word]` is doubtful, and a letter in square brackets is supplied.

The King

Illustrious Prince, cousin, our Viceroy and Captain General. After writing to you on the 13th of last month, we received your letter of 20 June, in which you give us a particular account **of the patrimony and its state, of the needs of that kingdom, and of the [g]reat burdens there beyond the ordinary, especially in the upkeep of the infantry, the galleys and La Goleta**, declaring to us that **they cannot be sustained without relief from elsewhere**.

All of which we have seen and understood, and in reporting it in such detail you show well the care you take of our service and of the good of that kingdom. And we would truly wish **to have the means to relieve and provide for the needs [?there], as we know them. But those of our other kingdoms are of the same kind or greater. And it is necessary that, however it can be had, the things needed for the defence, preservation and security of that kingdom be met, and above all, among the other things, La Goleta, the galleys and the upkeep of that infantry, which is s[o] necessary, as you know**. We ask and charge you as much as we can that, as you have done until now, you exert yourself in it, helping yourself with **all the means and e[x]pedients with which you can help yourself there, and [?writing] to us** whatever else occurs to you and seems possible, assuming that it is necessary and that the said things cannot be left undone, **and that no help can be had at present from elsewhere, because of the needs there are everywhere**.

And in particular we charge you strongly with the provision **of La Goleta, in view of the Turk's fleet**, and that you take very great care of it and provide all that its security **may need**, as you know how much it matters.

What you sent to communicate to the Viceroy of Naples through the Gallego needs no comment, except that it was all very well thought out, given what was understood at the time. Since then you will have learned what has followed, and from the Gallego what the Viceroy answered him. And we are confident that, as things turn out, each will do what he can for his part, as you have been written, so as **to help where the greatest need may arise**.

As for the fortifications of the strongholds and important towns of that kingdom, since we have left it to you to do whatever seems fit for its security, we have nothing to add. We only charge you, putting it in the best order that can be, to see that it costs our treasury as little as the case allows.

**From the spies and other reports that we have, it is understood, as you were written last, that the main effort of the French is coming to the frontier of Perpignan**, and they also **were threat[en]ing on the side of Navarre**. To make the defence on both sides, the necessary provisions are being made, and you will be told what happens.

The pay of the Count, **the pay of the salary of the Count of Anguillara**, we charge you with again, **for the reasons already written**, and that there be no failure in any way in fulfilling it.

We also commend to you strongly the payment of what is owed to the Viscount Cicala for the pay of his galleys, and there is all the more cause for it **because it is understood that he is being solicited to take another contract, which we would not want to happen for lack of his being paid what he is owed**.

Monzón, 11 August 1542.

Duplicate, [?11] August.

I the King

Idiáquez

## On the back

To the Illustrious Don Fernando de Gonzaga, Prince of Molfetta, my cousin, Viceroy and Captain General in my Kingdom of Sicily.

Duplicate of 11 August.

In another hand: From the Emperor, 11 August, 42.
