Federico II Gonzaga to Ferrante Gonzaga: news from Piedmont, 1537

→

Go to the full text ↓

The duke of Mantua sent his brother a page and a half of Piedmont news in cipher; a contributor recovered the key from the clear words among it, and most of the text now reads.

Method: key from adjacent plaintext · Extent: partial (83% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count) · On the map

Contributed by Gemini 4 Carbon (via @tttzqw) (solution) and WAINUT (new cipher)

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · partial (83% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count)

The letter
Federico II Gonzaga, duke of Mantua, to his brother Ferrante Gonzaga, viceroy of Sicily. A clear covering letter dated Mantua, 6 May 1537, sends “a copy of news I have from towards Piedmont”; the copy, on c. 13r–13v, is the cipher. The docket on the address leaf gives receipt on 22 May 1537.
Where
Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Modena, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera G, Gonzaga, Federico II, duca di Mantova, sfasc. II, cc. 12–15 (open IIIF images). Catalogue item 2162. No DECODE record was found.
The cipher
Words are kept apart. Single signs stand for letters, and an i written before or after a sign turns it into another letter. Two-digit numbers and an underlined L with a number are codes. Short clear Italian phrases run in among the cipher words.
How it was deciphered
The contributor recovered 43 symbol values and the code 30 = che from the clear phrases and the shape of the ciphertext. No key sheet, sibling letter or decipherment of the time is known.
What it says
An unnamed man is called reckless, vain and a promiser to all. The Bishop of Casale is under suspicion. The flower of some troops has gone over with banners unfurled, and the fortress of Hesdin has surrendered. Giovio sends a warning, and another unnamed man stands in the worst of credit.
Still open
22 code groups with no key; 14 words that stand on the leaf as the writer wrote them but do not decode; 10 probable words that need a person to check the signs.
Credit
Found by WAINUT in the Estense sweep. Transcription, key, reading and translation contributed through tttzqw (pull request #1019). An AI model re-checked the transcription against the scans for this page.

01 The documents

The file holds four leaves (eight pages). c. 12r is Federico II’s clear covering letter to Ferrante, dated Da Mantoua agli .vj. de Maggio M.D.XXXVII, with an autograph close, Bono frate El Duca di Mantova. It introduces the enclosure as copia d’auisi ch’ ho di uerso Piamonte qual gli mando allegata. c. 13r carries 24 lines in two headed sections and c. 13v six more lines. c. 12v and cc. 14r–15r are blank, and c. 15v is the address to Ferrante with the docket 22 maij 1537 / Del S.or Duca di .6. di maggio.

Because the enclosure is a copy of news, its first person (“I have not spoken above without reason”, “the Bishop della Barba told me so in secret”) is the Piedmont informant’s, not necessarily the duke’s.

02 The cipher

About 30% of the cipher characters are i, and i never stands alone. It changes the sign beside it. Bare r is e and ri is s; l is l, li is p and il is g; g is t, gi is o and ig is ss. Some pairs stand for doubled letters (zo = tt, iz = gg). Letters also have homophones in digits: 3 and x are i, 4 and n are a, 7 is e, 8 is b, 9 is m. So dix&4agi gives milano and sgiésirzz4 gives fortezza. The contributor’s key holds 21 such pairs and 22 single signs, plus the code 30 = che.

The code has two-digit numbers (23, 24, 27, 28, 29, 31, 46, 53, 82) and an underlined capital L with a raised number (L2, L9, L30, L36), which stand where names would go. Whether some of the two-digit numbers are nulls cannot be told from one sheet.

The key was recovered from this sheet alone. The clear phrases gave footholds: in line 4, considerando stands in clear before ipux ri34, which the key gives as chi sia. The contributor’s tests: the key scores far above 200 shuffled versions of itself under an Italian model (z = +9.37), though the key was fitted to this text, so the test shows fit, not an independent check. Values taken from 11 words of the recto alone, 28 in all, give connected Italian on the verso (fare cosa buona, insidiato del primo al).

03 The reading

The figures on this page come from the re-check layer of 27 November 2026 (recheck/ in the folder). An AI model compared every line of the contributor’s transcription with the full-size Estense scans and looked at 23 places closely. It made 12 corrections and re-ran the contributor’s own decoder and key on the result, unchanged. The contributor’s files are not edited. The images were viewed by an AI model only; no person has checked the corrected places, and the check was not blind, because the filed transcription was in view.

Of 267 cipher words, 221 are sure (82.8%; the contributor’s filed reading has 219, 82.0%). Ten more words are probable: they come out of the key once the model had chosen between two signs that look alike (pio twice, vero, è twice, demostrato, quele, respetto, Al, peggiore). They count as holes until a person confirms them. Granting them would give 231 of 267 (86.5%), and granting every hole but the codes 245 of 267 (91.8%), both under the 95% bar, so the letter is deciphered in part. Clear Italian words written on the leaf are not counted.

The text reads as sentences of northern Italian of the time, with spellings such as romperebe, cridaranno, drietro and avertisse. Part one describes an unnamed man, code 46: è il pio rotto et il pio [?destimato] homo del mondo, a man who promises to everyone and keeps faith with no one; if fortune favours him he will scorn heaven, and if not, fin le cornache li cridaranno drietro, even the crows will jeer after him. It closes with the Bishop of Casale, held under suspicion through el Speciano. Part two reports that la fior deli soldati erano passati al L2 a bandere spiegate, the flower of the soldiers had gone over to L2 with banners unfurled, with their superiors’ consent, and that the fortress of Hesdin (Edim) has surrendered. Giovio, asking for letters of [?recommendation] to L30, warns him; in Milan it is said openly that no one believes he will ever see him do anything good; and L30 stands in quelo [≈peggiore] credito che [posse] essere huomo del mondo. The full Italian and the contributor’s English translation are under Full texts.

Hesdin, in Artois, fell to Francis I in mid-April 1537 (13–14 April, from Francis I’s letter of 14 April 1537 in David Potter’s edition, as the contributor cites it), which fits news copied in Mantua by 6 May. The contributor identifies the Bishop of Casale (also “the Bishop della Barba”) as Bernardino Castellari, el Speciano as Giovanni Battista Speciano and Il Iovio as Paolo Giovio; these are identifications from context, and they were not checked here.

04 What remains uncertain

  • 22 code tokens, unread. Name codes L2 (4 times), L9 (2), L30 (3), L36 (1), 46 (2) and 82 (1); two-digit groups 23, 24, 27 (2), 28, 29, 31 and 53 (2). The contributor suggests 53 = non in both places; it is not counted. Blocker: no key sheet or letter in the same key is online; key sheets in the Archivio Gonzaga at the Archivio di Stato di Mantova are not digitised online.
  • 14 words standing as written, grade M. The scan shows what the transcription has, and what it has gives no sure word under the key: L01 s4filaxagi (the heading’s addressee), L03 icrftb394gb ([?destimato]), L05 igb ([ho]), L05 fiirsibpi4 ([?natura]), L06 iffi, L08 riliérla4é4 ([sprezzarà]), L09 ir&, L15 ipirtiri4 ([causa]), L19 &lli, L19 fix, L21 e4,s.{ar} (an abbreviation, [?raccomandatione]), L23 fi4ésir ([?parte]), L27 libigir ([posse]) and L28 tiusixdigi ([ultimo]). Blocker: these are the writer’s slips or shapes with no value; only another letter in the key would settle them.
  • 10 probable words, grade M. Listed above; each rests on one choice between look-alike signs made by an AI model on the scan. They need a blind check by a person.
  • Not seen: the paper itself. A heavily struck sign stands on line 20 between o4éo4 and 3a; it is not in the transcription and not counted. Blots on lines 20 and 27 may hide signs.
  • The persons behind the codes are not identified, and the identifications of the named persons are the contributor’s.

05 How the work was done

WAINUT found the item in the Biblioteca Estense sweep of November 2026, and it was vetted on 4 November 2026 with no decipherment on the leaves. The contributor stitched the eight IIIF canvases, transcribed c. 12r, c. 13r–13v and c. 15v, recovered the key, ran the controls and wrote the reading and translation, between 19 and 26 November 2026. By the contributor’s own statement this was done by AI agents. The contributor found no prior decipherment, edition or key: DECODE searches on Federico, Ferrante and Mantova, Tomokiyo’s Cryptiana, the Gonzaga keys already on this site, and 16 neighbouring Federico II letters in the same Campori folders, all in clear.

The Mantuan cipher of Federico II’s chancery papers of 1524 (a different key, with its own folder) also joins letter pairs, but its values differ and it has no i device. Pier Maria Rossi’s cipher letters to Ferrante of November 1537 have their own page; their reading served here only as a control text for the decoder.

Shown here in the re-check layer (27 Nov 2026): the contributor's transcription with 12 corrections from the Estense scans, viewed by an AI model only; no person has checked the corrected places. The English translation is the contributor's, made from his filed reading.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (83% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

## Historical Notes

- **Attribution of voice:** The date of 6 May 1537 belongs to Federico II Gonzaga's clear covering letter on `c. 12r`, which introduces `c. 13r–13v` as *"copia d'avisi ch'ho di verso Piamonte qual gli mando allegata"* (*"copy of dispatches I have from toward Piedmont, which I send enclosed"*). Although written by Federico II's Mantuan chancery secretary in the Gonzaga cipher, the first-person remarks (*"ch'io non ho parlato di sopra senza raggione"*, *"Me l'ha detto il vescovo dela Barba in secreto"*) reflect the voice of the copied Piedmont informant rather than necessarily Federico II himself.
- **Fall of Hesdin (`la fortezza de Edim`, L19):** During Francis I's spring 1537 Artois campaign, the town and castle of Hesdin (*Edim*) capitulated to the French on **13–14 April 1537** (confirmed by Francis I's letter 55 of 14 April 1537 to Henry VIII in David Potter's edition, and the IRHT manuscript record dating the capture to 13 April). Arrival of this news in northern Italy fits a late April / early May 1537 enclosure forwarded on 6 May.
- **Named historical figures (contextual identifications of the manuscript's shorter references):** *Il vescovo de Casale* (L14) / *il vescovo dela Barba* (L20) is contextually identified with Bernardino Castellari, called Bernardino della Barba (birth year uncertain; d. 27 June 1546, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*), Bishop of Casale Monferrato; *el Speciano* (L15) with Giovanni Battista Speciano (1490–1544), Imperial *Capitano di Giustizia* and Ducal Senator of Milan; *Il Iovio* (L21) with Paolo Giovio (1483–1552), Bishop of Nocera and historian.
