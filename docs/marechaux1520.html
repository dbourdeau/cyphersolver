Rome → Francis I · homophonic cipher of graphic signs · c. October 1534

Cardinal Campeggio’s articles for the French crown, c. 1534

→

BnF Français 3081 ff. 41–42 (DECODE R2322), catalogued as “French marshals to the French King, 1 Jan 1520”. Two pages wholly in cipher. George Lasry recovered the key and deciphered the first page (f. 41r) in 2022. The second page (f. 41v) had no published decipherment; his key was applied to it here and decrypts all of it except one symbol and one code group.

01 The document and the key

DECODE R2322 carries four images from Gallica: f. 41r and f. 41v in cipher, f. 42r blank, and on f. 42v the endorsement. The cipher is a homophonic substitution with invented signs and no word division. It has 20 letters with one to four signs each, plus signs for CON and ET. Lasry found that the same key also decrypts BnF Dupuy 265 f. 132, a letter of the bishop of Mâcon to the King of 29 May 1535. Mâcon was then ambassador in Rome. On f. 41v the key needed a few adjustments:

  • The three-stroke sign that Lasry lists third under H stands for Y: roy, iceluy, quoy, veoyant, moyennant.
  • The “Rg” sign is P, not T: penser, pourveoir, pouvoir, partie, persuasion.
  • A looped “4” stands for R: pour, pourveoir.
  • Lasry’s first “unknown” sign, a bold ʃ, is probably S: “par [s]es dictes persuasions”.
  • His second “unknown”, a cluster of strokes, occurs once, in “devers [·] et ses successeurs”. It is probably a code for the Emperor (grade M).

02 The decipherment of f. 41r (Lasry, 2022)

Lasry’s decipherment of f. 41r was published only as red overlays on three crops of the page. On 23 October 2026 it was transcribed letter by letter from those crops and checked against the figures of the DECODE image at seven places: one overlay letter was corrected (l. 7, “par armes”), two were confirmed on the figures (l. 22 “pacificque”, l. 28 “si esplore”), and one look-alike in l. 1 is left as a hole (texts/f41r_reading.md, with English). Spelling is the figures’; word division and punctuation are added. The page opens:

Articles iures par monsieur le cardinal Campege es mains de messeigneurs les cardinaulx de Bourgon, Loraine et Tornon.

Aiant moi, Laurens cardinal de Campege, [?par] longue experience cogneu quant de doumage a porte a la crestiente, et mesmement au siege apostolicque, la disention des princes crestiens, nee par divers desirs de augmenter leur seigneurie et dition.

Campeggio recalls the wars among Christian princes, which brought the Holy See and Christendom almost to the end; he has worked for a more stable and universal peace, so that Christendom might recover the provinces held by the infidels, fight “le grant Thurc” and root out the heresies of “ce temps si esplore et calamiteulx”. None of this can be done without the counsel, help and power of the Most Christian King. The sentence runs on to f. 41v.

03 The decipherment of f. 41v

Quoted from texts/f41v_decryption.md: words divided, spelling as the figures give it. [brackets] are restorations or a code group, and … marks short gaps.

… [tres chre]stien et serenissime seigneur Francois roy de France, la Maieste duquel a esté souventefois recherchée de contribuer a l’entreprinse de l’expedition contre iceluy grant Turc, qu’il a tousiours niée, le pouvant faire sinon que premier sa Maieste feust reintegrée et restituée en la duché de Milan, conté d’Ast et seigneurie de Gennes, desquels en avoit esté spoliée violentement et par force. Et pendant que sa Maieste n’estoit reintegrée en ses seigneuries, ne povoit pencer a une expedition si loingtaine ne aux chouses oc[c]upées d’iceluy grant Thurc. Par quoy, veoyant la volunté et grande envie dudit treschrestien roy n’estre si loing des armes qu’il ne les puisse reprendre, en maniere que la paix, qui n’a aucun lien ferme, … soit pour engendrer bien toust et prestement en Italie aultre plus grande fla[m]e de guerre que devant; si moyennant l[e]s … l’on ne vient a penser d’y pourveoir, ce que par grande experience se cognoist ne pouvoir faire sans contenter en quelque partie le dit tres crestien roi. Et pour ce, s’il plaist a Dieu me donner grace d’estre pape, suis venu avecques les negotiateurs de Sa Maieste aux articles ci souscripts et aux conventions par moy iurees.

Et premierement ie [s]aierai avecques toute demonstration, persuasion et exhortation que l’on pourroit user, ainsi devers [code: the Emperor?] et ses successeurs comme vers les detenteurs du duche de Milan et tout l’estat du dit duche, conte d’Ast et seigneurie de Gennes, que les dicts estatz et seigneuries soient rendus et restitues es mains du dit seigneur roi tres crestien ou des illustrissimes seigneurs ses enfans et successeurs. Et quant par ses dictes persuasions et voie …

Translation. The Most Christian King has often been asked to join the expedition against the Turk. He has always refused unless he were first restored to the duchy of Milan, the county of Asti and the lordship of Genoa, which were taken from him by force. Unless that is provided for, the peace, which has no firm bond, will soon kindle a greater war in Italy than before. So the cardinal, if God gives him the grace to become pope, has agreed with the King’s negotiators to the articles below and sworn to them. First, he will use every persuasion with the Emperor(?) and with the holders of Milan to have Milan, Asti and Genoa returned to the King or to his sons. The page ends in the middle of the second clause.

04 Method

The DECODE images were fetched with the project’s login. Two Claude subagents transcribed f. 41v sign by sign, one taking lines 1–19 and the other lines 20–37. They used Lasry’s key table and his red overlays on f. 41r as a reference for this scribe’s hand, then decrypted the transcription with the key. The adjustments in section 01 came from words that repeat across the page. The line-by-line transcriptions are in the repository folder.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Two further passes settled every doubtful place but two. In l. 12 the group after “chouses” is OCVPEES, “oc[c]upées” (C). In l. 15 a sign first taken for E is Lasry’s second M sign: “des armes” (C). Counted on the transcription, 1,339 of the page’s 1,341 letter tokens have a value (99.9%).
  • One symbol in l. 19 is outside the key. “fla[m]e” is from context (grade C).
  • The code cluster taken as the Emperor rests on context only (grade M). The value S for Lasry’s ʃ sign is grade C.
  • The scribe wrote one A twice in l. 16.
  • f. 41r, l. 1: “[?par]” rests on a look-alike sign (the figures give D, the look-alike of P).
  • The rest of the articles are missing: f. 41v breaks off and f. 42 is blank but for the endorsement.
  • The endorsement’s “marechaulx” does not fit the content, and the date of 1534 rests on the three cardinals named. Neither has been checked against a history of the 1534 conclave.

# BnF fr. 3081 f. 41r: Cardinal Campeggio's articles for the conclave (October 1534), English

Translation of `f41r_reading.md` (George Lasry's decipherment, figures checked at seven places). Same holes.

Articles sworn by my lord Cardinal Campeggio into the hands of my lords the Cardinals of Bourbon, Lorraine and Tournon.

I, Lorenzo, Cardinal Campeggio, having learnt [?by] long experience how much harm the discord of the Christian princes,
born of their various desires to enlarge their lordship and dominion, has brought to Christendom, and above all to the
Apostolic See: as each has sought to overpower the other by arms and each to defend his own, it has come to war and to
arms among Christians, cruelly, for many years. And some of the past popes have been forced to undergo and enter into
the same danger and expense of war, so far that the Apostolic See [sic: repeated] and Christendom were almost brought
low to the very end. Having many times striven against this covetousness, with those officers to whom it belonged,
being about divers princes, I have put all my study into raising and settling among them a more stable and universal
peace, so that, Christendom remaining at peace and the princes content within their borders, it might think of
recovering so many provinces occupied by the infidels, and of subduing and fighting the common enemy of our faith, the
Great Turk, and of rooting out the many heresies that run in these times so woeful and calamitous. Which things,
so very hard and difficult, cannot be done, not to say thought of, without the good counsel, help and power of the most
Chris[tian] king

(continues on f. 41v: "... and most serene lord Francis, King of France, whose Majesty has often been asked to
contribute to the enterprise of the expedition against the said Great Turk ...")

f. 41v

Francis I refuses to join a crusade against the Turk until Milan, Asti and Genoa are restored to him; Cardinal Campeggio swears that, if elected pope, he will press the Emperor and the holders of Milan to return them. Tournon became a cardinal in 1530 and Campeggio died in 1539, so these are articles for the conclave of October 1534; the page breaks off mid-sentence.

Sign notes from this pass (beyond key3x.png):
- "ⵌⵌⵌ" (three/four vertical strokes on a bar; key lists as H row 3) reads Y in context (ROY, ICELVY). Treated as Y.
- Diamond with underline (key T top) = T; "ʀʒ" = P (row 3), "ʀc" = D (row 2): the two are easy to confuse.
- "ɓɳ" with loop above = CON sign.
- "cɷ" = X (key X top).

# BnF fr. 3081 f. 41v: Campeggio's articles for the conclave [October 1534], continued: English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `texts/f41v_decryption.md`, the running text of f. 41v deciphered with
George Lasry's key. Same holes: `…` an unread stretch, `[x]` letters supplied, `[code: the Emperor?]` a code sign read
by context. The sentence comes over from f. 41r (`texts/f41r_en.md`); f. 41v breaks off mid-sentence with no
signature.

## Translation

… [most Chri]stian and most serene lord Francis, King of France, whose Majesty has often been sought to contribute to
the enterprise of the expedition against that same Grand Turk, which he has always refused, not being able to do it
unless first His Majesty were reinstated and restored in the duchy of Milan, the county of Asti and the lordship of
Genoa, of which he had been despoiled violently and by force. And while His Majesty was not reinstated in his
lordships, he could not think of so distant an expedition, nor of the things occupied by that same Grand Turk.
Wherefore, seeing that the will and great desire of the said Most Christian King is not so far from arms that he
could not take them up again, in such a way that the peace, which has no firm bond, … is likely to engender very soon
and promptly in Italy another flame of war greater than before, if by means of th[e] … one does not come to think of
providing against it; which, by great experience, is known not to be possible without satisfying the said Most
Christian King in some part. And for this, if it please God to give me the grace to be pope, I have come with the
negotiators of His Majesty to the articles written below and to the conventions sworn by me.

And first, I shall [t]ry, with every demonstration, persuasion and exhortation that could be used, both towards [code:
?the Emperor] and his successors and towards the holders of the duchy of Milan and the whole state of the said duchy,
the county of Asti and the lordship of Genoa, that the said states and lordships be returned and restored into the
hands of the said lord, the Most Christian King, or of the most illustrious lords his children and successors. And
when by his said persuasions and way …
