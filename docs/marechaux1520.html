Cardinal Campeggio’s articles for the French crown, c. 1534

→

BnF Français 3081 ff. 41–42 (DECODE R2322), catalogued as “French marshals to the French King, 1 Jan 1520”. Two pages wholly in cipher. George Lasry broke the cipher and read the first page in 2022. The second page had no published reading and is read here with his key.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 21 September 2026

Summary. The catalogue entry took its sender and date from the endorsement, “Dupplicata des articles envoyez par messrs les marechaulx françoys au roy”, and from the volume’s other letters of the 1520s. Shinsuke Tomokiyo’s cryptiana page on the ciphers of Francis I reports that George Lasry solved it in 2022. His key table and three overlay crops (dated 24 May 2022) read f. 41r: “Articles iurés par monsieur le cardinal Campege es mains de messeigneurs les cardinaulx de Bourbon, Lorraine et Tornon”. They do not cover f. 41v, a second full page of about 37 lines. Applied to that page, Lasry’s key reads it in full but for one sign and one code group. Francis I refuses to join a crusade against the Turk until Milan, Asti and Genoa are restored to him. The cardinal then swears that, “s’il plaist a Dieu me donner grace d’estre pape”, he will press the Emperor and the holders of Milan to return them. Tournon became a cardinal in 1530 and Campeggio died in 1539, so these are articles for the conclave of October 1534, not a letter of 1520 from the marshals. The page breaks off mid-sentence. One sign and one code group are open: 99.9% of the page’s letters have a value.

01 The document and the key

DECODE R2322 carries four images from Gallica: f. 41r and f. 41v in cipher, f. 42r blank, and on f. 42v the endorsement. The cipher is a homophonic substitution with invented signs and no word division. It has 20 letters with one to four signs each, plus signs for CON and ET. Lasry found that the same key reads BnF Dupuy 265 f. 132, a letter of the bishop of Mâcon to the King of 29 May 1535. Mâcon was then ambassador in Rome. On f. 41v the key needed a few adjustments:

  • The three-stroke sign that Lasry lists third under H reads Y: roy, iceluy, quoy, veoyant, moyennant.
  • The “Rg” sign is P, not T: penser, pourveoir, pouvoir, partie, persuasion.
  • A looped “4” acts as R: pour, pourveoir.
  • Lasry’s first “unknown” sign, a bold ʃ, is probably S: “par [s]es dictes persuasions”.
  • His second “unknown”, a cluster of strokes, occurs once, in “devers [·] et ses successeurs”. It is probably a code for the Emperor (grade M).

02 The reading of f. 41v

The text is normalised: words divided, accents added. [brackets] are restorations, [?] marks signs not understood and … marks short gaps.

… [tres chre]stien et serenissime seigneur Francois roy de France, la Maiesté duquel a esté souventefois recherchée de contribuer a l’entreprinse de l’expedition contre iceluy grant Turc, qu’il a tousiours niée, le pouvant faire, sinon que premier sa Maiesté feust reintegrée et restituée en la duché de Milan, conté d’Ast et seigneurie de Gennes, desquels en avoit esté spoliée violentement et par force. Et pendant que sa Maiesté n’estoit reintegrée en ses seigneuries, ne povoit pencer a une expedition si loingtaine ne aux chouses oc[c]upées d’iceluy grant Thurc. Par quoy, veoyant la volunté et grande envie dudit treschrestien roy n’estre si loing des armes qu’il ne les puisse reprendre, en maniere que la paix, qui n’a aucun lien ferme, … soit pour engendrer bien toust et prestement en Italie aultre plus grande fla[m]e de guerre que devant, si moyennant l[e]s … l’on ne vient a penser d’y pourveoir, ce que par grande experience se cognoist ne pouvoir faire sans contenter en quelque partie le dit tres crestien roi. Et pour ce, s’il plaist a Dieu me donner grace d’estre pape, suis venu avecques les negotiateurs de Sa Maiesté aux articles ci souscripts et aux conventions par moy iurées.

Et premierement i’[es]saierai avecques toute demonstration, persuasion et exhortation que l’on pourroit user, ainsi devers [the Emperor?] et ses successeurs comme vers les detenteurs du duché de Milan et tout l’estat du dit duché, conté d’Ast et seigneurie de Gennes, que les dicts estatz et seigneuries soient rendus et restitués es mains du dit seigneur roi tres crestien ou des illustrissimes seigneurs ses enfans et successeurs. Et quant par ses dictes persuasions et voie …

Translation. The Most Christian King has often been asked to join the expedition against the Turk. He has always refused unless he were first restored to the duchy of Milan, the county of Asti and the lordship of Genoa, which were taken from him by force. Unless that is provided for, the peace, which has no firm bond, will soon kindle a greater war in Italy than before. So the cardinal, if God gives him the grace to become pope, has agreed with the King’s negotiators to the articles below and sworn to them. First, he will use every persuasion with the Emperor(?) and with the holders of Milan to have Milan, Asti and Genoa returned to the King or to his sons. The page ends in the middle of the second clause.

03 Method

The DECODE images were fetched with the project’s login. Two Claude subagents transcribed f. 41v sign by sign, one taking lines 1–19 and the other lines 20–37. They used Lasry’s key table and his red overlays on f. 41r as a training sheet for this scribe’s hand, then read the letters through the key. The adjustments in section 01 came from words that repeat across the page. The line-by-line transcriptions are in the repository folder.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Two further passes settled every doubtful place but two. In l. 12 the group after “chouses” is OCVPEES, “oc[c]upées” (C). In l. 15 a sign first taken for E is Lasry’s second M sign: “des armes” (C). Counted on the transcription, 1,339 of the page’s 1,341 letter tokens have a value (99.9%).
  • One sign in l. 19 is outside the key. “fla[m]e” is from context (grade C).
  • The code cluster read as the Emperor rests on context only (grade M). The value S for Lasry’s ʃ sign is grade C.
  • The scribe wrote one A twice in l. 16.
  • The rest of the articles are missing: f. 41v breaks off and f. 42 is blank but for the endorsement.
  • The endorsement’s “marechaulx” does not fit the content, and the date of 1534 rests on the three cardinals named. Neither has been checked against a history of the 1534 conclave.
