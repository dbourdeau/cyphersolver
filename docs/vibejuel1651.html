Peder Vibe (Copenhagen, later Bierreds and Flensborg) to Peder Juel, Danish resident in Stockholm, 1651 to 6 May 1655: eleven letters or pages in Danish with passages in a number cipher.

Each cipher word begins with an opener (1–3 or 24–39) that sets a shift; the other numbers are letters of a 25-letter alphabet, shifted. Two- to four-digit code numbers stand between the words, by the sense for people and places.

01 The letters

Peder Vibe wrote to Peder Juel, the Danish resident in Stockholm, from Copenhagen and later from Bierreds and Flensborg. The letters lie in the resident’s embassy archive at the Rigsarkivet, numbered in an old hand. Most carry a few lines of numbers between the clear Danish.

In January 1649 Vibe had promised Juel a new alphabet (“dett ny Alphabet”) and gone on writing in the old cipher meanwhile. The letters on this page are the ones in that new alphabet: N. 316 (image 77, a slip) and N. 319 (images 78 and 79 left), both undated, which the contributor places in 1651; N. 334 (6 January 1653); N. 332, the end of a letter on image 83, N. 331, N. 333 and N. 335; N. 336 (Bierreds, 13 September 1654); N. 143 (1654); and N. 167 (Flensborg, 6 May 1655). The letters of 1649 to 1651 in the same file are in the old cipher, a different key, with its own page: Vibe to Juel, the old cipher.

One letter, N. 319, is the way in. Someone at the time wrote small letters over about a third of its cipher words:

02 The cipher

A cipher word is an opener followed by a body. The opener fixes a shift k, and each number of the body is the rank of a letter plus k.

  • The alphabet has 25 letters: a to u without j and v, then w, x, y, z, ø (w = 21, x = 22, y = 23, z = 24, ø = 25). It does not wrap round.
  • The opener table is the same in every letter: 24–29 give a shift of 0, 30–34 give 1, 35–39 give 2, and 1–3 give 3.
  • In the body 32, 33 and 34 stand for 1, 2 and 3.
  • A long word may be cut in pieces, each with its own opener.

The first word on the figure, 3.7.8.21.5.4.23, has the opener 3, so the shift is 3: 7 is d, 8 is e, 21 is s, 5 is b, 4 is a, 23 is u. It runs on in the next line and gives “desbauscheret”, as the gloss above it has it.

Between the cipher words stand two- to four-digit numbers, a nomenclator: 73 of them in these letters, 49 different numbers. 872 comes nine times; in the letter of 1655 a new list runs up to 1771. None has a value from a key. In January 1653 Vibe adds two numbers to the list in clear (349 and 561), but the names he gives them are hard to read and are left out of the reading. None of the 49 occurs in the old-cipher letters.

The key is key.tsv in the repository folder, released by the contributor under CC BY 4.0. A short script decodes the transcription with this one fixed table and no language model.

03 The tests: one failed, one passed

Brice Dudout’s agents wrote each test down and hashed it before opening the letters it would be run on, and ran each once. (The order of events is by the contributor’s record: the pull request is a single commit and the timestamps are his own.) The score is the mean log-probability per character of the decode under a 4-gram model of old Danish; the bar set in advance was the best control plus 0.30.

Test 1 failed. It declared that one opener table, that of N. 319, held for the whole file, with a 23-letter alphabet. The held-out set was three letters never opened: N. 330 (image 74), N. 316 (image 77) and N. 334 (image 79 right), 54 words and 335 numbers. The decode scored −3.068; the best of 600 controls scored −3.281. The margin was 0.21, under the bar of 0.30. The set was not measured again.

The rule was then changed, and the contributor says so. After test 1, N. 330 turned out to be in Vibe’s old cipher. Test 2 was written with a weaker claim: a shift per word, with the opener table not given but fitted on each letter. Every control got the same fitting.

Test 2 passed. The new held-out set was five images never opened (six letters or pieces), all in the new alphabet (images 81, 83, 84, 87 and 90; 55 words, 339 numbers). The decode scored −2.713; the best of 300 controls scored −3.555. The margin was 0.841. The raw decode also gave connected Danish sentences, the second criterion. One word of image 87 had been deciphered in the head while transcribing, before the hash; that is declared in the frozen file.

What test 2 shows is that the letters are written with a shift per word. It does not test the fixed table of section 02: the table was fitted afresh on each letter, and the 25-letter alphabet with w and ø was settled only after the measurement. The fixed table rests on other evidence:

  • The gloss of the time on N. 319 agrees with the mechanical decode wherever it is legible.
  • The tables fitted blind in test 2 agree with the table of N. 319 for most openers on images 83, 87 and 90, partly on image 84 and not on image 81, which has five cipher words; the contributor's summary is “four letters of five”.
  • When the pull request was verified, both tests were run again and gave the same figures. The fixed table was then scored on the second held-out set with no fitting: −2.611 against a best control of −3.174, a margin of 0.563. Table and set had both been hashed before the set was opened.
  • Also at verification, the three letters of test 1 were scored one by one. The two in the new alphabet scored above their controls (−2.353 against −2.961, and −2.632 against −3.094); the old-cipher letter scored −3.980 against −3.125 and sank the set. On the final transcription the fixed table scored −1.987 against a best of −2.568 for 200 random opener tables.

The checks made at verification came after the fact. They are controls, not pre-registered tests.

04 What the letters say

The cipher words give Danish that reads with the clear text. But the subject or object of most sentences is a code number, so the reading tells what was done and seldom who did it. In the quotations, capitals are cipher words, {braces} mark a probable word, and #876 is a code number.

N. 316, a slip. “TIL AT FAA #876 SIGNET RET UDSTUCKET er fornøden att #355 TRYCKES #876 ORDINARIE SIGNET UDI LACK to eller tre gange del KIENDELIG. Daa kand man DEREFTER LADE GIØRE IT ANDET som kand were IUST EFTER DET ANDET, att alt kand were WFORMERCHT.” To have the seal of #876 rightly engraved, #355 must press the ordinary seal of #876 in wax two or three times, clearly; then another can be made exactly like it, so that all stays unnoticed.

N. 319. Some time ago #545 debauched the boy of #818, who took a big packet of letters. #485 took his leave with the valet of #254, to do his best “til att DESBAUSCHERE MIN FORIGE {TIENER} CHARLES, OM HAND KUNDE FORSCHAFFE nogle aff #872 BREFUE”: to debauch my former servant Charles, to see if he could procure some letters of #872. “Quo poterant ibant, quo non poterant ibi stabant.” Work is going on to intercept letters of #872 of importance.

N. 334, 6 January 1653. #353 leaves with the first occasion for #770, and #397 likewise. Vibe will say nothing of their instruction; something can be dealt with at a border meeting (“GRENTZE MØDE”).

Image 83, the end of a letter. Würtz (with the code #623 before his name) will persuade #454 that #770 has resolved something against #217. “Der sigis dog for wiss att HAND SKAL WERE NYLIGEN NOBILITERET aff #222 UDI #770 OCH FAAET GODS”: it is said for certain that he has lately been ennobled by #222 in #770 and received estates, so that one does not know how far to trust him.

N. 333. Since #222 is having so many fireships built (“BRANDT #791”), it would be needful to learn by what name or sign each can be told from the other ships.

N. 332, N. 331 and N. 335 are short or broken: a young lady Hedwig and courtiers (N. 332); Würtz’s credit in Sweden (N. 331); letters, copies and a report of #872 (N. 335), of which only single words are in cipher.

N. 336, 13 September 1654. Vibe asks what is decided according to the order #397 has received, and “HUAD #879 BREF SOM BLEF OPBRUT INDHOLT”, what the letter of #879 that was opened contained.

N. 143, 1654. He begs Juel, by their friendship, to tell him “HUO DEN ER SOM #879 TROER BEST”, who it is that #879 trusts most, and swears in French to keep it secret.

N. 167, 6 May 1655. A passage almost wholly in code numbers, 24 of them: #1590 has a design, which leaves the States General “NOGET PERPLEX”, somewhat perplexed, and something is to “LIGGE … UDI SUNDET”, lie in the Sound.

The contributor notes two guesses that the sense invites, 770 for Sweden and 222 for Queen Christina. No key supports them and they are not part of the reading.

05 What remains open

The count uses one rule. A cipher word counts when every number is legible and the word is Danish (certain, 132) or when one number had to be corrected for a stated reason (probable, 14). A word supplied from the sense (1) or unresolved (2) is a hole, and so is every code number (73).

  • Cipher words given a value: 146 of 149, 98.0%.
  • Read share with every code as a hole: 132 of 222, 59.5% (certain words only; probable, restored and unresolved words and every code are holes). With the 14 probable words it is 146 of 222, 65.8%. That is under the 95% bar, so the text is deciphered in part. The pull request gave one figure for both of Vibe’s ciphers together, 71.8%.
  • Two pieces are under 95% on cipher words alone: the end of a letter on image 83 (17 of 18) and N. 167 (12 of 13).

Open pieces:

  • The 73 code tokens, 49 numbers. No code list is in the file, and the gloss on N. 319 covers letters only. Juel’s replies, the rest of NKS 1299 e at the Royal Library, or a code list in the Juel papers could give them.
  • Two unresolved cipher words: on image 78, 31.34.26.13.20 (“bømt”, read clearly, glossed only “°m t” at the time); on image 93, 30.?.9.2.14=5.6.20 (a blot after the opener).
  • One restored word: on image 83 the cipher reads “hide” where the sense wants “hende”, her.
  • The 14 probable words. Each rests on a corrected number, a re-divided tick, a supplied letter or an unusual spelling, listed with its reason in the reading file. None was compared with the image by a second reader.
  • Prior art. The catalogue vetting found nothing on DECODE, at Cryptiana or in George Lasry’s lists, and the contributor found no edition of the cipher passages. Neither search was repeated when the pull request was verified, and the Royal Library and the Danish printed collections were not searched at length.
  • All readers so far were AI agents working from the microfilm scans online. At verification about 95 numbers of image 83 were compared with the image and agreed; no person has checked a passage.

Three letters of 1648 from a writer named Krag, in the same file, are in a third system that is not solved. They are kept as a lead, not on this page.

Secret passages of Vibe's letters from Copenhagen, Bierreds and Flensborg, 1651 to May 1655: a seal of #876 to be copied from wax impressions; a servant debauched to get a packet of letters, and work to intercept letters of #872; #353 and #397 leaving for #770 with an instruction, and a border meeting (1653); Würtz said to be ennobled by #222; fireships built by #222; an opened letter of #879 (1654); a design that leaves the States General perplexed, and something to lie in the Sound (1655). The 49 code numbers that name the actors are not identified.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (60% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

# vibejuel1651 - reading of the cipher passages in the new alphabet (Danish, spelling of the letters)

Letters of Peder Vibe to Peder Juel, 1651 to 6 May 1655, in the new alphabet ("ny Alphabet"; Arkivalieronline unit 261583, images
77-93). Read by sense from `decryption.txt` with `key.tsv` (Brice Dudout's agents, PR #410), after the measurements
(`validation.txt`). The re-reading of the held-out sets was done AFTER the measurements; the measurements were not run again. The
letters of 1649-1651 in the old cipher are in `targets/vibejuel1649/reading.md`.

Marks: CAPITALS = cipher words, certain (every number read, the word is Danish of the time); {X} = probable (one number corrected
with a reason taken from the image, or an old spelling; see the list); [x] = restored (word supplied from the sense, not counted);
<...> = unresolved; #211 = nomenclator code, not identified; lower case = clear text of the letter, abridged with "...". Word
division and punctuation are the contributor's. Spelling of the time kept (u for v, w for v, ff, hafuer).

## What the letters say about the cipher, in clear

- Image 62 (11 Jan 1649, an old-cipher letter): the new alphabet ("dett ny Alphabet") will be sent.
- Image 80 (end of N. 334, 6 Jan 1653): two numbers are to be added to the common nomenclator ("sub N° 349" and "N° 561"); Juel
  writes his numbers in letters ("dine bogstaffuer ... denotere tallet") and is asked to write t and k more clearly.
- Image 93 (P.S., 1655): the last cipher sent by Erich Krag had no code for the States General nor for the Øresund.

## Why the holes

The cipher words in letters read to 98.0%. What does not read are the 73 nomenclator codes (49 different numbers): no list of
them is in the unit, the interlinear gloss of N. 319 covers letters only, and only two codes are defined in clear (349 and 561,
on image 80, names read as probable only). Some invite a guess from the sense (770 Sweden? 222 Queen Christina?), but a guess is
not a value, so they stay holes.

# vibejuel1651 - English translation of the cipher passages in the new alphabet (Vibe to Juel, 1651-1655)

Translation of `reading.md`, letter by letter (the contributor's, PR #410). Clear and cipher words are translated together.
Marks: #211 = nomenclator code, not identified (left as a number); [x] = restored word; {x} = probable word; <...> = unresolved
word; ( ) = gloss or doubt. Guesses at the codes are given only as such, in the notes; none is part of the reading.

**Image 77, N. 316.** To have the seal (signet) of #876 rightly engraved, #355 must press the ordinary seal of #876 in wax two or
three times, clearly; then another can be made exactly like the first, so that all stays unnoticed.

**Images 78-79, N. 319.** Some time ago #545 debauched the boy of #818, who took a big {packet} of letters; #818 wrested it from
him and carried it {away} {secretly}, although there was nothing in it that could be {prejudicial} to him. Perhaps there were some
of #873's and #479's letters. The impudent #485 took his leave with the valet of #254, to do his best to debauch my former
{servant} Charles, to see if he could procure some letters of #872. Quo poterant ibant, quo non poterant ibi stabant. Work is now
going on to intercept some letters of #872 of {importance}, for which the wedding, or the Diet (Herredag) <...>, will give an
occasion ... so that one may find some cause to take his estates in #73.

**Image 79 right, N. 334, 6 Jan 1653.** #353 leaves with the first occasion for #770; #397 likewise with #353. I will say nothing
here of their {instruction}, since it will be communicated to you; it can be dealt with at a border meeting (grentze møde) ...
negotiated here, so everything is referred there.

**Image 81, N. 332.** #876 and the young lady (frøken) Hedwig, whether they too were among the {courtiers} who have done ...

**Image 83 left.** Würtz #623, creature and confidant of #376 #357, will persuade #454 that #770 has resolved #463 against #217. It
is said for certain that he has lately been ennobled by #222 in #770 and has received estates from [her]; whereupon one does not
know how far one may trust him here.

**Image 83 right, N. 331.** ... which informs me of Würtz's (#620) credit and esteem in Sweden. Meanwhile I only let you know that
#872 (sends) a {procurator}, #95, with a recommendation to #583.

**Image 84, N. 333.** Since #222 has so many fireships (brandt) #791 built, it would be needful to learn by what name or sign each
of these fireships can be told from the other ships; for there is no doubt they will be fitted out like the other ships.

**Image 87, N. 335.** I will not fail to let you know that {letters} by the new post have come to her from #872 ... what is found
... your and #872's relation ... #872 writes that #601 #397 shall have ... copies of ... letters of #872 ... that #397 ... that no
act was given in writing.

**Image 90, N. 336, 13 Sep 1654.** Let me know what is decided {with} him according to the order that #397 has received about it;
and also what the letter of #879 that was opened contained.

**Image 91, N. 143 / 321, 1654.** I beg and urge you, by our trusted friendship, to let me know who it is that #879 trusts most:
is he the worst, and the best {friend} of #872? I swear (in French) on the salvation of my soul to keep it secret, and I wish to
know it only to manage my friendships hereafter.
