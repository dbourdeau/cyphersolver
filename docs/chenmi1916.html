Tokyo ↔ Shanghai · Chinese telegraph code, the 辰密 key · 3–13 June 1916

Huang Xing’s 辰密 telegrams, June 1916

Four intercepted telegrams from Huang Xing’s household in Tokyo to the Hunanese group in Shanghai, in a Chinese telegraph code whose pages run backwards; Brice Dudout recovered the key from the ciphertext alone.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 5 November 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: complete · On the map

Contributed by Brice Dudout (new cipher with solution) and Satoshi Tomokiyo (identification)

The telegrams
Four telegrams from Tokyo to Shanghai, 3 to 13 June 1916 (N05, N13, N16, N26), 239 four-digit groups: Huang Xing on the North–South settlement after Yuan Shikai’s death, and Zhang Xiaozhun on a small payment.
Where
Diplomatic Archives of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, via JACAR: B03050089200, B03050089300 and B03050089400. Telegraph-office forms copied by the police.
The cipher
Chinese telegraph code numbers in groups of four digits. The first two groups of a form are in clear and name the code book: 6591 1378 = 辰密. N05 has no prefix.
How it was deciphered
The key was found by annealing, with no crib, on three telegrams; the fourth reads with it unchanged and the day code at the end of N16 matches the date on its form.
Credit
Brice Dudout, with Claude (mainly Opus 5.5): digits re-read on the scans, the key (published 19 October 2026), translations and context. Satoshi Tomokiyo, Cryptiana: the identification of the key as a renumbered 明密 code book; his section credits the formula to Dudout. This project checked the key with its own decoder.
What it says
Huang Xing declines to word a joint telegram to Li Yuanhong and the provinces and asks the Shanghai friends to take a firm line (N13), and Zhang Xiaozhun suggests a reliable division for the new president (N16).
Still open
Seven characters are probable readings resting on one digit or on the sense. The key is a reconstruction: no copy of the 辰密 book has been found.

01 The forms

When Yuan Shikai died on 6 June 1916, Huang Xing was in Tokyo, living at Aoyama Minami-machi 3-53, and his allies were in Shanghai. The Japanese police copied the telegrams that passed between them onto the telegraph office’s own forms, and the copies survive in a Foreign Ministry series of mail and telegrams of watched persons. Each form holds a grid of digits written as kanji numerals. Brice Dudout reads the grid in columns, right to left, each column from the top, and cuts the stream into four-digit groups.

The first two groups are in clear and name the code. Hsueh Chun-tu’s edition of Huang Xing’s 1916 drafts notes a code name at the head of each telegram, one per correspondent. On that evidence 辰密 is a code book issued to particular correspondents, and it needed its own key. The same files hold telegrams in two other numeric keys with their own pages: 容密 and 共密.

02 The 辰密 key

This key adds nothing to the number: it is the telegraph code itself, reordered. The code is laid out in pages of a hundred characters. In 辰密 the first two digits give the page, counted backwards over the numbers that contain no zero: 99 is page 00, 98 page 01, down to 91 = page 08, then 89 = page 09 (90 is skipped), and so on to 11 = page 80. The tens and units are permuted:

digit0123456789
tens3987102645
units19543267810

Example. 9950 4220 6219. In 9950, page 99 is 00, tens 5 is 0, units 0 is 1: 0001, 一. In 4220, page 42 is 52, tens 2 is 8, units 0 is 1: 5281, 興 (Huang Xing’s signature). In 6219, page 62 is 34, tens 1 is 9, and units 9 counts as 10, carrying into the tens: 3400 + 90 + 10 = 3500, 灰, the day code for the 10th.

How it was found. No 辰密 telegram had a known plaintext, and the 共密 and 容密 keys did not fit. Two features pointed to a reordered book: no 0 in the first two places, and a quarter of the text on three “pages” (99, 96, 93). Annealing a model with permuted pages, tens and units on three telegrams (N13, N16, N26: 201 groups) gave readable text, scored with a language model of telegraph-code characters. The page table it found followed the zero-free countdown, which was then imposed. Two wrong characters, 劈 for 力 and 滠 for 灰, both from groups ending in 9, gave the rule that 9 stands for 10.

As a renumbered book. Satoshi Tomokiyo, in the Cryptiana section on the mingmi (明密) format codes, reads this key, and the 共密 and 容密 keys, not as arithmetic but as one standard 明密 code book whose pages, columns and rows were rewritten by hand, as in a June 1916 edition he shows. In his picture the tens digit is a column number and units 9 stands for the tenth row, which is why xxx0 sits at the foot of the previous column. The two descriptions give the same table; the repository file key.md states the key both ways.

Dating. Dudout published the 辰密 page, key and four telegrams on 19 October 2026 (GitHub commit of the Wenmi repository, 15:15 UTC). Tomokiyo’s section is undated, the page was last modified on 24 October 2026, and Wenmi cites it as of 23 October. The section credits the formulas to Dudout (“He discovered the pages are numbered backwards, skipping any number containing a zero”) and adds the renumbered-book reading.

03 The checks

His checks. N05 was left out of the search and has no prefix, yet it decodes straight through. N16 ends with 灰, the 10th, the date on its header. The names that come out belong to people holding the right offices in June 1916 (Zhao Hengti, Tang Hualong). Every digit was re-read blind on the scans by Brice Dudout, without seeing the decipherment.

The check made here (20 October 2026). His verify.py reproduces the published raw texts from his digits and scores the 辰密 text at 3.77 against 200 random keys of the same form (best −1.59) and 200 shufflings of the digits (best −1.18). A decoder written here from the key statement alone, using the Unihan telegraph-code table already in this repository, gives the same character as his published text for all 239 groups. The 辰密 search itself was not rerun here. This confirms that the key as stated turns his digits into his text; it does not re-read the scans. Only the first dozen digits of the N16 form shown above were compared by eye, and they match.

04 What they say

Hsueh Chun-tu’s edition of Huang Xing’s 1916 drafts has nothing between the pieces of 8 and 20 June, and these telegrams are mostly dated in between. Translations below are Dudout’s, shortened.

On 3 June (N05, no prefix) Huang Xing tells Liewu that if the Shandong side wants him to take charge of unifying matters he may accept directly, and that Puquan (Zhang Ji) should go with him. On 9 June (N13) he writes to three friends in Shanghai that the telegram to Li Yuanhong, Cen Chunxuan and the provinces is very hard to word: if the positions are not unanimous the party risks looking divided, and if it writes as their telegram says people will see it as opportunism. “Japan’s attitude is very much wait-and-see” (日態度頗觀望); the friends in Shanghai should take a firm stand.

On 10 June Zhang Xiaozhun asks the same group to give the new president troops he can rely on (N16, shown above): Zhao Hengti, “always trusted by Li”, then training troops under Governor Tang, should form an elite division at his disposal, and arms “should be possible”. The last telegram (N26, 13 June?) is a single line: three hundred yuan to Baili, signed 準.

05 What is open

  • Seven characters are probable readings, four resting on a single digit and the others on the sense: 一不 in N13 is probably a slip for 不一, and 盻 stands for 盼. Nothing is left unresolved.
  • The key is a reconstruction: no historical copy of the 辰密 book has been found. The 1916 edition of the telegraph code is not identified.
  • The texts have not been found in the 黃興集 or in Li Yuanhong’s correspondence, which are still to be searched. The translations are proposals.

## N05 — 3 June 1916

Please pass on to brother Liewu: if, on the Shandong side, they want you to take charge of unifying matters, you may accept directly; ask Puquan (Zhang Ji) to go with you. From here we will help with all our strength. What do you think? Awaiting your reply. Xing.

Note: Read as a blind check: not used to find the key. Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N13 — 9 June 1916

To the three brothers Xiao, Yong and Feng: the telegram to Li [Yuanhong], Cen [Chunxuan] and the provinces is very hard to word. If our positions are not unanimous, we risk looking divided; if we write as your telegram says, people will see it as opportunism. Please obtain the agreement of the National Protection Army and have the draft written and sent from your side. Japan’s attitude is very much wait-and-see; the comrades in Shanghai should take a firm stand, and the Military Council above all must be clear. Please cable me how things have changed over the last two days. Xing.

Note: Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N16 — 10 June 1916

Please also pass on to Zu, Feng, Xiao and the others: unless Li [Yuanhong] has one or two reliable divisions, his orders will hardly be carried out. Zhao Yiwu (Zhao Hengti) has always had Li’s trust and is now training troops under Governor Tang; could you discuss with Jiwu (Tang Hualong) and the others how to have him quickly train an elite division at Li’s disposal? As for weapons, we will spare no effort and it should be possible. Yesterday Ke[qiang] cabled asking for the Protection Army’s agreement and for the draft to be written and sent on his behalf: please settle that point quickly. Zhun. The 10th.

Note: 灰 = 10 June, as on the header. Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N26 — 13 June 1916 ?

Please hand three hundred yuan to Baili (Jiang Fangzhen). Zhun.

Note: Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.
