St Petersburg → The Hague · three-digit codebook with marked digits · 8 March 1782

Johan Isaac de Swart to Pieter van Bleiswijk: mediation, the Greek project and the Armed Neutrality

Nationaal Archief, 3.01.25 (archive of P. van Bleiswijk), inv. 610 · DECODE R1036 · nine pages, 1,719 code groups. Sister record: Nationaal Archief 1.01.02 (Staten-Generaal), inv. 7408 · DECODE R1040, 29 November 1787.

DECODE lists no decipherment of either letter and says their codebook “could not be identified”. The 1782 despatch is in the 1765 Croiset codebook for the Russia legation, whose key survives as DECODE R1038. That key, 34 values recovered from context and 41 entries transcribed from the key scans decode 96.7% of the groups. No earlier decipherment was found.

Summary. Johan Isaac de Swart was the Dutch Republic’s resident at St Petersburg. Two of his ciphered letters are on DECODE with no decipherment: a despatch of 8 March 1782 to the Grand Pensionary Pieter van Bleiswijk (R1036) and a letter of 29 November 1787 to the States-General (R1040). Both are written in three-digit groups. A mark on one digit (two short strokes, an underline, a caret, a small circle, or a circle with a bar) makes a different group, so 340 and 340 underlined are unrelated. DECODE also holds the Croiset codebook of 1765 for the Russia legation with an authenticated partial transcription of its key (R1038). Parsed with the marks kept, that key gives 5,961 entries and decodes 84.2% of the 1782 despatch as it stands. Thirty-four further values, each checked against every occurrence, and 41 entries transcribed directly from the codebook scans bring the decoded share to 1,663 of 1,719 groups (96.7%). The despatch covers three matters: the French and Spanish answers to the Austro-Russian mediation, Catherine II’s “Greek project” for the young Grand Duke Constantine, and Prussia’s accession to the Armed Neutrality. The 1787 letter changes codebook part-way through, as Karl de Leeuw noted in 2000. Its second part decodes with the same 1765 key. The first 1,928 groups use another codebook, which is missing, and remain open.

01 The documents and the key

R1036 has nine scans. Two leaves are duplicate copies of the same passages (images 5419/5421 and 5420/5422). DECODE’s transcriptions of each pair agree at 96.7% and 99.6%, and they were merged, keeping the group that exists in the key where they differ. After merging, the despatch has 1,719 groups and 596 distinct ones.

The key is the codebook that Croiset prepared in 1765 for the Russia legation (DECODE R1038). Its values are words, syllables and single letters, so many words are built from pieces: Spa·nje, Dene·mar·ken, Enge·land. DECODE’s authenticated transcription of the key is partial, which is why about a sixth of the groups have no value on the first pass. The marks are part of each group. If they are dropped and the groups reduced to their three digits, unrelated entries collide and the decoding fails.

02 What the despatch says

The opening, normalised to modern spelling. Brackets mark a group whose value is inferred or open:

Waarbij ingesloten waren de antwoorden van de hoven van Frankrijk en Spanje op de propositie, welke de beide oorlogende hoven dezelve hadden laten volgen op de bewuste preliminaire propositie […], welke de Oostenrijkse minister zondag van zijn hof ontvangen heeft … Betrekkelijk zowel op de bewuste antwoorden als op dat van Engeland … bleef even zo min als tevoren enig middel over, waardoor de voornoemde oorlogende hoven [haar] mediatie van […] enige verdere werkzaamheid zouden kunnen geven.

(“Enclosed were the answers of the courts of France and Spain to the proposal that the two belligerent courts had made in reply to the preliminary proposal, received on Sunday by the Austrian minister from his court … As to those answers and to England’s, there remained no more means than before by which the belligerent courts could give her mediation any further effect.”)

The despatch covers three matters:

  1. The mediation. The French and Spanish answers are unwelcome to Russia. England insists on keeping the American colonies in some form, and Spain will not negotiate while the question of Gibraltar, or an equivalent for it, is unsettled. There is doubt whether the Emperor is dealing honestly with Russia (of nu den Keizer in dezen met haar oprecht handelt) or only means to amuse her.
  2. The Greek project. The Empress has not abandoned her project. For some time the young Grand Duke Constantine has been in the hands of a Greek woman and is being taught Greek, and she has lately sent orders for twelve Greek boys to be procured. She keeps silent about the project even towards her confidants, but de Swart does not conclude that she has dropped it.
  3. The Armed Neutrality. Prussia is acceding. The Vice-Chancellor has spoken with the Swedish and Danish ministers about the accession act. The Empress wants it signed pure et simpliciter, without the secret articles, and asks for a firm and unalterable law for the rights of neutrals. The despatch also discusses the Prussian minister’s report to Berlin and the King’s reply on the fourth and sixth articles.

The passage on the Emperor and the Greek project, normalised in the same way. Brackets mark a supplied word or an open group, and a doubtful word carries its grade: M uncertain, I inferred from the context or from the entry’s place in the codebook. Where the codebook has only an infinitive (stellen, zenden, observeeren), the participle is given.

Of nu de Keizer in dezen met haar oprecht handelt, [dan] (M) of hij daardoor maar zoekt om haar te amuseren en aan zijn lijn te houden, gelijk velen (I) met [grond] (M) veronderstel[len], is niet wel zeker te ontdekken; maar het is zeker dat zij tot nog toe van de Keizer zeer [gunst]ig (I) ingenomen is, en dat zij omtrent de bevordering van dit haar [favoriet] (I) project op Zijne Majesteit veel rekent en staat maakt. En tot bewijs dat zij het project geheel niet geabandonneerd heeft, zo dient dat, behalve dat zij de jonge grootvorst Constantijn, gelijk [ik] reeds de eer heb gehad in een mijner voorgaande aan U HoogEdelGestrenge te melden, in hand[en] van [een] Griekse vrouw al sedert een geruime tijd gesteld heeft, en hem de Griekse taal laat leren (I), en [verder] (I) een Griekse educatie (I) [hooggedachte] (M), zij heeft ook onlangs aan de veld[maarschalk] (M) Ru[man]zow (M) ordre gezonden om twaalf Griekse jongens te bezorgen, en het […]

Alhoewel het sedert enige [tijd] van het bewuste Griekse project geheel stil is, en de Keizerin daaromtrent voor haar confidenten (I) […] diep (I) stilzwijgen heeft geobserveerd, zo kan men doch daaruit niet besluiten (I) dat het bij haar geheel vervallen zou zijn; veelmeer schijnt haar retenue op dit subject veroorzaakt te zijn door de ontijdige [geruchten] (M), die daarover hier gelijk elders […]

(“Whether the Emperor is dealing honestly with her in this, [or] whether he only seeks to amuse her and keep her on his line, as many suppose [with reason], cannot well be discovered for certain; but it is certain that she is so far very favourably disposed towards the Emperor, and that for the furtherance of this [favourite] project of hers she counts and relies much on His Majesty. And as proof that she has by no means abandoned the project: besides having for a considerable time placed the young Grand Duke Constantine in the hands of a Greek woman, as I have already had the honour to report to Your Honour in one of my previous letters, and having him taught the Greek language, and [further] a Greek education, she has also lately sent orders to Field [Marshal] Ru[man]zow to procure twelve Greek boys, and […] … Although it has been entirely quiet about the known Greek project for some time, and the Empress has kept […] deep silence about it towards her confidants, one cannot conclude from this that she has wholly dropped it; rather, her reserve on this subject seems to have been caused by the untimely [rumours] about it, here as elsewhere …”)

This passage needed corrections to the key and to the transcription of the despatch, checked on the scans; the machine text does not have them yet. In the codebook the word stands to the left of its code, so 54 with two strokes is alhoewel, 314 with two strokes behalven and 941 underlined observeeren; the words to their right belong to the next column. 47 underlined is k, not l, which also gives Denemarken wherever the machine text has Denemarlen. The mark that DECODE’s transcription puts before 380 and 303 is a dash after the group before them, so they stand for van and die. The group after jong is 191, ens, written above the line with a caret. The group given as [dan] is 601 with a bar, komen; elsewhere the writer spells dan as 601 and 368 with two strokes, so the bar is probably a slip. In the codebook 644 underlined begins Veld Ma…, the rest lost in the shadow of the binding, and 339 underlined is faint; the officer is probably Field Marshal P. A. Rumyantsev, which has not been checked. The first line of the next page is cut off in the photograph, so the end of the Greek sentence is lost.

Three more groups were decoded from their place in the codebook on 30 September 2026. 738 stands between 736 graaf and 739 Gu…; its word begins gro…, and met grond veronderstellen, “to suppose with reason”, is the idiom (M). 299 stands between 298 fau… and 300 femme, with a faint F…ri…; favoriet gives “this favourite project of hers”, the usual name for Catherine’s Greek project (I; Februari, also possible from the scan, gives no sense). 231 with a circle stands between 230 verdedig… and 232 vereenigen, with verd… legible; verder, “further”, fits the list of things done for the Grand Duke (I). Still open: 77 with a circle, which the codebook spells Stans or Stanz (between 75 Staat and 78 Ster…) and which does not yet give a word before diep stilzwijgen, and the line lost at the top of image 5417.

The full token-by-token decoding is targets/deswart1782/R1036_machine_decipherment.txt, with every group beside its value in R1036_tokens.tsv. The machine text is the authority. The normalised passages here are editorial.

03 Method: values recovered from context

The key as transcribed decodes 1,448 groups. The rest were filled only where a value fits every occurrence of the group in the letter. Examples:

  • 881 = nje, completing Spa·nje each time; 340 underlined = mar in Dene·mar·ken; 311 underlined = land in Enge·land.
  • 176 with a caret = mpli, found twice in pure et si·mpli·citer.
  • 741 = haar, used twelve times for the Empress: met haar oprecht handelt, om haar te amuseeren, haare mediatie. 39 = hun, as in hun acte and hun onderdaan.
  • 681 underlined = mogendheden (de oorlogende mogendheden van Frankrijk, Spanje en Engeland); 363 with a circle = tusschen in tusschen·komst; 3 with a circle = stel in veronder·stel·d and vast te stel·len.

Each inferred value is labelled “contextual inference” in R1038_key_parsed.tsv, apart from the entries transcribed from the manuscript key. decode_1765.py regenerates everything.

04 The letter of 1787 (R1040)

Karl de Leeuw noted that the letter of 29 November 1787 is coded, “presumably by accident”, with two codebooks. The change comes at group 180 of image 5459, where the 1765 key begins to give dit alles. From that point to image 5474, the 1765 key decodes 84–90% of each full page. The text reports signs of an alliance between Austria and France, close attention to it from the Prussian and British representatives, news from Berlin, Kaunitz and the Bavarian exchange, and the likely positions of the four powers:

Dit alles, gepaard met vele andere omstandigheden, schijnen als indicien te zijn welke de hiervoor gedetailleerde [berichten] wegens de effectief, zo niet reeds gesloten, ten minste op het tapijt zijnde alliantie tussen de hoven van Frankrijk [en Oostenrijk] zeer waarschijnlijk maken.

The first 1,928 groups are in a different codebook, probably the one used under Rechteren. It does not survive online, though the Rechteren cipher filed as Nationaal Archief inventory 158 may be it. Three plain/cipher pairs from the same legation (R2032, R2051 and R2052, from 1784 and 1786) were aligned against it. Transfers from them cover only 2–3% of the segment and give no connected prose, so no decoding of it is claimed.

05 What remains

  • In R1036, 56 groups (51 distinct codes) have no value. A third pass transcribed the entries that DECODE’s transcription of the key left illegible, straight from the codebook scans. The word stands to the left of its code, and the parallel columns of homophones confirm faint words. It kept the 41 values that fit the letter, such as 781 = geheel (“het Griekse project geheel stil”), 748 with two strokes = commercie (“de vrijheid van de commercie der neutralen”), 488 underlined = Zijne Keizerlijke Majesteit, and 515 with a circle = twaalf, the twelve Greek boys. The groups still open are entries lost in the binding shadow of the scan, blank in the book, or too faint to use.
  • Inflections are sometimes uncertain where the key gives a stem only, as in hot for hof/hoven.
  • The first 1,928 groups of R1040 are not decoded, pending the Rechteren-family codebook.
