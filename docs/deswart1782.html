St Petersburg → The Hague · three-digit codebook with marked digits · 8 March 1782

Johan Isaac de Swart to Pieter van Bleiswijk: mediation, the Greek project and the Armed Neutrality

Go to the full text ↓

Nationaal Archief, 3.01.25 (archive of P. van Bleiswijk), inv. 610 · DECODE R1036 · nine pages, 1,719 code groups. Sister record: Nationaal Archief 1.01.02 (Staten-Generaal), inv. 7408 · DECODE R1040, 29 November 1787.

DECODE lists no decipherment of either letter and says their codebook “could not be identified”. The 1782 despatch is in the 1765 Croiset codebook for the Russia legation, whose key survives as DECODE R1038. That key, 34 values recovered from context and 41 entries transcribed from the key scans decode 96.7% of the groups. The machine decipherments split words into letters and carried open codes in most lines, and a check on 22 October 2026 classed both letters decoded, not read. On 23 October the corrections and a context pass were applied and the 1782 despatch and the second part of R1040 divided into Dutch words group by group: both now give sentences, with scattered open groups, but under the 95% bar: measured on the words of the readings, the 1782 despatch reads at 91.1% and the second part of R1040 at 88.8%. The first part of R1040 is in a missing code. No earlier decipherment was found.

The letters
Two ciphered letters from Johan Isaac de Swart, the Dutch resident at St Petersburg: a despatch of 8 March 1782 to Grand Pensionary Pieter van Bleiswijk (R1036) and a letter of 29 November 1787 to the States-General (R1040).
Where
Nationaal Archief, 3.01.25 inv. 610 (R1036, nine pages) and 1.01.02 inv. 7408 (R1040). DECODE lists no decipherment and says the codebook “could not be identified”.
The cipher
Three-digit groups for words, syllables and letters. A mark on one digit (strokes, underline, caret, circle) makes a different group, so 340 and 340 underlined are unrelated.
How it was read
R1036 uses the 1765 Croiset codebook for the Russia legation, whose key is DECODE R1038. Parsed with the marks kept it decodes 84.2%; 34 values checked in context and 41 entries transcribed from the scans bring it to 1,663 of 1,719 groups (96.7%).
What it says
The French and Spanish answers to the Austro-Russian mediation, Catherine II’s “Greek project” for the young Grand Duke Constantine, and Prussia’s accession to the Armed Neutrality.
Still open
The 1787 letter changes codebook part-way (as Karl de Leeuw noted in 2000). Its second part decodes with the same 1765 key; the first 1,928 groups use a missing codebook and stay open.

01 The documents and the key

R1036 has nine scans. Two leaves are duplicate copies of the same passages (images 5419/5421 and 5420/5422). DECODE’s transcriptions of each pair agree at 96.7% and 99.6%, and they were merged, keeping the group that exists in the key where they differ. After merging, the despatch has 1,719 groups and 596 distinct ones.

The key is the codebook that Croiset prepared in 1765 for the Russia legation (DECODE R1038). Its values are words, syllables and single letters, so many words are built from pieces: Spa·nje, Dene·mar·ken, Enge·land. DECODE’s authenticated transcription of the key is partial, which is why about a sixth of the groups have no value on the first pass. The marks are part of each group. If they are dropped and the groups reduced to their three digits, unrelated entries collide and the decoding fails.

02 What the despatch says

The reading of 23 October 2026 (R1036_reading.md): the decode, with the corrections of 30 September and the context values, divided into words group by group, in the spelling the code gives (antwoort, ontfangen, reekent). [?word] is partly carried, [word] supplied, […] unread, and a bracketed number is a group with no value. The opening:

Hoog Edele Gestrenge Heer! Ik gedraage my eerbiedigst aan myne onderdaenige laatste aan UW Hoog Edele Gestrenge van den 26 jongstleeden en 1 deezer loopende maand, waar by ingesloten waren de antwoort van de hof van Frankryk en Spanje op de propositie welke de byde oorlogende hof aan dezelve hebben laaten volgen op de bewuste preliminaires propositie, de courier welke de Oostenrijkse Minister Zondag van zyn Hof ontfangen heeft

(“I respectfully refer to my humble last letters to Your Honour of the 26th ultimo and the 1st of this month, in which were enclosed the answers of the courts of France and Spain to the proposal which the two warring courts had sent them following the known preliminary proposal, the courier whom the Austrian minister received from his court on Sunday …”)

The despatch covers three matters:

  1. The mediation. The French and Spanish answers are unwelcome to Russia. England insists on keeping the American colonies in some form, and Spain will not negotiate while the question of Gibraltar, or an equivalent for it, is unsettled. There is doubt whether the Emperor is dealing honestly with Russia (of nu den Keizer in dezen met haar oprecht handelt) or only means to amuse her.
  2. The Greek project. The Empress has not abandoned her project. For some time the young Grand Duke Constantine has been in the hands of a Greek woman and is being taught Greek, and she has lately sent orders for twelve Greek boys to be procured. She keeps silent about the project even towards her confidants, but de Swart does not conclude that she has dropped it.
  3. The Armed Neutrality. Prussia is acceding. The Vice-Chancellor has spoken with the Swedish and Danish ministers about the accession act. The Empress wants it signed pure et simpliciter, without the secret articles, and asks for a firm and unalterable law for the rights of neutrals. The despatch also discusses the Prussian minister’s report to Berlin and the King’s reply on the fourth and sixth articles.

The passage on the Emperor and the Greek project:

Of nu den Keizer in dezen met haar oprecht handelt, [?dan] of hy daardoor maar zoekt om haar te amuseeren en aan zyne lyn te houden, gelyk veele met [738] veronderstel[len], is niet wel zeker te ontdekken; maar het is zeker dat se tot nog toe van den Keizer zeer [592]ig ingenomen is, en dat se ontrent de [?bevordering] van dit haare favoriet project op zyne Majesteit veel reekent en staat maakt. En tot bewys dat se het project geheel niet [?geabandonneerd] heeft, zo dient dat, behalven dat se de jonge Grootvorst Constantyn, gelyk [ik] reeds de eere hebbe gehad in een myner voorgaande aan U HoogEdelGestrenge te melden, in hand van [een] griekse vrouw al sedert een geruymen tyd stellen heeft, en hem de griekse tael laat [?leeren], verder een griekse [922^"]ucatie [553], zy heeft ook onlangs aan den [644^_] Rumanzow ordre zenden om twaalf griekse jongens te bezorgen, en het […] te maaken.

(“Whether the Emperor is dealing honestly with her in this, [?or] whether he only seeks to amuse her and keep her on his line, as many suppose with [738], cannot well be discovered for certain; but it is certain that she is so far very [592] disposed towards the Emperor, and that for the [?furtherance] of this favourite project of hers she counts and relies much on His Majesty. And as proof that she has by no means [?abandoned] the project: besides having for a considerable time placed the young Grand Duke Constantine in the hands of [a] Greek woman … and having him [?taught] the Greek language, further a Greek [?education] [553], she has also lately sent orders to [644^_] Rumanzow to procure twelve Greek boys …”)

This passage needed corrections to the key and to the transcription of the despatch, checked on the scans on 30 September; they were applied to the decoder on 23 October 2026. In the codebook the word stands to the left of its code, so 54 with two strokes is alhoewel, 314 with two strokes behalven and 941 underlined observeeren; the words to their right belong to the next column. 47 underlined is k, not l, which also gives Denemarken wherever the machine text has Denemarlen. The mark that DECODE’s transcription puts before 380 and 303 is a dash after the group before them, so they stand for van and die. The group after jong is 191, ens, written above the line with a caret. The group given as [dan] is 601 with a bar, komen; elsewhere the writer spells dan as 601 and 368 with two strokes, so the bar is probably a slip. In the codebook 644 underlined begins Veld Ma…, the rest lost in the shadow of the binding, and 339 underlined is faint; the officer is probably Field Marshal P. A. Rumyantsev, which has not been checked. The first line of the next page is cut off in the photograph, so the end of the Greek sentence is lost.

Three more groups were decoded from their place in the codebook on 30 September 2026. 738 stands between 736 graaf and 739 Gu…; its word begins gro…, and met grond veronderstellen, “to suppose with reason”, is the idiom (M). 299 stands between 298 fau… and 300 femme, with a faint F…ri…; favoriet gives “this favourite project of hers”, the usual name for Catherine’s Greek project (I; Februari, also possible from the scan, gives no sense). 231 with a circle stands between 230 verdedig… and 232 vereenigen, with verd… legible; verder, “further”, fits the list of things done for the Grand Duke (I). Still open: 77 with a circle, which the codebook spells Stans or Stanz (between 75 Staat and 78 Ster…) and which does not yet give a word before diep stilzwijgen, and the line lost at the top of image 5417.

The full token-by-token decoding is targets/deswart1782/R1036_machine_decipherment.txt, with every group beside its value in R1036_tokens.tsv. The reading of the whole despatch, with its English translation, is R1036_reading.md and R1036_en.md; DECODE’s constant misreadings in its transcription of the key (hot = hof, chi = che) are listed in R1038_key_misreadings.tsv.

03 Method: values recovered from context

The key as transcribed decodes 1,448 groups. The rest were filled only where a value fits every occurrence of the group in the letter. Examples:

  • 881 = nje, completing Spa·nje each time; 340 underlined = mar in Dene·mar·ken; 311 underlined = land in Enge·land.
  • 176 with a caret = mpli, found twice in pure et si·mpli·citer.
  • 741 = haar, used twelve times for the Empress: met haar oprecht handelt, om haar te amuseeren, haare mediatie. 39 = hun, as in hun acte and hun onderdaan.
  • 681 underlined = mogendheden (de oorlogende mogendheden van Frankrijk, Spanje en Engeland); 363 with a circle = tusschen in tusschen·komst; 3 with a circle = stel in veronder·stel·d and vast te stel·len.

Each inferred value is labelled “contextual inference” in R1038_key_parsed.tsv, apart from the entries transcribed from the manuscript key. decode_1765.py regenerates everything.

04 The letter of 1787 (R1040), second part: the same 1765 key

Karl de Leeuw noted that the letter of 29 November 1787 is coded, “presumably by accident”, with two codebooks, so this page treats it as two parts, one per key. The change comes at group 180 of image 5459, where the 1765 key begins to give dit alles. The segment runs to the end of image 5473 (5,625 groups); image 5474, with its own marks and nonsense under the 1765 key, belongs to the first, missing code (23 October 2026). A context pass on 23 October fixed 28 values, each fitting every occurrence in R1040 and R1036 (34 underlined in, w+78 Weenen, 922 Hertog, 617 courier), and the segment was divided into Dutch words (R1040_part2_reading.md, with English); the French quotations in it are deciphered only in part, and the segment builds coherent sentences but reads at 88.8%, under the 95% bar. The text reports signs of an alliance with France, close attention to it from the Prussian and British representatives, news from Berlin, Kaunitz and the Bavarian exchange, and the likely positions of the four powers:

Dit alles, [?gepaard] met veele andere omstandigheden, schynen als indicien te zyn welke de hiervoor [?gedetailleerde] [917^^] wegens een effectief, zo niet reeds [?gesloten], ten minsten op het [?tapyt] zynde allientie tusschen de oorlogende hof en Vrankryk zeer [?waarschynlyk] maaken.

(“All this, [?coupled] with many other circumstances, seems to be indications which make very [?probable] the [?above-detailed] [917^^] about an effective, if not already [?concluded], at least [?pending] alliance between the warring court and France.”)

05 The letter of 1787 (R1040), first part: a missing code, not solved

The first 2,259 groups (images 5453–5458, the first 179 groups of image 5459, and image 5474) are in a different codebook, probably the one used under Rechteren, the code of Rechteren’s letter of 1785. It does not survive online, though the Rechteren cipher filed as Nationaal Archief inventory 158 may be it. Three plain/cipher pairs from the same legation (R2032, R2051 and R2052, from 1784 and 1786) were aligned against it. Transfers from them cover only 2–3% of the segment and give no connected prose, so no decoding of it is claimed.

06 What remains

  • In R1036, 38 groups have no value after the 23 October pass (56 before), and the line cut off at the top of image 5417 is missing. A third pass transcribed the entries that DECODE’s transcription of the key left illegible, straight from the codebook scans. The word stands to the left of its code, and the parallel columns of homophones confirm faint words. It kept the 41 values that fit the letter, such as 781 = geheel (“het Griekse project geheel stil”), 748 with two strokes = commercie (“de vrijheid van de commercie der neutralen”), 488 underlined = Zijne Keizerlijke Majesteit, and 515 with a circle = twaalf, the twelve Greek boys. The groups still open are entries lost in the binding shadow of the scan, blank in the book, or too faint to use.
  • Inflections are sometimes uncertain where the key gives a stem only, as in hot for hof/hoven.
  • The first 2,259 groups of R1040 are not decoded, pending the Rechteren-family codebook, probably Nationaal Archief 1.02.13 inv. 158, which is not scanned online.
  • R1040 second part: about 240 groups without a value, and the French quotations (about 8% of the segment) deciphered only in part; their cells in R1038 are lost in the binding shadow or too faint.

# R1036, De Swart to Van Bleiswijk, St Petersburg, 8 March 1782: reading

Rests on: DECODE's transcription of the cipher (`R1036_transcription.txt`, one interlinear group added: 19 -> 191 after
362^_ on image 5416), decoded token by token with the 1765 Croiset codebook (DECODE R1038) as parsed in
`R1038_key_parsed.tsv`, including the 30 Sept 2026 corrections now applied in `decode_1765.py` (token audit

Conventions: words the code spells in pieces are joined (oost+en+rijk+se = Oostenrijkse, p+r+u+y+ss+is+chi =
Pruyssische, with DECODE's key misreadings "chi" = che, "hot" = hof/hoven, "cerd" = eerd, "ock" = ook, "toc" = toe,
"hetten" = hebben, "tri" = f, "voornocond" = voornoemd, "zaaon" = zaam, "geneeg" = genoeg, "dezolve" = dezelve,
"maaden" = maaken, "hek" = het, constant in every occurrence; see the log). `[?word]` partly carried, `[word]` supplied,
`[…]` unread, `[617^"]` a group with no value. Spelling as the code gives it (antwoort, ontfangen, reekent).
