Tokyo ↔ Shanghai · Chinese telegraph code, the 共密 key · 10 June – 2 July 1916

Huang Xing’s 共密 telegrams, June 1916

Nine intercepted telegrams on Huang Xing’s conditions for the new president and on Beijing’s plan to summon provincial delegates, in a telegraph code with a scrambled tens digit; Brice Dudout recovered the key from the ciphertext alone.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 5 November 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: partial (96% of the cipher) · On the map

Contributed by Brice Dudout (solution) and Satoshi Tomokiyo (identification)

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · partial (96% of the cipher)

The telegrams
Nine telegrams of 10 June to 2 July 1916 (N17, N18, N23, N27, N30, N32, N33, N35 and, from volume 4, V4E), 555 four-digit groups: five from Tokyo to Shanghai (Huang Xing), four from Shanghai to Tokyo.
Where
Diplomatic Archives of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, via JACAR: B03050089300, B03050089400, B03050089500 and, for V4E, B03050090000. Telegraph-office forms copied by the police.
The cipher
Chinese telegraph code numbers in four-digit groups, the tens digit swapped through a table and the group subtracted from 9000. The first two groups of a form are in clear: 0364 1378 = 共密.
How it was deciphered
The key came from an exhaustive search over the constant with the best tens table for each, scored with a telegraph-code language model, with no crib. It was found on N17 and N18 and reads the other telegrams; V4E, never used, reads unchanged.
Credit
Brice Dudout, with Claude (mainly Opus 5.5; Codex made the first trials): digits re-read on the scans, the key (published 17 October 2026), translations and context. Satoshi Tomokiyo, Cryptiana: the identification of the key as a renumbered 明密 code book; his section credits the formula to Dudout. This project checked the key with its own decoder.
What it says
Huang Xing’s five conditions to be put to Li Yuanhong, beginning with the 1912 Provisional Constitution; efforts to raise money from Japanese contacts; and the warning that Beijing’s summons of provincial delegates is meant to destroy the Constitution and Parliament.
Still open
21 characters are kept as decoded but not understood (names, signatures and date codes) and 24 more are probable readings. Another telegram under the same prefix, N07, does not read with the key. The key is published as a candidate: no copy of the 共密 book has been found.

01 The forms

When Yuan Shikai died on 6 June 1916, Huang Xing was in Tokyo, living at Aoyama Minami-machi 3-53, and his allies were in Shanghai. The Japanese police copied the telegrams that passed between them onto the telegraph office’s own forms, and the copies survive in a Foreign Ministry series of mail and telegrams of watched persons. Each form holds a grid of digits written as kanji numerals. Brice Dudout reads the grid in columns, right to left, each column from the top, and cuts the stream into four-digit groups.

The first two groups are in clear and name the code. Hsueh Chun-tu’s edition of Huang Xing’s 1916 drafts notes a code name at the head of each telegram, one per correspondent. On that evidence 共密 is a code book issued to particular correspondents, and it needed its own key. The same files hold telegrams in two other numeric keys with their own pages: 容密 and 辰密.

02 The 共密 key

Swap the tens digit through a fixed table, then subtract the group from 9000. The tens table is

tens written0123456789
tens meant9761405283

Example. N17 opens 1188 4080 4113 7528. In 1188 the tens digit 8 stays 8, and 9000 − 1188 = 7812, 黎. In 7528 the tens 2 becomes 6, giving 7568, and 9000 − 7568 = 1432, 就. The four groups give 黎總統就, “President Li has taken [office]”. N17 ends 3887 3739 5550, 聞興灰: “for your information. Xing. The 10th.”

How it was found. By trying every constant from 0000 to 9999 and, for each, choosing the best tens table exactly (an assignment problem), scored by a model of telegraph-code pairs trained on telegrams of 1915–16 already published on the Wenmi site. Only 9000 and this table give Chinese. Codex made the first trials. The key was found on N17 and N18 and reads the other telegrams.

As a renumbered book. Satoshi Tomokiyo, in the Cryptiana section on the mingmi (明密) format codes, reads this key as a standard 明密 code book with its pages numbered backwards (written page 89 is page 00) and its columns and rows renumbered by hand; the subtraction from 9000 is this double backward count. The two descriptions give the same table over all 10,000 values; the repository file key.md states the key both ways.

Dating. Dudout published the first partial 共密 edition, with the key as a candidate (N17 and N18), on 17 October 2026 (GitHub commit of the Wenmi repository, 21:35 UTC), and added N23 on 19 October. Tomokiyo’s section is undated, the page was last modified on 24 October 2026, and Wenmi cites it as of 23 October. The section credits the formula to Dudout (“The formula found by Dudout is to first map the tens digit C to φ(C)”) and adds the renumbered-book reading.

03 The checks

His checks. The key found on N17 and N18 reads the other seven telegrams. V4E, from the later volume, was not used and reads unchanged. The names and offices that come out fit June 1916. Twenty digits in N30 to N35 were re-read by him on the scans after the first reading (looks-alike 一, 二 and 三) and are in the transcription here. In N35 two groups carry an 8 where the other 共密 telegrams have a 3 (表, 袁).

The check made here (20 October 2026). His verify.py reproduces the published raw texts from his digits and scores the 共密 text at 3.49 against 200 random keys of the same form (best −1.45) and 200 shufflings of the digits (best −1.22). His search_gongmi.py, rerun from scratch, finds 9000 and the tens table exactly. A decoder written here from the key statement alone, using the Unihan telegraph-code table already in this repository, gives the same character as his published text for all 555 groups. This confirms that the key as stated turns his digits into his text; it does not re-read the scans. N35 sheet 1 was compared on the JACAR scan here (B03050089500, PDF p. 2): the prefix and groups 1 to 8 match his page.

04 What they say

Hsueh Chun-tu’s edition of Huang Xing’s 1916 drafts has nothing between the pieces of 8 and 20 June, and these telegrams are dated in between. Translations below are Dudout’s, shortened.

On 10 June (N17) Huang Xing, writing that “President Li has taken office and the general situation is beginning to settle”, asks the Shanghai friends to consult Tang Hualong and Tan Yankai in secret and to have Li Yuanhong carry out five points, the first two being to restore the old Provisional Constitution and the Parliament and to issue no amnesty. The next day (N18) he adds that if Li and Duan sincerely support the Republic the demands can be met, but “armed force is needed as support”.

On 13 and 15 June (N23, N27, N30) the telegrams turn to money: Zhang Ji reports that Tanaka and Koike have both wired Aoki about a loan; Huang Xing answers that funds from two provinces are hopeless, and that a Japanese contact would lend a small sum if “our friend Pu” asked the consul general in Shanghai to introduce him. On 14 to 16 June (N32, N33, N35) Shanghai reports Beijing’s plan that each province send three men to discuss the Constitution; Huang Xing replies that its purpose is “to destroy the Constitution and the Parliament”, and that the colleagues should convene by themselves in a safe place and dissuade the independent provinces from sending delegates. The last telegram (V4E, 2 July) says that Guangdong has sent someone, that money is urgent, and asks when the addressee is coming back and by which ship.

05 Why only partial

The share of characters that read as sense is above 95%, but the class is kept as the Wenmi page gives it: it publishes these nine telegrams as a partial critical edition. The key C51 is a candidate reconstruction (the historical code book is not identified; a printed edition of the telegraph code of uncertain date agrees on fourteen entries checked). 21 characters are unresolved: the endings of N32 (會逵境) and N33 (會城), names in N27, N30, N35 and V4E, and two names in N17. And N07, which carries the same clear prefix 共密 but only its first sheet transcribed, does not read with the key: it may be another copy of the book, or a faulty digit stream, and there is no plaintext to test it. The Rongmi and Chenmi keys, in eight reading orders, and the renumbered-book model that read the 乾密 telegram N09, were tried on N07 and fail. Satoshi Tomokiyo’s Cryptiana and Hsueh Chun-tu’s edition of Huang Xing’s drafts do not contain these texts.

06 What is open

  • 21 characters kept as decoded but not understood (〔x〕), and 24 probable readings (in braces in reading.md): no one-digit variant gives an evident character, so they need the digits re-read on the police copies, or names and date codes.
  • N07 does not read with the key (above). In V4E the digit file includes framing repairs (a digit missing or extra) inferred from the decoding, still to be checked on the JACAR scan.
  • The key is a candidate: no historical copy of the 共密 book has been found. The texts have not been found in the 黃興集 or in Li Yuanhong’s correspondence, which are still to be searched. The translations are proposals, and the identifications of Japanese and Chinese names are candidates.

共密 (Gongmi), 9 telegrams, 10 June - 2 July 1916 read in part

Huang Xing's five conditions to Li Yuanhong (restore the 1912 Provisional Constitution and the old Parliament, no amnesty, Duan's cabinet to resign, move Wang Zhanyuan, bring the party leaders to Beijing); Japan will not lend to Beijing before unification; reports from Shanghai on the Constitution and on Beijing's plan of provincial delegates.

## N17 — 10 June 1916, Tokyo → Shanghai

黎總統就任，大局粗定。鄙意首先宜慰撫人心，排却挾制。最要最急之事，請密商濟武、組安諸兄，{速}請黎公〔漠〕行：一、恢復舊約法及國會；二、勿發赦令及免{訴}令；三、使段等辭職，另加任命爲臨時總理，但閣員中曾助袁謀叛者勿再加入，俟開國會，{重}新組織正式內閣；四、將王占元調開，委民軍中握{重}兵者爲{都}督；五、招民黨要人入京協商一切。再，日當局決定：非南{北}統一，〔廖〕不借款於北京政府。似此黎公應不至受制。並聞。興灰

# 共密 (Gongmi): English translation

Working translations from the Wenmi page (https://bdudout.github.io/wenmi/en/gongmi.html, Brice Dudout with Claude, CC BY 4.0).
Identifications of people are candidates; uncertain passages are marked as on the site.

## N17 — 10 June 1916

President Li has taken office and the general situation is beginning to settle. In my humble view, we must first calm people’s minds and remove coercion. For the most important and urgent matters, please consult secretly with Jiwu, Zu’an and the others, and quickly ask Mr Li to carry out: 1. restore the old Provisional Constitution and the Parliament; 2. issue no amnesty and no order dropping prosecutions; 3. make Duan and the others resign and appoint anew a provisional premier, without readmitting cabinet members who helped Yuan’s rebellion, then, once Parliament meets, form a regular cabinet anew; 4. transfer Wang Zhanyuan and appoint as governor a commander of the people’s army holding strong forces; 5. invite the leading men of the people’s party to Beijing to consult on everything. Moreover, the Japanese authorities have decided not to lend to the Beijing government unless North and South are unified. So Mr Li should not be constrained. For information. — Xing. The 10th.

Note: 濟武 = Tang Hualong, 組安 = Tan Yankai. Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N18 — 11 June 1916

Your telegram of the 10th received. If Li and Duan sincerely support the Republic, the people’s party’s demands can naturally be accommodated, and it will not be hard to bring the State onto the proper path of the rule of law. To reach this goal, armed force is needed as support. I fear that for now we cannot let things take their course. The return home will be decided according to circumstances. — Xing. The 11th.

Note: Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N23 — 13 June 1916

Brother Ke: Tanaka and Koike have both wired Aoki; […] agreed to a loan for me; please, brother, go in person to press the matter — it is essential. — Zhang Ji.

Note: 小池, 田中, 青木: candidate identifications. Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N32 — 14 June 1916

The old Provisional Constitution can be hoped to be restored; Liang’s faction is favourable. The leading men of Beijing and Shanghai have no hard-line position. Zhongshan has wired his troops to cease fighting and asked your opinion by wire; the reply has already appeared in the press and he will certainly publish it: be careful. Ge, Yin and Huang have wired repeatedly; you must reply. […]

Note: Signed 葛、殷、黃 = Ge Jing’en, Yin Ruli, Huang Fu (cf. N24). Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N27 — 15 June 1916

About Pu’s funds: we have discussed it with the two provinces of […]: no hope. — Xing.

Note: Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N30 — 15 June 1916

Koike has just said again that if our friend Pu, in his own name, asks Consul Ariyoshi to introduce him, a military […] could lend a small sum. Decide for yourself. — Xing. The 15th.

Note: 溥 = Puquan (Zhang Ji); 有吉 = Ariyoshi Akira, Japanese consul general in Shanghai (candidate). Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N33 — 15 June 1916

Feng is very ambiguous. Another telegram from Beijing: Duan and the others have decided that each province will send three men to Beijing to discuss the Constitution. The Shanghai politicians long knew Liang’s schemes: [to] the Constitution, obtain the cabinet in exchange, with Duan as president. Huang Qun has arrived in Beijing; […] Duan has changed; the Shanghai members of Parliament will meet at once to oppose it. If the old Constitution is not restored, we fear fighting. Watch quietly for now. […]

Note: Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## N35 — 16 June 1916

Beijing is summoning delegates of the provinces to discuss the Constitution, intending to destroy the Constitution and the Parliament. Let our colleagues […] quickly choose a safe place and convene by themselves, and find a way to persuade the independent provinces not to send delegates. The salt revenue has still not been handed over; at present […] is not free. Duan seems to be taking up Yuan’s old tricks; Parliament should deliberate […], and the diplomatic representatives of the people’s army should say […], asking the foreigners to wait until true unification before handing over [the funds]. — Xing.

Note: Here two groups have 8 where the other Gongmi telegrams have 3 (表, 袁). Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.

## V4E — 2 July 1916

Guangdong has sent someone: the matter is urgent and money is needed. If you care for Guangdong, I, Wen, should go back quickly; I hope you will first advance some funds. […] has already entered Beijing. When are you coming back, and by which ship? Please let me know in advance. […] Wen.

Note: Digits re-read by Brice Dudout.
