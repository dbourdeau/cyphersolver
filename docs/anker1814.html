Carsten Anker to Prince Christian Frederik, a leaf of 28 June 1814

A leaf of 28 June 1814 with no decipherment, in the symbol cipher of 1811, was deciphered at 98.8% of its signs with a key rebuilt from the decipherment of that earlier letter and validated on a held-out part.

01 The leaf

A leaf headed “fr. 28 Junii 1814” (“ad Anker C. 101a”) in the Riksarkivet in Oslo, RA-EA-5938 Christian Frederik, F/Fc/L0217/0002, PDF page 59: about ten lines of a symbol cipher mixed with clear Danish and French, from Carsten Anker in London to Prince Christian Frederik. It has no decipherment in the file and is not in C. J. Anker’s printed edition of the correspondence (Christian Frederik og Carsten Anker’s Brevveksling 1814, 254 letters searched in full text by the contributor). The same archive extract (RA-EA-5938) holds Anker’s letter of 31 December 1811 in the same kind of symbol cipher, with interlinear glosses and a decipherment of the time (printed as no. 32), and the London dispatch of 28 June 1814 in a four-digit code (printed as no. 88); both are existing readings. The contributor, Brice Dudout (L’Europe chiffrée, with Claude Opus 5.5 agents), read the leaf; the images are not reproduced here.

02 The key and its test

The cipher is a homophonic substitution in symbols with a few logograms (# for jeg, a double dash for Sverige); word gaps are mostly kept. The key (36 labels, key_1811.tsv) was learnt on page 9 of the 1811 letter only, from its glossed lines and from the lines aligned with the decipherment of the time and print no. 32, and frozen before measurement.

The contributor ran two pre-registered tests; the second protocol was hashed before the key was built. The first used the archive’s pencil key card “KN-06” on eight held-out lines of the 1811 letter and failed (0.025 against 0.018 for the best shuffled key; a third of the signs have no value on the card). The second measured the rebuilt key once on the held-out left column of page 10 of the 1811 letter (ten lines) against the decipherment of the time: 0.950 of characters against at best 0.019 for 200 shuffled keys, which passes the thresholds fixed in advance (0.85 and a margin of 0.30). Declared limit: print no. 32 had already been studied in full before, so the held-out part was transcribed by shapes and not blind. Applicability to the leaf was fixed before decoding: 0.937 of its signs have a value in the 1811 key, against 0.369 matching the Bergen 1814 alphabet. The project did not repeat these measurements and did not see the images.

03 The reading

Of the leaf’s 252 cipher signs, 249 carry a value as certain or probable (98.8%); the other three are the restored logogram, and one stained sign and the word ‹felv› are among the 249 as uncertain. One logogram, three occurrences, is restored as [D.K.H.] (“Deres Kongelige Høihed”, Your Royal Highness) from the clear “D. K. H.s” and “[D.K.H.]s Portrait”; one word, ‹felv›, decodes letter by letter but its sense is uncertain.

In the contributor’s reading (reading_p59.md, with an English translation) the match of Princess Charlotte of Wales with the Prince of Orange is “broken off for ever”; Anker revives his “old idea” of a match with Christian Frederik, provided the prince is not bound and Norway’s horizon clears; the prince’s portrait will be engraved and published within eight to fourteen days; “only let Your Royal Highness not bind yourself!”; Hardenberg and Anker agree. The project itself is known: Christian Frederik writes of the intended connection with Princess Charlotte in his printed letters to Anker of 24 June, 11 October and 12 December 1814 (nos. 31, 39, 43), and Anker reports on the Charlotte–Orange match on 26 September (no. 94). What the contributor found new is Anker’s own proposal of 28 June, in cipher. The princess’s name and the proposal to the prince are the contributor’s reading from the context of the leaf and the printed letters of the time; the leaf writes only “Prindcesen”, “Pr: af Oranien” and “min gamle Idée”. No secondary study of the project was found in a quick search.

04 What remains uncertain

  • ‹felv› (line 9 of the leaf): decoded letter by letter, sense not established; a second reader on the shapes is the next step.
  • The logogram restored as [D.K.H.] is not in the 1811 key; the Bergen 1814 sheet’s logogram list could confirm it.
  • One reader, no second reader, and no human palaeographer has checked the sign shapes; the images were not seen by the project.
  • The held-out part of the test was not transcribed blind, as declared above.

Leaf "fr. 28 Junii 1814", Carsten Anker (London) to Christian Frederik

The match between Princess Charlotte of Wales and the Prince of Orange is broken off for ever; Anker revives his old idea of marrying Christian Frederik to her, though she may not leave England, if the prince is not bound and Norway's horizon clears; the prince's engraved portrait will be published within a fortnight; the prince must not commit himself; Hardenberg agrees.

# Translation (English): leaf "fr. 28 Junii 1814", Carsten Anker (London) to Prince Christian Frederik

Cipher passages in **bold**, clear in *italic*; [x] restored; ‹…› unresolved. The princess is Charlotte of Wales (her engagement to the Hereditary Prince of Orange was broken off in June 1814); the identification is from the context, not written on the leaf.

**The match with the Prince of Orange is broken off** *for ever.* **Now my old** *idea* **comes up again: the Princess is handsome, has a very firm character, and must absolutely be married. — She will not be allowed to leave the country, and one would have to** *put up with that. — If now* **[Your Royal Highness] were not bound, and** *Norway's horizon brightened, the idea would be well worth weighing. — The foundation, and the very building, of Norway's lasting happiness, peace and prosperity would then be laid at a stroke and nearly finished. Within 8 to 14 days* **[Your Royal Highness]'s portrait will be finished, well engraved and published. But this** *is a lengthy matter that needs time.* **Only let [Your Royal Highness] not bind yourself!** *God knows that no one can wish more ardently than I for Your Royal Highness's true happiness. My gracious, noble and beloved Lord!* **That you do not belong to them ‹felv›. Hardenberg and I** *have talked about this a great deal and we are agreed.*

Summary: on 28 June 1814 Anker reports from London that the Charlotte-Orange match is off and revives his "old idea" of marrying Christian Frederik to the princess, provided the prince is not bound elsewhere and Norway's situation clears; he announces the prince's engraved portrait and urges him not to commit himself; Hardenberg agrees.
