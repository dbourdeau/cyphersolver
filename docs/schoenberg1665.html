Schönberg’s journal of the Dresden negotiations: a cipher passage, December 1665

Go to the full text ↓

A nine-line cipher passage in a journal of the Erfurt negotiations at Dresden was partly deciphered with a regular substitution key recovered from the cipher: the Elector is said to have won thirty thousand thalers for the Erfurt rights.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 9 November 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: partial (91% of the cipher)

Contributed by Brice Dudout (solution)

01 The passage

Nr. 107 of the Schönbergische Sammlung (“Acta Reiffenbergica”) in the Landesarchiv Thüringen – Staatsarchiv Altenburg is Schönberg’s journal of the negotiations at Dresden between the Wettin houses and Electoral Mainz over Erfurt, the Reiffenberg affair, 26 October to 23 December 1665. One passage of nine lines on fol. 114r (image 0233, early December) is in cipher, between “… ihn aber auch in einer sache also gangen –” and “Frankreich habe …”. No other cipher and no decipherment appear in the volume (396 images checked by the contributor). The ThULB Jena digitisation (urn:nbn:de:urmel-stat-262972) is licensed CC BY 3.0, the same archive as thumbshirn1647. The contributor, Brice Dudout (L’Europe chiffrée) ran Claude Opus 5.5 agents that transcribed, solved and read it under his protocol; no human palaeographer has checked the transcription, and no images are reproduced here.

02 The key and its test

A simple substitution in Latin letters with a regular key over a 24-letter alphabet: odd plaintext letters map to z y x w u t s r q p o n (reversed), even ones to a b c d e f g h i k l m; word division is kept. Known keys were tried first (Thumbshirn’s numeric key of Nr. 85, the Dresden nomenclators) and none applies. The transcription was frozen before any key was tried and the protocol hashed before any decoding; the key was learnt on lines 1–5 only. One measurement on the held-out lines 6–9 (16 words; two of the ten lexicon hits come from word cuts at the binding edge, and the key learnt on lines 1–5 lacked m and o) gives a German lexicon share of 0.625 against at best 0.3125 for 200 shuffled keys, which is partial (validated needs 0.70).

Declared after the measurement: the key (e, h, l, m and o) was completed from its regular pattern, and five transcription errors were corrected on re-reading the image (capital A and not L in “Boyneburg”, capital W and not L in “Gott”, q for g, t for b, and a probable w). The frozen cipher transcription was not modified; the corrected text is transcription_0233.txt. The project did not repeat the test and did not see the image.

03 The reading

Boyneburg – rede viel ‹diengs›, der Churfürst habe dreysigtausend Thaler pro iuribus {Erfordensibus} ge[w]onnen: Gott möchte ih[n] beh{üten}, er habe den {seinen} bey [T]eufel holen geschwohren, und Leute darüber zu {Zeugen} angerufen.

Braces mark readings that are probable, square brackets letters restored at the binding edge, as in the contributor’s reading. Boyneburg is the former Mainz minister Johann Christian von Boyneburg, on the contributor’s identification; which Elector is meant (Saxony or Mainz) is not stated in the cipher.

In English, as the contributor gives it and not word for word: Boyneburg says much (the word ‹diengs› is unread); the Elector is said to have won thirty thousand thalers for the Erfurt rights (the verb is partly restored, and “demanded” is also possible); may God preserve him; he swore, by the devil, that his own should be fetched (the sense of “den seinen” is uncertain), and called people to witness. Of 32 words, 25 are certain, 4 probable, 2 restored at the binding edge and 1 unresolved; without the restored letters that is 29 of 32 (90.6%), under the 95% bar, so the page calls it partial.

04 What remains uncertain

  • ‹diengs’ (cipher buxswi): the letters are certain, the sense is not (Dings or dienst?).
  • Two letters are uncertain at the binding edge, in ge[w]onnen and [T]eufel, and need the original volume.
  • The key passed its held-out test only partially, and was completed from its pattern after the measurement.
  • No edition of the journal or the passage was found (the LATh and ThULB records cite none; the printed histories of the Erfurt affair were not checked volume by volume); no decipherment of the time exists in the volume.

## Text (German, with the clear around it)

(… ihn aber auch in einer sache also gangen -) **Boyneburg - rede viel ‹diengs›, der Churfürst habe dreysigtausend Thaler pro iuribus {Erfordensibus} ge[w]onnen: Gott möchte ih[n] behü{ten}, er habe den {seinen} bey [T]eufel holen geschwohren, und Leute darüber zu Zeu{gen} angerufen.** (Frankreich habe …)

## Count (32 words; "dreysigtausend" counted as 2)

- **Certain (25)**: Boyneburg, rede, viel, der, Churfürst, habe, dreysig, tausend, Thaler, pro, iuribus, Gott, möchte, ihn (ih + abbreviation stroke), er, habe, den, bey, holen, geschwohren, und, Leute, darüber, zu, angerufen.
- **Probable (4)**: {Erfordensibus} (the cipher gives "erfordenisbus", a copyist's si/is metathesis; Erfordensis = of Erfurt); {behüten} (first sign of L6 at the binding edge); {seinen} (the cipher gives "neinen": s enciphered for i); {Zeugen} (first sign of L9 at the binding edge).
- **Restored (2)**: ge[w]onnen (a letter lost in the binding at the start of L5; "ge[s]onnen", demanded, is also possible); [T]eufel (first letter in the binding).
- **Unresolved (1)**: ‹diengs› (cipher "buxswi" read with certainty; sense "Dings" = things? or a slip for "dienst").
- **Read by sense, restored excluded: 29/32 = 90.6%**; with the 2 restored words 31/32 = 97%. Under Daniel's 95% rule (restored words are not read as sense): **read in part**.

## Translation

(… but it went with him in one matter thus -) Boyneburg says a great deal ‹…›: the Elector is said to have obtained 30,000 thalers "for the Erfurt rights"; may God keep him; he swore to his people "may the devil take me", and called people to witness it. (France is said to …)
