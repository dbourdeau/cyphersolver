At a glanceCiphertext-only break · complete

The letters
Four letters of Afonso VI, signed Rey and countersigned by the Count of Castelo Melhor, to Sancho Manuel de Vilhena, Count of Vila Flor, governor of arms of the Alentejo: 13 May, 15 May (a long letter and a short note) and 26 May 1663, while Don John of Austria besieged and took Évora.
Where
Arquivo Nacional Torre do Tombo, Luís de Miranda Pereira, liv. 2 (PT/TT/LMP/0002), on DigitArq: images 87; 53–57 and 59; 69 and 71; 37 and 39. Three of the four letters survive in two ciphered copies. Catalogue no. 391, which dated them 1660–61: they are of May 1663.
The cipher
A letter-for-letter substitution on Portuguese with a few homophones: numbers from 2 to 53 and letter-like symbols, separated by points. The greeting and the date stay in clear, and in the long letter whole paragraphs.
How it was deciphered
Brice Dudout recovered the key from the 26 May letter alone, froze it, and tested it on the 13 May letter, which had been transcribed without it. Second copies enciphered with other homophones, and a sentence clear in one copy and in cipher in the other, confirm it.
What it says
Cavalry on its way, money for the pay, and a plan for the King to come to Setúbal; then the order to relieve Évora “a todo o risco”, even at the cost of a battle, repeated on 26 May as the King’s final resolution.
Still open
One symbol used once (ʒ) and a struck-out name in the 13 May letter; about fifteen encipherer’s slips there corrected from the sense. Of the long 15 May letter, only a 174-symbol passage is transcribed in the repository.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée), working with Claude Opus 5.5: transcription, key, decipherment, Portuguese text, translation and notes. His write-up.

01 The letters and the book

In spring 1663 Don John Joseph of Austria invaded the Alentejo. From Lisbon the court, governed by the Count of Castelo Melhor, escrivão da puridade, in the name of the young Afonso VI, wrote almost daily to the general of the Portuguese army, the Count of Vila Flor. Book 2 of the Luís de Miranda Pereira collection at the Torre do Tombo (864 images on DigitArq) keeps letters of that correspondence, among them four in cipher. Each is signed Rey, countersigned by Castelo Melhor and addressed P[ar]a o Conde de V[ila] Flor.

Most letters were sent twice, the original and a via by another courier, and both copies are bound here, each enciphered separately with other homophones and other line breaks. The two copies of the long letter of 15 May are interleaved: their third leaves are swapped in the binding, so that copy 1 runs over images 53, 54 and 59 and copy 2 over 55, 56 and 57. The cipher itself proves it, since a word runs on across the leaves (estam r|[ec]olhidos). The catalogue entry (no. 391) dated the cipher letters 1660–61; every one of them is dated in clear in May 1663.

02 The cipher

A substitution of single letters, on Portuguese, with no code words. The symbols are numbers and letter-like shapes written with points between them. Several are ordinary cursive letters standing for other letters, which makes a run look like garbled Portuguese: x is c, h is l, n is f, r is g, a is p, d is x. The repository transcribes the special shapes by the letter they resemble: Z for a barred z (Ƶ), D for a delta (Δ), B for a long-stemmed B (ß), F for a barred 7 (ꝼ), K for an overlined x (Ӿ).

The other letters use a few more homophones, set on the 13 May letter itself (K2): 9 = c, a looped 9 (g) = e, e = q, 2 = f, p = a, Q = u/v, S = b, 53 = d, u = m, & = e. The long letter of 15 May was deciphered with K3; the K2 and K3 files in the repository list the same forty values. No key has a symbol for j, k or y, or for the cedilla and the tilde, and u and v are not distinguished. Numbers of two or more figures are written in clear inside the cipher (203 cavallos, 56, 55), and so are some dates, which is a trap: in the 13 May letter 10 and 9 are days of the month, although 10 and 9 are also the symbols for h and c.

03 How the key was recovered

One letter, no crib. The 26 May letter carries no decipherment, and no key survives in the volume. Brice Dudout transcribed it symbol by symbol (634 positions, one of them lost in a tear of the right margin) and recovered K1 from it alone, with Portuguese letter statistics and the words such a letter must contain: que, exercito, cartas, risco. The key was then frozen, its SHA-256 fingerprint recorded, before any test.

A test fixed in advance. The 13 May letter, transcribed without the key (636 symbols), was decoded with the frozen K1 and scored with a Portuguese language model: −2.61 per character. The same letter with its symbols shuffled scored at best −4.52, and keys with permuted values at best −5.52 in 200 trials, while a Portuguese text enciphered with K1 with 3% of simulated errors scores −2.58. K1 covers 92.8% of the 13 May symbols; the extra homophones of K2 were then set on that letter.

The other copies. The second copy of 26 May (image 39) and the second copy of the 15 May note (image 71) are enciphered with other homophones and other line breaks; each gives the same text, and between them they settle every doubtful symbol of the first copies. The frozen K1 decodes the note at −2.25 per character, against at best −4.72 for the controls. Of the long 15 May letter both copies were deciphered with K3: they agree with the established text at 98.7% and 99.0%, and with each other at 0.927, against at best 0.131 when one copy’s symbols are shuffled (20 trials).

A sentence in clear. Copy 1 of the long letter writes one sentence in clear that copy 2 enciphers (image 57, lines 20–26). The key gives 164 of its 166 characters; the two differences are spelling (quasi and quazi, thenente and tenente):

K3 on copy 2: a defensa e quazi no mesmo tempo tinha enviado o tenente de mestre de campo general luis ribeiro para que tomando as noticias convenientes encaminhe dali as carruagens e outros socorros pela parte mais segura
copy 1, clear:    E quasi no mesmo tempo tinha enviado o thenente de m[estr]e de campo g[ene]r[a]l Luis R[ibeir]o, para q[ue] tomando as noticias convenientes encaminhe dali as carruagens, e outros socorros p[e]la parte mais segura.

In the cipher the slip is the encipherer’s: quazi is written with F (h) where K (z) was meant.

04 The statistical check

The project’s statistical check (docs/_validate.py) was run on the two letters deciphered with K2, 13 May and the 15 May note: 1,139 symbols of 37 kinds, scored with a Portuguese letter model. The reading scores −2.36 per letter. Random keys score −7.41 on average, 6.8 standard deviations lower, and a key fitted by the annealer to the same symbols in shuffled order reaches at best −3.64. The annealer run on the real text from random starting keys reaches −2.50, close to the reading, so the search does find this key. The verdict is pass: with about thirty occurrences per symbol, the letter statistics separate this key from any key fitted to noise.

13 May 1663: the cavalry on its way

Conde amigo. Eu ElRey vos envio muito saudar como aquelle q[ue] amo.

A ultima carta que se recebeo vosa foi de {10} do corrente, e fico com cuidado de se nam hav{e}r recebido outra, e se entende pod[e]ria t{o}mar-se o correo. Despacho {es}te para vos avizar que Manoe[l] Freire de Andrade partio d{e} Coimbra em 9 deste á tarde com 203 cavallos, e escreve q[ue] nam faria outra detença mais q[u]e em montar alguns que hião desmontados; com elle deve hir tambem tudo o que faltava do socorro da Be{i}ra; conforme a isto pod{e}reis fazer conta de quando ahi poderá {c}hegar. Das companhias de cav[a]los desta cidade pasa amanhã a de Luis Lo[bo] ‹aal› de Agalega com 56 cavalos; no dia seguinte parte a do comisario Gonçalo da Costa, e no outro Diogo Luis Ribeiro e Jorge Furtado, e cada hum leva 55 cavalos pelo menos. Nam se oferece outra couza de q[ue] vos avizar mais que estar-se esperando {c}oreio com todo o cuidado.

Escrita em L[isbo]a a 13 de Mayo de 1663. Rey.

Written the day before Évora was invested. The last letter from Vila Flor received at court was of the 10th, and the court fears the courier has been taken. Manuel Freire de Andrade left Coimbra on the 9th with 203 horse and the rest of the Beira relief; of the Lisbon cavalry companies, one leaves tomorrow with 56 horse, then those of Gonçalo da Costa, Diogo Luís Ribeiro and Jorge Furtado, at least 55 horse each.

15 May 1663: the long letter

Partly in clear and partly in cipher. The cipher carries everything the enemy should not learn: the Beira reinforcements reported by Pedro Jaques de Magalhães (1,801 foot in the terços of Manuel Ferreira Rebelo and Paulo de Andrade, 408 horse), Manuel Freire de Andrade’s 203 horse, three Lisbon cavalry companies embarking with 170 horse and Jorge Furtado following with 60; the King’s plan to make Setúbal a place of arms and to come himself to Setúbal or Palmela; 44,000 cruzados for the pay that left on Thursday and have taken shelter somewhere still unknown, with boots and other stores, money for the French and a thousand cruzados for the English, “e que tudo se tem entendido estar em Evora”; and the mestre de campo António Soares da Costa and an engineer sent to Montemor. In clear are the greeting, the doctrine that an army raised and paid by the kingdom exists to fight when it must, and the order to send a courier every day, “ainda que não haja couza de novo”.

15 May 1663: “a todo o risco”

Conde amigo. Eu ElRey vos envio muito saudar como aquelle que amo.

Demais do que vos digo em outra carta desta mesma data, tornando-sse a considerar a materia principal de que nella se trata, fui servido rezolver que, em cazo que o inimigo persevere no sitio de Evora e reduza aquela cidade a termos perigozos, ajunteis todas as forças dessa provincia, e com os socorros que das outras espera{i}s a procureis socorrer a todo o risco, ainda que vos exponhais a[o] ultimo transe de huma batalha, porque assim convem á defença do reino e a meu serviço, pella importancia da praça e conse[q]uencias que se seguirão d{o} sucesso que ella tiver; o que espero executareis com a rezoluçam e valor q{ue} he necessaria.

Escrita em L[isbo]a a 15 de Mayo de 1663. Rey.

A note added to the long letter the day after Évora was invested, wholly in cipher between the greeting and the date. The main question has been considered again: if the enemy keeps up the siege and puts the city in danger, Vila Flor is to gather all the forces of the province and the help expected from the others, and relieve it at all risk, “even exposing yourself to the last extremity of a battle”, for the defence of the kingdom requires it.

26 May 1663: the final resolution

Conde Amigo. Eu ElRey vos envio muito saudar como aquelle q[ue] amo.

Despois de hontem vos haver escrito as cartas cujo duplicado vai com esta, pareceo tornar-vos a dizer por ultima rezolução que o que convem a conservaçam de meus reinos he socorrer Evora em todo o cazo e a todo o risco, sem se duvidar desta rezoluçam; o modo e a forma haveis vós de dispor como vos parec{e}r. Esta minha carta mostrareis aos cabos desse exercito, de cujo valor e rezolução espero que todo este reino lhes deva sua conservação. Tambem me pareceo dizer-vos que logo, sem tardança, deveis logo mover esse exercito, tanto para animar essa gente como para entender o inimigo que he esta a minha rezoluçam; e dentro de dous dias partirá daqui dinheiro consideravel, que baste para paga do exercito e das carruagens; o q[u]e podeis fazer notorio a todos, [p]ara que o tenham entendido.

Escrita em Lixb[o]a a 26 de Mayo de 1663. Rey.

Written four days after Évora surrendered. The King tells Vila Flor again, “as my final resolution”, that the preservation of his kingdoms requires the relief of Évora in any event and at all risk; the manner is left to him. He is to show the letter to his commanders, move the army at once, both to hearten the men and to show the enemy the King’s resolve, and announce that within two days a considerable sum will leave Lisbon for the pay of the army and the wagons.

06 The siege of Évora

Don John appeared before Estremoz, the army’s base, on 11 May 1663; unable to attack it, he marched on Évora and invested it on the 14th. The 13 May letter was written while Lisbon did not yet know his target. Vila Flor’s council of war resolved to march to the city’s relief, and the army left Estremoz on 22 May, the day Évora surrendered (Ericeira, book VIII). The order was renewed on 26 May. On 8 June Vila Flor and Schomberg defeated Don John at Ameixial, and Évora was retaken in June.

The letters fill in what Ericeira’s Historia de Portugal Restaurado only alludes to, “the King’s repeated orders and the urgent pressure of the Count of Castelo Melhor”. Several details agree with his narrative: the Beira terços of Manuel Ferreira Rebelo and Paulo de Andrade were with the army on 22 May, Manuel Freire de Andrade joined it with the Beira horse, and Ericeira counts eighty thousand cruzados sent from Lisbon among the funds of the Évora garrison when the siege began, which fits the 15 May letter’s belief that the pay convoy had taken shelter in the city. The plan to bring the King to Setúbal or Palmela was kept in cipher, and the contributor found no trace of the journey in Ericeira.

07 What remains uncertain

  • 13 May, ʒ: a symbol used once, in Despacho {es}te, taken as es from the sense (grade I); it is the one position in the four letters without a value from the key.
  • 13 May, Lo[bo] ‹aal› de Agalega: after Lo the encipherer struck out two symbols, probably bo (Luís Lobo is named in the long letter of 15 May); aal de Agalega is not explained.
  • 13 May, slips: about fifteen symbols corrected from the sense (grade M): three times 30 (m) for 3 (e), in hav{e}r, d{e} Coimbra and pod{e}reis; twice K (z) for c, in {c}hegar and {c}oreio; A (t) for i in Be{i}ra; letters left out. This letter survives in one copy, so the corrections rest on the sense alone.
  • 15 May note: five slips of the first copy (espera{i}s, a[o], conse[q]uencias, d{o}, q{ue}), all settled by the second copy.
  • 26 May: one symbol lost in the tear, restored from the sense as [p] in [p]ara; parecor written for parecer.
  • 15 May, long letter: agardeço for agradeço in both copies, so the draft’s spelling; the engineer sent to Montemor, Adam Fr[ancis]co de Pontes in both copies, is not identified. Only the 174-symbol passage shown in section 03 is transcribed in the repository; the full transcriptions of the two copies (2,213 and 2,357 symbols, as the contributor counts them) and their decryptions are not, so the long letter’s text cannot yet be checked symbol by symbol here.
  • The K2 homophones were set on the 13 May letter itself and are not tested independently of it; the test in section 03 is of K1.
  • Prior work. Ericeira’s Historia de Portugal Restaurado, part II (1698), book VIII, and its 1751 edition do not quote the letters. The blog Guerra da Restauração, which publishes documents of the 1663 campaign, gives Vila Flor’s letter of 26 May, not the King’s. António Álvares da Cunha’s Campanha de Portugal pella provincia do Alentejo na primavera do anno de 1663 (Lisbon, 1663) is not digitised as far as the contributor found and has not been checked. Web searches in Portuguese and English found no edition (27 October 2026).

# 26 May 1663: "my final resolution": established text (Portuguese)

Conventions: {x} probable; ‹x› clerk's slip or unresolved; [..] editorial.

Conde Amigo. Eu ElRey vos envio muito saudar como aquelle q[ue] amo.

Despois de hontem vos haver escrito as cartas cujo duplicado vai com esta, pareceo tornar-vos a dizer por ultima rezolução que o que convem a conservaçam de meus reinos he socorrer Evora em todo o cazo e a todo o risco, sem se duvidar desta rezoluçam; o modo e a forma haveis vós de dispor como vos parec{e}r. Esta minha carta mostrareis aos cabos desse exercito, de cujo valor e rezolução espero que todo este reino lhes deva sua conservação. Tambem me pareceo dizer-vos que logo, sem tardança, deveis logo mover esse exercito, tanto para animar essa gente como para entender o inimigo que he esta a minha rezoluçam; e dentro de dous dias partirá daqui dinheiro consideravel, que baste para paga do exercito e das carruagens; o q[u]e podeis fazer notorio a todos, [p]ara que o tenham entendido.

Escrita em Lixb[o]a a 26 de Mayo de 1663. — Rey. — [Conde de Castelo Melhor]. — [Adresse :] P[ar]a o Conde de V[ila] Flor.

# 26 May 1663: "my final resolution": English translation

Count, my friend. I the King send you warm greetings, as one whom I love.

Having written to you yesterday the letters whose duplicate goes with this one, it seemed good to me to tell you again, as my final resolution, that what is fitting for the preservation of my kingdoms is to relieve Évora in any event and at all risk, with no doubt to be cast on this resolution; the manner and the form you shall arrange as you see fit. You shall show this letter of mine to the commanders of that army, to whose valour and resolution I hope this whole kingdom will owe its preservation.

It also seemed good to me to tell you that at once, without delay, you must move that army, both to put heart into those men and to make the enemy understand that this is my resolution; and within two days a considerable sum of money will leave here, enough for the pay of the army and for the wagons; which you may make known to all, so that they may take note of it.

Written in Lisbon on 26 May 1663. The King. — The Count of Castelo Melhor. — To the Count of Vila Flor.
