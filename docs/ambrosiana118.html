Carmina edita per mfnpflrfp
a→b · e→f · i→l · o→p · u→x

Milan, Biblioteca Ambrosiana · vowel shift · undated, probably 15th century

Ciphered heading over a Latin poem, Ambrosiana A 118 inf. f. 154r

A framed Latin poem on a tyrant carries one enciphered word in its heading, the likely poet’s name. The vowel shift behind it is clear, but undoing it gave no name.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 9 November 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

not solved

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The document
A guard-leaf insert with a 16-line Latin poem on tyrants and war, headed “Carmina edita per” and one enciphered word of nine letters. The rest of the leaf is plain Italian verse, partly Petrarch.
Where
Milan, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, A 118 inf., unit 1, n. 6, f. 154r; catalogued as “Carme cifrato”, digitised by the Ambrosiana digital library.
The cipher
A vowel shift: each vowel is written as the next letter of the alphabet. Only the heading is enciphered, not the poem.
How it was tried
The shift was inferred from the nine letters and undone by hand, along with a plain Caesar shift of one.
Why it resists
Undone, the word reads ?-e-n-o-e-i-?-e-o, which is no Latin or Italian name. Its first and seventh glyphs are ambiguous at the 1513-pixel image size.
Still open
The word itself, and who wrote the poem. Other manuscripts of the poem, or a sharper image of the heading, would settle it.

01 The leaf

The framed poem begins “Altisono similis credit regnare tirannus”. It warns that a tyrant who trusts flatterers and allies is deceived, and that war brings plague and slaughter. It is plain Latin in a rapid cursive with many abbreviations; a diplomatic transcription is in poem.txt. Below it and in the right column are Italian verses, one column headed “Petracca” and carrying Petrarch’s Pace non trovo and Benedetto sia ’l giorno. The catalogue’s “Carme cifrato” therefore describes the heading alone.

02 The cipher

The heading word is mfnpflrfp. It has no a, i or u, and f, p, l stand where e, o and i would. Each of these is the letter after a vowel in the alphabet, so a vowel shift fits: a→b, e→f, i→l (j and k skipped), o→p, u→x, consonants unchanged. Nine letters cannot test this statistically; it rests on that pattern and on the catalogue’s description.

03 What was tried

Undone, the word reads ?-e-n-o-e-i-?-e-o, with the seventh glyph read as r or as x (x being u). Neither reading is a Latin or Italian word or name. A plain Caesar shift of one gives “lemoekqeo”, also no word. The first glyph is a ligature the catalogue reads as m, and in this hand m, n, u and i are minim letters that are easily confused, so the nine letters themselves are not secure. Web searches on the incipit and on lines of the poem found no edition and no attribution, and the OPAC record lists none.

04 What would finish it

  • A witness of the same poem in another manuscript, which would give the author’s name to match against the heading.
  • A sharper image of the heading than the 1513-pixel one served by the digital library.

05 Sources

  • Biblioteca Ambrosiana OPAC, A 118 inf., unit 1, n. 6, and the codex record A 118 inf.
  • Ambrosiana digital library, A 118 inf., canvas 319 (f. 154r).
