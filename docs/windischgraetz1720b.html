Charles VI to Count Windischgrätz in the private key, 1720–1722

Go to the full text ↓

Three letters of the Emperor to his envoy in the private key he sent him in 1720, deciphered with that key, which survives in the family archive: a Spanish marriage for his children, proposed in 1720 and again in 1722.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026 · updated 23 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: complete · On the map

At a glanceKnown key applied · complete

The letters
Charles VI to Count Leopold Viktorin Windischgrätz: Vienna, 13 April 1720 and 24 July 1720 (to The Hague), and 11 April 1722 (to Cambrai). Nos. 2, 3 and 6 of the family’s register.
Where
Státní oblastní archiv v Plzni, Rodinný archiv Windischgrätzů: autograph inv. 694 karton 5 (R5021), register copies inv. 5 book 5 pp. 404–416 (R5022, R5023, R5024). Catalogue item 69.
The cipher
The private key R5018: each letter has a number from 10 to 38 and a graphic sign, the vowels a second number; about 300 codes for words and names.
How it was deciphered
The key, on DECODE and in the same archive, was transcribed and applied by hand, matching the copyist’s signs to the key’s drawings.
What it says
Orléans and Spain, the burden of the Barrier; Beretti Landi’s proposal of a marriage between the Emperor’s children and Philip V’s sons, “beede so klein”.
Still open
The cleartext around the cipher is summarised, not edited; Nos. 4, 5 and 7 of the register are not on DECODE.

The first letter of the series, of 3 February 1720, is in the Court Chancellery’s nomenclator R5017 and announces this key; it is a different cipher with its own page: Charles VI to Windischgrätz, 3 February 1720.

01 The private key

R5018 is the new private key, on a single sheet. Each of 24 letters has a number from 10 to 38 and a graphic sign, and the vowels have a second number. About 300 codes follow, as numbers 39–210 or two-letter groups (ab Anjou, fb Spanien, zi Hof, 69 Beretti Landi, 151 Oesterreich, 159 Orleans), and the nulls are 3 4 5 6 7 9. Several names were added later in paler ink, among them Fleury, Walpole and Windischgrätz himself. The docket on the back reads “Ziffern mit J[hr]: K[ayserlichen] M[ajestät] …”. Mírka noted in 2012 that the later letters are in this key.

02 The three letters

In R5018 the letters are spelt out one symbol at a time, mixing the number and the sign for the same letter freely, and names are given as codes. Decrypted from the register copy:

  • No. 2, 13 April 1720. “Das ob und Orleans sich endlich wider miteinander verstehen werden, ist schir zu besorgen”; and a project for how one might free oneself entirely “von dem onere der Barriere”, the Emperor’s cost of the Dutch garrisons in the Barrier fortresses.
  • No. 3, 24 July 1720. The Spanish envoy Beretti Landi, in the name of his court, has proposed “eine Heyrath zwischen ein oder andern meiner Kinder und Anjou Söhne”. Charles would stay “in guter Verstendnus mit seinen Hof”, but as for die Heyrath, “weren beede so klein” that nothing should yet be made known. His eldest daughter, Maria Theresa, was three.
  • No. 6, 11 April 1722, to Windischgrätz as plenipotentiary at Cambrai, opening with the Emperor’s grief at the death of his Oberststallmeister. The cipher covers what he writes “wegen des Beretti”, especially what he discussed “mit Beretti wegen einer Heyrath” between “Spanien und mir”. Beretti is “erbittert wider mich und Oesterreich”, yet it is not bad “sich des Beretti zu gebrauchen” and to show “ihm all Convenienz in utili et honorifico”.

The marriage theme runs from 1720 to 1722, well before the Ripperda negotiations of 1725 that promised the Emperor’s daughters to Philip V’s sons.

03 Method and what remains

The key sheet was transcribed from tiled crops of the DECODE image, with a confidence mark on each cell. The letters were decoded by hand from the register copies (and the autograph of No. 2), because the copyist’s signs have to be matched to the key’s drawings. The copyist uses one sign for D and N, and one sign for Z and G; the numbered letters around each sign decide which is meant.

  • No. 2, first passage: the cipher opens with a V-sign, giving “und Orleans”. The subject, Spain, is inferred from the context. M.
  • No. 3, “ein Proposition” and “die Heyrath” each contain a copyist’s wrong letter; the words are H.
  • All other passages are H. Letters 4, 5 and 7 of the register (book pp. 409–414, 417 onward) are not on DECODE and were not seen. The cleartext of Nos. 2, 3 and 6 is summarised here, not edited.

# Letters in the second key (R5018): decryptions

Key R5018 (see key5018.md): each letter has a number from 10 to 38 and a graphic sign; the vowels have a second
number. Codes 39–210 and two-letter groups stand for words; 3–7 and 9 are nulls. Words are written
continuously, one symbol per letter. Numbers are from the register copy (SOA Plzeň, RA Windischgrätz, inv. 5,
book 5). Signs are given as the letter they stand for, in brackets, e.g. [E] for the reversed-C sign.
The copyist's sign forms drift: the q-with-crossbars sign serves D and, once, N (in "convenienz"), so the
reading of each sign is settled by the letter-number around it. Every passage below reads as German that fits its clear context.

## No. 2, Vienna, 13 April 1720 (R5021 autograph, R5022 copy, book pp. 404–407)

1. p. 405: "…gut zu machen. Das ob **[V] 23 29 159** sich endlich wider **[M] 36 14 38 [I] 23 35 [N] 29 [E] 19 15 28 [R] 16 14 [E] 23** werden, ist schir zu besorgen…"
   - V N D + 159 → **und Orleans**
   - M I T E I N A N D E R V E R S T E N → **mit einander versten[den]**
   - Reading: *ob … und Orleans sich endlich wider miteinander verstehen werden* (whether … and the Duke of
     Orleans will in the end come to terms with one another again). The subject is presumably Spain, named in the
     clear text just before, which is not transcribed here.
2. p. 405: "…project, hätte wie man sich völlig **37 34 23 [D] [E] [M] 20 23 [E] 19 38 29 28 [R] 54** befreyen könnte…"
   - V O N D E M O N E R E D E R + 54 → **von dem onere der Barriere**
   - Reading: *how one could entirely free oneself from the burden of the Barrier* (the Dutch Barrier fortresses in
     the Austrian Netherlands, whose upkeep the Emperor paid).

## No. 3, Vienna, 24 July 1720 (R5023 copy, book pp. 407–409; autograph not found)

All the cipher is on p. 408.

1. "Was nemblich **29 38 19 69 [E] 37 [C] 24 23 35 [M] 28 [N]** seines **zi** über **[E] 36 23 18 19 [P] 34 16 36 [Z?] [I] 20 23** gethan eine **24 28 11 [R] 35 14 [H] [Z] 10 22 [S] 31 24 [E] 23** ein oder andern **38 [R] [M] 38 22 23 [E] [R] [K] 36 23 [D] 28 19** und **ab [S] 34 24 [N] [E]** belangt…"
   - D E R + 69 = **der Beretti Landi**; E U C H N A M E N = **euch Namen**; zi = **Hof(s)**
   - E I N P R P O S I ? I O N = **ein Proposition** (the copyist wrote P for O and Z for T)
   - H E Y R A T H Z W I S C H E N = **Heyrath zwischen**
   - E R M E I N E R K I N D E R = **[ander]er meiner Kinder**
   - ab S O H N E = **Anjou Söhne** (ab = Anjou, i.e. Philip V of Spain)
   - Reading: *As for what Beretti Landi put to you in the name of his court: a proposal of a marriage between
     one or other of my children and Anjou's sons…*
2. "…die antwort erhalten, das **36 [C] 24** (absonderlich da sich die conjunctur so weit nun geben haben)
   allzeit nun gehen in **[G] 15 14 28 19 37 [E] [R] [S] 14 38 23 29 23 15 16** mit **16 28 22 [N] [E] 23 zi** stehen würde…"
   - I C H = **ich**; G V T E R V E R S T E N D N V S = **guter Verstendnus**; S E I N E N zi = **seinen Hof**
   - Reading: *that I, especially now that the situation has turned this way, would always be ready to stand on good
     terms with his court.*
3. "Was aber **29 22 [E] [H] 38 28 [Y?] 19 35 14 24** betrifft, wer **28 23 32 [E] [D] 28 [S] 34 [K] 21 [E] 22 23**, das wohl noch davon nichts kund gemacht werden…"
   - D I E H E Y R A T H = **die Heyrath** (the copyist wrote an extra E); E N B E D E S O K L E I N = **[wer]en beede so klein**
   - Reading: *As for the marriage: they are both so small that nothing should be made known of it yet.*

## No. 6, Vienna, 11 April 1722, to Windischgrätz as plenipotentiary at the Congress of Cambrai
(R5024 copy, book pp. 414–416)

1. p. 415: "…was ihr **10 38 27 [E] [N] 29 28 16 69** schreibt, absonderlich was ihr auch **[M] 22 [T] 69 10 28 [G] 38 23** eine**19 24 [E] 11 [R] 35 14 24** gerissen, **fb 37 [N] 29 25 36 [R]** unterredt habt…"
   - W E G E N D E S + 69 = **wegen des Beretti**; M I T + 69 + W E G E N = **mit Beretti wegen**
   - R H E Y R A T H = **[eine]r Heyrath**; fb V N D M I R = **Spanien und mir**
   - Reading: *what you write about Beretti, above all what you discussed with Beretti about a marriage between Spain and me…*
2. p. 416: "…aber sich des **69 [G] 37 32 [R] 35 [V] [C] 24 [E] 23** findt eins theil nit übel…"
   - + 69, G V B R A V C H E N = **Beretti gebrauchen**: *but to make use of Beretti is in some ways not bad…*
3. p. 416: "…erbittert **V[?] [N] 29 28 19 25 36 31 [H]** und **151**…"
   - V N D E R M I C H + 151 = **under (wider) mich und Oesterreich**: *embittered against me and Austria*
4. p. 416: "…kan man suchen **22 24 [M]** all **[C] 20 [N] 15 38 23 36 [E] 23 13** inutili et honorifico zu machen…"
   - I H M + all + C O N V E N I E N Z = **ihm all convenienz** *in utili et honorifico*: *to show him every
     consideration in profit and honour.*

# Letters in the second key (R5018): decryptions

Key R5018 (see key5018.md): each letter has a number from 10 to 38 and a graphic sign; the vowels have a second
number. Codes 39–210 and two-letter groups stand for words; 3–7 and 9 are nulls. Words are written
continuously, one symbol per letter. Numbers are from the register copy (SOA Plzeň, RA Windischgrätz, inv. 5,
book 5). Signs are given as the letter they stand for, in brackets, e.g. [E] for the reversed-C sign.
The copyist's sign forms drift: the q-with-crossbars sign serves D and, once, N (in "convenienz"), so the
reading of each sign is settled by the letter-number around it. Every passage below reads as German that fits its clear context.
