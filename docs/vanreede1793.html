Van Reede to the Secret Committee, 24 March 1793

Four pages of marked three-digit groups from the Dutch envoy in Berlin: a large code with no key and no crib, and too little text to rebuild it.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

not solved

At a glanceNot solved · 696 groups, 513 distinct forms

The letter
Arend Willem van Reede, the Dutch Republic’s envoy in Berlin, to the Secret Committee of the States General, 24 March 1793, in Dutch: four pages of code, marked “Secret”.
Where
Nationaal Archief, The Hague, Staten-Generaal 1.01.02, inv. 6695; DECODE R1893 (catalogue item 231, which DECODE tags as Hungarian).
The cipher
A code of three-digit groups in which single digits carry one of five marks (tilde, caret, bar, dots, cross): 696 groups in 513 distinct marked forms, so a code of several thousand values.
How it was tried
Against the keys on DECODE that belong to the same year and office: the 1793 Fagel key bundle (R2846, R2848, R2850, R2851) and the deciphered control R2243; and against the Grand Chiffre of van Reede’s earlier despatches.
Why it resists
None of the keys fits, and there is no crib. With most forms occurring once or twice, a code this large cannot be rebuilt from 696 groups.
What would finish it
The Secret Committee’s key, or a deciphered copy from the States General’s secret files (NA 1.01.02).
Other ciphers
Van Reede’s despatches of 1787–88 to Van de Spiegel are in William V’s Grand Chiffre and are deciphered on their own page.

01 The letter

The despatch is filed among the Staten-Generaal’s secret papers, addressed to the Secret Committee. DECODE gives a transcription (D3608) in which each mark is written after its digit; 696 groups, 513 distinct marked forms (analyze_1893.py). The marks are systematic, not slips: the same marked forms recur, and van Reede’s despatches of 1787–88 in the Grand Chiffre have none.

02 What was tried

  • Known keys. The 1793 Fagel key bundle on DECODE (R2842–R2852): the pages of R2846, R2848, R2850 and R2851 were fetched and their numeric cells indexed, and the cells R1893’s groups would address in R2851 were cut out and compared; the deciphered control R2243 was tried too. None gives Dutch. The 1782 Grand Chiffre (R1024) that reads van Reede’s earlier letters is another system.
  • From the ciphertext. 513 distinct forms in 696 tokens leave most forms with one or two occurrences. A code of that size cannot be rebuilt from so little text without a crib, and none was found.

03 What would finish it

The key of the Secret Committee’s 1793 correspondence, or a deciphered copy in the States General’s secret files (Nationaal Archief 1.01.02). Attempted, not solved.

04 Sources

  • DECODE (de-crypt.org) record R1893 (Nationaal Archief 1.01.02, Staten-Generaal, inv. 6695, images 9360–9363, transcription D3608); key records R2842–R2852 and R2243.
