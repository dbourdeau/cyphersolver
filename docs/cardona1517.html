The letters
Ramón de Cardona, viceroy of Naples, to Charles I of Spain (emperor-elect from 1519). Two of the four letters bound together carry cipher: Naples, 5 October 1517, on the infantry in the kingdom and on the affairs of Rome and Siena, and Naples, 18 June 1520, on the proceedings in Rome and the pay of the troops.
Where
Madrid, Biblioteca Nacional de España, MSS/20210/11/1 and /4, in Cartas de Ramón de Cardona, Virrey de Nápoles (BNE Digital 0000173169, public domain). No decipherment on any page; no edition or study found. No DECODE record.
The cipher
An Aragonese-chancery nomenclator of the family of Charles de Lannoy’s Naples cipher: about 60 homophonic letter signs, some for letter pairs, and three-letter code groups that stand for whole words (que, en, de, lo, el papa). One table serves both letters.
How the key was recovered
By Michael Brackx with Claude Code, from the ciphertext alone: a joint annealer for sign and group values, given the class rule of Lannoy’s key and a list of whole-word options, then five rounds of fixing firm values and reading by context.
Checks
Matched and noisy controls on Lazarillo de Tormes; a statistical test that passes; and two names that the key reads without having been built on them, Borghese Petrucci (1517) and Giampaolo Baglioni (1520).
What it says
The infantry cannot be kept or paid and the Pope is asked for money; Borghese Petrucci, brother of the executed cardinal, wishes to live and die under the Pope’s protection; in 1520 the proceedings after Giampaolo Baglioni’s beheading. About 75% of the plaintext letters read as sense; about half the sentences have no hole.
Still open
22 code groups and five rare signs without a value, and three garbled runs; the published scan is about 1,070 px per page.

Michael Brackx (issue #262, pull request #264): transcription, key and reading, with Claude Code.

01 The letters

The BNE bound four letters from Naples to the Crown under one title. Two of them, MSS/20210/11/2 (2 March 1519) and /3 (24 December 1520), are wholly in clear. The other two carry cipher in separate passages inside clear text:

partdateciphertokens

The clear text around the blocks gives the setting: the 260 lances Cardona could not bring, the pay of the Count of Potenza’s men, the infantry that sacked Fabriano on its way to the kingdom, Don Ramón de Perellós sent to the ambassador Don Pedro de Urrea to ask the Pope for money para entretener la dicha ynfanteria; in 1520 the defence against the Turkish fleet on the Apulian coast and houses to be built on the Monte di Gaeta.

02 The cipher

The transcription has 1,663 tokens: 706 tokens of 115 three-letter code groups, 30 tokens of 11 two-letter groups (mo, mj, fe …) and 927 sign tokens. Each cipher piece opens and closes with frame marks. The system is the family of Charles de Lannoy’s Naples cipher of 1522–27 as Olga Kolosova describes it (El lenguaje secreto de la diplomacia de Carlos V, Valencia 2017, pp. 65–89), with its own table:

  • Letter signs. A homophonic alphabet of about 60 types in use. Several stand for letter pairs: Lo is re, A is ll, x# is qu, and the capital E is rr, as in Lannoy’s table.
  • Code groups. Three letters, consonant, vowel, consonant, with initials f g m n p r s t z; a few have two letters. They stand for whole words. The short ones are now and then also used as syllables (par-la-me-nto), but there are no syllable groups as in the 1511–15 Vich ciphers (a different cipher, with its own page); treating the groups as words is what made the solution possible.
  • A class rule. From Lannoy’s key: the initial and the final letter of a group fix the first letter of its word, the vowel is free (here fed is lo and fid is la).
  • One table for both letters. 53 of the 67 code types in the 1520 letter also occur in the 1517 one.

It is not Lannoy’s table: his values, such as tum for razón or fus for Milán, do not fit, and his letter values decode Cardona barely above chance. It is not the 1511–12 Vich table either. The BRAH 9/15 “Cifra del visorrey” (Galende Díaz 1994; DECODE R9815–R9820) was not compared, because its images need DECODE access.

03 How the key was recovered

Michael Brackx, working with Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) on 4 November 2026, used the joint annealer he built for the Vich letter of 1515: every sign gets a letter or one of the 40 commonest letter pairs, every code group an entry from an option list or “unknown”, scored by a Spanish character five-gram plus the cost of the encipherment. The steps, including the ones that failed:

1Own solvers (letter n-gram annealing, code groups as words or syllables) and a control on vich1511 N.60failed the scoring itself preferred wrong keys

A value was fixed only when it read in every occurrence. The runs’ value was overruled where the image or every context disagreed: E is rr (the runs gave st; they cannot produce rr), A is ll (si) from Ballón, degollado and preuillegio, and ll is e (on) from recelo, terra, padre and vieren. The last two show that values all runs agree on can still be wrong where a sign is rare and its neighbours unsettled. The key is partial:

04 Checks

  • Controls. A Lazarillo text enciphered the same way at Cardona’s size is recovered by the same annealer at 10M iterations; with a noisy option list and the class rule the structured control reaches 88–91% of signs in 5 of 16 runs. Cardona’s own runs agree at 85–94% once the options are whole words.
  • Statistical validation (docs/_validate.py, firm values only, 1,517 read tokens of 132 types): the reading scores −1.96 per letter on a Golden Age Spanish four-gram model, random keys of the same shape −3.40 (z 8.9), and a key annealed onto the shuffled ciphertext at best −2.37 (mean −2.40, z 17.7). Verdict: pass.
  • Two names the key was not built on. 1517, block C: …lo que el papa esto le dize … al magnífico Burgès de Petrucis, Borghese Petrucci, former lord of Siena and brother of Cardinal Alfonso Petrucci, executed in the summer of 1517 for the plot against Leo X. 1520, block D: [Io]han Paulo Ballón and [Io]han Paulo, que a su padre han degollado, Giampaolo Baglioni, lord of Perugia, beheaded in Castel Sant’Angelo on Leo X’s order in June 1520; the phrase fits his sons.
  • Clear text. Readings tie the cipher to the clear passage around it: entretener la dicha gente … pues que no tene con que pagarles against the clear entretener la dicha ynfanteria.

05 What the decode says, as far as it builds

Long passages decode, but many sentences still have holes: about 75% of the plaintext letters read as sense (2,162 of about 2,890, measured), and docs/_check_reading.py finds about half of the sentences (0.486) without a hole. So the letters are classed as decoded, not read, and the reading below gives only the passages that build. The full line-by-line decryption and English are in decryption/reading.md. 06 What remains open

  • Code groups. 22 code types without a value and 12 doubtful, most occurring once or twice. The commonest open one is gie (3 tokens); others are probably names (las cosas de nac y feg); doubtful gnd (mes) reads in neither place. The candidate key is BRAH 9/15 ff. 1–6 (DECODE R9815–R9820), whose images need DECODE access.
  • Rare signs. =, c#, 7+, S, ^L# (1–7 tokens each); o+ may be two shapes (B09 prefers al). At about 1,070 px per page several small signs are hard to separate, and no larger image is published.
  • Garbled runs. A09 pedrona … rroarribare, C18 nuuosacredo, D07 ato…rondacreracho, and fax (5 tokens: esto in C16 but cardenales in D03 and A11, perhaps two groups confused). The published scan is the largest available; a recheck needs a better image from the BNE.
  • Doubtful readings. A = ll and ll = e are probable (grade I, by context); zes and sim are probably nulls; mel mando, toq da and tud dar are tentative (I).

## Document 1: Ramón de Cardona to Charles I, Naples, 5 October 1517 (MSS/20210/11/1)

**Summary.** Cardona reports to the young king on the troops in the kingdom of Naples and on Rome:
- the men cannot be kept or paid; he asked the Pope for a loan to keep the infantry, and the cost requires
  discharging fifty men;
- the affairs of Siena after Leo X crushed the cardinals' plot of 1517. Cardinal Alfonso Petrucci was executed that
  summer; his brother Borghese, the former lord of Siena, wishes to live and die under the Pope's protection.

**Context in clear.**
- Before block A: the 260 lances Cardona could not bring.
- After A: the count of Potenza's men want their back pay.
- Before C: the infantry coming to the kingdom sacked Fabriano, in the March of Ancona; Cardona sent Don Ramón de
  Perellós to the ambassador Don Pedro de Urrea, asking the Pope to lend *dineros para entretener la dicha
  ynfanteria*.

# cardona1517: reading of the cipher passages (draft 4, 4 Nov 2026)

**Status: decoded, not read.** The sign alphabet and the frequent code groups are secure. Two historical names
confirm the key: *Burgès de Petrucis* (Borghese Petrucci) in 1517 and *[Io]han Paulo Ballón* (Giampaolo Baglioni) in
1520. But many sentences still have holes, so only the passages that build sense are given as sentences.

Draft 4 follows a context pass (NOTES.md, step 12): signs `A` *ll* and `ll` *e*, and the code groups `nag` *que*,
`tic` *dezir*, `sal` *cosa* and `gec` *necesario*, among others.

The full line-by-line decryption is `decode.txt`; transcription line numbers are in brackets.

Notation:
- `[...]` unread, `[?word]` doubtful, `[word]` supplied;
- spelling as decoded (*u* for *v*, *z* for *ç*).

## Document 1: Ramón de Cardona to Charles I, Naples, 5 October 1517 (MSS/20210/11/1)

**Summary.** Cardona reports to the young king on the troops in the kingdom of Naples and on Rome:
- the men cannot be kept or paid; he asked the Pope for a loan to keep the infantry, and the cost requires
  discharging fifty men;
- the affairs of Siena after Leo X crushed the cardinals' plot of 1517. Cardinal Alfonso Petrucci was executed that
  summer; his brother Borghese, the former lord of Siena, wishes to live and die under the Pope's protection.

**Context in clear.**
- Before block A: the 260 lances Cardona could not bring.
- After A: the count of Potenza's men want their back pay.
- Before C: the infantry coming to the kingdom sacked Fabriano, in the March of Ancona; Cardona sent Don Ramón de
  Perellós to the ambassador Don Pedro de Urrea, asking the Pope to lend *dineros para entretener la dicha
  ynfanteria*.
