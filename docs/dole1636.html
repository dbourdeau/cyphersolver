The siege of Dole, 1636: the “points” cipher

Go to the full text ↓

Letters smuggled out of Dole while Condé besieged it, in a cipher the Parlement had already warned was broken, read with a key taken from a decipherment written in the margin at the time.

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: complete

The letters
Four letters from Dole, besieged by the Prince of Condé from 27 May to 15 August 1636, to the Marquis of Conflans and Jean Girardot de Beauchemin, who were raising a relief army: the Parlement and the Archbishop (7 June and 22 July), the commis au gouvernement (25 June) and the procureur général Antoine Brun ([1 July]).
Where
Besançon, Bibliothèque municipale, Ms 1594 (Ms 1591–1595 vol. IV, papers of Jean Girardot de Nozeroy), fols. 127, 181, 183, 191 and 200; clear copy fol. 182. Not on DECODE and not in the project’s catalogue.
The cipher
A simple substitution of one number (1–54) per letter, the numbers separated by points and set inside clear text. 23 distinct numbers; c has two (20 and 29).
How it was deciphered
The key was learnt from block 1 of fol. 200, whose 177 numbers line up one to one with the marginal gloss, and frozen. One measurement on block 2 of the same folio (341 numbers) gave 0.907 of letters against at most 0.229 for 200 shuffled keys. The unglossed letters were then read with the frozen key.
What it says
Come as soon as you can with the cavalry relief; bring rations and tools to reap; burn Rigny, Talmay, Pontailler and Mirebeau and destroy the forge of Drambon where the bombs thrown on Dole are made.
Still open
Four words: a struck-out group, a word under a blot, ?atlons and o…. No number for k was seen. Fol. 106 (12 August) is in another cipher, not broken.
Credit
Brice Dudout transcribed the numbers, recovered and tested the key and read the letters, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents. The clear text of the letters was already printed by É. Longin (1912), a fact found after the reading.

01 The letters

Dole, capital of the Franche-Comté, was besieged by the Prince of Condé from 27 May to 15 August 1636. The Parlement and the governor wrote to the relief army of the Marquis of Conflans and of Jean Girardot de Beauchemin, and the letters that reached it are kept among Girardot’s papers at Besançon. Only the words that mattered were enciphered. The rest is clear French, with the cipher passages set in it as strings of numbers.

The cipher did not stay secret. Antoine Rossignol deciphered the intercepted dispatches in Paris, and in July Richelieu sent him to Condé’s camp with “the decipherment of the letters that those of Dole were writing to the Marquis of Conflans” (Longin, no. CCLXXVIII). In late July and early August the court warned Conflans three times that “the enemy has found the key of the cipher”, and the letter of 12 August (fol. 106) changes to another cipher.

02 The key and its test

The numbers run in two stretches: p = 1, then o 5, n 7, m 9, l 11 going up by two; a = 35, b 31 going down; q = 36, r 38, s 40, t 42, u 44 going up. Fol. 200 carries two blocks of cipher, each glossed in the margin at the time. Block 1 gave the key: 177 numbers aligned with 177 letters of the gloss, the majority letter per number, frozen before anything else was decrypted.

Block 2 (341 numbers) was the test, with the protocol written and hashed before the measurement: letter agreement with its gloss 0.9072, against a best of 0.229 and a mean of 0.131 for 200 shuffled keys. The thresholds (0.85, and the best control plus 0.30) were fixed in advance and the key passed. Three numbers had no value in training (17, 21, 50) and were given h, f and x by the gloss afterwards. The value 20 = c comes from sense (cavalerie), 54 = z from the clear copy fol. 182 (douze).

03 The reading

Fol. 191 (Brun, [1 July]) and fol. 127v (7 June) carry no gloss. The frozen key gives French from the first line:

le plus tost que vous pourez venir sera le meilleur … le secours de cavalerie envoyé par son altesse … a vous aller joindre a Salins

Brun asks the army to come at once with the cavalry relief, not to go to Salins to join it, and warns that four or five days lost would be a great risk. He recalls the diversions, the signals and the recognition marks, and the cutting of the enemy’s communications; an attack by day would be more honourable and more useful than one by night. They are to bring flour and corn and tools to reap, the garrison of Gray is to burn Rigny, Talmay, Pontailler and Mirebeau, and above all to destroy the forge of Drambon and the others where the bombs thrown on Dole are made. Of 128 cipher words on fols. 191 and 127v, 113 are certain, 11 probable and 4 unresolved (96.9%). Fol. 127v reads “c’est[oit] un piège pour l’ennemi”.

Fol. 183 (25 June) read with the key matches the period clear copy fol. 182 apart from vous/nous and ou/a: the commis describe the French quarters, with a separate attack by the bridge of three or four hundred horse and six hundred foot, and elsewhere one of twelve hundred men. Fol. 200 (22 July) is the glossed folio itself: the Parlement praises the relief army’s resolution to come without delay and says it is better to risk something than to lose everything.

The reading was made from the numbers alone. Only afterwards was the clear text found, printed by É. Longin in Le siège de Dole par le prince de Condé (1912, nos. LIV, CXXXII, CLXXVIII and CCLXXXVII) from Brun’s drafts, the words to be enciphered in brackets. Letter agreement of the mechanical decryption with Longin’s text is 0.967 (584 of 589 letters on fols. 191 and 127v). Longin used the drafts, not the letters received, and gives no key; his text differs in “par S. A.” for par son altesse and “à plein” for à plain.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Four unresolved words on fols. 191 and 127v: a struck-out group, a word under a blot, ?atlons (Longin: rations) and o… (Longin: c’estoit).
  • Longin brackets “de Mercy et de Forgats” and “accueillir” in the 1 July letter; they were not read on fol. 191 and have not been checked against the image.
  • No number for k has been seen. 17, 21 and 50 were given by the gloss after the measurement.
  • Fol. 106 (12 August) is another cipher with no key material here.
  • The images are from the library’s Mémoire vive and are not reproduced here; the numbers are given as read.

The Parlement of Dole, 22 July 1636 (fol. 200r, both blocks glossed in the margin)

On 22 July the Parlement of Dole praises the relief army's resolution to come without delay, admits some slight danger in an arrival put off too long, and says it is better to risk something than to lose everything, hoping all will go to their glory and the confusion of their enemies. The text is the period marginal gloss, which the key was learnt from and tested on.

f200_translation.md

# English translation of fol. 200r (22 July 1636)

## Block 1

We praise God for the good and courageous resolution you have taken to come to us without delay, to which we invite you again as far as we can, because every consideration presses.

## Block 2

Although we see some slight danger in your arrival, having been put off too long, it is nevertheless not possible to delay it any further, and it is better to risk something than to lose everything; but we hope that, acting in good order, boldly and courageously as you know how to do, and we on our side making the rest of our efforts to second you, all will go to our glory and to the confusion of our enemies.

A. Brun, [1 July 1636] (fol. 191) and the Parlement, 7 June 1636 (fol. 127v)

On 1 July (fol. 191) Brun writes that they must come as soon as possible with the cavalry relief sent by “His Most Serene Highness” (not identified here), without going to join it at Salins; that letting four or five days pass is a great risk; he recalls the diversions, the signals and recognition marks, the cutting of the enemy’s communications; an attack by day would be more honourable and more useful than by night. They are to bring rations, flour and corn, with tools to reap; the Gray garrison is to burn Rigny, Talmay, Pontailler and Mirebeau, and above all destroy the forge of Drambon and the others where the bombs thrown on Dole are made; raids are to go into the Duchy, Bassigny and Bresse.

## Summary

On 7 June (fol. 127) the Archbishop and the court urge Conflans to attack quickly; a letter they had addressed to him “was a trap for the enemy”.
On 25 June (fols. 181, 183; clear on fol. 182) the commis describe the French quarters: on the side of the bridge, a separate attack with three or four hundred horse and six hundred foot; elsewhere, an attack of twelve hundred men.
On 1 July (fol. 191) Brun writes that they must come as soon as possible with the cavalry relief sent by “His Most Serene Highness” (not identified here), without going to join it at Salins; that letting four or five days pass is a great risk; he recalls the diversions, the signals and recognition marks, the cutting of the enemy’s communications; an attack by day would be more honourable and more useful than by night. They are to bring rations, flour and corn, with tools to reap; the Gray garrison is to burn Rigny, Talmay, Pontailler and Mirebeau, and above all destroy the forge of Drambon and the others where the bombs thrown on Dole are made; raids are to go into the Duchy, Bassigny and Bresse.
The cipher was broken at the time. Antoine Rossignol deciphered the intercepted dispatches in Paris; in July Richelieu sent him to Condé’s camp with “the decipherment of the letters that those of Dole were writing to the Marquis of Conflans” (Longin, no. CCLXXVIII). In late July and early August the court warned Conflans three times that “the enemy has found the key of the cipher”; the letter of 12 August (fol. 106) says so again and changes to another cipher, which we have not broken.
