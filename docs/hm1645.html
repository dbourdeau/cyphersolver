Henrietta Maria’s household to Charles I, 1645–46: already read by George Lasry

→

The National Archives, Kew, SP 106/10 ff. 213–247: six letters from the queen’s circle in France to the King (DECODE R785, R787, R790, R923, R924, R929), catalogued here as undeciphered and still on Tomokiyo’s unsolved list.

The keys and plaintexts have been on the DECODE records since October 2020. This page records that and one independent check.

Daniel Bourdeau · resolved 19 September 2026

Summary. Catalogue entry 59 grouped six numerical ciphers from SP 106/10, written from Henrietta Maria’s household at St Germain and Paris to Charles I in the last year of the first Civil War. DECODE lists them as “partially decrypted” and dates them only by the handwriting, so they looked open. Fetching the record pages shows otherwise. Every letter in the run, ff. 207–247, carries George Lasry’s reconstructed key and his plaintext, uploaded on 23 October 2020, beside Sofia Pettersson’s 2019 transcription. Lasry reconstructed four keys, one for each period of the correspondence, and dated the letters from their content (July 1645 to January 1646). The six letters of entry 59 use three of them. “Partial” refers only to the three-digit nomenclator numbers for names and some words, which stay in brackets. Applied here to the raw transcription of f. 247, without his plaintext file, his key reads the letter at once. Nothing about the text is new here. The sister entries 54 (R752), 55 (R928, Goffe from The Hague) and 60 (R789, Digby) are covered by the same work and were removed from the catalogue with this one.

The systems are numerical homophonic alphabets of one- and two-digit values, with up to five signs for a vowel, and a nomenclator. Three-digit numbers stand for names and words (in Lasry’s September 1645 key, 126 the King, 127 the Queen, 279 and). The key of January 1646 (SP106-5) writes its nomenclator as a letter and a digit (n3 the, k1 of). Lasry notes no nulls in any of the four keys. The signatures are numbers: 120 in R785 and R923, 316 440 on f. 247.

02 The reading

The plaintexts are Lasry’s and are on the DECODE records. The openings of three letters give their tone:

ST GERMAIN 1 SEPT. I HAVE VERY LITTLE TO ADD TO MY LAST MORE THAN TO TELL YOU THE <295> OF THE <498>S <326> ADVANCE EXCELLENTLY, HALF THE MONEY BEING ALREADY GIVEN … A LETTRE ALSO FROM THE DUKE OF ORLEANS WRIT AS WEE COULD HAVE WISHED IS IN THE QUEEN’S HANDS (R785)

I HAVE RECEIVED THIS WEEK A LARGE <330> FROM <491> K DIGBY. THE SUBSTANCE IS THAT <528> IS NO HOPE OF MONEY FROM THENCE BUT UPON NEW TERMES PROPOSED … A TOTAL TAKEING AWAY OF AL THE PENALL LAWES AGAINST PAPISTS AND TOLERATION OF RELIGION SETTLED IN ENGLAND (R790)

THE DUKE OF LORRAIN PUTS US IN SOME LITTLE REMOTE HOPES BUT THAT OUGHT NOT TO BE BUILT UPON … THE RELEIF OF THE PLACE WILL GIVE US THAT WHICH WEE ONLY OUGHT NOW TO LOOK FOR AFTER SO MANY DISASTERS, THE ADVANTAGE OF WINTER (R923)

03 The check made here

Lasry’s SP106-5 alphabet was applied mechanically to Pettersson’s raw transcription of f. 247 (R929), without his plaintext file. It gives “…ARIS … IAN … SR … RECEIVED <425>S … DECEMB … IANVARY … THANKES … CONTINUATI[ON] … WEKLY ADVERTISEMENTS … STATE … AFFAIRES HOW BAD … EVER … DESPERATE … ABLE … SUBS[IS]T … WITHOUT FARTHER LOSSES … TILL OUR ENDEAVOURS … RELEIF … BROUGHT … EFFECT … LIKEWISE CONCERNING THE EMBASSAGE AND MARRIAGE”. The gaps are the letter-and-digit nomenclator groups, which the alphabet does not cover. The result agrees with his plaintext. The images themselves were not viewed.

04 What stays open

  • The three-digit nomenclator numbers left in brackets in Lasry’s plaintexts (for example <425>, <241> and <502>). Most look like persons, and many could be recovered from the context and the other letters of the run.
  • Who the writers are: “120” at St Germain, probably of the queen’s secretariat (Jermyn is Lasry’s guess for the second Paris letter on ff. 245–246), and “316 440” at Paris.
  • Tomokiyo’s unsolved list still carries these letters.
