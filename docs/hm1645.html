THE QUEEN IS PERFECTLY …

St Germain → Charles I · homophonic numerical cipher with a nomenclator, Lasry’s “TNA SP 106” key · September 1645 · existing decipherment

Henrietta Maria’s household to Charles I, September 1645

→

Three letters from the queen’s circle at St Germain to the King in one numerical cipher, deciphered by George Lasry on DECODE in 2020; his key, applied here as a check, gives back his text.

Method: read from existing decipherment · Extent: complete

At a glanceExisting decipherment · complete

The letters
Three letters from Henrietta Maria’s household at St Germain to Charles I: 1, 22 and 29 September 1645. Two are signed with the number 120.
Where
The National Archives, Kew, SP 106/10 ff. 213–215, 230–232 and 233–234; DECODE R785, R790 and R923. They were part of catalogue item 59, which grouped six letters in four different keys.
The cipher
One- and two-digit numbers for letters, up to five for a vowel, and three-digit numbers for names and words. The numbers are separated by colons.
How it was deciphered
George Lasry reconstructed the key and deciphered the letters on DECODE in October 2020. His plaintext is the text. Applied here group by group to the transcriptions, his key reproduces it on every line that can be compared; on 22 October 2026 the three letters were written out from that decode in the figures’ spelling, compared with his plaintext, and translated.
What it says
The queen’s recovery and money for the King. Sir Kenelm Digby at Rome finds no papal money without the repeal of the penal laws. The Duke of Lorraine offers only “little remote hopes”.
Still open
About 30 three-digit nomenclator numbers (40 occurrences) still without a value, of the 58 Lasry left in brackets, and a few low figures with no value in the key.
Other keys
The other three letters of catalogue item 59 are in different ciphers and have their own pages: R787 (SP106-2), R924 (SP106-8) and R929 (SP106-5).

01 The letters and the key

This page used to cover all six letters of catalogue item 59. George Lasry reconstructed four keys for the SP 106/10 run, one for each period of the correspondence. On 14 October 2026 the entry was split so that each page presents one key. The three letters below are in his “TNA SP 106” key; the others are a different cipher each, with their own pages (R787, R924, R929).

RecordFoliosDate and content (Lasry)Groups
R785213–215St Germain, 1 Sept 1645: “the Queen is perfectly recovered”; half the money already given; a letter from the Duke of Orléans; Ned Progers arrived. Signed 120.1882
R790230–232St Germain, 22 Sept 1645: Sir Kenelm Digby at Rome, no papal money except on terms: repeal of the penal laws against Catholics and toleration in England769
R923233–234St Germains, 29 Sept 1645: the Duke of Lorraine’s “little remote hopes”; the writer fears a battle for a relief and wants the advantage of winter. Signed 120.722

The key is a numerical homophonic alphabet of one- and two-digit values, with up to five signs for E, I and U, and a nomenclator of three-digit numbers: 124 England (Lasry’s query), 126 the King, 127 the Queen, 157 the Prince of Wales, 279 and. Lasry’s file dates the key to September 1645 and says it has no nulls. The same key, in his files, also covers the sibling records R752 (ff. 207–210, the Queen’s own French letter of 15 September), R754 (ff. 211–212) and R788 (ff. 218, 220–221).

02 The three letters

George Lasry’s plaintexts, on the DECODE records since 2020, are the decipherment of these letters. On 22 October 2026 each letter was decoded again with his key from the DECODE transcription, compared with his plaintext line by line, and written out in the figures’ own spelling (texts/R785_reading.md and the others, with modern English). Two key values were corrected (292 = by, 435 = next), 126 in R923 was read as 127 (the Queen), and 31 nomenclator numbers were given values from the alphabetical bands of the codebook (texts/hm1645_codes.tsv), shown in {braces}; [?word] is a doubtful word and a bracketed number an open code. R785 is signed “A Cowley”. In R923 the figures give “some forrain [?succour]”, checked on the image, where Lasry read “Lorrain support”. The openings of the three letters:

I have very little to add to my last more than to tell you the {business} of the {ship}s [?doth] advance excellently, half the money being already givenn, and the [?remainder] will be got. A lettre also from the Duke of Orleans, writ as wee could have wished, is in the Queen's hands to return tomorrow (R785)

I have received this week a large {dispatch} from {Sir} K Digby. The substance is that [?there] is no hope of money from thence but upon new termes proposed by [the] [?Pope] to {Sir} K D: a total takeing away of al the penall lawes (R790)

and again the Duke of Lorrain puts us in some little remote hopes, but that ought not to be built upon yet. {Do} I beleive some forrain [?succour] is so like to be had that I already apprelend the comeing togethere of our forces for the releef of [85], least that draw on a battail (R923)

04 What stays open

  • Of the 58 three-digit nomenclator numbers Lasry left in brackets, 31 were given values on 22 October 2026 from the codebook’s alphabetical bands and the context (for example {business}, {ship}, {dispatch}, {Sir}); about 30 stay open (120, 148, 208, 212, 228, 234, 237, 257, 300, 334, 344, 348, 350, 353, 424, 469, 486, 488, 489, 500, 507, 522, 525, 529, 535, 541, 543, 545, and the two-figure 85, a place, and 60).
  • Eleven one- and two-digit figures (205 occurrences; 78 and 77 most often) that have no value in the key. Lasry writes them as “_”. They fall between words (“ST _ GERMAIN”), so they look like word dividers or nulls, although his key file says there are no nulls. This is not settled here.
  • Who “120” is: a writer at St Germain, probably of the queen’s secretariat. R785 is signed “A Cowley”, and its f. 215 is a second letter in the same key.
  • Measured on the words of the readings, R785 reads at 95.5%; R790 (93.0%) and R923 (93.8%) build coherent sentences but stay under the 95% bar, mostly for the open codes above.
  • Tomokiyo’s list of ciphers with no known decipherment still carries these letters.

## Notes

- Lasry's plaintext reads 292 as "because" and 435 as "of"; every occurrence needs "by" (here by the Queen, by the
  next, by degrees, by unbusying, by his just acknowledgments) and "next" (by the next ocation; until next week in
  R923). Both changes are in the key decode above.
- "if [into]": the figures give 384 (if); 383 is into. Not checked on the image. "anothere thing ... is grown if
  expectation": the same group, where "of" is meant.
- "sone, sone way or other": soon, some way or other.
- "your" for "you" twice at the end (desire your, if your sent): as the figures give it.

# R785, ff. 213-215: St Germain, 1 September [1645], in modern English

The Queen is perfectly recovered.

St Germain, [1] September.

I have very little to add to my last, except to tell you that the business of the ships is going forward excellently:
half the money has already been given, and the [?remainder] will be found. A letter from the Duke of Orléans, written
just as we could have wished, is also in the Queen's hands, to be returned tomorrow; so on our side everything that was
asked has been done. I hope they will not fail in [?what] [was] promised on [522]'s [part].

Ned Progers has arrived, by [541] I have received [letters] of yours; I have also talked with him at length. I do not
wholly excuse you, though you seem to have some reason for your discharge in the business of [228]: you ought not to be
open to provocation, so necessary are both the effect and the example of your agreeing.

I hear [148] is angry with me over a letter I wrote to you [541] he was concerned. I shall wonder both how he could be
angry if he saw it and how he came to see it; but I hear this from others, and from him [488] nothing but the
expressions of the kindness that in truth he owes me. So I believe this is rather the doing of those who want to give
him occasion to be displeased than the truth of the matter.

The Duke of Bouillon, being at Rome, has made a proposal to Sir Kenelm Digby to raise and ship into England for the King
his [500] 1,500 horse and [500] dragoons, at his own expense, without expecting repayment until the end of the war; and
he has asked that a gentleman be sent from the King to meet him at Cologne, with powers to treat and deal with him. He
has asked for many things to be done for him here by the Queen, which are too long and not important enough for you to
need to know. The Queen has decided to send Dudley Wyatt back to give his opinion on this business. He intends to give
your Lordship a fuller account by the next post. I remain for [?ever] your Lordship's most obedient servant, A. Cowley.

[Second letter, f. 215, beginning in mid-sentence:] ... him, to deal with him. I am of the opinion that this money,
laid out on well-armed foot, and foot raised [541] they will turn out to be men who had served before, will not only be
of greater use in England but can be shipped over with fewer difficulties; but we should take advice on this. I put it
to you not to fail to send me your opinion as soon as [535], whether horse or foot will be of more use in England now. I
see this as a very hopeful thing. Little by little I have embarked on a longer letter than I intended, and I have still
not finished.

From the Queen I must tell you that she is not yet of the opinion that the marriage treaty with [the Prince of Orange?]
should be made [?public]. Another thing has today become more expected than before: a speedy [507] with [237], a thing
that ought to wake us up in England. It bears on us in several ways. It is true that [543] we [348] falling into
the rebels' hands, [nothing] is so likely as that to deliver us, and in that respect, in the present emergency, the [507]
is to be wished for. But as it is a remedy for this great evil, it might become as great a one, by freeing the hands
that our present disorders might enable to master us. But more of this by the next occasion.

A patent is to be prepared for a baronetcy for Master Wright; you have the particulars from W. Crofts, who will lose
[500] if it is delayed any longer. It must soon, one way or another, be done without returning the money for it, for
that is not to be had: use your ingenuity on it, and no excuses, for I will accept none in this matter. Please dispatch
it and return it by the [344] [334]. I thank you heartily for your care of Campsfield and ask you to continue it; he
deserves it of you by his due acknowledgments. If you sent the letter from [212] to you to clear your [488] of that
foolish scandal, I cannot help taking it unkindly. I am entirely yours.

## Notes

- "from the Queen this post": the transcription has 126 (the King); on the image the last figure is a 7 written over a
  6, i.e. 127, the Queen (Lasry: "the King (Queen?)").
- "to tell your": 554 (your) on the image, its 4 written over a 5 (555, you); the writer's correction, kept.
- "some forrain [?succour]": the figures are 341 (for) r a 382 (in) 497, i.e. "forrain" (foreign), checked on the
  image; Lasry read "Lorrain support". 497 sits in the S block of the codebook.
- [85]: a two-figure number with no value, twice, each time after "the releef of"; a place (perhaps Chester, whose
  relief was attempted in late September 1645), not identified.
- "cea[60]": 60 has no value in the key (seize?).

# R923, ff. 233-234: St Germains, [29] September [1645], in modern English

St Germains, September [29].

[529] my dispatch of last week to the King and to [120], there is more to add now. I write only for the sake of
writing, and to tell you that the King will hear more from the Queen by this [?post]; she had a migraine today, which
is a very fair excuse. Our design for the [424] is going well, and the Duke of Lorraine again gives us some small,
distant hopes, but nothing should be built on that yet. I do believe some foreign [?help] is so likely to be had that I
already fear our forces coming together for the relief of [85], lest that bring on a battle, which I think ought to be
avoided as much as the relief of [85] ought to be attempted. Relieving the place will give us what alone we ought now
to hope for after so many disasters: the advantage of the winter without any further ill effects of them; and the
battle will take that from us if we lost it. I am leaving for Fontainebleau this very moment and have no time to tell
you why until next week. I am entirely yours.

Please send me word whether the [257] [300] ever had any order to seize the person of [148], for Porter has told us
here, and his father too, that it was intended by them to be done. If there was no [489] order and no cause for it, as
I suppose there could be none, it will be necessary to free him from his father's suspicions on this occasion; [...]
needs give him [...]
