Piedmont · two-part code (petit chiffre) · 1691

Feuquières to Catinat, Pignerol, 25 January 1691

Go to the full text ↓

Mémoires et correspondance du maréchal de Catinat (Paris, 1819), t. II pp. 283–284 · 418 groups · the surprise of Veillane (Avigliana) fixed for the 27th

The code was recovered from a second letter in the same code, Louvois to d’Herleville of 6 September 1690, which the same edition prints with its contemporary translation; 586 of the 601 groups of the two letters have a value. Bazeries deciphered this despatch in 1893 but never published the text, and his working has not been found. The d’Herleville letter gives French sentences that its printed translation confirms; the Feuquières despatch has twelve unread groups spread over five of its eleven sentences, so it is decoded, not read.

01 The documents

Antoine de Pas, marquis de Feuquières, took over the governorship of Pignerol at the end of the 1690 campaign from the marquis d’Herleville. In January 1691 he pressed Catinat, commanding in Piedmont from Suze, to let him surprise Veillane (Avigliana), a Savoyard post half way between Suze and Turin: five hundred Germans of Lorraine and the Croix-Blanche in the castle, four companies of Macel’s dragoons in the town. Catinat consented on condition of directing the attack himself, sent his “Mémoire pour l’entreprise de Veillane” on 19 January (rendezvous at the head of the ponds, the roads by Gumiane and Javan or by Trane, four days’ bread, tools, grenades, twenty horses to be asked of Feuquières for the cannon), and the two agreed by letter to march on the evening of the 26th and strike at daybreak on the 27th. The attack failed and the two blamed each other for the rest of their lives. This despatch is Feuquières’ side of the arrangement.

The key came from volume I of the same edition. Among Louvois’ letters on the contributions of Piedmont the editor printed one to d’Herleville, “gouverneur de Pignerol, sur Mondovi”, dated Versailles 6 September 1690, whose body is in figures (183 groups, none above 366), followed by a traduction. The two Grand Chiffre letters to Catinat of the same days, also printed with translations, decode with Bazeries’ table and show that these translations are the clerks’ free renderings, not word-for-word decipherments.

02 Structure

  • One code, two letters. 72 of the d’Herleville letter’s 91 groups occur in the Feuquières letter; both have 46 % of their tokens on groups below 100; twenty bigrams and the trigram 25 355 42 (de Mon-de[vis]) are common to both. The union is 179 groups over 601 tokens, within Bazeries’ figure of 367 for the petit chiffre of 1691.
  • Design, from the Grand Chiffre. Bazeries says the small cipher “ne différait du grand que par son nombre de groupes”, and so it proves: letters with two to four homophones each (i/j and u/v counted as one letter), consonant-vowel syllables, a stock of word stems (de, que, les, pour, tout, mesme, comme, lettre, honneur, le roy, Monsieur, M. de Catinat, scavoi[r]), and nulls doubling as full stops. Numbers and place names are spelled: dix, vingt-quatre, quatre-vingts, ve-il-la-ne, ia-va-n, Saint-Ambroise.
  • No order. Neither block is alphabetical; the numbering is a two-part list, as in the Grand Chiffre.

03 The key, and how it was found

Every automatic route failed, and the failures are documented in the repository: a beam aligner for exact known plaintext (which recovers 60 of 69 units on synthetic encodings) finds no consistent alignment of the d’Herleville cipher with its printed translation, because the translation paraphrases and because u and v are one letter; annealers scored on a French 7-gram model with the Grand Chiffre inventory, on the translation’s own 5-grams, or with a partial key fixed all return fluent nonsense, as they do on a matched synthetic control.

The key was then built by hand. The last 31 groups of the d’Herleville letter can be divided in only one way into Grand-Chiffre-style units for comment aura réussi vostre entreprise sur le chasteau de Villefranche in which every repeated group carries the same unit (91 = t twice, 184 = au twice, 135 = ra twice, 21 = s twice, 56 = le twice, 101 = c twice). Carried into the Feuquières letter those units gave heures du matin, du vingt-quatre, m’escrire, duplicata; each word fixed further groups; the thrice-repeated 301 151 310 became Veillane; 150 = ve gave enleveront, trouve, doivent; 243 = ns gave dragons, chemins, maisons; the clause faire passer à … gave Saint-Ambroise, the village between Avigliana and Rivoli. Each new value was checked in both letters before it was kept.

04 The text

Seventeenth-century spelling kept; groups without a value in brackets; a few readings marked with a query in the notes. The Feuquières despatch is decoded, not read: five of its eleven sentences have unread groups

Feuquières to Catinat, Pignerol, 25 January 1691. Je reçois, Monsieur, à dix heures du matin la lettre que vous me faites l’honneur de m’escrire du vingt-quatre, avec le duplicata. Vous aurez veu [par] le retour du courrier que je vous dis [54 257]is [go] exécuter vos ordres. [Par] cecy vous sçaurez que je suis tout prest, [333] qu’on ne pense [9 go ri 122] les [des] que vous [122]u les commencer. [À] Veillane, persuadé qu’ils enlèveront le [19] au bruit de l’attaque de Veillane, le peu de monde qu’il y a et les moyens d’y entrer, tout doi[ven]t faire croire qu’ils ne viendront pas à le défendre sérieusement. Je ne sçais du tout par où faire passer à Saint-Ambroise les quatre-vingts maistres que vous [vous] demandez ; les chemins pour y aller du costé de Javan e[t] [a 132 257 l] vous coupe[s] [249 86]. En approchant de Veillane on trouve deux chemins, dont l’un vous conduit au fauxbourg des Trois Couronnes, l’autre aux maisons [292] et le long de l’estang. J’attaqueray [353] deux endrois, et surtout [mes se 271 se s] prendray garde que les dragons ne puissent m’eschaper. [342 103 190]
Louvois to d’Herleville, Versailles, 6 September 1690 (the control). [8 groups] Je mande à M. de Catinat que le roy vous permettoit de demander la contribution au pays de Mondevis, et que si vous croyez qu’il convien[s]t au servi[ce s] le roy de les exempter, mesme de permettre aux habitans dudit pays de razer la citadelle du[d]it Mondevis, de le faire. — J’atendray avec impatience l’arrivée du prochain ordi[n]aire pour sçavoir comment aura réussi vostre entreprise sur le chasteau de Villefranche.

English translation

Feuquières to Catinat, Pignerol, 25 January 1691. I receive, Sir, at ten in the morning the letter you do me the honour of writing on the twenty-fourth, with the duplicate. You will have seen [by] the return of the courier that I tell you […] to carry out your orders. [By] this you will know that I am quite ready, [and] that no one thinks […] the […] that you [wish?] to begin them. [At] Veillane, persuaded that they will carry off the [post] at the noise of the attack on Veillane, the few men there are and the ways of getting in all must make one believe that they will not come to defend it seriously. I do not at all know by what way to pass the eighty troopers you ask [of us] to Saint-Ambroise; the roads for getting there from the Javan side cut across […]. Approaching Veillane one finds two roads, one of which leads you to the faubourg of the Three Crowns, the other to the houses […] and along the pond. I shall attack [at] two points, and above all […] shall take care that the dragoons cannot escape me. […]
Louvois to d’Herleville, Versailles, 6 September 1690. […] I am writing to M. de Catinat that the King permitted you to demand the contribution of the country of Mondovì, and that if you believe it suits the King’s service to exempt them from it, even to allow the inhabitants of the said country to raze the citadel of the said Mondovì, to do so. — I shall await with impatience the arrival of the next post to learn how your enterprise against the castle of Villafranca will have succeeded.

“Troopers” renders maistres, the period word for a mounted soldier; enlever le poste, to carry off a post, is the technical term for surprising and taking a garrison; Villefranche is Villafranca Piemonte, which d’Herleville took from Pignerol on 2 September 1690.

The Feuquières letter answers Catinat’s letter of the 24th, not the memoir of the 19th directly, and it is written the day before the march. The eighty maistres (troopers) are Catinat’s request, to be posted at Sant’Ambrogio on the Turin side to cut off the garrison’s relief and retreat; Feuquières does not see how to get them there unseen, because the roads from Javan cross the approach. His two roads into Avigliana (one to the faubourg, one to the houses along the pond) are Catinat’s “se saisir des Capucins et du faubourg” and “la teste des estangs”. The last sentence states the aim of the operation as Feuquières later described it in his Mémoires: “ce régiment de dragons qu’on vouloit enlever dans la ville” must not be allowed to retire into the castle. On the 27th it did, because the two columns did not arrive together.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Twelve groups have no value (9, 19, 54, 122, 132, 194, 257, 271, 292, 333, 353 and the opening and closing groups taken as nulls). All occur once or twice; nothing in either letter fixes them.
  • Des Trois Couronnes is the reading that fits 237 322 46 280 153 78 113 51 310 200 (des t-ro-is co-u-ro-n-ne-s) after fauxbourg; a faubourg or inn of that name at Avigliana has not been verified.
  • 241 reads vous in three places (que je vous dis, l’un vous conduit) but gives que vous vous demandez in the fourth; nous is possible.
  • Two clerk’s slips are treated as such: ordi-au-i-re for ordinaire and du-i-t for dudit in the d’Herleville letter.
  • The originals are in the Archives du dépôt de la guerre at Vincennes (vol. 1079 holds Catinat’s report of 29 January on the same affair); Bazeries’ own decipherment, if it survives, is in his papers there. Neither has been seen.

06 Method

  1. Ciphertext collated against the Munich page images (three corrections to the copy on Tomokiyo’s site). Structure tests: 46 % of tokens below 100, no step-10 chains, no alphabetical order.
  2. Bazeries’ Le Masque de fer (1893) examined throughout: p. 272 note 1 says he deciphered this despatch with the “petit chiffre” of 367 groups; his Grand Chiffre table (pp. 280–289) transcribed from the Gallica images into a machine-readable file and verified on the 19 August 1691 despatch he prints as proof and on three Louvois–Catinat letters of September 1690 in the 1819 edition.
  3. The OCR of all three 1819 volumes pulled from the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek’s API and searched for runs of figures. Volume I pp. 140–142 holds the d’Herleville letter with its translation; volume II pp. 297–343 holds some 12,000 groups of Grand Chiffre letters of 1691 without translations, which the table now deciphers and which have not yet been examined.
  4. Exact known-plaintext alignment, three annealers and a design-faithful synthetic control run and recorded as failures.
  5. Values fixed by hand on the consistent 31-group stretch, then each unit carried between the two letters and checked in both before it was kept; 167 of 179 groups.

What it says: Feuquières acknowledges Catinat's letter of the 24th (received at 10 a.m. with its duplicate), says he
is ready and that the return of the courier carries his execution of Catinat's orders; the enemy will not defend
Veillane seriously (few men inside, ways in known); he does not see how to get the 80 horse Catinat asked for to
Sant'Ambrogio (the village between Avigliana and Rivoli) because the roads from the Javan side cut across; two roads
lead into Avigliana, one to the faubourg "des Trois Couronnes", the other to the houses along the pond; he will
attack at two points and above all keep the dragoons (Macel's, in the town) from escaping into the castle. This is
exactly the plan of Catinat's "Mémoire pour l'entreprise de Veillane" of 19 Jan (rendezvous at the head of the
estangs, the Javan and Trane roads, the faubourg, the dragoons) and matches Feuquières' own account in his
*Mémoires* ("ce régiment de dragons qu'on vouloit enlever dans la ville"). The letter's date, 25 January, the time
"dix heures du matin", and "du vingt-quatre" are all internal.

The last three groups (`342 103 190`) and the first eight of the d'Herleville letter (`39 249 190 86 40 103 190 11`,
with `103 190` common to both) are almost certainly nulls or a stock closing/opening, as in the Grand Chiffre, where
paragraphs begin and end with two to four "point ou nulle" groups.

## English translation

> **Feuquières to Catinat, Pignerol, 25 January 1691.** I receive, Sir, at ten in the morning the letter you do me the
> honour of writing on the twenty-fourth, with the duplicate. You will have seen [by] the return of the courier that I
> tell you [...] to carry out your orders. [By] this you will know that I am quite ready, [and] that no one thinks [...]
> the [...] that you [wish?] to begin them. [At] Veillane, persuaded that they will carry off the [post] at the noise
> of the attack on Veillane, the few men there are and the ways of getting in all must make one believe that they will
> not come to defend it seriously. I do not at all know by what way to pass the eighty troopers you ask [of us] to
> Saint-Ambroise; the roads for getting there from the Javan side cut across [...]. Approaching Veillane one finds two
> roads, one of which leads you to the faubourg of the Three Crowns, the other to the houses [...] and along the pond.
> I shall attack [at] two points, and above all [...] shall take care that the dragoons cannot escape me. [...]

> **Louvois to d'Herleville, Versailles, 6 September 1690.** [...] I am writing to M. de Catinat that the King
> permitted you to demand the contribution of the country of Mondovì, and that if you believe it suits the King's
> service to exempt them from it, even to allow the inhabitants of the said country to raze the citadel of the said
> Mondovì, to do so. — I shall await with impatience the arrival of the next post to learn how your enterprise
> against the castle of Villafranca will have succeeded.

*Maistres* = mounted soldiers; *enlever le poste* = to surprise and take a garrison; Villefranche = Villafranca
Piemonte, taken by d'Herleville from Pignerol on 2 September 1690.

Louvois to d'Herleville, Versailles, 6 Sept 1690 (control) read in part

Printed in cipher with its contemporary 'traduction' in the 1819 Mémoires de Catinat, t. I pp. 140-142; the alignment of the two gave the key.

# Louvois to d'Herleville, Versailles, 6 Sept 1690: decryption, copied verbatim from NOTES.md (section 'The d'Herleville letter'). Group-by-group alignment: reading.txt.

## The d'Herleville letter (control: it matches its printed translation, with the clerk's own wording)

> [8 groups] Je mande à M. de Catinat que le roy vous permettoit de demander la contribution au pays de Mondevis, et
> que si vous croyez qu'il convien[s]t au servi[ce s] le roy de les exempter, mesme de permettre aux habitans dudit
> pays de razer la citadelle du[d]it Mondevis, de le faire. — J'atendray avec impatience l'arrivée du prochain
> ordi[n]aire pour sçavoir comment aura réussi vostre entreprise sur le chasteau de Villefranche.

The 1819 "traduction" reads *si vous jugez qu'il convient au service du roi* and *Mondovi*; the cipher has *si vous
croyez*, *Mondevis*, *razer*, *M. de Catinat* as one group (218), *le roy* as one group (102). That the translations
were free renderings, not verbatim decipherments, is also visible on the Grand Chiffre letter of the same day to
Catinat (t. I pp. 142-144), which decodes with Bazeries' table as *importantes nouvelles de la reussite* where the
printed translation has *des nouvelles*. This is why every exact known-plaintext aligner failed (see Method).
