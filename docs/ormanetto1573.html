Madrid → Rome (Cardinal of Como) · numeric cipher with dotted and marked digits · spring 1573 · attempted, not read

Philip II’s letter to the nuncio Ormanetto, 1573 — two cipher passages not read

Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Spagna 7, ff. 303r–304r and address leaf (DECODE R116; catalogue item 259). The record is catalogued as “Ormanetto to the nuncio of the Secretariat, 7 January 1573”. It is in fact the nuncio’s copy of a letter from the King, sent to Rome with two passages in figures.

Daniel Bourdeau · attempted 21 September 2026

Summary. The catalogue’s date and correspondents come from the volume heading. The item is a copy, headed Copia della lettera di S. M.tà al Nuntio, of Philip II’s letter to “el obispo de Padua”, Niccolò Ormanetto. The King says he has been ill and is up again, and answers in clear on jurisdiction, the Turkish slaves in Rome and the bishop of Liège. Two passages, 13 and 10 lines of figures, are in cipher. The address leaf sends the packet to the Cardinal of Como, Gregory XIII’s secretary of state, and is docketed 17 June 1573. The figures are digits with dots, a cross-stroke joined to some digits, and later underlining. The same system appears on the nunciature’s R118 of 1576–77. Five keys were tried and none reads it: Lasry’s 1568 Spain nunciature key, Meister’s 1561 Crivelli key, and his three Como-era keys (one with a Spain nomenclator). Homophonic and polyphonic annealers that read controls of the same length produce nothing here. The passages stay unread until the Rome decifrato or the nunciature’s ordinary key is found.

01 The document

f. 303r gives the King’s titles and the address to the bishop of Padua. He has received the nuncio’s letters but could not answer them sooner “por mi indispusicion, de que al presente (gracias á Dios) quedo bueno y levantado”. The nuncio may write this to His Holiness by the courier now leaving for Italy, and thank God for him. The King will answer the points raised on the Pope’s behalf. Passage A follows. An Italian slip pasted over the heading reads Della sanità ragguagliata del Re.

f. 304r has three numbered clear answers. (1) Jurisdiction: the King has written to Don Juan de Zúñiga, his ambassador in Rome, and refers to that. (2) The order for dividing the Turkish slaves held in Rome has been sent. (3) He will look into the affairs of the bishop of Liège that the Pope recommends. Passage B follows, marked “B.”. The Roman summary at the head lists only the three clear points.

The address leaf reads All’Ill.mo et R.mo S.r mio Col.mo Mons.r Ill.mo Cardinal di Como · à Roma · Per servitio di N. S.re, with the docket 73 / a 17 Junii. The ciphered passages are what the King or the nuncio kept out of the clear copy.

02 The cipher

  • 1,098 digits in 23 lines, from DECODE’s 2020 transcription, spot-checked against the images: A 756 signs, B 568.
  • Dots over digits are frequent: 0̇ 148 times, 4̇ 46, 5̇ 36. A cross-stroke follows 2 (74 times), 4 (59) and 5 (51), rarely anything else.
  • There is no word division and no pair structure: the parity of every second digit is 60/40 at both offsets, line by line.
  • Over 31 working signs the index of coincidence is 0.055. That is too low for plain substitution into Italian or Spanish.

03 Keys tried

  • Spain nunciature 1568, Lasry’s polyphonic key (one digit stands for two letters, 0 null), which reads the Alessandrino letters. It gives nonsense here.
  • Crivelli, nuncio in Spain 1561 (Meister, p. 259): polyphonic, with codes ending in 9, 3 or 5. Nonsense.
  • “Cifra col cardinal di Como” (Meister VI.1, pp. 262–264, from Ottob. 3208–09). Its Spain nomenclator includes secretaries Zayas and Idiáquez, Cardinal Deza, Antonio Pérez and the Cruzada, and it marks codes by crossing or hooking the second digit, much like the stroke here. Letters with an odd second digit do not parse. Idiáquez and Deza date it to Sega’s nunciature after 1578.
  • Meister VI.2 and VI.3. VI.2 comes with the rules of “la cifra ordinaria di mons. nuntio di Spagna”: null 8 at the end of every word, a dot over the codes for che, chi, qua, que, qui, et, mente. Its letters do not parse. VI.3 (nuncio in Spain to nuncio in Flanders) uses only even digits, and this text has 330 odd ones.

04 Ciphertext-only attacks and their controls

  • Homophonic substitution over three sign sets (digit; digit with dot and underline; dotted 0 and the stroke joined to the digit), annealed against Italian and Spanish 4-gram models with a letter-frequency penalty. The same annealer largely reads an Italian control of the same length with 28 signs. On R116 nothing readable comes out in any setting.
  • Polyphonic “one digit, two letters”, the 1568 family, with the pairs annealed through a Viterbi pass. On 1,100 digits of an Alessandrino letter it recovers 7 of Lasry’s 9 pairs at −2.18 per letter. On R116 it scores −2.30 to −2.47, and five restarts give five different keys, in either language, and again with 1 as a null.
  • A first quadgram solver failed its own control (it preferred repetitive strings to the true text), so its results were discarded. Only runs whose control reads are counted above.

05 What would move it

  • The Rome decifrato of this despatch, which may be elsewhere in Spagna 7 or in the nunciature registers.
  • The King’s minute, in AGS Estado (Roma, 1573), if the passages were enciphered in Madrid. PARES describes only Ormanetto items of 1575.
  • Carini, Mons. Niccolò Ormaneto … nunzio apostolico alla corte di Filippo II (Rome 1894), which prints nunciature documents. It was not found online.
  • The table of the Spain nuncio’s ordinary cipher of 1572–77, of which Meister prints only the rules. With the system known, R116 and R118 together (about 1,500 digits) are long enough for a second attack.
