John Quincy Adams at St Petersburg: the lines of No. 88 that Ford left out

Adams wrote in his own legation code, not Armstrong’s; its key was rebuilt from the State Department clerk’s decodes and Ford’s printed decipherments, and the passages Ford left out of despatch No. 88 now decrypt: by the word count of 14 November 2026, 97 of 100 words of the nine lines and 164 of 177 of the remainder. 01 The premise checked

The starting point was a note the Madison editors attach to Armstrong’s letters (Founders Madison/02-10-02-0369 and 02-12-02-0197): the code was “also used by John Quincy Adams at St. Petersburg and by Jacob Lewis at Saint-Domingue; key not found but substantially reconstructed by the editors”. On Adams to Madison, 7 January 1811 (03-03-02-0121), the editors speak of “a code provided by the State Department to John Armstrong in France and to both William Short and Adams”, citing Ford 3:328. If that held, the 580-group Armstrong table already rebuilt on this site (Armstrong’s postscript) would decrypt Adams’s coded despatches. It does not.

  • What Ford’s own text says. Secretary Smith’s instructions of 1809 (Ford 3:327–28) say that “the cypher with which you are furnished” is “the same with that of our minister at London” (William Pinkney), and tell Adams to “obtain at Paris, a copy of General Armstrong’s cypher also, for the like purpose”, that is, to correspond with Armstrong. In 1810 Adams acknowledges “the copy of General Armstrong’s cypher” (Ford 3:370). He held both. The despatches to Washington use the other one.
  • What the microfilm shows. Every coded despatch checked on M35 reel 3 (1811 and 1812) uses a code in which 1385 = the, 1576 = of, 1384 = that, 668 = and, 1401 = I. In Armstrong’s code the is 972 and of is 1354. Run through the Armstrong table (592 entries as it now stands), these pages come out as noise. Reel 2 frame 0155, of 1810, is in the same Adams code.

So the table already held cannot decrypt Adams’s despatches. The editors’ note on the 7 January 1811 letter concerns a private letter to Madison, not a despatch, and was not checked against its manuscript, which is in the Forbes collection and not online; it may be right for that letter.

02 Adams’s code rebuilt

The method is the one used for Armstrong on M34 roll 13, with a second source added. On M35 reel 3 the Department clerk wrote the decode above the groups of several despatches; every pair on frames 0047, 0048 (1811), 0182 (No. 80, 29 February 1812), 0183, 0184, 0185, 0189, 0190, 0194, 0198, 0206 (No. 88), 0210–0211 and 0247 was recorded with frame, page side and line. For most pages there is no interlinear decode, but Ford printed the Department’s decipherments. Coded blocks were transcribed from the film and aligned with Ford’s plaintext, group against syllable, and checked by eye: 39 blocks of 1811, and then eight despatches of October 1811 to September 1812, No. 95 against the Department’s own clear copy on frames 0230–0232. All the pairs, 3,127 from the clerk (with the earlier pair list) and 2,785 from Ford, were merged into one table of 1,166 numbers, each with a grade:

The code is a numbered syllabary of about 1,600 numbers: syllables, word-parts and common words, with 1–100 mostly whole words (13 observation, 16 Emperor, 29 United States, 44 negotiation, 78 President). An earlier version of this page described it as alphabetical blocks of fifty; the larger table does not bear that out. Values climb alphabetically only inside short runs, such as 1384 that, 1385 the, 1387 them, 1388 then, 1391 there, 1392 these, 1393 they, 1394 think, 1399 this, or 1592 on, 1594 one, 1595 ong, 1597 only, and then the run breaks (1304 tain and 1312 form sit among 1305 Bal, 1310 be, 1317 been). The alphabetical slot therefore brackets a value only where the neighbours on both sides are in order, and inference from it is kept to grade I. A superscript o after a group adds -s; a mark under a group changes it (a doubled letter, -ed, or another word: 1107 paper, 1107 with the mark well; 1457 last, with the mark ll; 1535 al, with the mark all). A few groups carry two values in the sources (508 rect and ear; 1570 of and occurr; 1173 when and would).

In this hand 5 and 6, 7 and 9, and 0, 6 and 8 are easily confused. Re-transcribing No. 88 from zoomed crops corrected seven groups over two passes (168, not 158; 1426, not 1425; 302, not 502; 792, not 492; 1061, not 1081; 588, not 888; 1384, not 1385), showed that two groups of the last line are digits Adams himself overwrote (1383 than and 1097 sho, each with its last digit written over another), and showed that the underlined 1102 at the foot of the left page of frame 0206 is a catchword repeating the first group of the next page, not a group of the text. The table, its sources and the conflicts are in the repository (targets/jqa/code_table.tsv, targets/jqa/consolidate.py, targets/jqa/code_conflicts.txt, targets/jqa/overrides.tsv, where every hand decision carries its reason).

03 No. 88, 25 June 1812: the nine lines

No. 88 was written at St Petersburg on 25 June 1812, the day after the Grande Armée crossed the Niemen, when that news had not yet reached the capital. Ford prints it at 4:355–58, addressed to James Monroe. After Adams’s account of Prince Kurakin’s passports and the French ambassador’s demand for his, the paragraph ends: “On the other side I have reported to you what Count Romanzoff said to me the day before his departure for Wilna. Certainly negotiation then was his wish and expectation. [Nine lines of this paragraph not decyphered.]” The microfilm has the triplicate (M35 reel 3, frame 0206), and on it the clerk’s pencil stops exactly where Ford’s gap begins.

The block runs from the last four decoded groups to the end of the paragraph: 133 groups, 129 of them in Ford’s gap, with the clear words scarcely (twice), undoubtedly and In the German Gazettes written in the line. The rebuilt table gives every group a value (python render.py gap88.txt), and all 133 give sense in the running text (python measure.py; group count of 22 September, the word count of 14 November is in 4.2): 106 on clerk-attested values, 10 on Ford-fixed, 13 on uncertain and 4 on inferred ones; without the inferred values, 129 (0.970). In the decryption below, words in square brackets rest on grade-I values; groups in braces have a value that does not make sense in the sentence. The opening words in brackets are the clerk-decoded close of the sentence that Ford prints.

Opposition (83), the key word of the first sentence, is now grade C from No. 73. Four groups of the passage remain inferred (grade I): 1434 journ and 168 ey, 1479 riv and 1382 th. 1398 thither is graded M (code_table.tsv: sthey, also thither) and is not counted among them. That was the state of 22 September. The code sheet (section 4.2) has since confirmed journ and ey, and gives 1398 as thirty: thither is withdrawn, and the word after arrival is a restored [there]. The film also shows the stroke of a group cut by the gutter after Chan-ce-ll-or with its plural mark, so the phrase is “the Chancellor’s … and advice” with one word lost (section 4.2).

3.1 What it says

“He” is Count Nikolai Rumyantsev (Adams’s Romanzoff), Chancellor and minister for foreign affairs, who had gone to Wilna with Alexander. Adams reports that the Emperor’s journey to Wilna itself went against the Chancellor’s advice; that whatever influence he still had would in any case have been affected by his illness, an apoplectic stroke soon after his arrival there; that since then he has had a second and worse one; that the German gazettes had reported him dead, wrongly; and that he can hardly remain much longer at the head of foreign affairs.

This fits what is known: Rumyantsev had a stroke in 1812 that cost him his hearing, and he left office in 1814. Adams’s own diary for September 1812 records Romanzoff speaking of two strokes of apoplexy suffered with the Emperor at Wilna. It differs on the timing from standard accounts (Britannica, Wikipedia), which tie the stroke to the news of the crossing of the Niemen on 24 June. Adams, writing at St Petersburg on 25 June before that news could have reached him, places the first stroke soon after Rumyantsev reached Wilna and a second, worse one before the 25th. Ford printed none of this.

04 The rest of No. 88

Ford ends the despatch with “[remainder of letter with exception of few lines undecyphered.]”. That remainder is on frame 0207, left leaf: thirteen lines of code, then clear sentences (manifestos of both parties expected; peace with Turkey signed a third time; “It is not General Kutuzoff but Count Rostopchin who is appointed Governor, civil and Military, of Moscow”), then five more lines of code. It has 257 groups, 7 of them with digits lost in the binding gutter at the line ends; 249 have a value and 247 give sense (0.961; group count of 22 September, the word count of 14 November is in 4.2): 195 clerk-attested, 23 Ford-fixed, 15 uncertain, 14 inferred; 233 (0.907) without the inferred values. Square brackets are grade I, braces a value that gives no sense, and angle brackets a gutter-cut group restored from the context only, which is counted as open:

The ambassador is Lauriston, the French ambassador at St Petersburg; Kurakin’s note is the Russian ambassador’s demand at Paris in April 1812 for the evacuation of Prussia, which Napoleon treated as a declaration of war. “Even after the battle of Friedland” (June 1807, before Tilsit): France had never proposed evacuating Prussia even when Russia was beaten. The last lines expect Napoleon, if the war opens well for him, to strike directly at the centre of the empire rather than along the Baltic shore. 4.2 The check against the surviving code sheet, 14 November 2026

A sheet of this code survives: NARA M97 roll 13, frames 1056 (alphabetical) and 1057 (numerical), the code Ralph Weber prints as WE028. Andrew Beling transcribed it for the Morris despatches (1,600 entries). Brice Dudout applied it, unchanged, to No. 88. Before using it he tested it against the 1,742 grade-H pairs of the clerk’s interlinear decodes, with the threshold written down first, by his record: 1,624 agree (0.932), and 200 shuffled keys reach at most 0.044. Re-run here, the script gives the same figures; it accepts a pair when one value begins with the other, and with exact matches only the share is 0.877 (1,528 pairs). Adams’s code is therefore the code of that sheet, and the sheet is the better key: 138 of the 823 clerk-attested and Ford-fixed values of the rebuilt table differ from it, by Dudout’s count.

Decoded group by group with the sheet (targets/jqa/we028_no88.tsv), No. 88 changes in these places:

  • Eight inferred values are confirmed: moderate (143), fla (350), ib (1403), negociat (426), bat (1309), along (1540), journ (1434), ey (168).
  • Two groups of the remainder were mistranscribed: line 10 has 262 (st, demon-st-ration), not 252, and line 11 has 649 (min, de-ter-min-ed), not 549. Both were checked here on frame 0207 and are right.
  • Seven groups are one digit away from the value the sense wants (1864 for 1364 tain, 669 for 569 to, 588 for 568 tle, 508 and 818 for 518 ect, 1575 for 1515 adv, 1585 for 1535 al in Alexander). Dudout proposed the seven corrections; what the film shows for each is in the table below.
  • 927 is oud on the sheet, so pire is withdrawn. Dudout reads 727 rather: “to that centre of the em[pire] [rather] than along the shores of the Baltic”, with rather restored and empire written only as em.
  • 1508, 1185 and 570 have sheet values that give no sense (acious or account, wick, tol or toe): each is more likely a slip or a misread digit than a missing value.

4.3 Adams’s despatches of 1810: a held-out test of the table, 22 November 2026

Adams used the same code in 1810. Six of his despatches of August–October 1810 on NARA M35 reel 2 (Nos. 21, 23, 24, 25, 27, 28) have coded passages that can be paired with an independent plaintext: Ford’s print for No. 21 (3:469) and the decipherments made in the State Department for the others. None of this went into the table, so Andrew Beling, with AI agents, used the 22 passages (3,083 groups) as a test. No person has checked it; the two scores were run again from his script when the contribution was reviewed. The plan and pass mark were written and hashed before the run: agreement of at least 0.80, and at least 0.30 above the best of 100 shuffled tables.

Registered result: a miss. The table gives a value to 92.2% of the groups. 73.0% of those values fall on the witness’s letters, against at best 12.5% for a shuffled table. That is far from chance, but under the 0.80 set, so it is recorded as a miss. Counted strictly, the table alone reads 1,186 of the 2,331 words (51%).

Each new blind reading alone: about 0.81. After the miss, helper agents made fresh blind transcriptions from the film alone, so that each despatch has two blind readings. Scored one at a time, with no filter (No. 21 left out), the two readings give 0.816 and 0.814. This check was made after the fact, outside both plans, by our reviewer, a separate AI agent (Claude Opus 5.5) that had not built the package, on the night of 22–23 November 2026. Most of the first round’s shortfall was therefore in our first transcriptions, not the table.
