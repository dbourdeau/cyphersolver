John Quincy Adams at St Petersburg: the lines of No. 88 that Ford left out

→

Go to the full text ↓

Adams wrote in his own legation code, not Armstrong’s; its key was rebuilt from the State Department clerk’s decodes and Ford’s printed decipherments, and the passages Ford left out of despatch No. 88 now decrypt: by the word count of 14 November 2026, 97 of 100 words of the nine lines and 164 of 177 of the remainder.

The letter
John Quincy Adams, American minister at St Petersburg, to the Secretary of State (James Monroe), despatch No. 88, 25 June 1812, the day after Napoleon crossed the Niemen.
Where
National Archives, RG 59, Despatches from U.S. Ministers to Russia, microfilm M35 reel 3, frames 0206–0207 (the triplicate). Ford’s Writings of John Quincy Adams 4:355–58 prints it with “[Nine lines of this paragraph not decyphered.]” and the remainder “undecyphered”.
The cipher
A numbered syllabary of about 1,600 numbers (syllables, word-parts, common words), in which the is 1385 and of 1576. It is not the Armstrong code (the = 972), which the Madison editors name.
How it was decrypted
The key was recovered to 1,166 numbers: from the clerk’s pencil decodes on fourteen frames of the same reel, and by aligning coded blocks of 1811–1812 with the Department’s decipherments that Ford prints.
What it says
Chancellor Rumyantsev had opposed the Emperor’s journey to Wilna, had two apoplectic strokes there, and could not stay long in office; France called Kurakin’s note a proposal it “had never thought of making to Russia even after the battle of Friedland”.

Seven groups cut by the binding gutter on frame 0207 and one on frame 0206, the overwritten digit of 1585 (Alexander), 669 where the sense wants 569 to (all need the original), and three single groups (1508, 1185, 570), whose values on the surviving code sheet give no sense. 01 The premise checked

The starting point was a note the Madison editors attach to Armstrong’s letters (Founders Madison/02-10-02-0369 and 02-12-02-0197): the code was “also used by John Quincy Adams at St. Petersburg and by Jacob Lewis at Saint-Domingue; key not found but substantially reconstructed by the editors”. On Adams to Madison, 7 January 1811 (03-03-02-0121), the editors speak of “a code provided by the State Department to John Armstrong in France and to both William Short and Adams”, citing Ford 3:328. If that held, the 580-group Armstrong table already rebuilt on this site (Armstrong’s postscript) would decrypt Adams’s coded despatches. It does not.

  • What Ford’s own text says. Secretary Smith’s instructions of 1809 (Ford 3:327–28) say that “the cypher with which you are furnished” is “the same with that of our minister at London” (William Pinkney), and tell Adams to “obtain at Paris, a copy of General Armstrong’s cypher also, for the like purpose”, that is, to correspond with Armstrong. In 1810 Adams acknowledges “the copy of General Armstrong’s cypher” (Ford 3:370). He held both. The despatches to Washington use the other one.
  • What the microfilm shows. Every coded despatch checked on M35 reel 3 (1811 and 1812) uses a code in which 1385 = the, 1576 = of, 1384 = that, 668 = and, 1401 = I. In Armstrong’s code the is 972 and of is 1354. Run through the Armstrong table (592 entries as it now stands), these pages come out as noise. Reel 2 frame 0155, of 1810, is in the same Adams code.

So the table already held cannot decrypt Adams’s despatches. The editors’ note on the 7 January 1811 letter concerns a private letter to Madison, not a despatch, and was not checked against its manuscript, which is in the Forbes collection and not online; it may be right for that letter.

02 Adams’s code rebuilt

The method is the one used for Armstrong on M34 roll 13, with a second source added. On M35 reel 3 the Department clerk wrote the decode above the groups of several despatches; every pair on frames 0047, 0048 (1811), 0182 (No. 80, 29 February 1812), 0183, 0184, 0185, 0189, 0190, 0194, 0198, 0206 (No. 88), 0210–0211 and 0247 was recorded with frame, page side and line. For most pages there is no interlinear decode, but Ford printed the Department’s decipherments. Coded blocks were transcribed from the film and aligned with Ford’s plaintext, group against syllable, and checked by eye: 39 blocks of 1811, and then eight despatches of October 1811 to September 1812, No. 95 against the Department’s own clear copy on frames 0230–0232. All the pairs, 3,127 from the clerk (with the earlier pair list) and 2,785 from Ford, were merged into one table of 1,166 numbers, each with a grade:

GradeMeaningNumbers
Hthe clerk’s interlinear decode over the group431
Cfixed by Ford’s printed decipherment between known neighbours393
Mthe split of the syllables or the digit is a judgement324
Iinferred from the alphabetical slot and the sense only18

The code is a numbered syllabary of about 1,600 numbers: syllables, word-parts and common words, with 1–100 mostly whole words (13 observation, 16 Emperor, 29 United States, 44 negotiation, 78 President). An earlier version of this page described it as alphabetical blocks of fifty; the larger table does not bear that out. Values climb alphabetically only inside short runs, such as 1384 that, 1385 the, 1387 them, 1388 then, 1391 there, 1392 these, 1393 they, 1394 think, 1399 this, or 1592 on, 1594 one, 1595 ong, 1597 only, and then the run breaks (1304 tain and 1312 form sit among 1305 Bal, 1310 be, 1317 been). The alphabetical slot therefore brackets a value only where the neighbours on both sides are in order, and inference from it is kept to grade I. A superscript o after a group adds -s; a mark under a group changes it (a doubled letter, -ed, or another word: 1107 paper, 1107 with the mark well; 1457 last, with the mark ll; 1535 al, with the mark all). A few groups carry two values in the sources (508 rect and ear; 1570 of and occurr; 1173 when and would).

In this hand 5 and 6, 7 and 9, and 0, 6 and 8 are easily confused. Re-transcribing No. 88 from zoomed crops corrected seven groups over two passes (168, not 158; 1426, not 1425; 302, not 502; 792, not 492; 1061, not 1081; 588, not 888; 1384, not 1385), showed that two groups of the last line are digits Adams himself overwrote (1383 than and 1097 sho, each with its last digit written over another), and showed that the underlined 1102 at the foot of the left page of frame 0206 is a catchword repeating the first group of the next page, not a group of the text. The table, its sources and the conflicts are in the repository (targets/jqa/code_table.tsv, targets/jqa/consolidate.py, targets/jqa/code_conflicts.txt, targets/jqa/overrides.tsv, where every hand decision carries its reason).

03 No. 88, 25 June 1812: the nine lines

No. 88 was written at St Petersburg on 25 June 1812, the day after the Grande Armée crossed the Niemen, when that news had not yet reached the capital. Ford prints it at 4:355–58, addressed to James Monroe. After Adams’s account of Prince Kurakin’s passports and the French ambassador’s demand for his, the paragraph ends: “On the other side I have reported to you what Count Romanzoff said to me the day before his departure for Wilna. Certainly negotiation then was his wish and expectation. [Nine lines of this paragraph not decyphered.]” The microfilm has the triplicate (M35 reel 3, frame 0206), and on it the clerk’s pencil stops exactly where Ford’s gap begins.

The block runs from the last four decoded groups to the end of the paragraph: 133 groups, 129 of them in Ford’s gap, with the clear words scarcely (twice), undoubtedly and In the German Gazettes written in the line. The rebuilt table gives every group a value (python render.py gap88.txt), and all 133 give sense in the running text (python measure.py; group count of 22 September, the word count of 14 November is in 4.2): 106 on clerk-attested values, 10 on Ford-fixed, 13 on uncertain and 4 on inferred ones; without the inferred values, 129 (0.970). In the decryption below, words in square brackets rest on grade-I values; groups in braces have a value that does not make sense in the sentence. The opening words in brackets are the clerk-decoded close of the sentence that Ford prints.

[Certainly negotiation then was his wish] and ex-pect-ation. But the [journ-ey] it-self of the Emperor Alex-an-der to Wil-na was in opposition to the Chan-ce-ll-or and adv-ice, and if his ac-tive in-flu-ence had not been im-pa-ir-ed before it, it can scarcely fa-il to have been af-fect-ed by his ill-ness im-medi-ate-ly after his ar-[riv]-al [thither], which was undoubtedly an a-po-ple-ct-ic str-oke. Since then he has had a second and a more sever-e one. In the German Gazettes even his de-a-[th] has been an-no-un-ced; but that was a mis-take. At all events it is scarcely pos-sible that he should rem-ain much long-er in the depart-ment of foreign affairs.

im-pa-ir-ed: the groups on the page are 1424, then the catchword 1102 at the foot of the left leaf, then 1112 1433 520 at the top of the right leaf. The catchword is the group Adams meant to begin the page with, 1102 pa (Ford-fixed nine times), and in No. 83 (frame 0190) he codes un-im-pa-ir-ed as 1477 1424 1102 1435 520 (1435 ir, Ford-fixed three times). The 1112 1433 on the new page are his own copying slips, one digit each, for 1102 1435: taken as he intended, impaired. Chan-ce-ll-or is 1385 1025 1016 1457 908 with the doubling mark under 1457, the same five groups Adams wrote for “the Chancellor” in No. 73 (frame 0165, Ford 4:256). The group before his illness is 792 by: the earlier transcription 492 use took an open hook for this hand’s looped 4.

Most of what the first pass of 18 September inferred is now attested: itself (1081 self, clerk 0184, 0185, 0190), advice (1575 adv, Ford 0132; 1405 ice, Ford 0036 and clerk 0190), apoplectic (1141 ple; 1060 ct, clerk 0182), stroke (1586 oke), second (1077), severe (1091 sever), remain much longer (743 rem, 162 much, 888 long), mistake (134 mis, clerk 0182), announced (479 un), At all events (283 event, clerk 0048), foreign affairs (68, 1517), and 1186 is attested as will and Wil- (clerk 0182, 0184). Opposition (83), the key word of the first sentence, is now grade C from No. 73. Four groups of the passage remain inferred (grade I): 1434 journ and 168 ey, 1479 riv and 1382 th. 1398 thither is graded M (code_table.tsv: sthey, also thither) and is not counted among them. That was the state of 22 September. The code sheet (section 4.2) has since confirmed journ and ey, and gives 1398 as thirty: thither is withdrawn, and the word after arrival is a restored [there]. The film also shows the stroke of a group cut by the gutter after Chan-ce-ll-or with its plural mark, so the phrase is “the Chancellor’s … and advice” with one word lost (section 4.2).

3.1 What it says

“He” is Count Nikolai Rumyantsev (Adams’s Romanzoff), Chancellor and minister for foreign affairs, who had gone to Wilna with Alexander. Adams reports that the Emperor’s journey to Wilna itself went against the Chancellor’s advice; that whatever influence he still had would in any case have been affected by his illness, an apoplectic stroke soon after his arrival there; that since then he has had a second and worse one; that the German gazettes had reported him dead, wrongly; and that he can hardly remain much longer at the head of foreign affairs.

This fits what is known: Rumyantsev had a stroke in 1812 that cost him his hearing, and he left office in 1814. Adams’s own diary for September 1812 records Romanzoff speaking of two strokes of apoplexy suffered with the Emperor at Wilna. It differs on the timing from standard accounts (Britannica, Wikipedia), which tie the stroke to the news of the crossing of the Niemen on 24 June. Adams, writing at St Petersburg on 25 June before that news could have reached him, places the first stroke soon after Rumyantsev reached Wilna and a second, worse one before the 25th. Ford printed none of this.

04 The rest of No. 88

Ford ends the despatch with “[remainder of letter with exception of few lines undecyphered.]”. That remainder is on frame 0207, left leaf: thirteen lines of code, then clear sentences (manifestos of both parties expected; peace with Turkey signed a third time; “It is not General Kutuzoff but Count Rostopchin who is appointed Governor, civil and Military, of Moscow”), then five more lines of code. It has 257 groups, 7 of them with digits lost in the binding gutter at the line ends; 249 have a value and 247 give sense (0.961; group count of 22 September, the word count of 14 November is in 4.2): 195 clerk-attested, 23 Ford-fixed, 15 uncertain, 14 inferred; 233 (0.907) without the inferred values. Square brackets are grade I, braces a value that gives no sense, and angle brackets a gutter-cut group restored from the context only, which is counted as open:

If this pro-po-si-tion has in any form been dis-close(d) to the Rus-sian govern-ment, it was cer-[tain]-ly nei(ther) un-reason-able nor im-[mod](erate) nor in-con-sis-tent with a system rig-our-ous-ly and ⟨in⟩-[fl]-ex-[ib]-ly [def]-en-[sive], to meet it with a pro-po-si(tion) for the e-va-cu-ation of Prussia by the French troops as {1508} ⟨pre⟩-li-mi-na-ry to negotiation. But the very point upon which the am-bass-⟨ad⟩-or manifest-ed the most sen-si-bi-li-ty in sp-ea-king of Prince Kurakin’s last no-te was that it held out a pro-po-⟨si⟩(tion) with which it would be dis-hon-(our)able in France to [comply]. He {1185} it as a [dem]-on-str-ation that the Emperor Al-ex-an-der had de-ter-[min]-ed not to [negotiate] at all — it {tol} he ⟨sa⟩-id — such a pro-po-sal as France had never thought of making to Russia even after the [bat]-tle of Fri-ed-la-nd.
[clear text] … an army of re-serve is form-ing there, and it is ⟨…⟩ probab-le that if great suc-cess on the, on the side of the French should mark the commence-ment of the war, the Emperor Na-po-le-on will at-⟨tem⟩-p-t to pe-[net]-[rate] di-rect-ly to that cen-(t)re of the em-[pire] than [along] the sho-res of the Baltic.

The ambassador is Lauriston, the French ambassador at St Petersburg; Kurakin’s note is the Russian ambassador’s demand at Paris in April 1812 for the evacuation of Prussia, which Napoleon treated as a declaration of war. “Even after the battle of Friedland” (June 1807, before Tilsit): France had never proposed evacuating Prussia even when Russia was beaten. The last lines expect Napoleon, if the war opens well for him, to strike directly at the centre of the empire rather than along the Baltic shore. The groups are in targets/jqa/rem88.txt, the groups that give no sense, with the reason for each, in targets/jqa/sense88.tsv.

4.2 The check against the surviving code sheet, 14 November 2026

A sheet of this code survives: NARA M97 roll 13, frames 1056 (alphabetical) and 1057 (numerical), the code Ralph Weber prints as WE028. Andrew Beling transcribed it for the Morris despatches (1,600 entries). Brice Dudout applied it, unchanged, to No. 88. Before using it he tested it against the 1,742 grade-H pairs of the clerk’s interlinear decodes, with the threshold written down first, by his record: 1,624 agree (0.932), and 200 shuffled keys reach at most 0.044. Re-run here, the script gives the same figures; it accepts a pair when one value begins with the other, and with exact matches only the share is 0.877 (1,528 pairs). Adams’s code is therefore the code of that sheet, and the sheet is the better key: 138 of the 823 clerk-attested and Ford-fixed values of the rebuilt table differ from it, by Dudout’s count.

Decoded group by group with the sheet (targets/jqa/we028_no88.tsv), No. 88 changes in these places:

  • Eight inferred values are confirmed: moderate (143), fla (350), ib (1403), negociat (426), bat (1309), along (1540), journ (1434), ey (168).
  • Two groups of the remainder were mistranscribed: line 10 has 262 (st, demon-st-ration), not 252, and line 11 has 649 (min, de-ter-min-ed), not 549. Both were checked here on frame 0207 and are right.
  • Seven groups are one digit away from the value the sense wants (1864 for 1364 tain, 669 for 569 to, 588 for 568 tle, 508 and 818 for 518 ect, 1575 for 1515 adv, 1585 for 1535 al in Alexander). Dudout proposed the seven corrections; what the film shows for each is in the table below.
  • 927 is oud on the sheet, so pire is withdrawn. Dudout reads 727 rather: “to that centre of the em[pire] [rather] than along the shores of the Baltic”, with rather restored and empire written only as em.
  • 1508, 1185 and 570 have sheet values that give no sense (acious or account, wick, tol or toe): each is more likely a slip or a misread digit than a missing value.

The decipherments printed above in sections 03 and 04 are those of 22 September and keep their marks; the readings under “Full texts” are the revised ones. Dudout counts 172 of the 177 words of the remainder as giving sense (0.972: 161 sure, 11 probable) and 98 of 99 in the nine lines. This project counts a probable or restored word as a hole, which gave 161 of 177 (91.0%) in the remainder and 95 of 99 (96.0%) in the nine lines when his check was merged.

The seven groups on the film. Frames 0206 and 0207 were fetched again from the National Archives Catalog and each of the seven groups was looked at enlarged. The film is high-contrast black and white, so a blotted digit cannot always be told. By the rule adopted on 14 November 2026 (README, Conventions), a group one digit away from the value the sense wants counts as read when the key is the surviving key of the period (here the M97 sheet, not the table rebuilt in section 02), the correction changes one digit, and the group is part of a word whose other groups are deciphered with the key. None of the seven turned out to be a plain misreading of ours: where the digit can be told, the film has the digit transcribed, and the slip is Adams’s (the leaf is his triplicate). The same look at frame 0206 showed that the left leaf also runs into the gutter: after 908 with the plural mark (Chancellor’s) the stroke of a further group is visible, and the sentence wants a noun there (“in opposition to the Chancellor’s … and advice”). Recounted: the nine lines 97 of 100 words (97.0%; holes: Alexander, the lost word, the restored [there]), the remainder 164 of 177 (92.7%). Thirteen words of the remainder stay holes: six on gutter-cut groups, to, empire (written only em), the restored [thus] and [rather], and the three unresolved groups. The remainder is under 95%, so the extent stays partial. rem88.txt now has 262 and 649, and the rebuilt table no longer carries 252 str, 549 min, 927 pire or thither for 1398.

05 What remains open

Grades as in section 02: H clerk’s decode, C Ford-fixed, M uncertain, I inferred. No. 88 is decrypted; the groups below keep it from 100%. Measured on the words of the readings as revised on 14 November 2026, with probable and restored words counted as holes and the one-digit slips of section 4.2 counted as read, the nine lines stand at 97.0%, and the remainder builds coherent sentences but stands at 92.7%, under the 95% bar (93.8% on 24 October, before the words on gutter-cut groups and digit slips were marked).

  • Seven gutter-cut groups (needs the original). 142x, 21x, 15xx, 11xx, 10xx, 55x, 13xx: the last group of seven lines of the remainder lost digits in the binding gutter on the microfilm of frame 0207. Context restores most of them (in-, pre-, sa-id, at-tem-p-t), but they are counted as open. The original and the duplicate of No. 88 are not on reel 3, and Adams’s letterbook copy at the Massachusetts Historical Society is not online; with either the remainder would pass 95% (the six gutter-cut words alone take it to 170 of 177, 0.960).
  • Two places on frame 0206 (needs the original). The overwritten third digit of 1585 and the missing ex (Alexander: the groups as written give al-an-der), and the group cut by the gutter after Chancellor’s, both at line ends of the left leaf.
  • 669, remainder l. 6 (needs the original). The sense wants 569 to; the film has 669. A one-digit slip, but the group is a whole word, so it is counted as a hole.
  • 1508, remainder l. 5. le/bable (M) in the rebuilt table, acious or account on the code sheet, where “as a pre[liminary]” wants a; no other occurrence in the decoded pairs.
  • 1185, remainder l. 9. “He [took?] it as a demonstration”: in no decoded frame or Ford block examined so far; wick on the code sheet. The image shows 1185 and a stroke cut by the gutter.
  • 570, remainder l. 11. Valued tol (C; tol or toe on the code sheet) but it gives no sense in “it tol he said”; the image shows 570, not 500 was.
  • Inferred values (I) that the reading rests on. Nine lines: 1434 journ, 168 ey, 1479 riv, 1382 th (1398 thither is graded M in code_table.tsv, not I). Remainder: 1864 tain, 143 mod, 350 fl, 1403 ib, 815 def, 239 sive, 108 comply, 821 dem, 549 min, 426 negotiate, 1309 bat, 432 net, 726 rate, 927 pire, 1540 along. Without them 362 of the 390 groups of No. 88 give sense (0.928). This is the list of 22 September: the code sheet has since confirmed eight of these values and withdrawn thither and pire, and 549 was a misreading of 649 (section 4.2).
  • The route to the open groups. The code sheet gives 1508, 1185 and 570 a value, and none reads, so a further occurrence would not help: each is a misread digit or a writer’s slip, and only the original or the duplicate of No. 88, or Adams’s letterbook copy, could show which. The 1812 blocks of Nos. 77, 78 and 81 and the remaining lines of No. 85 (frame 0196, left, lines 12–23) are not yet aligned with Ford. Frames 0056–57 are a clear decyphered copy, and 0228–0233 No. 95 in code only.
  • Ford’s other gaps. No. 51 (26 May 1811, “[One line and a half of cypher not decyphered]”, frame 0085, transcribed and partly rendered) and No. 55 (22 June 1811, “[one-half line of cipher not deciphered]”), both on reel 3; and reel 2 frame 0155 (1810), a long coded passage with no decode on the NARA copy.
