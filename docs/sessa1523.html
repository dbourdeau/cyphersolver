The Duke of Sessa to Charles V, February–April 1523 — seven “unknown” letters, four already calendared

Real Academia de la Historia, Colección Salazar y Castro A-27 (9/27) ff. 141–441 · DECODE R9660–R9666, catalogued as Luis Fernández?, Sesa, to unknown recipient, Non-decrypted

The recipient is the Emperor, the cipher is the one already rebuilt for Sessa’s 1524 letters, and four of the seven are in Bergenroth’s Calendar from the court’s decipherments.

Daniel Bourdeau · resolved 21 September 2026

resolved

Summary. The Unsolved Catalogue listed DECODE R9660–R9666 (110 pages) as seven ciphered despatches of “Luis Fernández?” from Sesa to an unknown recipient, 1523, with nothing known of their content. The images were fetched and every date line read. They are the Duke of Sessa’s despatches to Charles V: 20 February 1523 from Sessa (his duchy), then from Rome 4 March, 11 and 12 April, and 25, 26 and 27 April. Salazar A-27 is the volume Bergenroth calendared in CSP Spain vol. 2. His nos. 534, 540, 541 and 544 are these letters or their duplicates, and he calendared them from the court’s contemporary decipherments, which sit on other leaves of the same volume (ff. 190, 342–343, 424–430) and are not in the DECODE records. No. 545 is the clear note of 27 April on the arrest of Cardinal Soderini. The cipher is the Sessa nomenclator of 1523–25, recognised at sight from its code groups (gap que, sof de, qib el, coh Su Santidad, bas V.Md.). The key already rebuilt in the Sessa 1524 write-up applies unchanged. As a check, one cipher paragraph of the uncalendared 20 February letter was read with it, and about half its tokens resolve at once. Status: mostly in print; the two uncalendared letters (20 February, 26 April) are read in part only. A full reading is transcription work with a known key, not cryptanalysis.

The Emperor’s side of the same exchange (Valladolid, 8 and 15 February 1523, answering Sessa’s letter of 17 December) is in CSP nos. 528–530. Sessa’s 20 February letter opens with that December letter: A los xvij de dizienbre escrevi a V. M.

02A sample: the 20 February letter, f. 141r

Read by eye with the 1524 key. Known groups are rendered as words, spelled letters are hyphenated and unvalued groups are in brackets:

¶ muy despues [cag] a [d3]-a-do a V.Md. de la con-[…] y forma que el papa [cas] / … el p-l-l-i-g-r-o m-a-n-i-f-… en que se ponian / la cosa … al [kor] con la c-e-r-t-i-z-a que el [yim] de … estonces dava / de la [bog]-da de franceses / el [bao] se [set] de [bla] a ve-r a Su Santidad, mas con color de … de ningun-… disposicion de entender / … y si no es … a lo que V.Md. [kim] se / entre tanto avisarle.

The clear resumes: Hizose asy. Fuimos en Roma a los xxvj del pasado; entro con muy buen recibimiento, y el posar fue en palacio. The full token list is in sessa1523/reading_f141_sample.txt.

03The two uncalendared letters, read through (21 September 2026)

R9665, 26 April: read in full. The letter is in clear apart from three short runs. The first, on f. 437r, reads with the key as Su Santidad por esta bula es declarado contra Francia, and a note in the margin, written when the letter arrived, says the same thing word for word. The note also gives one group the 1524 key lacked: hef = por. The two runs on f. 437v were deciphered between the lines at the time: …al de Ancona (Cardinal Accolti, praised as muy buen servidor de V. Mt.) and Su Sd. mejoro ayer.

R9660, 20 February: transcribed in full, read in part. About 2,050 cipher tokens on ff. 141r–147r, of which about 57% read with the key extended by 15 values found in context: yim duque, bla ir, boz -io, loz obisp-, gam como, and the spelled V-o-l-t-e-r-a, which fixes three letter signs. The letter covers Adrian VI’s irresolution towards the French and his refusal to join the league or give French envoys a safe-conduct; the viceroy Lannoy’s visit; Francis I’s plan to take a third of church revenue in France; the Swiss envoys’ pay; the Duke of Bari; Modena and Reggio against Alberto Pio, committed to Cardinals Volterra, Monte and Iacobacci; Soderini waging war on the Medici como enemigo; Siena after Petrucci’s death; the bishopric of Tortosa. Readings: sessa1523/reading_r9660.txt, reading_r9665.txt.

04What remains

  • About 150 code groups of R9660 (about 40% of its tokens; the commonest cas, cag, min, rep) have no attestation in any decipherment on DECODE. The court’s decipherment leaves of the sister letters (ff. 190, 342–343, 424–430) would value them and are only at the RAH.
  • The calendared letters are in print only as English abstracts. The Spanish decipherments are on RAH leaves outside these records.
  • The groups still open in the 1524 key (vo, per, hay) are still unvalued; ruc turns out to be a verb root, not a person. The sample’s word-level readings are grade C.
