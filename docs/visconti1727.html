Key to the Visconti Italian cipher, 1727

British Library Add MS 32270 f. 42 (DECODE R7931) was catalogued as a ciphered letter of Count Visconti whose contents were unknown. It is the key itself: the Deciphering Branch’s fair copy of the nomenclator it had rebuilt for the correspondence of Count Visconti at Brussels with the Marquis Visconti in London. The leaf holds no ciphertext; the table is transcribed here.

At a glanceNot a cipher

The document
Add MS 32270 f. 42: a ruled key table numbered 1 to 300, endorsed “Count Visconti at Bruxelles with Marq.s Visconti here, 1727, Italian Cypher”.
Where
British Library, Add MS 32270 f. 42, DECODE R7931. The catalogue (item 73) called it a partly decrypted letter of one page.
Why it is not a cipher text
Nothing on the leaf is enciphered. Numbers 1–144 carry letters, homophones and syllables, and most of 145–261 carry words such as Walpole, Vienna and Ostenda.
How it was identified
All but four of the volume’s 130-odd DECODE records are keys. The next leaf (R7932) is the branch’s draft of the same table, and R7933 is a second, French key between the same men.
What was added
The key is transcribed (193 filled places) and a correction is queued for DECODE.
Still open
No letter in this cipher is bound in the volume, and the two Viscontis are not identified beyond the endorsement. Two readings are uncertain.

01 What the record is

From the 1720s the Deciphering Branch in London broke the ciphers of intercepted foreign letters. It kept each key it rebuilt, and many of those keys are now bound in the British Library’s Additional Manuscripts. Add MS 32270 holds the keys for the German courts and for the Empire: Palm, Kinsky and Wasner for Vienna, then Bavaria, Brunswick, Hesse-Kassel and the Palatinate. Folio 42 sits among the imperial keys. The endorsement dates it to 1727 and names the parties. “Here” is London, where the branch worked. The table is headed by the ministers whose names a Brussels–London correspondence needed in 1727: Walpole, Townshend, Sinzendorff (the imperial chancellor), Staremberg, Richelieu. The places it names are Ostenda, Ollanda, Annover and Parigi. The words include Commercio, Trattato, Congresso and Pace. Together they point to the dispute over the Ostend Company and the congress that followed it.

The table is the branch’s reconstruction of the key. About a hundred of the 300 places are empty; 262–300 are blank. Some values have a dotted underline, which marks a doubtful value (Governo, Rialso, progetto, Camara bassa, Arciduchessa). Three places are guesses, written “p.n. of a country”, “p.n. of a place (Londra)” and “p.n. of a Place (Amsterdam)”. At 172 Imperadore is struck out because it was already at 218. These marks show a key recovered from intercepted letters.

02 The key

Numbers 1–144 and 160–168 carry the alphabet with homophones: e = 2, 13, 126; a = 4, 37; i = 11, 131; u = 78, 94, 114. They also carry doubled letters (mm 8, ff 10, nn 39, rr 77, tt 104, ss 113, ll 128, pp 129) and consonant–vowel syllables. The syllables run mostly in blocks by vowel: 42–57 be, fe, le, pe, se, ze, da, ha, na, ra … te; 58–72 bu to uu; 105–110 bi to zi; 115–120 bo to zo; 161–168 co to no. The words fill most of 145–261:

03 What remains open

  • No letter written in this cipher is bound in Add MS 32270. Where the branch’s decipherments of the Visconti letters are now filed was not searched. Someone who finds them could test this key against them.
  • The two Viscontis are not identified beyond the endorsement.
  • Two readings are uncertain (grade C): 33 “Rialso”, which is also written that way in the draft, and the word written beside the struck-out 172 (“Reggenza”?). The other 191 places are H.
  • R7933, the French Visconti key of 1727/8, was only looked at for its endorsement.
