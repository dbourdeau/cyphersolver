Tokyo → Peking · two-digit kana grid inside romaji · February–May 1901

General Staff drafts to Fukushima, 1901

Go to the full text ↓

Five telegram drafts from the Japanese Army General Staff to Major-General Fukushima in Peking, with their key words in digits; Harumi-Matsuba rebuilt the table from the clear romaji around them; two drafts are deciphered completely and three in part.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 10 November 2026

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: partial (88% of the cipher) · On the map

Contributed by Harumi-Matsuba (solution, transcription)

The drafts
Five drafts of telegrams from the Army General Staff in Tokyo (Maj.-Gen. Tamura) to Major-General Fukushima in Peking, February to May 1901.
Where
National Institute for Defense Studies, file 参謀本部 雑, 通報綴 (January–June 1901), via JACAR: C09122808300, C09122808400, C09122809400, C09122810100, C09122812300. Catalogue entry 715.
The cipher
The telegrams are plain romanised Japanese; only the sensitive words are in digits, two per kana, on a grid where the tens digit gives the vowel and the units digit the consonant. Five values lie outside the grid.
How it was solved
No contemporary translation was found. Three words guessed from the romaji right after the digits (ノゾミヲ before mitasigatasi, オオシマノ, リコウシヨウ) fixed 13 values, and the grid rule gave the rest.
Credit
Harumi-Matsuba, with Claude, Codex and Sonnet (issue #371, issue #374, kagigo1901-public, November 2026).
What it says
Moves of officers (Kamio, Ōshima, Kusunose, Shiba); Li Hongzhang, Zhang Zhidong and a secret agreement; a visit to Japan by “the Field Marshal” from Kiaochow Bay; relief troops.
Still open
Five off-grid values (ten occurrences), a handful of doubtful digits and kana, and the rest of the file. The digits rest on AI transcriptions; no person has compared them with the images, and the images could not be reached from here.

01 The five drafts

In the General Staff’s file of communications for January–June 1901 are five drafts to Fukushima written in romanised Japanese, with runs of digits in groups of five where the names and other sensitive words stand. The October 2026 sweep of JACAR catalogued them as entry 715 and took the Latin strings for enciphered romaji. Harumi-Matsuba read them from the images as plain romaji (JACAR’s record text garbles the handwriting) and noticed that all 23 digit runs have an even number of digits: two digits per kana, cut into fives.

02 The table

A grid. In the lower half (01–50) the tens digit is the vowel row (0 a, 1 i, 2 u, 3 e, 4 o) and the units digit the consonant column: 1 the bare vowel, 2 k, 3 g, 4 s, 5 z, 6 t, 7 d, 8 n, 9 h, and 0 b, which takes the slots 10, 20, 30, 40, 50. In the upper half (51–95) the tens digit runs 5–9 for a–o and the units are 1 p, 2 m, 3 y, 4 r; 55 is ワ and 65 is ン. So ア 01, カ 02, シ 14, オ 41, ノ 48, マ 52, ミ 62, リ 64, ヨ 93. ヲ is sent as オ. Sino-Japanese words keep the kana spelling of the time (条約 ゼウヤク, 張 テウ).

This is not the table of the incoming telegrams of the same months that Harumi-Matsuba reconstructed first (issue #371: a serial count from ア 00 to ン 67, rebuilt from telegrams whose translations were filed with them). Tried on these drafts, it puts 21 of the 148 pairs out of range and gives no words. Nor is it the 1902–04 table of the Army’s numeric kana cipher, a different cipher with its own page.

03 How the table was found

No contemporary translation of these drafts was found. The way in was the clear romaji right after each run. In C09122812300, 48456 241 mitasigatasi invites ノゾミヲ (望ミヲ充タシ難シ, “the wish is hard to meet”); its spacings, ノ to ゾ 3 and ゾ to オ 4, match the o-row of the incoming table. Then 41411 45248 kotowa fits オオシマノ (“the matter of Ōshima”), and 64422 11493, whose third pair repeats as the sixth, fits リコウシヨウ, Li Hongzhang. Those three words fix 13 values; the rule of the grid fills the rest, and everything else came out of it: ヒメジ, クスノセ, シバ, テウシドウ (Zhang Zhidong), セイアン (Xi’an), ゲンスイハコウシウワンヨリニホンニ (“the Field Marshal … from Kiaochow Bay to Japan”).

Controls by the contributor: counting the kana covered by Japanese dictionary words (JMdict, three kana or more), the real table covers 58, against at most 37 for 1,000 grids with permuted digits and at most 36 for 1,000 random reassignments. A blind second transcription by OpenAI Codex from the images agrees on 285 of 296 digits.

04 The reading

Digits in code type, the kana from the table, and the clear romaji in italics. [nn] is a value outside the table, 〔 〕 a doubtful kana. Translations are Harumi-Matsuba’s.

C09122808300, February 1901. 02624 14832 4221 カミオノ〔ケ〕コウ yurusazu 1982 1518 ヒメジニ yarutumori. “Kamio’s 〔…〕 is not permitted; we intend to send him to Himeji.”

C09122808400, 14 February 1901, No. 21. オオシマノ kotowa ima soodantiu, mosi ikeneba クスノセハ ikaga シバハトニ kaku kaesu. Tamura. “The matter of Ōshima is under discussion now. If he cannot go, how about Kusunose? Shiba will be sent back in any case.”

C09122809400, 2 March 1901. The longest: Li Hongzhang’s answer to Japanese advice that a secret agreement was disadvantageous (“the advice is hard to accept”), Zhang Zhidong’s request “that this treaty should not come into being”, and “a telegram of the same meaning has come from Xi’an”. Four of the five off-grid values stand here.

C09122810100, 8 March 1901. ゲンスイハコウシウワンヨリニホンニ kitaru nozomi a… “The Field Marshal wishes to come to Japan from Kiaochow Bay …. We are sounding out how 〔…〕[89][80] would be placed to receive him. If it looks as though we shall receive him suitably, recommend the 〔pleasure〕 trip to him.” The contributor takes the Field Marshal to be Count Waldersee.

C09122812300, 1 May 1901. キテウスル yooni … nareri; mata コウタイヘイノスウハ[80]ガ kimarazareba ノゾミオ mitasigatasi. Tamura. “… is to return home. Also, the number of relief troops: unless [80] is settled, the wish is hard to meet.”

Two checks against content, from the contributor: Fukushima’s own telegram of 22 April 1901 (C09122801000, read with the incoming table) says the Field Marshal has received the Emperor’s permission to go to Japan, and his telegram of 25 February (C09122805900) says Shiba is to leave at the beginning of the next month. Not re-checked here.

05 What was checked here

A decoder was written here from the stated rule alone, without the contributor’s code. Over the first transcription (made before the table was known) it finds 23 runs, none of odd length, 148 pairs, 136 of them in the table; The contributor’s four re-read digits bring it to 138: two that the blind second reader also has (42291→42211, 94427→94421, which change kana but not the count) and two it does not (43962→43162, 16598→16593, the doubtful 〔チ〕 and 〔ヨ〕), which bring the out-of-table pairs 96 and 98 into the table. Every kana string agrees with the contributor’s. The incoming table, decoded beside it, gives nothing. The JACAR images could not be reached from this session, so no digit was checked against them, and the dictionary control was not re-run (there is no Japanese model in the project’s language library).

06 Still open

  • Values 59, 60, 80, 89, 99, ten occurrences, outside the grid. They look like word codes (the contributor suggests countries or offices: Russia, China, Germany, a minister), but nothing confirms them.
  • C09122809400: the first group (99506, or 77306 for the second reader); 22652, which gives マ where ヤ would make ミツヤク, 密約; two digits re-read with the table in hand that the blind reader did not confirm (〔チ〕, 〔ヨ〕).
  • C09122810100: 09626 gives ミ where マ would make マンユウ, 漫遊; 〔モチ〕. C09122808300: 〔ケ〕コウ, word not identified.
  • The rest of the file after C09122810000 has not been viewed; which officers Kamio, Ōshima, Kusunose and Shiba are has not been checked.
  • The transcriptions are AI work (Claude first, Codex blind second); no person has compared them with the images.

# C09122810100: English

The Field Marshal wishes to come to Japan from Kiaochow Bay .... We are sounding out how 〔...〕[89][80] would be placed to receive him. If it looks as though we shall receive him suitably, recommend the 〔pleasure〕 trip to him. Tamura.

1 May 1901

Tamura to Fukushima, 1 May 1901: someone is to return home; the number of relief troops cannot be met until a matter (word code 80) is settled.
