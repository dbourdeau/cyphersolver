two-digit figures, most ending in 5 or 0, set inside clear Italian

Eger → Cardinal Ippolito d’Este · two-digit numerical cipher · 25 July 1520

Alfonso Cistarelli, Eger, 25 July 1520

Go to the transcriptions ↓

A page of two-digit figures from the Cardinal’s agent at Eger: the key numbers the alphabet in steps of five, but the output is still fragments, and the photograph does not settle the digits.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026 · updated 26 November 2026

Method: ciphertext-only break · Extent: none (key obtained, text not read)

With Brice Dudout (new cipher) and Chimdindu Denalex Orakwue (key corrected)

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · none (key obtained, text not read)

The letter
Alfonso Cistarelli, Cardinal Ippolito I d’Este’s agent at Eger, to the Cardinal, 25 July 1520: four pages, the cipher on the second, clear text before and after it.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Modena, Ambasciatori, Ungheria b. 4, Cistarelli no. 3 (b. 4/26); DECODE R1137 (catalogue item 158), Vestigia 1904.
The cipher
Two-digit figures written without spaces, most ending in 5 or 0, with a few letter signs (f, k, u, y): 584 figure groups and 83 other signs, set inside clear Italian. The main figures number the alphabet in steps of five (a = 5, b = 10 … t = 95, with 50 unused and 96 for u/v), with ten secondary values beside them.
How it was tried
A ciphertext-only solver (simulated annealing on a sixteenth-century Italian model), checked against the same figures shuffled; cribs from Caprile’s letters of the same months, checked against control words. Then Chimdindu Denalex Orakwue saw the step-of-five pattern in the figures (issue #843).
The key
Orakwue’s table has 29 values. On the two stored transcriptions it gives a value to 675 of 708 tokens; 33 are left without one, one of them a blot. That is a count of tokens with a value, not of text read.
Why it resists
With the key, several lines still give no coherent prose, and no share of sure words can be given. Two readings of the same lines disagree on one group in seven, and no key or decipherment exists in the file, on DECODE or in print.
The October letter
Cistarelli no. 7 (Kraków, 14 Oct 1520, to Duke Alfonso I; Vestigia 1908), in the same figures. Only one line of its cipher is legible in the surviving photocopy, and it does not decode.
What would finish it
A better photograph from the archive, to settle the digits and test Orakwue’s proposed digit corrections, then the lines with the key again.
Other ciphers
Giuliano Caprile’s letters in the same box are in other ciphers, each with its own page: his 1520–21 sign cipher and his 1519 two-tier cipher.

01 The letter

Cistarelli was the Cardinal’s agent at Eger, the Hungarian see Ippolito d’Este held; Vestigia has twenty of his records. Two of them carry cipher, no. 3 of 25 July 1520 and no. 7 of 14 October 1520 (section 02), and neither has a decipherment, a clear copy or a gloss. Pages 1 and 3 are clear text and page 4 the address; the cipher is on page 2. DECODE’s “partially decrypted” probably reflects the clear passages (this is not from the sources and was not checked here).

02 The October letter

Brice Dudout pointed out a second letter in the same figures (28 October 2026): Cistarelli no. 7, 14 October 1520, Vestigia 1908, not on DECODE. Vestigia names Duke Alfonso I as the addressee and Kraków as the place, and the clear text agrees. It answers the Duke’s letter about “la grave infirmità e disperata salute” of the writer’s lord and reports his death: Cardinal Ippolito died on 3 September 1520. It goes on about the Queen, the King of Hungary, and the bishopric and castle of Eger. The groups are the same as in July: two digits ending in 0 or 5, mixed with the letter signs f, k, u, y, x, h, g and m.

Line 7 can be transcribed (34 tokens), and so can seven groups at the end of line 27. The roughly six lines of cipher below them are mostly lost in the dark bands of the old photocopy that the online image shows. With the July letter’s search key, line 7 decodes as “adlmodegmo?racentogseti?tipodtare”. A new search over both letters gives “…framento…portare”. Orakwue’s step-of-five key gives “qmllomegla?ramento?se?a?tiportare”. These are fragments, not a reading. Brice Dudout has asked the archive for the original.

03 What was tried

  • Ciphertext-only search. Simulated annealing on the 667 tokens settles part of a key (e = 0, 25; a = 86; o = 70; i = 45; t = 95; r = 85; s = 90; n = 65) and phrases such as “lettere per”, “presto”, “saria meglio”, “non se poteria” and “dua milla” (grade M). On the same tokens shuffled the score is lower (−3.05 against −2.69 per token), so there is language in it; but the output does not run into sentences.
  • The step-of-five key (21 Nov 2026). Chimdindu Denalex Orakwue noticed that the main figures number the alphabet in order, five apart: a = 5, b = 10, c = 15 and so on to t = 95, with 50 unused and 96 for u/v. To these he added ten secondary values (86 and k for a, 0 for e, u for l, x for m, y for n, z for o, h and 84 for r, f for t). The table agrees with every value the search above had settled. Applied to the unchanged transcriptions it reproduces the 27 lines he filed, and gives a value to 675 of the 708 tokens. Words such as “ducati”, “cambiare” and “lettere per” come out, but several lines still give no coherent prose, so this is a key in part and not a reading. His proposed corrections of single digits are not applied until a better image can check them. Table, output and check: orakwue-step5/.
  • Known keys. Every key of the series, the archive and the decade available here is an alphabet of signs, not of figures (Caprile’s 1519 and 1520–21 keys, Bonzagni 1512–14, Sadoleto 1482, Maffeo 1489); DECODE has no Este key of 1490–1550. None could be applied.
  • Cribs. Words from Caprile’s letters of the same months (custode, Agria, Alfonso, debito, Buda, episcopato) were placed at every consistent position and re-annealed, against six control words that should not occur. The cribs gain no more than the controls.
  • The transcription. A second reading of four lines against the image disagrees with the first on 13 of 92 groups (8 or 9, 4 or 9, 0, 5 or 6, 80 or 86, and where a group starts). The digits are about 25 pixels high on a faded page, so the image itself does not settle them.

04 What would finish it

Better photographs of Cistarelli no. 3, and of the original of no. 7 rather than a photocopy, from the Archivio di Stato di Modena, then a fresh transcription, a check of the proposed digit corrections, and the key applied again to the lines that still give no prose. Whether noise in the transcription is really what stops the reading is a judgement, not a proof. Key recovered in part from the ciphertext; the text is not read.
