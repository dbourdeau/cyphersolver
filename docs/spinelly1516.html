Thomas Spinelly to Henry VIII, Brussels, 1 February 1517

British Library, Cotton MS Galba B V ff. 40–41 (DECODE R8416, “Thommas Spynelly to unknown recipient”, 1516, non-decrypted). A clear English despatch with five lines in cipher. The cipher is a simple substitution with a few word signs; the passage is read in full.

Summary. The Unsolved Catalogue (item 100) listed this DECODE record as a ciphered letter of “Thommas Spynelly” to an unknown recipient, London, February 1516, not decrypted. The four images show Spinelly’s own despatch to Henry VIII (“Please it your hyghnes”), written at Brussels on 1 February “M.D.XVJ”, old style, so 1517. It reports the Emperor Maximilian and King Charles at Lier and Mechelen, the French embassy at Vilvoorde and the handing over of Verona to Lautrec. All of it is in clear except five lines on f. 40v. There is no contemporary decipherment. The cipher is a substitution of invented signs with word division kept. A repeated nine-sign word fits “audensier”, spelled that way in clear on the same page, and gives the rest of the alphabet. Deciphered, the passage says that the Audiencier and Luigi Marliano expect the King’s entry into Brussels at the end of the week, and the Emperor at Cambrai about the 15th. Letters and Papers II no. 2862 (1864) already summarised the Cambrai clause. It gave no text of the passage and does not mention Marliano or the entry. Two word signs are read from context: the star is the Emperor, and one sign is probably the King.

01 The document

DECODE imaged four pages. In the order of the leaves they are P1 f. 40r, P3 f. 40v, P4 f. 41r and P2 f. 41v, which carries the end of the letter and the subscription “your most humble and … seruant Thommas Spynelly”. The dating clause reads “Written at Methlyn Bruxells the fyrst day of february M.D.XVJ”. The old foliation 33–34 is the one used by Letters and Papers. So DECODE’s place (London) and recipient (unknown) are wrong: the letter was written in Brussels to the King. The year is 1517.

Spinelly was Henry’s resident agent in the Low Countries. The despatch reports the meetings of Maximilian and Charles with Chièvres, the Chancellor, the Audiencier and Hans Renner, the French embassy, and the cession of Verona to Lautrec under the treaty of Brussels. It also covers Cardinal Schiner’s anger at that cession and the Emperor’s intention to keep his amity with England.

02 The cipher

The cipher is a monoalphabetic substitution. Most signs are Greek or Latin letter forms given other values. Word division is kept, and a few signs stand for whole words.

Word signs: ϑ = of; ꝁ = this; w with a bar = that; a star with a superscript = the Emperor; and a small sign after “the” at the end of line 2, probably the King.

03 The reading

The clear text runs into the cipher mid-sentence (“and of this opinion is the”):

Audensier and misser Loys de Merlien. The whiche Audensier saythe that the [King] in the laterende of this wicke shall make his entree in to this town, and [the Emperor] about the xv day of the present monethe so to Cambray.

The Audiencier is the head of Charles’s chancery secretariat, named only by office throughout the letter (probably Philippe Haneton). “Misser Loys de Merlien” is Luigi Marliano, Charles’s physician and councillor, later bishop of Tuy. The clear postscript on f. 41v, written after the King came back from Vilvoorde and the Emperor went back to Mechelen, says that the coming of “his magestye” into this town is put off because he “loke[th] for certeyn princes oute of Almayne, the which he wol have at his entree”. “His majesty” there may be the Emperor, which leaves open whose entry the cipher means (see section 05). The Emperor did go to Cambrai, where the treaty with France was signed on 11 March 1517.

04 Method

The nine-sign word φδʒobʃeoc occurs twice, and its fourth and eighth signs are the same. “Audensier”, spelled that way in clear two paragraphs above, fits the pattern. It gives a, u, d, e, n, s, i and r; φbʒ is then and. The frequent kρo is the, and k7 is to. After that the rest is plain reading: present monethe, entree, town, and 4φyτcφe = Cambray. Every value except p and b occurs at least twice. Letters and Papers was checked only after the reading, and its summary (“about the 15th of this month the Emperor will go to Cambray”) agrees with it.

05 What remains uncertain

  • The star sign = the Emperor: H, from context and the calendar.
  • xbʒφe = “xv day”, with b written for the numeral v: H, from the calendar’s “15th”.
  • The sign after “the” at the end of line 2 = the King: C. It is the subject of “make his entree” and sits against the gutter. The Emperor has his own sign later in the same sentence, which favours the King.
  • σ as a second sign for l in “Loys”: C.
  • The Audiencier as Philippe Haneton: C.
