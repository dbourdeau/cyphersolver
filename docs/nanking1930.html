Nanking → Lhasa · plain Chinese telegraph code · September 1930

Chiang Kai-shek and Ma Fuxiang to the 13th Dalai Lama, September 1930

→

Go to the full text ↓

Two Nanking telegrams to the Dalai Lama, copied on the Indian line and summarised in a British abstract of 1930, are given in Chinese by Brice Dudout with the public telegraph code; nine codes of 230 are emended or open.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 12 November 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: partial (96% of the cipher) · On the map

Contributed by Brice Dudout (solution, transcription, verification) · Also credited · Foreign and Political Department, Government of India (earlier solution) · National Archives of India (New Delhi) (pointer to a source)

At a glanceKnown key applied · partial (96% of the cipher)

The telegrams
Chiang Kai-shek (155 groups) and Ma Fuxiang, Chairman of the Mongolian and Tibetan Affairs Commission (75 groups), from the Nanking telegraph office to "Dalai Buddha, Lhasa", September 1930.
Where
National Archives of India, External Affairs Department, Sikkim (Political), 1930, File No. 5-P, pp. 14–15 (Abhilekh Patal NAIDLF03824535). Not on DECODE.
The code
The Chinese standard telegraph code, four digits for each character, with no encipherment. The last group of each telegram is a day code.
How it was decoded
The public code table was applied to the groups in typing order. 221 of 230 codes give the character as written; six fit only after a change of one digit and three give no sense.
What it says
Chiang thanks the Dalai Lama for his letter brought by Kun Kanpu and writes 從此中藏一家，完成統一, "from now on China and Tibet are one family and unification is completed".
Credit
Read by Brice Dudout with an AI agent (pull request #434). The Government of India had an English abstract of the two messages in 1930; the file holds no Chinese text. Lead found in Daniel Bourdeau's Abhilekh Patal sweep.
Still open
The Chinese reading was made by the contributor’s agent and re-read at integration; no sinologist has re-read it. Three codes unresolved and six emended; Ma Fuxiang's telegram stays just under 95% (94.7%). The transcription comes from the viewer's text layer and has not been checked against the images by a second reader. The Dalai Lama's code-word telegrams in the same file are not read.

01 The file

The file is titled "Interception of cypher telegrams addressed to Dalai Buddha, Lhasa, from Nanking Hojep telegraph office" and has 49 pages. Brice Dudout read it after logging in to the archive's online reader and described these pages:

PageContent
14Nankinghojep to Dalai Buddha, Lhasa: 155 four-digit groups, typed seven to a line (Chiang Kai-shek).
15Nankinghoje to Dalai Buddha, Lhasa: 75 groups (Ma Fuxiang).
9, 11, 12, 22–23Telegrams of the Dalai Lama in ten-letter code words (those dated are of 22 September and 13 October 1930): another system, not read (section 06).
16Weir, Political Officer in Sikkim, Gyantse, 6 October 1930, sends "the cypher and marginally noted code messages" to Simla and asks for paraphrases.
18Foreign and Political Department to Weir, 29 September 1930: an English abstract of the Nanking messages.

No image is shown on this page. The pages are on Abhilekh Patal behind a registered login, the site shows a human check to scripts, and the contributor sent transcriptions only, since no reuse licence is shown for the images. The project has not seen the images. What is said here about the file rests on the contributor's description of it.

The groups were taken from the reader's OCR text layer and checked by the contributor's agent against his screen captures. Two groups, 6615 on p. 14 and 7308 on p. 15, were half hidden by the cursor on the captures and are confirmed by the sense only.

The same public code table reads two other entries. The 20 plain-code telegrams of Sun Yat-sen's circle copied in Japan in 1913–16 are in the repository folder ctcplain1913, listed on the intercepts page. A Peking telegram of October 1912 on the Dalai Lama's title, from another file of the same archive and sent in five-digit groups, has its own page.

02 Chiang Kai-shek's telegram

棍堪布來，面遞惠書及答覆八事，{均}悉。並荷賜寄玉照、珍物，傳述嘉言，欣感無似。中藏關係本極密切，前因主國者不以真誠相見，致失國師及藏衞民衆心理，隔閡未去，{音}問遂疏。今幸國師慧[?]明心，洞達時局，以真誠擁護我中央政府。據棍堪布面陳各節，益見愛國之誠溢於言表。從此中藏一家，完成統一，至堪欽慰。除{切}囑蒙藏委員會馬委員長福祥慎重辦理外，先此覆謝，並祝健康。[?]蔣中正。元。

"Kun Kanpu has come and handed me in person your letter and your replies on the eight points; all noted. I am also obliged for the photograph and the precious gifts you sent, and for the kind words he conveyed; my joy and gratitude are beyond words. China and Tibet have always been very closely bound. Formerly, because those who ruled the country did not deal with you in sincerity, the hearts of the State Preceptor and of the people of Ü-Tsang were lost; estrangement remained and correspondence became rare. Now, happily, the State Preceptor, wise [?] and clear-minded, understands the times and sincerely supports our central government. All that Kun Kanpu has reported to me in person shows still more the patriotic sincerity that overflows your words. From now on China and Tibet are one family and unification is completed, to my great satisfaction. Besides earnestly instructing Ma Fuxiang, Chairman of the Mongolian and Tibetan Affairs Commission, to handle the matter with care, I first reply with my thanks and wish you good health. [?] Chiang Chung-cheng. [13th.]"

Braces mark a code emended by one digit, [?] an unresolved code; the punctuation is added. 國師, "State Preceptor", is the Dalai Lama. 蔣中正 (Chiang Chung-cheng) is Chiang Kai-shek. 155 groups: 150 give the character as written, 3 are emended, 2 are unresolved.

03 Ma Fuxiang's telegram

暌違廿餘載，馳念良殷。頃晤棍堪布，詢悉道躬佳勝，至深欣慰。國師竭誠擁護中央，極荷{蔣}主席嘉{許}，詳情已由主席電覆。所有尊事，福祥{竭}誠辦理，以期公私兩[?]。祈釋慧念。敬祝嘉福。馬福祥。庚。

"We have been apart for over twenty years and I have often thought of you. I have just met Kun Kanpu and learned that Your Holiness is in good health, to my deep satisfaction. The State Preceptor's devoted support of the central government has earned Chairman Chiang's high praise; the details have been answered by the Chairman by telegram. All your affairs I, Fuxiang, will handle with full devotion, so that public and private interests are both [served]. Please set your mind at rest. With respectful good wishes. Ma Fuxiang. [8th.]"

75 groups: 71 give the character as written, 3 are emended, 1 is unresolved.

Each of the nine may be a slip of the telegraph, a typing slip in the Indian copy, or an error of the OCR text layer the transcription came from. Only the images can tell, and they have not been checked by a second reader.

05 The code and the checks

The telegrams are in the Chinese standard telegraph code, a public code book that gives one four-digit number to each character; nothing is enciphered. The table used is the one in the Unicode Unihan database, the Taiwan field first and the Mainland field where the first is empty.

The contributor wrote a protocol before the attack and recorded its SHA-256 hash (frozen/PREREG.md). The order of events rests on the contributor's record: the pull request is a single commit. The protocol fixed a list of expected Chinese words taken from the British abstract, and controls: 1,000 random orders of the groups and 1,000 random four-digit additives.

The control script in the pull request does not use the protocol's list. The crib was changed after decoding: the script has 棍堪布, a spelling of Kun Kanpu's name that could only be known once the telegrams were decoded, and 誠, and it drops five entries of the protocol (貢覺仲尼, 貢覺, 堪布, 誠意, 函). Both sets of figures, for crib words of two or more characters:

Cribp. 14p. 15Best of 1,000 random orders
The list fixed in the protocol (rerun at verification, 12 November 2026)1294 and 3
The list changed after decoding (the contributor's script)14104 and 3

With the protocol's own list the reading still stands well clear of the controls. The contributor's run of 1,000 random additives (with the changed crib) scored at most 2; with the protocol's crib, the best of all 9,999 additives scores 5 and 3 against 14 and 11 for the plain reading.

The pull request was verified independently for the project on 12 November 2026. A separate decoder, with a code table built from the Unihan file, gives the claimed character for all 221 codes read as written; the nine exceptions are the nine codes in the table above. Each of the six emendations is exactly one digit.

The English abstract of 29 September 1930 on p. 18 agrees with the Chinese on each of its points, as the contributor summarises it: the letter brought by Kun Kanpu, the Dalai Lama's support of the central government (擁護中央), China "not honest or considerate" (不以真誠相見), "complete unity" (完成統一), greetings from Ma Fuxiang. The abstract is a summary and not a translation. Who decoded the telegrams in 1930 is not stated in the pages described.

The last group of each telegram is a rhyme-code day: 元 is the 13th and 庚 is the 8th. The month comes only from the file.

06 Still open

  • The image check. Nothing here has been compared with the images by anyone but the contributor. The archive shows a human check to scripts, and no way round it was tried. The nine odd codes, and the groups 6615 and 7308, should be read again on pp. 14–15 by someone logged in. If the image shows the emended digits, those codes count as read.
  • Three unresolved codes: 4156, 5492 and 5129. A difference between the 1930 code book and the Unihan table is not excluded.
  • The order of the two telegrams. Ma Fuxiang's, dated the 8th, says 詳情已由主席電覆, "the details have been answered by the Chairman by telegram", but Chiang's is dated the 13th.
  • Prior art in Chinese. Web searches on distinctive phrases found no printed text. The Chinese printed document collections on Tibet and the central government were not searched, and a telegram of Chiang Kai-shek to the Dalai Lama may well be printed there.
  • The Dalai Lama's code-word telegrams (pp. 9, 11, 12, 22–23) are a different system and are not part of this reading. Each ten-letter word is two five-letter code words, in the patterns consonant-vowel-consonant-vowel-consonant and vowel-consonant-vowel-consonant-vowel in turn. The telegrams of pp. 11 and 12 share their opening and closing words. No code book was found and there is no crib, since the British abstract covers the Nanking telegrams only. Two tries failed: a table of syllables standing for digit pairs was inconsistent, and the alphabetical rank of the words gives nothing. The contributor's transcriptions of those pages are in the notes and were not tested at verification. The contributor's transcriptions of pp. 9, 11 and 12 are in the notes; pp. 22–23 are not transcribed.

# nanking1930: English translation

## p. 14: Chiang Kai-shek to the Dalai Lama

Kun Kanpu has come and handed me in person your letter and your replies on the eight points; all noted. I am also obliged for
the photograph and the precious gifts you sent, and for the kind words he conveyed; my joy and gratitude are beyond words.
China and Tibet have always been very closely bound. Formerly, because those who ruled the country did not deal with you in
sincerity, the hearts of the State Preceptor (國師, the Dalai Lama) and of the people of Ü-Tsang were lost; estrangement
remained and correspondence became rare. Now, happily, the State Preceptor, wise [?] and clear-minded, understands the times and
sincerely supports our central government. All that Kun Kanpu has reported to me in person shows still more the patriotic
sincerity that overflows your words. From now on China and Tibet are one family and unification is completed, to my great
satisfaction. Besides earnestly instructing Ma Fuxiang, Chairman of the Mongolian and Tibetan Affairs Commission, to handle the
matter with care, I first reply with my thanks and wish you good health. [?] Chiang Chung-cheng (Chiang Kai-shek). [13th.]

## p. 15: Ma Fuxiang to the Dalai Lama

We have been apart for over twenty years and I have often thought of you. I have just met Kun Kanpu and learned that Your
Holiness is in good health, to my deep satisfaction. The State Preceptor's devoted support of the central government has
earned Chairman Chiang's high praise; the details have been answered by the Chairman by telegram. All your affairs I, Fuxiang,
will handle with full devotion, so that public and private interests are both [served]. Please set your mind at rest. With
respectful good wishes. Ma Fuxiang. [8th.]

Note: 棍堪布 is Kun Kanpu (Könchok Jungné, the Dalai Lama's representative, also written 貢覺仲尼); 國師 "State Preceptor" is
the Dalai Lama.

Notes added at integration (12 Nov 2026)
- "wise [?]": the code as transcribed (4156) gives no sense; 4158 目 "eye", one digit away, is a candidate (慧目明心).
- "both [served]": the code as transcribed (5129) gives no sense; the set phrase 公私兩便 would need 0189, two digits away.
- Six words rest on codes emended by one digit: "all noted" (均), "correspondence" (音), "earnestly" (切), "Chiang" in "Chairman
  Chiang" (蔣), "praise" (許) and the second "devotion" (竭). See decryption.txt.
- Order of the two telegrams: Ma Fuxiang's, dated the 8th, says the Chairman has already answered by telegram; Chiang's is
  dated the 13th. Open.
- The identification of 棍堪布 (Kun Kanpu) with Könchok Jungné (貢覺仲尼) is the contributor's and was not checked here.
