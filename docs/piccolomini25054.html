Piccolomini letter 25054

Go to the full text ↓

The letter mixes Italian prose with numerical cipher, including a passage about Piccolomini’s return.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 26 November 2026

Method: key from adjacent plaintext · Extent: partial (78% of cipher words sure, not the sign count)

Contributed by Anna Beck (solution) and Larry Beck (solution)

01 What the text says

The letter is in Italian. Most of it is plain; some words are written in two-digit numbers. The three passages that carry cipher say the following (square brackets mark what is uncertain).

[Someone] had a conversation with me and reassured me by saying that he [two cipher words unresolved] certain [of] Your Excellency’s return.

… it seems to me that he thinks little of us. And [S.A., a title not yet expanded] replied to me: “It is true: of our generals, the enemy values only Piccolomini.”

… if Your Excellency is to be assigned to return there, you would do me a great favour by letting me know.

So the writer, who seems to be an officer, reports a talk about the addressee’s return and the remark that the enemy values only Piccolomini among “our generals”, and asks to be told of the assignment. The plain lines around the cipher, as the contributors transcribed them (several words bracketed as uncertain), mention an enemy who may attack again, ask that the letter be shown only to the secretary who will decipher it, and promise to add any news below. The translation does not say who the enemy is, where the return is to, or who the addressee is.

The sources give no date for the letter. The notes record no basis for the year the site’s listing gives (1646), and that year was not checked here. The title that looks like S.A. recurs on lines 4, 6, 8, 11, 15 and 16, and its expansion is open. Full Italian and English texts are under Full texts below.

02 The cipher and key

The cipher is a simple substitution: each plain letter is written as a two-digit number, and the same number always stands for the same letter. The key has 17 values, one letter per number, with no second number for any letter (no homophones) and no code words for names or phrases (no nomenclator). The letter did not come with its key; the key was recovered from the letter itself.

Cipher number1213141521222425313233344144515254
Plain letteralohcdusepmtgibnr

All 157 signs of the letter are in this table. Number 51 (b) does not occur in the letter, so the letter uses 16 of the 17 values. The key has no value for 11, 23 or 35, and none of those numbers appears in the transcription.

Worked example, the first line of the cipher: 15 12 is h, a, ha (“has”); 34 31 52 24 34 14 is t, e, n, u, t, o, tenuto (“held”); 33 31 21 14 is m, e, c, o, meco (“with me”). Together: ha tenuto meco un discorso, “had a conversation with me”.

The literal output keeps the transcription’s strings exactly as they stand, and the reading repairs them. In the transcription a slash is a visible separator that sometimes splits a word, so the literal output has hatenut o and the reading joins it as ha tenuto. Some strings have the right letters with the wrong word division (deino strigenerali for de i nostri generali, stimac he for stima che). Others are damaged: cistmi for ci stimi (one sign missing) and ritornatci for ritornarci (the eighth sign of the run is provisionally 34, t, with 54, r, as the alternative). The strings poiea and eder are kept as they stand, not replaced by fluent Italian. The figure above plays the literal output with its words spaced as the reading divides them.

Who did it and how it was checked. Anna Beck and Larry Beck, working with ChatGPT, recovered the 17 values from the small period glosses (plain letters written above the numbers) listed for frame 453, together with the Italian context; the notes count eight of the 25 sure words as covered by a period gloss. Václav Vlnas’s thesis supplied the photographs. The contributors compare the key with a deciphered sibling letter, inv. 25040, including the title and name Baron Engfurt. That comparison, the name, and the sibling’s “same values” are the contributors’ statements and are not checked here. The notes say the table printed in Vlnas’s thesis differs from this one; they do not say in which values. Every observation about the images (the raised correction on the fifth sign of line 1, the glosses, the crowded final strokes on line 2) rests on the contributors’ descriptions and an earlier review, and this project has not recompared the transcription with the images. What was checked here is that every one of the 157 signs in the transcription has a value in the key and that the literal output follows from those values.

03 What is deciphered and what remains open

Two measures are kept apart. Of the 157 signs, all 157 have a value in the key. Of the 32 cipher words, 25 are sure (78%), below the 95% the project requires, and seven are doubtful, so the entry stays partial on the word measure. The notes give the blocker as illegibility in the photographs and do not itemise the seven words. The passages they flag are:

  • tenuto on line 1, whose fifth sign uses a raised correction (52, n);
  • poiea on line 2 and eder on line 3, kept as written and unresolved;
  • ecclentia on line 4, nine letters, probably “Excellency”, with missing letters that are not silently inserted;
  • stimi on line 7, where the transcription has stmi and the i is supplied;
  • ritornarci on line 14, with 34 (t) provisional and 54 (r) the alternative;
  • the clear title that looks like S.A., whose expansion is open.

That is six cipher words (the title is clear text), and the notes do not say which seventh word is doubtful, so none is guessed. The sibling comparison, the name Baron Engfurt, the difference from Vlnas’s printed table and the date are marked not checked above.

Letter with cipher on frame 453 read in part

An officer writes in Italian, with numbers for some words, about a conversation in which he was reassured of the addressee's return. He quotes the reply that the enemy values only Piccolomini among their generals, and asks to be told if the addressee is assigned to return there.

Transcription

physical-lines.md

Inv. 25054, frame 453, film 31/2. The photograph has 20 lines of base text. These are the 11 lines containing cipher, numbered both by position on the leaf and by the labels in our first transcription. Lines 5, 6, 11, 12 and 16 through 20 contain only Italian prose. Small plaintext letters above the numbers are recorded separately in the [gloss inventory](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/issues/790#issuecomment-7034991952).

Angle brackets ⟨ ⟩ enclose each run of numbers. Each pair corresponds to one cipher sign. A slash denotes a visible separator even when it splits a word. The fifth sign on A1 uses the raised correction, 52. Word spacing, punctuation and points in the apparent title S.A. are editorial where indistinct. A question mark in brackets flags a doubtful reading of the handwriting. A terminal hyphen records continuation on the next physical line.

On line 2 the earlier submission read col dire. The word may be dirmi; the final strokes are crowded. On line 7 si completes rispo- in the preceding line. On lines 10-11, argo- continues as mentare i sensi (argomentare). On line 13 ticolare completes par- on the preceding line. The same contracted form resembling S.A. recurs on lines 4, 6, 8, 11, 15 and 16. Its expansion and the person's identity remain open. On line 14 the eighth sign of the second run is still provisionally 34 (alternative 54). The 11 rows retain 15 runs and 157 signs; the additional slashes written at the transition into prose do not add any signs. Reading

reading.md

A: ha tenuto meco un discorso et consolato mi [clear: col [dirmi?], ch'egli] [poiea] [eder] certo il ritorno di uostra ecclentia.

B: [clear: mi pare ch'] ci st[i]mi poco [clear: e [S.A.?] mi rispose è vero l'inimico] de i nostri generali [clear: non] stima che il Picolomini.

C: [clear: se V.E.] sara destinato [clear: a] ritorna{t→r}ci [clear: mi farà somma gratia] di auisarmelo.

The broken strings poiea eder are preserved, not replaced by fluent Italian. In B, the inserted i is explicitly bracketed. In C, the proposed t-to-r repair is explicit. Ecclentia is the literal nine-letter cipher result; its likely intended sense is “Excellency,” but the missing letters are not silently inserted. Period u/v spelling is retained in the literal data.
Translation

translation.md

These are the three cipher-bearing passages, with enough clear Italian to connect them. Square brackets identify editorial qualifications. This is not a translation of every clear word on the page.

> [Someone] had a conversation with me and reassured me by saying that he [two cipher words unresolved] certain [of] Your Excellency's return.
>
> ... it seems to me that he thinks little of us [“stimi” supplied from defective “stmi”]. And [S.A., title uncertain] replied to me: “It is true: of our generals, the enemy values only Piccolomini.”
>
> ... if Your Excellency is to be assigned to return there [one group uncertain in “ritornarci”], you would do me a great favour by letting me know.

“Values only Piccolomini” renders non stima che il Picolomini. It does not identify who the enemy is or where Piccolomini is to return. The historical context does not supply missing cipher letters.
