A Gonzaga agent in Rome to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, 9 August 1428

A letter wholly in signs, about a marriage for the Gonzaga, deciphered with a key recovered from the ciphertext alone.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 22 September 2026 · updated 23 October 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: complete

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · complete

The letter
An unnamed Gonzaga agent, signing with a lozenge mark, to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, lord of Mantua, Rome, 9 August 1428. All 37 lines are in cipher.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Mantova, Archivio Gonzaga. DECODE R1854, image 2: the record has five images, catalogued as one letter of unknown sender and recipient.
The cipher
A homophonic substitution in graphic signs and digits: about forty letter signs, a dozen nulls, signs for con, et and -rum, and two word codes.
How it was deciphered
A solver (simulated annealing on a Latin language model) recovered the key from the ciphertext alone; every sign was then checked on the image.
What it says
A countess agrees to her daughter’s match with a Gonzaga son, but her subjects object that Mantua is too far and “Carolus est nimis iuvenis ad talia”.
Still open
One word code used once, and about 45 signs in doubtful spans.

DECODE R1854 holds four letters to Gianfrancesco Gonzaga, each in a different cipher, and each has its own page: Pandolfo Malatesta, 22 April 1428 (not solved), an informant at Forlì, 6 November 1428 and Francesco de’ Cattanei, February 1430. They were written up together until 14 October 2026.

01 The record and the register

DECODE R1854 “Mantova enciphered letters” has five images (I8411–I8415), catalogued as “unknown” with graphic signs. Each sheet has an archivist’s note at the top: “1428 · 22 ap(ri)le Roma” (stamped 23), “1428 · 9 Ag.to Roma” (24), “1428 · 6 novembre Forlì” (26), and so on. This page is image 2. The images are not in the public domain, so none is reproduced here.

The companion record R1853 “Mantova keys” is the Gonzaga chancery register of ciphers, begun about 1401. Its first folio lists “Zifra cum Malatesta d(omi)ni Pandulfi”, “Zifra nova cum fratribus de Malatestis” and two keys “cum Karolo de Malatestis”, and later folios have keys dated 1406–1419. All are simple substitutions in letters and digits, and none gives sense on this letter. The other Mantua records on DECODE (R7858–R7888, Archivio Gonzaga E.I.2 busta 423) are sixteenth- and seventeenth-century keys.

02 Rome, 9 August 1428: a marriage for the Gonzaga

The whole letter is in cipher: 37 lines of graphic signs and digits, dated in clear “Rome die viiii augusti”. It uses about forty letter signs, a dozen nulls, syllable signs for con, et and -rum, and two word codes, one of them twice in “in domo [·] Senensis” and “cum prefato [·]”, that is, the cardinal of Siena. The opening, as deciphered:

Heri Iohannes de Orlandis, quam primum me vidit, dixit michi quod ser Victorius eomet die venit ad ipsum dicens dominam comitissam velle facere id totum quod placet et Iohanni predicto. Verum quia ipse incipiebat tunc prandere, dixit se ad eum reversurum et dicturum latius de ista materia. Postea cum heri sero plures essemus congregati in domo [cardinalis] Senensis pro quodam actu scolastico et cenaturi cum prefato [cardinali] …

Ser Vittorio goes on to report the countess’s answer: as far as she is concerned she agrees to the match of her daughter, but her subjects are not content, “quia dominatio vestra nimium distat, si ibi contingeret aliquod sinistrum; et quod Carolus est nimis iuvenis ad talia”. The bridegroom under discussion is Gianfrancesco’s son Carlo, then eleven. The cousins of domina Palma and Antonio Colonna, the pope’s nephew, appear as parties to the negotiation. Line 28, read on 5 October 2026 from the re-fetched image: persuaserat matri et filie ut vellent vendere terras ipsas. The full text, with the doubtful spans marked, is in reading_p2.md.

03 Method

The transcription was made from the DECODE image, in crops at 4–13×. The triangle sign is followed by sign 17 in 21 of its 29 occurrences, which fixed q and u. A homophonic annealer on the project’s shared Latin model then produced Latin words, and per-sign and per-occurrence rescoring separated nulls and look-alike glyphs, confirmed on the image: a bold ∇ is s while a thin dotted ∇ is a null, and a tailed circle is p while a plain circle is h. The key was tested against chance and overfitting with a letter model (docs/_validate.py, re-run on this letter alone on 14 October 2026): it passes.

04 What remains uncertain

  • 1,757 of 1,802 signs (97.5%) read as sense, re-counted 5 October 2026.
  • The word code in “in filium [·]” (once), and about 45 signs in doubtful spans (lines 15, 27, 31–32), for example “habet [in] num[ero] de ve[..]”; grades M–I.
  • The writer is not named; he signs with a lozenge mark.

## English (summary translation)

Yesterday Giovanni de Orlandis told me, as soon as he saw me, that ser Vittorio came to him that same day saying the
countess would do everything that pleased (you) and the said Giovanni; but as he was then starting his dinner, he
said he would come back and tell him more. Later, yesterday evening, when many of us were gathered in the house of the
cardinal of Siena for an academic act and to dine with the cardinal, ser Vittorio — who is often there because his son
lives there — came up to me in the hall before dinner and, after long talk, said in conclusion that last Thursday,
coming from Calabria, he passed through Rome with a cousin of lady Palma who was escorting his own sister, married
beyond Viterbo, and returned the day before; that on behalf of the three other cousins of lady Palma, Antonio Colonna
has always excused himself, saying they are not content …; that the countess has sent to say, as before through him,
that as far as she is concerned she agrees to the match, but her subjects did not seem content, because your
lordship is too far away if anything untoward happened there, and because Carlo is too young for such things; and the
girl was not content either … The mother, doubting …, (will) do what (you) order; her wish seems to be that it be done.
