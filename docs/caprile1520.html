Giuliano Caprile’s sign cipher, 1520–1521

The letters of Cardinal Ippolito d’Este’s secretary in Hungary in his 1520–21 cipher: the key, rebuilt from a 2025 decipherment, deciphers one letter almost in full (97%); two others decode only into phrases (decoded, not read).

At a glanceKey from external plaintext · partial (12% of the cipher)

The letters
Giuliano Caprile, canon of Eger and secretary of Cardinal Ippolito d’Este, to the ducal secretary Bonaventura Pistofilo and to Duke Alfonso I, March 1520 to April 1521: five texts in one cipher.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Modena, Ambasciatori, Ungheria b. 4; DECODE R1136, R1138, R1139 (catalogue item 157), and the Hungarian database Vestigia, where the letters of 16 and 25 February 1521 (Vestigia 1920, 1921) are not on DECODE.
The cipher
A homophonic cipher of invented signs with no word division, crossed ligatures for s and q.
How it was deciphered
One postscript of 16 February 1521, in both its versions, was deciphered by Judit W. Somogyi in 2025. Her two printed texts, aligned against a transcription of the signs, gave the key. On 2 October 2026 the letter of 25 February 1521 added five values, and second transcriptions of R1139 and R1136 at full resolution were deciphered with them.
What it says
Ippolito’s estate at Eger after he left Hungary: the custode, the governor, the see, money and debts. On 25 February 1521 Caprile reports that il custode è stato quel ch’à persuaso others to say Eger had been given away before the new bishop came, and advises the Duke to reclaim what is due sopra soi beni.
Measured
V1921: 161 of 166 cipher signs give sense (97.0%), 147 at grade C. R1139: 453 of 505 decoded letters inside deciphered phrases (89.7%, 3 October 2026). R1136: 378 of 565 (66.9%). These are shares of decoded letters inside phrases: R1139 and R1136 still have holes in most lines, so they are decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026).
Still open
Stretches of R1139 and a third of R1136, limited by doubtful sign separations.
Other ciphers
Caprile’s 1519 letters use a different two-tier cipher, with its own page; Alfonso Cistarelli’s numerical cipher of July 1520, in the same box, also has its own page.

02 The key

The cipher is a run of invented signs with no word division: φ, ψ, λ, ÷, ss, ff, a crossed b, m and n with a cross, an inverted T, and some forty more. There are 49 labels in all, over 2,278 signs in five texts, transcribed from the DECODE and Vestigia images. Somogyi calls it homophonic, and it is. The key was built in two steps:

  1. A ciphertext-only solver run (simulated annealing) over all five texts, scored with a sixteenth-century Italian n-gram model. It gave a rough key in which scritti, regno, signore and Ungaria could already be recognised.
  2. That key seeded a monotone alignment (Viterbi with gaps, re-estimated until stable) of the transcription against Somogyi’s two texts. 714 letters align, and 90% of alignments agree with the majority value of their sign.

The values:

a = q, T, ⊥ · c = φ, θ · d = ψ, r · e = y, ss · g = w, u · i = ƀ, ɔ, L · l = ff, n · m = z, Ħ · n = ÷, p · o = ɛ, the stacked q/b, oσo · p = & and its looped variant · r = *, x · s = m+, n+ · t = h, f · u = λ, a

The letter of 25 February 1521 corrected and extended these values (2 October 2026). The lone o, taken at first for a null, is a homophone for h and u (“ha”, “ch’à”, “qui”, “suo”), as the alignment counts had already shown; two lone o together are b (“beni”, and R1139’s “debito”); z and x with a cross, and x with the looped p, are q (“qui”, “quel”, “quantunque”); the looped p doubles for f (“oficio”); and K is et once. The second transcriptions of R1139 and R1136 added o with a cross = s, the crossed 3 and 3 with the looped p = q, the triangle = b or x, plain c = o, and o with a lead-in = h. The figure 8 is a null in one place.

A statistical check on the letter of 25 February (161 tokens, 42 sign types) scores the reading well above random keys (z 6.6), but a key annealed onto the shuffled ciphertext reaches the same score, so the test alone cannot confirm the key on so short a text: verdict underdetermined. The key’s support is the alignment with Somogyi’s two texts and the sense of the three letters it deciphers.

03 The letter of 25 February 1521 (Vestigia 1921)

Caprile to Duke Alfonso I from Esztergom, 25 February 1521 (Caprili 1520–21 no. 12). It is not on DECODE; Vestigia has it as item 1921, flagged as containing cipher. The letter is in clear until its last third, where six cipher runs are set inside clear sentences. They were transcribed sign by sign on the Vestigia image, with four corrections to the first transcription, and deciphered with the key on 2 October 2026.

161 of the 166 cipher signs give sense (97.0%); 147 of them (88.6%) at grade C, with every sign at its key value. On a sixteenth-century Italian model the reading scores −1.79 per character, against −4.12 for the same letters shuffled. Clear text in roman, the deciphered runs in bold:

… il tutto per adviso e scarico di chi ne ha colpa. E me dicixe [= disse?] ha un omo qui qual oltra che fa mille pacie & da poca riputation ale cose del patron, dice ogni cosa e revelta [?], e ha ditto haver scritto al suo mazo che il custode è stato quel ch'à persuaso [..] a dire che [.] Agria era data d'anti il suo advento, e[t] che lo toglia in oficio, e molto sturbato le sue cose, e chi l'ha messo in gran disgratia di suo signore; il che quando cossi sia, como mi acerta alcuno ch'à veduto le lettere, a V. Ex.a sera poca fatica i cominciar a repeter il suo sopra soi beni, quantunque siano in reg[no?]. E S. V. Ex.a a questo modo …

“And he told me there is a man here who, besides committing a thousand follies and bringing little credit to the master’s affairs, tells everything and [gives it away]; and he has said he wrote to his [messenger] that it was the custode who persuaded [them] to say that Eger had been given away before his arrival, and that he should take him into office … and put him in great disgrace with his lord. If that is so, as someone who has seen the letters assures me, it will be little trouble for Your Excellency to begin reclaiming what is his from his goods, even though they are in the kingdom.” The patron and the claim are Ippolito d’Este’s Eger bishopric, the subject of R1136, R1138 and R1139. Dicixe (for disse?) and revelta are grade M. Open: two minim strokes after persuaso with no key value, one sign before Agria (probably a slip), and two signs at the torn edge after in reg.

04 R1139, 6 April 1521 (decoded, not read)

A slip pasted into the letter of 6 April. Nine lines of cipher, then the clear text “Expettamo sino a S. Georgio…”. It is the estate business of Somogyi’s article: the keeper (custode) and the governor of Eger, the money of the late bishop’s debt, and someone who says he wants to go to Italy.

Six passes since September 2026 have worked on it: the signs re-checked on the image wherever the decrypt did not read (three transcription errors found, among them the tall-stem r of custode), letters proposed from the key’s sign counts with an Italian model, a second transcription from the DECODE image at full resolution, the values from the letter of 25 February, a table of look-alike sign pairs over all five texts, and a study of pen shapes. After them 453 of the 505 decoded letters fell inside phrases (89.7%, 3 October 2026). The reading audit of 22 October 2026 asked whether the slip builds sentences: it does not, and it is decoded, not read. On 22–23 October the cipher was re-read on strips of the native image at 2.2 times, five sign labels were corrected, and every token was decoded again with the key; no new value holds across every occurrence (4 = e, 1 = v, 1 7 = ve were tested and rejected). The partial reading, aligned word by word with the signs (R1139_reading.md; two of its eleven sentences are clean):

Post. Il custode, quando a Buda, com[?e] i' scrisi, et è tornato gubernator de [4817]. […] decime, et fa ogni cosa per […] io [17] […]; a si tenuto Agria in oficio. [?E] credo dia ogni [?ato] e qualche cosa a le ma […] custode; per anchora si n'è intrato [?ci] in castelo. Et cosi la venuta sua in Italia, [?a] de che vi scrivi, qua[…] li teneva per ferma, è andata in [?vero]. Il che vedendo io, bel belo li go fato moto deli danari del suo debito, come mi dise meser [?a] Alfonso. Lui m'ha resposto, dice [?te] voler andar in Italia quando al dare te […]. […] Milan […] andiate. Parlaremo a longo sopra ciò, et se i bisionran danari in [?presto], per andar ve ne darò. Vedrò quel mi darà, [?e] il tut[o] reportarò a V. Ex.

“Postscript. The custode, when [he was] at Buda, as I wrote, has also come back as governor of [4817]. […] tithes, and he does everything for […]; [he] has thus held Eger in office. [?And] I believe he gives every [?act] and something into the hands […] custode; as yet he has not entered [?there] into the castle. … Seeing this, I gently raised with him the money of his debt, as messer [?a] Alfonso told me. He answered me, saying he wants to go to Italy … We shall talk at length about it, and if they need money on [?loan], I will give you some for the journey. I shall see what he gives me, [?and] I shall report the whole to Your Excellency.” Go is the Venetian-Ferrarese gh’ò (I have); fato moto is fatto motto (mentioned). The groups 4817 and 17 look like figures or code signs and have no key value.

05 R1136, 8 March 1520 (decoded, not read)

Twelve lines after a clear opening (“e la susseguente matina”), written the day after Ippolito crossed out of Hungary for the last time (7 March 1520). A second transcription at full resolution (2 October 2026) put 378 of the 565 decoded letters inside phrases (66.9%), but the lines do not build sentences. The 22–23 October re-read confirmed the sign labels at twice the size on contrast-stretched strips; the breaks turn on signs the key cannot separate or value, and a stain covers about six signs at the left of lines 1–3. The partial reading (R1136_reading.md; none of its nine sentences is clean):

[?Asi] transferse a Buda il custode […]. […] [?altro] li a in oficio questo episcopato, et a epso custode […]. […] gu[…]ernator […] Lactantio, ho inteso, qual […] a in gran presia […]amato a Buda, et dicono eser conclusi […] congregati […] Bachiensis, che ogni episcopa[…] […] il man[…] […] cosi [17] ne tenir […]. […] et l'altro in titulo, [?ma] questo modo excluderà […]. […] li daran poco […] vo[…]stra Excelentia, quel si […] potrà far al custode par questa via de Ungaria sopra il su[…] […]. […] secondo patrone, […] in questo loco niente […]. […] tornato el sia […] canto, e vedrò intendere il […] pensier suo […] sopra tal debito.

The custode moves to Buda; the see of Eger, its custode and governor, Lactantio, the prelates gathered (Bachiensis is the archbishop of Kalocsa-Bács), and the advice that the Duke “potrà far al custode par questa via de Ungaria” on his goods, the claim Caprile repeats a year later on 25 February 1521.

06 What stays open

  • R1139 and R1136, decoded, not read. After second transcriptions at full resolution, two more passes on 3 October 2026 and the re-read of 22–23 October, 10% of R1139’s decoded letters and 33% of R1136’s fall outside phrases, and neither builds sentences. In R1139 these are the figure-like groups 4 8 1 7 and 1 7 and short stretches of lines 2, 4–5 and 8; in R1136, a stain over about six signs at lines 1–2 and the stretches limited by doubtful sign separations. A table of the look-alike sign pairs over all five texts (pass 5) settled only two of them (CE = o, crossed xp = q), and added mi per Milan in R1139 line 8; a study of the pen shapes (pass 6) found no shape class with a consistent value. 4 8 1 7 and 1 7 fit neither as letters nor as numerals, and are probably nomenclator codes with no list.
  • V1921. Five of 166 signs stay open (no key value, a slip, the torn edge); dicixe and revelta are grade M.
  • What would finish it. Hand-placed crops of every occurrence of the doubtful sign pairs, or more text in the same key. No further letter in this cipher exists in the file.

# Caprile to Alfonso I d'Este, 16 February 1521: the ciphered postscript (R1138 / Vestigia 1919)

## Reading

Se v. ex.a fa conto repeter al regno li soi quaranta milia et li otto milia a re et li altri crediti de che si hanno e
scritti, bisogna che si facia eodem die testamenti con diti un codicillo nel qual si contenga chel signore lega a re,
per lobservantia vers lui et consuetudine del regno, acio il testamento suo sia valido, un mille o duo milia; perché
questoro dicono che eo quod non legasset, che omnia essent devoluta a re. Ma quando li dissi quanto scrivo a V. Ex.a, li
detti d'una mano sul volto. Li scritti ho io portaro o mandaro per via secura. Per laltra cavalcata mandai questa
zifra.

# Caprile to Alfonso I d'Este, 16 February 1521: the ciphered postscript (R1138 / Vestigia 1919), English translation

If Your Excellency means to reclaim from the kingdom his forty thousand, and the eight thousand from the King, and the
other credits for which there are writings, a codicil must be made the same day as the testament, with the said
[testament], in which it is stated that the lord [the late bishop] leaves to the King, out of observance towards him
and by the custom of the kingdom, so that his testament may be valid, one or two thousand; because these people say
that, since he did not leave him a legacy, everything has devolved to the King. But when I told him what I write to
Your Excellency, I gave him a slap in the face [I silenced him]. The writings I shall bring or send by a safe way. By
the other mounted courier I sent this cipher.

Vestigia 1921, Esztergom 25 Feb 1521

Six cipher runs inside clear sentences: someone at Esztergom blames the custode of Eger for the claim that the see had been given away before the successor arrived, and Caprile advises the Duke to reclaim Ippolito's due from that man's goods. 97.0 % deciphered as sense.

Translation (gist): "And he told me there is a man here who, besides committing a thousand follies and bringing
little credit to the master's affairs, tells everything and [gives it away]; and he has said he wrote to his
[messenger] that it was the custode [of Eger] who persuaded [him/them] to say that Eger had been given away before his
[the new bishop's] arrival, and that he should take him into office — and has much disturbed his affairs, and put him
in great disgrace with his lord. If that is so, as someone who has seen the letters assures me, it will be little
trouble for Your Excellency to begin reclaiming what is his from his goods, even though they are in the kingdom."
The "patron" and the estate claim are Ippolito d'Este's Eger bishopric, the subject of R1136, R1138 and R1139.

## Key findings (values fixed here)

- **Lone o is not a null.** It is a homophone for h (L01 "ha", L02 "ch'à", L04 "che") and for u (L01 "qui", L03 "suo",
  L06 "quantunque"). The EM counts already said so (o: o 28, h 13, u 10); V1920 confirms ("PHI o y" = che).
- **oo (two lone o together) = b**: "beni" (L06); also R1139 L06 "de[b]ito" (r y o o L h).
- **z+ = q** (z with a cross, not z + t): "qui" (L01), "quantunque" (L06); V1920 "quaranta" has the same z+ LAM.
- **x+ = q**: "quel" (L03). **x followed by the looped p is also q** (xp ligature): "quantunque" (L06), and
  R1136 L10 "x ρ a ss m+ f o/o" = questo. Plain x stays r (21×); its 7 "q" counts in the EM are probably x+ written as x t.
- **EL = f** in "oficio" (L04), as AMP = f in R1139's "Alfonso": the p-signs double for f.
- **K = et** (M) in "e[t] che" (L04); K is t 4× in the counts.
- The m+ ligature can be written with the cross as a looped descender (L02 "m p" = MT, "è sta|to").
- Z = x in "dicixe" (L01), as in V1920's "8 Z q" = Ex.a.

## Remaining gaps (V1921)

- L03 "1 1" after "persuaso", before the clear "a dire": two minim strokes; neither is in the key (no-key-material).
  Perhaps "li" (persuaded them), not claimed.
- L03 QB before "Agria": one sign unexplained (QB is o/t), probably a slip or null (left open).
- L06 last two signs "K PHI" at the torn page edge, after "in reg": uncertain shapes, "regno" expected but not
  enciphered so (illegible).
- Grade-M words: "dicixe" (= disse? a Venetian "dixe" with an extra syllable) and "revelta" (revela?).
