Rome → Florence · homophonic letter signs, words kept whole · January 1478 (st. fior.; 1479 modern) · key from a sibling’s decipherment

Giovanni Mazzinghi to the Medici, January 1479

Go to the full text ↓

A Medici agent in Rome warns of a bull annulling the Church’s privileges and of a plot against them, in a cipher whose key comes from a sibling letter’s decipherment.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 6 November 2026

At a glanceKey from external plaintext · partial (76% of the cipher)

The letters
Two bare cipher letters: one signed by Giovanni Mazzinghi, 16 January 1478 (Florentine style, so 1479), and an unsigned one of 13 January in the same hand and signs. No place is written; the content puts both in Rome. Both are addressed to the Medici.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Firenze, Mediceo avanti il Principato, filza LXXXIX 142 and CXXXVII 252. The key source is the glossed sibling LXXII 170. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A homophonic substitution of letters in letter-like signs (a is +, ^ or d; e is x, y or c; i is s, 5, 1 or 3; o is r …), words kept whole, numerals for persons, and clear words between the cipher words.
How it was deciphered
The key was fitted on 69 cipher-word/plain-word pairs written over the sibling letter LXXII 170 at the time, then completed by context and a five-gram model. A second session broke 2307 from the ciphertext alone, and the two keys agree on the main values. The validation verdict is weak.
What it says
A bull under lead has been published in the Chancery annulling the privileges the Church had granted; a very cruel process is being made against “voi”; two Pistoiese, Iacopo de’ Rossi and a Cola, are involved in a plot (“ordinò la morte” of a person coded 30); “aiutatevi l’occhio”.
Still open
92 cipher words (394 signs) that do not decode, six code numerals, and no code list for this cipher.
Credit
Key and reading by Daniel Bourdeau with Claude agents; the independent ciphertext-only break by a second session (PR #301). The decipherment on the sibling letter is the clerk’s of January 1479.

01 The letters

All three letters say “gennaio 1478”. A Florentine writer counted the year from 25 March, so this is very probably January 1479 in modern style: nine months after the Pazzi conspiracy of 26 April 1478, during the Pazzi War, with Florence under the interdict of Sixtus IV. 2307, signed on the verso “da G. Mazinghi / Cifra”, names no place; the Pope, the Chancery and, in the sibling, Castel Sant’Angelo put the writers in Rome. The first 2307 line reads “In nome di Dio a dì xvj di gennaio 1478”.

It is not the cipher of Siena no. 11, Acciaiuoli’s letter from Rome of June 1478, which is deciphered with Lasry’s key.

02 The key and its tests

MAP LXXII 170, of January 1478, carries a decipherment written over the cipher words in a second hand. From 69 aligned pairs a key was built (held-out letters 57–74%), refined by context and a five-gram model of 16th-century Italian, and applied to the finer transcriptions of the two bare letters after a sign-by-sign recheck of doubtful words at five to six times magnification.

The project’s validation of the refined key is weak. The claimed score of −2.27 per letter beats 200 random keys (z = 4.4) and six keys refitted to shuffled signs (best −2.74), but with only six refits the p-value cannot fall below 0.14, and the annealer started from random keys reaches −2.5 on the real text. A second session therefore worked on 2307 without the sibling’s key, with its own coarser transcription (29 sign classes) and a homophonic annealer on the ciphertext alone, which returned sense at once. Its control, an Italian passage of 537 signs in a random 29-sign key, came back 655 of 667 letters right. The two keys, reached separately, agree on the main values (a, e, i, o, n, t, r, d, u, s, m, g, b, p).

03 The reading

Counted by script, 1,253 of the 1,647 cipher signs of the two letters sit in words that read as sense: 63.9% of the unsigned letter (371 of 581) and 82.7% of Mazzinghi’s (882 of 1,066). The ciphertext-only break, on its own transcription, reads 111 of 189 cipher words (58.7%), with 30 read with doubt.

The unsigned letter of 13 January says that the Pope has sent a brief, that a letter was presented the day before to a person coded 10, and that the king is so enraged that the writer fears Antonio del Palagio, Pagolo Rucellai and Lionardo di Giunta will be seized and put in Castel Sant’Angelo; it was written in haste. Mazzinghi, three days later, writes “io, persona fidata” that this morning a bull under lead was published in the Chancery annulling all the privileges granted to them, that a very cruel process is made “contra di voi”, that “credo abbino el diavolo in corpo”, that Iacopo de’ Rossi of Pistoia has told him of “la fine vostra”, and that a person coded 30 was ordered killed and the same order seems to stand over them; he closes “aiutatevi l’occhio, ché ogni cosa fanno per la vostra fine”. The reading, with the code numerals marked, is under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • 92 cipher words (394 signs) do not decode to sense. The signs are 8–14 pixels high at the viewer’s maximum (1,272 px wide); they were rechecked at five to six times magnification and tried against look-alike variants, and no higher-resolution image is online.
  • The code numerals 10, 30, 40, 51, 231 and 10/3, and the recurring group OLDP, DLDP or EDP, are open. Only 12 = Zanobi is glossed in the sibling; its gloss over 10 reads “Duca” and an illegible word, so 10 is a duke, which one is open. None of the Medici key sheets of 1472–1494 is this cipher (MAP LXXII 178–189 bis, CXIX 115–127), and no code list for this cipher was found. If 30 is Lorenzo, the letter warns of a murder plot nine months after the conspiracy; that is not claimed.
  • The two independent drafts differ in places: “abbiatevi” against “aiutatevi”, “rine” against “fine vostra”, “accennando la…” against “dannando la croce”. They are to be settled against the image.
  • Lorenzo de’ Medici’s Lettere II and III do not cite either shelfmark; volume IV was searched for another letter only, so the check is to be repeated for these two. DECODE has no MAP records.
  • No human has checked the transcription against the images; the images are not reproduced.

# Reading of MAP CXXXVII 252 and LXXXIX 142 (January 1478 st. fior. = 1479)

Key rebuilt from the contemporary interlinear decipherment of the sibling MAP LXXII 170 (`build_key.py`, `key.tsv`),
completed by context and a 5-gram model (`refine.py`, `key_refined.tsv`), applied to the transcriptions `t252.tsv`
and `t142.tsv` after a sign-by-sign recheck of doubtful words at 5-6x (`recheck.tsv`). Raw decodes: `draft252.txt`,
`draft142.txt` (cipher words in capitals). Below, the cipher words are given in normal spelling, clear words in
*italics* where they matter, `[…]` = cipher word not read, `[10]` = code numeral (persons; 10 is glossed "Duca" plus an
illegible word in LXXII 170, 12 is glossed "Zanobi"; 30, 40, 51, 231 not identified), `?` = doubtful word.
Measured (`measure.py`): 252: 72 of 121 cipher words, 371 of 581 signs read (63.9%); 142: 166 of 209 cipher words,
882 of 1,066 signs (82.7%); together 1,253 of 1,647 signs (76.1%).

## Notes on the reading

- "Duca Filippo": the privileges are those granted "dal duca Filippo infino a questo dì"; the clause is read as
  decoded and not interpreted further here.
- Code 30: the letter warns the recipients ("la fine vostra", "sopra a voi"); 30 is most likely Lorenzo de' Medici
  or one of the household, but the code is not identified from any source.
- Code 10: the sibling's gloss over the same numeral reads "Duca" followed by an illegible word (re-read at 6x on
  6 Nov 2026: perhaps "nostro"; "di Calabria" is not supported). Which duke is left open; none of the Medici key
  sheets of the period is this cipher (keysheets.md).
- OLDP / DLDP / EDP (five times) behave like a code group for a person (perhaps "il papa" or a cardinal); not read.
