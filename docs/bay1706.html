231 Mosqua · 270 Svéd · 285 Vajda · 252 Porta

Jászvásár (Iaşi) → Ferenc Rákóczi II · Hungarian syllabic nomenclator · 8 March 1706

András Bay to Rákóczi, from the Moldavian court, 1706

Go to the full text ↓

MNL OL G15 Caps. D. Fasc. 81. 5 (DECODE R478), two pages, catalogued as a homophonic letter with no decryption and no key. Each of the 94 Rákóczi chancery keys on DECODE was tried on it; one, DECODE R609, fits and was applied unchanged. 97.6% of the cipher groups decipher as sense; measured on the words of the reading, the letter builds coherent sentences but reads at 94.2%, under the 95% bar. No printed decipherment was found.

The letter
András Bay to Ferenc Rákóczi II, Jászóvásár (Iași), 8 March 1706.
Where
Hungarian National Archives (MNL OL), G15 D 81/5; DECODE R478, listed as undecrypted with no key.
The cipher
A two-digit syllabary with a nomenclator of places and peoples and a row of nulls.
How it was read
Each of the 94 Rákóczi chancery keys on DECODE was tried and scored against Hungarian; R609 gave running Hungarian at once. DECODE’s transcription was corrected in seven places.
What it says
A report on the Muscovite and Polish envoys at the Moldavian court, the Tsar suing Charles XII for peace, and the Swedish army at Vilna.
Still open
Group 50, missing from the key, is taken as ba from context; about ten groups on page 2 decrypt letter by letter but not to clear sense.

01 The letter and the key

The letter is one sheet written on both sides. Clear Hungarian passages alternate with runs of comma-separated numbers from 1 to 300 and two signs. It is dated at the foot Jászvásár 8. Martii and signed Bay András. The key is DECODE R609, a Rákóczi chancery key of 1703–1711 in the same fonds. Its letters have homophones (A 7/70, E 8/80, I 9/90, O 6/60, U 5/59), and it has two-digit consonant–vowel syllables (30 ba to 148 vu), short words (27 és, 28 az, 13 hogy, 14 mért) and a nomenclator from 149 to 293 (231 Mosqua, 221 Lengyel, 270 Svéd, 285 Vajda, 252 Porta, 236 Nagyságod). The nulls are 15–19, 200, 300, Ψ and +. The letter uses the nulls 16, 18, 19, 200, 300 and Ψ. An earlier issue of the same table, R561, scored second.

02 The decipherment

Normalised Hungarian. Clear passages are in «»; square brackets mark words supplied or doubtful.

«Kegyelmes Uram! Valamint ez előtt negyed nappal küldött levelemben nyilván meg írtam,» Moszkva és lengyel követek jöttek ide; az lengyel vissza is ment, a moszkva még itt vagyon. Az mint expiscálhattam, olyat mondottak mind ketten: a moszkva békességre kéri a svédet, minden elvött várakat és ezen hadakozásban lött expensáit a svédnek megígérvén, «[ha oly] conditióval, hogy …» ellene a moszkva hadát declarálja-é a svéd «is» colligatióban «légyen». … Az moszkva követ «innét» Constantinápolyba «vagy» Erdélybe megyen, én nem tudhatni; mert csak maga beszéllett az vajdával, görögül. «…» Tegnapi napon az vajda tolmácsával conversatióban lévén, «… in discursu» Pekri uram általa olyat izent a vajdának, hogy csak Nagyságod a magyar nemzet dolgát complanálhassa a némettel, … minden szándéka Nagyságodnak a török ellen való hadakozás. «…» Az tolmácsot [… ?] tudván az barát az Portának szemének és fülének lenni, még nem mondotta; «nékem is» lelkemre kötötte, hogy ki ne adjam őtet. A svéd Vilnához szállott, mely Litvánia és Moszkva-Oroszország között vagyon. «… Jászvásár 8. Martii … Bay András.»

In English. As I wrote four days ago, Muscovite and Polish envoys have come here; the Pole has gone back, the Muscovite is still here. As far as I could fish it out, both said that the Tsar is asking the Swede for peace, promising the Swede all the fortresses he has taken and his expenses in this war, on condition that the Swede declare [war?] against… Whether the Muscovite envoy goes on to Constantinople or to Transylvania I cannot tell, because he talked to the voivode alone, in Greek. Yesterday I talked with the voivode’s interpreter. Pekri had sent the voivode word through him that if only Your Highness can settle the Hungarian nation’s affairs with the Germans [the Emperor] …, Your Highness’s whole intention is war against the Turk. Knowing the friar to be the Porte’s eyes and ears, the interpreter has not yet told him, and he made me swear on my soul not to give him away. The Swede has moved to Vilna, between Lithuania and Muscovy.

03 Method

The record, the DECODE transcription (AJ, 2020) and both images were fetched with the project’s DECODE session. The transcriptions of the G15 key records R549–R646 were fetched too. score_keys.py decrypted R478 with each of them and ranked the results by coverage and a Hungarian 4-gram score: R609 came first at −2.57 per letter, well clear of the unrelated keys, which scored below −3.7. The transcription was then compared with the page images, and seven slips were corrected: iv→10 (jöttek), a clear accented é transcribed as 0, 1257→127 (vajdától), l, i, 86, i→6, 1, 86, 5, a dropped 74 (fülének), a dropped 63 (lelkemre) and the crossed-out signs after 300, 16. R609 gives 59 both as U and as ho; ho fits here (Vilnához). decode.py writes the decipherment.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Group 50 is not in R609. It occurs once and is taken as ba from context (barát, the friar), grade C.
  • Page 2, lines 5–6: tolmácsot ha az ha az tizéntee [s.] asséraltadé [ö], about ten groups. Every group has its key value, but the sense is not clear; possibly a slip by the encipherer (grade I).
  • The clear passages are cursive and only partly transcribed here. Their words in brackets above are grade M.
  • Corrected 30 September 2026: five places in the normalised Hungarian had drifted from the decrypt and now follow the groups and their R609 values: vissza is ment (was viszont ismét [elment]), a moszkva békességre kéri a svédet with no hogy (was hogy a moszkva békességet kéri a svédtől), minden elvött várakat, all the fortresses taken (was mindenekelőtt várakat, above all fortresses), Pekri uram általa, through him (was által), and minden szándéka, his whole intention (was mindenben szándéka). The English follows.

Measured: 414 cipher groups, all with a value (one from context); about 404 decipher as sense (97.6%).
