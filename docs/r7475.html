An unsigned adviser to Hetman Sieniawski, about 1707

A Polish letter warning the hetman against the Bishop of Chełmno’s mediation and Stanisław’s offers, deciphered letter by letter between the lines at the time; transcribed here with its gloss.

Method: read from existing decipherment · Extent: complete

At a glanceExisting decipherment · complete · 2,434 of 2,444 numbers glossed (99.6%)

The letter
An unsigned adviser in Sieniawski’s camp to Hetman Adam Mikołaj Sieniawski, about 1707 by its content, in Polish with Latin phrases.
Where
Archiwum Narodowe w Krakowie, Zbiór Dzieduszyckich 26/32 nr 13 pp. 29–30 (DECODE R7475, catalogued with the Brâncoveanu letters of the same bundle; status N/A).
The cipher
Numbers separated by dots, two to four for each letter in alphabetical order (a = 17, 18; b = 20, 21; c = 22, 23 … z = 56, 57; the vowels apart), and one code group, 600 = król szwedzki.
How it was deciphered
A second hand wrote the decipherment letter by letter over the numbers. Aligning it with the cipher gives the reading and rebuilds the table.
What it says
Do not trust the Warsaw gazettes, the Bishop of Chełmno’s mediation or Stanisław’s treaty offers: “lubo nie wierzam gazetom warszawskim”.
Still open
About ten numbers under blots or struck through, and the writer’s name.

DECODE catalogues this record with letters in other keys, which have their own pages (a different cipher in each): Brâncoveanu and Cantacuzino (sieniawski1708), news from Bender (turculet1709), Chomentowski at the Porte (chomentowski1713) and a Vienna agent to Baron Schenck (schenck1706).

01 The letter

The writer, who does not sign, advises Sieniawski against trusting the mediation of the Bishop of Chełmno (Teodor Potocki) and the treaty offers of King Stanisław: “lubo nie wierzam gazetom warszawskim, nec respektem acceptowania mediacyi biskupa chełmińskiego y dania paszportów od WMM Pana na przyjazd do Warszawy”, and later reports that the King of Sweden will go towards Moscow (“pewną wiadomość że pójdzie król szwedzki na Mos[kwę]”). That places it about 1707, before Charles XII marched on Russia. A few Latin words stand in clear among the numbers (plus quam, cuncta, sine, ante); other Latin phrases, such as sub specie amicitiae, are enciphered with the Polish.

02 The table

Aligned with the gloss, the 2,444 numbers give a homophonic table in alphabetical order, its own and not the Wallachian chancellery’s key A or the Turculeț key B:

a 17 18 · b 20 21 · c 22 23 · d 24 25 · e 11–14 · f 26 27 · g 28 29 · h 30 31 · i 9 10 · k 32 33
l/ł 34–37 · m 38 39 · n 40 41 · o 7 8 · p 42 43 · r 46 47 · s 48 49 · t 50 51 · u 5 6 · w 52 53 55 · y 3 4 · z/ż 56 57

2,434 of the 2,444 numbers carry a legible gloss aligned letter for letter (99.6%). Where gloss and table disagree (50 glossed z seven times against t 76 times; 47 glossed p three times against r 32 times) the cause is most likely the shapes of the figures in this hand (50/56, 47/42, 57/52), not true homophones. The writer’s own spelling slips (waszawskim, chłminskiemu) are kept.

03 What remains

  • Ten numbers without an aligned gloss (ink blots, a smudged figure, a few slips of the pen), a struck passage (line 58) and a lone unglossed “1.”.
  • The writer is not identified.

04 Sources

  • Archiwum Narodowe w Krakowie, Zbiór Dzieduszyckich 26/32 nr 13 pp. 29–30: DECODE R7475.
