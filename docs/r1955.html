Frederick the Great’s ministerial letter to Louis Michell, 28 December 1751

The second letter sent to Michell that day, in the code of his own reports: a table from two contemporary Dutch decipherments gives about a quarter of its groups, enough for fragments but not for the sense.

The letter
Frederick II to his resident in London, Louis Michell, Berlin, 28 December 1751: the ministerial rescript, issued in the King’s name through the foreign department. It opens in clear, Toutes vos dépêches m’ont été bien rendues.
Where
Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, archive of Prince William V, inv. 198, the Dutch interceptors’ copy. DECODE R1955, listed as not decrypted.
The cipher
A large numerical code: groups from 2 to 5346, half-groups such as 150½, and homophones even for de and que. It is not the King’s cabinet code.
How it was tried
WAINUT built a table of 271 code values from the Dutch decipherments written over two of Michell’s own reports (DECODE R1050, R1051) and found that it fits this letter.
What it says
Fragments only: an avis, a negotiation with Vienna, the Prince Stadholder, the States, ce projet.
Still open
The letter as a text: 265 of the 360 groups have no value, so it is decoded, not read. More decipherments of Michell’s reports are not online. On 23 October 2026 the Hague decipherment registers on the Nationaal Archief IIIF were checked heading by heading: Fagel 5208 holds the Dutch clear decipherments of the ministerial rescripts to Michell in this code, nos. 1–8 (2 November to 7 December 1751), and Fagel 5177 the cabinet-code letters, but the rescript of 28 December is in neither, and the copies carry no figures, so they add no values.
Credit
The match and the partial decoding are WAINUT’s (pull request #29, 11 October 2026); checked here.

This letter was written up together with the King’s own letter of the same day until 14 October 2026. That letter, DECODE R1957, is in Frederick’s cabinet code and has its own page, Frederick II to Louis Michell, 28 December 1751: a different cipher, with its own page.

01 The letter

The King wrote to his London resident through two channels: rescripts from the foreign department under Podewils, and his own immediate letters. On 28 December 1751 Michell was sent one of each. Both went by post through the Dutch Republic, where the Stadtholder’s cabinet opened and copied them; the copies are in the papers of William V, inv. 198. The immediate letter tells Michell to report to the King alone, with no copies to the department, which is why the second letter exists.

The copy is headed Lettre du Roi de Prusse au Secrétaire Michell du 28 Decemb. 1751 and signed Féderic. The opening is in clear: Toutes vos dépêches m’ont été bien rendues et la dernière est du 14e de ce mois. Then come the code groups, separated by points, and the date line. The Politische Correspondenz prints only one letter to Michell of that date, the immediate one, so this text is not in print.

02 The code

The two transcriptions agree on 352 of 360 groups. This project’s of 19 September counts 362, because it splits a run of nine digits written without separators; WAINUT’s of 11 October keeps it as one group. 296 of the 362 groups are distinct and the commonest occurs only five times. That is a large code with several numbers for each common word, and a single letter of it gives nothing to a ciphertext-only attack.

It is not the code of the King’s immediate letters. The cabinet key that WAINUT rebuilt for R1957 gives values to 2.8–5.6% of these groups, against 69.5% of R1957’s. DECODE R1953, von der Hellen’s letter of 4 January 1752 from the same archive, is in a different, smaller code.

03 The partial decoding

DECODE R1050 (London, 5 September 1752) and R1051 (London, 12 November 1751) are Hague copies of Michell’s own reports to the King, with the contemporary Dutch decipherment written over the numbers. WAINUT paired DECODE’s transcriptions of them token by token and got 512 code-word pairs for 271 codes. The code held over the ten months between the two reports: of 40 codes found in both, 32 have the same commonest value.

That table gives a value to 95 of R1955’s 360 groups (26.4%). On R1957 it gives only 5.5%, so R1955 is in Michell’s code. The decoded words also sit together sensibly more often than chance: 19 of 30 adjacent pairs are word pairs found in 18th-century diplomatic French, against 11.1 when the same values are shuffled (p = 0.001).

The fragments include avis … sur … negociation avec … de Vienne … les difficulte(s) … article … du Prince Stadhouder … maison … cet avis et … états … depuis avis est … ce projet … nouvelle … doit etre … des esperance(s) de (spelled as the table gives them). The letter is about Dutch affairs and a negotiation involving Vienna. More than that cannot be said from a quarter of the groups.

WAINUT also tested whether the rescript repeats the wording of a royal letter of the same days printed in the Politische Correspondenz, which would supply its text. The rule was fixed in advance. No letter of 22 December 1751 to 10 January 1752 scores above the controls (best z = 2.69, highest control 4.85), while a 17% sample of a real letter is found at z above 27.

04 What remains uncertain

  • 265 of the 360 groups have no value. The table comes from two letters ten months apart, and a changed or homophone value could mislead.
  • The table depends on DECODE’s transcriptions of the R1050/R1051 decipherments, which were not checked against the images line by line.
  • Further decipherments of Michell’s reports are probably in the Koninklijk Huisarchief (A31-1181), not online. A contemporary decipherment of this letter may be in the Nationaal Archief, 1.01.50 inv. 159, not digitised.
  • Uncertain digits: l. 10 194? (9 or 7), l. 21 2?07 (1 or 4), and the nine-digit run on l. 16 (best split 2 2407 2398).

Frederick II (ministerial rescript) to Louis Michell, Berlin, 28 Dec 1751 (DECODE R1955) decoded, not read

The ministerial rescript in the King's name to his envoy in London, opening in clear 'Toutes vos dépêches m'ont été bien rendues et la dernière est du 14e de ce mois', then 362 code groups. WAINUT decoded 95 of 360 groups (26.4%) with the envoy table rebuilt from the Dutch decipherments of Michell's reports R1050/R1051: fragments only, the subject cannot be stated with confidence.

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.
