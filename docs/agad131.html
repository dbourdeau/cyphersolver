01 The letter

A letter dated “D. 11. July 1757, z Wilna”, ending “manu notus” and unsigned, in AGAD Warsaw, fonds 338, unit 131 (five scans). The Polish is in clear and the cipher parts are French. The ledger (catalogue 1580) listed it among the letters partly in cipher, with no reading. Prabal Gupta, directing AI models that made the transcription passes and the reading, read it with the key he had found for letter 446 (the page branicki1757), applied without change; the project could not reach the scans and worked from his files.

02 The key and its test

A reciprocal alphabet of twelve pairs of letters plus a number code: a–h, b–q, c–r, d–s, e–l, f–t, g–z, i–m, k–x, n–o, p–u and w–y. Numbers from 830 up, and the opening row of numbers, act as punctuation or nulls. The value 580 is Pétersbourg; the contributor proposes 185 = Branicki and 275 = Dantzig (conjectures); 190 stays open. A count-matching fit on this letter alone, on French next-letter counts, gives the same key for 97.7% of letter tokens (the contributor’s figure, not repeated here).

Checked here by an AI model (no human has repeated it), with an own script: the 64 cipher lines of the transcription decrypt with the twelve pairs to the contributor’s decryption in 62 lines and at 99.9% letter agreement; the two other lines differ by struck-out marks, and the counting rule for “cipher lines” is the script’s own. A word-list test (about 80 common French words) puts 46.3% of the letters in listed words, against a mean of 4.5% and a best of 19.1% for 200 shuffled keys. The word list shares vocabulary with the reading, so this shows that the key makes French, not how many words are right.

03 The reading

In the contributor’s reading (reading.md, with an English translation):

  • The Castellan of Brest [?Abramowicz] and Count Pociej, Strażnik of Lithuania, ask His Excellency [?Branicki], Grand General of the Crown, for protection and a subsidy for the coming Tribunal. Beyond the French subsidy the Prince Grand General of Lithuania gave a thousand ducats, and their borrowing reached two hundred thousand francs.
  • The Castellan asks support for a memorial of his nephew Ciechanowiecki, Starost of Opsa, to go to the French ambassador, the Count de L’Hôpital.
  • Strutyński, Starost of Szakinów, came armed to the Brasław dietine, in the contributor’s reading to kill a Ciechanowiecki (the word “massacre” is a conjecture), fled because he did not trust the courts, and is shielded by Field Marshal Apraksin, who stopped the Tribunal’s case. Rumours follow of a crime at [?St Petersburg] about 1730 and of a betrayal of King Stanisław at [?Danzig].
  • In clear Polish a secretary, perhaps Bouwen, did not write by this post; the closing is “obligowany sługa”.

Konopczyński (1909, p. 216) and Matuszewicz’s memoirs (vol. II, p. 237; vol. III, pp. 84 and 136) tell the Strutyński affair; the contributor found that they do not give this letter on those pages. The writer is probably Matuszewicz, an inference from letter 1398, which ends with the same memorial and is signed “Matusewic”; the names, the identification of the addressee and the history are his reading and were not checked here.

04 What remains uncertain

  • The contributor estimates that about 25 of some 460 cipher words are doubtful or hold an unread code (so about 94.6% read, under the 95% bar; not recounted here), three cipher signs are unread, and the code group 190 is open. Doubtful words include Autres, Ce pourquoi, par de, massacre, cris/crimes, d’être entré, tous, marque, and the places Pińsk, Szkultyn, Königsberg and Bruwen/Bouwen.
  • Two transcription passes agree on 88% of 2,514 tokens; about 300 differences are not settled one by one on the scan. The transcription is an AI’s; the contributor re-read the hard lines on enlarged images, but no one at the project has compared it with the scans.
  • Unit 83 (Beck, 1757), the rest of the fonds, key sheet 1196, and the Radziwiłł and Branicki papers in other archives were not searched.

The identities of writer and addressee and the two rumours at the end are the contributor's inference.

Monsieur Moi osobliwy Dobrodzieiu

Na każdym mieyscu maiąc w obligowanym sercu dozgonną WMPanu Dobrodzieiowi wdzięczność, nie omieszkiwam nayniższą moię prezentować WMPanu Dobrodzieiowi submissyą.

Co do Interessu mego, abym długiem czytaniem nie importunował WMPana Dobrodzieia, a do tego że y czas mi nie wystarcza, do JMCi JMPana Starosty Brańskiego oznaymuię y upraszam go, aby moy list cum introcontentis Kommunikował WMPanu Dobrodzieiowi.

Le Castellan de Brzesc et le comte Pociey, Strasznik de Lithuanie, sont très obligés à son Excellence [?Branicki: 185], Grand Général de la couronne.

J'ai leur dit que son Excellence déclare donner sa protection et procurer le subside, le [190], pour le Tribunal prochain.

Ils ont dit que, outre le subside donné de la France, le Prince Grand Général de Lithuanie a donné mille ducats. Le Strasznik de Lithuanie, le Castellan de Brzesc et son frère ont prêté chez monsieur Biallozor, Woyski de [?Pinsk], trente mille francs dans le commencement du Tribunal. Et chez les [?autres], ce qu'ils ont emprunté d'argent est devenu jusqu'à la somme de deux cent mille francs.

[?Ce pourquoi] ils supplient son Excellence de leur faire accorder par sa protection pour le grand subside. Je vous prie très humblement de proposer cela adroitement, que je ne tombe pas dans la disgrâce.

Le Castellan de Brzesc supplie son Excellence, pour le mémoire de son neveu monsieur Ciechanowiecki, Starosta Opeski, lieutenant de son Excellence, pour recommander à son Excellence monseigneur le comte de Lopital, ambassadeur de France, lequel mémoire est tenoris sequentis.

Monsieur Strutynski, Starosta de Szakinow, connu en Lithuanie par ses actions de désordres, principalement dans l'action avec monsieur Ciechanowiecki, frère du Starosta de Mscislaw, au sujet de sa procédure, avec son frère germain monsieur Ciechanowiecki, Starosta Opeski, colonel de son Excellence le Grand Général.

Qui monsieur Strutynski, s'étant attroupé de toute sorte de ses gens, vint les armes à la main à la diétine de Braslaw, dans le dessein de massacrer monsieur Ciechanowiecki, Starosta Opeski. Et [?par de] cette famille il exécuta ce [?massacre] dans la personne dudit monsieur Ciechanowiecki, frère du Starosta de Mscislaw.

Le corps du défunt fut porté à Wilna et présenté juridiquement devant le Grand Tribunal de Lithuanie, l'année passée. Là-dessus, sa famille ayant fait le procès à monsieur Strutynski, Starosta de Szakinow, tâcha de le convaincre du crime commis.

Secrètement, dans l'autre action avec monsieur [?Szkultyn], gentilhomme de Samogitie, avec qui monsieur Strutynski, Starosta de Szakinow, étant en procédure au sujet des limites, a subordonné de le tuer.

C'est à cause des [?cris], ledit monsieur Strutynski, ne se fiant pas à la justice, se retira [?à l'armée] de Russie, pour se rendre à la protection de son Excellence monseigneur le comte d'Apraxyn, feldmaréchal des armées de sa Majesté Impératrice Russienne.

Déjà les procédures estoient faites contre ledit délinquent dans le Tribunal de Lithuanie, mais le comte d'Apraxin a empêché de continuer. Il s'est opposé même de son autorité à la conviction de ce gentilhomme, par rapport à la protection qu'il lui accorde, sous prétexte [?d'être entré] au service de l'armée de Russie.

On dit qu'il a fait à Pétersbourg une action noire environ de l'année mil sept cent trente. Le Palatin de Belz l'a tiré d'affaire de perdre la vie.

On dit aussi que monsieur Strutinski à [?Dantzig: 275] a voulu trahir le Roi Stanislas.

On dit que dans le jugement du Grand Maréchal de la couronne il y a des décrets contre [?tous] ses désordres.

JMPan de [?Bruwen], Sekretarz [?Posła] JMCi Dobrodzieia, exkuzował się, że przez tę pocztę nie miał czasu pisać do WMPana Dobrodzieia. Y gdym mu mowił, że ieżeli będzie miał czas, czy będzie pisał, poszedł do Sua Exci w tymże pokoiu będącego y coś cicho mowił. Nie wiem tedy, czy pisał na tę pocztę do WMPana Dobrodzieia. Iest człowiek prosty,

et souvent il est [?sous] le comte d'Apraxin.

Déclare déjà les gouvernemens de Keynixbergue et des autres pays de la Prusse, et [?marque] le palatinat de cette province aux seigneurs de Lithuanie publiquement. Il a [?réponse] au Castellan de Witebsk à Kowno qu'il conserve les tables pour la mémoire des siècles, sur lesquelles étoit le conseil de la guerre.

Oddaię się nieodmienney łasce, będąc na całe życie moie [?WMMPana] Osobliwego mego Dobrodzieia obligowany sługa, manu notus.

D. 11. July 1757, z Wilna.

## Limits

- The final paragraph's subject, sentence boundary and two proposed words remain uncertain. The literal “sous” may conceal “soûl” (drunk), with a new sentence about Apraksin.
- The family connective and “massacre” remain conjectures. The literal wording is “par de” and “mattare”. The French also leaves “cris” or “crimes” and the phrase about entering Russian service open.
- Pinsk probably means Pińsk. Keynixbergue probably means Königsberg. Both identifications remain tentative.
- The clear Polish remains a working reading. Bruwen/Buwen/Bouwen and the title Posła remain doubtful. Xięcia is also possible. The closing honorific has an uncertain extra M.

# AGAD 1/338/0/1/131: English translation

From `reading.md`, with the same holes. The first two paragraphs and the paragraph on Bouwen translate clear Polish. The rest translates the cipher French.

Sir, my especial Benefactor,

Everywhere I carry in my obliged heart a lifelong gratitude to Your Honour. I do not fail to present my humblest submission to Your Honour.

As to my affair, I do not want to trouble Your Honour with long reading. Time also runs short. I inform His Honour the Starost of Brańsk and ask him to share my letter and its contents with Your Honour.

The Castellan of Brzesc and Count Pociej, Strażnik of Lithuania, are very obliged to His Excellency [?Branicki: 185], Grand General of the Crown.

I told them that His Excellency declares that he will give his protection and obtain the subsidy, the [190], for the coming Tribunal.

They said that France gave a subsidy. The Prince Grand General of Lithuania also gave a thousand ducats. The Strażnik of Lithuania, the Castellan of Brzesc and his brother [?borrowed] thirty thousand francs from Mr Białłozor, Wojski of [?Pinsk]. This was at the start of the Tribunal. With what they borrowed from the [?others], the money reached two hundred thousand francs. [The French says “lent” for the first sum. The later borrowing passage supports the interpretation here.]

[?For this reason] they beg His Excellency to have a large subsidy granted to them through his protection. I ask you very humbly to propose this skilfully, so that I do not fall into disgrace.

The Castellan of Brzesc begs His Excellency to recommend his nephew's memorial to the Count de L'Hôpital, ambassador of France. His nephew is Mr Ciechanowiecki, Starost of Opsa, a lieutenant of His Excellency. The memorial is of the following tenor.

Mr Strutyński, Starost of Szakinów, is known in Lithuania for his disorderly actions. The text singles out the affair with Mr Ciechanowiecki, brother of the Starost of Mścisław. It refers to his lawsuit and to his full brother, Mr Ciechanowiecki, Starost of Opsa, a colonel of His Excellency the Grand General. [The source leaves the connection incomplete.]

This Mr Strutyński gathered all sorts of his men. He came, arms in hand, to the dietine of Brasław with the intention of massacring Mr Ciechanowiecki, Starost of Opsa. And [?through/of] this family he carried out this [?massacre] on the said Mr Ciechanowiecki, brother of the Starost of Mścisław. [The family connective remains unclear. The French has “par de” and “mattare”.]

They carried the dead man's body to Wilno. They presented it in law before the Grand Tribunal of Lithuania last year. His family brought suit against Mr Strutyński, Starost of Szakinów. They then tried to convict him of the crime committed.

Mr Strutyński, Starost of Szakinów, had another lawsuit over boundaries. It was with Mr [?Szkultyn], a gentleman of Samogitia. Strutyński secretly commissioned someone to kill him.

Because of the [?outcries], the said Mr Strutyński withdrew [?to the army] of Russia. He did not trust the courts. He sought the protection of His Excellency Count Apraksin. The count was Field Marshal of the armies of Her Majesty the Russian Empress. [The French “cries” may mean outcries or conceal “crimes”. The word before “de Russie” has an unread sign.]

The Tribunal of Lithuania had already begun proceedings against the said offender. Count Apraksin prevented them from continuing. He even used his own authority to oppose this gentleman's conviction. He granted him protection under the pretext [?of having entered] the service of the Russian army. [The French “de trentre” does not fully spell the proposed phrase.]

They say he committed a black deed at St Petersburg about 1730. The Palatine of Bełz saved him from losing his life.

They also say that Mr Strutyński at [?Danzig: 275] tried to betray King Stanisław.

They say that the court of the Grand Marshal of the Crown has issued decrees against [?all] his disorders.

Mr de [?Bruwen], secretary to His Excellency [?the envoy], my Benefactor, excused himself for having no time to write to Your Honour by this post. When I asked whether he would write if he had time, he went to His Excellency in the same room. He spoke quietly. I therefore do not know whether he wrote to Your Honour by this post. He is a plain man,

and he is often [?under] Count Apraksin. [The French says “sous”. It may conceal “soûl” (drunk), with a new sentence about Apraksin. The secretary's name may be Bruwen, Buwen or Bouwen. His title may mean envoy or prince.]

[?He] already announces the governorships of Keynixbergue and the other lands of Prussia. He publicly [?assigns] this province's palatinate to Lithuanian lords. He [?gave an answer] to the Castellan of Witebsk at Kowno that he should preserve the tables for the memory of future ages. The council of war took place on those tables. [Two proposed words and the subject remain uncertain. Pinsk probably means Pińsk. Keynixbergue probably means Königsberg. Both identifications remain tentative.]

I give myself to your unchanging favour. I am, for my whole life, Your Honour's, my especial Benefactor's, obliged servant, known by my hand. [The closing honorific has an uncertain extra M.]

11 July 1757, from Wilno.
