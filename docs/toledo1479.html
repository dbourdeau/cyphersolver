An unsigned cipher letter from Castile to Lorenzo de’ Medici, Toledo, 1479

01 The letter

An unsigned letter “De Spagna” to Lorenzo de’ Medici, dated 31 August 1479 on the verso and ending “In Toledo”, in the Archivio di Stato di Firenze (Mediceo avanti il Principato, filza LXXII, doc. 171). It is Italian with cipher runs set between clear words: one sign for each letter, and code numbers and symbols for persons. The printed inventory (vol. III, 1957, p. 183) lists it as “Anonimo” with no content; the ledger (catalogue 2322) listed it as a bare cipher letter. Prabal Gupta, directing AI models that transcribed the archive’s images, found the key and read it in part; the project could not reach the images and worked from his files. According to the contributor the images are served at 1,272 by 1,848 pixels, so the cipher signs are only 8 to 14 pixels high.

02 The key and its test

The key has 20 signs (two of them write h), found from the cipher with the clear words between the cipher runs as cribs. The contributor says it is not the key of the 1478 letters of the same filza (a cross is a there and d here); that was not compared here. About 60 code numbers and nomenclator symbols (a Φ with two dots, a dotted square, a dotted circle, a dotted triangle) stand for persons and other words; none has a value.

Checked here, with an own script: the 108 cipher words that are runs of letter signs decrypt with the key to the contributor’s literal decodes in every case. A word-list test (about 90 common Italian words) puts 55.1% of their letters in listed words, against a mean of 9.4% and a best of 27.7% for 200 shuffled keys. The contributor reports 65.3% against a mean of 1.9% and a best of 11.4% with a larger 15th-century list, a blind check of 31 words (30 agreed) and a method check on test texts. The word list shares vocabulary with the reading, so this shows that the key makes Italian, not how many words are right.

03 The reading

In the contributor’s reading, with persons left as code items, no sentence of the cipher is whole, but the clauses are Italian:

  • the writer sent his last letter, it seems through a merchant’s house, perhaps at Seville (both doubtful); a league “a difensione degli stati” has been or will be concluded;
  • the parties show “sì poca fermeza”; someone “farà loro buona cera e gl’enpierà di buone parole” (will greet them warmly and fill them with fine words);
  • in a passage naming Santiglana and Osma someone “mostra esere afezionato a V. M.” (letters doubtful) and “ve ne potete fidare”; a brother, a code item, is the “chomandatore magore d’Alchantara” and a close friend of the writer;
  • “con lei si farà l’acordo” (the queen); a betrothal; a legate; “una decima a preti”, a tithe on the clergy, of which one party “tocha la metà”.

The contributor links the letter to the peace of Alcáçovas of 4 September 1479, which Queen Isabella negotiated with Beatriz of Portugal and which arranged royal betrothals, and to Florence’s war with the Pope and Naples after the Pazzi conspiracy of 1478. The letter names neither event in clear; the link is his inference, and the background itself is his and was not checked here. The writer is not named.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Of 113 cipher words, 88 are read in full (77.9%), 21 hold a doubtful letter and 4 are open; counting the 60 code items for persons that stay unread, 88 of 173 cipher items are read (51%). Seven spans (lines 4, 8, 12, 13, 22 and 29) hold 43 unsettled letter positions, and the clear hand of lines 19–20, 28 and 33–35 is only partly read. The issue text says 668 of 730 letter positions (91.5%); the contributor’s folder says 663 (90.8%), which is used here.
  • No coded person is identified. The archive’s images are too small to settle most doubtful signs; sharper photographs are needed.
  • The transcription is an AI’s, re-read against the images by the contributor’s models; no person has compared it with the images, and the project did not see them.
  • Rubinstein’s edition of Lorenzo’s letters (vols. III and IV) and studies of Florentines in Castile in 1478–80 were not checked, so a printed reading is possible.

Cipher letter 'De Spagna' to Lorenzo de' Medici, Toledo, 31 August 1479 (ASF, MAP LXXII 171) read in part

An unsigned letter from the court of Castile to Lorenzo de' Medici, in the contributor's reading: the writer's last letter went through a merchant's house at Seville; a league 'a difensione degli stati' has been or will be concluded; the parties show 'sì poca fermeza'; someone is devoted to Lorenzo ('ve ne potete fidare') and a brother of his is the comendador mayor of Alcántara and the writer's close friend; an agreement with the queen is to be made; a betrothal, a legate, a tithe on the clergy of which one party 'tocha la metà'. Persons are code items and stay unread; the context (the peace of Alcáçovas, 4 September 1479) is the contributor's inference.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (51% here); the holes are marked in the reading.
