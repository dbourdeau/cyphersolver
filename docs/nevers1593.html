Nevers to Pisany, 1593: two letters in the numerical key no. 46

Tomokiyo’s Nevers cipher no. 46, a known key, gives a value to every figure of Nevers’s two letters to Pisany of autumn 1593, but the register copies carry wrong figures, and neither letter yet builds sentences throughout: both are decoded, not read.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 17 September 2026 · updated 26 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: none (key obtained, text not read)

At a glanceKnown key applied · none (key obtained, text not read)

The letter
Seven letters of the Duke of Nevers from the road to Rome, autumn 1593, to Revol and Pisany, while he sought absolution for the newly converted Henri IV from Clement VIII.
Where
BnF fr. 3985 and fr. 3986; the catalogue lists them as “avec chiffre” and treated them as one key with one passage deciphered.
The cipher
Two keys. The Pisany letters use the numerical cipher Tomokiyo catalogued as no. 46: a two-digit alphabet with homophones, barred and plain figures for words, double-dotted figures for names and the digit 1 as a null. The Revol letters use the symbol cipher no. 60.
How it was read
The key was transcribed from the full-resolution table and checked against the office’s own decipherment of a Gondi letter in the same key. It gives a value for every figure of both Pisany letters.
What it says
The 8 September letter is deciphered completely: the Pope is not to be contented and one should not go. The 14 October letter, after Possevino said at Poschiavo that Nevers would not be received, is read in long stretches.
Still open
The Revol letters: 27 August is in clear throughout, 9 October carries two and a half lines of symbols, and three were not reached on the image. They need the symbol key matched to the copyist’s hand.

01 The letters

The catalogue took seven ciphered copies of Nevers’s letters as one office’s key. They are in two: the five to Revol are in the Court’s symbol cipher no. 60 (their own page), the two to Pisany in no. 46, below.

ItemDate and placeToWhere on the imageCipher
fr. 3985 no. 116Nevers, 8 Sept 1593Pisanyf. 209 (canvas 419)no. 46, decoded, not read
fr. 3986 no. 81“Ville” [Edolo], 14 Oct 1593Pisanyf. 168 (canvas 332)no. 46, decoded, not read

Both volumes are microfilm digitisations on Gallica with every canvas labelled NP; the folio is close to half the canvas number, and the 1400 px canvases do not resolve two-digit figures, so the pages were fetched at full IIIF resolution (about 4,900 px wide). Tomokiyo’s Henri IV article states the division of keys: Gondi and Nevers used the symbol cipher no. 60 with the King and Revol from July 1593, and the numerical no. 46 with Vivonne (Pisany). The catalogue entry had not taken that in.

02 The key

Tomokiyo’s published image of the key is 225 pixels wide; the Gallica image of the sheet is 7,677. The table has a barred series from 99 down to 2 for common words (80̄ de, 56̄ je, 51̄ luy, 53̄ la, 11̄ que, 9̄ quil, 38̄ ne, 73̄ est, 5̄ recherche), a plain series on the figures the alphabet leaves free (24 sa, 28 sans, 29 se, 87 si, 45 tout, 62 vous, and the provinces), and a double-dotted series of names (99 Pape, 98 Roy, 88 Duc de Nevers, 70 Cardinal Gondi, 89 Duc de Mantoue, 54 Revol, 34 De Pizany, 1 Ambassadeur). In the letters the digit 1, and once 0, is dropped inside a group as a null, the device the sister key no. 45 prescribes in words. The conventions were checked against a letter the office deciphered interlinearly in the same key, Girolamo Gondi to Pisany of 22 September 1593 (fr. 3986 ff. 64–65v): personne, rencontre, congregation, absoudre, sainct, siege, ladicte, Romme all come out figure for figure.

03 8 September 1593, fr. 3985 f. 209

Three passages, 122 figure groups, all in the key. Decoded, not read: passage 2 holds as a sentence, but passages 1 and 3 rest on figures the copyist wrote wrong. Passage 1’s figures were looked at again on the Gallica IIIF image on 23 October 2026 and are as transcribed, so these are slips in the copy, not in the transcription. The partial reading is texts/f209_reading.md.

  • “Car j’estois sur ce mesme faict” l’on dissuadoit [Pape] de rechercher [Pape] luy don[n]es[t] l’absolutio[n]. The copy has the Pope’s sign twice; one of them is probably the King’s (98̈) in sense. Tomokiyo deciphered this passage with the figure 14 for 74 and the name as Beaulieu; the figures are 74 and, in the next passage, 54 Revol.
  • “Car” [Duc de Nevers] alléguoit qu’il n’estoyt besoin qu’il a[l]la[s]t sans cela, parce qu’il ne feroit rien, then in clear “Je suys attendant des nouvelles de” [Revol] “à qui j’ay escript”. Nevers is named by his sign in his own letter; the 61 in allast is a slip for 65.
  • “Je suys d’opinion que” l’on n’a [11] volonté d[14]e contanter le Pape, que l’on n’[y a]ille poynt, and in clear “Et suys bien ayse que soyez de mesme volonté”. Several figures in this passage are the copyist’s (11 where the u of volonté is expected, 14 inside de, 57 83 84 where y a i is expected).

04 14 October 1593, fr. 3986 f. 168

Seven full lines of figures, a short run and one more line: 269 groups, 268 in the key. No decipherment, original or edition of this letter is known. On 22 October 2026 the figures were re-read on the IIIF image (4,948 px): fourteen corrections, mostly bars set on the wrong figure in the first pass and groups wrongly divided (texts/f168_corrections.tsv). The reading (texts/f168_reading.md, English f168_en.md) now holds in about half its sentences:

[le Duc de Nevers] ne sera receu come [Ambassadeur] pour pluseurs resons.

Je luy ay demandé come [...] [le Pape] voloit que j’y alase; el[le] m’a dit n’avoir autre charge, ne me declarans que j’y alle ou non.

Ce qu’il faict tenir segret, et le [faire] savoir a [Cardinal Gondi] [...].

Lines 4, 5 and the last line keep holes where the copyist’s figures are corrupt (24 for a, 27̄ for 29̄, dropped figures), legible but wrong, so the letter stays decoded, not read. Tomokiyo’s fragment stopped at “Je luy ay demandé”. Only a second witness, Pisany’s original, which has not been located, could restore the corrupt groups.

05 The letters to Revol

Nevers’s letters of the same months to Louis de Revol, secretary of state, are in the Court’s symbol cipher no. 60, a different cipher, with its own page.

## fr. 3985 f. 209, Nevers to Pisany, 8 September 1593 (`ct_3985_f209.txt`, `decode_f209.txt`)

One page, three passages in figures inside a clear letter. Transcribed from the full-resolution image, 122 tokens.

1. After "Car j'estois sur ce mesme faict": `50̄ 54 73 17 25 74 55 1 46 83 12 99̈ 80̄ 5̄ 1 42 99̈ 51̄ 76̄ 79 18 53̄ 90 17 36 77
   25 12 83 29` = **l'on dissuadoit [Pape] de rechercher [Pape] luy don[n]es[t] l'absolutio[n]**. Tomokiyo's reading of
   this passage (with 14 for 74 and "Beaulieu") is confirmed and corrected. One of the two 99̈ is probably a slip for
   98̈ Roy ("l'on dissuadoit le Roy de rechercher que le Pape luy donnast l'absolution"); the copy has 99̈ both times.
   Final 29 (se) stands where 26/36/46 + 57/58 (on) is expected.
2. After "Car": `88̈ 97̄ 40 36 76 13 9̄ 38̄ 73̄ 46 76 12 90 69 17 46 73 58 9̄ 34 61 74 13 28 85̄ 53̄ 26̄ 29 11̄ 63 77 38̄ 71 69 42
   83 12 32 63 79 58` + "de" `54̈` = **[Duc de Nevers] alléguoit qu'il n'estoyt besoin qu'il a[l]la[s]t sans cela, parce
   qu'il ne feroit rien … de [Revol]** (61 is a slip for 65 l; Tomokiyo read "atat" and "Beaulieu" for 54̈ = Revol).
   Nevers speaks of himself in the third person, or the copyist substituted the name sign for "je".
3. After "Je suys d'opinion que": `65 46 58 57 34 11 46 77 26 58 13 55 14 69 92 36 57 12 74 58 13 79 42 77 69 21 / 34 52
   79 19 35 79 65 26 58 58 57 83 84 77 65 1 79 21 46 76 57 13` = **l'on n'a [11] volonté d[14]e contanter le Pape, que l'on
   n'[y a]ille poynt**, followed in clear by "Et suys bien ayse que soyez de mesme volonté". 11 stands where u (15) is
   expected and 14 (x) inside "de"; 57 83 84 where y a i is expected. Sense: if there is no will to satisfy the Pope,
   do not go to Rome at all.

## Notes, line by line

- Line 1: unchanged from the first pass (Tomokiyo's fragment, confirmed). 66 and the lone 9 1 5 are nulls; 32 69
  [66] 9 1 5 79 15 = re·ceu.
- Line 2: `76 55 69 45̄ 58 80̄` = y de ma n de (demandé). `82̄` = come (the first pass read 81̄ dans: the second figure
  is the copyist's 2, the same shape as in 92). `7 69 2` is unread (1/7 and 2/9 are close in this hand; no value set
  tried gives a word: "comment", "si le", "ce que" were tested). `11̄ 56̄ 76 34 65 74 18 69` = que je y alase (the
  first pass read `5_ 67 63 46 57 41 08`: the bar over 5 runs on over 6, and the ı before ϑ is a 1, not 1 0).
  `79 77 45̄ 96 54 83 12` = el ma [null] dit: "el[le] m'a dit", elle = Sa Sainteté.

# Nevers to Pisany, 14 October 1593 (BnF fr. 3986 f. 168): the cipher in English

⟨... what he says of it, and⟩ that [the Duke of Nevers] will not be received as [Ambassador], for several reasons.

I asked him how [...] [the Pope] wished me to go there; he [His Holiness] told me he had no other charge, not
declaring to me whether I should go or not.

I was content [...] with that, and complained to him of the [letter] which is [...] to me.

[...] made [?alleging] several reasons, and [...] to speak of it [...] you have [...]

[Cardinal Gondi] [the Duke of Mantua] with [them?], to advance further in order to do what has been written to me,
which agrees with ours.

Which he keeps secret, and [will] make it known to [Cardinal Gondi] [...].

⟨... also makes him⟩ [the] [letter] [much] [...]

[...] is our [...]question; it is rumoured [...] secret, all will go [...].
