At a glanceKnown key applied · partial (98% of the cipher)

The letter
A letter of 16 April 1587 (“Ce xvj Avril 1587”), probably from Henri, duc de Guise, to his Lorraine cousin Mercœur. It speaks of “mon frere” and Bellièvre.
Where
BnF fr. 15564 f. 78, DECODE R4158. The catalogue listed three records here as an unknown sender to Mercœur, 1587, none decrypted.
The cipher
A French cipher with code groups, the same as f. 27 (R4155), which George Lasry deciphered in 2022.
How it was read
Lasry says he solved f. 78 but did not publish it. Every sign was transcribed and decrypted with his key, which fits unchanged. A French language model chose between ambiguous key values, and three passes against the images added about fifteen values.
What it says
The writer asks Mercœur to delay his decision a few days until his brother can take the place once Bellièvre arrives. He also discusses the levy of reiters and a lady who meddles in affairs.
Still open
Three code groups and twelve signs.
Credit
Lasry’s key and his f. 27 decipherment are on Satoshi Tomokiyo’s Cryptiana. f. 151 (R4167) is a different cipher, closed as too short in a separate write-up.

In English, in outline: the writer is glad that “their” advice agrees that the troubles make Mercœur’s presence in his government of Brittany necessary, since the people there fear being abandoned. He asks Mercœur to delay his decision a few days, until his brother can go and take the place once Bellièvre has arrived. The reiter levy is certain, and there is some assurance from the Queen of England. In cipher: the Duke of Parma’s cavalry and troops cannot stay, and it is more important than ever to settle “our affairs” and stop the passage. The matter of “23” should be settled “par la douceur”. A lady has meddled too far in affairs and has persuaded her husband that “his brother and we” seek only his own interest, to his cost. He wants his pensions paid “par la reddition des places”. A letter from Mercœur to “Monsieur de [8ı]” would serve the common good.

03 Method

  1. Cryptiana’s GL.htm (“Duke of Guise? in BnF fr.15564”) gives Lasry’s 2022 key table and his decipherment of f. 27, and says he solved ff. 27, 78, 119 and 142.
  2. The DECODE images of R4155, R4158, R4167 and neighbouring records were downloaded. The f. 27 overlay was used to calibrate the sign shapes against Lasry’s table.
  3. Every sign of f. 78 was labelled, one line per cipher run: 952 labels, with each nomenclator group as one label (998 signs when group members are counted singly).
  4. Where a label had more than one key value, a French letter model of about 1600 (5-grams) chose among them. A stricter solver (simulated annealing) that forced one value per label merged distinct homophones and gave worse text, so it was set aside.
  5. Three sign-by-sign passes against native-resolution crops added values missing from Lasry’s sheet or wider than it: ɋ/ƀ = M, ɛ = F, ≈ʃ = P, the R shape = B, :: = H, ∽ = G, ∩ = Y, and the groups ʃʃɤ = MON, ʃbn = NOUS, gb = MONSIEUR, g2 = DUC.

04 What stays open

  • Three code groups that are not in the key, each used once: ʃʃ in run 3, ẜʃʃ in run 7, ≈ƀ in run 22. The guesses from context (MAIS, QUELQUE, PEU DE) are graded M.
  • Twelve unresolved signs in runs 3, 17, 18 and 19 (“EN P[?]”, “[??] EMPESCHER”, “CE[T ORAGE?]”). They are legible, but no key value or French fits the letter count.
  • Name codes in the clear text: 8ı (“Monsieur de …”), 23 and 12 N. Each occurs once or twice and is not on Lasry’s sheet.
  • Together with the doubtful readings, these holes leave 92.1% of the words of the decrypted text read (measured 24 October 2026), under 95%.
  • The sender is not named in the letter. Guise is the most likely writer (Tomokiyo: “probably from the Duke of Guise”), but that is an inference.

## Translation

I have received the letters which it pleased you to write me, and I am very glad that ⟨their advice agrees with what
we had, given the broils, [judged] to be necessary, that our presence in your government [code ʃʃ]is(?), because [?]
the loss, those who(?) would think themselves abandoned, given the fear they are in.⟩ I shall beg you very humbly to
be willing ⟨to delay your resolution only by [code: ?some] few days, waiting until my brother can go and take the
place, as he will do as soon as Bellièvre has arrived, and he will report to you all that we shall have heard of his
charge, so as to take their good advice on it, as in all things. We beg you to believe(?) that we shall follow it
with all the fidelity you could expect from the person in the world who most desires to do you service.⟩

I still hold certain the levy of reiters which I am told is [?that/for the] 12 N(?), and that there is some assurance
on the part of the Queen of England that she will not draw(?) them from France. But I [?see] how little likelihood
there is that such people would wish to make their stay in so barren a country, of whose inconveniences they have
given testimony, ⟨seeing even that the cavalry and the troops of the [code: Duke] of Parma cannot live or remain
there, being on this occasion much disbanded. I(?) hold that certain for their aim and retreat, and always ready in
p[?] and upon us; and it is more important than ever to settle the state of our affairs and [??] prevent the passage,
and drive back th[?is storm], in which we all have a particular interest, Monsieur.⟩

I find your advice very good, that the matter of 23(?), and I am quite of opinion that it would be very fitting ⟨to
settle it by gentleness⟩, having written of it to ⟨Monsieur de [code 8ı]⟩ that [..] massieur(?) to do in this what
we shall advise him. But I must tell you freely that he ⟨has a lady(?) who with [?little] prudence has gone too far
into affairs, having been so ill advised as to want to persuade her husband that her brother and we seek only his
private satisfaction [?] his disadvantage⟩, wanting to draw the payment of his pensions and assignations ⟨by the
surrender of the places⟩. I am in truth ashamed(?) to speak to you of this particular, but, being of one house, this
freedom must be allowed us, thinking(?) that a(?) letter from you to ⟨Monsieur de [code 8ı], with counsel on what
[code K1: ?you] judge fitting, the general good will be much served by it⟩. And I beg you very humbly to be willing to
write to him freely of it, and I kiss your hands very humbly.

This 16th of April 1587.
