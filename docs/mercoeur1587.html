Guise to the Duke of Mercœur, 16 April 1587

→

BnF français 15564 f. 78 (DECODE R4158): one page of clear French with 26 cipher runs. It is read here with the key George Lasry rebuilt in 2022, which he published without a decipherment of this letter.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 22 September 2026

read
On this pageThe three recordsThe readingMethodWhat stays openSources
Summary. The catalogue listed three DECODE records in BnF fr. 15564 as an unknown sender to Mercœur, 1587, none decrypted. One of them, f. 151 (R4167), is a different cipher, and it was closed as too short in a separate write-up. Another, f. 27 (R4155), was read by George Lasry in 2022; his key table and his decipherment of f. 27 are on Satoshi Tomokiyo’s Cryptiana. Lasry says that he also solved f. 78, but his decipherment of it was not published. Here f. 78 is labelled sign by sign and read with Lasry’s key, which fits unchanged. A beam search under a French language model chose among the homophones, and three sign-by-sign passes against the images added about fifteen values. Of 998 cipher signs, 983 read as sense. Three code groups and twelve signs stay open. The letter is dated “Ce xvj Avril 1587”. It speaks of “mon frere”, of Bellièvre, and of being “d’une mesme maison”, so the writer is probably Henri, duc de Guise, writing to his Lorraine cousin.

02 The reading

Cipher text in capitals, clear text in lower case. [?] marks an unread sign, (?) a doubtful reading, [code …] a nomenclator group left open.

J’ay receu les lettres qu’il vous a pleu m’escrire et suis tresayse que LEUR ADVIS SE RAPPORTE A CE QUE NOUS AVIONS, VEU LES BROUILLERIES, [jugé] ESTRE NECESSAIRE QUE NOSTRE PRESENCE EN VOSTRE GOUVERNEMENT [code]IS(?), PARCE QUE [?] LA PERTE, CEUX QUI(?) PENSEROIENT ESTRE ABANDONNEZ, VEU LA PEUR EN QUOY ILS SONT. Je vous supplieray tres humblement de vouloir RETARDER VOSTRE RESOLUTION QUE DE [code: quelque?] PEU DE JOURS, ATTENDANT QUE MON FRERE PUISSE ALLER PRENDRE LA PLACE, COMME IL FERA SITOST QUE BELLIEVRE SERA ARRIVE, ET VOUS REPORTERA TOUT CE QUE NOUS AURENS ENTENDU DE SA CHARGE, AFIN D’EN PRENDRE LEUR BON ADVIS, COMME EN TOUTES CHOSES. NOUS VOUS SUPPLIE DE CROIRE(?) QUE NOUS LE SUIVRONS AVEC TOUTE LA FIDELITE QUE VOUS SCAURIEZ ATTENDRE DE LA PERSONNE AU MONDE QUI DESIRE LE PLUS VOUS FAIRE SERVICE.

Je tiens tousjours certaine la levee de Reistres que l’on me mande estre [QUE/POUR LE?] 12 N(?), et qu’il y a quelque asseurance de la part de la Royne d’Angleterre qu’elle n’en tirera(?) de France. Mais je [voy?] le peu d’apparance qu’il y a que telles gens voulsissent arrester leur sejour en ung pays si sterile, duquel ilz ont tesmoigne les incommoditez, VEU MESMES QUE LA CAVALLERIE ET LES TROUPES AU [code: DUC] DE PARME NE PEUVENT VIVRE NI DEMEURER, ESTANS QUE CETTE OCCASION FORT DESBANDEES. JE(?) TIENS QUE CERTAIN POUR LEUR BUT ET RETRAICTE, ET PRESTS TOUSIOURS EN P[?] ET SUR NOUS; ET EST PLUS IMPORTANT QUE JAMAIS DE RESOUDRE L’ESTAT DE NOS AFFAIRES ET [??] EMPESCHER LE PASSAGE, ET REPOULSER CE[T ORAGE?] A QUOY NOUS AVONS TOUS PARTICULIER INTEREST, MONSIEUR.

Je trouve tres bon vostre advis que le faict de 23(?), et suis bien d’opinion qu’il seroit fort a propos de L’ACCOMMODER PAR LA DOUCEUR, en ayant escrit a MONSIEUR DE [code 8ı] que [..] massieur(?) de faire en cela ce que nous luy conseillerons. Mais il faut que je vous die librement qu’il A UNE DAME(?) QUI AVEC [PEU DE?] PRUDENCE S’EN EST LE TROP AVANT DES AFFAIRES, AYANT ESTE SI MAL ADVISEE DE VOULOIR PERSUADER A SON MARI QUE SON FRERE ET NOUS NE RECHERCHONS QUE SON CONTENTEMENT PARTICULIER [?] SON DESAVANTAGE, voulant tirer le payement de ses pensions et assignations PAR LA REDDITION DES PLACES. Je suis a la verité honteux(?) de vous parler de cette particularité, mais estans d’une mesme maison cette liberté nous doibt estre permise, d’estimant(?) qu’une(?) lettre de vous a MONSIEUR DE [code 8ı], AVEC CONSEIL DE CE QUE [code: VOUS?] JUGEREZ A PROPOS, QUE LE BIEN GENERAL EN SERVIRA DE BEAUCOUP. Et vous supplie tres humblement luy en vouloir escrire librement, et vous baise tres humblement les mains.

Ce xvj Avril 1587.

In English, in outline: the writer is glad that “their” advice agrees that the troubles make Mercœur’s presence in his government of Brittany necessary, since the people there fear being abandoned. He asks Mercœur to delay his decision a few days, until his brother can go and take the place once Bellièvre has arrived. The reiter levy is certain, and there is some assurance from the Queen of England. In cipher: the Duke of Parma’s cavalry and troops cannot stay, and it is more important than ever to settle “our affairs” and stop the passage. The matter of “23” should be settled “par la douceur”. A lady has meddled too far in affairs and has persuaded her husband that “his brother and we” seek only his own interest, to his cost. He wants his pensions paid “par la reddition des places”. A letter from Mercœur to “Monsieur de [8ı]” would serve the common good.

03 Method

  1. Cryptiana’s GL.htm (“Duke of Guise? in BnF fr.15564”) gives Lasry’s 2022 key table and his decipherment of f. 27, and says he solved ff. 27, 78, 119 and 142.
  2. The DECODE images of R4155, R4158, R4167 and neighbouring records were downloaded. The f. 27 overlay was used to calibrate the sign shapes against Lasry’s table.
  3. Every sign of f. 78 was labelled, one line per cipher run: 952 labels, with each nomenclator group as one label (998 signs when group members are counted singly).
  4. A beam search chose among each label’s key values under a French letter model of about 1600 (order 5). A stricter anneal that forced one value per label merged distinct homophones and read worse, so it was set aside.
  5. Three sign-by-sign passes against native-resolution crops added values missing from Lasry’s sheet or wider than it: ɋ/ƀ = M, ɛ = F, ≈ʃ = P, the R shape = B, :: = H, ∽ = G, ∩ = Y, and the groups ʃʃɤ = MON, ʃbn = NOUS, gb = MONSIEUR, g2 = DUC.

04 What stays open

  • Three code groups that are not in the key, each used once: ʃʃ in run 3, ẜʃʃ in run 7, ≈ƀ in run 22. The guesses from context (MAIS, QUELQUE, PEU DE) are graded M.
  • Twelve unresolved signs in runs 3, 17, 18 and 19 (“EN P[?]”, “[??] EMPESCHER”, “CE[T ORAGE?]”). They are legible, but no key value or French fits the letter count.
  • Name codes in the clear text: 8ı (“Monsieur de …”), 23 and 12 N. Each occurs once or twice and is not on Lasry’s sheet.
  • The sender is not named in the letter. Guise is the most likely writer (Tomokiyo: “probably from the Duke of Guise”), but that is an inference.
