Erving to Madison, 1807: the holes in Madison’s decode

Erving’s report of Bonaparte’s offer of Hanover to Godoy is printed from Madison’s own decode with the key “not found”; collated with both received copies and Pinckney’s despatches, every hole in that decode closes.

01 The letter and the printed decode

George W. Erving, chargé at Madrid, wrote privately to Madison on 24 March 1807 (No. 21) “In the Cypher of the Legation”, the code Charles Pinckney had used in Spain. He reports a secret conversation between the Prince of the Peace (Godoy) and the Prussian chargé Henry: Bonaparte has offered the Prince the Electorate of Hanover for himself, and the Prince hopes by holding Hanover to make a separate peace with England and exchange it for Gibraltar. The Madison editors (PJM-SS 13:529–31) print the letter from Madison’s interlinear decode on the first received copy and state that the key has not been found (Weber, pp. 152–53). The decode has holes: “Haweder”, “Ha——er”, “Giblartar [sic]”, “see the he was more so”, “sp[eak]”, and at note 8 “to the […]” where Madison wrote “se al e”.

Three sources were used: the first received copy with Madison’s decode (NARA RG 59, Despatches from Spain vol. 10, microfilm M31 reel 12, frames 0283–0286); the “Duplicate” in the Madison Papers at the Library of Congress (mjm014714), coded throughout and not decoded; and Pinckney’s own despatches in the same code (M31 reel 7 frames 0346–0352, 22 February 1803, one copy decoded by Jacob Wagner on frame 0348; reel 8 frames 0083–0084, January 1804). Pinckney’s pages confirm the common values: 1651 the, 133 of, 1343 and, 244 to, 1578 that, 69 in (Wagner: “the In-tend-ant” = 1651.69.1586.103), 1515 late.

02 The holes closed

Founders printsGroups (both copies agree)ReadingEvidence
“to the […]” (n. 8; Madison “se al e”)69.1651.1183.1497.1298in the scale 69 is in: Madison himself so decodes it (“his retreat in Ha—er”, “went in-cog”), as does Wagner in 1803; his “to the” is a slip. 1497 is al (Madison, and in Gibraltar). Madison reads 1183 as se (se-em-ed), and se-al-e can only be scale: “might be of importance in the scale; again, to raise up an insuperable obstacle … & lastly to get the Prince within his grasp”, the second of Erving’s three objects. Either the key’s 1183 is sc or Erving took the neighbouring group.
“Haweder”, “Ha——er” (n. 5)724.529.934 (five times)Hanover 724 Ha and 934 er in all five; Madison left 529 blank or guessed “wed”, but decoded the same three groups “Hanover” himself at “exchange Hanover for Gibraltar”. 529 = nov.
“see the he was more so” (n. 6)1578.926.390.720.1219see that he was more so 1578 is that everywhere else, as the editors note; the code is right and Madison’s “the” is a slip.
“Giblartar [sic]”1619.1508.1497.1594 (twice)Gibraltar At the second occurrence Madison wrote “Gibraltar”; with 1497 = al the groups spell the name correctly. The [sic] belongs to Madison’s decode, not to Erving.
“sp[eak]” (n. 4)244.1212.133to sp[eak] of 1212 is sp (gra-sp, 408.1013.1212). The rest of the word is missing in both copies, so the omission is Erving’s. It stays as the editors print it.
“been lately doing” (n. 7, coded only in the Duplicate)…1164.1515.38.769.66…been late-ly do-ing 1515 late (Pinckney 1803), 38 ly, 66 ing: the Duplicate’s coding agrees with the clear text of the first copy.

So every hole in the printed text closes, and the only real loss is Erving’s own dropped -eak. Madison had the key: the holes are his slips, not missing entries. The contribution is small. The letter was already in print and legible; two of the corrections (that, speak) the editors had themselves suggested in their notes, and Hanover they had nearly established. What is new is in the scale, the proof that the “Haweder” and “Giblartar” forms are Madison’s and not Erving’s, and the demonstration that the Pinckney code is recoverable from the microfilm. The decipher-along above runs the recorded groups of the Hanover passage through the Pinckney key as it was later rebuilt for the August letter (targets/erving1807aug/key_pinckney.tsv); groups that key does not list are shown as unknown.

03 What remains open

  • In this letter, nothing. The missing -eak after 1212 sp is absent from both copies. Some line alignments of the opening paragraph in the group list (erving_groups.txt) are approximate; the groups at the holes were checked on both copies.
  • Erving to Madison, 10 August 1807 (M31 reel 12, frames 0362–0366), several pages in the same code with no decode on the NARA copy: done since, by an outside contribution, on its own page (Erving to Madison, August 1807), where the Pinckney key was rebuilt to 414 rows.
  • A full Pinckney key. Pinckney’s decoded despatches of 1802–04 (Founders 02-03-02-0170, 02-03-02-0313, 02-04-02-0419, 02-04-02-0611, 02-06-02-0346 with Brent’s three-page decipherment, 02-06-02-0560, 02-07-02-0022) are on M31 reels 7 and 8 and should give a few hundred more pairs.
