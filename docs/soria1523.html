At a glanceKey recovered · key A from the ciphertext alone (complete); key B from external plaintext (partial)

The letters
Eleven ciphered letters of Lope de Soria to Charles V, 1523–24: five despatches from Genoa, June to 13 August 1523, most in two or three copies, one to the chancellor Gattinara. DECODE lists them as not deciphered.
Where
Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid (DECODE R9488–R9498 and related); Bergenroth calendared only the letter of 13 August, from its clear parts.
The cipher
Two ciphers; Soria switched in late July. Key A has about twenty-five graphic signs and a few code words; key B is a homophonic alphabet with a large code of three-letter groups (sub que, xab Francia).
How it was read
Key B was rebuilt by aligning a Soria letter of December 1523 (R9844) with the court’s decipherment beside it. Key A had no crib: a simulated-annealing solver scored on Castilian state papers found the alphabet from about a thousand transcribed signs.
What it says
The league with Venice being closed, the French king’s expected descent on Italy, a proposal to open war on the Venetians, and Andrea Doria offering his galleys to the Emperor.
Still open
The June and July letters are deciphered nearly throughout; key-B passages are deciphered in main content. Code groups rip, pur, qed and mul in key A and a dozen in key B are open.

02The two keys

Key B (from late July 1523) is a homophonic alphabet of Latin-looking letters and signs, with two to four signs for the commoner letters, and a large code of three-letter lower-case groups: sub que, zog de, yed en, xab Francia, soc rey, zic principe, pic tab su Magestad, pup tab V. Magestad. The same cipher is in DECODE R9844 (BRAH 9/30 ff. 34–37), Soria to Gattinara, 30 December 1523. Its decipherment is on ff. 36r–36v, and aligning the two gave the first thirty groups and most of the letters. The Genoa letters then added the rest.

Key A (June–July 1523) has about twenty-five graphic signs (ʒ, crossed ʒ, ⊖, α, ε, φ, π, ꜑, the tt ligature, the figures 2 3 7 9) and a handful of lower-case code words. The writer kept the word divisions. Its values:

03How key A was recovered

The key-A block of the July letter (f. 444r–445v, 32 lines) was transcribed into ASCII names for the signs, with the code words set apart. The counts pointed to a near-monoalphabetic cipher. There were few signs; one sign stood alone ten times, which had to be y; and one pair, c before the tt ligature, never separated, so it was a single sign. A simulated-annealing search over sign-to-letter maps was scored with a character 4-gram model of Danvila’s edition of Castilian state papers of 1520–21. It returned la liga general, cinco mil infantes, exercito and con la esperança from random starts in a few seconds. The remaining values, the homophones for a, o and e, and the code words were then fixed by hand. They were checked against the two other copies of the June letter and the duplicate of the July letter, which use different homophones in the same places. The work took one session; the model was given the images and nothing else for this cipher.

04June 1523 (R9488–R9490, key A)

… escriven de Milan como tenian nueva que era arrivado en Venecia un secretario del duque de Milan, el qual avia traido el mandado del Infante para concluir la liga con los venecianos, y que por esto el duque de Milan avia prometido al dicho Infante diez y ocho mil ducados, y que con esto tenian por cierto que se concluiria la dicha liga, y por esto se partia con diligencia el protonotario Caracciolo para ir a Venecia. Y ansi mismo escriven de Milan como aviendose [comen]çado de amotinar la infanteria … les avian enviado una paga … an escrito el duque de Milan y el señor Prospero al duque [de Genova] que quiera esforçarse de ayudar con alguna suma de dineros, y el respondio que no puede dar dineros, pero que tomara quinientos infantes de los del exercito para que esten aqui y los pagara por un mes … de manera que nadie atiende sino a su propio interese.

The league of Venice with the Emperor was signed on 29 July and published on 15 August 1523. Here it is still being bought, with Milanese money on the Archduke Ferdinand’s mandate.

05July 1523 (R9492–R9493, key A)

[…] de Leon escriven a los veinte y tres del mes pasado como lo esperavan, que vendria muy presto el Rey de Francia para pasar en Italia; pero hasta agora no se juntava gente de guerra para que puedan pasar tan presto con exercito. Podria ser que pensasen de pasar con solo la gente d’armas, con esperança que calaran los [qed], y con la esperança de los venecianos, y pensando valerse de los cinco mil infantes que tiene en Provença … Y creo que los dichos [rip] usaran con [pur] los tratos que uso Otaviano Campofregoso quando se fizo la liga general y pasava este Rey de Francia en Italia, que una vez nos deçia que querria entrar en la liga y otras veçes dava palabras por dilatar, fasta que paso el dicho Rey, y a la ora el se declaro nuestro enemigo … fuera bueno començarles luego la guerra. Desto se seguirian dos buenos efetos: lo uno, que se desgravaria el estado de Milan del aposiento del exercito y estaria mejor alojado en el Bergamasco y Bresano; y lo otro, que luego seria tomado Bergamo, de donde podrian sacar dineros para alguna paga a la infanteria. Y no seria milagro que se tomase presto Bresa o Verona.

The [rip] can only be the Venetians or their government, still undecided a fortnight before they joined the league. Soria recalls how Ottaviano Fregoso, Doge of Genoa in 1515, strung the league along and then declared for France once Francis I had crossed the Alps. The closing and the post data of this letter are in key B. They report that Monsieur de Beaurain has arrived; Adrien de Croÿ, sieur de Beaurain, was the Emperor’s envoy to the Duke of Bourbon that summer.

0620 July 1523, to Gattinara (R9491, key B)

A long paragraph on Siena. The Sienese “comunidades” might be freed from their “tiranos” and governed as a republic. Soria argues that things voted by many are hard to carry through where opinions are divided, and that the Emperor would be better served by one man governing “de mano de su Magestad”. Y al cabo creo que avra de venir a esto, porque los seneses, de la condicion que son, que cada qual querra ser señor, y los otros no piensan sino solo en su propio interese. The first seven lines keep several open groups. A short passage names Beaurain again, at Turin.

0726 July 1523 (R9494–R9496, key B)

Beaurain carries this letter. Three days earlier a Piedmontese courier left by land for the Abbot of Nájera. Soria asks that the Emperor provide twenty-eight thousand escudos to a doctor at Constance to pay certain Swiss who have agreed to descend on France, and twelve thousand to a treasurer. The names of the doctor and the treasurer are not deciphered.

0813 August 1523 (R9497–R9498, key B)

Andrea Doria y el principe de Salerno estan muy descontentos del rey, porque les avia quitado la enpresa de esta ciudad y la avia dado al bastardo de Saboya; y a me [hablado] uno que se dize Jeronimo Doria, que es uno de los principales de esta ciudad, de parte del dicho Andrea Doria, y dizen que si V. Mag. [quiere] aceptar en su servicio al dicho Andrea Doria con sus quatro galeras, que el lo servira de muy buena voluntad …

Andrea Doria and the Prince of Salerno are discontented with the King of France. He had taken the enterprise of Genoa from them and given it to the Bastard of Savoy. Jeronimo Doria, one of the leading men of the city, spoke to Soria on Andrea Doria’s behalf: if the Emperor would take him into his service with his four galleys, he would serve “de muy buena voluntad”; the same was offered for the Prince of Salerno. The passage was collated with the duplicate R9498, which spells in code some words R9497 spells in letters (pep un, tun parte, xap le) and so fixed quitado, uno and quatro. [hablado] (xuf yic) is graded I and [quiere] (sob e; seb sob∞ is qualquiera in R9491) M. A second passage: Soria has pressed Antoniotto Adorno to make the republic pay what it owes under the capitulation [made in the time of Pope Leo]. Adorno has asked Martin Centurion to take it up with the Emperor. Andrea Doria came over to Charles V only in 1528, so this is an early approach.

09What remains uncertain

  • Key A code words not fixed: rip (3x, the Venetians, graded C), pur (3x, the Emperor’s side, M), qed (2x, the Swiss, M), mul, dus, cop, and the opener TZ.
  • Key B groups still open or guessed: yac, fob, taf, tu, pel, pug, zib, sib, xif, sab, 7 pes, per; guessed: paf tiempo, tin papa, sud respuesta, xa hasta (M).
  • Decipherments of R9491 lines 1–7 and the personal names in R9494 are graded M. The written-out reading of R9491 builds coherent sentences but reads at 88.1% of its words (measured 24 October 2026), under the 95% a complete reading needs. The rest of the key-B passages are C, and the June and July key-A texts H/C.
  • Not collated sign by sign: R9490 (spot checks only), R9493 (checked at the doubtful words), R9496, and R9498 beyond the Doria passage.

## English

They write from Milan that news had come that a secretary of the Duke of Milan had arrived in Venice with the
Infante's mandate to conclude the league with the Venetians; for this the Duke of Milan had promised the Infante
18,000 ducats, and so they took the league as certain, and the protonotary Caracciolo was leaving in haste for
Venice. They also write that the infantry having begun to mutiny, a pay had been sent them, with which they hoped
to quiet it for now; and to finish paying what is owed, the Duke of Milan and Signor Prospero [Colonna] have
written to the Doge [of Genoa] to make an effort to help with some sum of money. He answered that he cannot give
money, but will take five hundred infantry of the army to be here and pay them for a month; they think this
relieves the army of cost, guards this city better, and ends the habit of those in charge of [..] asking him for
money and his of giving it: so that no one looks to anything but his own interest.

Context: the Venetian league with the Emperor was concluded on 29 July 1523 and published 15 August (CSP Spain
vol. 2 no. 587); Marino Caracciolo was the imperial protonotary sent to Venice. 'El Infante' = Archduke Ferdinand.

## English summary

Soria reports that someone (the [yac], maybe the Cardinal or a party in Siena) wants Siena to be governed as a
republic. He argues that decisions taken by vote of many are hard to carry out where opinions are divided, and that
it would serve the Emperor better to have one man governing Siena "de mano de su Magestad". That man would keep the
Emperor content out of self-interest, and a single man can be ordered in ways a republic cannot. He grants that
freeing the Sienese communes from their tyrants looks well, but says the city will end up under one ruler anyway,
because every Sienese wants to be master and thinks only of his own interest. He asks Gattinara to think about
securing the government of the city for the Emperor.

From Lyon they write on the twenty-third of last month that they expected that the King of France would come here very soon to cross into Italy; but so far men of war were not being gathered so that they could cross so soon with an army; it might be that they thought of crossing with the men-at-arms alone, in the hope that the [qed] would come down, and in the hope of the Venetians, and having thought to make use of the five thousand infantry he has in Provence to put in the fleet at sea. And I have word from Brescia that from here [an] [fec] [?that] [?the] [rip] are carrying two hundred bales of corselets [Qo] [Locia], the land of the Grisons, and I think they are to arm the [qed]; and I believe that the said [rip] will use with [pur] the dealings that Ottaviano Campofregoso used when the general league was made and this King of France was crossing into Italy, who one time told us he wanted to enter the league, and at other times gave words to delay until the said King had crossed, and then declared himself our enemy. And so I think the [rip] will do: they will delay the agreement with [pur] until they see what the King of France will do; and if they see that he has the means to come or to send an army into Italy, they will be his friends and will make no agreement with [pur].
