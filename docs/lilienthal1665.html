Moscow → Stockholm · homophonic letter code in six number series · 1665–1667 · key rebuilt from contemporary glosses

Johan von Lilienthal in Moscow, 1665–1667: the key of his code

Go to the full text ↓

The Swedish resident’s German despatches from Moscow were deciphered at the time between the lines; the homophonic key, rebuilt from those glosses and tested once on a page not used to build it, is now a reusable table.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 11 November 2026

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: complete · On the map

With Brice Dudout (transcription, verification, solution) · Also credited · Contemporary key or decipherment (earlier solution)

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · complete

The letters
Despatches of Johan von Lilienthal, Swedish resident in Moscow, to Charles XI (the Regency), in German, 1665–1667, with the sensitive passages in number code.
Where
Riksarkivet, Stockholm, Diplomatica Muscovitica, SE/RA/2113/2113.1/1/36/82 (137 images, series R0000814). Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A German letter substitution with homophones, with numbers also for ch, sch, st, ss, ll, ff and tz: groups of two or three digits in six series, each running through the alphabet. Not a nomenclator.
How the key was found
158 values learned from the glossed runs of images 42 and 47, frozen by SHA-256, then measured once on image 45: 0.887 of the letters against 0.047 for the best of 200 shuffled keys.
What it says
On the measured lines: someone “bestehet feste auff seiner instruction” (holds firmly to his instruction), and great boyars, Dolgoruka, Ilia Danielewitz and Almasi among them, discussed it together after yesterday’s conference.
Credit
Key, transcription and test by Brice Dudout (pull request #384). The decipherment itself is the clerk’s of the 1660s. The lead came from the project’s Riksarkivet sweep.
Still open
Values added after the test are untested; codes 107–120 and 303–320 never occur in the runs read. Every run seen carries its gloss.

01 The despatches

The volume holds the despatches of the Swedish resident in Moscow to the Regency that governed for the young Charles XI. They are in German, and the passages to be kept secret are written in dotted numbers. The German decipherment of the time is written between the lines: the project’s leads review of 6 November 2026 found it over every run it zoomed and noted that “only the key is of further use”. This target supplies that key.

Brice Dudout used three images. Images 42 (both pages) and 47 (left page) were used to learn the key. On image 45 the left page, lines 1–10, was held out: its first 160 groups were measured once. Code runs fill nearly every image from 28 to 49; beyond these three images they were not transcribed, since the gloss over them is already their decipherment.

02 The key

There are six code series: 23–59, 63–100, 103–150, 153–190, 293–349 and 353–390; numbers ending in 1 or 2 do not occur. Each series runs through the alphabet (23 a, 24 b, 25 c, 26 ch … 59 y). Series 293–349 repeats the order of 23–59 and series 353–390 that of 63–100. Besides single letters there are numbers for ch, sch, st, ss, ll, ff and tz, and u, v and w share their numbers. Codes 107–120 and 303–320 were never seen: they may hold words or names, or simply be unused.

The first notes on this lead took the code for a nomenclator of about 390 values. That came from low-resolution views; the amended protocol still called it one, and the contributor corrected it once the key had been rebuilt.

The frozen key has 158 codes, learned without a conflict from 1116 aligned groups. After the measurement, the order of the table and the sense of image 45 gave 89 = st (feste), 27 = ff (auff), 64 = c (instruction), 54 = tz (Danielewitz) and 348 = z (zusammen); structure alone suggests 379 st, 344 tz, 75/365 k, 378 sch and 68/358 ff. These rows are marked “post” in key.tsv and were not tested. The key table is released under CC BY 4.0.

03 The held-out test

The first protocol treated the code as a nomenclator and held out image 42. It was amended before any measurement: image 42, already seen in part, moved to training, and image 45, not yet looked at closely, became the held-out page. The key was then learned from images 42 and 47 only and frozen. On image 45 the first 160 groups were transcribed and frozen first; only then was the gloss above them read and frozen. One measurement compared the decode with the gloss letter by letter (a local alignment, after normalising spelling such as u/v/w and umlauts):

  • 0.887 of the decoded characters agree with the gloss;
  • the best of 200 keys made by shuffling the same values gives 0.047 (mean 0.024), a margin of 0.840;
  • the thresholds fixed in advance were 0.85 and a margin of 0.30, so the key passes.

The 5 groups outside the frozen key counted as wrong. One bias is declared: the gloss is on the same image and was visible while the numbers were transcribed. On integration the measurement script was run again on the frozen files and gave the same figures, the SHA-256 hashes check out, and the decode of the transcription with key.tsv matches the decryption file line for line.

04 The measured lines

Lines 1–10 of image 45 hold 185 groups. Of the 160 measured, 155 are in the frozen key and 5 are the post rows; none is cut. The decode with the full key (u/v/w written as the sense needs):

[s]ondern bestehet feste auff seiner instruction und gehanen propositiones s haten auch etliche von den grossen boiaren als s
dolgoruka ilia danielewitz almasi | gestern nac[h] der conference zusammen discurrirend e | verlauten lassen mit e...

The gloss of the time reads:

von wissen sondern bestehet hatte auf seiner instruction und geheimen propositiones gethan und welche von den grossen boiaren als
dolgoruka elia danielewitz almasi gestern nach der conference zusammen discurrirende verlauten lassen

The gloss is not always word for word. Where the cipher has “feste auff” the gloss writes “hatte auf”, and for “s haten auch etliche von den grossen boiaren” it writes “gethan und welche von den grossen boiaren”: it summarises. “Gehanen” for “geheimen” may be the cipher clerk’s slip. In English: “… but [he] holds firmly to his instruction and [secret] propositions. Also some of the great boyars, such as Dolgoruka, Ilia Danielewitz, Almasi, … yesterday after the conference, discussing it together, let it be known with …”. The contributor takes Dolgoruka for a Prince Dolgorukov, Ilia Danielewitz probably for Ilya Danilovich Miloslavsky and Almasi probably for Almaz Ivanov, head of the Ambassadorial Office; these identifications were not checked against the clear text of the letter.

05 What remains open

  • The post rows of the key are untested: each rests on one or two words of image 45 or on the order of the table, and the one held-out page is spent.
  • Codes 107–120 and 303–320 do not occur in the runs read.
  • A test with the gloss out of view cannot be made on the pages seen: every run carries its gloss on the same leaf. The tops and bottoms of pages 28–49 other than 38, 42, 45 and 47 were not checked at full resolution, so a short unglossed run there is not excluded.
  • One reader (an agent) on 3000 px images, with no human palaeographic check. The dates of the letters on images 42, 45 and 47 are in their clear text, which was not transcribed.

The code of Adolf Ebers, the Swedish commissary in Moscow in 1662–1666, is a different cipher, with its own page: Ebers in Moscow, 1662–1666.

translation.md

# lilienthal1665 - English rendering of the measured portion (image 45, left page, lines 1-10)

From our decode of the cipher (the gloss differs in places, see `decryption.txt`). The clear German written between the runs
and the line before L01 are not transcribed, so the passage starts and ends in mid-sentence.

> ... but [he] holds firmly to his instruction and [secret] propositions. Also some of the great boyars, such as Dolgoruka,
> Ilya Danilovich, Almaz, [...] yesterday after the conference, discussing it together, let it be known with ...

Notes: "Dolgoruka" is a Prince Dolgorukov; "Ilia Danielewitz" is probably Ilya Danilovich Miloslavsky; "Almasi" is probably
Almaz Ivanov, head of the Ambassadorial Office (Posolsky prikaz). These identifications are ours and were not checked against
the clear text of the letter.
