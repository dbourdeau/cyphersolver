The Milanese embassy at Buda, 1489–1490

The documents
Despatches of Maffeo da Treviglio, Ludovico Sforza’s man at Buda from summer 1489. Two had no decipherment: Buda, 22 November 1489 (four pages) and Buda, 12 January 1490.
Where
Milan state archive, Carteggio Sforzesco, Ungheria 642 and 650; Vestigia 2831 (1489) and DECODE R1098 (“Partially decrypted”, 1490).
The cipher
A monoalphabetic sign cipher with nomenclator signs, reconstructed by Judit W. Somogyi (2016), who printed the key only as two-digit numbers with no sign shapes.
How it was read
Her numbers follow first appearance in the letter of 2 April 1490, which survives with its clear copy (Vestigia 267/268). Matching the two restores the signs; about twenty more came from the decipherment itself.
What it says
In January 1490 the king has left for Vienna and the ambassadors follow on the 15th. The November 1489 letter covers the peace with the Emperor, the Diet, the Corvinus marriage and a warning that intercepted cipher letters have been deciphered.
Still open
Four signs are unvalued; page 3 of the 1489 letter is read only in patches (about a third of its words); lines 5, 6 and 9 of the 1490 letter and three nomenclator shapes are unconfirmed.

The key reconstruction is Judit W. Somogyi’s (Verbum 17, 2016). The 1489 letter is the oldest cipher text this project has deciphered; the previous oldest was of 1497.

01 The documents

Five letters of Maffeo survive with both a ciphered original and a contemporary clear copy; Somogyi used them to build her key. Two others have no decipherment beside them; those are the ones deciphered here. One of them, R1098, turned out to have its contemporary extract printed in 1878.

02 Turning Somogyi's numbers back into signs

Somogyi's appendix prints the key as a table of numbers: a = 11, 29, 39; m = 12; b = 13; and so on through five nulls, three word signs (et, in, che), five doubled-letter signs, thirty-two syllable signs and six nomenclator entries (“il vescovo varadino”, “S. V.”, “ser.mo Re de Hungaria”, “madonna Bianca”, “zo. Corvino”, “la sua Maestà”). She also prints the first two lines of the 1489 letter twice, once as numbers and once deciphered. She prints no image of a sign, and her numbers are her own labels for the fifteenth-century signs.

The order of her numbers gives the link. Her numbers 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 stand for a, m, b, sa, to, re, which is “ambasatore”, the first word of the letter of 2 April 1490. 17, 18, 19 are d, e, l (“del”), 20 to 23 t, u, r, co (“turco”). The numbering simply follows that letter word by word, so the ciphered original supplies the shapes in the same order. From there the clear copy carries the alignment to the end of the letter and fixes about forty signs.

The rest came from the two letters without a decipherment, each fixed by a word that could be nothing else: the long 7-shaped stroke is f (“Fedrico”, “fosse”, “satisfare”); an n with a crossed 4 is ss (“essere”, “potesse”); x with a tick is do (“quando”, “havendo”); a with a colon is gli (“meglio”); a looped b joined to o is st (“stato”, “questo”); d with a colon is fi (“figliolo”, “iustificare”); an n with a long crossbar is tt (“littere”); and a capital with a mark stands for the correspondent’s title (the original key gives a# = “Vostra Dominatio”; it had been taken for “Ex.tia Vostra”). Numerals are written as figures, not enciphered: “ali 8 del presente”, “ali 15”, “in 20 o 25 di”. Latin words sometimes stand in the clear inside the cipher (“etiam apud”, “Sed hoc non fallet divinam sapientiam”).

The original key. On 23 October 2026 the key itself was found among the Sforza chancery’s keys: Sforzesco cart. 1597 no. 111, DECODE R5411, undated and unattributed there, headed “Cum Mapheo Triviliensi”. It has the alphabet (torn at the upper right, from T to &), doubles, nulls, syllables and a nomenclator of 22 entries (transcribed in key_R5411.md). It confirms Somogyi’s reconstruction where both exist and names the word signs: M+ is Regina (read “la sua Maestà” before), a# Vostra Dominatio, o+ Imperatore, M̄ Zuan Corvino, Q. Duca de Milano, Ra madonna Bianca, + Vescovo Varadino; x̄ is ma and ƀ fa. Words of the September reading that the signs do not carry were withdrawn. A second sheet headed “Cum Mafeo Triviliensi” (R5407) is a different alphabet.

03 Buda, 12 January 1490: the king rides to Vienna

The letter's clear first page is about church appointments; the cipher begins where the news turns delicate.

Questo Ser.mo Re partite de qua ali 8 del presente per andare ad Vienna, non ta[nto] per che la speranza de la dieta lo tiri, quanto per proprio desiderio de andarli; et che sia el vero, questi prelati mi hano dicto che in questo camino divertira ad tutti li lochi ove ha facto [...] edificare, che sono molti, et non si condura ad Vienna piu [...] presto che in 20 o 25 di. Lo legato et li altri oratori hano, et io con loro havemo, deliberato partire ali 15 de questo, et siamo rimasti dreto, perche Sua Maiesta mi fece dire che andassimo recta via ad Vienna, perche lei voleva andare spassando. Non sono piu [...].

“This Most Serene King left here on the 8th of this month to go to Vienna, not so much because the hope of the Diet draws him as from his own wish to go there; and in proof, these prelates have told me that on this journey he will turn aside to all the places where he has had building done, which are many, and will not reach Vienna sooner than in 20 or 25 days. The legate and the other ambassadors have, and I with them have, decided to leave on the 15th of this month, and we have stayed behind because His Majesty had me told that we should go straight to Vienna, since he wished to travel at leisure.”

Lines 5–10 were re-read on 23 October 2026 from native crops of the DECODE image with the original key, and the result was set against the contemporary decipherment printed by Nagy and Nyáry in 1878 (MDE IV no. 91, pp. 161–162, “Extractus Ziffri Maphei Triviliensis. 12. Januarii 1490”): the re-read signs agree with it at each place (“tutti li lochi ove ha facto edificare”, “siamo”, “andassimo”, “spassando”); “mi” and “perche” in the last lines are taken from the print. The September reading’s guess “Tulln” was wrong. The letter also confirms the decipherment from inside: its own second page says in clear words that “lo legato et li altri oratori et io siamo insieme deliberato partire alli 15 de questo … andare per la via ad Vienna”. The cipher says the same thing in the same order, and the word “Vienna” is spelled with the same signs in lines 2, 6 and 10.

04 Buda, 22 November 1489: peace, the Diet, and two marriages

Four pages. Somogyi printed the first two lines as a specimen; no contemporary decipherment, copy or edition of the rest was found. Matthias Corvinus had six months to live, his war with Frederick III was in suspense, and two marriages were in play for his illegitimate son John Corvinus: Bianca Maria Sforza, Ludovico's niece, and a daughter of don Federico of Aragon, Naples's candidate. Queen Beatrice of Aragon opposed the succession of her stepson.

Every cipher line of page 1, page 2 lines 1–17 and page 4 was re-read sign by sign from per-row crops of the Vestigia plates and decoded with the original key (23 October 2026); page 2 has 31 lines, and its first line had never been transcribed. The unread groups were then put through the shared Italian model over every alternative symbol value, and five more words were adopted where every letter is carried. Spelling is as decoded; […] marks unread groups, [?word] a word only partly carried by the signs. The opening of page 1:

La Ex.tia vra intende ad quali termine la pratica de pace, et per consequente questa dieta. Questo Ser.mo Re de Hungaria e tenero del honore suo, como ho scripto altre volte; non e dubio che […] desidera la pace, ma non tanto pero ch'el voglia recevere una […] […] de la […] sua. Non […] ne […] e[x]presamente de andare ad la dieta, ben che ancora in Imperatore non veda [?fondamente] de pace, perche non se gli potesse imputare che per lui fosse rotta la pratica. Gli pare anche duro andarli per non fare niente et riceverne scorno; et pero desiderana havere littere da Imperatore che lo […] andare affirmando li essere ben disposto a la pace, et con questo si [?contenta] andarli, anchora che fosse certo che niuna conclusione ne ne dovesse riuscire, perche gli pare […] potersi sempre ben iustificare et satisfare al honore suo.

“Your Excellency understands at what point the peace negotiation stands, and consequently this Diet. This Most Serene King of Hungary is jealous of his honour, as I have written at other times; there is no doubt that […] desires peace, but not so much that he will accept a […] of his […]. … It also seems hard to him to go there to obtain nothing and receive scorn; and so he wants letters from the Emperor … affirming that he is well disposed to peace, and with this he is [?content] to go, even if he were sure that no conclusion would come of it.”

The page goes on to the new Neapolitan ambassador, who has again proposed the daughter of don Federico, and to the Queen, who says freely that the Milan match was made in spite of her. Page 2 opens with the rumour that Zuan Corvino would marry don Federico’s daughter:

et respondendoli colui parerli imposibile, essendo cocto quello de Milano, solenemente replico essere vero, dicendo ch'el parentato de Milano era gia […]. Sono ancora stati de cortesani li quali, havendo inteso de la deliberatione che era facta, che [?messere] Francisco andisse ad Napoli, gli hano dicto: voi andarete ad disfare quello che haveti cocto.

“…and when that man answered that it seemed impossible to him, the Milan match being cooked, he solemnly replied that it was true, saying that the Milan marriage was already […]. There have also been courtiers who, having heard of the decision that had been made, that [?messer] Francesco should go to Naples, told him: ‘You will go and undo what you have cooked.’” Maffeo warns that the Queen will try everything to turn the King from his purpose, “et in questo non cedera a la isacia[b]ile ira de Iunone”. The two cipher lines of page 4 end: “Sed hoc non fallet divinam sapientiam de la Vostra Dominatio.”

Page 2 lines 18–31 and page 3: decoded, not read. They were re-read sign by sign from the plates with the original key (b25, 23 October 2026), re-cropped at full resolution for page 3 and segmented with the shared Italian model; the partial text (v2831_p2p3_r2l_reading.md) touches on intercepted cipher letters, Francesco’s mission to Naples, the Turk and the Bishop of Várad. An independent second reader then transcribed eight lines blind from the plates: 93.3% of the signs agree, but 9 of 92 words of the reading (9.8%) are not carried by the signs after each disputed sign was re-checked. At that rate across the unsampled lines these pages fall below the bar, so they are shown for checking and not counted. The next step is to bracket those words and check every word of the two pages against the signs the same way.

05 The clear postscript: an ambassador's expenses

Nothing on this page is secret, and it was never enciphered. It is also the only part of the letter that is about Maffeo himself.

Credo che la Ex.tia V.ra havera facto dar quelli ducento ad m. Fran.co; che, se cosi e, la ringratio infinite volte de la clementia et bonta sua infinita. Et perche quelli denari non mi portavano fin al fine de decembre, et partendosi lui non saperia da chi imprum[ut]are uno quatrino se non dal Thexaurero Regio, che seria con graveza de la persona vostra propria … Se de questa spesa non gli ne faria honor, sonno contento che non me ne proveda; io gli posso affirmar etiam cum iuramento che, doppoi che sonno qua, ultra quello ho havuto et tolto in nome del Ill.mo S. V.ro, ho speso di mei piu de centocinquanta ducati tra mi, veste et cavalli … che la provisione che me e ordinata non mi basta, dovendo tener questo loco secundo la dignita sua. Et perche in questa novita facta de Cancelleri forse poteria esser dome[n]ticato, La supplico ordini ad chi li par che provedi sia compreso in el numero de li altri Can[cellieri] et in lassignatione facta ad la Cancellaria. A V.ra Ex. de continuo me recomando. Bude xxij Novembris 1489. E[iusdem] Ill.me D[ominationis] V[ostre] servus, Magister Trivilien[sis].

“Since I have been here, beyond what I have had in Your Lordship's name, I have spent more than a hundred and fifty ducats of my own on myself, clothes and horses … the allowance assigned me is not enough to keep this post according to its dignity. And because in this recent reshuffle of the chancery clerks I might be forgotten, I beg you to order whoever you think fit to see that I am counted among the other chancellors.” The phrase “et in lassignatione facta ad la Cancellaria” is the explicit by which the archive catalogues the letter.

06 Method

The images are the Vestigia plates (3240×4320, no login needed: the record page at vestigia.hu/documents/<id> carries the whole record as JSON, and the originals are served from /media/<file id>/<file name>) and, for R1098, the DECODE scans of the same negatives.

The working loop was: connected-component segmentation of each page into glyph boxes, line assignment by the peaks of the horizontal ink projection, merging of the two-stroke signs and of superscripts with the letter below them; then numbered strips, one line at a time, transcribed into values by hand against a sheet of sign exemplars cut from the letters themselves; then the values segmented into Italian, and any place where the Italian failed sent back to the image. The 2 April pair was aligned by dynamic programming against its clear copy to seed the key. Several rounds were needed: the first pass on the 1489 letter produced letter strings, the second, working from contrast-stretched crops of whole lines rather than from boxes, produced sentences.

Two practical notes for anyone continuing. The automatic line segmentation misassigns glyphs in the lower half of pages 2 and 3 of the 1489 letter, where the lines converge; transcribe those from the plate with per-row crops. And the stacked line crops drift from line 18 onward on page 3, which cost one pass its accuracy.

07 What remains uncertain

  • Letter groups of the 1489 letter that decode letter for letter but give no word, between secure words: page 1 lines 3, 4, 8, 11, 12, 17, 19–22, 25–29; page 2 lines 15, 16, 19–21, 25, 28–31; page 3 lines 2, 3, 5–8, 15, 18, 21, 22, 27, 29, 31; page 4 line 2. Four passes and a model segmentation over every alternative symbol value left them; they need the values in the torn columns of R5411.
  • Signs in the torn columns of R5411 (x, y, z, &, part of t), the r-ring on page 3 line 15, and the o-form, read t in most words but b by the key.
  • Page 3 line 15: one or two signs under an ink blot; page 1 lines 21–29: the last words of each line under a stain at the right edge (re-checked at three times on 23 October 2026; the Vestigia plate is the only image).
  • Page 2 lines 18–31 and page 3: decoded, not read (section 04).
  • The letter of 12 January 1490: the two signs after “ove ha facto” (the key’s o+ is Imperatore) have no word in the printed decipherment and stay open, and the cipher’s last words, “non sono piu lon[…]”, are not in the printed extract.
