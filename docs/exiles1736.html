The letter
An unsigned letter in Polish with long Latin stretches, dated 4 February (1736), from one of the Polish exiles who had followed King Stanisław Leszczyński, answering the news that France cannot keep him on the throne with two conditions for his abdication.
Where
Archiwum Główne Akt Dawnych (AGAD), Warsaw, Zbiór Anny z Potockich Ksawerowej Branickiej (zesp. 338), 1/338/0/1/1401; catalogue 1579. The card guesses it is to Branicka. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
One number for each letter (30 signs), a dash between words, and a few code groups for names. A different cipher from the three French letters of the same catalogue entry, which have their own page (branicki1757).
How it was deciphered
Prabal Gupta found the key from this letter alone with a count-matching algorithm on Polish next-letter counts (98.2% of the letter tokens), then settled the rare signs by reading the numbers against the Polish. Checked here: the key is one-to-one, and the decrypted words are 31% in a word list against at most 6% for any of 300 shuffled keys.

01 The letter

In 1733 France put Stanisław Leszczyński back on the Polish throne against Russia and Saxony; by the winter of 1735–36 she had agreed preliminaries with Vienna and could no longer keep him there, and he abdicated in January 1736. This letter, from one of his Polish followers, is the exiles’ answer to that news, written in the manner of the day in Polish with Latin tags (ob insuperabiles rationes, pretio pacis universalis). It sits in the Branicki family archive without writer, addressee or year: the date line reads “D. 4. Feb:”, and the year 1736 is the contributor’s inference from the contents (the preliminaries of Vienna, and a courier sent on 11 January). The catalogue’s guess that it is to Branicka looks wrong, because the text addresses a man and asks what the Turks plan. The history in this paragraph is from memory and was not checked here.

02 The key and its test

Each letter is one number, and words are separated by dashes: 3 is a, 1 is e, 45 is n, 101 is t, 100 is r, 79 is y, 120 is z, with Polish ł, ą, ę and the Latin æ and œ as signs of their own. A few undotted groups stand for names: 125 for Stanisław (twelve uses, every one fitting), 455 for Augustus III, 136 and 142 for Warsaw, where the clear text beside them names Warsaw. The contributor reports that his count-matching algorithm reached the key from this letter alone and that a solver on a Polish letter model agrees on 93.1% of the tokens; he also notes that his Polish model is of modern text, that the letter has long Latin stretches, that no held-out half was run, and that he changed the model once after a first look.

Check made here, with the scans out of reach: the 30-sign key is one-to-one, and the 491 dotted words of the cipher, decrypted with it, are 31.2% in a word list made of the letter’s own clear words and about 90 common Polish and Latin function words, against 5.7% at most for any of 300 shuffled keys. That shows the key makes words of this letter; it is not an independent measure of correctness, since the clear words share the vocabulary. A Polish language model could not be built from this environment, so the project’s n-gram validation is not applicable.

03 The reading

530 words of the letter are in cipher; 12 are holes (nine unread code groups, two uses of the guessed name Monti, and one supplied letter), so 518 are deciphered, 97.7%. About twenty more code groups are read as names from context and are not marked. In English: “Orlik is sent after him in person, to inform the said minister there in everything of what he heard, saw and understood here.” The exiles’ answer sets two conditions: “that Stanisław first be recognised by all the powers of Europe as King of Poland, duly and lawfully elected … and that he issue letters for a diet, convoke all the estates of the Republic, and there use the royal rights … and abdicate only according to our laws”. The Polish, Latin and English texts are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • No transcription was compared with a scan by the project. The contributor’s two passes agree on 99.1% of 2,844 tokens, and he settled each of the 23 differences on the scan.
  • Nine code groups are unread (145, 156, 175, 195, 289, 505, 613, 715, 5455) and 675, read as the French envoy Monti, is a guess: no text names him minister to the confederated estates in 1736.
  • The signature is a flourish: the writer and the addressee are unknown, and the year is inferred.
  • The clear Polish and Latin parts are the contributor’s working reading, not an edition, and no historian has checked the reading.

An unsigned letter among the Polish exiles: France has agreed preliminaries with Vienna and an armistice and says it cannot keep King Stanisław on the throne; the courier Orlik brings it as good news and the exiles receive it in despair; they answer with two conditions, that Europe first recognise Stanisław as lawful king for life and that he call a Diet and abdicate only there; the estates refuse the haste; two lords leave for Warsaw; the writer asks what the Turks and the Khan plan.

# AGAD 1/338/0/1/1401: reading

**Bold** text is cipher, and plain text is clear in the letter. The language is Polish with long Latin phrases, in the clear and in the cipher. Word division and punctuation are mine. The long sentences are the letter's own.

This letter has its own cipher: one number for each letter, with a dash between words, and a small code of number groups for names. Its key is `key.tsv`. It is not the key of letters 446 and 1398 to 1400.

`[175]` is a code group that stays unread, and `[?word]` is a doubtful reading. A letter in square brackets is supplied. `../branicki1757/numbers.md` (the groups of letter 1401 are listed there) gives the code groups and the evidence for each.

Monseigneur.

Posłaniec ten, iako mnie upewnił, od WMCPana posłany, nie wiem quo casu aż do tego czasu przytrzymany. Jam dla choroby moiey nie mogł dać na to należytey attencyi, a co większa, tenże sam dopiero się przed tygodniem opowiedział przez JMCią Panią Pacową.

Instantanee tedy, **dawszy mu na drogę w czerwonych złotych z woli Pańskiey,** expedyuię go, krociusienko donosząc **niepomyślną a bardziey nieszczęśliwą sytuacyą naszą.**

Albowiem iuż to plusquam certum, że **protectrix potentia nostra,** nie wiem quo fato, bo nieprzymuszona armis neq potentia nieprzyiaciół swoich, **iuż się z tym klarygowała, że Stanisława ob insuperabiles rationes, a bardziey dla uporu rossyiskiey y niemieckiey potencyi, na tronie polskim** ullatenus **utrzymać nie mogąc,** pretio pacis universalis, a bardziey dla [?uspokoienia] prędszego ginącey Polski naszey, condescendit naypierwey ad praeliminaria puncta Tractatus z Dworem Wiedeńskim, iuż podobno optime nota WMCPanu, y razem zaraz umowione Armistitium inter Partes belligerantes dla spokoynieyszego futuri tractatus generalis dotarcia publikowała.

A gdy się to wszystko stało **ultra scitum kolligackich potencyi tak [175] iako też [195],** przysłany do nas z tą nowiną, iakoby to iuż naypomyślnieyszą, umyślny kuryer, **Pan Orlik, który śmiał między nami te proferre słowa:** „Jam tu przysłany [?à Christianissimo Rege] Pana mego, w tey nadziei, **że mnie Stanisław, któremu spokoynieysze, przy konserwacyi zupełnego honoru, prokurowane życie,** y cała Konfederacya, która cum plena securitate honorum et bonorum atque Personarum do Państw y dobr swoich powrocić secure może, z wesołą przyimą tu miną”. **Aż ia tandem ze wszystkich stron smutno et inprospere przyięty** &c.

Bo iakoż tak było, kiedyśmy **tę odebrali wiadomość nigdy niespodziewaną** po tak wielu assekuracyach y ubespieczeniach ratunku wolności naszey, attoniti wszyscyśmy zostali, y nie było tego, któryby **cum execratione** non incusaret takiego **zawodu y opuszczenia całey Oyczyzny naszey,** zguby honoru wolnego Narodu, a co większa sakryfikuiących się na poczciwych Oyczyzny Synow, dla windykowania wolności swoiey przez ledwie kiedy praktykowane **do potencyi francuskiey przywiązanie się.**

Gdy tedy tu **Orlik cale ingratissime był przyięty od [613],** y całe z tą rezolucyą expedyowany, że ex hoc passu, a nay bardziey z oddalenia niespodziewanego **od tronu polskiego Pana naszego a saeculis ad salutem wolności polskiey, absolute kontente bydź nie mogą;** nie daią inakszey rezolucyi, tylko że na Punkta Preliminarne y na Abdykacyą Pana, ieżeli go iuż całe Potentia Francuska fato nostro odstąpić miała, aliis conditionibus pozwolić nigdy nie mogą tylko his.

1mo. **Żeby Stanisław wprzody ab omnibus potentiis Europae był przyznany za krola polskiego rite et legitime obranego, y żeby hoc titulo gaudeat przez całe życie swoie, day Boże nigdy nieskończone.**

2do. Aby **wydał uniwersały na seym, konwokował omnes status Rzpltey** y na tymże Seymie **utatur regalibus, to iest wakanse porozdawał, y dopiero według praw naszych na tymże seymie abdykował, a po abdykacyi Stanisława** cum consensu Rzpltey odprawioney aby się zaczynało dopiero **panowanie Augusta.**

Z takową tedy Rezolucyą był expedyowany **Orlik y z takim memoryałem iuż in antecessum z Jegomością Panem Oboźnym Koronnym, a posłem** Naszym Kommunikowanym, który in Copia przydaię WMCPanu.

Tandem gdyśmy się wszyscy spodziewali pomyślney na to, z Statecznego Serca Naszego et ex indissolubili vinculo **wieczney przyiaźni y kointelligencyi naszey z Naiaśnieyszą Potencyą Francuską,** rezolucyi, aż powtornym wyprawiony z Wersalu d. 11. January stawa tu u nas we dwunastu dni Kuryer **z tą finalną od Dworu Francuskiego repliką,** że z temi punktami wysłany do Dworu Wiedeńskiego Mr Du Theil, premier Commis du bureau des Affaires Etrangeres, et quidem instantanee, aby popierał ile możności iego te wszystkie punkta na tey tam negocyacyi Wiedyńskiey.

**Y Orlik za tymże personaliter** expedyowany, aby co tu **słyszał, widział y porozumiał,** we wszystkim tamże informował pomienionego Ministra, znosząc się tu iuż **ze Stanisławem et avec [?Monti] iako destynowanym ad status confoederatos Ministrem,** a interea aby **Stanisław instantanee abdykował koronę polską,** ponieważ **ta abdykacya pro basi et fundamento przyszłego zupełnego y chwalebnego całey Polski, na teyże Wiedyńskiey negocyacyi, uspokoienia położona, et quidem sine qua nihil.**

Y ieszcze to insuper iniunctum **a [?Monti], aby doniosł** statibus Confoederatis, że gdyby **na tę abdykacyą nie mieli dać consensum suum, że na ten cza[s] [145]** nie powinien bydź **obwiniony, kiedy rzeczy nie poydą ad mentem et placitum Rzpltey.**

Sic tedy stantibus rebus, gdy extrema nas wszystkich [?nieszczęśliwych] Polakow tu rezyduiących ogarnęła apprehensio et desperatio, wszyscy prawie universaliter decydowali, że ex rationibus status sui y Praw swoich żadną żywą miarą na Abdykacyą Konsensu swego dać nie mogą, **poki nie zostaną ubespieczeni, że Stanisława electio pro iusta et legitima uznana będzie, y to że Stanisław usque ad terminum utinam quam longissimum vitae suae conservabit zawsze titulum krola polskiego y W. Xa Litt., y że August panować nie będzie aż po iego abdykacyi.** Konfederacya zaś Dzikowska żeby się dopiero na Seymie nastempuiącym Pacificationis rozwiązała, kiedy ta Dominatio ogłoszona będzie cum plena satisfactione Reipublicae.

**Stanisław ex persona sua fortiter promovebat abdykacyą swoię,** annuendo votis **protectricis potentiae.** Expostulował po kilka razy **per delegatum suum Xiędza Sekretarza Koronnego,** aby dogadzaiąc bonis intentionibus **potencyi francuskiey status confoederati praebeant suum consensum na nią.**

Odpowiedzieli y ultimarie **cum plena submissione, że abdykacya Stanisława, wolnemi głosami na tron polski obranego,** iuż to iest ultimus gradus zguby Wolności y Nieszczęścia całey Rzpltey, per consequens praecipitare w nim ullatenus nie mogą, y Supplikowali o pacyencyą do kilku Niedziel ad deliberandum.

Interea nie opuszczaiąc rąk w tey ostatniey toni y zgubie naszey, **[715] z podskarbiem W. Litt. ultro se obtulerunt statibus confoederatis iechać do Warszawy** dla porozumienia się tamże cum **ministris extraneis, osobliwie z posłem cesarskim y rossyiskim, ieżeliby iaka w Warszawie** in hunc finem miała iuż bydź zaczęta negocyacya.

Jakoż pomienieni JchMC, napisawszy do Warszawy do JMCi Kizerlinga, Posła Pełnomocnego Carowy JMCi, otrzymali sobie paszporty cum plena facultate eundi et redeundi, y iuż od dni czterech iak do Warszawy wyiechali. Co tam za faciem rerum zastaną y iako tam przyięci będą, quam sortem dla nas samych et pro statibus confoederatis odbiorą, czas to krotki pokaże, **bo pewnie sami w osobach swoich powrocą tu do nas et referent referenda.**

My zaś wszyscy, lubo sine mediis ulterioris subsistentiae **(bo ten traktat, ktory był z nami niedawno zawarty, w niwczym niewykonany y niewypełniony),** iuż conclusimus doczekać tu patienter ostatniey determinacyi fortunae nostrae et Publicae, tak od negocyacyi Wiedyńskiey iako też od ułożenia zupełney pacifikacyi Rzpltey ad referendum statibus Confoederatis przez pomienionych JchMCw do Warszawy expedyowanych.

JWMC Panom **życzy Stanisław** per omnia, y my wszyscy communem sortem z niemiż oczekuiący, **abyście się z mieysca nie ruszali aż do ostatniey de eventu nostro wiadomości;** a ieżeliby też kogo taedium brało et impossibilitas subsystencyi, y na tamtym mieyscu obligowała **szukania salutem et securitatem fortuny swoiey, nikt temu sprzeciwiać się nie będzie, et plena każdemu według ostatniey potrzeby iego conceditur facultas.**

O JMCi Xiędzu Nuncyuszu przeszłym spargitur tu także fama, y Gazety Warszawskie iuż toż samo confirmant, że tam miał stanąć, y iuż privatam et Secretam uczynić Recognitionem Electora Saskiego Nomine SSmi. Ale co w Konsystorzu Publicznym w Rzymie do tych czas to non subsecutum, ani subsequetur, poki Negocyacya Wiedyńska non determinabit fata Nostra et Reipublicae, bo my iuż sami z siebie **iesteśmy extra omnem statum defensionis naturalis.**

Interea, Kochany Dobrodzieiu, co tam **potencya turecka zamyśla in hoc casu,** iakie się z [?zaczepki] **Moskwy przez Ham** pokazuią tam Konsekwencye, **ieżeli woyna przeciwko Tatarom y Ham od [505] nie deklarowana,** ieżeli **Han in hunc finem nie są iuż w marsie,** o czym tu iuż invaluerunt rumores, prosiemy nam dać znać przez umyślnego; a my też wzaiemnie przez tegoż samego communicabimus ultimas sortes nostras.

Y to też per curiositatem donoszę WMCPanu, że **[156] absolute non praetendit abdykacyi Stanisława ani tego** ullatenus nie życzy, żeby się nasza pacyfikacya **generalnym traktatem zakonczyła,** a to z tey racyi, żebyśmy żadnego nie zostawili vestigium, **że nasza elekcya była poczciwa a ich niesprawiedliwa,** y żeby na potym, kiedybyśmy particulariter czy universaliter przystąpić mieli, za partykularną tylko Kombinacyą czyli za Traktatem, **wolno go było w każdym czasie infringere na seymy, nie pozwalaiąc nas zdzieraiąc y [5455] nigdy z [289] nie wyprowadzaiąc.**

WMMC Pana uprzeymie życzliwym Bratem y Służącym Sługą. The signature is a large flourish, and I do not read it.

D. 4. Feb: [?1736].

P.S. Proszę WMC Dobrodzieiu kommunikować tego wszystkiego JMX Ki Biskupowi Kiiowskiemu y wszystkim Iustae Causae nostrae Manutentoribus. JMść Pani Starościna [?Pekrzyńska] stanęła tu w Prusiech szczęśliwie, ale nie dociekaiąc do Krolewca w [?Białey] teraz nad Granicą rezyduie.

# AGAD 1/338/0/1/1401: English translation

From `reading.md`, with the same holes. `[175]` is an unread code group, `[?word]` is a doubtful reading, and a word in square brackets is supplied. Latin phrases are translated with the Polish. The reading marks the cipher in bold.

Monseigneur,

This messenger, as he assured me, was sent by Your Honour. I do not know by what chance he was held back until now. I could not give this due attention because of my illness. Moreover, he made himself known only a week ago, through Madame Pac.

So I send him off at once, having given him red zlotys for the road, at my lord's wish. I report very briefly our unhappy, or rather wretched, situation.

For it is now more than certain that our protecting power has already declared the following. I do not know by what fate, because the arms and power of its enemies did not force it. It says that for insuperable reasons, and above all for the stubbornness of the Russian and German powers, it cannot in any way keep Stanisław on the Polish throne. At the price of a general peace, and rather for the faster [?calming] of our perishing Poland, it first agreed to the preliminary articles of a treaty with the Court of Vienna, probably well known already to Your Honour. It also published an armistice, agreed at once between the warring parties, for a calmer conclusion of the future general treaty.

All this happened without the knowledge of the allied powers, both [175] and [195]. A special courier, Mr Orlik, was sent to us with this news, as if it were the happiest. He dared to speak these words among us: "I am sent here [?by the Most Christian King] of my lord, in the hope that Stanisław and the whole Confederation will receive me here with a cheerful face. He is assured a calmer life, with his honour kept whole. The Confederation can safely go back to its states and estates, in full security of honours, goods and persons." In the end he was received sadly and without success on all sides, etc.

For so it was. When we received this wholly unexpected news, after so many assurances and pledges of help for our freedom, we were all thunderstruck. There was no one who did not curse such a let-down and such an abandonment of our whole fatherland. It was the ruin of the honour of a free nation, and worse, of the honest sons of the fatherland who sacrificed themselves to win back their freedom, through a loyalty to the French power hardly ever seen.

So Orlik here was received most ungratefully by [613], and he was sent back wholly with this resolution. They cannot be at all content with this step, least of all with the unexpected removal of our lord from the Polish throne, a throne that has stood for ages for the safety of Polish freedom. They give no other answer than this. To the preliminary articles and to the abdication of the lord, if the whole French power must by our fate abandon him, they can never agree on other conditions, only on these.

First. That Stanisław be first recognised by all the powers of Europe as King of Poland, rightly and lawfully elected, and that he enjoy this title for all his life, may God let it never end.

Second. That he issue the summons to a Diet, convene all the estates of the Republic, and at that Diet use his royal rights, that is, give out the vacant offices. Only then, according to our laws, should he abdicate at that Diet. Only after the abdication of Stanisław, carried out with the consent of the Republic, should the reign of Augustus begin.

Orlik was sent with this resolution, and with such a memorial, already sent ahead through His Honour the Field Quartermaster of the Crown, our envoy. I add a copy of it for Your Honour.

Then, when we all hoped for a good answer, given our steadfast hearts and the unbreakable bond of our eternal friendship and understanding with the Most Serene French Power, a second courier came. He was sent from Versailles on 11 January and reached us here in twelve days. He brought this final reply from the French Court: Mr Du Theil, chief clerk of the bureau of Foreign Affairs, has been sent with these articles to the Court of Vienna, and at once, to support all these articles as far as he can at the negotiation in Vienna.

Orlik is sent after him in person, to inform the said minister there in everything of what he heard, saw and understood here. He is to deal here already with Stanisław and with [?Monti], as the minister named to the Confederated Estates. Meanwhile Stanisław is to abdicate the Polish crown at once, because this abdication is laid down, at that Vienna negotiation, as the basis and foundation of the future full and glorious peace of all Poland, and without it nothing can be done.

Moreover, [?Monti] was ordered to tell the Confederated Estates this. If they will not give their consent to this abdication, then [145] must not be blamed at that [time] when things do not go to the mind and pleasure of the Republic.

Things standing so, an extreme fear and despair seized all of us [?unhappy] Poles living here. Nearly all decided as one that, for reasons of their estate and their laws, they can in no way give their consent to the abdication until they are assured of two things. One is that the election of Stanisław will be held just and lawful. The other is that Stanisław, until the end of his life, may it be as long as possible, will always keep the title of King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania, and that Augustus will not reign until after his abdication. And the Confederation of Dzików should be dissolved only at the coming Diet of Pacification, when this rule is proclaimed with the full satisfaction of the Republic.

Stanisław himself strongly pressed for his own abdication, to meet the wishes of the protecting power. Several times he demanded, through his delegate the Priest Secretary of the Crown, that the confederated estates give their consent to it, to please the good intentions of the French power.

They answered, finally and with full submission, that the abdication of Stanisław, elected to the Polish throne by free votes, is already the last step in the ruin of freedom and the misfortune of the whole Republic. So they cannot in any way rush into it, and they begged for patience for some weeks, to deliberate.

Meanwhile, not giving up in this last drowning and ruin of ours, [715] and the Treasurer of Lithuania offered of their own will to the confederated estates to go to Warsaw. They would there come to an understanding with the foreign ministers, above all with the imperial and Russian envoys, in case some negotiation to this end had already begun in Warsaw.

So the said gentlemen wrote to Warsaw to Mr Keyserling, Envoy Plenipotentiary of Her Majesty the Tsarina, and obtained passports with full power to go and come back. Four days ago they left for Warsaw. What state of affairs they will find there, how they will be received, and what fate they will bring back for us and for the confederated estates, a short time will show. For they will surely come back here in person and report what is to be reported.

All of us, although without means of further living (for the treaty lately made with us is in no way carried out or fulfilled), have now decided to wait here with patience for the last decision of our own fortune and the public fortune. It depends both on the Vienna negotiation and on the settling of the full pacification of the Republic, to be referred to the confederated estates by the said gentlemen sent to Warsaw.

Stanisław wishes the Illustrious Lords, in all things, and all of us who await a common fate with them, that you not move from your place until the last news of our outcome. But if weariness seizes anyone, or the impossibility of living there, and obliges him to seek the safety and security of his fortune, no one will oppose it. Everyone is granted full freedom according to his final need.

Of the late Nuncio a rumour also spreads here, and the Warsaw Gazettes already confirm the same, that he was to come there and has already made a private and secret recognition of the Elector of Saxony, in the name of the Holy Father. But this has not yet followed in a public Consistory in Rome, and will not, until the Vienna negotiation decides our fate and the Republic's. For we by ourselves are now beyond any state of natural defence.

Meanwhile, dear Benefactor: what does the Turkish power plan in this case there? What consequences appear there from the [?provocation] of Muscovy by the Khan ("Ham")? Is the war against the Tatars and the Khan ("Ham") not declared by [505]? Is the Khan ("Han") not already on the march for this purpose? Rumours of this have already grown strong here. We ask you to let us know through a special messenger, and we in turn, through the same man, will tell you our last fate.

And I also report this to Your Honour for curiosity. [156] does not at all demand the abdication of Stanisław, and does not wish in any way that our pacification end in a general treaty. The reason is that we should leave no trace that our election was honest and theirs unjust. Afterwards, when we were to join, one by one or all together, only by a private arrangement or by a treaty, he would be free to break it at any time at the Diets, not allowing us, stripping us, and never withdrawing [5455] from [289].

Your Honour's cordially well-wishing Brother and serving servant. The signature is a large flourish, not read.

4 February [?1736].

P.S. I ask you, dear Benefactor, to tell all this to His Grace the Bishop of Kiev and to all upholders of our just cause. Madame the Starost's wife of [?Pekrzyn] has arrived here in Prussia safely. But she does not go on to Königsberg, and she now stays in [?Biała] on the border.
