Pedrezuela → Rome · code groups and signs, unidentified system · 26 October 1515

Ferdinand the Catholic to Jerónimo de Vich, Pedrezuela, 1515

The last ciphered letter of Ferdinand to his Rome ambassador in the Vich papers uses a new code that no deciphered letter shares; it was transcribed in full but not broken.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: not solved · Extent: text not obtained

At a glanceNot solved · 0 of 3,177 tokens

The letter
Ferdinand the Catholic to Jerónimo de Vich, his ambassador at Rome, Pedrezuela, 26 October 1515. Three months before the King’s death; almost all in cipher.
Where
Archivo Histórico Nacional, Estado 8715 N.73 (PARES 12751418), in the Vich family papers. No decipherment on the leaves or in PARES.
The cipher
Mostly three-letter code groups (sin, sud, no, zre, xed) mixed with a smaller set of signs. Not the 1511–12 key.
How it was tried
Compared with every other cipher in the Vich papers, then annealed against a Spanish five-gram model in three ways. Nothing converged.
Why it resists
No sibling in the same key has a decipherment, and the runs of signs between code groups are too short for a letter model.
Still open
The whole cipher. Terrateig’s 1963 edition of the Vich correspondence may print it; it is not online.

Ferdinand’s letters to Vich of 1511–12 are in another cipher and have their own page, Ferdinand the Catholic to Jerónimo de Vich, 1511–12: a different cipher, with its own page. This letter was listed with them until 14 October 2026.

01 The letter

The Vich papers in the Archivo Histórico Nacional hold Ferdinand’s letters to Jerónimo de Vich, his ambassador at Rome from 1507. Most of the ciphered ones were filed with a clerk’s decipherment. N.73 was not. Its only clear text is the docket a 26 de octubre 1515, the opening words videlicet iterum, and the dateline en Pedrezuela a xxvj de otubre de dxv. Pedrezuela lies north of Madrid.

02 The cipher

The transcription (n73_transcription.txt) has 124 lines and 3,177 tokens of 197 kinds. 1,326 are lower-case code groups as written, the commonest sin (151 times), sud (97), no (93), zre (89) and xed (68). 1,851 are signs, the commonest labelled X (171), TH (170), PHI (163) and RH (151). None of the 1511–12 values carries over.

No deciphered letter in this key is known. N.74 of 1519, the nearest in date, is Charles I’s and is a third system (there sex is papa and sin is de). N.79, filed as a clave de cifra, is a simple letter table that fits none of the letters.

03 What was tried

Annealing against a Spanish five-gram model, with the code groups treated as runs of letters, as whole units, and as breaks between runs of signs (solve73.py, solve73g.py), gave scores of about −2.7 to −3.5 per character, well below Spanish, and seeds that disagreed. The runs of signs are short (median three), so a letter model has little to hold on to. This letter needs a crib or a deciphered sibling.

04 What remains uncertain

  • The whole text. Nothing is known of its content beyond the date and place.
  • The Barón de Terrateig, Política en Italia del Rey Católico 1507–1516. Correspondencia inédita con el embajador Vich (CSIC, Madrid 1963), prints Ferdinand’s letters to Vich from this archive and has plates of ciphered letters with their decipherments. It is not online and was not seen, so it may print this letter.
  • The transcription is a first pass from line strips; a few tokens are marked illegible.

05 Sources
