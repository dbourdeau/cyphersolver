Dresden cipher recipes

Two recipes use a cipher alphabet copied on the facing leaf.

01 What the recipes say

The enciphered pages hold two short recipes in German, with the instructions written in a symbol alphabet. They are historical claims from the manuscript, not advice.

f. 249r, a cure for leprosy. A man who has been visibly leprous for a whole year is to be bathed for nine hours in fresh well water with a sprig of the solis herb in the kettle, fed the broth of a cooked swan boiled with a little of the herb’s root, and let to sleep. When he wakes, blood is let from the median vein of both arms until he is pale; a measure of the last blood is put with the herb in a flask and passed over gently in a water bath, and the water that results is given him to drink at night. He will recover “without fail through the great [virtue] of the herb”.

f. 249v, writing in the sun. Take lion’s blood at the hour the sun enters Leo, mix it with egg white and the juice of the sun-root, and write wishes on the little leaves of the solis herb, one leaf bearing the character of the angel of the month’s sign and the character of the sun. Then burn them on a fire of rosemary wood when the sun shines in a clear sky. “You will see wonders”; but beware of the smoke, which would turn the body as yellow as gold for good.

f. 250r, a third text. Four cipher lines open a different text: “[Now?] I will write out for you the characters of the seven planets, first Saturn, then the others in order”, followed by seven diagrams. It is kept apart from the recipes and from their word counts.

The submission gives no date; the project catalogue lists the volume under 1596–1700 from the manuscript catalogue, which was not checked here. The herb solis and the sun-root are not identified to species. Full German and English texts are under Full texts below.

02 The cipher and key

The cipher is a symbol alphabet, one shape for each letter, and the manuscript carries its own key: the scribe copied the alphabet on f. 248v, the leaf facing the recipes. Reading is therefore a matter of looking each sign up, not of breaking a code. The transcription writes each sign as a stand-in Latin letter (the lead image shows the shapes), and the table gives the plain letter each stand-in has under the manuscript’s alphabet. Words are divided by red marks in the manuscript, which the transcription keeps as slashes; the alphabet has separate signs for long ſ and short s, and for i and y.

Transcription letterABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXY
Plain letterabcdefghiklmnopqrſstuwxyz

Worked example, the first word of f. 249r: the signs transcribed V I K T are w, i, l, t, which spell wilt, “you wish”, the opening of “Wilt einem mentschen…”, “If you wish [to help] a man…”. The reading spells words as the scribe did (uon for von, mentschen, ſchwan), and the nine hours of the bath are written as the Roman numeral VIIII, left unenciphered.

Who did it and how it was checked. Larry Beck and Sonny Panesar, working with ChatGPT, applied the facing alphabet to the recipes on ff. 249r and 249v and transcribed all 53 physical rows with their red dividers. Earlier review checked the line-by-line sign output and sampled the dividers; a follow-up check compared all 123 signs transcribed i (63 on f. 249r, 54 on f. 249v, 6 on f. 250r) with the alphabet and found none that securely showed the dotted sign for y. These checks rest on the contributors’ descriptions of the manuscript images; this project has not recompared the transcription with the images.

03 What is deciphered and what remains open

Two measures are kept apart. Of 1,470 symbols on the three pages, 1,463 carry a value; seven are unread. Of the words, 150 of 154 are sure on f. 249r and 155 of 156 on f. 249v, 305 of 310 (98%) in the two recipes, above the 95% the project requires. The entry as a whole stays partial because the four-line fragment on f. 250r is decrypted but without a reading yet, and further leaves of the cipher section (catalogue 1164) are not part of this reading.

  • four doubtful words on f. 249r: helfe? (“help”, tentative), ſt?delin (“little sprig”), ſo?l (“should”) and tugen? (“virtue”), each with one sign in doubt;
  • the unidentified mark on line 18 of f. 249r, where a quantity of blood is given: no unit is guessed;
  • the last sign of the heading on f. 249v, which seems to say “circle” (krei?);
  • the phrase in iren on f. 249v, whose letters are clear and whose sense is not, and a sentence there that stops without a verb;
  • the first sign of f. 250r.

Reading

249r-reading.md

Wilt einem mentſchen, ſo ein gantz iar ſichparlich mit der maletzei beladen gewest, [helfe?]?

℞ ſolis crut, in ſiner zit gebrochen, mit wurtzen und krut. Tuons in ein kolben.

℞ Darnach den kranken ſetz ine inn ein bade, uon friſchem brunnwasser gemacht, darin uonn disem krut ein [ſt?delin] inn kessel geworfen. Las ine VIIII ſtunt darinnen baden. Darnach tuon ine us bade, leg ine nider. Gib ime uon einem ſchwan, ſo kochet, die brieen, dabi uon diser wurtzen des kruts ein wenig gesotten ſein [ſo?l]. Lass ine daruf ſchlafen.

℞ Darnach ine, ſo er erwacht, las ime uf beiden armen zuo der median. ſo wirts louffen, und ſo lang las es loufen, biss er gantz bleich wird. ℞ Darnach [MEASURE] dess letsten pluots. Tuons och inn uorgemelten kolben. Las es ſittlichen im balneo marie abgon.

℞ Dannethin das ſelbig wasser, ſouil ſein ist, und gibs ime zuo nacht zuo trincken. Er wird one felen gesunt durch die grosse [tugen?] dess kruts.
Translation

249r-translation.md

Do you wish to [help?] someone who has been visibly afflicted with leprosy for a whole year?

Take the *solis* herb, gathered in its season, roots and foliage together. Put it into a flask.

Then put the sick man into a bath made with fresh well water. A [little sprig?] of the herb has been thrown into the kettle. Let him bathe in it for nine hours. Then take him out and lay him down. Give him the broth of a cooked swan, with which a little of the root of this herb [should?] have been boiled. Let him sleep afterwards.

When he wakes, let blood from the median [vein] in both arms. It will flow. Let it flow until he becomes quite pale. Then take [an unresolved measure] of the last of the blood and put that too into the flask mentioned above. Let it pass over gently in a water bath.

Then give him the resulting water, however much there is, to drink at night. He will recover without fail through the great [virtue?] of the herb.

Reading

249v-reading.md

Wann du wilt inn der ſonnen [krei?] ſchreiben, das mans lesen mag.

℞ lewen bluot in der ſtunt, ſo die ſon in lewen gad. Dises ist dann fir und fir guot. Welchs monats du dan ſchreiben wilt, ſo hab acht uf die ſonnen ſtunt und ſchreib uf die bletlin des ſolis craut mit dem lewen bluot, ſo mit aier clar und dem ſaft der ſonnen wurtzen angemacht wirdt inn der ſelben ſtunt. Und mach des zeichens engels caracter, das im ſelben monat regiert, mit ſampt der ſonnen caracter zuosammen uf ein bletlin. Uf die andren ſchreib dein begeren, welchs beschehen.

℞ Rosmarin holtz. Zint ein fir damit ane, und die bletlin, wann die ſonn ſcheint und der himmel clar ist, leg daruf in iren und uerbrens. ſo wirst wunder ſehen. Uor dem rauch bewar dich bei deinem leben, dann wo dir der rauch an den leib keme, wurdest du wie golt ſo gel. Gieng auch nimmer mere ab.
Translation

249v-translation.md

If you wish to write in the sun’s [circle?] so that it can be read.

Take lion’s blood at the hour when the sun enters Leo. It is then good from that time onward. Whatever month you wish to write in, observe the hour of the sun and write on the little leaves of the *solis* herb with the lion’s blood, which is mixed in that same hour with egg white and the juice of the sun-root. On one leaf put the character of the angel of the sign that rules in that month, together with the character of the sun. On the other leaves write your wish, what [you want] to happen.

Take rosemary wood and light a fire with it. When the sun is shining and the sky is clear, lay the leaves on it [*in iren*, sense uncertain] and burn them. Then you will see wonders. On your life, beware of the smoke. If it touched your body, you would turn as yellow as gold, and the colour would never come off again.
