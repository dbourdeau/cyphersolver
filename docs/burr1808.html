Aaron Burr to Theodosia, Baltimore, 1808

Two sheets of numbers filed with Burr’s 1804 letter turn out to be three letters to his daughter from the winter of 1808, hiding at Baltimore before he left for Europe; the key was recovered here.

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · complete

The letters
Aaron Burr to his daughter Theodosia Burr Alston: a cover note of 10 February, a copy of his letter of 31 January, and a short letter of 18 February, all from Baltimore. The year is not written in the cipher; a docket on one sheet reads “Balt.” and “1808”.
Where
Gilder Lehrman Collection, GLC01523, where the two sheets are counted as pages two and three of Burr’s July 1804 letter to Joseph Alston; one is pencilled “1523A”. That letter has its own short cipher and its own page.
The cipher
A Vigenère over the alphabet numbered a = 1 to z = 26, with a six-place key (shifts 6, 18, 22, 7, 24, 8) that runs on through the letters and starts again at each section. The numbers of a word are joined by commas; words are kept apart.
How it was read
The key was recovered from the numbers alone: the index of coincidence rises at period six, and fitting each of the six columns to English letter frequencies gives the shifts. A control text of the same length, enciphered with 20 random keys, is broken by the same method every time.
What it says
Burr, “at Baltimore incog.”, still means to visit her secretly at The Oaks (“let it be believed, except by mari, that I am in Philadelphia”) and waits “hourly” for “money for the long voyage”.
Still open
One group of four numbers on the 18 February sheet, rubbed on a fold.

01 The sheets

Gilder Lehrman’s catalogue describes GLC01523, Burr’s letter to Joseph Alston of July 1804, as three pages of which “the second and third pages are in cypher”, and the Institute’s Hamilton Education Program shows the cipher as part of that letter, “not yet decoded”. The July letter does carry a cipher, ten lines of figures and stroke signs on its clear page, read on its own page. The two sheets of numbers are something else.

The larger sheet (pencilled “1523A” at the foot) is written full in three sections divided by ruled lines. The first section begins “Below the black line is copy of my last” and is dated in the text “the tenth February”; the second is that copy, the letter of “this blessed thirty first day of January”; the third, below the second line, is the end of it. The smaller sheet has three lines at its head, the letter of 18 February, and a docket in another hand: “Balt.” and “1808”.

The year fits nothing but 1808. After his acquittal at Richmond in September 1807 Burr went east, was hanged in effigy by a crowd at Baltimore in November, and spent the winter out of public view while he raised money to go to Europe; he sailed from New York for England on 7 June 1808. Theodosia, often ill, was with her husband in South Carolina. The letters are to her, not to Alston: “the hope of seeing me keeps you at the Oaks at the hazard of your life”, “except by mari” (French, her husband), the pet name at the end of the copy. “Gamp” is Burr himself, as in the journal he later kept for her in Europe.

02 The reading

Burr writes no punctuation in the cipher; it is added here. Spelling as enciphered. [?] marks a word read against one number that disagrees with the key; [s] a letter Burr left out.

Baltimore, 10 February [1808]

Below the black line is copy of my last, by the schooner Wolf, Capt. Jones, to sail about the eighteenth. You will hear again. Keep up your spirits. My plans are not changed. Yet at Baltimore incog., the tenth February. Business now detains me. We must submit, submit, submit to ten days more of suspense. The promise in[?] my last was rashly made. Affairs begin to look up. J’y travaille. What would Gamp not give to know that you are all well.

Copy of the letter of 31 January [1808]

My last letter was written eight days ago. Poor Gamp is still at Baltimore on this blessed thirty-first day of January, still hoping and still persisting in all the plans so often written to you. It distresses me to think that the hope of seeing me keeps you at the Oaks at the hazard of your life. Indeed my be[s]t endeavors are used and shall be used to get to you. If after ten days further delay it be found impracticable, a person shall be sent express to you, and for you just four hours work, huzzey.

No person must suspect that I mean to visit you in S.C. Let it be believed, except by mari, that I am in Philadelphia.

Baltimore, 18 February [1808]

Baltimore, eighteenth Feb. Nothing new. The same hopes and intentions continue. Have[?] patience. [four numbers rubbed] hourly expectation of money for the long voyage, and nothing else detains[?] me.

“J’y travaille” is French, “I am working at it”. The plan of the copy is a secret journey south: Burr would come to The Oaks himself, or, if that proved impossible after ten more days, send an express. By 18 February the journey is not mentioned; only the money for “the long voyage” keeps him.

03 The cipher

Each letter is numbered a = 1 to z = 26 and shifted by one of six amounts in turn:

key place123456
shift618227248
a is written719238259

Cipher number = letter number + shift, counted round from 26 to 1. The key moves on with every letter, not with every word, and starts again at the head of each section; the same word therefore looks different in different places, except when it falls at the same key place, as submit (26,19,10,19,1,16) does three times running. No keyword lies behind the six shifts (as letters, a = 0: G S W H Y I).

04 How it was deciphered

  1. The note of 10 February (80 words, 341 numbers) was transcribed first. All 26 values occur and the commonest has only 8.5% of the text: not a simple substitution. Some words could not be English under one alphabet (a doubled number in 21,11,23,23; 3,22 and 23,12 as two-letter words).
  2. The index of coincidence is 0.0435 for the whole and 0.0618 for every sixth number: a six-place key.
  3. For each of the six columns, the shift that best fits English letter frequencies (chi-square) was taken. The note read at once: “below the black line is copy of my last by the schooner wolf”.
  4. The rest was transcribed and reads with the same key. Two places fell out of step. A pass over the words scored with an English language model found them: Burr wrote 10,11,16 for best, leaving out the s but moving the key on; and on the 18 February sheet the rubbed group stands before a fall-back of one key place.
  5. Control: Burr’s own letter of 18 July 1804 (Davis’s text), cut to 341 letters and enciphered with 20 random six-place keys, is broken by the same method 20 times out of 20.
  6. A statistical check (docs/_validate.py, tokens tagged by key place): −2.21 per letter against −7.47 for 200 random keys and −4.18 for keys refitted to shuffled text: a pass.

05 What remains uncertain

  • 18 Feb, before “hourly”: four numbers, the second rubbed on a fold (read 2?, 11?, 1, 23). No word fits at any key place; “in” would need 17, 20. Open, blocked by the image.
  • in[?] (10 Feb, 15,16): gives “ix”; the key needs 15, 6. Burr’s slip or a misreading. Grade C.
  • have[?] and detains[?] (18 Feb): one number each disagrees with the key (11 for 14, 13 for 18), easy confusions in this hand. Grade C.
  • impracticable and for (31 Jan): the last digit of one number in each is torn away at the edge; the key needs 25 and 24, and the visible 2 fits both. Grade H.
  • huzzey and mari are read as written. Grade C for the gloss.
  • Kline and Ryan’s edition of Burr’s papers (1983) prints no letter of January or February 1808; its editorial note “The Road to Exile” (vol. 2, p. 1040) was not read and may refer to these letters.

## 1. Baltimore, 10 February [1808] (img1, above the first ruled line)

## 2. Copy of the letter of 31 January [1808] (img1, between and below the ruled lines)

My last letter was written eight days ago. Poor Gamp is still at Baltimore on this blessed thirty-first day of

be used to get to you. If after ten days further delay it be found impracticable, a person shall be sent express to
you, and for you just four hours work, huzzey.

Philadelphia.
