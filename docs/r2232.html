Wolff to Princess Wilhelmina of Orange, 25 July 1801

Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, archive of Wilhelmina, Princess of Prussia (A32), inv. 192 (DECODE R2232, catalogued as homophonic with no decipherment). Three pages, cleartext with ciphered passages, signed Wolff: a royalist offers his services and asks for British half-pay. The key Wolff sent on 8 September 1801 is filed in the same inventory number as DECODE R2233; applied unchanged, it decrypts the whole letter. No earlier decipherment was found.

The letter
Wolff, at The Hague, to Wilhelmina of Prussia, wife of the exiled Stadholder William V, 25 July 1801, in French.
Where
DECODE R2232, three images out of order; no decipherment listed.
The cipher
Each group is a row number with a superscript 1–4, so a letter has up to four homophones. Framing sentences are in clear.
How it was read
The key is on the next record, R2233: Wolff’s covering letter of 8 September 1801 with “la clef ci-jointe”. It decrypted the whole letter at the first attempt.
What it says
Wolff wrote a plan for a landing at Le Havre and a bombardment of Paris. He offers his services and asks for British half-pay from the day of his arrest.
Still open
A few groups are one place off from slips, and a few words of the clear text were not transcribed.

01 The letter and the key

The three DECODE images are out of order. The letter begins on image 2 (“Madame! Je demande Grace à Votre Altesse Royale, du mystérieux que je mets dans cette Correspondance”), continues on image 3 and ends on image 1 with the date “La Haye ce 25 juillet 1801” and a ciphered signature. The framing sentences, the forms of address and some whole sentences are in clear; the substance is in figures.

The key lists one sequence, 0 ⅛ ¼ ½ 1 2 … 9 à a b c … n ô o p q r s t û u v w x y z a e i o u a, in overlapping windows of four: row N holds values N to N+3. The writer picks a row and marks the place in it with a superscript; a bare number means the first place. So a can be written 15, 14², 13³ or 12⁴, and the vowels at the end of the sequence give a, e, i, o more choices still. DECODE’s note on R2233 describes the system correctly, but neither record gives a decipherment.

02 The decrypted text

Ciphered passages in brackets; spelling lightly modernised.

Madame! Je demande grâce à Votre Altesse Royale du mystérieux que je mets dans cette correspondance; l’importance de sa matière m’y contraint. Je crois être sûr que ma lettre du 1er de juin est bien parvenue, et j’ai l’honneur de lui exposer ce qui suit. [Je suis l’auteur d’un plan qui a été envoyé à] Votre Altesse Royale et [au roi à Berlin en mars ou avril 1798, lorsque j’étais dans les villes anséatiques, d’un rassemblement … une descente au Havre de Grâce, et fait le bombardement de Paris; une autre en Hollande; un corps attaque l’Yssel et le reste opère sur le continent; de la pacification de ce pays-ci; d’un nouveau genre de commander des armées combinées], et d’autres choses trop longues pour être transcrites ici, et qui finit par le vœu de pouvoir faire comme [Coriolan].

Puisque cette pièce a été faite après [la paix] de [Campo Formio], et que les circonstances se ressemblent, je n’ai pas voulu manquer de me faire connaître, pour offrir très-humblement mes faibles services, pour co-opérer à son exécution en général, et spécialement dans l’endroit où j’aurais déjà alors voulu me voir employé … Outre cela j’ai [offert à feu le prince Frédéric de lui amener quelques milles hommes de la brigade suisse avec l’élite des officiers, par une lettre que j’ai expédiée le matin du 14 septembre 1795, jour que je fus arrêté; sans cet accident je serais entré alors à la solde de l’Angleterre, je n’aurais point soupiré 39 mois et 5 jours dans la prison, et je ne serais pas dans le besoin où je me trouve à présent. Je ne veux pas offusquer le mérite des autres]; mais s’il m’est permis de le dire, je crois pouvoir me flatter d’avoir aussi mérité en quelque façon [pour la bonne cause], que je ne suis pas indigne pour implorer la [générosité bienfaisante du gouvernement britannique] pour en obtenir la [demi-paye] depuis le jour de [mon arrestation; j’avais comme quartier-maître 50 florins] par [mois] courant, [ou un secours] conforme à sa [magnanimité] …

… Daignez, Madame, m’accorder cette grâce avec la même bienveillance qu’il a plu à Votre Altesse Royale de s’intercéder pour moi, lorsque j’ai eu l’honneur de m’adresser à Elle en [1791, à Berlin] … et agréez [de disposer de moi …, lui fournir des occasions où je puisse donner des preuves de ma reconnaissance], ainsi que de [mon adhérence à la bonne cause], et de [mon attachement] aussi [sincère] que très [profondément respectueux à la personne et à la maison] de Votre Altesse Royale, dont [les ordres pourront me parvenir] par cette [même voie sans adresse], avec [3 marques ▽] … [Wolff]. La Haye ce 25 juillet 1801.

A royalist who had been in the Hanseatic towns in 1798 claims authorship of a two-pronged invasion plan against France and the Batavian Republic sent to the Princess and to Frederick William III. With peace in the air again in 1801, he offers to help carry it out. He recalls an offer of Swiss troops to Prince Frederick of Orange (d. 1799), cut short by his arrest on 14 September 1795 and a 39-month imprisonment, and asks the Princess to get him British half-pay as a former quartermaster.

03 Method

The key record was the next number on DECODE. The ciphered year and place (“en 3³ 9³ 11³ 3³, 15, 15² 17³ 31³ 24³ 20⁴ 27²” = “en 1791, à Berlin”) and the signature decrypted at the first test. The rest was transcribed from the three scans (1,065 groups, 119 distinct) and decoded mechanically line by line; no search was needed.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Slips: a few groups come out one place off, from a superscript miswritten or misread. The signature’s 25³ gives m for l; “bienfaisante” decodes as bie?gck; “subsistance” as subsiston; “voué” as vov. Corrected from context (H).
  • Two clear-text passages are summarised, not transcribed word for word. A few words after “proportionné”, “si j’ai le [c…]” and the last symbols after “3 marques” (“▽ en”?) are uncertain (C).
  • Wolff is not identified. His dates (arrested 14 September 1795, about 39 months in prison, quartermaster at 50 florins a month) would be the lead.

Wolff to Wilhelmina, Princess of Orange, The Hague, 25 July 1801 (DECODE R2232)

A royalist named Wolff writes to the exiled Princess of Orange in London that he is the author of a plan sent to her and to the King of Prussia in 1798 (landings at Le Havre and in Holland, a bombardment of Paris, an attack on the IJssel) and offers his services to carry it out now that circumstances are alike again; he recalls being arrested on 14 September 1795 and 39 months in prison, and asks for British half-pay or other relief.

# R2232, Wolff to Wilhelmina, Princess of Orange, The Hague, 25 July 1801: English translation

Translated from `reading.md` (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026), the whole letter: clear text in plain type, the parts
deciphered with the key in *italics*, as in the reading. `[?word]` = doubtful in the reading; … = unread.

## Translation

Madam! I beg Your Royal Highness's pardon for the mystery I put into this correspondence; the importance of its
matter forces me to it. I believe I can be sure that my letter of 1 June arrived safely, and I have the honour to
set out to her what follows.
*I am the author of a plan that was sent to* Your Royal Highness and *to the King in Berlin in March or April 1798,
when I was in the Hanseatic towns: of an assembly … a landing at Le Havre de Grâce, and the bombardment of Paris;
another in Holland; one corps attacks the IJssel and the rest operates on the continent; of the pacification of
this country; of a new way of commanding combined armies*, and other things too long to be copied here, and which
ends with the wish to be able to do as *Coriolanus* did.
Since this piece was written after *the peace* of *Campo Formio*, and circumstances are alike, I did not wish to
fail to make myself known, to offer most humbly my feeble services, to co-operate in its execution in general, and
especially in the place where I should already then have wished to see myself employed, had the thing taken place
in full and had I been at liberty to go there as I pleased.
Besides that, I *offered the late Prince Frederick to bring him some thousands of men of the Swiss brigade with the
pick of the officers, by a letter that I sent on the morning of 14 September 1795, the day I was arrested; but for
that accident I should then have entered the pay of England, I should not have sighed 39 months and 5 days in
prison, and I should not be in the need in which I find myself at present. I do not wish to cast a shadow on the
merit of others*; but if I may be allowed to say so, I believe I may flatter myself that I too have in some way
deserved well *of the good cause*, so that I am not unworthy to implore the *beneficent generosity of the British
government* (at this point) to obtain from it *half-pay* from the day of *my arrest; as quartermaster I had 50
florins* a *month* current; *or relief* in keeping with its *magnanimity*; or else to receive from it such other
*reward* as it may award me, and suited to *my need*, and proportioned [?to my services] … if I have the [?heart],
and my *life*, which at all times has been and always will be *devoted to the old order*, depends on it. My
*subsistence* … Deign, Madam, to grant me this favour with the same kindness with which it pleased Your Royal
Highness to intercede for me when I had the honour of addressing myself to her in *1791, in Berlin; I in the …*, and
be pleased *to dispose of me in the …; to furnish me with occasions where I may give* proofs of my gratitude, as also
of *my adherence to the good cause*, and of *my attachment*, as *sincere* as it is most *deeply respectful, to the
person and the house* of Your Royal Highness, whose *orders may reach me* by this *same way, without address*, with
*3 marks ▽ in …*.
I have the honour to be, with the deepest respect, Madam, Your Royal Highness's most humble and most submissive
servant and valet, *Wolff*.
The Hague, 25 July 1801.
