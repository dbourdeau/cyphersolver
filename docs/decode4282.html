01 The manuscript and its catalogue record

DECODE R4282 identifies the item as Chifferklaver_låda_II_113 at Riksarkivet, Stockholm. Its three attachments are a wrapper, 25745, and text page 1, 25746, followed by text page 2, 25747. The two text pages contain 25 and seven cipher lines respectively. The second text page is the left-page view selected by the source PDF.

The catalogue's 1600–1699 range supplies no authenticated year, and its sender and recipient fields are blank. A collection note mentions Oxenstierna correspondence, but does not identify this letter's author or recipient. Ferdinand and the dukes of Alba and Savoy appear in the reading; those mentions do not establish the correspondents or a precise date.

The record was labelled “Non-decrypted” and “Inline Cleartext: No” when consulted on 14 October 2026. Four cleartext passages are visible in the manuscript and are retained below. The database label records its cataloguing state; it does not establish that no earlier decipherment existed.

R4282 was previously included in the grouped Riksarkivet survey. That survey's September 2026 attempt did not obtain a Latin reading from R4282. This page records George Lasry's subsequent contribution for this letter alone; the earlier attempt remains part of the research history.

02 The recovered key

The key is homophonic: several letter values have more than one written form. A separate symbol stands for qu, and 11 symbol types are nulls. Identifying the nulls and separating similar written forms were essential to the decryption.

Non-null assignment coverage is 800/805, or 99.38%. Including assigned nulls, coverage is 1,079/1,084, or 99.54%. These figures measure symbol assignments, not certainty about every word or historical interpretation. Cleartext and 115 separator or punctuation entries are excluded from the cipher-token count.

A conservative coherence count also excludes all 27 mapped symbol occurrences in the five contextually restored words. That leaves 773/805 non-null occurrences, or 96.02%. The literal transcription and the editorial reading are kept distinct, and the three unidentified word signs remain open.

04 Method and contribution

Solved by George Lasry. He transcribed the graphic cipher using CTTS (CrypTool Transcriber and Solver), and recovered the key using CTTS's built-in homophonic solver. The primary key recovery was ciphertext-derived. GPT-6 Astra then improved and finalized the key and decryption through manuscript checks, corrections to symbol assignments and examination of repeated Latin patterns. The inline cleartext supplied context during this later refinement.

The work separated letter forms from nulls, established the QU and X values and checked similar symbols across their occurrences. The resulting transcription and key preserve source positions so that the mappings can be checked against the images. The displayed Latin adds word division and punctuation; its five substantive contextual restorations are bracketed and itemized below.

Both text pages are covered. The contribution does not authenticate the catalogue date, identify the correspondents, or claim that every phrase has an independently verified historical interpretation. Its relationship to the earlier grouped attempt is documented in the target notes.

The submitted key also passed the repository statistical validation: the literal Latin score was −2.170 per letter, compared with a random-key mean of −4.847 and a best shuffled-ciphertext refit of −3.479. This tests the substitution reading; it does not identify the three open word signs or turn the five contextual restorations into proven mappings.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Unidentified word signs [37], [38] and [39]: M (uncertain). There are five occurrences across three types. Their grammatical positions support word or name functions, but their meanings have not been established. No historical identity is substituted for them.
  • Five contextual restorations: I (inferred from context). They are displayed in brackets and are not additional verified key values.
  • Cleartext ending facimus: I. The damaged ending is supplied from the visible word and its construction. The rest of each cleartext passage is retained inline in regular type.
  • Correspondents, place and date: M. The broad catalogue range is retained. Ferdinand and the dukes of Alba and Savoy are people mentioned in the text; they are not identified here as its sender or recipient.

[Page 1]

[37] has nothing to do here, and that person's name is assailed everywhere with insults.

The Spaniards are contemplating departure from here.

[37] seeks [38] for himself, or for Ferdinand's son.

Yet he sent that person a necklace, which is said to be worth sixty thousand pounds.

During this war he received a large sum of money and assistance from here.

The papist religion still retains its vigour, and there is uncertainty about what its future condition will be.

I am unsure what will become of us.

For all England continually begs [38] to marry an Englishman. They utterly reject a foreign marriage.

They will never accept a husband unless he comes here himself and solemnly promises that he will remain here permanently. I would never advise you to do this.

An arrogant, proud people, they look down on everyone.

We shall let no opportunity pass, but nevertheless in such a way that the matter may remain at your discretion.

We dare not put the matter forward openly before the coronation, for we might perhaps cause that person considerable harm.

We are incurring very great expenses, and are going to incur enormous ones at the coronation.

[Page 2]

Let a reply and a large sum of money be sent to us - quickly, quickly.

For we shall make no progress at all without very great largesse.

The dukes of Alba and Savoy are said to be about to come here on behalf of [39], so that what [that person] has no confidence of achieving through the merits of the case, [that person] may accomplish through their authority.
