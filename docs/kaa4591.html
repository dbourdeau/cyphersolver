System A′: the Bavarian agents’ reports from Hungary and a letter of King John, 1534–35

→

Six letters to the Dukes of Bavaria in one homophonic cipher of graphic signs, whose key was recovered from a gloss of the time and a sibling letter; nine words in ten are deciphered.

The letters
Reports of the dukes’ agents at Buda in 1534–35 (Jörg Weinmeister, Ofen 8 February 1534; an intelligence report of early 1534; letters of June–July 1534 to the secretary Waisenfelder; a newsletter of April 1534; a letter of 1 April 1535), and King John Zápolya’s Latin letter to the dukes, Buda, 30 June 1534.
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 236–247, 252–256, 287; DECODE R9408, R9409, R9410, R9413, R9427, part of catalogue entry 161 (“unknown sender to unknown recipient, 14 ciphertexts”). The images may be published only with the archive’s permission, so none is shown here.
The cipher
A homophonic substitution on invented signs, Greek-looking letters and numerals, written without word division, with code signs for F.G. (Fürstliche Gnaden, in Latin Dominationes) and for King Ferdinand. The hands differ, so the key varies a little from letter to letter.
How it was deciphered
A faint gloss of the time over ten lines of R9427 gave the first key; it was improved on the sibling letter R9407 (deciphered on its own page) and re-learned on each letter by a statistical fit and a decoder that keeps whole period words; every line was then deciphered by hand.
What they say
King John’s diplomacy with Bavaria, the Turk, the Pressburg diet, Katzianer, Rincon at Constantinople, mining in Hungary. King John writes on 30 June 1534 that he has only a truce with his adversary: nos cum adversario nostro pacem non habere sed … inducias.
Still open
695 words of the six letters, legible signs whose values the letters do not settle; no letter reaches 95%.

Until 15 October 2026 this page covered all fourteen ciphertexts of catalogue entry 161, which are letters in at least six ciphers. The others now have their own pages: Łaski’s cipher (R9322, R9323, R9417, with R9403), the Fulda note of 1576 (R9325), a son’s letter of 1535 (R9367), R9416 (a servant’s letter and the Pressburg report), R9424 (1583, not solved) and R9319 (1583 grilles, blocked). R9291 is a key fragment, not a letter. Jörg Weinmeister’s letter of 1533 (R9407), in the same system, has its own page; its improved key was carried back here.

02 System A′: the five letters of 1534–35

R9408, R9409, R9410, R9413 and R9427 share one sign family, which is also that of Jörg Weinmeister’s letter of 1533 (R9407, written up separately). R9427 has a faint gloss over ten lines. Aligned symbol by symbol, it showed that the commonest group w3vX is “ewr F.G.” (X, an hourglass, is a code symbol for Fürstliche Gnaden) and gave the first homophonic key: 4 and ω e, □ and 8 n (“48” = -en), ↓ r, ÿ t, 5 a, π b/w, the slanted ‡ g, the upright H u/v, and so on. With that key the five letters gave German in stretches, with many local errors.

The key carried back from R9407 (2 October 2026). The key was then improved on R9407, a letter of the same system deciphered to 96.7% of its words. That improved key was carried back to the five letters by the shapes of the signs and re-learned on each letter: a statistical fit of the symbol values against a model of sixteenth-century German, seeded with the R9407 values, then a decoder that keeps whole period words (Deutsches Textarchiv word list) and re-estimates the key from its own output. The hands differ, so each letter keeps its own variant: E is d and ch everywhere, π b/w, 5 a and often e; in R9408 “my” is m; in R9413 the symbol k is the word ich; in R9427 a lone R is und. Look-alike signs were checked at the images: in R9408 the transcribed y is two signs (dotted ÿ t, undotted γ r) and 4 is two (closed 4 e, open ч i); in R9409 one symbol takes da, ch and f. Separating them confirmed the values the decipherments had already given, without opening further words. Every line of the five letters was then deciphered by hand from the machine output and the values each symbol can take.

“Words with sense” counts the words of each decipherment that are not marked unread. The strict measure aligns each decipherment to its symbols and counts a word only if every symbol under it takes a value that symbol takes at least 5% of the time in the letter. By the same lexical measure the old decrypts segmented into period words at 0.55–0.69, the re-learned ones at 0.84–0.88. A further re-key from the hand decipherments did not improve on them. The figures are those of 5 October 2026, after every line holding an unread word was re-transcribed at the image and read again under fixed rules (a real word, fitting the sense, every symbol value attested elsewhere, other occurrences agreeing); the strict measure is that of 2 October. The rounds added names: Ferenc and Imre Bebek (R9413, R9427), Debreczen and Késmárk (R9427), the janissaries and Transylvania (R9408), Schongau (R9409). The K word symbol is King Ferdinand by context (grade B): it sends Hieronymus of Zara and his son Vespasian to the Turk (R9408), the Hungarian lords “who hold with [K]” meet at Pressburg (R9413), and the Württemberg war is “against [K]” (R9413, R9427). No letter reaches the 95% bar.

R9408: Jörg Weinmeister, Ofen, 8 February 1534

Signed “… Weinmaister” in cipher, like R9407. Back from his mission, he has handed over a ratschlag, put into Latin. The instruction, he says, was written without knowledge of the Turk, who pays foreign soldiers in cash and thinks himself her[r] der welt. There are sums of 100,000 and twelve times 100,000 gulden. On mining he names Friedrich Schmaltz and his brother-in-law Reinhart, miners to be sent from Schwaz and a silver purchase through Nuremberg. He reports Ferdinand’s peace with the Turk, Hieronymus [Zara] and his son Vespasian sent back to the Turk, Katzianer two months before Kaschau (Košice), and Emericus, provost of Stuhlweissenburg and bishop-elect of Neutra, who married and was imprisoned. He also reports bad half-batzen bearing the dukes’ stamp: er habe dise halbbatzen mit eur F.G. gepreck. Image P1 is a separate news page: Emperor Sigmund’s 6,000 men, Constantinople and Syria.

R9409: an intelligence report of early 1534

The longest of the five, with clear phrases. The dukes and the Roman king are to agree on the confederation. A Turkish embassy has come, and the Sultan is to come to Constantinople. The royal commissioners at Pressburg and Vienna achieved nothing. Katzianer’s troops and Rascians are on the Transylvanian border, Majláth is forcing Hermannstadt (in der Hermanstat … Meilat), and there is dearth in Transylvania. Then the voivode’s practices, a truce in the Zips, Poland against Muscovy, seven burghers of Késmárk held for ransom, and the advice of the French and English kings to make war. Numbered articles follow on a mining concession: Antoni Slesinger, Schmaltz as mining captain, Freiberger, the gold-exchangers and silver-buyers, and the export of gold and silver refused. A postscript begins Auf den Reichstag and closes Datum ut in literis.

R9410 f. 244r: “des ersten aprilis Anno 35”

A treaty is in hand; the King of France has also gehandelt nit so vil als vor, done less than before. Someone will accept no treaty unless the dukes are included, kain vertrag annemen allein eur F.G. sollen … in vertrag eingeschlossen sein. The parties are to hold together bruederlich against the Turks, and the sender commends himself to the dukes als seinen lieben herren und bruedern. The writer closes in aller gehorsamen underthenigkait, dated the first of April [15]35.

R9413: an agent to the dukes, June and July 1534

Three letters (July, 7 June, and “8 Julij”), addressed to the dukes’ secretary Waisenfelder. The dukes’ letters were received with joy; there is no reliable news of the Turk. Cornelius was delayed because der kunigs von franckreich orator antonius rincon had come to Constantinople. Then Katzianer’s riders and Transylvania, the bishop of Wardein before Margrave Georg’s castle of Hunyad, the Württemberg war and the Landgrave. An ore sample found near Ofen assays at 2½ marks of silver. The Turk is at sea with about 200 galleys, and the Hungarian lords meet at Pressburg. Herr Caspar is to be dispatched, and Antoni kept at the dukes’ cost.

R9427: a newsletter of April 1534

Dated 15, 21 and 24 April [15]34, with a postscript of 25 April. It opens durchleuchtig hochgeborn fursten gnedig …, as the contemporary gloss over the first lines has it. Its subject is the Württemberg affair (the Bund, that is the Swabian League, the dukes of Württemberg, the Landgrave, “kain vertrag”), with Ferdinand, Christendom and Hungary. A provost and priest alhie has taken a wife and been imprisoned. The dukes’ letter came durch Stehan on 23 April.

03 The Latin letter of King John, Buda, 30 June 1534 (R9410 ff. 246–247)

The record R9410 holds a second letter on ff. 246–247 (images P3, P4 and P6; P5 repeats P4). It was transcribed by hand on 2 October 2026, 65 lines and 2,342 signs. A contemporary gloss over its first line (accepimus literas vestras) and the clear phrases show that it is in Latin. The German key of f. 244r, re-learned against a Latin model, gave Latin at once. Here the sign that is und in German is et (and in before q), and the hourglass follows Vestre and Vestrarum as F.G. does in German: it is the code for Dominationes.

… In quantum autem Vestre [Dominationes] certiores fieri cupiunt de statu rerum nostrarum, sciant nos cum adversario nostro pacem non habere sed … inducias, quas cum nos sancte et inviolabiliter servaremus et servare parati essemus, … tam ipse quam ad eum pertinentes non cessarunt neque cessant inducias huiusmodi multis modis infringere …

(“As for Your Lordships’ wish to know the state of our affairs, know that we have no peace with our adversary but … a truce, which, though we kept it holy and inviolate and were ready to keep it, he and those who belong to him did not cease and do not cease to break in many ways.”) The letter thanks the dukes for their letters and for passing on news of the Landgrave and of the Duke of Württemberg, and says the king had written that he would send his own envoy. It thanks God for preclaram victoriam, a famous victory which it believes good for all Christendom. It speaks of the adversary’s people writing to the dukes tam impudenter pro libito, and of a man back at Constantinople, from whom letters dated at Constantinople came that very day. It closes by offering omnem amiciciam et fraternitatem nostram: Datum Bude ultima Iunii, Anno (15)34. The royal “we”, the “adversary” with whom there is only a truce, and Buda as the place make it a letter of King John Zápolya to the Dukes of Bavaria. The letter does not say which victory it means. 375 of its 422 words have a sense (88.9%).

04 What stays open

  • System A′: 695 of the 6,827 words of the six letters are not deciphered (5 October 2026). R9408, 123 words (parts of the news page P1, the greeting, the clauses of the ratschlag, the signature’s forename); R9409, 304 (the salutation and date, about half of P7, names); R9410 f. 244r, 42 (lines 3–5, a two-sign group, the place in the date line); the Latin letter, 47 (the opening, two clauses, two name groups at P4.12 and P4.20); R9413, 94 (the opening of P1, the description of the ore, a sign group on P5, the address); R9427, 85 (line ends, names on P2, the signature). The signs are legible; their values are not settled by the letters. All are open code or value questions (grade I or open), not missing material.
  • The K word sign is no longer open: King Ferdinand by context (grade B), though no key in the volume gives it.
  • Grades: the System A′ passages quoted above are C (key and context agree); the identification of the Latin letter’s sender as King John is inferred from its content (I), as is “Hieronymus [Zara]” in R9408.
