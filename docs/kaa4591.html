The Bavarian key volume: fourteen ciphertexts in Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591

→

BayHStA Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 · DECODE R9291–R9427 · catalogue entry 161, “unknown sender to unknown recipient”

Separate letters in at least six systems, which DECODE catalogued as one group. Three are deciphered completely: one with the key filed in the same volume, two with a key recovered from their interlinear glosses. One leaf was deciphered at the time. Eight are partly deciphered, with keys recovered from contemporary glosses, taken from a key register in the volume, or in one case recovered from the ciphertext alone. One is blocked, one is attempted and open, and one is a key fragment, not a letter. No edition of the volume was found.

The documents
Fourteen ciphertexts bound among the keys of the Bavarian chancery, 1529–1583, which DECODE catalogued as one “unknown sender” group. They are separate letters in at least six systems: Łaski and King John of Hungary in 1529–30, Bavarian agents in Buda in 1534–35, a Fulda protest of 1576, two pieces of 1583.
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591; DECODE R9291–R9427, by login. The images may be published only with the archive’s permission, so none is shown here.
The ciphers
Graphic substitutions, most of them homophonic (several signs per letter), with code signs for titles and names. The largest group, System A′, uses invented signs, Greek-looking letters and numerals without word division.
How they were deciphered
R9325 with the key filed beside it; the Łaski letters and System A′ with keys recovered from contemporary glosses, the A′ key improved on the sibling letter R9407 and re-learned on each letter; R9416 f. 263 from the ciphertext alone.
What they say
King John’s diplomacy with Bavaria, the Turk, the Pressburg talks, mining in Hungary, the Fulda affair. On 30 June 1534 King John writes in Latin that he has only a truce with his adversary, nos cum adversario nostro pacem non habere sed … inducias.
Still open
About 11% of the words of the six System A′ letters; most of R9416 f. 263 as a clean text; R9367 in part; R9424 (no key); R9319 (cover letters not imaged).

02 The Fulda protest, 1576 (R9325)

The key sheet five leaves earlier (R9324) has the same six null signs as the note, which is how the two were matched. It is a letter alphabet with doubled letters and a nomenclator of the Fulda affair: Pope, nuncio, the Abbot, Chapter and Dean of Fulda, the Bishop of Würzburg, the Emperor’s councillors, the Duke of Bavaria. Every sign resolves:

Protestatus [est] abbas secreto coactum [se] ad litem acceptare Pontificis inhibitionem. Rogatus Pontifex ut contradicendo se interponat, declaret sive sponte sive vi contra canones esse. Si id fiat, Abbas agere cessat et cum Pontifice litigabit; … Consiliarii Caesaris iudicabunt, aut Pontifex tum facit quod iam voluit. Rogatus Dux Bavariae auxilio sit; Nuncius suadeat Pontifici ita ad protectionem via[m].

It is the deposed abbot Balthasar von Dernbach’s case: he protests that he was forced to accept the papal inhibition, and asks for the Pope’s declaration, the Duke’s help and the nuncio’s intercession.

03 Hieronymus Łaski and King John, 1529–30 (R9322, R9323, R9417)

A graphic monoalphabetic cipher with three word signs. Interlinear glosses over some lines gave 153 word pairs, and the key from them deciphers the unglossed lines. The word signs follow from context: the Sultan “will have [†] in no other place than that of his brother” (King John); “concord between [†] and [X]” (Ferdinand).

  • R9322 f. 121, Buda, 24 November 1529: King John followed the dukes’ counsel; the Sultan, driven home by plague in his army, left John the kingdom of Hungary without tribute, with fifty guns and a thousand hundredweight of powder, and will return next summer against Ferdinand unless there is a concord.
  • R9322 f. 122: “Joannes Dei gratia rex Ungarie” to Duke Ludwig of Bavaria, sending Lazarus the Jew as envoy.
  • R9417, Kraków, 16 June [1530]: the siege of Buda; a concord best made through the imperial princes; Nicolaus Min… sent to the King of France; envoys to meet at Coburg.
  • R9323: Łaski tells the secretary of his pact with the Turk and warns the princes not to draw the Turks on themselves. Lines 4 and 8–10 are too faint to transcribe in full.

04 The Pressburg report and the son’s letter (R9416, R9367)

R9416. Folio 262 carries a contemporary German decipherment over every line. A servant asks the duke for seven years’ unpaid pension and for the office of Oberrichter at Straubing. Folio 263 has a faint gloss over its first twelve lines only. Its key was recovered from the ciphertext alone by a solver (simulated annealing) against a language model built for this target from the Deutsches Textarchiv’s German prints of 1472–1609. The faint gloss on those lines agrees word for word with the solver’s text: “…von beden kunigen zu entlicher handlung gen Pressburg gesetzt…”. It reports the Pressburg negotiations between Ferdinand and John, a Turkish embassy, Imre Czibak offering tribute, and “den hern Griti abgefertigt” (Alvise Gritti, killed September 1534). One sign still has to be set by hand: the sense requires ch where the solver gives s.

Lines 11–16 of the recto have since been deciphered word by word from the image (30 September 2026; r9416/reading_p3_11-16.txt). The symbol the first transcription wrote as one e is two shapes: the zig-zag with one bar is e, the dagger with two bars is s. With that split the passage reads, in normalised spelling:

… daraus der Tag zu Pressburg erfolgt ist, dahin auch des [Ferd.] Tail mit grossem Pomp kummen sint, auch volmechtigen Gwalt anzaigt, und sich zu beden Tailn entliche Vertrage versehen. Es hat sich aber zutragen, das am Ertag in der Fasnacht von dem turkischen Kaiser ain treffliche Botschaft ankummen …

(“… from which the diet at Pressburg followed, whither also [Ferdinand’s] party came with great pomp and showed full powers, and on both sides a final treaty was expected. But it came about that on Shrove Tuesday a distinguished embassy arrived from the Turkish emperor …”) The faint contemporary gloss over lines 11–12 agrees: “das aus den tag zu bresburg erfolgt ist dahin auch / des Ferd tail … mit grossem bumbb kummen sint auch”. [Ferd.] is a code symbol, identified only by that gloss (grade C, the gloss faint); three symbols after “Tail”, passed over by the gloss, are not rendered. The rest of the folio has not yet had this treatment.

R9367, a son to his father, 22 March 1535, is deciphered with block 2_4 (“H. Wilh.”) of R9423, a chancery register copying out nineteen keys of the 1520s–30s. The ciphered passages say he was questioned and denied everything, fears for his safety, and reports the Turks coming up; the postscript names a Friedrich and “Schmalcz”. Three signs are nulls. One sign and six lines remain open.

05 System A′: the five letters of 1534–35

R9408, R9409, R9410, R9413 and R9427 share one sign family, which is also that of Jörg Weinmeister’s letter of 1533 (R9407, written up separately). R9427 has a faint gloss over ten lines. Aligned sign by sign, it showed that the commonest group w3vX is “ewr F.G.” (X, an hourglass, is a code sign for Fürstliche Gnaden) and gave the first homophonic key: 4 and ω e, □ and 8 n (“48” = -en), ↓ r, ÿ t, 5 a, π b/w, the slanted ‡ g, the upright H u/v, and so on. With that key the five letters gave German in stretches, with many local errors.

The key carried back from R9407 (2 October 2026). The key was then improved on R9407, a letter of the same system deciphered to 96.7% of its words. That improved key was carried back to the five letters by the shapes of the signs and re-learned on each letter: a statistical fit of the sign values against a model of sixteenth-century German, seeded with the R9407 values, then a decoder that keeps whole period words (Deutsches Textarchiv word list) and re-estimates the key from its own output. The hands differ, so each letter keeps its own variant: E is d and ch everywhere, π b/w, 5 a and often e; in R9408 “my” is m; in R9413 the sign k is the word ich; in R9427 a lone R is und. Look-alike signs were checked at the images: in R9408 the transcribed y is two signs (dotted ÿ t, undotted γ r) and 4 is two (closed 4 e, open ч i); in R9409 one sign takes da, ch and f. Separating them confirmed the values the decipherments had already given, without opening further words. Every line of the five letters was then deciphered by hand from the machine output and the values each sign can take.

“Words with sense” counts the words of each decipherment that are not marked unread. The strict measure aligns each decipherment to its signs and counts a word only if every sign under it takes a value that sign takes at least 5% of the time in the letter. By the same lexical measure the old decrypts segmented into period words at 0.55–0.69, the re-learned ones at 0.84–0.88. A further re-key from the hand decipherments did not improve on them. The figures are those of 5 October 2026, after every line holding an unread word was re-transcribed at the image and read again under fixed rules (a real word, fitting the sense, every sign value attested elsewhere, other occurrences agreeing); the strict measure is that of 2 October. The rounds added names: Ferenc and Imre Bebek (R9413, R9427), Debreczen and Késmárk (R9427), the janissaries and Transylvania (R9408), Schongau (R9409). The K word sign is King Ferdinand by context (grade B): it sends Hieronymus of Zara and his son Vespasian to the Turk (R9408), the Hungarian lords “who hold with [K]” meet at Pressburg (R9413), and the Württemberg war is “against [K]” (R9413, R9427). No letter reaches the 95% bar.

R9408: Jörg Weinmeister, Ofen, 8 February 1534

Signed “… Weinmaister” in cipher, like R9407. Back from his mission, he has handed over a ratschlag, put into Latin. The instruction, he says, was written without knowledge of the Turk, who pays foreign soldiers in cash and thinks himself her[r] der welt. There are sums of 100,000 and twelve times 100,000 gulden. On mining he names Friedrich Schmaltz and his brother-in-law Reinhart, miners to be sent from Schwaz and a silver purchase through Nuremberg. He reports Ferdinand’s peace with the Turk, Hieronymus [Zara] and his son Vespasian sent back to the Turk, Katzianer two months before Kaschau (Košice), and Emericus, provost of Stuhlweissenburg and bishop-elect of Neutra, who married and was imprisoned. He also reports bad half-batzen bearing the dukes’ stamp: er habe dise halbbatzen mit eur F.G. gepreck. Image P1 is a separate news page: Emperor Sigmund’s 6,000 men, Constantinople and Syria.

R9409: an intelligence report of early 1534

The longest of the five, with clear phrases. The dukes and the Roman king are to agree on the confederation. A Turkish embassy has come, and the Sultan is to come to Constantinople. The royal commissioners at Pressburg and Vienna achieved nothing. Katzianer’s troops and Rascians are on the Transylvanian border, Majláth is forcing Hermannstadt (in der Hermanstat … Meilat), and there is dearth in Transylvania. Then the voivode’s practices, a truce in the Zips, Poland against Muscovy, seven burghers of Késmárk held for ransom, and the advice of the French and English kings to make war. Numbered articles follow on a mining concession: Antoni Slesinger, Schmaltz as mining captain, Freiberger, the gold-exchangers and silver-buyers, and the export of gold and silver refused. A postscript begins Auf den Reichstag and closes Datum ut in literis.

R9410 f. 244r: “des ersten aprilis Anno 35”

A treaty is in hand; the King of France has also gehandelt nit so vil als vor, done less than before. Someone will accept no treaty unless the dukes are included, kain vertrag annemen allein eur F.G. sollen … in vertrag eingeschlossen sein. The parties are to hold together bruederlich against the Turks, and the sender commends himself to the dukes als seinen lieben herren und bruedern. The writer closes in aller gehorsamen underthenigkait, dated the first of April [15]35.

R9413: an agent to the dukes, June and July 1534

Three letters (July, 7 June, and “8 Julij”), addressed to the dukes’ secretary Waisenfelder. The dukes’ letters were received with joy; there is no reliable news of the Turk. Cornelius was delayed because der kunigs von franckreich orator antonius rincon had come to Constantinople. Then Katzianer’s riders and Transylvania, the bishop of Wardein before Margrave Georg’s castle of Hunyad, the Württemberg war and the Landgrave. An ore sample found near Ofen assays at 2½ marks of silver. The Turk is at sea with about 200 galleys, and the Hungarian lords meet at Pressburg. Herr Caspar is to be dispatched, and Antoni kept at the dukes’ cost.

R9427: a newsletter of April 1534

Dated 15, 21 and 24 April [15]34, with a postscript of 25 April. It opens durchleuchtig hochgeborn fursten gnedig …, as the contemporary gloss over the first lines has it. Its subject is the Württemberg affair (the Bund, that is the Swabian League, the dukes of Württemberg, the Landgrave, “kain vertrag”), with Ferdinand, Christendom and Hungary. A provost and priest alhie has taken a wife and been imprisoned. The dukes’ letter came durch Stehan on 23 April.

06 The Latin letter of King John, Buda, 30 June 1534 (R9410 ff. 246–247)

The record R9410 holds a second letter on ff. 246–247 (images P3, P4 and P6; P5 repeats P4). It was transcribed by hand on 2 October 2026, 65 lines and 2,342 signs. A contemporary gloss over its first line (accepimus literas vestras) and the clear phrases show that it is in Latin. The German key of f. 244r, re-learned against a Latin model, gave Latin at once. Here the sign that is und in German is et (and in before q), and the hourglass follows Vestre and Vestrarum as F.G. does in German: it is the code for Dominationes.

… In quantum autem Vestre [Dominationes] certiores fieri cupiunt de statu rerum nostrarum, sciant nos cum adversario nostro pacem non habere sed … inducias, quas cum nos sancte et inviolabiliter servaremus et servare parati essemus, … tam ipse quam ad eum pertinentes non cessarunt neque cessant inducias huiusmodi multis modis infringere …

(“As for Your Lordships’ wish to know the state of our affairs, know that we have no peace with our adversary but … a truce, which, though we kept it holy and inviolate and were ready to keep it, he and those who belong to him did not cease and do not cease to break in many ways.”) The letter thanks the dukes for their letters and for passing on news of the Landgrave and of the Duke of Württemberg, and says the king had written that he would send his own envoy. It thanks God for preclaram victoriam, a famous victory which it believes good for all Christendom. It speaks of the adversary’s people writing to the dukes tam impudenter pro libito, and of a man back at Constantinople, from whom letters dated at Constantinople came that very day. It closes by offering omnem amiciciam et fraternitatem nostram: Datum Bude ultima Iunii, Anno (15)34. The royal “we”, the “adversary” with whom there is only a truce, and Buda as the place make it a letter of King John Zápolya to the Dukes of Bavaria. The letter does not say which victory it means. 375 of its 422 words have a sense (88.9%).

07 What stays open

  • System A′: 695 of the 6,827 words of the six letters are not deciphered (5 October 2026). R9408, 123 words (parts of the news page P1, the greeting, the clauses of the ratschlag, the signature’s forename); R9409, 304 (the salutation and date, about half of P7, names); R9410 f. 244r, 42 (lines 3–5, a two-sign group, the place in the date line); the Latin letter, 47 (the opening, two clauses, two name groups at P4.12 and P4.20); R9413, 94 (the opening of P1, the description of the ore, a sign group on P5, the address); R9427, 85 (line ends, names on P2, the signature). The signs are legible; their values are not settled by the letters. All are open code or value questions (grade I or open), not missing material.
  • The K word sign is no longer open: King Ferdinand by context (grade B), though no key in the volume gives it.
  • Grades: the System A′ passages quoted above are C (key and context agree); the identification of the Latin letter’s sender as King John is inferred from its content (I), as is “Hieronymus [Zara]” in R9408.
  • R9416 f. 263: lines 11–16 are clean; the rest is a working decrypt that needs the same re-keying by hand.
  • R9424 (1583): statistically monoalphabetic, but no solver run gives German, Latin or Italian. The likely key R9422 is cut off at the left in the photograph; code groups are likely (glosses put place-names over single groups).
  • R9319: needs the cover letters on f. 115–118, which DECODE did not image.
  • R9291: a key fragment; there is no text to decipher.
