The Bavarian key volume: fourteen ciphertexts in Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591

BayHStA Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 · DECODE R9291–R9427 · catalogue entry 161, “unknown sender to unknown recipient”

Three letters read in full with keys from the same volume or rebuilt from interlinear glosses, one read at the time, eight broken and read in part; one blocked, one not a letter.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 21 September 2026

Summary. KAA 4591 is the Bavarian ducal chancery’s volume of cipher keys, with about a hundred key sheets from the 1520s to 1620 and fourteen ciphertexts bound in among them. DECODE catalogued the fourteen as one “unknown sender” group. They are separate letters in at least six systems. The Fulda note (R9325, 1576) reads in full with the key filed five leaves earlier (R9324). Three letters of Hieronymus Łaski and King John of Hungary (R9322, R9323, R9417, 1529–30) read in full or nearly from a key rebuilt from their interlinear glosses. A servant’s petition (R9416 f. 262) was deciphered at the time. Seven German reports of 1534–35 on Hungary, the Turks, Rincon and France are broken but only read in part: one (R9416 f. 263) from ciphertext alone with a new sixteenth-century German language model, five (R9408, R9409, R9410, R9413, R9427) from one faint contemporary gloss on R9427, and one (R9367) with a key in the volume’s own key register. The dot-grille of 1583 (R9319) is blocked because DECODE never imaged the cover letters it lies over, R9424 (1583) is attempted and open, and R9291 is a key fragment. No edition of the volume was found. Status: read in part. The working decrypts are machine output with gaps and are marked as such; none of the eight partial readings is yet a clean text.

02 The Fulda protest, 1576 (R9325)

The key sheet five leaves earlier (R9324) has the same six null signs as the note, which is how the two were matched. It is a letter alphabet with doubled letters and a nomenclator of the Fulda affair: Pope, nuncio, the Abbot, Chapter and Dean of Fulda, the Bishop of Würzburg, the Emperor’s councillors, the Duke of Bavaria. Every sign resolves:

Protestatus [est] abbas secreto coactum [se] ad litem acceptare Pontificis inhibitionem. Rogatus Pontifex ut contradicendo se interponat, declaret sive sponte sive vi contra canones esse. Si id fiat, Abbas agere cessat et cum Pontifice litigabit; … Consiliarii Caesaris iudicabunt, aut Pontifex tum facit quod iam voluit. Rogatus Dux Bavariae auxilio sit; Nuncius suadeat Pontifici ita ad protectionem via[m].

It is the deposed abbot Balthasar von Dernbach’s case: he protests that he was forced to accept the papal inhibition, and asks for the Pope’s declaration, the Duke’s help and the nuncio’s intercession.

03 Hieronymus Łaski and King John, 1529–30 (R9322, R9323, R9417)

A graphic monoalphabetic cipher with three word signs. Interlinear glosses over some lines gave 153 word pairs, and the key from them reads the unglossed lines. The word signs follow from context: the Sultan “will have [†] in no other place than that of his brother” (King John); “concord between [†] and [X]” (Ferdinand).

  • R9322 f. 121, Buda, 24 November 1529: King John followed the dukes’ counsel; the Sultan, driven home by plague in his army, left John the kingdom of Hungary without tribute, with fifty guns and a thousand hundredweight of powder, and will return next summer against Ferdinand unless there is a concord.
  • R9322 f. 122: “Joannes Dei gratia rex Ungarie” to Duke Ludwig of Bavaria, sending Lazarus the Jew as envoy.
  • R9417, Kraków, 16 June [1530]: the siege of Buda; a concord best made through the imperial princes; Nicolaus Min… sent to the King of France; envoys to meet at Coburg.
  • R9323: Łaski tells the secretary of his pact with the Turk and warns the princes not to draw the Turks on themselves. Lines 4 and 8–10 are too faint to read in full.

04 The German reports of 1534–35

R9416. Folio 262 carries a contemporary German decipherment over every line. A servant asks the duke for seven years’ unpaid pension and for the office of Oberrichter at Straubing. Folio 263 has no gloss past its first lines. It was broken from ciphertext alone by annealing against a language model built for this target from the Deutsches Textarchiv’s German prints of 1472–1609. The faint gloss on its first lines agrees word for word with the solver’s text: “…von beden kunigen zu entlicher handlung gen Pressburg gesetzt…”. It reports the Pressburg negotiations between Ferdinand and John, a Turkish embassy, Imre Czibak offering tribute, and “den hern Griti abgefertigt” (Alvise Gritti, killed September 1534). One sign still has to be set by hand: it reads ch but the solver gives s.

System A′. R9408, R9409, R9410, R9413 and R9427 share one sign family. R9427 has a faint gloss over ten lines. Aligned sign by sign, it shows that the commonest group w3vX is “ewr F.G.” (X is a code sign for Fürstliche Gnaden) and gives a homophonic key: 4 and ω e, □ and 8 n (“48” = -en), ↓ r, ÿ t, 5 a, π b/w, the slanted ‡ g, the upright H u/v, and so on. Every transcription was then re-checked sign by sign on the images, and look-alike signs that had been merged were split. A decoder applies the key and chooses d or ch for one ambiguous sign from context.

  • R9410 (1535): “…vertragen … behandlung … sachen halben … kunig von Frankreich also gehandelt nit so vil als vor … bruederlich halten … wider den Turcken … gehorsamen underthenigen … datum”.
  • R9413 (7 June, 11 July, 8 July 1534): with the key refitted to this letter, running German: “…genedig furst … empfangen … der orator des kunigs von Franckreich … Antonius Rincon … Constantinopel …”, with Niclas Jurisitz and “Cornelius”.
  • R9427 (24 April 1534, postscript 25 April): “durchleuchtig hochgeborn furst gnediger … neue zeitung … underthenig”.
  • R9408 (1534, addressed in Latin to “Sp…nio”, perhaps Cornelio Sperantio): Hungary, the Turk, Constantinople, Syria, “dreissig tausent man aus dem teutschen land gefurt”.
  • R9409 (11,300 signs): intelligence with numbered articles and answers and a postscript “Auf den Reichstag…”; its decode is still noisy.

R9367, a son to his father, 22 March 1535, reads with block 2_4 (“H. Wilh.”) of R9423, a chancery register copying out nineteen keys of the 1520s–30s. The ciphered passages say he was questioned and denied everything, fears for his safety, and reports the Turks coming up; the postscript names a Friedrich and “Schmalcz”. Three signs are nulls. One sign and six lines remain unread.

05 What stays open

  • The eight partial readings are working decrypts with gaps, not clean texts. R9408 and R9409 probably use their own variants of the key, as R9413 does.
  • R9424 (1583): statistically monoalphabetic, but it does not solve as German, Latin or Italian. The likely key R9422 is cut off at the left in the photograph; code groups are likely (glosses put place-names over single groups).
  • R9319: needs the cover letters on f. 115–118, which DECODE did not image.
  • R9291: a key fragment; there is nothing to read.

06 Sources

Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591, via the DECODE database (records R9277–R9428, login). The images are not in the public domain and are not reproduced. Keys used: R9324 (f. 124–127), R9423 (f. 272–273), the interlinear glosses of R9322, R9417, R9416 and R9427. Language model: Deutsches Textarchiv prints 1472–1609 (lang/ model de-1500s).

Transcriptions, keys, decoders and working decrypts are in the repository folder kaa4591/.
