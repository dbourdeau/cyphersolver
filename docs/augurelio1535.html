“Augurellio” is the cover: Jörg Weinmeister to the Dukes of Bavaria, 10 December 1535

Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 232–235 · DECODE R9407, catalogued as Augurellio to …, Partially decrypted, content unknown · Unsolved Catalogue no. 164

The letter is signed in cipher, twice, at the foot of the slip and of the address leaf. The key of the volume’s System A′ was rebuilt and extended until f. 233 and f. 235 read as continuous German: the siege of Palota, the diet of Pressburg, and fifty thousand Turks marching from Belgrade on Buda.

Summary. The Unsolved Catalogue carried DECODE R9407 as a ciphered letter of “Augurellio”, five pages, language unknown, with nothing known of what the cipher hides. The name is only the cover address on the outer face of the address leaf; the letter itself is signed in cipher at the foot of two leaves, and the signature reads Iorg Weinmaister — Jörg (Georg) Weinmeister, a Bavarian agent writing from Hungary. The cipher is the volume’s System A′, the homophonic sign-and-numeral cipher of the 1534–35 letters R9408–R9413 and R9427. The key was taken over from R9410’s contemporary gloss by the shapes of the signs, then extended by machine (an EM-learned emission table, a Viterbi decoder over per-sign candidate values, and a word-aware beam using sixteenth-century German word frequencies) and by reading rounds, each of which was aligned back to the signs to re-estimate the key. Both main pages were then checked sign by sign against the images, which corrected about forty transcription errors and separated three shapes that had been read as one. Measured strictly, f. 233 reads 96.4% and f. 235 96.1%; the slip and the subscription 92%; the column on f. 234, which the photograph takes at an angle with its right margin outside the frame, 65%. About 89% of the letter reads as sense. The letter reports the Hungarian lord László Móré holding the castle of Palota with four thousand men, the diet of Pressburg that left Palota out of the settlement, a relief refused because of the peace with the Turk, Török Bálint drunk at Vienna, the bishop of Veszprém sent with the recruitment, the taking of Simontornya after Easter, and a Rascian messenger’s news that fifty thousand Turks had marched out of Belgrade towards Buda. Status: read in part. What stays open is the f. 234 column, which needs a straight photograph of the leaf, and a handful of signs that occur once.

01 The record and its leaves

DECODE R9407 is described as “Augurellio to …”, five pages, Partially decrypted, alphabet with graphic signs and numerals, language unknown; its date, 1475/1625, is the span of the whole volume, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591, the Bavarian ducal chancery’s collection of cipher keys with ciphertexts bound in among them. Seven images are attached to the record, and they carry six different things:

  • f. 232v — the address, in clear: Dem durchleuchtigen hochgebornen fursten und herrn … Hertzogen in Bayern, that is Wilhelm IV and his brother Ludwig X, with the chancery docket X Decembris 35. That docket dates the letter: 10 December 1535.
  • f. 233 — a full page of cipher, thirty-four lines, with a contemporary interlinear gloss over the first three lines and two clear Latin sentences inside the text.
  • f. 234 — two blocks on one image: a column of thirty-two cipher lines, and, laid beside it, a slip (Zettel) of eight lines with the news from Belgrade.
  • f. 235 — a second full page of cipher, thirty-one lines, with a clear Latin sentence.
  • the address leaf — three cipher lines at the head, the subscription, and on its outer face the superscription Mag.co domino … Augurello that gave DECODE its title.

There is no image P6: the record’s image numbers skip from P5 to P7, and the missing one would carry the rest of the f. 234 leaf. The images are not in the public domain, so no crop of them is reproduced here.

02 Who wrote it

“Augurellio” is the name on the outside of the folded packet. The writer names himself inside, in cipher, at the foot of the slip and again at the foot of the address leaf, with the same fifteen signs both times:

ʏ m ↓ I π ω ʏ □ 7 ϝ ʏ ʏx ÿ σ γ  =  I o r g   W e i n m a i s t e r

A faint later gloss above the second of the two reads “… weinmeister”, so a reader of the volume had seen as much. The letter is therefore a despatch of Jörg (Georg) Weinmeister, one of the Bavarian dukes’ agents in Hungary; Augurelio, whose name the record carries, is the man the packet was addressed through. The volume has a key of its own made out for him — R9369, f. 172, headed Dno Aurelio Augurelio — and that key is emphatically not the key of this letter: in it ω stands for l, where here it is e.

03 The cipher and the key

The cipher is the volume’s System A′: a homophonic cipher of invented signs, Greek-looking letters and numerals, written without word division, the same system as the 1534–35 newsletters R9408, R9409, R9410, R9413 and R9427. The letters of the alphabet have one to three signs each; there is no separate nomenclator, but there are word signs and two ligatures:

  • letters: ω e, □ n, 4 e and ʏ i, x̸ u, ʏx s, ÿ t, 3 u, ↓ r, 8 n, π b/w/o, ⊔c, ӊ k/ch, I g/z, A p, δ l/h, 5 a, ∃ d/ch;
  • ligatures: ɔo = h, ꝛo = sp (so that Casparn is written c–a–[sp]–a–r–n);
  • word signs: X = “F.G.”, the fürstliche Gnaden of the address, which is the same word sign as in the sibling key R9427; ♖ = ll; and three signs (ʒ, K, Ω) that occur once or twice and remain unidentified.

Three shapes that a first transcription had taken for one sign turned out to be three, and separating them was what made the letter readable: the double cross ‡‡ (g, and u), a curved form (sch, h), and ӊ (k, ch), besides the upright Ħ that is u. Several signs are genuinely two-valued in this hand (E d/ch, n b/w, m i/o, σ a/e, T g/z), which is normal in the family.

04 The reading

The gloss over the first lines of f. 233 — a contemporary hand, only partly legible — reads Genedig fursten und herrn … Caspar … das leger, and that is how the letter opens. What follows is a report on the war in Hungary in the autumn of 1535, between King Ferdinand and King John Zápolya, with the Turk in the background.

f. 233

Herr Caspar was sent into the camp. A powerful Hungarian lord, whom the cipher names More Lesli — László Móré, the robber baron of Palota — holds the strong castle of Palota (Várpalota) with four thousand foot and horse, Hungarians, Turks and Bohemians (… mit 4000 zu fues und ros, ungern, turcken und Behemen …), and has long carried on great morderei there. At the diet of Pressburg the two kings left Palota out of the settlement (uon beiden auf dem tag zu Presburg ist ausgeschlossen, also das ieder kunig welcher zuuor kombt …), and the castle had been pledged by Stuhlweissenburg four years since for something over a hundred thousand gulden. Although the place was first bombarded in vain, against herr Caspar’s advice, he now hopes to achieve something mit graben und maur werffen — by digging and by battering the wall. A relief (entsetzung) has not been allowed, because of the peace the king believes he has with the Turk; and so a Hungarian, Török Bálint, who one evening at Vienna was well drunk (der eins abents wol bezecht), was sent to speak of the relief, and is to be spoken with in the morning wan er nuechter sei, when he is sober. The bishop of Veszprém was sent with the recruitment. What was negotiated at Pressburg, at Constantinople and elsewhere, the writer says, cannot yet be understood.

f. 235

The second page warns the dukes not to trust the other side’s many words (was inen seiner het alweg mit uil worten sagen, dardurch … uerfuert), discusses how the truce (anstand) stands and how long it will last, and reports that the man in question writes that he has made peace with the Turk and will keep to it (das ers mit dem turcken ein frid gemacht, demselben nach wol er sich halten). It speaks of the siege of Móré being raised or the besieged relieved, of herr Caspar, and of a castle taken after Easter, heist Simantorna, ist ser fest — Simontornya. Then business: burghers and a merchant to deal by word of mouth, a factor at Vienna, all letters from herr Caspar to be forwarded one den uerzug and secretly, the affair to be kept “stiller und uleissiger”, and a note that it will not be necessary to bring the Jews into it further.

The slip on f. 234

The slip is the news that prompted the packet:

Anheut dato ist ein Retz[?] komen mit briefen, komen sagt wie zu Kriechischen Weissenburg funftzig tausent turcken sind auszogen hieher auf Ofen zu, und sagt das er wol uon seinem frid, so wais er doch nit was fur ein frid ist, wie dan eur F.G. aus [dem] schreiben zu uernemen haben. Ist zu besorgen, auch wie mir [er] selbs hat gesagt, der … werde der krieg den frid haben. Datum ut in litteris. Iorg Weinmaister.

— today a Rascian (Retz, a Serb) came with letters, saying that fifty thousand Turks have marched out of Kriechisch Weissenburg (Belgrade) up towards Ofen (Buda); the man speaks of his peace, but he does not know what kind of peace it is; it is to be feared that it will be war rather than peace. The slip is not separately dated: it says Datum ut in litteris, dated as in the letter — which is exactly what the faint later gloss above that line had been garbling, and an independent check on the key.

The column on f. 234 and the subscription

The column is the covering letter: what he has learnt from the man, that there is good fortification, Your Graces’ letters, letters passing to the enemy so that no one is detained, a separate copy sent aus schuldiger pflicht, the clear Latin Quid autem actum sit, the agreement with the Turk and the peace against it, the messenger, the recruiting. It is the page the photograph treats worst, and it is read only in part. The address leaf ends the letter: … schreiben, denn ich mich [E.F.G.] als meinen genedigisten herren in demuetiger undertenigkait beuelhe. Datu[m]. Iorg Weinmaister.

Four Latin sentences stand in clear inside the cipher: Rarum novarum autem quae hic habemus, Et propter illum hominem plures, Quid autem actum sit, and Illud tamen summe necessarium esse existimo.

05 How it was read

The key of R9369, the one addressed to Augurelio, was tried first and rejected against the gloss. The letter’s system was then recognised from the gloss pairs as the volume’s System A′, and the key that a contemporary hand had glossed over R9410 was carried across by the shapes of the signs. That alone gave running German — fursten, turcken, ungern, Constantinopel, gulden — and everything after it was a loop:

  1. machine decoding. Annealing from random keys failed (the model collapses onto “nenen”-like text), and so did greedy hill-climbing. What worked was, first, an EM-learned emission table — Baum–Welch on a letter-trigram HMM, learning P(sign | letter) with the sixteenth-century German model fixed — then a Viterbi decoder in which each sign carries a small set of candidate values, and finally a beam decoder that inserts word breaks and scores them against word frequencies from the Deutsches Textarchiv.
  2. reading rounds. Each draft was read line by line against the signs, in the letter’s own language, which resolves what an n-gram cannot.
  3. a supervised key. Every reading was aligned back to its sign sequence by dynamic programming and the values counted, which gave the next round a sharper key.
  4. the images. Two passes separated look-alike shapes (4/ʏ, the lollipop and the θ-form, plain and hooked x), and then every line of f. 233 and f. 235 was compared with its crop, which corrected about forty signs — o– read as ō, ӊ read as the double cross, the curved form merged into it, the two ligatures, and a few dropped signs.

The measurement is deliberately strict: a word counts as read only if it carries no query and no more than one of its signs takes a value that occurs nowhere else in the letter. On that basis f. 233 reads 96.4%, f. 235 96.1%, the slip and the subscription 92%, and the f. 234 column 65%.

06 What remains

  • The column on f. 234, about a third of its words — the DECODE photograph takes this column at an angle, with the right margin outside the frame, so that the ends of nine lines are simply not in the image, and the lower third is curved and faint. Image P6, which would carry the rest of the leaf, is not in the record. A straight photograph of f. 234 would close this.
  • Three word signs — ʒ (column, line 3), Ω (column, line 32) and K (f. 235, line 11). No key in the volume gives them, and each occurs once or twice.
  • A handful of single signs whose value occurs nowhere else in the letter: f. 233 lines 2, 7, 30–31 and f. 235 lines 16 and 20. The sense of the sentence is not in doubt in most of them; the exact word is.
  • Grades for the doubtful readings, in the repository’s convention: the passages quoted above are C (confirmed by the key and by context); Retz on the slip and Hansen on f. 235 are M (the signs allow another word); the unread column lines are I.
