The Duke of Sessa to Charles V, Rome, 18 April 1524

Real Academia de la Historia, Colección Salazar y Castro A-31 (9/31) ff. 128–131 · DECODE R9877 and R9878, catalogued as Luis Fernández, Rome, April 1424, Non-decrypted · the imperial ambassador reports to the Emperor on the Swiss, the Bishop of Veroli’s letters and a request to the Pope for money

Chosen because the catalogue date, 1424, would have made it the longest ciphertext before 1450; the letter is dated 1524 on its own last page. The key was recovered from adjacent plaintext: Sessa’s sibling letters in the same volume carry the court’s decipherments. The nine cipher lines are deciphered word by word except two code groups and one spelled clause; two longer despatches, of 14 April 1524 and 24 February 1525, were then decoded with the same key. The reading audit of 22 October 2026 found an open group or a non-word in most of their sentences; written out as continuous Spanish on 23 October, the letter of 14 April 1524 now reads, while that of 24 February 1525 stays decoded, not read. No earlier decipherment of the 18 April letter was found; Bergenroth did not calendar it.

The letters
The Duke of Sessa, imperial ambassador at Rome, to Charles V: the despatch of 18 April 1524 and its duplicate, and since 2 October 2026 two more of his despatches, 14 April 1524 and 24 February 1525, the day of Pavia.
Where
Real Academia de la Historia, Colección Salazar y Castro A-31 (9/31) ff. 128–131 (DECODE R9877, R9878, catalogued as “Luis Fernández, April 1424”), A-31 ff. 79–86 (R9873) and A-34 ff. 150–156 (R9898).
The cipher
One nomenclator for all of Sessa’s letters of 1523–25: three-letter code groups for words and syllables, single signs to spell, inline with clear Spanish.
How it was deciphered
The key was rebuilt from sibling letters that carry the court’s decipherment, over seventeen passes. Pass 16 used six marginal decipherments found on R9898, which had been thought to have none, and Bergenroth’s abstracts of R9873 as a check on the order of topics; pass 17 pooled the open groups of three letters and took tentative values that read in every occurrence.
What it says
On 18 April, the Swiss and a request to the Pope for money. On 14 April, the disbanding of the army, the Archbishop of Capua’s truce articles, a Pope who “ha estado en grandissimo conflito”. On 24 February 1525, the Datary’s French partisanship and Albany’s march on Naples: dueleme grandemente hallarme a lo que por razon y no por caso temo que suceda.
Measured
Tokens read as sense: f. 128, 104 of 116; R9873, 2,295 of 2,474 (92.8%; 87.8% if the pass-17 tentative values are counted as open); R9898, 2,441 of 3,059 (79.8%; 77.9% strict). Both stay partial, under 95%. These are tokens given a value, not reading figures: as text, R9873 builds sentences (23 October 2026) but reads at 88.3% of its words (measured 24 October 2026), under the 95% a complete reading needs, and R9898 does not build them.
Still open
On f. 128, vo, per and one spelled clause. In the two new letters, about 240 code groups seen once, with no deciphered occurrence anywhere, and on R9898 the bleed-through pages, where the cursive r, u and n cannot be told apart.

02The cipher

A nomenclator in a rounded chancery hand. Three-letter groups stand for words and syllables: gap que, sof de, mus la, dim se, coh Su Santidad, fad rey, Jep paz, bob tregua, cum suyços, po3 Francia, mef Inglaterra. Single signs spell: α a, 4 o, x e or i, ꝺ r, nf p, ott t, # g, tto z, and a p with a loop is the plural -s. A flourished f marks each change between clear and cipher, and v db opens each ciphered paragraph as a null.

03The letter of 18 April 1524

The letter opens in clear. Sessa had seen a letter of the Bishop of Veroli from Constance and sent to the Pope for particulars. Felice, the papal secretary, told him the bishop’s letters of the 6th and 7th reported that the Swiss diet was over and that Lucerne had not agreed to help the French. Bern, Fribourg and Solothurn, the letter goes on, were spreading word that the Swiss in Lombardy were surrounded, so as to raise eight thousand men. The bishop’s friends in Switzerland wanted him to urge the Pope towards some form of peace. Esto es lo que me ha referido.

Then nine lines of cipher. Code groups are resolved; a hyphen joins letters spelled sign by sign; brackets mark what is not deciphered; bold is clear text written between the cipher runs.

las dichas [—]s no le las mostraron, que le ponen sospecha que desea aver algo que refiera de lo susodicho, aunque no / me parece que concuerda mucho con lo primero y postrero que el dicho obispo dize al maestro de postas / hablé en la ora a Su Santidad a pedirle que haya provisión de [—]-r-a los casos de suyços, persuadiéndole los m-re que r-ido los que no m-r-e-van / pues tiene color para ello, aviendo començado la plática de la paz y tregua … entender lo que mas podré. Al visorrey escrivo en el mismo punto.

In English: The said [letters] they did not show him, for they make him suspect a wish to get something out of them that he might report of the aforesaid, although not; it seems to me that it agrees closely with the first and last of what the said bishop writes to the postmaster. I spoke at once to His Holiness to ask him to make provision [of money] for the Swiss business, persuading him … since he has a pretext for it, having begun the negotiation of the peace and truce … to learn what more I can. I write to the Viceroy at the same moment.

Bergenroth’s abstract of the 22 April letter (CSP Spain 2, no. 643) continues the story: the Pope and the Swiss disapprove of the disbanding of the army, and there is “no provision of money from His Holiness or the Florentines beyond what I wrote”.

04The letter of 14 April 1524 (R9873)

A holograph of eight leaves (A-31 ff. 79–86), clear with cipher runs on ff. 80v–84r and a postscript wholly in cipher on f. 86r, dated in clear A xiiij de Abril 1524. No decipherment is bound with it. Bergenroth calendars it as no. 637, “deciphered by Don Manuel de Goicoechea” for him in the nineteenth century; no. 636 is the triplicate of the same despatch with a contemporary deciphering, and no. 638 a court abstract. Those English abstracts were used for the order of topics only: no group value was taken from them without a second Spanish context.

The whole letter was transcribed on 2 October 2026 and read with the pass-16 key. 2,165 of 2,473 cipher tokens read as sense (87.5%), each assembled word scored on a Golden Age Spanish model; 256 tokens are in open code groups and 52 spelled letters make no word.

Pass 17 (3 October 2026) pooled the open groups of R9873, R9898 and R9660 (Sessa’s letter of 1523): 84 groups occur in two or more letters, and a value was taken only where it reads in every occurrence, for example cas = tiene, var = agora, rac = dize, pam = paga, xoc = socorr-, sur = parte, yer = Florencia. All are tentative: none has a deciphered occurrence. With them, 2,295 of 2,474 tokens read as sense (92.8%); counting them as open, 87.8%. About 100 groups (145 tokens) and 34 spelled letters remain without sense; the non-words were re-checked at full zoom and stand as written, apart from one sign (f. 83r, pudiesse).

Written out, 23 October 2026. The reading audit of 22 October 2026 set R9873 back to decoded, not read: the working file was a line-by-line decode with an open group or a non-word in most sentences. It was then written out as continuous Spanish in its 1524 spelling, every word aligned with the decode or the clear text (r9873_reading.md, English r9873_en.md), with further values pooled from R9898, R9660 and a new sibling, R9899 (A-34 ff. 194–195, March 1525), whose one marginal decipherment gives hog = querria. Adopted: gal embia-, hog querria, kaz -mente, and, from sense only and flagged, reb dezi-, dis servido, sur/luc parte, gom quatro and others. The sentences hold through ff. 80v–82v, 83v–84r and the postscript, and agree topic by topic with CSP Spain 2 no. 636; f. 83r ll. 19–25 still do not hold. Counting every sense-only value and every look-alike letter as a hole, the letter stays just over the reading bar, so it reads (grade B); the flagged values await the triplicate’s contemporary decipherment (Gayangos papers) or Kolosova’s key. R9898 was written out the same way (r9898_reading.md) but stays decoded, not read: about 140 code groups occur once, and its bleed-through pages do not resolve.

  • ff. 80v–81r. The disbanding of the army in Navarre was known in Rome; the Emperor is said to be holding cortes in Burgos, then in Aragon and Catalonia, and coming to Italy with the grant, which does not please this court. The Archbishop of Capua left Blois on the 7th with the truce articles. Sessa proposed two things: that the archbishop go from France to England first, para satisfazer a la autoridad del rey, and that the King of France pay the King of England his old pension for the length of the truce.
  • ff. 81v–82r. The 4,000 ducats the Lodi men took at the Stradella, sent by the Pope under another name; the Pope at first muy bravo, then cooled. He will write to the Cardinal of Cortona that his nephew Ippolito is to assist at the government of Florence, has made good the 4,000 ducats and sends 4,000 more; Su Sd. ha estado en grandissimo conflito, not willing to disdain the enemies; those around him keep him irresolute, si no Micer Agostino Foyeta (Foglietta).
  • ff. 82v–83v. Money found on exchange for the viceroy’s army; the nuncio’s letters of 26 March from England: the King has no heart for the war; Wolsey would hear of a truce if the Pope got the King of France to send someone debaxo de alguna onesta color; the English will not contribute in Italy por ninguna via. Advice on the Emperor’s standing with friends and enemies, his nephews’ marriages, Alberto Pio, Count of Carpi.
  • f. 86r, the postscript. The Pope and his ministers are grieved beyond measure at the disbanding; he will not declare himself en liga defensiva ni ofensiva, though there was never a time so fit para acabar de ronper las alas a franceses; what holds him back is fear.

The order and substance match Bergenroth’s no. 636 point for point (the cortes, Capua from Blois, the two proposals, the pension, the 4,000 ducats, the nuncio’s letters of 26 March, the English refusal, Madame Margaret). That agreement is the check on the reading.

05The letter of 24 February 1525 (R9898)

A long despatch in a secretary’s hand (A-34 ff. 150–156), dated in clear De Roma a xxiiij de hebrero 1525, the day of the battle of Pavia and so written before the news. It is not calendared in CSP Spain iii.1. An earlier pass here said it carried no decipherment despite DECODE’s status; that was wrong. Six cipher runs have a contemporary marginal decipherment in a second hand (ff. 150r, 152r, 153v, 154r, 154v three times, 156r), and they were the cribs of pass 16: v em cierto, li mi, le me, net halla, sud datario, yol aqui, kun duque, and the name Micer Agostino Foyeta.

2,361 of 3,059 cipher tokens read as sense (77.2%) after pass 16; 300 tokens are in open groups, and 398 spelled letters make no word, most of them on ff. 150v–151r and 155v, where bleed-through on the images hides the difference between the cursive r, u and n. With the pass-17 tentative values, 2,441 (79.8%), or 77.9% counting them as open; 237 tokens in about 140 groups and 381 letters on the bleed-through pages remain. Re-cut at 1.5 times, those pages do not resolve further, and no other image of A-34 could be found.

  • ff. 150r–152r. The Pope did not answer the Emperor’s letters of 15 November and 15 December; the Datary’s French partisanship que abiertamente se conoce; the French have tried the English ambassador; the Pope offered to secure his master’s pension during the truce. In the margin: aqui ninguna cosa nos comunican, sabiendo que las entendemos y conocemos; por conjeturas sabemos lo que ocurre.
  • ff. 152v–154r. Albany’s march on the Kingdom of Naples; lo de Inglaterra se va calentando; the Archbishop of Capua kept at Piacenza con color de la platica de la paz; in the margin, se de buena parte que el datario ha tentado de reduzir al duque a franceses.
  • f. 154v. Sessa sent to San Germano by the viceroy; in the margin, y cierto si mi obligacion no me forçara … y viendo la malissima providencia que ha avido, dueleme grandemente hallarme a lo que por razon y no por caso temo que suceda.
  • ff. 155v–156r. Pavia expects relief; attacking the French in their strong position would be a manifest disadvantage. Postscript: the Duke of Ferrara has lent the King of France another 50,000 ducats, and (margin) no le plaze mucho al papa, aunque no quiere confessar, es en su menosprecio y que franceses le ayan tomado en su protecion.

06How the key was rebuilt

No key for this cipher survives, and none is in print. What survives is Sessa’s correspondence, and in a number of his letters the court wrote the decipherment on a separate leaf marked A Claro, B Claro. Each of those gives cipher and clear side by side. The work went through eight of them.

  • Three letters of April and May 1524 gave the first 60 groups: f. 138 against f. 140 (17 April), ff. 168v–171 against ff. 172–173 (22 April), f. 322 against ff. 324–325 (19 May).
  • Every cipher line was cut into images of single tokens by a script that splits the line at gaps in the ink, and each token was transcribed and aligned from its image. That separated look-alike signs the eye had merged: two p-loops (plural -s and b), two three-shapes (r and i), two hooked signs (d and u).
  • Six more letters were fetched from DECODE: January and February 1525, 24 and 29 July 1524, and seven of 1523. The January 1525 letter gave dicha; 24 July gave mostrar, quiere and hay. The February 1525 letter was at first taken to carry no decipherment; on 2 October 2026 six marginal decipherments were found on it (section 05).
  • Template matching then searched every downloaded page, about 110, for the target’s groups still without a value, at five scales. The September 1523 letter gave -on, twice, in dilataron and concedieron.

## 3. Reading of ff. 128–131 (2026-09-18, in part; eight passes, see key_working.md)

una letra del obispo Verulano que esta en Constancia, de que aqui embio copia. Luego embie a Su Santidad a
suplicalle me mandase avisar de alguna particularidad para dar noticia della do conviniese. Mandome a mi Felice su

- **f. 80v-81r.** The disbanding of the army in Navarre was already known in Rome; it is said there that the Emperor
