The Marqués del Carpio to Baltasar de Fuenmayor, 1677

Archives générales du Royaume, Brussels, Secrétairerie d’État et de Guerre, inv. nr. 2559 · DECODE R1002–R1011, ten letters, 29 pages · eight with a contemporary decipherment in the margin, two catalogued as non-decrypted

Key rebuilt from the letters themselves: a letter table, two syllable tables and a disguised syllabary of letter pairs · all ten letters read

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 19 September 2026 · updated 21 September 2026

Summary. DECODE catalogues ten ciphered letters that Gaspar Méndez de Haro, Marqués del Carpio, sent from Rome in the autumn of 1677 to Baltasar de Fuenmayor, the Spanish envoy at Copenhagen. Eight have their decipherment written in the margin, but no key. Two (13 and 20 November, R1004 and R1005) are listed as non-decrypted. The key was rebuilt here from the cipher itself. The Sept. 4 letter was lined up against its margin, and the structure then showed at once. Plain numbers 9–30 are single letters. Numbers 31–60 are consonant-vowel syllables in rows of five. Struck-through numbers are a second syllable table. Pairs of letters such as ge, bo and ma are syllables with the consonant disguised (ge = de, bo = co, ma = la). About twenty three-letter code words stand for names. Applied to DECODE’s transcriptions, the key reads all ten letters. The two “non-decrypted” leaves turned out to carry a faint marginal decipherment too, and it agrees with the key reading. What the letters say: the French fleet leaves Messina for Catania; the Prince of Orange raises the siege of Charleroi “intempestivamente”; anti-Spanish rumours and a quarrel with Innocent XI, who refuses Carpio an audience; the loss of Freiburg; Orange’s meeting with Charles II; and fear of war in the Milanese, “pues el Cardenal de Estrées, que está en Turín, no se descuida”. What stays open is edition work: the eight margin letters are summarised here from the machine reading, not edited line by line.

01 The letters

The file is one of several in SEG 2559 holding letters to Fuenmayor from Spanish diplomats, 1674–79. DECODE describes Carpio’s ten as partly decrypted: “the solution is written in the margin, without revealing the key.” R1004 and R1005 are “unsolved”. Each letter opens with a clear paragraph of compliments, then turns to cipher. The cipher is in one hand, in point-separated groups, with the null groups N, R, g and 4 struck through at the start and end of each block. The decipherment runs vertically in the left margin.

03 The two “non-decrypted” letters

13 November 1677 (R1004). Normalised; bracketed words are uncertain.

… lamentarme con V.S. de lo que en todas partes nos persigue nuestra mala fortuna, pues con la llegada del Cardenal de Estrées a Turín se teme alguna gran novedad en Italia, y las cosas de [Flandes] se hallan en el estado que V.S. sabe. Si bien en las materias del Congreso parece debemos suspender el juicio hasta ver lo que resulte del abocamiento del Príncipe de Orange con el Rey de Inglaterra. En los embarazos de esta corte no se me ofrece qué añadir a lo que diré a V.S. en mis antecedentes, sino que si bien aguardaba este correo las órdenes de S.M. no las he recibido, pero no dudo las traerá el siguiente, pues de Madrid me avisan que habían llegado allá las cartas en que di los primeros avisos sobre estas dependencias.

“…to lament with Your Lordship how our ill fortune pursues us everywhere: with Cardinal d’Estrées come to Turin some great novelty is feared in Italy … in the Congress [of Nijmegen] we must suspend judgement until we see what comes of the Prince of Orange’s meeting with the King of England … I expected His Majesty’s orders by this post and have not had them.”

20 November 1677 (R1005).

… más de que nos tienen con gran cuidado las apariencias y evidentes señales de que la próxima campaña habrá guerra en Milán, pues el Cardenal de Estrées, que está en Turín, no se descuida.

“…except that we are much concerned by the appearances and evident signs that next campaign there will be war in the Milanese, for Cardinal d’Estrées, who is at Turin, is not idle.”

04 The other eight, in brief

  • 4 Sept (R1010). The French fleet has left Messina. It was said to be bound for Brindisi, but its design is Catania. Carpio is dismayed by news from Brussels that Orange raised the siege of Charleroi “intempestivamente”.
  • 18 Sept (R1011). False rumours are raised in Rome against the Spaniards after Charleroi. Carpio has had no satisfaction from the Pope or his ministers. The militia is called out and guns are mounted on Castel Sant’Angelo.
  • 25 Sept (R1009). He got neither remedy nor inquiry. He consults the cardinals of the Spanish party, the viceroy of Naples and the governor of Milan.
  • 2 Oct (R1006). The offences continue. The French fleet and galleys, after holding off Catania, are said to have gone back to Toulon badly battered.
  • 9 Oct (R1007). Still no satisfaction. On the cardinals’ advice he stays away from the papal audiences while he waits for the viceroy of Naples.
  • 16 Oct (R1008). No news. He holds firm until orders come.
  • 4 Dec (R1002). On the King’s orders he asked for an audience of Innocent XI about the disputes. It was refused twice, and the Pope would take the King’s business only through a cardinal. Carpio will not treat the principal business until the point is settled.
  • 11 Dec (R1003). The loss of Freiburg (taken by Créqui, 16 November) and its consequences beyond the Rhine. The Pope refused the audience again. He waits for the Marqués de los Vélez.

05 Method

DECODE’s ciphertext transcriptions (by RP and MEG, 2020) were parsed into tokens. The 4 September letter has a legible marginal decipherment, and lining its first lines up against the cipher showed the layout. The letter pairs looked like Spanish syllables with the wrong consonant. ca 9 [42] ca gi 24 against “va a imbadir” fixed a = 9 and r = 24, and 36 = na in both Mecina and encaminava. The rows of five followed from 33 mi, 36 na, 39 no and 48 ri. Every token of all ten letters was then tallied in context, which settled the consonant changes (gi-os Dios, lu-ar-de guarde, ne-lo-cios negocios). The decoder (carpio1677/dec.py) applies the tables mechanically, and its output reads as Spanish throughout. The code words were read from context and from the historical events they fit: Charleroi, Freiburg, d’Estrées at Turin, Orange in London.

06 What remains uncertain

  • The readings come from DECODE’s transcriptions. A few misread numbers show as nonsense syllables, and some tokens as unread ({..} in the raw files). The two November letters were checked against the images; the other eight were not.
  • Code words: tan = Flandes, cun iles = españoles and lun = cart[as] are C (conjecture from context). sen, gin, gus, quol, ana, ten, pon, san are H: each is confirmed by a marginal decipherment or by an event.
  • Struck 14 is not fixed. It stands before -sta and -ticia, so possibly ju, which gives justa and justicia.
  • The eight margin letters are summarised here, not edited.
