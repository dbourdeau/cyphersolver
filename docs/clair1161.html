Ce Seigneur a par deulx fois escript et mandé à ceste royne qu’elle laissast passer en silence et dissimulatio…

BnF Clairambault 1161 · contributed by George Lasry · French

Unidentified to Unidentified, Unestablished;1554/1555 remains a hypothesis

Political notices concerning England and Flanders: English arms and the Tower, religious policy, ships bound for Spain, Genoese lending, foreign courtiers, and a reported plan to force entry at Hampton Court. Deciphered by George Lasry, working with ChatGPT Astra 6 and Anthropic Opus 5.5, in his BnF cipher research of October 2026: 3,149 elements, 3,046 identified; 5 further wordlike groups not classified.

03 The decipherment

f163r — complete eight-line cipher paragraph

Ce Seigneur a par deulx fois escript et mandé à ceste royne qu’elle laissast passer en silence et dissimulation beaucoup de choses quant au faict de la religion en ce royaume afin de regaigner sa noblesse et peuple, et que son filz et elle y puissent regner et commander en plus grand repos.

Translation. This lord has twice written and sent word to this queen that she should let many matters concerning religion in this kingdom pass in silence and dissimulation, in order to win back her nobility and people, so that her son and she may reign and command there in greater peace.

05 Our check

This result is George Lasry’s. This project did not re-solve the cipher: it checked one sample against the Gallica image and recorded what it found.

  • Batch A. Image checked: https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b90010063/f186.item
  • Folio matches the report: yes; cipher present: yes
  • What we checked: f162r (canvas 186) and f163v-164r (canvas 188, "Aultres advis") are all-cipher pages with signs matching his table (+, #, w-forms, 77=de, 76=le, 84, 52, /// null); no run decoded symbol by symbol; the f163r margin plaintext he cites was not on the canvases I fetched
  • Verdict: partly confirmed (method key recovered based on adjacent plaintext, extent partial). The check is a sample, not a re-solution.
  • Notes: Cipher is bound next to an unrelated 1707 certificate copy (f161v), so date and context depend on content only; 1554/55 (Mary Tudor, Hampton Court) is his hypothesis. Only 2/6 pages fully deciphered; 39 provisional labels.
