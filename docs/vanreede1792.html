Van Reede to William V, 1792: the lemon-juice letter

Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, Prins Willem V inv. 205; DECODE R1057, one page, “partially decrypted”. Read end to end with the Stadholder’s Grand Chiffre: the cipher is a cover note pointing to a despatch in invisible ink.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 21 September 2026

read

Summary. The Unsolved Catalogue listed this letter (item 217) from the DECODE record: a numerical nomenclator, partially decrypted, contents unknown. DECODE’s own note already named the code as William V’s “Grand Chiffre”, and the key survives as DECODE R1024; it had just read two other Van Reede despatches (Van Reede 1787). A contemporary hand glossed the first eight lines between the figures, but DECODE’s transcription of that gloss is garbled (“monsieur de Raney eal avenant… blanc he de ci r cle”) and no one had put the letter together. Decoded with R1024, all 62 message groups read at once, with one transcription correction (4204 → 4202, tro). The letter tells M. de Larrey that there is citron, lemon juice, on the four blank half-pages of the enclosure, and asks him to warn the Prince of Orange and hand him the letter. It ends: “les chiffres suivants sont des non-valeurs”. The 21 groups after it are nulls. The enclosure, which carried the actual news, is not on the record.

01 The document and the key

Berlin, 4 February 1792, Van Reede to “Monsieur”, in clear except for eleven lines of figures separated by dots, signed “A. W. van Reede”. 83 groups in all. The key is the 1782 one-part French Grand Chiffre of William V (KHA A29 inv. 301, DECODE R1024, values 1–5000), the same one that reads R1026, R1027 and the control R1028.

Monsieur de Larrey est averti qu’il y a du citron sur les quatre demi-pages blanches de l’incluse. Il voudra bien en prévenir Monseigneur le Prince d’Orange et avoir la bonté de lui remettre ma lettre, que je n’adresse point pour ne pas [pas-ser?]… Les chiffres suivants sont des non-valeurs.

“M. de Larrey is warned that there is lemon juice on the four blank half-pages of the enclosure. He will kindly tell His Highness the Prince of Orange and be so good as to hand him my letter, which I do not address [to him] so as not to … The figures that follow are nulls.”

Larrey is the Prince’s secretary. Syllables are spelled out in the code’s way: ci-tro-n, pa-ge, blanc-he, l-i-n-clu-se, vou-dra, na-dre-s-se.

The tail, 3633 3160 370 1229 3645 50 3139 3650 2768 2800 3370 1681 559 57 3268 2775 1391 1268 2239 2560 57, decodes to disconnected words (je vous remercie de, Conseil d’État, la Russie, facile, Russie, reconnoître, Archiduc): political-looking bait for anyone who opened the letter, which the plaintext itself declares void.

03 Method

Decoded DECODE’s transcription (XZ, 2020) group by group with R1024 and checked every group against the image and the interlinear gloss. One misreading corrected: 4204 (trois) is 4202 (tro), as the gloss has it. No solving was needed.

04 What remains uncertain

  • “pour ne pas 186 4115”: pais-ser as the key gives it; the intended word (perhaps passer or paraître) is uncertain (M). 604 here reads de in the key where the sense wants ne.
  • The invisible-ink enclosure itself is not on DECODE R1057; whether it survives in KHA inv. 205 is not known.
