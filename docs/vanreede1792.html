Van Reede to William V, 1792: a cover note for a letter in lemon juice

Go to the full text ↓

Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, Prins Willem V inv. 205; DECODE R1057, one page, “partially decrypted”. Decoded in full with the Stadholder’s Grand Chiffre, the key already identified for Van Reede’s letters, and checked against a contemporary gloss over the first eight lines. The coded text is a cover note pointing to a despatch in invisible ink.

At a glanceKnown key applied · complete

The letter
A. W. van Reede, Berlin, 4 February 1792, to “Monsieur” (M. de Larrey): clear text except eleven lines of figures, 83 groups in all.
Where
Project catalogue item 217, from a DECODE record listed as a numerical nomenclator, partially decrypted. A contemporary hand glossed the first eight lines, but DECODE’s transcription of the gloss is garbled.
The cipher
William V’s “Grand Chiffre”, a one-part French code with values 1–5000, surviving as DECODE R1024.
How it was read
Decoded with R1024, all 62 message groups give sense at once, with one transcription correction (4204 to 4202, tro). No cryptanalysis was needed.
What it says
There is lemon juice (citron) on the four blank half-pages of the enclosure; M. de Larrey is to warn the Prince of Orange and hand him the letter. “Les chiffres suivants sont des non-valeurs”: the 21 groups after that are nulls.
Still open
The enclosure, which carried the actual news, is not on the record. The word after “pour ne pas” is uncertain.

01 The document and the key

Berlin, 4 February 1792, Van Reede to “Monsieur”, in clear except for eleven lines of figures separated by dots, signed “A. W. van Reede”. 83 groups in all. The key is the 1782 one-part French Grand Chiffre of William V (KHA A29 inv. 301, DECODE R1024, values 1–5000), the same one that decodes R1026, R1027 and the control R1028.

02 The decoded text

Monsieur de Larrey est averti qu’il y a du citron sur les quatre demi-pages blanches de l’incluse. Il voudra bien en prévenir Monseigneur le Prince d’Orange et avoir la bonté de lui remettre ma lettre, que je n’adresse point pour ne pas [pas-ser?]… Les chiffres suivants sont des non-valeurs.

“M. de Larrey is warned that there is lemon juice on the four blank half-pages of the enclosure. He will kindly tell His Highness the Prince of Orange and be so good as to hand him my letter, which I do not address [to him] so as not to … The figures that follow are nulls.”

Larrey is the Prince’s secretary. Syllables are spelled out in the code’s way: ci-tro-n, pa-ge, blanc-he, l-i-n-clu-se, vou-dra, na-dre-s-se.

The tail, 3633 3160 370 1229 3645 50 3139 3650 2768 2800 3370 1681 559 57 3268 2775 1391 1268 2239 2560 57, decodes to disconnected words (je vous remercie de, Conseil d’État, la Russie, facile, Russie, reconnoître, Archiduc): political-looking bait for anyone who opened the letter, which the plaintext itself declares void.

03 Method

Decoded DECODE’s transcription (XZ, 2020) group by group with R1024 and checked every group against the image and the interlinear gloss. One misreading corrected: 4204 (trois) is 4202 (tro), as the gloss has it. No cryptanalysis was needed.

04 What remains uncertain

  • “pour ne pas 186 4115”: pais-ser as the key gives it; the intended word (perhaps passer or paraître) is uncertain (M). 604 is de in the key where the sense wants ne.
  • The invisible-ink enclosure itself is not on DECODE R1057; whether it survives in KHA inv. 205 is not known.

R1057, Berlin, 4 Feb 1792, Van Reede to William V

A cover note, not a despatch: Van Reede warns the Prince's secretary Thomas Isaac de Larrey that the real letter is written in lemon juice on the four blank half-pages of the enclosure, to be handed to the Prince of Orange; the last 21 groups are declared nulls.

## Reading

> Monsieur de Larrey est averti qu'il y a du ci-tro-n sur les quatre demi-pages blanches
> de l'in-clu-se. Il voudra bien en prévenir Monseigneur le Prince d'Orange et avoir la bonté
> de lui remettre ma lettre, que je n'adresse point pour ne pas se[r?]… Les chiffres suivants
> sont des non-valeurs.

## English

"M. de Larrey is warned that there is lemon juice [invisible ink] on the four blank
half-pages of the enclosure. He will kindly tell His Highness the Prince of Orange and be so
good as to hand him my letter, which I do not address [to him directly] so as not to [draw
notice]. The following figures are nulls."

The rest (3633 3160 370 1229 3645 50 3139 3650 2768 2800 3370 1681 559 57 3268 2775 1391
1268 2239 2560 57) are, by the letter's own statement, nulls; decoded they give a jumble
(Conseil d'Etat, la Russie, Archiduc, reconnoître) planted to occupy a reader. Larrey is
Thomas Isaac de Larrey, the Prince's secretary.

Open: "pour ne pas 186 4115" (pais-ser) — probably *passer*/*paraître*, wording of
one group uncertain.
