The mémoires
Armand de Bourbon, prince de Conti, generalissimo of the Paris Fronde, Paris, 26 and 27 March 1649, probably to the marquis de Noirmoutier, his envoy with Archduke Leopold Wilhelm.
Where
Bibliothèque nationale de France, Français 3854 nos. 41–42, ff. 113r–v and 115r (Gallica btv1b52520094g). Catalogue item 26.
The cipher
Lower-case letters for letters, mixed with clear words. Three letters have two signs each; five signs stand for two to four letters each. No nomenclator.
How it was deciphered
George Lasry deciphered both and printed the key and the text (HistoCrypt 2023). Here the three pages were transcribed afresh from the images and decrypted with his key, choosing among the readings of each polyphonic sign with a French language model.
What it says
The talks at court are about to break on Normandy and Mazarin; Noirmoutier is to conclude nothing before the last word, but be ready.
Still open
Three illegible signs; “Tenerande” unidentified; “dix” or “six mil hommes”.

Until 14 October 2026 this page also carried no. 43, Conti’s mémoire for Laigue of 5 March 1649. It is in a numeric code of letters and syllables, a different cipher, with its own page.

01 The documents

BnF Français 3854 (Gallica btv1b52520094g) is a recueil of Fronde papers from the prince de Conti’s side, January to April 1649. It holds his instructions to his envoys, the marquis de Noirmoutier and Geoffroy de Laigue, the Archduke’s Spanish answers, and three pieces in cipher. Omont describes these two as follows:

  • No. 41 (f. 113r–v): “Mémoire, avec chiffre, du prince de Conti. A Paris, ce 26 mars 1649”.
  • No. 42 (f. 115r): the same, “ce 27 mars 1649”, signed Armand de Bourbon.

The catalogue assumed that no. 43, which has a contemporary decipherment, would be the crib for these two. It is not: nos. 41 and 42 are written in lower-case letters mixed with clear words, no. 43 in numbers. Measured on the fresh transcription, the two mémoires hold 1,692 cipher signs; all but three illegible ones have a value under Lasry’s key.

02 Nos. 41 and 42: Lasry’s decipherment, and three corrections

Prior art. A web search before any work found George Lasry, “Armand de Bourbon’s Poly-Homophonic Cipher – 1649”, HistoCrypt 2023. Lasry deciphered both letters, printed their text with a translation, and described the system.

  • How it works. Each plaintext letter is written as a cipher letter. A, L and R each have two symbols (homophones). Five symbols each stand for two to four letters (polyphony): a = t/p/a/b, e = e/g, i = c/z, m = s/d/x, n = m/q.
  • The key. There is no nomenclator. The key’s second row spells J’aime c’est un grand mal, a Boesset air of 1642.
  • The catalogue entry. His paper cites the volume as “fr. 3584”, a typo, which is why the catalogue did not match it.

Decrypted again here. All three pages were transcribed symbol by symbol from the full-resolution images. Because five symbols are polyphonic, decryption means choosing a letter for each of them: a search over the possible letters, scored with a French quadgram model and then a period word list, made the choice. The text agrees with Lasry’s throughout, apart from the three points below.

Corrections to Lasry’s text of nos. 41 and 42

  • f. 113r: dans lequel temps les choses ne pouvant pas. The cipher has ge = NE, which the printed text drops.
  • No. 42: ordonne à ses députez de se joindre à ceux de son Altesse le prince de Conti pour insister à demander l’éloignement du cardinal Mazarin. The words are in the cipher (arldtgmtmaed) and in Lasry’s own figure, but not in his clean text.
  • f. 113v: Bretaigne is enciphered with the L symbol in second place, a slip of the encipherer’s.

“Monsieur de Tenerande”, with whom Longueville is to treat, decrypts the same under the key; the person is unidentified. “Dix” or “six mil hommes” cannot be settled, because one symbol serves both d and s.

Depuis la lettre que je vous escrivi hier j’ay cru vous debvoir advertir qu’on a donné aujourd’huy arrest au parlement qui ordonne à ses députez de se joindre à ceux de son Altesse le prince de Conti pour insister à demander l’éloignement du cardinal Mazarin. On ne doute plus après cela de la rupture … Ne concluez rien pourtant que vous n’ayez le dernier advis, mais si elle arrive, mettez-vous en estat d’en faciliter les suittes. No. 42, 27 March 1649

03 What remains uncertain

  • No. 41: Tenerande is unidentified, and “dix” or “six mil hommes” is open.
  • Three signs are illegible in the fresh transcription (1,689 of 1,692 have a value).
  • The addressee is Noirmoutier only probably (Lasry): the mémoire names him in the third person.
