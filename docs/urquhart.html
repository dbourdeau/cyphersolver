Thomas Urquhart’s Cyphral Octastich and Distich

The Jewel (1652), final leaf · eight lines and a “Decagram”, 285 numbers to 201 · Logopandecteision (1653), “The Cyphral Distich”, 64 numbers to 70 · Schmeh’s Top 50, no. 28

The rule Vals AI published on 31 August 2026 was applied to the EEBO-TCP text of The Jewel and a fresh transcription of the octastich’s 285 numbers from the 1983 edition, without using their plaintext; 238 of 284 numbers are first-occurrence hits against 0.43 for both controls. 274 of the 285 numbers give the letter the verse requires, including line 5, which Vals left open; one letter of it is still open. The rule claimed for the distich does not reproduce.

The cipher text
The cipher printed by Thomas Urquhart as an octastich of 285 numbers under a verse in The Jewel (1652), and a second cipher, the distich, after the Proquiritations of Logopandecteision (1653).
Where
The octastich leaf is missing from every digitised copy; the 1983 Jack and Lyall edition on the HCPortal record gives all 285 numbers, where the public transcription (Schmeh, 2015) is short by thirteen.
The cipher
A book cipher on initials: the k-th number is a word index into page k of the 1652 Jewel, taking the first word on the page that begins with the wanted letter.
How it was read
The habit makes the rule testable without plaintext: 238 of 284 numbers are first-occurrence hits, against 0.43 for the same numbers shuffled and 0.44 for random pages. The poem then decrypts as a prayer for Charles II and his reign.

01 The two poems

The distich stands under the heading “THE CYPHRAL DISTICH” after the thirty-two Proquiritations that close Logopandecteision, followed by six lines promising that “an honest skilful man” will find in it “his own heart’s wishes, and the Author’s minde”. It is printed on the last leaf of the 1653 Logopandecteision in some copies only: Reticuli Labs found it missing from the British Library’s copy (1 September 2026), but David Scheers photographed it in the National Library of Scotland copy H.32.a.39 (2021), and it is reprinted in the 1834 Works (p. 417). Both printings read 38 at the distich’s 56th number, where Schmeh’s transcription has 33. The octastich stands on the last leaf of The Jewel (1652), a leaf missing from every digitised copy; its public transcription is Klaus Schmeh’s, from a scan sent him in 2014, and the HCPortal record holds a scan with the verse beneath it, which a reader quoted in 2019:

To this Octastick if you will subjoyn / A Decagram of this same stuff of mine, / All gather’d out of my Exskybalorum, / You’ll find a Rule by which, with great decorum, / You may most comfortably regulate / Your actions, thoughts and speeches; and know that / I love an aphaeresified treason / Better then any prosthesized reason.

Aphaeresis is the loss of a word’s first letter. Urquhart says, in his way, that the cipher is made of initials taken out of his own book.

03 A test that never sees the plaintext

A book cipher of this kind is normally judged by whether the letters it yields make sense, which invites the objection that a determined reader can make sense of anything. Urquhart’s habit removes that objection. If he scanned each page from the top and stopped at the first word beginning with the letter he needed, then for the right page the number he wrote is the index of the first word of its own initial. That is a property of the number and the page alone. For a wrong page, or a wrong rule, it holds at the base rate, which in this book is about 43 % for a random page and index because small indices are common and the first few words of any page are first occurrences by construction.

The EEBO-TCP transcription of The Jewel was parsed into word lists per page. The 1652 printer numbered pages 34–35, 38–39, 42–43 and 46–47 twice and skipped 60 and 110, so pages are counted physically from p. 1, and three duplicated page breaks that the TCP carries for re-shot images are removed. On Schmeh’s 272 numbers (section 02) a dynamic-programme alignment that may skip pages still placed 231 first-occurrence hits in 267 and decrypted five lines. The full 285 decode straight, one number per page.

Line 1 alone makes the case: 28 of its 33 numbers are exactly the first word of the needed initial on physical page k, and the other five are one word off (25 for 26, 18 for 17, 23 for 22, 75 for 74) or a TCP variant, which is the size of difference one expects between a modern tokenisation and a 1652 page.

04 The decryption

One number gives one letter. The k-th number, counting straight through the poem and not restarting at each line, goes to page k of the 1652 Jewel; count the words from the top of that page, and the first letter of the n-th word is the letter of the plaintext. Pages are counted as they are turned from p. 1, not by their printed numbers, and running heads, marginal notes and catchwords are not counted as words of the page. The first five numbers:

Each of these words is also the first on its page with that initial: no earlier word on p. 1 begins with G, none on p. 2 with R, none on p. 3 with E. That is the habit the test in section 03 measures. The first row also shows a typical difference between the TCP and the 1652 page: the TCP text sets the drop-capital N of “NO” apart, so its word list counts “N” and “O” as two words and puts “given” at 26, and the block gives w (“Worcester”) for the first number; on the 1652 page “given” is word 25.

Each row of the block has one letter per number, so line 5 has 32: _ marks number 158, which the TCP cannot decode because its filming has a duplicate image there; the 1652 page gives W (section 04). Capitals are first-occurrence hits; a lower-case letter is the initial of a TCP word that is not the first of its letter on the page, usually a word or two from it. The decryption agrees with Vals’ published plaintext in every line but the fifth. There they printed AND [·········] THIS USURP’D AUTHORITIE, as did the first version of this page: positions 149–157 come out C-O-N-E-R-T-H-T-O and make no word. Their “one page used twice from position 159” is right, and David Scheers’s photographs of the 1652 pp. 156–159 (20 October 2026) show which page: [157] runs straight on to [158] (catchword know), so no leaf is missing, and numbers 158 and 159 both fall on printed [158]. There when is word 11 and hand word 19; Urquhart’s numbers are 12 and 20, the same +1 on both. From number 160 his page k is printed page k−1. Re-read on 5 October 2026, the line divides one place later. Usurp’d authoritie fills 162–177. His fills 159–161 once the TCP’s split of “Leopoldo’s” into “Leopoldo s” on p. 161 is undone. 05 The distich does not follow

The distich is a separate cryptogram in another book, and the octastich’s rule does not open it, so it has its own result: not solved.

The same test applied to the distich under Vals’ Proquiritation rule, using Reticuli’s tokenised Proquiritations, yields 34 first-occurrence hits in 64 and the string NTMAMNDN_OHTISEABRPTTSGSNTAATVTD, not O God uphold King Charls the second. Texts of thirty to a hundred words give high base rates, so 34 of 64 is chance. Page-index variants on either book, with either numbering, with the second line restarting at page 1, all sit inside the shuffled control. Reticuli’s ten infeasible positions stand. Scheers’s further suggestions (20 October 2026) were tested too: the last letter of the k-th word, the n-th letter of the page, the sums and differences of the two lines, each line alone and the lines interleaved, on both books at every starting page. The best decode scores −2.94 per letter against −2.72 to −2.97 for shuffled numbers: chance. The distich is a different construction, or the same construction on a text not yet tried; no decipherment of it is established.

One more construction was tried on 23 October 2026: the k-th number as the n-th letter of the k-th Proquiritation, with and without signatures, offsets 0 and 1, line 2 forwards and reversed. The best scores −6.99 per letter against shuffled controls up to −6.22: no English. No decipherment of the distich exists on the 1653 leaf or in the 1834 Works, so it stays decoded, not read, and the key text is still to be found.

06 What remains

  • Measured against the verse (measure_sense.py, 5 October 2026), 274 of the octastich’s 285 numbers give the required letter (96.1%): 233 exactly and 41 within two words of the first occurrence. The distich is another construction and is counted on its own: none of its 64 numbers is deciphered. (Pooled, the two would make 274 of 349, 78.5%, which is neither cryptogram’s result.)
  • Number 149, line 5: word 2 of p. 149 is “Colonel”, where the line wants a one-letter word. The 1652 page would show whether Urquhart’s copy differs there.
  • Number 158, line 5: resolved on 20 October 2026 from David Scheers’s photographs of the 1652 pp. 156–159. No leaf is missing; Urquhart used printed page [158] for numbers 158 (when, W) and 159 (hand, H). Counting it, the octastich stands at 275 of 285 (96.5%).
  • Nine numbers (55, 66, 106, 151, 207, 232, 239, 250, 283) point at a word that does not give the letter, and no word within two places does. Each was re-checked on the 1983 photographs and stands as printed, so these are slips by Urquhart or the compositor, or differences between the 1652 page and the TCP; the context fixes the letter.
  • The distich.

07 Method and reproduction

In targets/urquhart/: ct.py holds both transcriptions of the octastich; pages.py parses the TCP XML of A95749 into pages under both numberings; decode_1983_full.tsv gives every position with page, word, letter and first-occurrence flag; measure_sense.py scores every position against the plaintext (measure_sense.txt); align.py is the alignment used on the defective transcription; the controls are in the notes. The TCP text comes from the Text Creation Partnership’s GitHub mirror; the 1983 page photographs from the HCPortal record, whose API answers at api.hcportal.eu.

# Urquhart, the Cyphral Distich: no decipherment

The distich is not deciphered (outcome.parts: not solved, 0/64). The August 2026 claim (the i-th number indexes a
word of the i-th Proquiritation, first letters taken; claimed text `OGODUPHOLDKINGCHARLSTHESECONDANDMAKEHIMTHESUPREMERULEROFTHISLAND`, ct.py `CLAIM`)
does not reproduce here. From NOTES.md §6 (copied verbatim, 22 Oct 2026): "The first-occurrence test on Reticuli's
tokenised Proquiritations gives 34 of 64 hits and the string
`NTMAMNDN_OHTISEABRPTTSGSNTAATVTD / ASOTHDOSTTTBHPBTHBIPFSWTLVWTABV_`, not the claimed prayer." Page-index variants
and David Scheers's suggested variants (20 Oct 2026, `distich_scheers_variants.py`) score at chance.
