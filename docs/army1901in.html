Taku, Chungking, Hiroshima → Tokyo · serial two-digit kana table · February–April 1901

Japanese Army serial kana table, 1901

Go to the full text ↓

Seven Army telegrams of 1901 with their key words in two-digit kana; Harumi-Matsuba rebuilt the table from the translations filed with them, and it decodes 77 of 80 pairs, including a telegram earlier left unread.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 10 November 2026

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: partial (96% of the cipher) · On the map

Contributed by Harumi-Matsuba (solution) · Also credited · Satoshi Tomokiyo (pointer to a source) · Contemporary key or decipherment (key or decipherment used)

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · partial (96% of the cipher)

The telegrams
Seven telegrams of February–April 1901: Major-General Fukushima and Lieutenant-General Yamaguchi at Taku, Captain Idogawa at Chungking and Major-General Manabe at Hiroshima, to Major-General Tamura and the General Staff (Idogawa’s to Fukushima).
Where
National Institute for Defense Studies, General Staff files of 1901, via JACAR: C09122805800, C09122805900, C09122806000, C09122801000, C09122724200, C09122796200, C09122806100.
The cipher
Only the sensitive words are in digits, two per kana, numbered 00 to 67 through the syllabary row by vowel row, each voiced kana right after its plain one.
How it was solved
The telegrams were deciphered at the time: translations are filed with them. Five were aligned with their translations to rebuild the table; it was then applied to two more.
Credit
Harumi-Matsuba, with Claude, Codex and Sonnet (issue #371, kagigo1901-public, November 2026); Satoshi Tomokiyo’s alignment of C09122806100.
What it says
Appointments of officers (Akiyama, Harada, Aoki), the Field Marshal’s journey to Japan, the Viceroy of Szechwan asking for an interview, and an officer’s transfer.
Still open
Two values outside the table in C09122806100 (91 twice, 69); three digits of C09122724200 re-read with the table in hand. The digits rest on AI transcriptions; the images could not be reached from here.

01 The table

NumbersKana
00–14ア カ ガ サ ザ タ ダ ナ ハ バ パ マ ヤ ラ ワ
15–26イ キ ギ シ ジ チ ニ ヒ ビ ピ ミ リ
27–39ウ ク グ ス ズ ツ ヌ フ ブ プ ム ユ ル
40–52エ ケ ゲ セ ゼ テ デ ネ ヘ ベ ペ メ レ
53–67オ コ ゴ ソ ゾ ト ド ノ ホ ボ ポ モ ヨ ロ ン

38 values are attested in the telegrams; the other 30 follow from the arrangement. ヲ is written オ (53), and the particle ハ either ハ (08) or ワ (14). Numbers 68–99 are not in the table. The General Staff’s own drafts to Fukushima in the same months use a different table, a grid, with its own page; the 1902–04 table of the Army’s numeric kana cipher is a third.

The first five rebuilt the table. C09122796200 and C09122806100 were decoded after it was fixed; モ, ト and グ in C09122796200 occur in no other telegram and were set by the arrangement beforehand. C09122806100, a kana telegram with the digits in kanji numerals, was listed as unread on the 1902–04 page. Here 91 stands twice where the translation has ニ就キ / ニ就テ (“concerning”), as Satoshi Tomokiyo’s alignment on Cryptiana shows, and 69 where 任地 has ニ; the digits say テンシヨク (転職) where the translation has 転任.

03 What was checked here

A decoder written here from the stated table, without the contributor’s code, gives the translations’ kana for 77 of the 80 pairs; the three others are 91, 91 and 69. On the two test telegrams the real table matches 21 kana; 1,000 shuffled tables match at most 5. The contributor’s “16/16” counts the two test telegrams together (the ten kana of C09122796200 with ンチワ and フクム). A blind second transcription by OpenAI Codex agreed on 123 of 135 digits (lowest single telegram 75.0%, C09122801000). The JACAR images could not be reached from this session, and no person has checked the transcriptions.

04 Still open

  • 91 (twice) and 69 in C09122806100, outside the table; 91 looks like a code for ニ就キ (conjecture).
  • Three digits of C09122724200 were re-read with the table in hand (first readings 72542, 45075, 02948); the image is poor.
  • Whether this is the “Tokugō cipher (numeric part)” (特号暗号 数字ノ分) is not settled.
