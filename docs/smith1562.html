Summary. The catalogue listed six DECODE records of Sir Thomas Smith, ambassador in France from 1562, as not decrypted. They are leaves from the working file of Patrick Forbes, who in the 1730s copied only the ciphered stretches of each despatch, numbered them, and printed the decipherments in A Full View of the Public Transactions in the Reign of Q. Elizabeth (1740–41). The six records hold eighteen letters, from 17 January 1563 to 29 April 1566. Smith’s own key, “Sr Thomas Smith’s Cipher”, is filed in the same volume as DECODE R9261; Tomokiyo had pointed to it in 2024. With it the passages were read from the images. The five letters of January–April 1563 agree word for word with Forbes’s printed text. The thirteen later letters were deciphered at the time on the originals in The National Archives, but the Calendar of State Papers, Foreign gives only summaries; here they are read in Smith’s words, most of them in full or in large part, and none contradicts the calendar. Two headings are misdated (Avignon 4 October 1564, not 27 October; 22 March 1566, not 1567), and one word the key lists among the nulls, enos, turns out to mean “ambassador”. Some dotted-letter word signs are still read from context.

01 The records and the key

Add MS 4136 is Forbes’s deciphering file for his Full View. For each despatch he copied only what was in cipher, numbered the stretches (1), (2), … in the order they come in the letter, and headed the group with writer, recipient and date. The clear English place names scattered through the copies (London, Dover, Hastings, Lyn, Boston, Poole, Dunkirk…) and the words and, bycause, don, yf are Smith’s nulls: the key lists them under “Nothings”.

The alphabet gives each letter from a to m one to four invented signs, and n, o and p the figures 2–4, 5–7 and 8–10. The same figures with a dot are r and s, and with a colon t, u/v and y, so 6 is o, 6. r and 6: u. Common words are Latin letters marked with dots, colons or bars on different sides (·A and, A: all, :a at, b: be, :b by, F: from, :W which, Ẅ with), and da and ba are the endings -ing and -ed. About thirty signs stand for names: the Admiral, the Constable, the Duke of Guise, the Queen Mother, the Prince of Condé, the Cardinal of Lorraine, the Queen’s Majesty, England, Scotland, France, Spain, the Pope.

02 1563: the readings Forbes printed

The first test passage, in the letter to the Queen of 17 January 1563, came out as chief … on the other side, and Forbes (ii 287) prints: “I do take yt marvelous necessary, that some man were sent to be with the admirall who now is chief on the other side.” In the letter to Cecil of 27 January all 32 stretches run with Forbes’s text in order: the rendering of Tankervill; that is to have one on the other side with the admiral, and I have named him who, for th’acquaintance with the admiral and all of that faction, the knowledge of the countrey and all other respectes, is most fit. But you must dispach him streight; you must not stic for honest allowance in tyme of service; Monsieur Danville is to come over into England shortly; such noblemen of Scotland as favoreth the religion and wold contynew the amytie between Scotland and us. The letter of 13 April is read in full, the Blois letter of 7 March in about half.

03 1563–1566: the passages the calendar only summarises

Arles, 24 November 1564 (“chiefly in cipher, deciphered”, CSP no. 815): “[The Cardinal of Lorraine] and the [Prince of Condé] have had conferens and familiar banketting togither at Soissons and at Nanteuil chasteau, which was the late [Cardinal] of Burbons’ … They of Guyse … complaine that all letters that come from the Cardinal … be opened, principally at Paris and Lyons … Madame de Guise … daily in great dispatches to and from Scotland … This bearer Hume is altogether a Lidington, whom you shall find double, I am afraid, or rather triple, having … pencions of all three princes.”

Avignon, 4 October 1564 (CSP no. 714; Forbes’s heading had been taken as 27 October): the two designs of the French court, one of them of the papists, to bring “all again to the olde state” and “break the amity” of England and Scotland; “the Bishop of Glasgow is here greater, no embassador”; “the marriage of the Queen of Scots”, the Earl of Warwick, Lord Robert and the Prince of Spain, “two marriages for her”; “the house of Guise stronger, which is too stronge”; the Earl of Lennox’s practice “to marry his sonne”, and “they feare that she is enclined to this last offer of Lenox”.

Valence, 1 September 1564 (CSP no. 642): the amity between England and Scotland, the Earl of Murray, “such lords and great men … be the great favourers of religion … to do some great enterprise”, Huntly “died and his sonnes were taken”, Lethington, the Bishop of Glasgow’s pension, and “to marry Lord Robert”. On 3 September the two ciphered words are names: Nanion Cocborne and Villeroy.

25 January 1564 (CSP no. 103): the Spanish ambassador reports the French making ladders at Étaples and Boulogne, to surprise a town in England or fall on the Low Countries; the King of Spain has made no league with the Pope and means nothing against the Queen of Navarre, but wants “to make himself rich”.

The other letters are shorter. Arles, 20 November, on the bearer Adam Hume, runs almost word for word with the calendar up to “glad to be rid of him”. Narbonne, 7 January 1565, covers the posts out of Scotland, Beton and Hume going by land, and “the marriage between the Duke of Guise and the Queen of Scots”. From Bourbon-l’Archambault, 22 March 1566, come the ambassadors of Spain and Scotland with the Cardinal of Lorraine, the Bishop of Glasgow looking to be a cardinal, and a Scottish woman who is “the conveyor of [letters] out of Scotland to the [Lady] Lennox”. And 29 April 1566 names “Peter Clare … Almain captain … Albert de Rosemberg”, and the “continuation with the princes of Almain for the recovery of Metz and Calais”.

04 Folio 140: five earlier letters (DECODE R9236)

A seventh record, R9236 (Add MS 4136 f. 140, catalogue 94), is another of Forbes’s leaves: the ciphered passages of Smith to Cecil of 15 October, 20 October and 15/17 December 1562, to the Queen of 4 November 1563, and of “Sir Thomas Smith’s advertisements from France, 31 Jan. 1562[/3]”. The clear words scattered through them (London, Lyn, Poole, Boston, Dover, Hastings, Garnesey) are the nulls of the same key, R9261, not R9262, which DECODE names as the nearest key. Readings, added 21 September 2026:

  • 15 October 1562, all six passages: the Scots, the Queen of Scots, “break with you and to make war”, “that the hostages which you have shall be convoyed”, Flanders, Rollet and Monsieur Vyllemort. These agree with Forbes, Full View ii 119.
  • 15/17 December 1562, all six: “I see, you must hold ferme and fast: Frenchmen will come to reason but compellid … to trust no Frenchman of what religion soever he be … the Prince of Condé, which I do not believe … as soon as ever they be able, they will thither [bend] all their force”. These agree with Forbes ii 238.
  • 4 November 1563, to the Queen, read in full and confirmed by the calendar (CSP Foreign vi no. 1366): “…de Montaigne … whose carte of La Rochelle … under another pretence. I have given letters to my Lord Robert and Mr Secretarie … from Rouen … wrote of that same matter … I thinke he can do. But this at this tyme is most necessarie.”
  • 20 October 1562, all four passages, with Forbes ii 122 as the check: [Newhaven] is not so strong as supposed; if it were summer they would attempt to afray our men thence “with a garden of chamillion upon the hill on this side”; things begun there by [France] be not perfit.
  • 31 January 1562/3, the advertisements: only passage (2), “the queen of Navarre”, is read; (1), (3) and the last line remain fragments, and no printed text was found to check them against.

One variant appears on this leaf only: a double bar stands for i. Passage-by-passage readings are in smith1562/decipher_E.md.

05 Method

The six records and the Smith-series key records were fetched from DECODE. The first key record DECODE names, R9262, is Throckmorton’s third cipher. The record listing around it gave R9261, whose first page is Smith’s cipher and whose second is Sir James Croft’s. The passages were transcribed from the images and the key applied sign by sign. Forbes’s printed letters (vol. ii, from the archive.org text) and the calendar summaries were used only to confirm readings, and where they did not fit the signs the reading was left open. Readings added to the key:

  • enos, listed at the top of the key, is “Ambassador”, not a null; a marginal gloss on the 20 November letter spells it out.
  • M̅ and M̈ are marriage and marry, B: is Bishop, C̄ is Congregation, J̣ is is or are, ! is in or into.
  • The doubled long-s signs are st, the small open v is l, and a looped kite with a stem is w.
  • A variant of the England sign stands for Earl (before Murray, Lennox and Warwick). The flag sign, the key’s “Germany” or “the Emperor”, stands where the calendar names Archduke Charles.
  • Forbes’s copying slips: a single dot for a colon, 4 for e, ⊔ for π.

06 What remains uncertain

  • The hooked-T family of word signs (here, his, they, this, thing) is read from context; Forbes’s dots on it are inconsistent (C).
  • Unread word signs: ẓ, ï, C·, F̄ and the ḧ-like sign in 1 September passage 2.
  • Letters read only in part: 7 March 1563 (passages 2, 9, 10, 13, 14), 27 July 1563, Marseilles 13 November 1564 (3, 10), Arles 22 November 1564, 22 March 1566 (1, 2, 4, 10, 11, 15, 20, 25, 26). The 12 March 1563 letter is in Forbes and was not transcribed.
  • Marseilles (5): the key’s sign for Calais where the calendar has Dieppe.
  • Avignon passages 22–23 (“by litle and litle and by … great”) and 8–11 have no counterpart in the calendar summary (M).
