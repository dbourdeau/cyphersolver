01 The letters and the key

Claudia de’ Medici, widow of Archduke Leopold V and regent of Tyrol until her son came of age in 1646, wrote in 1646 to Ottavio Piccolomini, who then commanded for Philip IV in the Spanish Netherlands. Her letters are in the Piccolomini family archive at SOA Zámrsk, on a microfilm labelled “31/2”. Several 1646 letters on that film carry an interlinear decipherment made in the recipient’s office. Two do not: inv. 24793, one page with 16 cipher lines and 83 cipher words, and inv. 24800, dated in plain script “Inspruch a 25. di 7bre 1646” and signed Claudia, with 8 cipher lines and 33 cipher words.

The only images used are photographs of the microfilm frames in the appendix of Václav Vlnas’s 2017 thesis. They are rotated and soft. Vlnas lists both letters as not deciphered and gives no text for them. No edition or decipherment of them was found.

The cipher replaces each letter with a sign and keeps the word breaks. The key (key_frozen.tsv): 2 a, m b, 5 c, D d, r e, 3 f, l g, K h, g or y i, c l, 4 m, s n, + o, Q p, 7 q, d r, x s, 8 t, A u/v, I z. Code groups, each followed by a point: 18 Duca di Lorena, 34 S.A., 37 Re, 57 S.M.tà Catt., iio V.E. No sign for k, x or j was seen.

No image is shown on this page. The photographs are in the thesis appendix only; the signs above and below are typed as token names (SIGN_CONVENTIONS.md in the folder).

02 How the key was built

The key came from the glossed sibling letters. In inv. 24784 the office wrote the plaintext over 39 cipher words, and in inv. 24796 over three code groups (“del Re”, “di V.E.”, “con V.E.”). The 42 pairs are in pairs.tsv; the frozen key turns every one of them into its gloss letter for letter, and together they attest all 21 letter signs and all 5 code groups. The key also draws on the table Vlnas printed from inv. 24790 (his table 5), and agrees with it for every sign he lists.

The key was then frozen and tried on three partial lines of another glossed letter, inv. 24786 (9 Feb 1646), that had not been used to build it. Thirteen of 13 cipher words agree with the gloss in sense, 10 letter for letter; the other three are spellings (“communica”, “doppo” for “dopo”, “col” for “con il”). The test is small, the gloss was visible on the crops, and the order of freezing and testing rests on file times only.

03 Inv. 24793

Of 83 cipher words, 79 come verbatim from the signs (95.2%). One rests on a doubtful sign: in “forze” the fourth sign is an n-like form with a stroke over it, not the key’s z, and only z gives a word. Three carry a marked one-sign repair (“con”, “de’”, “dall’”). There are no holes. 79 of 83 (95.2%) is just over the 95% bar, but the sign copy was not made blind to the sense, and one more doubtful sign would put the letter under the bar, so it is kept as deciphered in part (75 of 79, 94.9%, without the code groups). Italian, cipher in normal type and plain script in italics:

… per suoi ch’ella accenna, dipende assai dall’operar di concerto e con presta risolutione, mentre le for[?z]e dell’armi catoliche bilanciano quelle de’ nemici. Così parmi che non si dovrebbe perder l’opportunità anco dal canto del Duca di Lorena, quando S.A. non havesse forsi qualche altri nascosti fini, retti delle sue passioni o dall’instabilità. Ha ben fatto prudentemente il Re ordinando a V.E. il fermarsi anche per questo anno in Fiandra, potendosi credere che i Francesi tentaranno ogni sforzo per proseguir imprese, contro quali sarà più che necessaria la di lei valorosa resistenza, nella quale consente Dio nostro Signore di concederle intiera vittoria. …

In English: success depends very much on acting in concert and with prompt resolution, while the forces of the Catholic arms balance those of the enemy; the opportunity should not be lost on the side of the Duke of Lorraine either, unless His Highness has hidden aims, ruled by his passions or his inconstancy; the King did well to order Piccolomini to stay in Flanders this year too, since the French will make every effort to press their enterprises; may God grant him complete victory. The plain script around the cipher was transcribed only roughly, for context.

04 Inv. 24800

Of 33 cipher words, 28 come verbatim from the signs (84.8%; 26 of 31 without the two code groups). Five carry marked repairs: a sign read as p in “per” whose loop is unclear, a cross read as m in “prudentemente”, one d added in “addosso” (the writer may mean “adosso”), and two changed signs in one phrase. The signs give indisciplinitelenti, read as “indisciplinate genti”; that phrase is a conjecture.

… che quantunque non molto buoni per gli interessi del Re, sono tuttavia da me tenuti … Mi dispiace non poco d’udir che le cose della Fiandra vadino così male, e che ne siano in gran parte cagione i disordini dell’[?indisciplinate] [?genti] del Duca di Lorena; perche potrebbono, come V.E. prudentemente discorre, tirarsi addosso anco maggior rovina. …

In English: the news, though not very good for the interests of the King, is held in due esteem; it grieves her to hear that affairs in Flanders go so badly, and that a great part of the cause lies in the disorders of the [?undisciplined] [?troops] of the Duke of Lorraine, which, as Piccolomini prudently observes, could bring still greater ruin.

05 Method and checks

Sign names were fixed first. Each bare frame was copied twice from separate crops and the two copies were reconciled on the image. The copy was not blind to the sense: the alphabet is small and the Italian showed through during the first pass. Where shape and sense disagree, the transcription keeps the shape and the reading marks the repair. A script in the folder (scripts/check_marks.py) rebuilds both decryptions from the transcriptions and the frozen key and fails on any unmarked departure.

An independent AI agent copied 13 lines of the bare frames and the three held-out passages blind (443 signs) before opening the solver’s files; after settling, three places differed. Its six requested fixes were made, and the “forze” sign was downgraded to doubtful, which moved 24793 under the bar (from complete to partial).

06 What remains open

  • 24793, “forze”: the fourth sign is not the key’s z shape; only z gives a word (doubtful sign).
  • 24793, “con”, “de’”, “dall’”: one changed sign each (an r-like sign for c, two g-like signs with no cross-bar for d).
  • 24800, “per”, “prudentemente”, “addosso”: one changed or added sign each.
  • 24800, “indisciplinate genti”: two changed signs in one phrase, a conjecture.
  • The day and month of 24793 are not on the frame, and the letter may continue on a page that is not among the photographs.
  • The held-out test is small; the other glossed sibling frames (inv. 24787, 24788, 24789, 24794, 24799, 24799_1 and 24806) would enlarge it.

Every one of these needs a clean scan of the frames from SOA Zámrsk; the online photographs have been used to their limit. No human palaeographer has checked any sign.

## Summary and context

Claudia de' Medici (1604-1648), regent of Tyrol for her son Ferdinand Charles, writes to Ottavio Piccolomini, who
in 1646 commanded for Philip IV in the Spanish Netherlands. The ciphered part comments on the 1646 campaign in
Flanders: success depends on concerted and quick action while the Catholic and enemy forces are even; the
opportunity should be used also with Charles IV, Duke of Lorraine (whose army served Spain), unless his hidden aims,
passions or inconstancy get in the way; the King of Spain was right to keep Piccolomini in Flanders for another year,
since the French will press their attacks. Code groups: 18 = Duca di Lorena, 34 = S.A. (here the Duke of Lorraine),
37 = Re, iio = V.E. (Piccolomini himself), all confirmed by glosses on sibling letters.

# Reading of inv. 24800 (Claudia de' Medici to Ottavio Piccolomini, Innsbruck, 25 September 1646)

## Marked text (line by line against the signs)

Marks: `{x/y}` the signs give x, read y; `{+a}` letter added; `[Name]` code group. Plain script in `<...>`.

```marked
L01: <... che quantunque non molto> buoni {a/p}er
L02: gli interessi del [Re] <sono tuttavia da me tenuti nella ...>
L03: <... Mi dispiace non poco> d'udir che le cose della Fiandra
L04: vadino così male <, e che> ne siano in gran parte
L05: cagione i disordini dell'indisciplin{i/a}te {l/g}en-
L06: ti del [Duca di Lorena] <; perche potrebbono, come V.E.> prudente-
```

Repairs: L01 the sign read as 2 (a) has a loop that may be the closed loop of Q (p) (transcribed `2/Q`); L07 a

two repairs in one phrase (y = i read as a; the c sign = l read as the tall sign = g). This is the weakest point of
the reading: the sense ("the undisciplined troops of the Duke of Lorraine") fits the context, but two changed signs
in one phrase are a conjecture, not a reading. Treat "indisciplinate genti" as doubtful.

## Summary and context

A short letter of thanks for news from Flanders, written in the weeks when the French under Enghien were besieging
Dunkirk (taken in early October 1646). The ciphered sentences say that the news is bad for the King of Spain, that affairs
in Flanders go badly, largely through the disorders of the Duke of Lorraine's troops, and that, as Piccolomini
himself wrote, this could lead to still greater ruin. Code groups: 37 = Re, 18 = Duca di Lorena.

# English translation, inv. 24800: Claudia de' Medici to Ottavio Piccolomini, Innsbruck, 25 September 1646

Translation of the Italian in `reading_24800.md`, with the same doubtful words in `[?...]`. The plain-script
passages around the cipher are read for context, not letter by letter.

With Your Excellency's letter of the 7th I receive the continuation of the news from those parts, which, although
not very good for the interests of the King, I nevertheless hold in due esteem, since they come to me from Your
Excellency's natural courtesy, for which I give you due thanks. It grieves me not a little to hear that affairs in
Flanders go so badly, and that a great part of the cause lies in the disorders of the [?undisciplined] [?troops] of the
Duke of Lorraine; for they could, as Your Excellency prudently observes, bring even greater ruin upon themselves.
May God our Lord be pleased to provide a timely remedy ... Innsbruck, 25 September 1646.
