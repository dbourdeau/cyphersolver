Reference · method · review

Method and review

What this project does, how a reading is made and checked, who checks it, and what the checks cannot show. It is written for a cryptologist, a palaeographer or an archivist who wants to judge a result without reading the code.

Daniel Bourdeau and contributors · Version française

01 What this project is, and is not

An open ledger of historical cipher texts worked with AI language models, and an informal measure of what those models can do. It is not a critical edition, and its results are not peer-reviewed. A reading published here is a proposal with its evidence attached: the transcription, the key and the reading are in the repository for anyone to check. Whether the unedited output of the key is kept apart from the reading depends on the date: the convention is new (16 November 2026), and the audit that prompted it could not compare 757 of 938 texts with their keys. Some readings are clear copies or glosses, which are labelled as such.

02 Where the texts come from

Each item is a document in an archive or library, cited with its holder, shelfmark and folio, and reached through public images (Gallica, the DECODE database, IIIF viewers and others). Before an item is treated as unread, earlier work is searched for and the result written down: the DECODE record, published lists of solved and unsolved ciphers, printed editions and calendars, and the literature. The rule is the catalogue vetting bar in the README. It has failed before: see the corrections.

03 Transcription

Many transcriptions are made by a model from the images, by one reader; the project does not count how many. There is no systematic second, blind transcription and no published agreement rate. In some targets the transcription and the key were frozen and timestamped before the reading was scored. Where a transcription was revised after the solver had run, the target’s notes should say so; not all do.

This is the weakest step. In November 2026 a check of one ten-page text against its images found that the first transcription had written one code for signs the manuscript keeps apart; 379 words of the transcription changed. In another target a file listed as a transcription had been generated from the reading.

04 How a key is obtained

Every page states one of the methods in the glossary. Three are cryptanalysis done here (from the ciphertext alone, from plaintext beside the cipher, from plaintext found elsewhere). Three apply a key or a decipherment that already existed. The home page counts the two families apart.

05 Decoded is not read

A document is in one of four states: read, read in part, decoded but not read, or not prose. It counts as read only when 95% of its words read as sense, measured on the reading file, and when it builds into sentences that a reader has read as sentences and that are translated into English. A word that is unread, guessed or supplied is a hole. Holes are counted by words.

The output of the key and the reading are two files. The decryption is the key applied to the transcription with nothing edited. The reading is that text made into sentences, and every letter or word in it that the key does not give must be marked. This rule was written down on 16 November 2026. An audit of the 54 targets its script flagged found words departing from the key, without a mark, in 35 of them. Marks were added in 31 targets (858 places; in a companion file where the reading is a contributor’s); texts that are a clear copy or a gloss are labelled instead. Some departures carry no mark: about 60 dropped letters, image 324 of bonde1676 (header line only) and R1954 of kauderbach1754. Counting marks as holes, eight texts classed as read now measure more than 5% holes; they have not been reclassed. The script could not compare 757 of 938 texts with their keys, so other readings may carry unmarked changes. The audit is in research/decrypt_audit.

06 Statistical checks against false positives

For a key recovered by cryptanalysis, the reading is scored with a period language model and compared with two nulls: random keys of the same structure, and keys fitted to the same ciphertext shuffled (the overfitting test). A result passes when it is at least 5 standard deviations above the random keys, above every fitted key, and at least 3 standard deviations above their mean. The test also measures its own power and can answer “too short” or “underdetermined”.

Verdicts on record: 166 pass, 99 not applicable, 24 underdetermined, 23 weak, 13 too short, 3 fail. Of 317 targets whose key was recovered by cryptanalysis and whose text is complete or partial, 252 carry this test; the others are mostly contributed results. What the test cannot show: it says the key yields language, not that each word is right. It does not cover code values filled in from context, it has no correction for many tests, and it has not been reviewed by a statistician outside the project.

07 Review

Each reading is graded A or B by a reader. The class of a reviewer comes from the name written in the review’s by field. Of 1,429 reading reviews on record, 1,344 name a model as the reader, 53 a person with a model (often the contributor of the reading), and 7 carry only a person’s name; 25 do not say. A bare name does not show that no model was used: of those 7, only one states that the person checked by eye against the images.

On independence: 82 reviews say in their own text that the reader was not independent of the work, and 85 say that it was. For the other 1,262 the record does not say, and 617 read only a model name such as “Claude Opus 5.5”. Some name the author’s own agents or session. The page cannot show that most reviews were a second reader; it can only show that a model read the text again, often within the same project or session, together with automated checks. It is not a human review, and it is not peer review.

A person has checked a passage against the image without any model, on record, in 10 targets. The home page counts more (a person’s name on a review of one text also counts there). These checks cover single signs, digits or lines, not whole readings.

The repository rule (CONTRIBUTING, “Review and merging”) is that two maintainers review and merge; a change to a result, such as a reading, a key or a write-up, needs a maintainer who is not its author, and a reading merges only with a review graded A or B recorded by the reviewer, not by the reading’s author. A ruleset on the main branch enforces part of this, but the repository’s administrator and the site’s rebuild job (through a deploy key) can push to main without a pull request, and the owner has pushed directly, so the rule does not bind every change. Readings waiting for an independent check are listed on the contributors page.

08 The role of AI

Each target’s record has fields for the models used and what the person did; some hold “unknown”. Steps done by a model, such as transcribing from an image or proposing a code value from context, cannot be replayed exactly, and the models are commercial ones. The scripts, keys and transcriptions are versioned and can be rerun. Cost is not recorded: the project publishes no figure for tokens, money or hours.

09 Credit and prior work

An earlier decipherment or key is credited by name on the page, also when it is found after the work was done; the result is then classed as read from an existing decipherment or with a known key. Contributors are credited for what they did. Text is released under CC BY 4.0; images stay under their holders’ terms and are credited one by one.

10 Every reading stays open to correction

To contest or check a reading, use the link at the foot of its page, the verification form (it needs a GitHub account), or email. Say which passage and which images, and where your reading differs. No response time is promised. A correction is made on the page with its date and entered in the corrections log (19 entries).

11 For researchers

Cryptologists, palaeographers, archivists and historians are invited to check a reading, to take on a series, or to co-sign a page. A checker is named on a page only with that person’s agreement, with what was checked and when. The project would rather be told that a reading is wrong than have it stand.

12 How to cite

Every page has “Cite this page” with the date it was posted and last changed and the repository commit it was built from. Pages change; cite the commit. There is no DOI yet.
