Method and review

What this project does, how a reading is made and checked, who checks it, and what the checks cannot show. It is written for a cryptologist, a palaeographer or an archivist who wants to judge a result without reading the code.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 16 November 2026 · Version française

01 What this project is, and is not

An open ledger of historical cipher texts worked with AI language models, and an informal measure of what those models can do. It is not a critical edition, and its results are not peer-reviewed. A reading published here is a proposal with its evidence attached: the transcription, the key, the unedited output of the key and the reading are all in the repository for anyone to check.

02 Where the texts come from

Each item is a document in an archive or library, cited with its holder, shelfmark and folio, and reached through public images (Gallica, the DECODE database, IIIF viewers and others). Before an item is treated as unread, earlier work is searched for and the result written down: the DECODE record, published lists of solved and unsolved ciphers, printed editions and calendars, and the literature. The rule is the catalogue vetting bar in the README. It has failed before: see the corrections.

03 Transcription

Most transcriptions are made by a model from the images, by one reader. There is no systematic second, blind transcription and no published agreement rate. In some targets the transcription and the key were frozen and timestamped before the reading was scored. Where a transcription was revised after the solver had run, the target’s notes should say so; not all do.

This is the weakest step. In November 2026 a check of one ten-page text against its images found that the first transcription had written one code for signs the manuscript keeps apart; 379 words of the transcription changed. In another target a file listed as a transcription had been generated from the reading.

04 How a key is obtained

Every page states one of the methods in the glossary. Three are cryptanalysis done here (from the ciphertext alone, from plaintext beside the cipher, from plaintext found elsewhere). Three apply a key or a decipherment that already existed. The home page counts the two families apart.

05 Decoded is not read

A document is in one of four states: read, read in part, decoded but not read, or not prose. It counts as read only when 95% of its words read as sense, measured on the reading file, and when it builds into sentences that a reader has read as sentences and that are translated into English. A word that is unread, guessed or supplied is a hole. Holes are counted by words.

The output of the key and the reading are two files. The decryption is the key applied to the transcription with nothing edited. The reading is that text made into sentences, and every letter or word in it that the key does not give must be marked. This rule was written down on 16 November 2026, after an audit found unmarked changes in 35 of 54 readings examined; they are now marked, and the audit is in research/decrypt_audit. Readings not yet examined may still carry unmarked changes.

06 Statistical checks against false positives

For a key recovered by cryptanalysis, the reading is scored with a period language model and compared with two nulls: random keys of the same structure, and keys fitted to the same ciphertext shuffled (the overfitting test). A result passes when it is at least 5 standard deviations above the random keys, above every fitted key, and at least 3 standard deviations above their mean. The test also measures its own power and can answer “too short” or “underdetermined”.

Verdicts on record: 165 pass, 99 not applicable, 24 underdetermined, 23 weak, 13 too short, 3 fail. Of 316 readings whose key was recovered by cryptanalysis, 251 carry this test; the others are mostly contributed results. What the test cannot show: it says the key yields language, not that each word is right. It does not cover code values filled in from context, it has no correction for many tests, and it has not been reviewed by a statistician outside the project.

07 Review

Each reading is graded A or B by a reader. Of 1,426 reading reviews on record, 1,341 were made by a model, 53 by a person working with a model and 7 by a person alone; 25 do not say. 82 say of themselves that the reader was not independent of the work. A person has checked a passage against the image without any model in 10 targets.

So the review of a reading is, in almost every case, a second model reading together with automated checks. It is not a human review, and it is not peer review. Changes from contributors arrive as pull requests that the maintainer approves; the maintainer and the site’s rebuild job can also change the main branch directly, so this is not a two-person rule. Readings waiting for an independent check are listed on the contributors page.

08 The role of AI

Each target’s record names the models used and what the person did. Steps done by a model, such as transcribing from an image or proposing a code value from context, cannot be replayed exactly, and the models are commercial ones. The scripts, keys and transcriptions are versioned and can be rerun. Cost is not recorded: the project publishes no figure for tokens, money or hours.

09 Credit and prior work

An earlier decipherment or key is credited by name on the page, also when it is found after the work was done; the result is then classed as read from an existing decipherment or with a known key. Contributors are credited for what they did. Text is released under CC BY 4.0; images stay under their holders’ terms and are credited one by one.

10 Every reading stays open to correction

To contest or check a reading, use the link at the foot of its page, the verification form (it needs a GitHub account), or email. Say which passage and which images, and where your reading differs. No response time is promised. A correction is made on the page with its date and entered in the corrections log (15 entries).

11 For researchers

Cryptologists, palaeographers, archivists and historians are invited to check a reading, to take on a series, or to co-sign a page. A checker is named on a page only with that person’s written agreement, with what was checked and when. The project would rather be told that a reading is wrong than have it stand.

12 How to cite

Every page has “Cite this page” with the date it was posted and last changed and the repository commit it was built from. Pages change; cite the commit. There is no DOI yet.

The rules in full: README, Conventions · CONTRIBUTING · the record schema · glossary. Figures on this page are counted from the records each time the site is built.
