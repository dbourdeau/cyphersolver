Carl Pawel → Frederick V · two-digit letter cipher with homophones and a code · 25 November 1624 · key identified from the period glosses

Carl Pawel to Frederick V, 25 November 1624

Go to the transcriptions ↓

A Palatine agent reports to the exiled King of Bohemia on a colonel’s recruiting, in a cipher deciphered at the time; its letter key turns out to be a sibling’s alphabet shifted by three.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 6 November 2026

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: complete

The letter
Carl Pawel to Frederick V, King of Bohemia and Elector Palatine in exile, “den 25 Novemb. 1624”, signed “741 / Carl Pawel”: a report on Colonel Fuchs’s recruiting in Württemberg.
Where
Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich, Clm 10382 (Collectio Camerariana 32), n. 46, digitised microfilm MDZ bsb00112117, scans 181–182. Halm (1874) dates it 15 November. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
Three systems mixed: a code of three- and four-digit numbers (2737 Ob. Fuchs, 3515 Stuttgart, 1732 Mansfelt, 2389 Liga), a few signs (a3 = zu, mm = so, rr = und), and runs of two-digit numbers that spell words, three numbers per letter.
How the key was found
The letter runs use the alphabet of the Johann Casimir–Camerarius letters with every number lowered by three. The shift was fitted on the glosses of scan 181 (0.921 of the letters, next best 0.737), frozen, and measured once on scan 182: 13 of 13 letters against 0.154 for the best shuffled key.
What it says
Fuchs came to Stuttgart and stayed there, accompanied; the letter speaks of the “pars victrix”, of someone who rode back, of Mansfeld and the League. The content is the period decipherment’s, not new.
Still open
The code groups and the clear German text are not transcribed; the held-out part is only 13 letters.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée) transcribed the runs, identified and tested the key, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents. The decipherment is the clerk’s of 1624.

01 The letter

In the autumn of 1624 Frederick V, driven from Bohemia and the Palatinate, lived in exile at The Hague, and his agents reported on the armies being raised in the Empire. Carl Pawel’s report concerns Colonel Fuchs’s recruiting in Württemberg. It is part of the Camerarius collection in Munich, the papers of Ludwig Camerarius, Frederick’s councillor.

The letter mixes clear German, a numerical code for names and common words, a few signs, and runs of two-digit numbers separated by commas that spell words letter by letter. A decipherment written above the lines at the time covers the code and the runs.

02 The key and its test

The runs use the same letters, in the same order and with three homophones per letter in blocks of consecutive numbers, as the Johann Casimir–Camerarius cipher of the same volume; only the origin differs. Starting from the casimir1629 key as it was frozen for that target, one parameter was fitted on the glossed runs of scan 181: the shift that best renders the glosses. A shift of 3 renders 0.921 of the letters; the next best, 4, renders 0.737, and the 22 others 0.42 or less. The shifted key was frozen before scan 182 was looked at.

Scan 182 has only two letter runs, degen and brauchen: 13 letters, all rendered by the frozen key, against 0.154 for the best of 200 permuted keys. The held-out part is small, so the stronger evidence is the whole letter: over all 17 runs (102 letters), the only differences from the glosses are the clerk’s spellings (vorharret, gunnen, scaf, uf), a gloss written victix for victrix, and an added h in a gloss (rückh). One unglossed run reads geritten.

03 What remains uncertain

  • The held-out test rests on 13 letters.
  • Numbers 1–10, 53–55 and 83 and up were not seen; by the casimir1629 key, 83 and up would be nulls.
  • Two runs (181.14, 181.15) were re-read at high magnification after the measurement and now agree with their glosses; the frozen key and the measurement are unchanged.
  • The code groups and the clear German text are not transcribed, so the runs are given as a word list with their glosses, not as a letter.
  • One reader (an agent) transcribed from a 1,334-pixel microfilm; no human has checked the numbers against the images.

n. 46 (scans 181-182): letter runs with their contemporary glosses not prose

Carl Pawel reports to Frederick V on Colonel Fuchs's recruiting in Württemberg: Fuchs came to Stuttgart and stayed until Tuesday the 23rd, accompanied; the letter speaks of the 'pars victrix', of someone who 'rode back', of Mansfeld and the League, and of men who could be used.

Clm 10419 (scans 131, 181, 225, 283-284): letter runs with their contemporary glosses not prose

Andreas Pawel sends Rusdorf news in French: Nancy, Spanish alarm, the King of England's war and Parliament, the Palatinate, Count Digby's recall (scan 131); the Duke of Bavaria and the Electoral College, Mainz, Dresden, the Papists (scan 181); on scan 284, a setback ('l'eschec') in January, resources, 'diplomata', something 'irreconcilable', the Swiss and the peace, troops on foot and on horse, and the King of England 'did the same'. Drawn from the glosses and the legible clear text.
