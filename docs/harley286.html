Sir Simonds D’Ewes’s cipher log of his son’s fits, 1635–36

British Library, Harley MS 286 f. 61 (DECODE R7759, “unknown sender to unknown recipient, 1635”): a small slip, one line for each convulsion fit of the antiquary’s infant son Clopton, written in D’Ewes’s private alphabet. No reading was known; the alphabet was rebuilt from the slip by crib and the log read.

Summary. DECODE lists R7759 as a one-page non-decrypted letter of 1635 from an unknown sender to an unknown recipient, cipher type unknown. The British Library’s own catalogue describes the leaf as “a short paper written by Sir Simonds D’Ewes, in his cipher”, though it files it under Harley MS 289 rather than 286; which shelfmark is right is not settled here. It is not a letter. It is D’Ewes’s private record, in Latin, of the convulsion fits of his son Clopton, born July 1634, beginning “Aprilis iii. mane circa horam nonam convulsionem habuit. die Veneris” and running, one fit to a line, into January 1636. The alphabet is a simple substitution D’Ewes invented at school; no key to it is printed, so it was rebuilt from the slip by crib on the recurring words convulsio, plena luna and difficilis. The dates check against the calendar: every weekday named matches the Julian date, most moon phases match, and D’Ewes’s Autobiography gives the same start, 3 April 1635, and a total of forty-four fits to 4 March 1636. The child died on 9 May 1636. 277 of 287 words read (96.5%, measured); open are the last line, trimmed under the mount, one word at the edge of line 13 and a faint phrase in lines 24–25.

01 The slip and its writer

Sir Simonds D’Ewes (1602–1650), the parliamentary antiquary whose manuscripts make up much of the Harleian collection, wrote his private papers in an alphabet of his own. His diary of 1622–24 (Harley MS 481) is partly in it, and a 1915 University of Minnesota thesis deciphered that diary without printing the key. Folio 61 is a small slip mounted on a larger leaf, headed 1635 in ordinary figures, with the margin dates 1635/1636 in clear beside the January entry and a running count in cipher, huc usque xxxii, “thus far 32”, at the end of the August entries. A rule separates the summer entries from those of September and January. The foot of the slip runs under the mount and its last line is cut.

02 The alphabet

One sign per letter, words divided, numerals written with the letter signs (x, v, i as ξ, S, r). Several signs are ordinary letters used for other values, which is what makes the text look like garbled Latin script at first sight.

The Autobiography gives the end: the fits came more thickly in the spring of 1636, twelve in one night on 8–9 May, and the child died about two in the afternoon of Monday 9 May 1636, a year and nine months old. D’Ewes blamed “a proud, fretting, ill-conditioned woman for a nurse”, an issue opened in the child’s neck and the violent remedies of Dr Despotine of Bury, and the rickets that followed.

04 Method

  1. The images of R7759 were fetched from DECODE; the second image is only the verso showing through. A search of the BL catalogue named D’Ewes.
  2. No key to D’Ewes’s alphabet was found in print: the Minnesota thesis on his diary and Notestein’s 1923 edition of his parliamentary journal mention the cipher but give no table.
  3. Crib: a long word recurring on almost every line, <oηSSθ2ro, fits convulsio; a two-word phrase 9θcηd θSηd fits plena luna; Xrφφr<rθr2 fits difficilis. The month names and numerals at the start of each line supplied the rest.
  4. Checks against the calendar: 3 April 1635 was a Friday (die Veneris), 23 April a Thursday (die Iovis), 23 May a Saturday (die Saturni). The moon was full on 23 April, 19 July and 18 August and new on 2 August, as the slip says. On 25 May (“plena luna”) and 12 June (“decrescente”) it was not; the numerals are unambiguous at high magnification, so these are D’Ewes’s own slips or loose wording.
  5. The Autobiography (Halliwell’s edition, 1845) confirmed the start on 3 April 1635 and the count of forty-four fits.
  6. Two full transcription passes over line crops: 92.7% of words read after the first, 96.5% after re-cropping the doubtful words at three times the size.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Line 30, the last line (about six words): trimmed under the mount; only “…am nonam” shows. Needs the leaf lifted.
  • Line 13, last word 9oθS∂- (p/g, o, l, u, a): cramped at the edge, unread.
  • Lines 24–25, sed an 2∂c<- / Scθ: faint and cramped; septima, pede and nativo in the same lines are doubtful (grade M).
  • Doubtful words elsewhere (grade C): ix and tum (line 3), the suspension after luna (line 6), mitis (line 7), tum (line 11), vexit (line 14), absolutus (line 24).
  • The shelfmark: DECODE says Harley MS 286, the BL catalogue Harley MS 289. Not resolved.
