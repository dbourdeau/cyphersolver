“Ex Conde” to Diego José Dormer, 1693–1696

Go to the full text ↓

Unnamed informants in the Catalan war send the Aragonese chronicler a cipher slip on the muster at Gerona and short ciphered phrases blaming the generals, read with a key recovered from the ciphertext.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 6 November 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: partial (92% of the cipher)

Contributed by Brice Dudout (solution)

The letters
Letters signed “Ex Conde”, “El Conde” or with pseudonyms (“Rojo”, “Zabulón”, “El Chismoso”) from Barcelona, Mataró, Hostalric and Zaragoza, 1693–1696, to Diego José Dormer, and a slip wholly in cipher. The writers are not identified.
Where
Biblioteca Nacional de España, MSS/8387, Cartas políticas y particulares dirigidas a Diego José Dormer, vol. VI (BDH bdh0000064262), views 132–197. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A simple substitution, one number from 1 to 25 for each letter, written with dots between the numbers. The alphabet runs in two pieces: a–h downward from 8, i–z upward from 11.
How it was deciphered
The key was recovered on the short passages of views 132–168 from frequencies and cribs suggested by the clear text around them, then frozen and measured on two held-out sets: 0.577 and 0.594 against 0.033 and 0.042 for shuffled keys, under the 0.60 threshold, so the key is partial.
What it says
The muster at Gerona held under a proclamation of death, but leaving out the sick and the new cavalry, “all to reduce the numbers so as not to march out of Gerona”; a night with no generals and no rounds; the viceroy Villena; later, “I am an enemy of Bavaria”.
Still open
The verso of the slip loses every line end in the binding (79.6% of its visible words). Doubtful signs at 120 ppi, three unseen numbers, and the identity of the writers.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée) found the cipher in the volume, recovered the key, transcribed, read and translated the pieces, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents. The volume came from Daniel Bourdeau’s BNE sweep (catalogue 1272).

01 The letters

Diego José Dormer (c. 1640–1705) was chronicler of Aragon, and his correspondence fills several volumes at the Biblioteca Nacional. In vol. VI, 26 of 269 views carry cipher: one line of geometric signs on view 10, which belongs to another system and stays open in the catalogue, and views 132–197, all in one number cipher. These are letters of the Nine Years’ War in Catalonia, when the French took Gerona and Palamós in 1694 and Barcelona fell in 1697.

Two documents are worked: a slip wholly in cipher, fol. 190r–v (974 signs, undated, before June 1694 since Palamós is still in Spanish hands), and the ciphered words and phrases inside letters in clear (1,735 signs). Three web searches found no source naming Dormer’s correspondents in Catalonia in these years, so the writers are left unnamed.

02 The key and its test

The values are 1 f, 2 g, 3 e, 5 d, 6 c, 7 b, 8 a, 9 h, 11 i, 13 l, 14 m, 15 n, 16 o, 17 p, 18 q, 19 r, 20 s, 21 t, 22 u, 23 x, 24 y, 25 z; 4, 10 and 12 never occur and are given no value. The key was built on the training passages of views 132–168 only: frequencies, a short list of cribs fixed in advance from the clear text around the passages (“amigo” before “Dormer”, “Cataluña”, “guardias”, “caballo”, “Villena”), then annealing on Spanish quadgrams constrained by those cribs. It was frozen before either held-out set was decoded.

With no decipherment of the time, the measure is the share of the decoded letters that Spanish words of five letters or more cover. On the slip (974 signs) the frozen key scores 0.577 against 0.033 for the best of 200 shuffled keys; on the passages of views 169–193 (261 signs) it scores 0.594 against 0.042. Both clear the margin by far and both miss the absolute threshold of 0.60. The slip’s verso, cut in the binding, and the very short passages of the second set pull the measure down; nothing was changed for them.

03 The reading

The readings were made after the measurements and counted by script, cipher words only. The slip’s recto reads at 98.5% (133 words), its verso at 79.6% of the visible words (103), and the held-out passages of views 169–193 at 98.1% (54 words). In all, 290 words: 255 certain, 11 probable, 16 restored and 8 unresolved, 91.7% without the restored words. The training passages decode to Spanish as well but are not counted, because the key was learnt on them.

The slip tells of the muster at Gerona, held under a proclamation of death and twelve doubloons to informers, which left out the sick and the newly arrived cavalry and did not merge the officerless troops into the old companies, “all to reduce the numbers so as not to march out of Gerona”; 2,562 foot were found inside Gerona and 1,500 could be drawn from Castellfollit and Palamós. The passages speak of a night with no generals and no rounds and the cavalry caught unsaddled (“El Chismoso”, 3 May 1694, before the battle of the Ter), the viceroy Villena, a deposit of money at the Treasury, and later of Montalto put at the head. The Spanish and an English translation are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • The slip’s verso: the end of all 24 lines is hidden in the binding; 14 words are restored and 7 unresolved.
  • View 193: one word restored ([s]e before Villena).
  • Doubtful signs at 120 ppi (15/19, 17/19, 5/13) are settled by the sense in the readings only.
  • Numbers 4, 10 and 12 never occur.
  • The writers and the pseudonymous addressees (“Zacarías”, “Astulio”) are not identified; the other Dormer volumes (MSS/8382–8386) were not opened.
  • No human has checked the transcription against the images.
