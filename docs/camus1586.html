Castets, Bordeaux, La Rochelle → Villeroy · homophonic letter cipher with word signs · March–June 1586 · read from existing decipherment, partial

Camus de Pontcarré to Villeroy, 1586

A royal agent's secret channel to an unnamed contact during the Guyenne campaign of 1586; three cipher blocks deciphered completely from the margin decipherments, checked against the corrected key.

At a glanceRead from existing decipherment · partial · 3 of 9 blocks read, 2 in part

The letters
Claude Camus de Pontcarré to Villeroy, the King's secretary of state, from Castets (21 March and 1 April 1586), Bordeaux (early and 12 April) and La Rochelle (June). Clear letters with cipher blocks.
Where
Bibliothèque nationale de France, fr. 15572 ff. 111v, 112r, 132v, 134v, 151r, 272r–v (Gallica). Not in DECODE or the catalogue.
The cipher
Invented and Latin-like signs, several for each letter, some signs with two or three values, word signs for de, la, le, qui/que, et, pour/par, vous, car, and nulls.
How it was deciphered
The cipher was deciphered at the time in the margins of ff. 111v–112r, 134v and 151r. Michael Brackx transcribed every block and applied Tomokiyo's key table, which he corrected against those decipherments; the readings follow the margin where it fits the figures and the figures where they differ.
What it says
Camus's carrier to “le parent” can no longer help; Villeroy is to send separate ciphered packets, with code names for the carrier (“le beau frere”) and for Camus (“le seruiteur”).
Still open
ff. 132v, 134v block A, the f. 151r margin and foot blocks and ff. 272r–v give phrases between gaps; 57 rare signs have no value.
Credit
Transcription, corrected key and readings: Michael Brackx (@brackxm) with Claude Code, 22–23 October 2026 (issue #91). Key table: Satoshi Tomokiyo, Cryptiana.

The same volume holds Matignon's and Mayenne's despatches in other ciphers (Mayenne–Forget) and Malicorne's letter of 21 April 1586 (Malicorne 1586), a different cipher with its own page.

The figures are the share of words read as sense, holes and doubtful words counted against it. Satoshi Tomokiyo's Cryptiana lists these leaves under “Camus's Cipher” with a key table and one opening phrase, but gives no reading of the blocks.

02 The key

Brackx corrected Tomokiyo's table where the margin decipherments and contact sheets of every sign required it: the sign 6 is c (and g), the plain round e is r and the looped e s, a circled G is g, ŋ is o (also l and u), φ stands for ff, f or mm, a large crossed L with a loop (Ⱡ) is the word et, ‡ is b, ɱ is l, and ʒo is the word vous. Each change rests on cribs, or on two contexts and a contact sheet. The plain 9 and ŋ still carry three values each. The label legend, with each sign's shape, is in the repository.

03 The reading

In the writer's spelling, without added accents; [?] an unread word, [?word] doubtful. Where the figures and the margin differ, the figures are followed.

Commandez ce que iugerez pour le mieux et pour le seruice du sieur que scauez, et serez obey et serui ainsi que le prescrirez, car ie uous [?] [?] [?]

Command what you judge best, and for the service of the lord you know of, and you will be obeyed and served as you prescribe, for I [?] you [?] [?]

04 How it was done

  • Transcription. Every block sign by sign from full-size line strips, with each sign's position; the margin decipherments and glosses re-read at full zoom. A blind re-transcription of the cribbed blocks agreed on 76–95% of signs; 22 changes were accepted on the image.
  • Decoding. A search over each sign's allowed values under a French letter model, scored against the margin texts. Agreement with the margin: 84.3% (f. 111v), 90.5% (f. 112r), 73.6% and 93.4% (f. 134v B, C), 90.1% (f. 151r), against 31–42% for shuffled keys.
  • A correction. The French word list behind the coverage figure had mixed in an Italian and a Middle English text. Rebuilt from Montaigne's Essais I–III, coverage over all blocks is 74.6% (79.3% as first posted), against 36–50% for shuffled keys. The margin agreements do not use it.
  • Measured here. Words read as sense per block, counting every hole, doubtful and supplied word against it: 0.961, 0.974 and 0.963 for the three complete blocks; 0.781 and 0.812 for f. 134v B and C. Reviewed as sentences, 24 October 2026.

05 What remains

  • f. 111v: the three groups after car ie uous, which run past the margin text.
  • f. 134v B: the name after sauue (the margin's C?main does not match the figures), and doubtful sur, Frenac, peult, on; C: the last group.
  • ff. 132v, 134v A, the f. 151r margin and foot blocks, ff. 272r–v: phrases between gaps, with no decipherment of the time to check them (only a paraphrase for 134v A and the margin block). A copy of these letters, or a split of the plain 9 and ŋ at higher resolution, is needed.
  • 57 rare signs (108 occurrences), probably codes, among them the sign for cy faict on f. 134v A.
