Vienna → The Hague · Court Chancellery nomenclator R5017 · 3 February 1720

Charles VI to Count Windischgrätz, 3 February 1720

The Emperor’s own letter to his envoy at The Hague, with its secret passages in the Court Chancellery’s nomenclator, deciphered with that key, which survives in the same family archive.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 19 September 2026 · updated 15 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: complete

Summary. DECODE catalogues the letter of 3 February 1720 from the Emperor Charles VI to Count Leopold Viktorin Windischgrätz, his envoy at The Hague, as two records (the autograph R5019 and the family’s fair copy R5020), with no decipherment. The record names the key: R5017, the old key with the secret Court Chancellery. It was transcribed in full from the DECODE images and applied as it stands, with two slips corrected; every cipher passage decrypts to German that fits the cleartext around it. With Alberoni just expelled from Spain, the Emperor fears France may make a separate peace with “the Duc d’Anjou” (Philip V) through Morville. Windischgrätz is to confide in Cadogan, cultivate Portugal and bring it into the Alliance, and refuse any change to the treaty; Sardinia for the Duke of Lorraine is “sehr ideal”, and the Emperor insists on the equivalent for Montferrat. The letter announces a new private key; the later letters, in that key (R5018), have their own page: Charles VI to Windischgrätz, 1720–22, in the private key. Jakub Mírka described the letters and keys in 2012 but printed no decipherment. Extent: all 26 passages decrypted; two nomenclator cells are uncertain.

01 The letter and its key

The letters are autograph notes that Charles VI sent through his Oberststallmeister, outside the Chancellery’s ordinary despatches. The family later copied them into a register (book 5 of inv. 5), numbering them No. 1 to No. 7. No. 1, of 3 February 1720, survives as the autograph (DECODE R5019) and the copy (R5020, pp. 401–404); its cipher is 26 passages, about 190 groups.

R5017 is headed Ziffer Schlüssel mit der geheimen Hof-Canzley. It is the deciphering half of a two-part nomenclator. The numbers 1–400 are a scrambled syllabary of letters, bigrams and a few trigrams (schli, stre, spra), plus nulls (errans), punctuation and the controls annulla praecedentia / sequentia. The numbers 401–977 are words and names in alphabetical order, from Aachen to zu: 458 Cadagan, 513 Duc d’Anjou, 657 Mr. Moroille, 749 D. Orleans, 814 Robethon, 819 Stanhop. In this letter the Emperor writes that “den Ziffer, so ihr mit mir zu brauchen” would follow: the private key R5018, used for the later letters, a different cipher with its own page.

02 No. 1, 3 February 1720: Alberoni, France and the peace terms

The letter is two-thirds clear German, written in Charles’s own phonetic hand. The cipher sits inside the sentences, as in “absonderlich 89 312 251 65 14 312 20 48 96 genau berichten” = den Pentenrider. The autograph and the copy agree group for group. The 26 passages, in order:

So the secret instructions are these. Keep Pentenriedter fully informed, confide in the British envoy Cadogan, and show France and the Regent no mistrust. Watch them all the same: now that Alberoni has fallen, France may look for a separate peace with Philip V (whom Vienna still called only “the Duc d’Anjou”) through Morville, the French minister at The Hague. Find out whether Orléans means to make one. Win over the Portuguese envoy and bring his king into the Alliance. On the peace terms, nothing in the Quadruple Alliance treaty is to change: Sardinia for Lorraine is impracticable, the Montferrat equivalent is owed, and the costs of the coming peace must not fall on the Emperor.

03 Method

The records were fetched from DECODE with the project’s logged-in cookie. The R5017 syllabary (1–400) was transcribed column by column from the full-resolution scan. The nomenclator (401–999) was transcribed from tiled crops, with a confidence mark on each cell. The ciphertext of No. 1 was taken from the copy, checked against the autograph, and decoded by a short script (dec.py). Two slips in the key show up in use. Cell 31 is written “el”, but all three times it occurs it must mean zu, which is cell 3. Cell 268 is written “em” and must be ein.

04 What remains uncertain

  • No. 1, passage N: cell 446, whose key entry was transcribed as “Bischoff”, does not fit. The sense of the passage (measures and an accession obstructed by Spain) is C.
  • No. 1, passage X: cell 638 is uncertain (“Kommen” or “Kammern” in the key). Graded C.
  • No. 1, H: “Daroqui” as Count Tarouca, the Portuguese envoy, is M. The syllables are certain, and the next passage names Portugal.
  • All other passages are H.
