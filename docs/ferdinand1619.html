01 The record, and what was known about it

Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 is the Bavarian ducal chancery’s collection of cipher keys, running from the 1530s to about 1620; DECODE has 102 key records from it (R9277–R9423), with a scatter of ciphertexts bound in among the keys. Two of those ciphertexts are dated 1619 and lie at the very end of the volume:

R9426’s record gives “Author: …”, no sender, no receiver, no origin city, no plaintext language, cipher type “Unknown”, symbol set “Numerical”. The images are behind a login and carry the archive’s reservation (“The image is not in the public domain. Eleven images cover ten pages: image 7 is a second photograph of the page on image 6, and pages 3 to 10 were photographed upside down.

The last page is not in cipher. It closes:

… und E. L. hierbei zu Bezeigung aller angenehmer freundbrüderlicher Dienste jederzeit geneigt. Geben in meiner Statt Bonn den 22. Decembris Anno 1619.
… williger Bruder und Diener — Ferdinand

Bonn was the Cologne elector’s residence. Ferdinand of Bavaria (1577–1650) had been Elector-Archbishop of Cologne since 1612 and held Paderborn, Münster, Hildesheim and Liège with it; Maximilian I, head of the Catholic League, was his elder brother, which is what “herr Schwager” and “freundbrüderlich” in the clear passages, and the letter’s presence in the Munich archive, together establish. The ciphered text confirms the identification from the inside: the writer speaks of mein Thumb[capitel], meine Landtstendt, meinem … Ertz[stift], of calling meinen Landtag, and of Paderborn: an ecclesiastical prince with a cathedral chapter, territorial estates and a diet to summon.

02 The cipher

Every cipher group is a number written between dots. The letters are the even two-digit numbers, in two series; the odd two-digit numbers and the three-digit numbers are the nomenclator, so the two can never be confused.

abcdef
101214161820
ghilmnopqrstuxz
303234363840424446485052545658

and a second series that supplies the homophones and the two letters the first series omits: a 60, 70 · e 62, 72 · i 64 · o 66, 76 · u 68 · k 74 · w 78. So each vowel has two or three equivalents and each consonant one. The values 22 to 28 never occur. Two symbols recur at every word boundary and carry no letter value; they are the word division. Single cipher letters also stand alone as initials, so that “E. L.” (Euer Liebden, the form the brothers use for one another) is written 18 . 36.

The design is conservative for 1619: an alphabet in order, with homophones only on the vowels, is a sixteenth-century habit, and it is what lets the key be recovered from 2,500 letters. The nomenclator covers what the alphabet does not: the groups stand exactly where a name, a title or an office belongs.

03 How the key was recovered

There was no key and no crib. The ten pages were transcribed line by line, with the clear passages bracketed and the cipher copied as written. A homophonic solver (simulated annealing) then searched for the assignment of numbers to letters that maximises the probability of the whole text under the repository’s de-1500s model, a five-gram character model built on German printed and chancery prose of 1470–1610. Six restarts over the first three pages (932 letters) were already producing German: gelegenen, Landtag ausschreiben, Quartiermeister. Ten restarts over all ten pages (2,482 letters) fixed the table, and the decryption gave continuous German.

What remained were transcription errors. This clerk’s 0 and 6, 3 and 5, 1 and 4, 2 and 7 are nearly the same stroke, and a mis-transcribed digit turns one letter into another. The decoder flagged every word the language model scores poorly, a repair pass proposed single-token substitutions restricted to those confusable digit pairs, and about sixty were accepted. They are corrections of the transcription, never of the key, and a sample of them was checked again on the images at full zoom (catolischen, einzuquartiern, wolermelt, Erwitte).

The control. The key was then applied, without changing a single value, to the neighbouring record R9425, a different letter of the same correspondence that this work had not touched. Its first cipher line is

60·36·12·48·18·14·32·52   54·42·40   30·66·52·52·18·50   30·40·10·16·18·40
→ “Albrecht von Gottes Gnaden”

This is Archduke Albert of Austria, sovereign of the Spanish Netherlands, whose troops are the subject of that letter. A key recovered from one text and applied unchanged to another that gives a correct opening formula and a correct name is the strongest check available here. On 2 October 2026 the whole of R9425 was transcribed and deciphered with the key, letter values unchanged; it is given in section 05.

04 The letter

Ferdinand has had Maximilian’s letter of the 10th “gestrigen tags”. The first half is logistics, and it is specific. On the frontier of the county of Horn and at Montfort companies are to be accommodated; the levies (Werbungen) are “nit sufficient”; the agent, Licentiat Morres, reports and is to negotiate. Troops are to be quartered in the villages nearest the city and at Deutz, across the Rhine from Cologne, under a particular ordinance for the quartermaster, and the march (Fortzug) is to follow when they are ready. Pay is fixed:

… iedtwedern zwei Kopfstückh oder zwei Ort [eines] Königs-Dalers täglich zu versprechen …

Then the constraint that runs through the whole letter: Ferdinand will not let his own territory be eaten. He must keep his Domkapitel and Landstände “in guetem Willen und Devotion”, he is about to call a Landtag, and his Estates must “nit alienirt werden”. Lintelo’s company is placed near the city; Herberstorff and other recruitments are to have every possible support, and “daran nichts erwinden lassen”. For Paderborn he asks a year’s respite, where troops are already levied and a contribution and a mustering-place have been granted; he can give no firm promise about the rest.

The second half is the argument for the war, and it is the passage the reveal below decrypts symbol by symbol. If the other side is reprimirt and the adversary is strengthened with so considerable a reinforcement while Catholic power is diminished, then

… den Catolischen ins gemein und ohne underschied [wird] geringer Zeit übrig sein, sich [vor] des andern Theils Einfal, Gwalt und Betrangnus zu entweren. Ich vermeine, das die gemeine Zusammensetzung zu Conservation unser und in kurzer Zeit unser catolischer Religion und Seelen Seligkeit …

Against that he sets the gemeine Zusammensetzung, the common union, for the preservation “of us” (unser, the genitive) and soon of the Catholic religion and the salvation of souls, and for posterity. (Corrected 30 September 2026: the clear words before the cipher read Ich vermeine, das die on the image, not Ich hoffe, and the words between “Conservation unser” and “catolischer” are group 248 = und and the clear in kurzer Zeit followed by unser in cipher; the passage now runs without a cut.) His agent’s relation shows that a resolution cannot wait: levies are to be feared “für dem Früeling”, before the spring. The closing ciphered lines name Alexandro, the King, his lands, and “die Austilgung der ca[t]olischen Religion”.

The date matters. Frederick V of the Palatinate had been crowned in Prague on 4 November 1619; Maximilian and Ferdinand II signed the Munich treaty in October and the League was mobilising through that winter. Ferdinand was one of the two brothers who were about to pay for and lead that mobilisation, and here works out in cipher where the men will sleep, what they will be paid a day, and how much of it his own bishopric can be made to carry.

05 The sibling: Archduke Albert’s resolution, 15 December 1619

R9425 (ff. 278–280) is four cipher pages (ff. 278r, 278v, 279r, 279v), a clear closing line on f. 280r, “Zur fernern nachricht und nachschreiben, den 15. 10bris Ao 1619”, and an endorsement on f. 280v: “Resolutio deß hertzogs Albrechts dem L. Morreo … ertailt”. It was transcribed from the DECODE images on 2 October 2026 and deciphered with the key recovered from R9426, every letter value unchanged. 1,524 of its 1,567 cipher tokens give sense: 97.3% (r9425/count_r9425.py; letters 1,471 of 1,482, nomenclator 53 of 85 tokens, of which 24 are nulls; 97.2% with the nulls left out of the count). The language check on the decrypt puts German first.

It opens “Albrecht von Gottes Gnaden” and is Albert’s written answer to what Licentiate Morreus, the agent of the Duke of Bavaria as head of the Catholic League, had put to him “so woll mündlich als schriftlich”. In order:

  • He thanks the Duke for commending to him the preservation of the Emperor and of “unsers hochlöblichen [Hauses] Angelegenheit”, and promises the same correspondence in return.
  • He agrees that the recovery and reconquest of the kingdom [of Bohemia] and the other lands calls for every possible effort, has done “alle müglichste getrewe gute Officia” with the Emperor and the King [of Spain], and awaits their answer.
  • Meanwhile, should the worst happen, the troops he has “anitzo auf den Beinen” are to go up to the Catholic army with orders to join it and obey its commander.
  • A muster place, a regiment of foot and five hundred [horse] he cannot grant at present: his own levies are destined for Luxembourg, because of its situation.
  • On the garrisoning of the fortress of Breisach he writes by this ordinary post to the King and to Oñate; for the thousand cuirassiers the League asks to raise in the King’s name he writes to Ambrosio [Spinola], and refers the Duke to him.

It is the answer to the same mission that R9426 reports on a week later, where the agent appears as “Morres”. The clear passages are transcribed as a working reading with gaps; only the cipher is counted.

What it gives back to R9426. R9425 settles five nomenclator groups as nulls: 414 stands in “Kön. 414 M. und L.” beside “Kön. 165 und L.”, in “unsers 414 hochlöblichen” and in “iren L. 414 L.”; 415 in “ire L. 415 L.”; 419 in “zu gedachtem 419 End”; 416 and 417 in the same formulas. It also fixes 242 = und (a homophone of 248, in the twice-written “Kön. M. 417 242 415 L.”), 165 = Mt. and 2 = Ambrosio [Spinola], where the decipherer’s red margin has “Ambr.”. With 416 a null, R9426’s “ertz 232 416” and “zu meinem 416 232” make 232 = stift close to forced. Probable values, not counted as read: 41 = Chur-, 218 = Rath, 169 = Kriegs-, 216 = Pferd, 154 = Haus, 47 = Mt., 31 = Vettern, 157 = Hilf.

Open in R9425 (32 tokens, 2.0%): the groups 45, 149, 225 (twice each), 160, 219, 153, 15, 215, 155, 221, 136, 140 and 249, and the probable-only values above; each has one or two contexts and no key survives. 149 (“Weil 149 L. die Vestung Brisach … bedencken tragen”) is probably Archduke Leopold; “dem 140 249 Oñate” probably the Spanish ambassador. Two or three signs on f. 278v run into the binding; four single signs are illegible; five single-token slips in words whose sense is clear are counted unread.

06 What remains uncertain

Measured over the enciphered text (count.py), 2,504 of 2,614 cipher tokens give sense: 95.8%. The letters are nearly complete, 2,483 of 2,519. Two kinds of item are open.

The nomenclator (31 of 32 groups, 74 tokens, 2.8% of the text). The groups that occur are 102, 122, 136, 142, 146, 154, 160, 165, 183, 200, 213, 215, 216, 221, 225, 232, 238, 239, 248, 258, 410, 412, 414, 415, 416, 417, 419 and the two-digit 33, 47, 49, 51, 57. From R9426 alone only 248 = und is established, and that from its own contexts (“Gwalt 248 Betrangnus”, “Eifer 248 Ernst”, “248 in kurzer Zeit”). The sibling R9425 (section 05) shows 414, 415, 416, 417 and 419 to be nulls: 23 of the 74 open tokens. Counting them would raise R9426 from 95.8% to 96.7% ((2,504 + 23) / 2,614); the figures on this page are not yet recounted, so they are left as measured. 410 and 412 (once each) are probably nulls of the same run but do not occur in R9425, and 232 is now close to forced as stift. No key for this cipher survives: the volume’s own key records were checked (R9419 at f. 267 is a Latin nomenclator on 1–99; R9420–R9423 are 1530s–1583 alphabets already worked through for the earlier letters in this file), and the sibling R9425’s red margin, enhanced by channel separation, is a contemporary abstract of that letter, not a group-by-group decipherment.

Three names and four words. Three words decrypt as proper names whose bearers are not identified: Leicker (the Doctor Ferdinand sends to negotiate), Dernersen (named beside Herberstorff), and Marreo/Morres (the agent). Four words, 37 tokens in all, do not decrypt to German: at P1.22, P2.05, P3.05 and P4.23. Each has a single mis-transcribed digit inside a word; at this scan resolution the strokes are ambiguous, and a return to the originals would settle them.

Neither 1619 letter was found in print. Briefe und Akten zur Geschichte des Dreißigjährigen Krieges, Neue Folge I/1 (ed. Georg Franz, Munich 1966), which covers January 1618 to December 1620, cites the Munich files by the old “Kasten schwarz” numbers rather than the current KAA shelfmarks, and nothing in its searchable text for November–December 1619 answers to this letter; Hurter’s Ferdinand II viii, Wolf and Breyer’s Maximilian I iv and Pastor were checked as well. “Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591” is cited nowhere in the literature that can be searched.

## Summary (modern English)

Archduke Albert, sovereign of the Spanish Netherlands, answers in writing what Licentiate **Morreus**, the agent of a
prince he calls "E. L." and "des Herzogen L." (the Duke of Bavaria, as head of the Catholic League), has put to him
orally and in writing. He thanks the prince for commending to him the preservation of the Emperor ("our most gracious,
beloved lord and cousin, his Imperial Highness") and of the interests of "our most praiseworthy House", and promises
the same correspondence in return.

On the substance: he agrees that the **recovery and reconquest of the kingdom [of Bohemia]** and the other lands calls
for every possible effort; the Catholic princes and Estates should give help and assistance to that end, and he has
already done good offices with the Emperor and the King [of Spain] and awaits further answer. Meanwhile he has resolved
that, should the worst happen, the troops he has now on foot will be sent up with orders to join the Catholic army and
obey its commander, and that Hungarian troops [being sent by the other side?] be countered by help for the oppressed
Catholic Estates. A **muster place, a regiment of foot and five hundred [horse]** he cannot grant at present, though
he would like to: his own levies are destined for **Luxembourg**, because of its situation. Since [Archduke Leopold?]
hesitates to garrison the fortress of **Breisach** with the [Elector's?] troops, Albert writes by this ordinary post to
the King and to **Oñate** (the Spanish ambassador in Vienna). For the **thousand cuirassiers** that the League asks to
raise in the name of the King [of Spain], he writes to **Ambrogio [Spinola]** and refers the Duke to him to confer.
If the Duke names qualified officers not in active command, Albert will give satisfaction. Dated 15 December 1619;
endorsed as Albert's *Resolutio* given to Licentiate Morreo.

The letter is the answer to the mission that R9426 (Ferdinand of Cologne, 22 December 1619) reports on: there the
agent "Morres" is named and the League's quarters and levies in the Rhine–Westphalian circle are the subject.

## Summary (modern English)

Archduke Albert, sovereign of the Spanish Netherlands, answers in writing what Licentiate **Morreus**, the agent of a
prince he calls "E. L." and "des Herzogen L." (the Duke of Bavaria, as head of the Catholic League), has put to him
orally and in writing. He thanks the prince for commending to him the preservation of the Emperor ("our most gracious,
beloved lord and cousin, his Imperial Highness") and of the interests of "our most praiseworthy House", and promises
the same correspondence in return.

On the substance: he agrees that the **recovery and reconquest of the kingdom [of Bohemia]** and the other lands calls
for every possible effort; the Catholic princes and Estates should give help and assistance to that end, and he has
already done good offices with the Emperor and the King [of Spain] and awaits further answer. Meanwhile he has resolved
that, should the worst happen, the troops he has now on foot will be sent up with orders to join the Catholic army and
obey its commander, and that Hungarian troops [being sent by the other side?] be countered by help for the oppressed
Catholic Estates. A **muster place, a regiment of foot and five hundred [horse]** he cannot grant at present, though
he would like to: his own levies are destined for **Luxembourg**, because of its situation. Since [Archduke Leopold?]
hesitates to garrison the fortress of **Breisach** with the [Elector's?] troops, Albert writes by this ordinary post to
the King and to **Oñate** (the Spanish ambassador in Vienna). For the **thousand cuirassiers** that the League asks to
raise in the name of the King [of Spain], he writes to **Ambrogio [Spinola]** and refers the Duke to him to confer.
If the Duke names qualified officers not in active command, Albert will give satisfaction. Dated 15 December 1619;
endorsed as Albert's *Resolutio* given to Licentiate Morreo.

The letter is the answer to the mission that R9426 (Ferdinand of Cologne, 22 December 1619) reports on: there the
agent "Morres" is named and the League's quarters and levies in the Rhine–Westphalian circle are the subject.
