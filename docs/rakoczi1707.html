A report to Rákóczi, not a letter from him

DECODE R902 was catalogued with Pompeio Cesoni—Rákóczi’s alias—as sender. Its clear address makes him the recipient. A matching key identifies 92.1% of the transcribed groups and reveals intelligence and lobbying from the Polish–Swedish theatre.

Result. The address leaf reads A Monsieur / Monsieur Pompeio Cesoni, so Ferenc Rákóczi II is the recipient, not the sender. DECODE R639 preserves the matching key. Applied to R902, it identifies 1,020 of 1,108 parsed groups and exposes a French report about Ráday’s approach to General Rehnskiöld, activity at the Swedish and Polish courts, Prince Konstanty, and Polish opposition to Swedish management in favour of King Stanisław. The key heading and context make Philippe Groffey the probable unsigned writer. The system is solved; a reliable continuous edition still requires a fresh transcription of the manuscript.

01 The catalogue had the direction backwards

DECODE R902 describes a ciphered letter of “Pompeio Cesoni (Ferenc Rákóczi II) to unknown recipient”, dated 15 October 1707. The clear third leaf settles the direction without cryptanalysis:

A Monsieur
Monsieur Pompeio Cesoni

Cesoni is therefore the addressee. The hidden text also addresses the recipient with the forms expected for Rákóczi and reports work undertaken on his behalf. The catalogue entry has been corrected here to an unsigned report to Rákóczi.

02 The key survived in the same archive

The matching table is DECODE R639, MNL OL G15 Caps. C. Fasc. 44/08. Its heading reads De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei. The rare entries, not just common letters, match R902: 489 Monsieur, 491 Monseigneur, 497 les Svedois, 506 Svede, 507 Pologne, 526 le Prince Constantin, and 533 Dantzig.

The key was not inferred from this letter. It is an archival table that can be tested directly on other documents. That distinction matters: the remaining uncertainty belongs chiefly to DECODE’s digit transcription and to the writer’s identity, not to the cipher family.

03 A one-part French nomenclator

The values run from 10 to 560. Word division is only partial. DECODE renders a raised leading digit as 1^.; the decoder must join it to the following one or two digits, while treating other carets as annotations. Missing that typographic convention destroys many three-digit groups.

04 Two interlinear controls, then R902

The table was first replayed against R852 and R912, same-series letters whose contemporary readings provide a control independent of R902. It then produced comparable coverage on the target.

R902 contains 326 distinct parsed groups. Eighty-eight occurrences are not in the transcribed table. Some may be genuine omitted values, but inspection of readable passages shows that many are one-digit errors or groups joined in the DECODE transcript.

05 What the report says

The existing transcription is good enough to establish subject and argument, but not to print every sentence as diplomatic plaintext. The excerpts below distinguish H (direct table reading), C (secure contextual repair of an evident digit slip), and M (meaning recoverable but wording still provisional).

GradeReadingWhy it matters

Other securely anchored names and phrases include the king of Poland, the king of Sweden, Russia, Prince Konstanty, Polish grandees and troops, the Emperor and Vienna, and English and Dutch actors. Taken together, the letter is an intelligence and lobbying report, not a private letter written by Rákóczi.

06 The unsigned writer was probably Philippe Groffey

R902 has no open signature, so the attribution must remain qualified. Three independent clues converge:

  • The exact key is headed De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei.
  • The letter reports Ráday’s approach to Rehnskiöld and activity at the Swedish and Polish courts.
  • Contemporary scholarship identifies Philippe Groffey, also written Grophey or Graffei, as Rákóczi’s representative around those courts and as a contact of Ráday.

This is strong historical attribution, not proof from a signature. The public title therefore retains the question mark.

07 What remains open

  • Exact prose: the present result is not a continuous diplomatic edition. One wrong digit can turn a word value into several plausible syllables.
  • Fresh transcription: the two cipher leaves should be re-entered group by group from the manuscript, with raised digits and joins checked visually.
  • Writer: Groffey is highly probable but the unsigned leaf supplies no final confirmation.
  • Priority: no prior reading was found in the sources checked, but an unlocated archival decipherment may exist.
