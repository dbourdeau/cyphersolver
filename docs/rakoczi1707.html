An unsigned report to Rákóczi, 15 October 1707

An unsigned French intelligence report to Rákóczi from the Swedish camp in Poland, deciphered with a key filed elsewhere in the same archive; after a fresh transcription from the images, 96.6% of its cipher groups read as sense.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 20 September 2026 · updated 5 October 2026

Method: read after matching with key from external sources · Extent: complete

At a glanceExternal key matched · complete · 982 of 1,017 cipher groups (96.6%)

The letter
An unsigned report, very probably by Philippe Groffey, to Ferenc Rákóczi II under his alias Pompeio Cesoni, dated “A Slupce, le 15 Octobre” 1707. DECODE had Rákóczi as the sender; the clear address makes him the recipient.
Where
Magyar Nemzeti Levéltár, G15 Caps. C. Fasc. 39, pp. 277–279; DECODE R902, catalogued as not yet deciphered.
The cipher
A one-part French nomenclator of groups 10–560: homophones for letters, syllables, common words and names, with fillers at the line ends.
How it was deciphered
Every G15 key on DECODE was scored on the letter; R639, headed De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei, fits and was checked on two interlinear siblings (R852, R912). Both cipher pages were then transcribed afresh from the images, group by group (30 September and 5 October 2026).
What it says
The Swedes’ settlement with the Emperor, their army near Poznań waiting for recruits landed at Stettin, the damage done by Ráday’s letters to Rehnskiöld, the writer’s own standing, and the Swedish complaint that Rákóczi would sacrifice himself to free Polish liberty.
Measured
982 of 1,017 counted groups read as sense (96.6%, measure_tr.py), twelve of them after a one-figure correction forced by the context (grade C).
Still open
Ten groups closing the first paragraph that decode to no word, and about twenty-five scattered single groups whose value gives no sense in place.

01 Sender and recipient

DECODE R902 describes a ciphered letter of “Pompeio Cesoni (Ferenc Rákóczi II) to unknown recipient”, dated 15 October 1707. The clear third leaf gives the recipient:

A Monsieur
Monsieur Pompeio Cesoni

Cesoni is therefore the addressee. The hidden text also addresses the recipient with the forms expected for Rákóczi and reports work undertaken on his behalf. The catalogue entry has been corrected here to an unsigned report to Rákóczi.

02 The key, in the same archive

The matching table is DECODE R639, MNL OL G15 Caps. C. Fasc. 44/08. It was found by decrypting R902 with each key transcription of the Rákóczi chancery fonds (G15) on DECODE and scoring the output. Its heading reads De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei. Rare entries as well as common letters match R902: 489 Monsieur, 491 Monseigneur, 497 les Svedois, 506 Svede, 507 Pologne, 526 le Prince Constantin, and 533 Dantzig.

The key is an archival table, applied as it stands, and it can be tested directly on other documents. The remaining uncertainty lies in DECODE’s digit transcription and in the writer’s identity.

03 A one-part French nomenclator

RangeFunctionExamples
10–120Homophonic letters and common endingsSeveral values for frequent letters; recurring terminal forms
121–460Syllables and common wordsay, aussy, honneur, vous, lettres
461–560Titles, peoples, countries, places, names, months and numbersMonsieur, les Svedois, Pologne, Prince Constantin
NullsCarry no plaintext97, 99, 101, 103, 105, 107, 109, 111, 113, 115, 117, 119

The values run from 10 to 560. Word division is only partial. DECODE renders a raised leading digit as 1^.; the decoder must join it to the following one or two digits, while treating other carets as annotations. Without that join many three-digit groups are lost.

05 What the report says

Both cipher pages were transcribed afresh from the images, group by group, on 30 September (p. 278 lines 10–18) and 5 October 2026 (p. 277 and p. 278 lines 1–9), and decoded with the R639 table. Square brackets mark groups not read; spelling is normalised.

A Slupce, le 15 Octobre. Monseigneur, Vostre Altesse aura veu par mes deux dernieres, de Gorlitz en Saxe et de Lubben en Silesie, de quelle maniere s’est denoüée l’affaire des Svedois avec l’Empereur. Il […] tout à present ici, à 8 lieues de Posnanie, où ils attendent les recrues et les munitions qui ont debarqué à Stettin. Leur confiance dans les Anglois et les Hollandois nous doit asseurer qu’ils ne s’arreste[…] point ici pour observer la conduite que ce Prince tiendra par rapport à l’execution des articles arrestez … [ten groups]

J’ay aussi eu l’honneur de vous rendre compte, Monseigneur, de l’impression que […] lettres de Monsieur Ráday au general Rehnskiöld avoit faite ici sur les esprits … qu’elle vous a privé de vos meilleurs amis à la Cour de Svede, et qu’il faud[ra] bien du temps [et] beaucoup d’adresse pour reparer le tort incroyable qu’elle a fait à Vostre Altesse. C’est pourquoy je vous conseillerois, Monseigneur, … d’envoyer secretement ou de chercher une personne ici qui eust les qualitez necessaires pour y pouvoir reussir. J’avoue de bonne foy qu’elles me manquent, outre que le malheur que j’ay eu d’estre soupçonné de Vostre Altesse … me fait comprendre que la continuation de mes services ne luy seroit pas agreable. Je ne me suis neantmoins pas encore relasché jusques à present sur mon zele à vostre egard, et je me suis donné tous les mouvemens dont j’ay esté [ca]pable, tant à la chancellerie svedoise qu’à la Cour de Pologne, […] pour ruiner incessament le bruit qui couroit, ou plustost l’opinion qu’on avoit, que Vostre Altesse avoit fait arrester les enfans du Palatin de Russie et l’envoyé du Roy de Pologne qui revenoit de chez le Prince Constantin … et amenoit avec […] un député de la Porte au Roy de Svede.

Your Highness will have seen from my last two, from Görlitz in Saxony and Lübben in Silesia, how the Swedes’ affair with the Emperor was settled. They are now here, eight leagues from Poznań, waiting for the recruits and munitions landed at Stettin … I have also reported the impression that M. Ráday’s letters to General Rehnskiöld made here: they have cost you your best friends at the Swedish court, and it will take time and skill to repair the harm. I would advise you to send someone secretly, or find someone here, fit for the task; I admit I am not, and the suspicion Your Highness has had of me makes me think my services are no longer welcome. Even so I have done all I could, at the Swedish chancellery and the Polish court, to quash the rumour that Your Highness had arrested the children of the Palatine of Ruthenia and the King of Poland’s envoy returning from Prince Konstanty with a deputy of the Porte to the King of Sweden.

The letter ends (p. 278, lines 10–18), with every group read:

… l’ont fait naître. On se borne présentement à se plaindre de la conduite de Vostre Altesse à l’égard des affaires de la Pologne, et de certains discours qu’elle doit avoir tenus en présence de gens qui en ont rendu compte, asseurant qu’elle avoit dit que, prenant les intérêts de la Pologne autant à cœur que ceux de la Hongrie, elle estoit résolue de se sacrifier elle-mesme s’il le falloit pour affranchir la liberté polonoise opprimée par les Suédois en faveur du Roy Stanislas. Je suis avec un très profond respect …

For the present they confine themselves to complaining of Your Highness’s conduct with regard to the affairs of Poland, and of certain remarks you are said to have made before people who have reported them: that, taking the interests of Poland as much to heart as those of Hungary, you were resolved to sacrifice yourself if need be to free Polish liberty, oppressed by the Swedes in favour of King Stanislas. In 1707 the Polish confederates opposed to Stanisław offered Rákóczi their crown; this is the Swedish side’s complaint about it.

Three values were added to the table from this letter, grade C: 212 is Fa (the F row is a syllable series like Ba, Ca, Ga; faveur, falloit), 93 is st (Stanislas; obstacle in R912), and 89 is Et (three places, filling a gap between 88 M and 90 N). An underlined number keeps only its first syllable: 342 pren-ant, 123 af-franchir, 507 Polo-noise, 124 Al-tesse. Twelve groups are corrected by one figure where the context forces it, all grade C: dernieres and recrues (118 for 128), maniere and opinion (298 for 248), denoüée (104 for 19), des articles (181 for 151), rendre (371 for 391), qualitez (348 for 345), soupçonné de (174 for 194), le bruit (12 for 112), qu’on (352 for 252), prejugez (247 for 297).

Other names and phrases fixed securely include the king of Poland, the king of Sweden, Russia, Prince Konstanty, Polish grandees and troops, the Emperor and Vienna, and English and Dutch actors. The letter is an intelligence and lobbying report addressed to Rákóczi.

06 The writer, probably Philippe Groffey

R902 has no signature, so the attribution is qualified. Three pieces of evidence point to him:

  • The exact key is headed De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei.
  • The letter reports Ráday’s approach to Rehnskiöld and activity at the Swedish and Polish courts.
  • Contemporary scholarship identifies Philippe Groffey, also written Grophey or Graffei, as Rákóczi’s representative around those courts and as a contact of Ráday.

No signature confirms it, so the title keeps a question mark.

07 What remains open

  • Measured. measure_tr.py counts every group of the fresh transcription except the line-initial and line-final fillers: p. 277 and p. 278 together, 982 of 1,017 groups (96.6%) read as sense.
  • The end of the first paragraph (p. 277 ll. 13–14, ten groups: 224 264 174 226 37 / 324 125 104 589 4). They are clearly written but decode to letters that make no word (gi les de gu O pe avec U): perhaps a name or a run of nulls. No sibling or printed text can check them.
  • About twenty-five single groups (85, 8, 120 twice, 297, 363, 129, 436, 860, 95, 92, 9, 194, 589, 121, 264, 89, 27 and the closing 4/5/567/609). Each was re-read on the image and the reading stands, but the table value gives no sense in place.
  • The twelve grade-C corrections rest on the context, not on the image, at 2,448 pixels a page; a sharper scan from the archive would settle them.
  • Writer: Groffey is probable; the unsigned leaf gives no confirmation.
  • Prior decipherment: none was found (DECODE; Thaly, Rákóczi leveleskönyvei II, 1873; Benda 1960); a decipherment elsewhere in the archive cannot be excluded.

Image rights. No manuscript image or crop is reproduced here. DECODE states: “The image is not in the public domain. Publishing it is only possible with the permission of the archive.”
