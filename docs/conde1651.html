Condé to Armand du Lau du Chambon, August–September 1651

Five Fronde letters of the prince de Condé with long cipher passages, deciphered by General de Brianson, who summarised them in 1983; his key, tested here on a held-out page, gives 478 of their 788 cipher words as sure.

The letters
Five letters of Louis II de Bourbon, prince de Condé, from Paris to Armand du Lau du Chambon, dated 6, 13, 17 and 25 August and 3 September 1651, three of them with a postscript. Clear French with cipher passages.
Where
Archives départementales de la Dordogne, 2 E 1841/18 (du Lau du Chambon papers), items 18-14, 18-12, 18-3, 18-11 and 18-10. Not on DECODE. Catalogue entry 1213.
The cipher
Numbers with a mark above them and about twenty symbols. Unmarked numbers and the symbols are a homophonic alphabet; tilde, acute and diaeresis numbers are syllables and short words, caron numbers are words and macron numbers are names.
How it was deciphered
With General Arnaud de Brianson’s key, left with his unpublished typescript decipherment in the same bundle. The key was transcribed and frozen, then tested on one page held out of the work: 0.869 agreement with his typescript against 0.293 for the best of 200 shuffled keys.
What it says
Condé steers his party at Bordeaux: keep the “Chauvesouris” out of the Parlement’s deliberations, keep the Parlement sitting through the vacation, hold certain men ready, and put things in a state to take up arms at Bordeaux whenever he wishes.
Still open
Name and word codes that neither Brianson’s key nor the context settles (96, 97, 99 and others), many words that are only probable, and the fourth page of the 17 August letter, whose photograph is unusable.
Credit
Prior decipherment by General Arnaud de Brianson (summary printed 1983). Photographs by Catherine Schunck (SHAP), a volunteer. Key test, transcription and reading contributed by Brice Dudout, with AI agents (PR #937).

02 The cipher

Two-digit numbers (some of one digit) carry a mark above them, and about twenty symbols stand beside them. Unmarked numbers from 15 to 40 and the symbols make a homophonic alphabet. Numbers with a tilde, an acute or a diaeresis are syllables in consonant series (ga…gu, la…lu, ra…ru and so on) and short words. Caron numbers are words (affaire, armes, ennemi, aboutissement…), and macron numbers are names: 16 Paris, 18 Bordeaux, 65 Guyenne, 74 le Président, 81 le chevalier, 82 Monsieur. It is of the same graphic family as a Condé–Barrière key of 1655, but with a different table.

03 Brianson’s decipherment

General Arnaud de Brianson deciphered all five letters. In 1983 he printed a letter-by-letter summary and a description of the system in the Bulletin de la Société historique et archéologique du Périgord (CX, 4th part), “Sur cinq lettres chiffrées du prince de Condé (1651)”. The bundle itself holds his complete typescript decipherment (capitals for the deciphered words, ?/n for codes he left open, and a later lower-case version with codes pencilled in) and his key: a handwritten syllable grid and a key sheet headed with the name of the prince and the months of the letters. Neither is published, and neither is reproduced here; no one has been asked about reproducing them.

The letter of 25–27 August also carries a few brown-ink interlinear glosses, period or early, which decipher some phrases. They agree with the cipher where they exist.

So the letters were already deciphered. What this work adds is a test of Brianson’s key on a page held out of the work, a sign-by-sign transcription, a reading with the frozen key, English translations, and a list of places where the cipher differs from his typescript.

04 Testing his key

Brianson’s key as left in the bundle was transcribed without looking at the transcription of the cipher, and frozen as K2 with a SHA-256 hash, together with the protocol, the scorer and the reference. The held-out portion is all the cipher of the second and third pages of the 17 August letter between its first clear passage and its last cipher run: 410 signs measured. The reference is Brianson’s capitals for the same passages, with his ?/n removed. It is kept outside the repository because it is his unpublished text; only its hash is published.

  • Result. K2 agreed with the reference at 0.869; the best of 200 shuffled versions of the key reached 0.293. The criterion was at least 0.85 and at least 0.30 above the best control: a pass.
  • A step declared after the run. The arbitrated transcription kept a “?” on 29 of the 412 held-out signs. Before scoring, those flags were removed and each sign kept as read. The protocol did not state this step, and its hash was added after the protocol was frozen. With the flags kept, the same scorer gives 0.792 against a best control of 0.296, which fails the criterion. The pass rests on that step.
  • Another key. The Condé–Barrière key of 1655 scored 0.205 on the same portion, below its own controls. Both keys were scored in the same run.

The transcription was made by AI agents from the photographs: two blind passes and a blind arbiter for each group of pages. The held-out page cannot be used again for a test.

05 The reading

Each letter was decoded with the frozen key. A word is sure only when every sign is in K2 and was transcribed without a “?”; otherwise it is written {probable}, [restored from the sense] or ‹unresolved’. Most of the holes come from symbols that the transcribers and the key name differently, not from the cipher. The cipher passages complete clear sentences, so many read only as phrases on their own. A review by an AI agent read each letter as sentences beside its translation and graded all five B, coherent but raw; the readability checker counts about 61–64% of the sentences clean.

6 August, postscript 8 August

Condé’s visit to the Palais-Royal has produced no “aboutissement”. Du Chambon is to keep his friends stirring so that the decree against the “Chauvesouris” (the Bats, a party nickname that the letters use in clear) is carried out, to keep them out of every deliberation, and to make “ces messieurs” understand the harm their division does to Condé’s affair and their own. He is to promise 96 every kind of favour. A sentence on 59 and Madame de Richelieu, who are at Richelieu, and a postscript on a marriage that 96 was made to accept by force, close the letter. 77 of 154 cipher words are sure, the lowest share of the five.

13 August

The visit has cooled the minds of “Messieurs de Bordeaux”. Condé has taken no decision yet, “ce n’est pas que” he lacks the will. 97 is to keep everything in the same state. From now on he will write on this matter to du Chambon alone, since the chevalier {Todias} has to show his letters. The Parlement is to give a decree that it keeps sitting through the vacation, and the Chauvesouris are not to enter its deliberations. 92 of 155 cipher words are sure.

17 August

The longest letter. His footman’s late return makes Condé uneasy about the Parlement. Du Chambon is to write to 96 and engage him; 97 is to handle “l’affaire {d’Espagne}” carefully. Someone spoke to Condé of an affair of this importance that 97 had confided to him, and Condé answered him “avec beaucoup de froideur”, as if he knew nothing of it; any news is to come to him in cipher. A marginal insertion with two name codes is unread. Near the foot of the third page the cipher says “ie ne doute plus du dessein que l’on a de me {pousser}”; the letter runs on to the lost fourth page. 170 of 264 cipher words are sure.

25 August, postscript 27 August

One must pay something no heed, the clause that the brown-ink gloss gives as “n’y avoir aucun esgard”. A phrase about “hommes en blanc” of 99 is not understood. Monsieur le Président {Dafis}, the return of the Parlement’s deputies and the outcome of 96’s affair with the Parlement are named. Then du Chambon is to write to someone, after the sending of his man, to hold himself ready, so that Condé finds him in the state needed on his arrival in Guyenne; and corn is to be brought for Bordeaux, where there is a {grand} {disete}. 66 of 113 cipher words are sure.

3 September, postscript 4 September

Condé may soon need 96: du Chambon is to send to him secretly and have him hold himself ready, and things are to be put in a state to take up “les armes a Bordeaux” whenever Condé wishes. One word does not fit: the key gives “ordonnance” where the sense wants “advis”. The clearest of the five: 73 of 102 cipher words are sure.

In all, 478 of 788 cipher words are sure (61%), 252 are probable, 28 restored and 30 unresolved. The figures follow the blind sign check of section 07, which moved them from 474.

06 Where the cipher differs

Brianson’s typescript was compared word by word after the reading. Each difference was checked against the signs; only the single words concerned are quoted.

  • 17 August. “division” (46¨ = di, two clear dots) where he typed REVISION; “plus” and “pas” (94′, 84′) swapped in his text; “chevalier” (81¯) and “l’alarme” where he restored other words; “m’en”, which he leaves out; his ?/1, twice, is Bordeaux (18¯ in his own key) and his ?/9 is “avec” (48ˇ).
  • 13 August. “entretienne” (all four signs sure) for his “entreprenne”; “ie” and “des” where he has nothing and “de”; his bare 18¯ is Bordeaux.
  • 6 August. “l’aboutissement dans” (50ˇ, 49¨, both in his key) where he restores another word; the cipher opens one passage with “utiles”, which he leaves out.
  • 25–27 August. He leaves out the cipher clause with “esgard”; “homme” is in cipher, where he reads the clear abbreviation before it as “vivres”; his open 18¯ is Bordeaux, and his restored province is Guyenne (65¯).
  • 3–4 September. “Bordeaux” where he leaves 18¯; “faire” and “d’”, which he does not have.

Where Brianson reads more than the frozen key gives, the words are counted here as holes. The codes he left open are still open (section 09).

07 The sign check

Fourteen doubtful or decisive signs were put to a blind sheet, with three control signs from sure words. Each question boxed one sign and offered shapes cut from sure words of the same hand, with no gloss, reading or value shown. The checker was Brice Dudout, a member of the project, not an independent reader; an AI model wrote the selection rule, cut the thumbnails, built the sheet and scored it. The rule was written and hashed before any answer.

  • All three controls were answered correctly.
  • The seven signs behind the corrections to Brianson (division, chevalier, l’alarme, plus, dans, Guyenne, homme) were confirmed.
  • Four words became sure (l’aboutissement and homme on 25 August, messieurs and disposition on 6 August): 474 to 478.
  • One proposed correction of the key (19′ = le) was not supported at the place tested; no sure word was lost.

It is a diagnostic on chosen signs, not a sample of the text, and no person audited the model’s part. It does not touch the open codes.

08 The name table

Three frames of the bundle hold a period list headed “Noms propres de personnes, villes, bourgs et rivières”: 110 names with Roman-numeral codes from 17 to 186, and a table of numbers written as capitals. The names belong to the Saintonge, Aunis and Guyenne war of 1651–1652, and “M. de Chambon” is among them. It is not the key of these letters: of nine names whose codes are known from the letters, none agrees (Bordeaux is 18 in the letters and 31 in the table). It resolves none of the open codes, and it is filed apart as a lead of its own.

09 What remains open

  • Name and word codes. 96 (bare, ten times), 97 (bare, six times), 99, 59 (bare), 66′, 98′, 12~, 35~, 62′, 8¯, 83¯, 29¨, and the marginal insertion of 17 August, ‹85› et tous ‹95›. Brianson left them open too; in 1983 he took 96 for probably the comte du Dognon and 97 for very probably the councillor Laisné. Another letter in this key, or its full table, would settle them.
  • Corrections not validated (K2b). About twenty values and the names of several symbols were found after decoding. No fresh held-out portion of 300 signs remains, so they give no sure word.
  • The fourth page of 17 August. The photograph is unusable; a new one at the AD Dordogne would give the end of the letter.
  • Not seen. Brianson’s papers at the Service historique de la Défense (GR 1KT 211, copies 1 Kmi 62); other bundles of 2 E 1841; Lenet’s papers and the Chantilly decipherment tables, for other letters in this key.
