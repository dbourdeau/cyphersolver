Gramont to Montmorency, Rome, 21 July 1530

Go to the full text ↓

Thirteen cipher lines from the new cardinal de Gramont, 532 of 536 signs deciphered with George Lasry’s 2023 key: a nuncio is to go to France to congratulate the King on the return of the Queen and the princes.

01 The letter and its key

The catalogue grouped this letter with three others to Montmorency (catalogue item 6). Each of the four is in its own key, and since 14 October 2026 each key has its own page: Langeac at Venice in 1529 (Avranches’ cipher 1), Gramont’s own letter of 1529 (Gramont’s cipher, 1529), and the cardinal de Mâcon in 1537 with his other letters in Mascon’s cipher. Each is a different cipher, with its own page. By July 1530 Gramont is a cardinal, and he uses a new key.

The key is George Lasry’s table for this very leaf, “Gramont’s cipher (1530)”, posted on Satoshi Tomokiyo’s cryptiana with his other tables of November 2023; Tomokiyo had a partial table of the same key from fr. 3019 f. 20, and the key also serves fr. 3040 ff. 12 and 18. Lasry deciphered this letter in 2023 (personal communication, 21 September 2026), but neither site gives any plaintext. Lasry lists five signs as unknown. Two are identified from this letter: 8Δ is pape (“de la part du 8Δ”, “8Δ trouve bon”), and the key-ring sign is roy (“congratuller avec le ⚷ de la venue de la royne”). The reading of 18 September 2026 took ʃʃ for a null at the head of a word; the transcription of 14 November 2026 does not confirm that. The sign g serves for B, V and E, and only context decides. The sign 4t, which the table has under S, reads et at its four places, and the COM sign writes con- as well as com-.

02 Gramont, Rome, 21 July 1530: the princes come home

The clear opening recalls how the Pope and all others in Rome took “la bonne nouvelle”, so great that everyone takes hope of things being restored, and promises a full account “dedans trois ou quatre jours par homme exprès” of the decision on Francisque’s coming. The cipher, in capitals, as decoded on 14 November 2026 from the sign-by-sign transcription; the four signs without a value and the two word signs are in brackets:

LE [ʃʃ] [Z̄] VOVLOIR EST TOVSIOVRS BON ET CONTINVE PLVS QVE IAMAIS. [#] IE SVIS APRES POVR LE FAIRE MECTRE A DEV[▣] EXECVTION. ET POVRCE QVE LES IMPERIAVLX POVRROIENT CONCEPVOIR PLVS GRANT SVSPECON SI IE Y ALLOIS, POVRCE COMMANCEMENT [PAPE] TROVVE BON QVE LEDICT FRANCISQVE Y AILLE SEVL POVR SCAVOIR QVEL IL Y FAICT, ET ME SEMBLE QV’IL N’I A QVE RIEN, D’AVLTANT QV’ESTANT YCI IE POVRRAY OBVIER A TOVT CE QVE L’ON LVY POVRROIT MECTRE AVANT POVR LE DIVERTIR. A QVOY VOVS PVIS ASSEVRER QVE L’ON FAICT TOVTE LA DILLIGENCE POSSIBLE. MONSIEVR L’EVESQVE DE FAYANCE, NEPVEV DV CONTE DE CARPI, PARTIRA DEDANS TROIS OV QVATRE IOVRS POVR DE LA PART DV [PAPE] SE ALLER CONGRATVLLER AVEC LE [ROY] DE LA VENVE DE LA ROYNE ET DE MESSEIGNEVRS.

In modern terms: the goodwill (whose, the two unread signs would say) is unchanged and stronger than ever, and Gramont is working to have it carried out. Because the Imperials could grow more suspicious if Gramont went himself, the Pope finds it good, for this beginning, that Francisque should go alone to learn what is being done there, and it seems to Gramont that there is nothing in it, the more so as by staying in Rome he can counter whatever might be put to him to turn him aside. The bishop of Faenza, nephew of the count of Carpi, will leave in three or four days to congratulate the King, on the Pope’s behalf, on the coming of the Queen and the princes.

The Dauphin and Henri, hostages in Spain since 1526, were handed back with Queen Eleanor on the Bidassoa on 1 July 1530. That dates the letter, and the “bonne nouvelle” of the clear opening is the same event. The nuncio is Rodolfo Pio, bishop of Faenza, nephew of Alberto Pio, count of Carpi, who did go to France in the summer of 1530. Gramont, a cardinal since June 1530, is in Rome. The matter of Francisque’s mission (a marriage, or the Pope’s meeting with the King) is not named in the cipher deciphered so far.

03 What remains uncertain

  • Four signs have no value, each occurring once: ʃʃ and Z̄ in line 1 (between le and vouloir), # in line 2 (between iamais and ie suis), and ▣ in line 2 (dev[▣] execution: it stands where the final e of deue belongs; X in Lasry’s table, which does not fit). The first three are among Lasry’s five unknown signs. No gloss, clear copy or decipherment of the letter exists.
  • The other letters in this key are where these signs could be fixed; one of them, Gramont to Villandry (fr. 2980 nos. 21–22, catalogue 328), is still open.
  • The reading of 18 September 2026 was made by eye and estimated at 85 %. On 14 November 2026 the thirteen cipher lines were transcribed sign by sign and decoded mechanically with Lasry’s table: 532 of 536 signs take a value, and 96.1% of the words of the reading read as sense (245 words, 10 holes). The transcription is a single AI transcription, not checked by a person against the image, with three doubtful signs (the words they stand in read); the extent is left at partial on that account.
  • The clear close of the letter, after the cipher block, is transcribed only in fragments.

## In modern French

... [le pape a] toujours bonne volonté et continue plus que jamais ... Parce que les Impériaux pourraient
concevoir un plus grand soupçon si j'y allais [moi-même], le pape a trouvé bon dès le commencement que ledit
Francisque y aille ..., et je pense qu'il n'y a là que du bien, d'autant qu'en restant ici je pourrai parer à
tout ce qu'on pourrait lui mettre en avant pour l'en détourner; à quoi je vous assure qu'on fait toute la
diligence possible. L'évêque de Faenza, neveu du comte de Carpi, partira dans trois ou quatre jours pour aller,
de la part du pape, féliciter le roi de la venue de la reine et de messeigneurs [les enfants de France].

## Date

"Rome, 21 July", no year. The congratulation on "la venue de la royne et de messeigneurs" is the return of
the Dauphin and Henri, hostages in Spain since 1526, with Queen Eleanor, handed over at the Bidassoa on
1 July 1530; the "bonne nouvelle" of the clear opening is the same. Rodolfo Pio, bishop of Faenza and nephew of
Alberto Pio, count of Carpi, did go to France as nuncio in summer 1530. So **21 July 1530**, and Gramont
(cardinal since June 1530) is in Rome. This fits Lasry's name for the key, "Gramont's cipher (1530)".
The "Francisque" sent in Gramont's place is presumably the same agent as in the clear text; the matter (a
marriage, or the Pope's meeting with the King) is not named in the cipher that has been read.

Gaps: the ends of lines 1-2 and the middle of lines 5-7 were read at lower confidence; about 85 % of the
cipher is read.

without a value stand by name in brackets, as there. "quel il y faict" is rendered "what is being done there" and
"n'i a que rien" "there is nothing in it": the letters are the decode's, the sense is the translator's.

## Translation

Monseigneur, I wrote to you the other day how it went with the Pope and with all others over here regarding the
good news you have let them know, which is such, and of so great effect, that everyone takes good hope from it that
things will be restored to their entirety; and little praise is given you for the perfection of the work. As I hope
to let you know more fully within three or four days by an express messenger, who will carry the full resolution
from here as to the matter of the coming of the sa[id] Francisque ...

⟨The [ʃʃ] [Z̄] will is still good and continues more than ever. [#] I am working to have it put into
du[▣] execution. And because the Imperialists might conceive greater suspicion if I went there, for this beginning
[the Pope] finds it good that the said Francisque should go there alone to learn what is being done there, and it
seems to me that there is nothing in it, the more so as, being here, I shall be able to forestall all that might be
put before him to turn him aside. As to which I can assure you that all possible diligence is being used. Monsieur
the bishop of Faenza, nephew of the Count of Carpi, will leave within three or four days to go, on behalf of [the
Pope], to rejoice with [the King] at the coming of the Queen and of my lords.⟩

I do not wish to forget [?to tell you] ... of good sums of promise ... and good ... that fourteen months ago ...
