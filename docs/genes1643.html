Eraso to Philip IV, Genoa, 1643

The letter
Juan de Eraso, Spanish ambassador at Genoa, to Philip IV, 18 January 1643, signed duplicate. Opening and closing in clear, one paragraph in cipher.
Where
Archivo General de Simancas, Estado, leg. 3598 no. 44 (PARES 3555178, image 1). The plain original (no. 42) is not online: the PARES record for no. 42 shows the images of no. 41. It is item 755 of this project’s catalogue, whose 1638 letters (a different table) are a separate target.
The cipher
The 1643 table of the Genoa embassy: syllabic series of five numbers (27 fa, 32 ta, 37 as, 42 na …), disguised bigrams and trigrams, single letters and code numbers (270 Vuestra Majestad, 223 república, 180 Francia).
How it was read
Key K4b learned by EM alignment on the pairs 3598,38/37 and 41/40 and frozen. First held-out test (3598,15 against 14): 0.816 of characters, 0.750 of words, below the criterion fixed in advance and reported. Main test, 3598,291 against 290: 0.881 and 0.834, against 0.298 and 0.121 at the 99th percentile of 200 permuted keys.
What it says
The Grand Duke defers to orders he will send his resident in Madrid; Eraso notes that he “will enjoy the benefit of time”.
Still open
Codes 149 and 210, found only here. Sixteen restored code words await a reproduction of the plain original.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée, with Claude): key, held-out tests, transcription, Spanish text and English translation. Contributed by email on 29 October 2026 (branch dudout/bundle-spain-italy).

01 The letter

In January 1643 Tortona had just been retaken, the war with France weighed on the Milanese, and Madrid pressed the Italian princes bound to Spain to send troops. Eraso had forwarded the King’s letter to Ferdinand II de’ Medici. The next item in the bundle (3598,43) is the Grand Duke’s answer in Italian, Florence, 1 January 1643, which refers to the care he must take of his own states. Eraso explains in cipher what this means: the matter goes to Spain, and the delay is the Grand Duke’s gain.

Of the ten cipher items of 1643–51 in entry 755, nine have their contemporary plain text in the same bundle; this is the one whose plain text is not online. The 1638 Genoa letters (target genes1638, still on an open pull request) use another table.

02 The cipher

Series of five numbers give syllables (27 fa, 32 ta, 37 as, 42 na, 47 ja, 52 al, 57 da, 62 an); disguised bigrams (ax = la, at = sa, ha = ma) and trigrams (sal = ra, gan = ca, tes = ve) stand for letter groups; single letters and a few code numbers complete the table. The transcription has 261 symbols.

03 The reading

Of 137 enciphered words, 115 are certain, 4 probable, 16 restored from the sense and 2 unresolved: 135 read, 98.5%. The restored words are word codes seen nowhere else (con que, órdenes, España, respuesta, cuenta), fixed by syntax and by chancery formulas such as “dar cuenta a V. M.”; for 166 the same sense would follow from “Madrid” or “la corte”. The full text and translation are below. To our knowledge this is the first published decipherment.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Code 149, before residente: perhaps a title or rank of the resident.
  • Code 210, between llegue and antes este aviso: perhaps “a manos de V. M.”.
  • Probable: Duque (130, attested nowhere else). Restored codes need the plain original 3598,42 (ff. 151–152) to be checked.

Juan de Eraso to Philip IV, Genoa, 18 January 1643 (AGS EST,LEG,3598,44) read in part

January 1643: Tortona has just been retaken, the war with France weighs on the Milanese, and Madrid is pressing the Italian princes bound to Spain to send troops. The ambassador at Genoa, Juan de Eraso, has forwarded the King’s letter to Grand Duke Ferdinand II de’ Medici. The answer comes back: the Grand Duke refers the matter to his resident in Madrid. Eraso tells the King, in cipher, that this is a way of playing for time.

# English translation

[In plain text: In the days this post has been held up by bad weather, I have received the answer] of the Grand Duke of Tuscany to the letter I wrote him, with which I forwarded Your Majesty’s royal letter, urging him to help the State of Milan with the men he is bound to provide;

and from the copy of the Grand Duke’s letter, which will go with this one, Your Majesty will be pleased to see how he evades me in the dispute over sending the men to the State, deferring to the orders he will give his ‹…› resident in Madrid to deal with this matter;

and he loses nothing by it, for he will enjoy the benefit of time in the steps that referring this business to Spain and waiting for an answer entail; and I at once give Your Majesty an account of the answer I have had from the Grand Duke of Tuscany, so that it may arrive ‹…› before this report,

[in plain text:] and Your Majesty may take whatever resolution you think best. God keep the Catholic and royal person of Your Majesty as Christendom and your vassals have need. Genoa, 18 January 1643. [Signature of Juan de Eraso.]
