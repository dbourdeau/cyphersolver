Louis XIII to Sabran, Dijon, 28 March 1631

A royal letter to the French resident in Genoa with two paragraphs in cipher about the Prince of Monaco; George Lasry solved the cipher in 2022, and nothing was added here.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: read from existing decipherment · Extent: complete

Credits · George Lasry (earlier solution) · Satoshi Tomokiyo (pointer to a source)

At a glanceExisting decipherment · George Lasry, 2022

The letter
Louis XIII, countersigned by Bouthillier, to Melchior de Sabran, his resident in Genoa, from Dijon on 28 March 1631. A clear letter in French with two passages in cipher.
Where
Bibliothèque nationale de France, Baluze 155 f. 79–80 (f. 80 blank). DECODE R2748, listed as not decrypted.
The cipher
About twenty lines of lower-case letters, two-digit numbers and signs, standing for letters, syllables and a few words, with a sign for a doubled letter.
How it was deciphered
George Lasry recovered the key and deciphered both passages in 2022. His key and his decipherment, written over the image of f. 79, are on Satoshi Tomokiyo’s Cryptiana. His key was not applied again here.
What it says
The cipher concerns the Prince of Monaco: the King will not take up the conditions proposed, but wants Sabran to keep the prince attached to the crown and assure him of the King’s goodwill and protection.
Credit
George Lasry (the solution); Satoshi Tomokiyo (who published it, and pointed to it from DECODE).

Until 14 October 2026 this letter shared an entry with two letters of Louis XIII to Brûlart de Léon in Venice (1615 and 1617), because DECODE lists all three under the King’s name. Those are in a different cipher, with their own page.

01 The letter

Melchior de Sabran was the French resident in Genoa from 1630 to 1637. The King writes from Dijon on 28 March 1631; Bouthillier countersigns. The clear opening says the King has seen Sabran’s despatch of 16 February and will answer its two main points. The first point begins in the clear, “luy regarde ce que vous avez negocie”, then goes into cipher for two lines and comes out again with “le degoust et desplaisir qu’en ont les Espagnolz”. The second point is a block of about sixteen lines of cipher opening “Sur le premier”. The letter returns to the clear with Sabran’s stay in Genoa.

02 The cipher

The cipher mixes lower-case letters, two-digit numbers (55, 26, 44, 28 …) and a few invented signs. In Lasry’s decipherment most signs are single letters; some are syllables (tion) or short words (vous, que, par, bon, prince), and one sign doubles the letter before it. There is no word division. Tomokiyo calls it the Louis XIII–Sabran cipher of 1631. Lasry found the same cipher in the brief cipher passages of Sabran’s own letters to Bouthillier, BnF fr. 4134 (1633) and fr. 4135 (1635), for which no contemporary decipherment is known.

Baluze 155 holds another cipher sent to Sabran, that of Abel Servien’s letters of late 1632. It is a different cipher, which Tomokiyo solved himself from two letters with some words in the clear.

03 What the cipher says

Lasry’s decipherment is published only as his capitals written over the image of f. 79 (Cryptiana, “George Lasry’s solutions”). Read from his overlay: the first passage names the Prince of Monaco. The second answers Sabran on him. Having seen the despatches and papers Sabran sent and the conditions proposed, the King did not think it right to take them up, for several good reasons. He wants Sabran to keep up the prince, with care, in the devotion he shows to the service of the crown, and to give him every assurance of the King’s goodwill and protection.

The Prince of Monaco in 1631 was Honoré II Grimaldi, then under Spanish protection; he went over to France ten years later by the treaty of Péronne (1641). The letter’s clear line about the Spaniards’ displeasure belongs to the same matter.

This summary was taken by eye from Lasry’s overlay. His decipherment was not checked here sign by sign, and no transcription of the cipher was made.

04 What remains open

  • Nothing in the text: Lasry’s decipherment covers both passages.
  • Here, the key was not applied to a transcription of f. 79r, so this page has no sign-by-sign replay. Sabran’s letters in fr. 4134 and fr. 4135 were not opened.
