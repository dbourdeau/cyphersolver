Intercepted letters summarised for Nevers, 1589

BnF Français 3977 no. 96 · a digest of “plusieurs lettres escriptes en chiffre par les ennemis du Roy”, sent to Nevers “pour estre interpretées” · catalogued with the ciphertext’s survival unknown

The summary is in clear French and the ciphered letters it describes are not in the volume, so there is nothing to decipher under no. 96. Eleven other intercepted ciphered letters in the volume carry their 1589 decipherments between the lines.

At a glanceNot a cipher

The document
A Recueil sommaire: a digest in fifteen numbered points, in clear French, of the principal points of letters the King’s enemies wrote in cipher in September and October 1589, sent to the duc de Nevers to be interpreted.
Where
BnF fr. 3977 f. 318r–319r (canvas 520), no. 96. The catalogue (item 31) did not know whether any ciphertext survives.
How it was tried
The sweep checked fr. 3977 around no. 96: every piece dated September or October 1589 is in cleartext, so the ciphered originals are not in the volume.
What it says
Mercoeur asks Spain for help; powder and money reach Nantes via Diego Maldonado “avec le chiffre général d’Espagne”; Mendoza at Bayonne writes on Arques and Dieppe.
What was added
Points 1–9 are transcribed. The sweep also located eleven other intercepted ciphered letters in the volume, February to August 1589, each with a contemporary decipherment above the cipher; they are not transcribed.

01 The question

The catalogue entry states what it does not know: “Only the ‘Recueil sommaire’ of the interpreted letters is described; whether the intercepts survive in the volume is unknown”, scored “unknown whether there is any ciphertext to attack”, with the instruction “sweep fr. 3977 around no. 96 for cipher leaves”. The task is to settle whether any ciphertext exists.

BnF fr. 3977 is btv1b9060546p, 715 canvases, a volume of the Collection Mémoires de la Ligue. Its catalogue notice carries a piece-by-piece dépouillement: 138 pieces, nos. 2–143, folios 3–423. Canvas labels are all NP, and the folio offset drifts: f. 125 is canvas 229, f. 318 is canvas 520.

02 No. 96, the digest

Folio 318 is canvas 520. The piece is headed, in a large chancery hand:

Recueil sommaire des principaux poincts contenuz en plusieurs lettres escriptes en chiffre par les ennemis du Roy — En Septembre et Octobre 1589 — et envoyées à S.A. pour estre interpretées en decembre aud. an.

It is in clear French, a digest in fifteen numbered points running fol. 318r–319r. The first nine:

  1. The duc de Mercœur has asked Spain for help; the King of Spain has sent powder and money by Diego Maldonado, despatched to Nantes “avec le chiffre g[e]n[er]al d’Espagne”, who arrived on 18 October 1589.
  2. Mendoza has lent the Prévôt des marchands a sum, to his master’s satisfaction; the Prévôt has since repaid it, and Mendoza is cautioned against gratifying him too promptly “à cause des envieux et calumniateurs”.
  3. The sieur de Mauléon is aggrieved at not being employed by Spain; Mendoza renews the signal agreed with him, “par tant la lettre escripte du chiffre qu’ilz ont ensemble”.
  4. The master of the posts of Bordeaux passes Spain’s advices, and what Matignon orders him for the King’s service.
  5. Villeroy has complained to Mendoza that the help the League expected never came.
  6. Mendoza writes repeatedly from Bayonne on what passed at Arques and Dieppe, “jusques a dementir les advis et les memoires du Roy”; the sornettes Madame de Montpensier tells him; and the King reproves him for not reporting particularly enough on the Cardinal de Bourbon, the duc de Guise, d’Elbeuf and the archevêque de Lyon.
  7. The commandeur de Diou presses his suit at Rome, and what the consistory means to allow him.
  8. The King of Spain presses the Pope, and has his adherents press him, to renew the excommunication against subjects who obey the King.
  9. Spain resolves to maintain the war in France, to continue the Leaguers’ agreed annual payments and to have them paid in full; Parma to send his forces into France; Moro, captain of light horse; Balagny.

03 No ciphertext under no. 96

Every piece in fr. 3977 dated September or October 1589 (nos. 81, 84, 85, 89, 90, 91, 92, 94, 95, 96, 97) is in cleartext. None is ciphered. The letters behind the digest were sent to Nevers to be interpreted and are not bound in this volume; what was kept is the interpretation.

Catalogue item 31 therefore has no ciphertext, and the item should be scored as answered. The scoring note “unknown whether there is any ciphertext” can be retired.

04 Eleven deciphered intercepts in the same volume

The same volume holds eleven intercepted ciphered letters, each with a contemporary decipherment. They are working papers of the same royalist deciphering service, three to seven months earlier:

Three letters in one catalogue entry. The three Mendoza–Parma letters are the longest of the group. The dépouillement files them in a single multi-folio entry, “Fol. 126, 128 et 130 • 43 à 45”, which a search for single entries of the form “Fol. N • M” misses. They are not described as ciphered in any single-folio line, and folio 125 shows no cipher in the catalogue at all.

His decipherment of the opening:

El Vizeseneschal de Montelimart, que V.A. conoce … aqui me escriva … un billete de Monsieur de Vienna en su creancia … y lo que por [Ma…] me ha dicho es el yr a dar intelligencia a V.A. de los abisos que tienen del Coronal Fifer … la leva que el haze por los de la Liga, y la que el Rey levanta de los 12.000 que los Cantones hereies le han acordado … puntos en que no me alargare, pues ellos … ha de referir a V.A.

These are eleven ciphertexts with their plaintext for Spanish ciphers of 1589, enough to rebuild the keys. They have not yet been used.

05 What is open

  • Points 10–15 of the digest, on fol. 318v–319r, are not transcribed.
  • None of the eleven deciphered intercepts has been transcribed, and no key has been reconstructed from them. They are located and one is photographed. Rebuilding the keys from them is a separate task, and a feasible one, since each leaf carries its own plaintext.
  • Whether the September–October 1589 originals survive elsewhere, in another Mémoires de la Ligue volume or among the Nevers papers, was not checked. The digest says they were sent to Nevers in December to be interpreted, so they were in his office at some point.
