The intercepts summarised for Nevers, 1589

BnF Français 3977 no. 96 · a digest of “plusieurs lettres escriptes en chiffre par les ennemis du Roy”, sent to Nevers “pour estre interpretées” · catalogued with the ciphertext’s survival unknown

The intercepts are not in the volume and the summary is in clear French — but eleven other intercepted ciphered letters are, each with its 1589 decipherment written between the lines

Arya Sanketbhai Patel · resolved 21 September 2026

Summary. Catalogue item 31 is a single piece: a Recueil sommaire of the principal points of several letters written in cipher by the King’s enemies in September and October 1589, sent to the duc de Nevers to be interpreted. The entry could not say whether the ciphered originals survive alongside it, and scored the target “unknown whether there is any ciphertext to attack”. They do not survive. Every piece in fr. 3977 dated September or October 1589 is in clear, and the summary itself is a clear-French digest in fifteen numbered points, read here: Mercœur asking Spain for help, powder and money carried to Nantes by Diego Maldonado “avec le chiffre général d’Espagne”, Mendoza at Bayonne writing on Arques and Dieppe, and Spain pressing the Pope to renew the excommunication. That is a negative result. The useful part is what the sweep turned up instead: eleven other intercepted ciphered letters in the same volume, February to August 1589, every one carrying its contemporary decipherment written word by word above the cipher — including three from Mendoza to the duke of Parma that the catalogue’s own piece list hides in a multi-folio entry. Status: closed from the evidence. No ciphertext under no. 96; the summary is read; the eleven deciphered intercepts are located but not transcribed.

01 The question

The catalogue entry is unusually candid about its own ignorance: “Only the ‘Recueil sommaire’ of the interpreted letters is described; whether the intercepts survive in the volume is unknown”, scored “unknown whether there is any ciphertext to attack”, with the instruction “sweep fr. 3977 around no. 96 for cipher leaves”. So the target is really a question, and the work is to answer it either way.

BnF fr. 3977 is btv1b9060546p, 715 canvases, a volume of the Collection Mémoires de la Ligue. Its catalogue notice carries a piece-by-piece dépouillement: 138 pieces, nos. 2–143, folios 3–423. Canvas labels are all NP, and the folio offset drifts — f. 125 is canvas 229, f. 318 is canvas 520.

02 No. 96, read

Folio 318 is canvas 520. The piece is headed, in a large chancery hand:

Recueil sommaire des principaux poincts contenuz en plusieurs lettres escriptes en chiffre par les ennemis du Roy — En Septembre et Octobre 1589 — et envoyées à S.A. pour estre interpretées en decembre aud. an.

It is in clear French, not cipher: a digest in fifteen numbered points running fol. 318r–319r. The first nine:

  1. The duc de Mercœur has asked Spain for help; the King of Spain has sent powder and money by Diego Maldonado, despatched to Nantes “avec le chiffre g[e]n[er]al d’Espagne”, who arrived on 18 October 1589.
  2. Mendoza has lent the Prévôt des marchands a sum, to his master’s satisfaction; the Prévôt has since repaid it, and Mendoza is cautioned against gratifying him too promptly “à cause des envieux et calumniateurs”.
  3. The sieur de Mauléon is aggrieved at not being employed by Spain; Mendoza renews the signal agreed with him, “par tant la lettre escripte du chiffre qu’ilz ont ensemble”.
  4. The master of the posts of Bordeaux passes Spain’s advices, and what Matignon orders him for the King’s service.
  5. Villeroy has complained to Mendoza that the help the League expected never came.
  6. Mendoza writes repeatedly from Bayonne on what passed at Arques and Dieppe, “jusques a dementir les advis et les memoires du Roy”; the sornettes Madame de Montpensier tells him; and the King reproves him for not reporting particularly enough on the Cardinal de Bourbon, the duc de Guise, d’Elbeuf and the archevêque de Lyon.
  7. The commandeur de Diou presses his suit at Rome, and what the consistory means to allow him.
  8. The King of Spain presses the Pope, and has his adherents press him, to renew the excommunication against subjects who obey the King.
  9. Spain resolves to maintain the war in France, to continue the Leaguers’ agreed annual payments and to have them paid in full; Parma to send his forces into France; Moro, captain of light horse; Balagny.

03 The answer: nothing to attack

Every piece in fr. 3977 dated September or October 1589 — nos. 81, 84, 85, 89, 90, 91, 92, 94, 95, 96, 97 — is in clear. Not one is ciphered. The letters behind the digest were sent to Nevers to be interpreted and are not bound in this volume; what was kept is the interpretation.

So for catalogue item 31 there is no ciphertext to attack, and the item should be scored as answered rather than open. The scoring note “unknown whether there is any ciphertext” can be retired.

04 Eleven that can be

The sweep the entry asked for turned up something better than the thing it was looking for. The same volume holds eleven intercepted ciphered letters, each with a contemporary decipherment — the working papers of the same royalist codebreaking effort, three to seven months earlier:

A parsing trap worth recording. The three Mendoza–Parma letters are the richest of the group and the easiest to miss: the dépouillement files them in a single multi-folio entry, “Fol. 126, 128 et 130 • 43 à 45”, which a regex written for “Fol. N • M” drops in silence. They are not described as ciphered in any single-folio line, and folio 125 shows no cipher in the catalogue at all.

What he read there:

El Vizeseneschal de Montelimart, que V.A. conoce … aqui me escriva … un billete de Monsieur de Vienna en su creancia … y lo que por [Ma…] me ha dicho es el yr a dar intelligencia a V.A. de los abisos que tienen del Coronal Fifer … la leva que el haze por los de la Liga, y la que el Rey levanta de los 12.000 que los Cantones hereies le han acordado … puntos en que no me alargare, pues ellos … ha de referir a V.A.

That is eleven complete cipher-and-plaintext pairs for Spanish ciphers of 1589 — the material from which the keys themselves could be rebuilt. It is unexploited, and it is the real find of this item.

05 What is open

  • Points 10–15 of the digest, on fol. 318v–319r, are not transcribed.
  • None of the eleven deciphered intercepts has been transcribed, and no key has been reconstructed from them. They are located and one is photographed; that is all. It is a separate piece of work and a promising one, since each leaf carries its own plaintext.
  • Whether the September–October 1589 originals survive elsewhere — in another Mémoires de la Ligue volume, or among the Nevers papers — was not pursued. The digest says they were sent to Nevers in December to be interpreted, so they were in his office at some point.
