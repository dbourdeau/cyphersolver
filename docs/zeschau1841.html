Zeschau to Seebach, 1841–1843

Hauptstaatsarchiv Dresden, 10731 Sächsische Gesandtschaft in Russland, Nr. 12, DECODE R5005–R5008: four despatches from the Saxon foreign minister to his minister resident in St Petersburg, one wholly in figures and three with cipher passages inside clear French or German.

The despatch of 18 January 1841 was transcribed in full and attacked ciphertext-only. The cipher system was identified from what survives of the erased decipherment, but the letters are not read.

Daniel Bourdeau · attempted 21 September 2026

Summary. DECODE lists four ciphered letters of Heinrich Anton von Zeschau to Albin Leo von Seebach as “partially decrypted”, with the note that an interlinear decipherment existed but was rubbed out. The first, of 18 January 1841 (R5005), is a despatch of about seventy lines of unseparated digits, 3,969 in all. It was transcribed here in full. The other three (R5006–R5008, 1842–43) are shorter passages inside clear letters. Two of those letters are in German. Background-subtracted crops bring back a few of the pencil words. Over 11 70 82 34 29 40 they give “la pre m i er e”, la première, and “que” stands over 46. The cipher is therefore a two-digit table of letters and syllables, not the homophonic letter cipher DECODE records. DECODE has no Saxon key later than 1806. Homophonic and syllabic annealers, checked on a synthetic control, produce no French. Status: attempted, not solved. What would open it is the key, UV or multispectral imaging of the erased pencil, or a transcription of the three sibling letters.

02 The cipher

The figures are written without separation. Read in pairs, 96 of the 100 possible groups occur. About a third of the lines have an odd number of digits, so a group can run over a line break. Each line’s pair phase was fixed by maximising the pair statistics. Long repeats such as 7778948206 (five times) and 06777818711001 (three times) should be names or set phrases.

The erased decipherment settles the unit size. Seven values can be read from it:

group11708234294046
valuelapremiereque

So the table mixes single letters with syllables (pre, er, que, la). Elsewhere the pencil gives stray words (“pour”, “ce”, “ne”) that cannot be tied to a group with any confidence. Show-through from the reverse side adds false strokes.

03 What was tried

  • Keys and print. Every Dresden key record on DECODE was listed. The latest dates from 1799–1806, and none belongs to fonds 10731. No edition of the Zeschau–Seebach correspondence was found.
  • Structure. Tested single-digit nulls (every set of one to three digits), a prefix code (the first digit fixes a group length of 1, 2 or 3) and pairs in both phases. Only plain pairs are consistent with the glosses.
  • Letter solver. A homophonic annealer with a French 4-gram model reads a synthetic 1,800-letter homophonic control (−2.18 per letter). On R5005 it reaches −3.05 to −3.17 under every segmentation, which is not French.
  • Syllable solvers. An annealer choosing each group’s value from letters plus about 150 common French syllables, with the seven gloss values fixed, was run in three variants: free, one cell per syllable, and length-neutral scoring. All three drift to fluent-looking nonsense.

04 What would finish it

  • The key, or Seebach’s deciphered copies, in the legation papers (HStAD 10731, other numbers) or Seebach’s own papers.
  • UV or multispectral imaging of R5005. The pencil decipherment was once complete, and a rubbed graphite trace often survives under raking or UV light.
  • Transcribing R5006–R5008, which add text in the same table, with clear frames on either side of each cipher passage.
