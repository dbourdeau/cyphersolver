Dresden → St Petersburg · two-digit syllabary · 1841–1843

Zeschau to Seebach, 1841–1843

Hauptstaatsarchiv Dresden, 10731 Sächsische Gesandtschaft in Russland, Nr. 12, DECODE R5005–R5008: four despatches from the Saxon foreign minister to his minister resident in St Petersburg, one wholly in figures and three with cipher passages inside clear French or German.

The despatch of 18 January 1841 was transcribed in full and attacked from the ciphertext alone. The type of cipher, a two-digit table of letters and syllables, and seven of its values were recovered from the traces of an erased contemporary pencil decipherment. No key was recovered and the letters have not been deciphered.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 26 October 2026

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The letters
Four ciphered letters from Heinrich Anton von Zeschau to Albin Leo von Seebach: 18 January 1841 (a despatch of about seventy lines, 3,969 digits), then three shorter passages of 1842–43.
Where
Hauptstaatsarchiv Dresden, 10731 Sächsische Gesandtschaft in Russland Nr. 12; DECODE R5005–R5008, “partially decrypted”.
The cipher
Unseparated digits forming a two-digit table of letters and syllables, not the homophonic letter cipher DECODE records.
How it was tried
Rubbed-out pencil words, brought back by background-subtracted crops, give la première over 11 70 82 34 29 40 and que over 46. Annealing solvers for homophonic and syllabic keys produce no French.
Why it resists
DECODE has no Saxon key later than 1806.
Still open
The key, UV or multispectral imaging of the erased pencil, or a transcription of the three sibling letters would finish it.

02 The cipher

The figures are written without separation. Divided into pairs, the digits use 96 of the 100 possible groups. About a third of the lines have an odd number of digits, so a group can run over a line break. Where each line’s pairs begin was fixed by choosing the division with the strongest pair statistics. Long repeats such as 7778948206 (five times) and 06777818711001 (three times) should be names or set phrases.

The erased decipherment settles the unit size. Seven values are legible in it:

group11708234294046
valuelapremiereque

So the table mixes single letters with syllables (pre, er, que, la). Elsewhere the pencil gives stray words (“pour”, “ce”, “ne”) that cannot be tied to a group with any confidence. Show-through from the reverse side adds false strokes.

03 What was tried

  • Keys and print. Every Dresden key record on DECODE was listed. The latest dates from 1799–1806, and none belongs to fonds 10731. No edition of the Zeschau–Seebach correspondence was found.
  • Structure. Tested single-digit nulls (every set of one to three digits), a prefix code (the first digit fixes a group length of 1, 2 or 3) and pairs in both phases. Only plain pairs are consistent with the glosses.
  • Letter solver. A homophonic solver (simulated annealing) scored with a French 4-gram model deciphers a synthetic 1,800-letter homophonic control (score −2.18 per letter). On R5005 it reaches −3.05 to −3.17 under every division into units, which is not French.
  • Syllable solvers. A solver (simulated annealing) choosing each group’s value from letters plus about 150 common French syllables, with the seven gloss values fixed, was run in three variants: free, one cell per syllable, and length-neutral scoring. All three drift to fluent-looking nonsense.

04 What would finish it

  • The key, or Seebach’s deciphered copies, in the legation papers (HStAD 10731, other numbers) or Seebach’s own papers.
  • UV or multispectral imaging of R5005. The pencil decipherment was once complete, and a rubbed graphite trace often survives under raking or UV light.
  • Transcribing R5006–R5008, which add text in the same table, with clear frames on either side of each cipher passage.
