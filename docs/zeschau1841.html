… la pre mi er …: the one phrase the erased pencil still gives

Dresden → St Petersburg · syllabary in groups of 2, 3 and 4 digits · 1841–1843

Zeschau to Seebach, 1841–1843

Hauptstaatsarchiv Dresden, 10731 Sächsische Gesandtschaft in Russland, Nr. 12, DECODE R5005–R5008: four despatches from the Saxon foreign minister to his minister resident in St Petersburg, one wholly in figures and three with cipher passages inside clear French or German.

The despatch of 18 January 1841 was transcribed in full and attacked from the ciphertext alone. Traces of an erased contemporary pencil decipherment show a table of letters and syllables, and Brice Dudout found that its groups are two, three or four digits long. No key was recovered and the letters have not been deciphered.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 11 November 2026

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The letters
Four ciphered letters from Heinrich Anton von Zeschau to Albin Leo von Seebach: 18 January 1841 (a despatch of seventy lines, 3,764 digits), then three shorter passages of 1842–43.
Where
Hauptstaatsarchiv Dresden, 10731 Sächsische Gesandtschaft in Russland Nr. 12; DECODE R5005–R5008, “partially decrypted”.
The cipher
Unseparated digits in groups of two, three and four, the length set by the first digits; the groups stand for letters and syllables. It is not the homophonic letter cipher DECODE records.
How it was tried
Rubbed-out pencil words, brought back by background-subtracted crops, give la première and que over a few groups. Annealing solvers for homophonic and syllabic keys, run on pairs of digits, produce no French. The division into groups was then found and checked on lines kept aside: 98.3% of their groups fit the rule, against about 70% at most for shuffled text.
Why it resists
DECODE has no Dresden key later than 1806, and none filed with fonds 10731.
Credit
The division into groups of two, three and four digits is Brice Dudout’s (L’Europe chiffrée, pull request 401, 11 November 2026), with AI agents; the measure was re-run here.
Still open
No solver has yet been run on the new groups, and 15 groups that break the rule are to be checked on the images. The key, UV or multispectral imaging of the erased pencil, or a transcription of the three sibling letters would also help.

02 The cipher

The figures are written without separation, and 28 of the 70 lines have an odd number of digits. The first attempt, in September 2026, cut them into pairs (96 of the 100 possible pairs occur) and read the erased pencil over line a5_03 as seven pair values: 11 la, 70 pre, 82 m, 34 i, 29 er, 40 e, and 46 que at the end of another line.

The groups are not pairs. On 11 November 2026 Brice Dudout sent a rule under which the first digits of a group fix its length:

Group starts withLengthForm
0 or 1 (not 00)2 digits0x, 1x
3, 4, 6, 82 digitssecond digit 1, 3, 4, 6 or 8
2, 5, 7, 9 (not 55, 77)3 digitslast digit 1, 3, 4, 6 or 8
00, 55, 774 digits00xx; 55xx and 77xx end in 1, 3, 4, 6 or 8

Groups run on from one line to the next. Under this rule the despatch is 1,309 groups of 190 kinds (337 of two digits, 795 of three, 176 of four, and one stray final digit); 15 groups (1.1%) break the forms and are probably slips of the transcription, to be checked on the images. Within a block of lines no group is immediately repeated. The rule was proposed by an AI model that was given the despatch without two of its eight blocks of lines (a3 and a7, 859 digits). It was then measured once on those two blocks, with the pass mark fixed beforehand at 95%: 291 of their 296 groups fit the forms, 98.3%. The same digits shuffled within their lines, or shuffled as pairs, fit at 63 to 64% on average and never above about 70% in 1,000 trials. Re-run here, the measure gives the same 291 of 296, and fresh shuffles a highest value of 71%. That the model had not seen the two blocks is the contributor’s statement and cannot be checked here; the contributor's own record says the two blocks had been seen as raw digits on 5 November in an earlier, failed test, though never divided into groups.

On whole groups the pencil words over line a5_03 (and the que at the end of a8_05), 06 711 708 234 294 02, fall as follows. These are the September readings re-aligned, proposed and not tested:

group711708234294746
valuelapremier (or re, ere)que

So the table mixes letters with syllables. The sequence 711 708 234 294 occurs twice. Long repeats such as 7778 948 206 (three times as whole groups) and 06 7778 18 (five times) should be names or set phrases. Elsewhere the pencil gives stray words (“pour”, “ce”, “ne”) that cannot be tied to a group with any confidence. Show-through from the reverse side adds false strokes.

03 What was tried

  • Keys and print. Every Dresden key record on DECODE was listed. The latest dates from 1799–1806, and none belongs to fonds 10731. No edition of the Zeschau–Seebach correspondence was found.
  • Structure (September). Tested single-digit nulls (every set of one to three digits), a prefix code (the first digit fixes a group length of 1, 2 or 3) and pairs in both phases. None gave a clear winner, and pairs were kept, and the glosses were read on them. The rule of 2, 3 and 4 digits in section 02 was not among the divisions tested.
  • Letter solver. A homophonic solver (simulated annealing) scored with a French 4-gram model deciphers a synthetic 1,800-letter homophonic control (score −2.18 per letter). On R5005 it reaches −3.05 to −3.17 under every division tried (pairs and two prefix codes), which is not French.
  • Syllable solvers. A solver (simulated annealing) choosing each group’s value from letters plus about 150 common French syllables, with the seven gloss values fixed, was run in three variants: free, one cell per syllable, and length-neutral scoring. All three drift to fluent-looking nonsense. They too worked on pairs.

04 What would finish it

  • A syllable solver run on the 1,309 groups of section 02, after the 15 groups that break the rule are checked on the images. This has not been tried.
  • The key, or Seebach’s deciphered copies, in the legation papers (HStAD 10731, other numbers) or Seebach’s own papers.
  • UV or multispectral imaging of R5005. The pencil decipherment was once complete, and a rubbed graphite trace often survives under raking or UV light.
  • Transcribing R5006–R5008, which add text in the same table, with clear frames on either side of each cipher passage.
