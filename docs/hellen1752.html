The Hague → Frederick II · numerical nomenclators · 1752–1763 · unsolved

Hellen’s intercepted letters to Frederick II

KHA, Prins Willem V, inventory 196 · seven catalogue targets and one related letter · French

The transcription has been audited. An archive collection of deciphered letters offers a lead for 1752, but no matching decipherment has yet been retrieved.

Daniel Bourdeau · attempted 20 September 2026

attempted
On this pageThe documentsWhat the audit correctedThe archival leadWhat remains openSources and working record
Summary. These letters remain unsolved in this investigation: no cipher plaintext or key has been verified. The work so far covers existing DECODE transcriptions and images, a search for parallel texts, and an audit of the parsing used in the earlier attempt. That audit found errors which turned uncertain numbers into definite ones, lost some ciphertext, and inflated the number of unreadable groups. A repeated passage thought to be a transcription duplication is actually present twice on the manuscript. The strongest new lead is Fagel inventory 5206, catalogued as digitized copies of deciphered Hellen letters from 1752–1753. Its scans could not be retrieved during the check on 20 September 2026. This is a progress report, not a decipherment or a proof that recovery is impossible.

01 The documents

W.B. von der Hellen’s reports from The Hague survive here as intercepted copies. The seven-record catalogue group spans 4 January 1752 to 5 July 1763. The related letter of 15 April 1763, DECODE R1046, was included as supporting material. The recipient is Frederick II of Prussia; “Frederick I” in the imported catalogue metadata was an error.

A segment is a nonempty portion of the transcription between separators. An unresolved segment can contain two groups whose separator is missing. Numeric counts ignore underlining for this comparison only; their digits are unambiguous in the transcription, not necessarily correct on the manuscript. The six 1763 letters contain 1,234 such numeric groups, with 634 distinct values.

02 What the audit corrected

The previous parser removed question marks before recognizing numbers: 2 1 ? ?, for example, became the definite value 21. It also counted empty newline fragments as unreadable groups, included the heading year 1752 as ciphertext, and discarded cipher before a cleartext tag on the same line. The revised parser retains uncertainty, source line and page references, underlining and carets, and ciphertext on both sides of cleartext tags.

On R1953, image 13448, the three-line passage beginning 1426 825 963 occurs twice on the manuscript. It must not be deleted as a DECODE transcription duplication. The two versions are not identical, and neither has been established as the correct reading.

The letters are provisionally grouped into 1752, 1756 and 1763 code families. Earlier frequency-density tests did not produce readable text, but that failure does not establish random codebook ordering, prove the proposed homophone clusters, or rule out a future ciphertext-only solution. Those stronger conclusions have been withdrawn.

03 The archival lead

The Nationaal Archief catalogue lists collection 1.10.29, Familie Fagel, inventory 5206 as digitized copies of deciphered Hellen letters from 1752–1753. A matching text for 4 January 1752 could provide known plaintext for reconstructing and checking that letter’s code. The date range alone does not establish that this particular letter is included.

During the 20 September 2026 access check, the old scan link returned 404, the catalogue service timed out, and the current viewer displayed an empty content area with a maintenance notice. This is a dated access problem, not evidence that the documents are unavailable permanently. Open the current archive viewer.

De Leeuw’s study points to Fagel 5177 for British decipherments with Lyonet’s additions, and Boerhaave Museum Arch 162-r for Lyonet and Croiset’s account. He reports no Prussian codes broken between April 1757 and October 1763. That makes a surviving Dutch decipherment a less secure expectation for the 1763 letters than for the earlier material; it does not prove that no parallel text survives elsewhere.

04 What remains open

All enciphered passages remain unread here. No plaintext values have been assigned a verified reading grade. The existing clear passages are context, not decipherments. A search of the printed Politische Correspondenz found replies and contextual references, but no matching plaintext for these targets.

The next steps are to retrieve Fagel 5206, check the 4 January date and opening against R1953, and use any genuine parallel text to derive mappings that can be tested on further passages. The source images also need a fuller collation of doubtful digits, missing separators and marks. A key catalogued as “Prussia 1746” in DECODE R2824 proved to be for a Dutch envoy going to Prussia, so it was rejected as a Prussian chancery-key lead.
