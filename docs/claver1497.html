19 June 1497 · Milan

Juan Claver to the Catholic Monarchs

Go to the full text ↓

Claver relays reports of troops leaving Burgundy after the archduke sought French support.

01 What the text says

This is a letter from Juan Claver in Milan to the Catholic Monarchs, dated 19 June 1497 and kept in the Archivo Histórico Provincial de Zaragoza (Colección Zurita). The letter is mostly in clear Spanish; one passage near the end is enciphered. In it Claver reports on relations among the French king, the archduke and the King of the Romans, and on troops said to be leaving a place in Burgundy after the archduke had sought French support. Claver adds that he does not know which of these reports is true. Who “this most illustrious duke” is was not checked for this page.

And since the archduke was displeased that those troops should be there, in order to drive them out he is said to have sent to ask [uncertain sign] for help from {the King of France}, who ordered the Prince of Orange to help the aforementioned archduke with four or five thousand men. And [upon learning?] this, {the King of the Romans} ordered Alvarado and the other troops to leave that place. All this is being reported, but I do not know what is true. Nevertheless, these matters appear displeasing to this most illustrious duke, and he is distressed that [damaged code] should pursue these things, which are damaging as regards what […] to his honour and reputation.

The English translation of the cipher passage, with its bracketed notes shortened; the full version with the sign notes is under Full texts. Braces mark the two signs that stand for whole words. The Spanish is further down and in the complete letter.

02 The cipher and key

The cipher is homophonic with nomenclator-like word signs. Most signs stand for one letter, and a letter can be written with two or three different signs (d with G12, G17 or G35; n with G05, G25 or G37; a with G09, G22 or G26). Two signs stand for whole words: G32 is rey de Francia and G34 is rey de los Romanos. The key is Ivan Parisi’s published reconstruction of the cipher used in the Ferdinand–Escrivà correspondence (Parisi 2004: alphabet no. 2 on p. 73, nomenclator on p. 72). The labels G01 to G43 are this project’s names for the forms in that alphabet, not numbers in the manuscript.

(V) marks a provisional graphical match with the printed form. Of the 43 key rows, 35 match the printed form (grade P), 5 are provisional (V) and 3 are unresolved (U). Counted over the 437 signs of the passage, 390 are P, 44 are V and 3 are U.

Worked example, the first words of the passage: G01 | G02 G03 G04 | G05 G06 | G07 G08 G09 G10 G11 gives y, que (G02 G03 G04 = q, u, e), no and plazi…, the start of y que no plaziendo. Homophones show in senior (G13 G04 G05 G11 G06 G14), where n is G05, and in gente (G24 G04 G25 G21 G04), where n is G25. The transcription keeps the scribe’s word divisions, and the decipherment is literal: it keeps spellings such as aiuda, onbres and sopiendo and does not modernise them.

Who found it and how it was checked. Alberto Garvía García matched the signs to Parisi’s published table and supplied the transcription and the reading (submission). The profile lists no model as used, so none is credited here. The key was then applied sign by sign to all 11 transcribed lines and gave the filed text. An independent review (Codex, 21 November 2026) reproduced all 11 aligned lines and counted 437 signs; it worked from the committed transcription and the record of sign doubts, not from the manuscript images. Earlier review sampled the manuscript. The comparison of the doubtful signs with the images (the five provisional sign classes and three unresolved signs) rests on the contributor’s description in sign-doubts.md; this project has not recompared them with the images. Whether the cipher passage of this letter was published in deciphered form before is not established in the sources: the profile records it as unknown, and it was not checked here. The documentary edition by Villanueva Morte and Fernández de Córdova prints the passages that were written in clear.

03 What is deciphered and what remains open

Two measures are kept apart. Of the 437 signs, 434 carry a value and three (G31, G40, G42) do not; 44 of the valued signs are provisional matches. Of the cipher words, 100 of 104 are sure (96%), counted by earlier maintainers with the provisional forms accepted on combined evidence; that is above the 95% the project requires. The sources do not list which four words are not sure. The open items below touch words and are the likely ones, but this was not checked. The entry keeps the class recorded in its profile and notes: the key is a published table matched here, not recovered from the ciphertext.

  • G31 in ademand?r (L03): probably a, completing demandar, joined to the preceding d; the contributor’s proposal, graphically uncertain;
  • G40 (L10), on a torn edge: probably rey de los Romanos (G34), which would give “that the King of the Romans should pursue these things”; proposed, not secure;
  • G42 (L11), also at the tear: possibly n (G05), without a complete word;
  • a tear at the start of L11, of roughly two signs, where the word extent is unknown and no letters are supplied;
  • sopiendo (L06): it is what the key gives, and the contributor cites a published discussion of medieval sopiendo (not checked here), but it is an unusual form and the earlier guess sabiendo is only a contextual conjecture;
  • the 44 provisional signs: G11 as i (26 times), G13 as s (12), G23 as h (4), G25 as n (1) and G39 as y (1) fit the printed forms less closely than the other 35 key rows, though each value gives sense every time it occurs.

04 The complete Spanish letter

The surviving clear passages were transcribed anew from the manuscript. The decipherment by Alberto Garvía García is inserted where the figures begin. Brackets record abbreviation expansions and gaps.

[Comienzo perdido] la diessen y muestra su ex[celenci]a algun descontentam[ien]to p[ar]eciendole q[ue] no devian levar esto en dilaçio[n] sino resolverse luego en lo q[ue] entendian de fazer. Y diziendole yo si tenia bien respondido me dixo me faria mostrar la respuesta; si lo fiziere dare della noticia a v[uest]ras a[lteza?]des.

Assi bien depues de scrito lo de arriba viniero[n] cartas de Torin por las quales scriven ciertos avises de Françia, de los quales yra otro sumario con la p[re]sente. Y quanto a la p[ar]te de la yda del rey de Françia a Molins y del ajuste q[ue] alli fazen de algunos cavalleros q[ue] mandan lamar, se cree aqua los leva el duq[ue] de Borbo[n] y ma dama su muger alli por fazer alguna resolucio[n] de lo q[ue] deve fazer el rey de Françia para paz o para guerra. Y como el dicho mons[eño]r de Borbo[n] y su muger y algunos de los que alli van son inclinados a la paz, se faze dello ante juizio de bien q[ue] de mal, aunq[ue] de otra p[ar]te este ill[ustrisi]mo duq[ue] sospecha q[ue] no sea sobre passiones p[ar]ticulares de aquellos cavalleros contra Samalo, visto q[ue] no se nombrava ya el al dicho ajuste.

Otrosi, por un sumario de los de Françia se dize como a la Borgonia avia ocho o X [mil] hombres juntos en dos p[ar]tes: la una era del rey de Romanos, entre la qual era Alvarado con la gente suya q[ue] levo de aqua, y el otra del rey de Françia, y q[ue] no sabian p[ar]a que. E por otra p[ar]te se dezia q[ue] era sobre una differencia q[ue] avia entre el rey de Romanos y el rey de Françia sobre un castillo y q[ue] se avia acordado ya, y q[ue] cada una de las p[ar]tes avia fecho retraer su gente. Y por otro avis q[ue] de Saboya se scrive dizen q[ue] la causa deste ajuste ha sido q[ue] el rey de Romanos embio a Dalvarado co[n] otras co[m]panias de gente en un lugar de la Borgonia.

Pasaje en cifra, descifrado

Y que no plaziendo al senior archiduque que allí estouiese la dicha gente, para poderla echar dizque enbio a demand[?]r aiuda al {rey de Francia}, el qual mandó al príncipe de Orania que aiudase al prefato senior archiduque con quatro o cinco mil onbres; y que [sopiendo: lectura anómala] esto, el {rey de los Romanos} mandó leuantar de aquel lugar a Dalvarado y a la otra gente que allí estaua. Todo esto se escriue, pero no sé qual es lo cierto. Con todo, parecen estas cosas mal a este illustrisimo duque y le pesa que el [G40: código deteriorado] procure estas cosas, las quales danian para lo que […][G42?]ale a su honrra e reputacion.

Final, escrito en claro

Y acreciente Dios las reales […] stado de v[uest]ras a[lteza?]des con victoria de sus enemigos como desean. Fecho [en] [M]ilan a xviiii de junyo de 97.

[De vuestras altezas?] omil criado y sclavo q[ue] sus reales manos y pies besa.

[Juhan Claver: firma parcialmente oscurecida en la imagen.]
