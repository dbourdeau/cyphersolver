12 May 1920 · Gowna

Gowna police telegram

Go to the full text ↓

The three surviving copies describe two vehicles connected to the burning of Gowna barracks.

01 What the text says

Three copies survive of a short police telegram in English. Put together, they say:

[opening group] Description of two motor cars used at burning of Gowna Barrack morning [tenth] instant. No. one, large-sized dark-coloured open Ford car, appeared to be an old one. No. two, a grey-coloured motor van about size of police van, fully covered. [closing group]

So the message describes two vehicles seen in connection with the burning of Gowna barracks: an old, dark, open Ford car, and a grey covered van about the size of a police van. “Instant” means “of this month”, so the morning in question is a day of the same month as the telegram. The day word is spelt OENTH in both complete copies; “tenth” is a conjecture, and the sources call the day doubtful. The first and last groups are letter groups whose meaning is unknown (section 03).

The submission also points to a separate reply from General Headquarters (GHQ) about a plan concerning a whiskey store, and says the two records should be shown together. That reply is not enciphered text on this page and its wording is not in this project’s files: its content is not checked here.

Not checked here: the date of the telegram (12 May 1920), and which copy belongs to Longford and which to Granard. These come from the contributor’s statement and the archive file as described by him; the project has not compared them with the images. The spellings BARACK, APEARED and FULY are the copies’, not corrections.

02 The cipher and key

The cipher is a reciprocal substitution on a keyword alphabet. Write the alphabet as INTERVAL followed by the remaining letters in order, with J left out (25 letters): INTERVALBCDFGHKMOPQSUWXYZ. Pair it letter for letter with the same alphabet backwards, ZYXWUSQPOMKHGFDCBLAVRETNI. Each cipher letter then stands for its partner, and the partner stands for it, so the same table enciphers and deciphers. G is paired with itself. Each plain letter has one cipher letter (no homophones) and there is no nomenclator.

Cipher letterINTERVALBCDFG
Plain letterZYXWUSQPOMKHG

Each column is a pair that works both ways: cipher I is plain Z and cipher Z is plain I, and so on (the 25-letter table is in key.tsv). The keyword INTERVAL reproduces the alphabet, but the keyword actually issued is unknown.

Worked example, the start of the Longford copy: the letters B Y O K become O N B D. The O N B is the opening group, whose meaning is unknown, and D begins the first word. Then V M U Z L X Z B Y becomes S C R I P T I O N, the middle of “description”. The copies run their letters together, so the word divisions in the reading are editorial.

Who found it and how it was checked. Larry Beck, working with ChatGPT, recovered the table from the damaged sheet using a dictionary and a substitution design informed by historical reciprocal alphabets; the contributors transcribed the three copies. The notes say these historical examples were known before the search, so the “ciphertext-only” label for the method is generous: the idea of the key type came from earlier cases, and what was recovered from the text was the table. The 19 filed rows (7 damaged, 6 Longford, 6 Granard) decode to the filed reading with the filed key; this was checked again for this page by decoding transcription.tsv with key.tsv. An independent claims review (grade A) read the combined English and checked the 19 literal lines. It used the filed transcriptions and did not recheck the images, and nor has this project: the transcriptions are as the contributors and the earlier review described the images.

03 What is deciphered and what remains open

Two measures are kept apart. By letters, the key gives a value to every transcribed letter except the signs marked “?”: 186 of 190 in the Longford copy (4 marked), 185 of 190 in the Granard copy (5) and 130 of 148 on the damaged sheet (18, a torn fragment). A value is not a sure reading: the Granard copy has slips of its own, for example MOTNOR for “motor” and DUEARED where Longford has “appeared”. By words, 42 of 45 are sure across the copies (93%): 43 words in the body, with the hyphenated compounds counted as their parts, and the two edge groups. Of the 43 body words, 42 are sure (98%). The entry stays partial because the edge groups count against it.

  • the opening group: ONB in the Longford copy, QPG in the Granard copy. The two complete copies differ and neither is settled;
  • the closing group: NVB (Longford) and N?B (Granard). No framing value for either edge group has been established;
  • the day word OENTH, identical in both copies; “tenth” is only the context’s suggestion;
  • the signs marked “?” on each sheet, which the contributor’s fresh reading kept unresolved; two letters on lines 2 and 4 of the damaged sheet (GYQOQ or EYQOQ, QUWK or RUWK) are transcribed differently by the contributor and the earlier review, and neither choice changes the word count;
  • the date of the telegram and the attribution of the copies, not checked (section 01).

Three copies: Longford and Granard (190 letters each) and a damaged sheet (148 letters).

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (93% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

Reading

reading.md

> [Opening group] Description of two motor cars used at burning of Gowna Barrack morning [tenth] instant. No. one, large-sized dark-coloured open Ford car, appeared to be an old one. No. two, a grey-coloured motor van about size of police van, fully covered. [Closing group]

Spaces, punctuation and standard spelling are editorial. Both complete copies give `BARACK` and `OENTH`. “Be” rests on the Longford form, `APEAREDTOBEANOLDONE`; the Granard copy is garbled at that point (`DUEAREDTOBRANO`). On Beck’s count that makes **42 of 45 units** secure in the combined reading (42 of 43 body words). The uncertain word is the day, for which the context supports “tenth”. The opening and closing groups can be transcribed in places, but their meaning is unknown. That count measures words understood across the copies. Individual sheets still have doubtful letters.
