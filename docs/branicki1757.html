The letters
Four cipher letters of the Branicki family archive: Hetman Jan Klemens Branicki to Antoni Potocki, palatine of Bełz, from Białystok on 22 March 1757 (the catalogue says May); a field report from the Russian army in July 1757; an insider’s report on the fund of the Lithuanian magnates to buy the dietines (1756); and an account of a bloody dietine at Brest, signed Boeven, of 11 September 1756.
Where
Archiwum Główne Akt Dawnych (AGAD), Warsaw, Zbiór Anny z Potockich Ksawerowej Branickiej (zesp. 338), 1/338/0/1/446, 1398, 1399 and 1400 (catalogue 1578 and 1579). Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A reciprocal alphabet: twelve pairs of letters that swap (a stands for h and h for a, b for q, c for r …), with i and j, u and v merged, and a nomenclator of three-digit numbers for titles, names and places in alphabetical order, with number words and nulls.
How it was deciphered
Prabal Gupta found the alphabet from the ciphertext of letter 446 with a count-matching algorithm on French next-letter counts; the same key reads the other three letters with no change, and he built the number code from clear text beside the cipher and the alphabetical order. Checked here: the decryption follows from the transcription and the key, and a word-list test covers 53–59% of the letters against at most 27% for any of 300 shuffled keys.
What it says
Branicki plans a manifesto against the abuse an envoy makes of his mission; the Russian army under Apraxin stands at the frontier of Prussia; a congress at Iwie deposits 146,000 francs to buy the coming dietines, and the five shares add up; at Brest a rival party that spent 200,000 francs has its own deputies elected.
Still open
The rough second page of 1398, some number codes, and a human check of the transcriptions against the AGAD scans, which the project could not reach.
Credit
The key, transcriptions and readings are Prabal Gupta’s (issues #316 and #317; repository, CC BY 4.0). The scans were not seen here and are not reproduced.

01 The letters

In 1756–57 the Seven Years’ War brought a Russian army to the frontier of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, whose magnates were divided into parties for and against the Czartoryski “Familia”. Jan Klemens Branicki was grand hetman of the Crown, and the catalogue calls him the head of the party against the Familia; the letters themselves speak of the dietines, the Tribunal and the money spent to win them. His family archive in Warsaw keeps letters in cipher that the archive’s cards mark as not deciphered. Letter 446 is one of his own, in French, with a clear opening and close around a paragraph in cipher. The three others are reports to a patron: 1398 from an agent with the Russian army, 1399 from a man close to the Lithuanian grand hetman Radziwłł, and 1400 from Nieśwież. How these men fit the parties is the contributor’s reading from a 1909 history, not checked here.

Letter 446’s dateline gives 22 March 1757. The catalogue’s “May” was a misreading at low resolution.

02 The key and its test

The key swaps letters in pairs: a-h, b-q, c-r, d-s, e-l, f-t, g-z, i-m, k-x, n-o, p-u, w-y. The contributor’s count-matching algorithm found it on the cipher of letter 446 and returned it, on 1399 and 1400 taken alone, on 99.4% and 98.9% of tokens; applied unchanged, it reads 1398, 1399 and 1400 as French. The three-digit numbers are a table in alphabetical order (215 Castellan, 330 Excellence, 575 Palatin, 620 Prince, 625 Prusse, 655 Russie, 740 Tribunal), with number words at 805 to 824, so that 1400 dates itself in cipher: 824 811 823 818 810, “mil sept cent cinquante six”. Where the writer left a name in clear next to the cipher, the number was matched to it: “Mr le Palatin de Trok” in clear and “Mr le [575] de Trok” in cipher.

Checks made here, with the scans out of reach: the decryption of 446 follows from the transcription and the key on every line; a list of about 140 common French words covers 0.53 to 0.59 of the decrypted letters of the four letters, against 0.27 at most for any of 300 shuffled keys; and the five shares of 1399, 53,000 + 25,000 + 50,000 + 10,000 + 8,000, add up to the 146,000 francs the letter states. A French language model could not be built from this environment, so the project’s n-gram validation is not applicable.

03 The reading

Letter 446 reads at 96.7% (12 holes in 369 cipher words), 1399 at 98.3% and 1400 at 99.4%; 1398 reads at 95.5%, but its second page has a rough transcription and a reading that repairs garbled stretches by sense, so it is the one part only partly deciphered. Over the four letters 97.5% of the cipher words are deciphered. In English, Branicki writes: “Although the plan agreed at Warsaw meets obstacles from our friends, I do not at all believe that we should lose sight of it … the most pressing matter is the abuse that Mr the Stolnik of Lithuania makes of his mission … I believe there is no time to lose for the publication of the manifesto that I want to make together with you and your whole house.” The contributor reads the envoy as Stanisław Poniatowski, later king, who held that title and was in St Petersburg; that identification, like his reading of Boeven as the hetman’s secretary on the French payment list, rests on one printed book that was not opened here. The Spanish, French and English texts are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • No transcription was compared with a scan: the AGAD images are not reachable from the project. The contributor corrected his first transcriptions against the scans on 7 November 2026 (820, 822 and 823 are 620, 622 and 623, and others).
  • Page 2 of 1398 is rough; the reading of the two transcription passes joined by the key and a French character model is the contributor’s, and several stretches stay open.
  • The number codes 195, 296, 367, 385, 386, 480, 496, 580, 775, 795 and 841 are not read; others (720 Stolnik, 350 France, 270 and 273 Czesnik) are guesses, shown as doubtful.
  • Letter 1399 is probably of 1756 and 1398 of 1757; neither has a year in clear. Letter 1401, listed with them in the catalogue, is not covered by this key.

Branicki to Potocki, Białystok, 22 March 1757 (AGAD 1/338/0/1/446)

Branicki tells Potocki that the plan agreed at Warsaw meets obstacles but must not be lost from sight, that the most pressing matter is the abuse an envoy (probably the stolnik of Lithuania) makes of his mission, and proposes a manifesto to be published together with Potocki's house and other patriots, to meet at the end of the next month.

Monseigneur,

J'ai reçu la lettre que Votre Excellence m'a fait l'honneur de m'écrire le 6 de ce mois, et je ne perds pas un moment pour y faire réponse.

## En chiffre

Quoique le projet concerté à Varsovie rencontre des obstacles de la part de nos amis, je ne crois nullement que nous devions le perdre de vue.

Je prévois même des [?événements] qui en rendront l'exécution indispensable.

Mais l'objet le plus pressant qui doit nous occuper dans ce moment est l'abus que fait Mr le [?Stolnik] [de] Lithuanie de sa mission.

N'est-il pas d'ailleurs très vraisemblable que la Russie fera valoir ce prétexte pour exiger de la [?Pologne] un dédommagement d'une partie des fraix de la guerre à laquelle ce prétendu ministre l'a invitée de la part du [?Roi] ?

J'en ai déjà prévenu Mr le Palatin [?de Kiovie] [?et le Czesnik] ; ce dernier est très résolu de se joindre à moi dans une affaire si intéressante pour la patrie, et je me flatte que d'autres bons patriotes se feront un devoir de suivre notre exemple.

Je prie votre Excellence de ne pas nous refuser sa présence dans une [?occasion] de cette importance, où nous pourrons en même tems nous arranger sur le parti à prendre suivant les conjonctures. [367]

Comme il n'y a encore rien de décidé sur la route que prendront les troupes russiennes, je réglerai ma résolution là-dessus par rapport au séjour que je pourrai choisir pour m'y fixer, en attendant le retour de la [?paix].

J'ignore en effet quelles peuvent être les vues de Mr le Staroste de Kaniow et de Madame la Castellane de Kamien, qui le guide.

En demandant la continuation de son amitié, je lui offre toute l'étendue de la mienne et les assurances de l'attachement respectueux avec lequel j'ai l'honneur d'être, Monseigneur, de Votre Excellence le très humble et très obéissant serviteur, Comte Branicki.

# AGAD 1/338/0/1/446: English translation

Białystok, 22 March 1757.

## In clear

Monseigneur,

I have received the letter that Your Excellency did me the honour of writing to me on the 6th of this month, and I do not lose a moment in replying.

## In cipher

Although the plan agreed at Warsaw meets obstacles from our friends, I do not at all believe that we should lose sight of it.

I even foresee [?events] that will make carrying it out indispensable.

But the most pressing matter that must occupy us at this moment is the abuse that Mr the [?Stolnik] of Lithuania is making of his mission.

The foresight and insight of Your Excellency are too well known to me for me to dwell on the unhappy consequences this sending will bring after it, the least of which is the general contempt into which the Republic will fall abroad, when people see that no one among us rises against so open a violation of the fundamental laws of the state.

Is it not, besides, very likely that Russia will use this pretext to demand from [?Poland] compensation for part of the costs of the war, to which this so-called minister invited her on behalf of the [?King]?

So I believe, my lord, that there is no time to lose in publishing the manifesto that I want to make together with you and with your whole house.

I have already told Mr the Palatine [?of Kiev] [?and the Czesnik]; the latter is firmly resolved to join me in an affair so important for the fatherland, and I flatter myself that other good patriots will make it their duty to follow our example.

I even have very positive assurances of this from some palatinates.

So I have agreed with Mr the Palatine [?of Kiev] [480] and with [?the Czesnik] of the Crown to confer with them toward the end of next month, asking them to come here at that time, or else to name a place where I can go and join them.

I beg Your Excellency not to refuse us your presence at an [?occasion] of this importance, where we can at the same time agree on the course to take according to events. [367]

As nothing is yet decided about the route that the Russian troops will take, I shall settle my decision on that, as to the stay I may choose in order to fix myself there, while awaiting the return of [?peace].

## In clear

I do not indeed know what the aims may be of Mr the Staroste of Kaniów and of Madame the Castellane of Kamieniec, who guides him.

I know that in this affair I carried my indulgence further than it should have gone, and I have good reason to repent of it, because I get no gratitude for it.

Mr Delachinal never asked me to spare him the arrest. It was at the urging of Mr the Palatine of Kiev and Mr the Czesnik of the Crown that I relaxed this part of the decree.

Besides, this officer has just left the service of the Republic, which he would not have done if he had not found himself caught in so vexing a lawsuit. Major Auspitz has been under arrest for more than three months.

The remarks that Madame the Castellane has made about me, and the steps being taken as a result, release me from all further consideration.

I send Your Excellency no news, knowing that your other correspondents keep you informed. Asking for the continuation of your friendship, I offer you the full extent of mine, and the assurances of the respectful attachment with which I have the honour to be, Monseigneur, Your Excellency's very humble and very obedient servant, Count Branicki.

Nieswiz, ce onze septembre mil sept cent cinquante six

Monsieur,

L'on apprend ici tous les jours des nouvelles fort tragiques des diétines de notre Lithuanie. De quel côté qu'on se tourne, l'on ne rencontre que des cadavres de gentilshommes massacrés à ces assemblées tumultueuses, qu'on mène aux grods et au Tribunal pour y être présentés, funeste suite de cette animosité qui déchire la République.

Il y eut cette fois à Brest de Lithuanie, pour l'élection des nonces à la Diète générale, une diétine des plus tumultueuses. Tout y respiroit que rage, fureur et carnage. Frère contre frère, parent contre parent s'y déchaînoit avec la dernière inhumanité.

Mr le comte Sapieha Palatin s'y étoit trouvé pour maintenir le bon ordre et tâcher d'amener les choses au paisible choix de nonces dans ce palatinat, qui puissent entrer dans nos vues. Mais hélas, ce seigneur, dont les remontrances n'ont pu faire la moindre impression sur la noblesse furieuse, est heureux d'être échappé de ses mains sain et sauf.

Tous, excepté un fort petit nombre, étoient du parti contraire, qui a élu arbitrairement pour nonces Mrs Oziemblowski et Suzin, lieutenant de Mr Sosnowski cassé par le décret connu, apparemment pour engager le Prince Grand Général à se relâcher en quelque façon de la rigueur de la sentence portée contre lui, quoique cette élection étant illégale ne pourra se soutenir.

Une douzaine de gentilshommes, qui ne manqueront pas aussi de se rendre à Varsovie pour empêcher l'activité de ces nonces illégitimement élus, ayant protesté solennellement contre cet acte au grod de Bielsk en Podlachie.

À la tête des mutins se trouvoit Mr [?Buchowiecki], notaire territorial dudit palatinat, autrefois de nos amis, mais qui depuis nous a tourné casaque. Celui-ci à cette diétine a bien taillé de la besogne à Mr le Palatin.

Ce turbulent prétend encore jusqu'à présent engager ce palatinat à une revue [385] de la noblesse, premier pas d'une confédération. Mais Mr Wereszczaka, enseigne de ce palatinat, soutenu de Mr le Palatin, s'y oppose fortement.

Tout ce remue-ménage provient de la libéralité du parti contraire, qui a répandu deux cent mille francs dans tous les palatinats et districts de Lithuanie. C'est pourquoi Mr le Palatin, prévoyant que la somme que nous étions sur le point de faire compter à ce palatinat n'effectueroit rien sur la noblesse déjà gagnée depuis longtemps par nos adversaires, a jugé à propos de ne la point hasarder inutilement.

Je suis, Monsieur, votre très humble, très obéissant serviteur Boeven.

P. S. À Nowogrod il fut impossible d'engager la noblesse d'insérer la réhabilitation du Sr Abramowicz dans l'instruction des nonces, la plupart, qui a des procès au Tribunal, appréhendant que [?la Famille] ne lui fasse payer bien cher une telle démarche.

Mr Oziemblowski, un des nonces illégitimement élus à Brest de Lithuanie, towarzysz de Mr le Palatin, a quitté le service militaire.

Nieśwież, 11 September 1756

Sir,

Every day we hear here very tragic news of the dietines of our Lithuania. Whichever way one turns, one meets only the corpses of gentlemen massacred at these riotous assemblies, which are carried to the courts and to the Tribunal to be presented there: the deadly result of this hatred that is tearing apart the Republic.

This time at Brest of Lithuania, for electing the deputies to the general Diet, there was a dietine of the most riotous kind. Everything there breathed only rage, fury and carnage. Brother against brother, kin against kin, were let loose with the utmost inhumanity.

Count Sapieha, the Palatine, was there to keep good order, and to try to bring about a peaceful choice of deputies in this palatinate who could serve our aims. But alas, this lord, whose protests made not the least impression on the furious nobles, is lucky to have escaped their hands safe and sound.

All of them, except a very small number, were of the opposite party, which arbitrarily elected as deputies Messrs Oziemblowski and Suzin, the lieutenant of Mr Sosnowski, who was struck down by the known decree. Apparently this was to get the Prince Grand General to relax somewhat the severity of the sentence passed against him, although this election, being illegal, will not hold.

A dozen gentlemen, who will not fail to go to Warsaw as well to block the work of these illegally elected deputies, have solemnly protested against this act at the court of Bielsk in Podlachia.

At the head of the mutineers was Mr [?Buchowiecki], territorial notary of that palatinate, once one of our friends, but who has since turned his coat. At this dietine he made plenty of work for Mr the Palatine.

This troublemaker still claims he will bring this palatinate to a review [385] of the nobles, the first step toward a confederation. But Mr Wereszczaka, ensign of this palatinate, backed by Mr the Palatine, strongly opposes it.

All this uproar comes from the generosity of the opposite party, which has scattered two hundred thousand francs across all the palatinates and districts of Lithuania. So Mr the Palatine, foreseeing that the sum we were about to pay out to this palatinate would achieve nothing with nobles long since won over by our adversaries, judged it best not to risk it for nothing.

I am, Sir, your very humble and very obedient servant Boeven.

P.S. At Nowogród it proved impossible to get the nobles to include the rehabilitation of Sieur Abramowicz in the deputies' instructions. Most of them, who have lawsuits before the Tribunal, fear that [?the Familia] would make them pay dearly for such a step.

Mr Oziemblowski, one of the deputies illegally elected at Brest of Lithuania, a towarzysz of Mr the Palatine, has left military service.
