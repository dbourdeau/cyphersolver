Paris → Sir William Cecil · homophonic symbol cipher with name signs · June 1567 – March 1568 · read in part

Sir Henry Norreys to Cecil, 1567–68 — the ciphered words of seven despatches

→

BL Add MS 4136 ff. 160–161 (DECODE R9251, and the foot of R9250) · Patrick Forbes’s 1730s copy of only the enciphered words, numbered · catalogue 90

No key survives on DECODE · the Cecil–Norris cipher, reconstructed by Satoshi Tomokiyo from a decipherment printed by mistake in Cabala (1663), reads the sibling letters against the Calendar of State Papers · the target letter’s three items read in part

Daniel Bourdeau · read in part 21 September 2026

Summary. DECODE lists R9251 as a non-decrypted letter of Sir Henry Norreys (Norris), Elizabeth’s ambassador in France, to Cecil, “9 Mar 1567”. It is three pages of Patrick Forbes’s deciphering file. Only the top of the first page is Norreys’s, and there Forbes copied just the ciphered words of three letters, 9 Feb, 24 Feb and 9 Mar 1567/8. The date is Old Style: the letter is of 9 March 1568. Four more Norreys letters (June–July 1567 and 6 Feb 1568) are on the page before. None of the keys filed in the volume fits, but Tomokiyo’s partial table of the Cecil–Norris cipher does. Under it the groups come out as demaund, reason, ruin, remain and Master Stewarde, words the Calendar’s summaries of the same letters contain (“Stewart’s name in cipher”). The same table reads the lord of Murrei (10 July 1567), Savoia … intend to ouerthrou … and England (4 June 1567) and Rothes, papystes, reaums, stayid (6 Feb 1568). With the letters shuffled among the same signs (1,000 trials) the groups give on average 0.2 dictionary words and at most 3, against 8 for the real table. The target letter has only three ciphered items. The first is the French King’s name sign, turned (H: the same sign stands for the French King on 6 and 9 February); the third, a closed-loop name sign, is probably the Queen of Scots (I, by position). The middle one, four signs, reads a t ? l and is not read: “Pope” and “Catholic” were both tested against the cipher passages printed in Cabala and rejected. The originals in TNA SP 70, which may carry the clerks’ decipherments, were not seen.

01The documents

Add MS 4136 is Forbes’s working file for his Full View of the Public Transactions in the Reign of Q. Elizabeth (1740–41). He copied the enciphered stretches of the ambassadors’ letters and filed the keys at ff. 177–190 (see the Smith write-up). The Norreys section is:

02The key

The key records in the volume (R9257 Percy, R9260 Throckmorton’s first and second ciphers, R9261 Smith and Croft, R9262 Throckmorton’s third) do not match Norreys’s signs. A crib search against the Calendar’s wording was inconclusive: the groups are too short and too many words fit. The name signs did give three identifications, because they recur in the same order in the 9 and 24 February letters, and both Calendar entries run “the Protestants … the Prince [of Condé] … the Admiral”.

The break came from Tomokiyo’s page on Elizabethan ciphers. Cabala (1663) prints Cecil’s cipher letters to Norris, and its “Postscript” to 27 September 1568 is really the decipherment of that letter’s cipher passages. From it Tomokiyo rebuilt a partial table. Signs may be turned in any orientation (⊏ = ⊔ = e, Γ = L = d), and single signs stand for persons. Applied to Norreys’s groups:

Letter, itemReadingCalendar
6 July 1567 (1)Master Stewarde“Signed: Stewart’s name in cipher”
4 June 1567 (6)#… Savoia … the in[t]e[n]d to ouerthrou # # a[nd] Engla[n]d“the King of Spain, the Emperor, with the Duke of Savoy intend to overthrow the Protestants of France, Flanders, and England”
10 July 1567 (2)the lord of Murrei … (rest garbled)“the Earl of Murray”
6 Feb 1568 (1)–(4), (7), (16)Leisly; [the] earl; Rothes; papystes; reaums (realms); stayid“one Lesley, brother to the Earl of Rothes, who came from the Papists of Scotland”
9 Feb 1568 (1), (3), (4)demaund; reason; peac[e]“whether now the demand of Calais were not very expedient … a mean to make peace”
9 Feb 1568 (5)–(9)the French King, the Protestants; ruin; the Prince, the Admiral“peace betwixt the French King and the Protestants, otherwise Norris much doubts the ruin of the Prince of Condé and the Admiral”
24 Feb 1568 (2)–(4), (6), (8)the Protestants, the Prince, the Admiral; money; remain“the Prince and the Admiral … that they may have money to pay the Almains, whom otherwise he much fears will not long remain with them”

Control. The script work/tomokiyo_apply.py counts dictionary words (the, and, lord, papists, realms, Rothes, earl, stayed, reason, ruin, master, steward…) in the decrypted groups. The real table gives 8 hits. With its letter values shuffled among the same signs (1,000 seeded trials) the mean is 0.22 and the maximum 3, and no shuffle produces a whole cribbed word such as Rothes, papystes, Steuarde, Murrei or Savoia. The table is Norris’s cipher, used from 1567 and not only in 1568.

03The letter of 9 March 1568

The Calendar (no. 2054): “Upon M. Montmorency’s first return from the treaty holden at Longjumeau the King despatched four gentlemen, viz., to the Pope, the Emperor, the King Catholic, and the Swiss … Thinks if peace be concluded some attempt will be made into Scotland for the delivery of the Queen.” Forbes copied three items:

ItemSignsReadingGrade
(1)ꝺ (turned)the [French] KingH: the same name sign as in 6 Feb (5), (13) and 9 Feb (5), where it matches the Calendar’s “the French King”
(2)crossed arrowhead, o, loop, ∞not read: a t ? lopen. Cabala p. 152 prints the crossed arrowhead inside the “all” before papists, where p is 4 and 4+, so it is not p, and “Pope” is withdrawn. + = a in Norreys’s own “earl” (6 Feb), o = t (papists), ∞ = l; the loop is unmatched. “Catholic”, the first guess, is withdrawn too
(3)∂ (closed loop)the Queen [of Scots]?I: by position against “for the delivery of the Queen”; the sign occurs nowhere else

04What remains open

  • 9 March (2), a t ? l, not read; (3) the Queen of Scots only by position (I). The loop sign is not in Tomokiyo’s table.
  • 4 June 1567 items 1, 3, 4 and 7, and the rest of 10 July (2): the token transcription of those lines needs redoing.
  • 6 Feb 1568 items 9, 12, 17, 20 and 23; 9 Feb 10 and 11; 24 Feb 1 and 7.
  • Cabala (1663) pp. 142–173 would give the signs missing from the table (g, k, q, w, the loop) and perhaps the name symbols.
  • The originals, TNA SP 70/96–97, probably carry interlinear decipherments by Cecil’s clerks. They were not seen.
