Walsingham to Edward Wotton in Scotland, July–September 1585

British Library, Add MS 32657 ff. 125–127, 140–141, 163–164, 194–195 (DECODE R4838, R4839, R4840, R4844). The Secretary’s letters to his ambassador during the Arran crisis, the replies to which are on the companion page.

The letters are in clear English; two were already in print as calendar abstracts, and the other two are transcribed here. The name code was recovered from the calendar’s glosses and from context, and most of its numbers are identified. The fourteen words in graphic signs of 10 September, about 120 signs, have no key.

At a glanceKey recovered from external plaintext · partial (49% of the cipher)

The letters
Four letters from Sir Francis Walsingham to Edward Wotton, English ambassador in Scotland, 28 July to 10 September 1585. They are in clear English, with persons and countries as two-digit numbers.
Where
British Library, Add MS 32657, DECODE R4838, R4839, R4840 and R4844. The Unsolved Catalogue (item 82) listed them as “Non-decrypted”.
The cipher
A two-digit name code, plus about fourteen words (some 120 signs) in 10 September spelled in an alphabet of geometric signs.
How it was read
The Calendar of State Papers, Scotland, vol. 8 glosses the numbers: 19 Arran, 39 the Master of Gray, 40 the Justice Clerk, 24 Farnihurst, 20 Morton. Two letters are calendared there; the others are transcribed here.
What it says
Walsingham advises Wotton on Scottish politics, for example urging that the King do justice after the killing of Lord Russell on the Border.
Still open
The sign words have no key, and the decipherment written over each was blotted out. Numbers 13, 14, 23, 30 and 37 are unidentified.
Credit
Name-code glosses: Calendar of State Papers, Scotland, vol. 8 (ed. Boyd, 1914).

03 The letters

28 July 1585, Nonsuch (R4838; CSP no. 40)

Walsingham is sorry to hear of a move in Scotland to ask for an English duchy for the King: it would offend the Queen and breed jealousy against the league. Wotton should dissuade it through Gray. On the slip of f. 126: 13 is “affected to 19” and Wotton should deal warily with 13 in anything that concerns Arran.

7 August 1585, the Court (R4839; CSP no. 65)

After the killing of Lord Russell on the Border, the Russells’ friends are incensed against Farnihurst (24) and Arran (19). Wotton is to insist that the King do justice and not set Arran at liberty.

21 August 1585, the Court (R4840)

“39 dothe complayne in a late letter wrytten to his frende here … of the alteration of your countenaunce towardes him.” Gray offers to prove himself if 28 and 29 come down to the Borders to join with 20 (Maxwell); without that, he fears that “10 wyll be carried awaye from 36, the party of 11 increasing”. Walsingham trusts none of them: “The best is to deale warely with them all.” He advises Wotton to tell Gray of the suspicion without naming its author. The Queen wants Farnihurst (24) imprisoned. Wotton’s draft answer of 30 August is on the companion page.

The opening, re-read on the image for the quotation list (30 Sept 2026; bracketed words are grade M readings of hard places in the secretary hand): “Sir, 39 dothe complayne in a late letter wrytten to his frende here, whom you knowe, of the alteration of your countenaunce towardes him, with the [courage] to procure the practice of 27 and 40, which as he professeth dothe greatly [abuse] you, being altogether bent to [ruin] 11 course; and for himself he voweth to be altogether at 3 devotion, whereof he offereth to make a most manifest proofe” (spelling restored on the images on 23 October 2026). The earlier transcription had “overthrow 11”; the manuscript has a short word and course after the number (“11 course”, as in Wotton’s answer of 30 August: “I could never perceive cause to suspect them in the 11 course”). 3 here is Gray’s object of devotion, so the Queen or England (M); Wotton’s draft of 25 August has “divert him from 3” but also “convey him towards 3 … into France”, so the number is not settled. The glosses of 28, 29 and 36 could not be checked again against CSP Scotland viii on 30 September (the HathiTrust text view now sits behind a bot check and the Internet Archive copy is lending-only); they stay grade M.

10 September 1585, the Court (R4844)

Decoded, not read. The sign words and the open name codes fall in nearly every sentence of this letter, so it counts as decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026); the 21 August letter reads. Wotton’s letters of 1 September have bred “some better lykinge of the [signs] set downe by 39”. This is Gray’s plan to seize the King. The 28 and 29 “wyll be [signs]”; 13 “wyll in no sorte neyther be [signs] nor otherwyse make [signs] [signs] a partye”. Wotton is to confer with the King so as to be “[signs] at the [signs]”, but must “warde your [signs]” and avoid [signs]. In the postscript Walsingham sends on a letter and reports that [23] “is the principal dealer in [37] between 10 and the Jesuites”. The working transcriptions of both uncalendared letters (reading_R4840.txt, reading_R4844_r2.txt, with the gaps read on the images merged into the second on 22 October 2026) keep the secretary’s spelling, re-read on the DECODE images on 23 October.

04 The sign words

The sign words are a second cipher, unrelated to the name code, and are counted on their own: about 23 words, none read. The name code alone reads at 34 of its 46 occurrences.

About fourteen words, some 120 signs in all, are written in an alphabet of geometric and letter-like signs: squares, triangles, =, ≠, ÷, a crossed 4, ω, and borrowed letters f, d, c, k, R. They also carry dots and dashes that look like nulls. The same signs (square, triangle, ≠) open many words, which suggests homophones. Over every sign word Wotton, or his secretary, wrote the decipherment and later scribbled it out in loops. Nothing of it can be read on the scan.

No key was found. DECODE has no key record for Wotton or Walsingham’s Scottish correspondence, and Tomokiyo’s survey of Elizabethan ciphers has none for 1585 (his “Wotton” cipher is Nicholas Wotton’s of 1548). Wotton’s drafts in the same volume use the numbers but no signs, so they give no crib. A homophonic alphabet with nulls cannot be recovered from the ciphertext alone when it has only 120 signs. The words are copied as far as they can be in targets/walsingham1585/NOTES.md.

05 What remains open

  • The fourteen sign words of 10 September (R4844), without a key. They would yield to Wotton’s cipher key or to any other letter in this alphabet with its decipherment, most likely among the sent despatches in TNA SP 52/37–38.
  • Numbers 13, 14, 23, 30, 37, unidentified; 27, 28, 29, 36 possible only.
  • The transcriptions of 21 August and 10 September are working texts with gaps, not editions.

R4840, Add MS 32657 ff. 163-164, the Court, 21 Aug 1585

Walsingham warns Edward Wotton, ambassador to James VI, that the Master of Gray [39] complains of Wotton's coldness and offers proof of his devotion if the banished lords join Maxwell [20] on the Borders, lest the King be carried away from England while the French party grows; Walsingham advises dealing warily with them all. # Walsingham to Wotton 1585: gap readings from the DECODE images

Grades: H certain, C probable, M possible, illegible (reason). Spelling as written where it matters.

## R4840 (21 Aug 1585), f. 163r = IMG_R4840_I28147_P1.jpg

1. l.12 "40 did greatly [..] in his [..] whilst he was here" -> **wauer** (waver). H.
2. same line, "in his [..] whilst" -> **wordes** (words). H. ("whyles he was here")
3. l.13 "27 was always [..] of late devoted unto 11" -> **vntle** / untill (until): v-n-t-l-e, last letters at the paper edge. C.
   Sense: "27 was always, until of late, devoted unto 11".
4. l.14 "for they are all [..]" -> **borne**. H.
5. l.14 "and all become [..]" -> the transcription is wrong here: the line reads
   **"for they are all borne under one planett"**. "and all become" is not on the page; it is **vnder one**
   (H) + **planett** (C: p-l-?-net with a cross-stroke through the p/l).
   So gaps 4+5 together: "they are all borne under one planet."
6. l.14 "it were not amiss [..] you acquainted 39" -> the page has "amysse" + a small e-like stroke + "doe" + a
   z-shaped y (the same y as in "they" on the line above) + "you acquainted". Letters seen: **"doe y[e]"**? M.
   Not certain of the syntax; "doe" itself is clear (H for the letters d-o-e).
7. l.15 "with the [..] of such suspicion" -> **groundes** (grounds). H.
   (Interlinear insertion above "proceeding" at the line end, apparently "the auctour of" or similar; not a gap
   in the transcription, not checked further.)
8. l.16 "suspicion, [..] in my judgement" -> **can**. H.
9. l.16 "in my judgement [..], but I leave it" -> **bee no grevance** (be no grievance): "bee no" H at line end,
   "grevance" C at the start of the next line.
   Transcription correction: after it the page reads "**and yet doe I leave** yt to your best judgement"
   ("and" abbreviated, then "yet doe I"), not "but I leave it".

## R4840, f. 164r(v) = IMG_R4840_I28147_P2.jpg

22. postscript l.26 "My letters out of 32 [..] I am advertised" -> nothing is written between "32" and "I am"
    on the line. The only extra word is a **struck-out interlinear word** under "letters" ("Comp..."?),
    blotted. No gap here: "My letters out of 32. I am advertised that 23 ...". ("out" C: o-u-t, could be "ont".)
    Also: above "37" there is a struck interlinear word ("Scot:"? blotted) not in the transcription.
23. postscript l.27 "I think the following [..] 19 [..]" -> "the followinge | wilbe" (**wilbe** C, w-l-b with a
    point), then a word p/x-e + a faded word under a stain ("...hm"), illegible (stain, crossed by a
    descender); after 19 **dyrectly** (directly) M (d-y-r-e-c-t-l-y, "ct" unclear).

## Transcription corrections found (both letters)
- R4840 f.163 l.14: "they are all [..] and all become [..]" is **"they are all borne under one planett"**.
- R4840 f.163 l.16: "but I leave it" is **"and yet doe I leave yt"**.
- R4840 f.164 l.18: "the stranger" is **"the ffrenche (French) K."**
- R4840 f.164 l.19: "specially the" is **"consideringe the"**.
- R4840 f.164 l.23: the word read "more" in "that the more [..]" looks like "mely/rely"; the next line starts
  "that the daye" (the transcription drops this "that"). Not resolved.
- R4844 l.7: "be as well [..] as 39" is **"as well falle out to 39"**; the [..] after "taken" is a deletion.
- R4844 l.12: "wherein 13 [is]" is **"thorowe 13"**.
- R4844 l.16: "I have not [..] that" is **"I hope notwithstanding that"**.
- R4844 postscript: "of the [..] father" is **"that he hath farther"**; "32 [..] I am" has no word in the gap.
