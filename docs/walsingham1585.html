Walsingham to Edward Wotton in Scotland, July–September 1585

→

British Library, Add MS 32657 ff. 125–127, 140–141, 163–164, 194–195 (DECODE R4838, R4839, R4840, R4844). The Secretary’s letters to his ambassador during the Arran crisis, the replies to which are on the companion page.

All four letters read; two already in print as calendar abstracts. The name code is identified from the calendar’s glosses; the fourteen sign words of 10 September are not.

Daniel Bourdeau · read in part 21 September 2026 · updated 22 September 2026

Summary. The Unsolved Catalogue (item 82) listed four DECODE records of Walsingham to Edward Wotton, 28 July to 10 September 1585, as “Non-decrypted; alphabet, graphic signs, numerical”. The images show signed letters in clear English, in a secretary’s hand, in which persons and countries are written as two-digit numbers (“39 doth complain in a late letter”). The Calendar of State Papers, Scotland vol. 8 (1914), read through the HathiTrust text view, already calendars the letters of 28 July (no. 40) and 7 August (no. 65) from Walsingham’s drafts, which name the persons. It also glosses the numbers in Wotton’s own despatches: 19 Arran, 39 the Master of Gray, 40 the Justice Clerk, 24 Farnihurst, 20 Morton. The letters of 21 August and 10 September are not in the calendar and are transcribed here. The letter of 10 September also carries about fourteen words spelled in a sign alphabet. Above each of them Wotton’s decipherment was written and then blotted out. With about 120 signs, no key and no crib, these words remain unread.

03 The letters

28 July 1585, Nonsuch (R4838; CSP no. 40)

Walsingham is sorry to hear of a move in Scotland to ask for an English duchy for the King: it would offend the Queen and breed jealousy against the league. Wotton should dissuade it through Gray. On the slip of f. 126: 13 is “affected to 19” and Wotton should deal warily with 13 in anything that concerns Arran.

7 August 1585, the Court (R4839; CSP no. 65)

After the killing of Lord Russell on the Border, the Russells’ friends are incensed against Farnihurst (24) and Arran (19). Wotton is to insist that the King do justice and not set Arran at liberty.

21 August 1585, the Court (R4840)

“39 doth complain in a late letter written to his friend here … of the alteration of your countenance towards him.” Gray offers to prove himself if 28 and 29 come down to the Borders to join with 20 (Maxwell); without that, he fears that “10 will be carried away from 36, the party of 11 increasing”. Walsingham trusts none of them: “The best is to deal warily with them all.” He advises Wotton to tell Gray of the suspicion without naming its author. The Queen wants Farnihurst (24) imprisoned. Wotton’s draft answer of 30 August is on the companion page.

10 September 1585, the Court (R4844)

Wotton’s letters of 1 September have bred “some better liking of the [signs] set down by 39”. This is Gray’s plan to seize the King. The 28 and 29 “will be [signs]”; 13 “will in no sort neither be [signs] nor otherwise make [signs] a party”. Wotton is to confer with the King so as to be “[signs] at the [signs]”, but must “ward your [signs]” and avoid [signs]. In the postscript Walsingham sends on a letter and reports that 23 “is the principal dealer in 37 between 10 and the Jesuits”. Working transcriptions of both uncalendared letters, with gaps, are in the repository folder.

04 The sign words

About fourteen words, some 120 signs in all, are written in an alphabet of geometric and letter-like signs: squares, triangles, =, ≠, ÷, a crossed 4, ω, and borrowed letters f, d, c, k, R. They also carry dots and dashes that look like nulls. The same signs (square, triangle, ≠) open many words, which suggests homophones. Over every sign word Wotton, or his secretary, wrote the decipherment and later scribbled it out in loops. Nothing of it can be read on the scan.

No key was found. DECODE has no key record for Wotton or Walsingham’s Scottish correspondence, and Tomokiyo’s survey of Elizabethan ciphers has none for 1585 (his “Wotton” cipher is Nicholas Wotton’s of 1548). Wotton’s drafts in the same volume use the numbers but no signs, so they give no crib. A homophonic alphabet with nulls cannot be recovered from 120 signs ciphertext-only. The words are copied as far as they can be in walsingham1585/NOTES.md.

05 What remains open

  • The fourteen sign words of 10 September (R4844), unread. They would yield to Wotton’s cipher key or to any other letter in this alphabet with its decipherment, most likely among the sent despatches in TNA SP 52/37–38.
  • Numbers 13, 14, 23, 30, 37, unidentified; 27, 28, 29, 36 possible only.
  • The transcriptions of 21 August and 10 September are working texts with gaps, not editions.
