At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · complete

The letters
Guy de Saint-Gelais, sieur de Lanssac, sent to Warsaw for the 1573 election of Henri d’Anjou as king of Poland, to Charles IX (26 April), to Anjou and to Catherine de Médicis (24 April), and to the King and Anjou again on 1 and 9 May.
Where
Bibliothèque nationale de France, Français 4735: f. 124 (no. 51), ff. 154–157 (nos. 60–61), ff. 160, 164, 174, on the Gallica microfilm btv1b9060724s. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A homophonic cipher of graphic signs, two or three per common letter, with a dozen signs for syllables and words (et, car, pour, Allemagne, l’Empereur, me/nous).
How it was deciphered
The key was recovered from the Court decipherer’s marginal glosses, cut by the gutter on the microfilm, and from a solver run with those values fixed, checked against shuffled and blind controls. It corrects S. Tomokiyo’s published table, whose first row is shifted by one letter. On 2 and 3 October 2026 the key was extended by about fifteen signs on ff. 154 and 156, and checked sign by sign against Tomokiyo’s current table, which supplied st, il, nous, par and a second t; ff. 160, 164 and 174 were then transcribed again at full scale.
What it says
On 26 April, the dangers French travellers met in Germany. On 24 April, the Lithuanian lords’ promise of their vote, the Sultan’s recommendation made “avec ruse”, borrowed money, and to Catherine that by Monday she would be mere des deux plus grans roys du monde. In May, Polish venality and the powers ranged against the election.
Measured
Signs giving sense, per letter: f. 124 96/100 (96%); to Anjou, ff. 154r–v, 660/677 (97.5%); to Catherine, ff. 156r–v, 921/961 (95.8%); f. 160 91/91; f. 164 203/207 (98.1%); f. 174 123/123. The four letters of 9 May on ff. 182–189 turned out to be in clear.
Still open
65 signs in all: line ends lost in the gutter of the microfilm or under the decipherer’s overwriting (Gallica has no other image of the volume), a few single signs, and one word sign on f. 164 that is in no key. Four signs of f. 124’s first passage carry a query.

01 The documents

BnF fr. 4735 is the second of the volumes of “pièces originales et de copies concernant l’histoire de France, 1569–1588” and, from no. 5 to no. 80, the Court’s file of the Polish election embassy: Monluc’s letters from Konin and Warsaw, the Polish memoranda on St Bartholomew, the King’s replies, and the letters of Lanssac and Gilles de Noailles from Warsaw and Płock in April and May 1573. The catalogue marks “avec chiffre et déchiffrement” against Lanssac’s letters of 24 April (nos. 60–61), 1 May (63, 65) and 9 May (70), and “avec chiffre” alone against no. 51 of 26 April. Gallica has the whole volume as a microfilm scan (btv1b9060724s, 614 unlabelled canvases); the folio of no. 51 is 124, canvas 242, not the 126 the catalogue entry here first gave.

What the catalogue calls the siblings’ decipherments is this same marginal gloss, line by line beside the cipher, on ff. 154, 154v, 156 and 157. The microfilm keeps only the last centimetre or two of each gloss line, but on f. 154v the first line of cipher stands beside “Car je n’ay pas cinquante escuz”, and the cipher line is exactly long enough for it.

02 The cipher

  • Homophonic letters. Separate graphic signs (crosses, bars, figures, letter-like forms) written continuously, with points, commas and short bars as word separators. Common letters have two or three signs: e ✕ ✱ Γ Δ, a ⊤ π H, r ϕ ⊨, s 9 Lo IIII, i ⊢ ϙ, n ſ o-o, u/v o+ ff o+o, m 8 ᥙ, l 3 ⊥, o ꞔ L, t ʓ ттт, p y N, d ‡, c 7 ⋔, g ∞, f o-, h +, qu ꞗ.
  • Syllable and word signs. et ♁, nt ꝸ, st 8 with a dot, de ᒐ, ou ᛏ, ns Λ, car Z, pour †, and Allemagne as a Z with a lower curl. Over the 741 signs transcribed from f. 124 and the first blocks of ff. 154 and 156, the merged frequencies are French: e-signs 17.6 %, then r, a, s, n, u, t, o.
  • Tomokiyo’s table. His “Lansac’s Cipher” (cryptiana, French Ciphers during the Reigns of Charles IX and Henry III) has the right signs but its first row is displaced by one letter from m onward (his ſ m, L n, б p, ⊨ q, 9 r, ʓ s, o+ t read n, o, qu, r, s, t, u), his Γ = i is e, his ‡ = h is d, and the “pour” cross is the letter h in one of its two shapes. The corrected table with the evidence for each sign is targets/lanssac/key.tsv.

03 The key, and how it was found

  1. The glossed crib. f. 154v line 1, Z ⊢✕ | ſπϙ | y⊤9 | 7⊢ſꞗπſʓ✕, beside “Car je n’ay pas cinquante escuz”, gives car ie nai pas cinquante and fixes eleven signs at once, including car = Z, qu = ꞗ and the shift in Tomokiyo’s row.
  2. Passage two by hand against its gloss “moins esté retenu … ou oultre”: † ⊥ ✕ · 8 ꞔ ⊢ o-o 9 · ✱ 8̣ Γ · ϕ ✕ ʓ ✱ ſ o+ = pour le moins esté retenu, then ᛏ Γ ᥙ y ✕ 9 7 + ✱ · ᒐ = ou empesché de, y π 9 Lo ✕ ϕ ꞔ ff 3 ттт ⊨ Γ = passer oultre, ᥙ · π ꟿ Lo = mais. Each new sign was checked in the siblings before it was kept.
  3. Simulated annealing over the whole transcription (French 5-gram model, symbol to letter or short code, the crib letters held fixed). The free signs come out the same across seeds and give words in the siblings that the cribs never touched and the glosses confirm: les dangiers, causé, l’autre, beaucoup, grant seigniur, du monde, changement, sur la teste, recommandé, doubte ou vous mettre.
  4. Decoding of f. 124: each sign was given its candidate values and the best-scoring sequence under the same model was kept. Passage one comes out as despuis les dangiers qu’encontrent en Allemagne les françois qui passent et vostre ser…, matching the three marginal fragments “…es dangier”, “…d’Allemagne”, “qui passent”.
  5. Controls. The key with the crib letters fixed scores −2193 to −2198 on the real sign order and −2924 to −3009 on three shufflings of the same signs. A blind run with nothing fixed reaches a higher score (−2033) but recovers only 6 of the 21 crib letters: on a transcription this noisy the language model alone does not find the key; the cribs and the glosses do, and the model then fills the rest consistently.

04 Decipherment

Lanssac to Charles IX, Warsaw, 26 April 1573. Sire, [despuis? les dangiers qu’encontrent en Allemagne les françois qui passent, et vostre ser{o r h e}] Les empeschementz et les plus grandes causes qui ayent esté depuis deux {ans} … toute la Turinge, Misnie et Marche de Brandebourg m’ont tellement retardé que je n’arrivay en Polongne à Mezeritz que le vingt-cinquiesme de febvrier. Et sans la faveur que j’ay receue d’aulcuns des serviteurs et pensionnaires de vostre Majesté, principalement des Sieurs Alexandre de Militiz et George Luelz(?), qui me y ont accompagné avec bonne et forte troupe de leurs amis et assisté de leurs moyens, j’eusse sans doubte [pour le moins esté retenu ou empesché de passer oultre. Mais], la grace à Dieu, je me suis rendu il y a desjà long temps avec Monsieur de Valence, lequel m’a enseigné l’estat auquel estoient les affaires qu’il a bien et dignement commencées et continuées jusques icy, et dont j’ay faict ung recueil ensemble de ce que j’ay peu juger, lequel j’envoye à vostre Majesté. Et ne sçachant à présent rien digne d’escrire oultre le contenu audict recueil, sinon que j’ay receu la lettre qu’il a pleu à vostre Majesté m’escrire du xxiii(?) de febvrier, en obéissant à laquelle je me fauldray(?) à me comporter de sorte que j’espère que le bien de vostre service et les affaires qui sont à promouvoir par deçà en demeureront advancez. Je priray le Créateur vous donner, Sire, en très parfaicte santé très contente et longue vie. De Warsauvia, le xxvie d’avril 1573.

English translation

Sire, [since the dangers that the French who pass through meet in Germany, and your ser{vice?}…] The hindrances, and the greatest causes there have been these two {years} … all through Thuringia, Meissen and the Mark of Brandenburg, delayed me so much that I did not reach Poland, at Mezeritz, until the twenty-fifth of February. And without the favour I received from some of your Majesty’s servants and pensioners, chiefly the Sieurs Alexander von Miltitz and Georg Luelz(?), who accompanied me there with a good strong troop of their friends and helped me with their means, I should without doubt [at the least have been detained or prevented from passing on. But], thank God, I joined Monsieur de Valence a good while ago, who has told me the state in which the affairs stood that he has so well and worthily begun and carried on until now, and of which I have made a summary, together with what I have been able to judge, which I send to your Majesty. And knowing nothing at present worth writing beyond what that summary contains, except that I have received the letter your Majesty was pleased to write me on the 23rd(?) of February, in obedience to which I shall not fail to conduct myself so that I hope your service and the affairs to be advanced here will profit by it, I pray the Creator to give you, Sire, in most perfect health a most contented and long life. From Warsaw, the 26th of April 1573.

The Miltitz were Saxon nobles in French pay; Mezeritz (Międzyrzecz) is the first Polish town on the road from Frankfurt an der Oder to Poznań. The “dangers” are the hostility to Frenchmen in Protestant Germany after St Bartholomew’s Day, which is why this passage is in cipher and the itinerary is not. Lanssac reached Monluc at Warsaw in time for the election Diet, which had opened on 5 April; Anjou was elected on 11 May. The recueil he announces is no. 52 in the volume, “Estat des affaires de Pollongne selon que le Sr de Lanssac y a peu aprendre”, in clear.

05 The election letters, deciphered with the same key

The catalogue credits Lanssac’s letters of 24 April, 1 May and 9 May with a “déchiffrement”. It is the Court decipherer’s note in the outer margin, and the microfilm keeps only the last word or two of each of its lines; the cipher passages themselves cannot be recovered from the scan. With the key they decipher in full, but for one word sign. The first pass (17 September) left runs open; on 3 October 2026 the three letters were transcribed again from the full-size scans, with the sign groups aligned to the words (ct_f160.txt, ct_f164.txt, ct_f174.txt, and read_f160_f174.md). It found that the first cipher line of f. 164 had been skipped, read the four open runs of f. 164, and showed the open run of f. 174 to be a misdivision. Measured: f. 160 91 of 91 signs, f. 164 203 of 207 (98.1%), f. 174 123 of 123. Braces mark what is not deciphered.

To Charles IX, Warsaw, 1 May 1573 (f. 160). … que pour en envoyer devant selon que les occasions des affaires premièrement surviennent. [Car je veoy que ceste nation est autant vénale et sujette à se laisser gaigner par argent comme sont les Alemans, leurs voisins.] Ce jourd’huy ilz mettent en délibération s’ilz doibvent prendre pour Roy ung Piaste ou ung prince estrangier, mais on sçait bien quelle en doibt estre la résolution.
To the duc d’Anjou, Warsaw, 1 May 1573 (f. 164). … c’est de latin et d’argent, et à bonne quantité, car il y aura de quoy l’employer [en toutes sortes pour venir à vostre desir]. … les traverses de nos contraires qui n’y espargnent rien pour nous empescher, [car si jusques cy {word sign} a despendu trente mil escus en ceste negotiation, l’Empereur en y a despendu plus de trois cens mil, et n’y faict toutefois riens qui vaille pour soy; mais seulement il l[e] trouble vostre desseing, faisant le pis qu’il peult contre vous.] Encores ne veulx-je faillir, Monseigneur, de vous ramentevoir … que vous fassiez donner ordre que les navires soient prestz pour l’embarquement.
To Charles IX, Płock, 9 May 1573 (f. 174). … ayant esté employé en un affaire de telle importance et qui est reüssy tant heureusement [contre la volunté et menées du Grant Seigneur, de l’Empereur, des princes de l’Empire, du Roy d’Espaigne, du Moscovite et du Roy de Suède, qui tous estoient bandez contre vostre Majesté]. Si est-ce que j’auray encores plus d’occasion de louer Dieu si je suis tant heureux que vostre Majesté me juge digne d’estre employé en ce second …

English

1 May, to the King. For I see that this nation is as venal and as ready to let itself be won by money as are the Germans, their neighbours. — 1 May, to Anjou. … in every way, to come to what you desire. … For if until now {word sign} has spent thirty thousand écus in this negotiation, the Emperor has spent more than three hundred thousand in it, and yet does nothing in it worth anything for himself; he only troubles your design, doing the worst he can against you. — 9 May, to the King. … an affair of such weight, which has succeeded so happily against the will and the intrigues of the Grand Seigneur, of the Emperor, of the princes of the Empire, of the King of Spain, of the Muscovite and of the King of Sweden, who were all leagued against your Majesty.

The 9 May list is the field of the 1573 election as the French embassy saw it: Archduke Ernest for the Emperor and Spain, Ivan IV, John III of Sweden, and a Habsburg-leaning party among the German princes. The “Grand Seigneur” (the Sultan) is the surprise, since the Porte was generally reckoned to favour the French candidate; every sign of the group now deciphers, with one irregularity forced by the gloss (Γ, elsewhere e, stands for the a of Grant). On f. 164 the Habsburg outlay of 300,000 is set against 30,000 écus spent by a party written with a single word sign that is in no key and whose gloss the gutter has taken: the King’s side, or a Polish faction. New values from these leaves (4 = b, three strokes = z as well as t, the tailed 4 = p, Λ = ns, the hooked C = qu, the 6-like sign = o, Δ = g, the o with cross below = et) are listed in the notes; the last makes f. 124’s “retenu ou empesché” rather “retenu et empesché”.

06 The letters of 24 April to Anjou and to Catherine (ff. 154, 156)

Lanssac’s two long letters of 24 April 1573 (nos. 60 and 61) carry the most cipher in the file: 37 lines on f. 154 to the duc d’Anjou and two blocks on f. 156, with most of the verso, to Catherine de Médicis. Both have the decipherer’s running text in the margin. On the rectos it is in the inner margin and the microfilm keeps only its line ends; on the versos it is in the outer margin and survives nearly whole. On 2 October 2026 all four cipher pages were transcribed again from the full-size scans, with the sign groups aligned to the words so that each group could be scored as deciphered or not, and the key was applied and extended. On 3 October every open group was cut out at twice the scale and read again against Tomokiyo’s current table; both letters are now over 95%.

To Anjou (f. 154)

[clear: … Depuis la conclusion desquelz] {…} j’ay aprins deux choses, l’une qui me donne gaing de cause, l’autre qui me trouble beaucoup. La premiere est que j’ay esté au devant des Lituans, les chefz desquelz m’ont promis ne donner leur voeu à aultre que à vous, [clear: qui sera de telle importance que] avec la bonne part que nous avons au royaume sans doubte ou vous mettre ceste grant couronne sur la teste, si la recommandation du Grant Seigneur, laquelle il a faicte de vous comme j’estime avec ruse par le vaivode qui(?) de la Valaquie, qui est(?) {5 signs} ameine quelque inespéré changement, qui est le second poinct qui me donne une traverse, de laquelle avec l’aide {4 signs} de Dieu nous esperons neaumoins sortir.
[f. 154v] Car je n’ay pas cinquante e[sc]us, combien que j’en ay emprunté plus de quinze cens. [clear: Aussi estois je party] bien belistre pour venir si loing et demeurer si longtemps et faire contrecarre à celuy qui [par] beaucoup de grans dons ne sçauront vaincre le[s] nostre, si inferieur {2} … Et espere que dans peu de jours tous ceulx-là avec beaucoup d’autres seront vos subjectz.

The Lithuanian lords have promised Anjou their vote, which with his party in the kingdom makes the crown certain, unless the Sultan’s recommendation of him, made (Lanssac thinks) with guile through the voivode of Wallachia, brings some unexpected change. Lanssac has not fifty écus left and has borrowed more than fifteen hundred; within days, he expects, the electors will all be Anjou’s subjects. Every surviving fragment of the recto gloss agrees with the decipherment (“…causé l’autre”, “…doubte ou vous”, “…sur la teste”, “…grand seigneur”, “…par le Vaynode de”), and the verso gloss gives the first block word for word.

To Catherine de Médicis (f. 156)

[clear: Vous advisant, Madame, que] {5 signs} les choses sont de present en telz termes que j’espere dedans lundi prochain vous serez mere des deux plus grans roys du monde, s’il n’advient quelque changement de voluntez pour quelque grande {1} inopinée occasion … il me semble que ce ne sera pas mal faict {2} vostre Majesté envoyast quelqu’ung {1} vers le roy de Dennemarc, non pour luy demander passage … mais bien pour luy faire entendre que les Imperiaulx, nos ennemis en ce faict …
[f. 156v] Mais de crainte que l’Empereur {[cepen]dant} il n’y soit prevenu, et que l[e] {…} roy de Dennemarc [clear: ayant obtemperé à sa volunté, ne] nous donnast beaucoup d’affaires de ce costé-là, à cause d’estroit qui n’a pas demie lieue de {la(rge)}, {1} des fortz situez des deux costez de par terre. … de mectre à la mercy de ceulx {1} qui … Mais se monstrent estre ennemis jurez de {1} France, et specialement de Monseigneur, et lesquelz [so]nt extremement marrys s’il fault qu’il se voient enclos de deux si puissantz roys comme ilz seront. … {6} ces seigneurs demanderont qu’il ameine quatre mil gascons … recueilliez le plus d’argent querez(?) ensem{ble af}in que … ambassadeurs porter la couronne à {Monseigneur, et y ar}riveront vers luy, vous ne soiez lors empeschée d’en trouver. Ca[r] … aussi à commencer à faire estat que {[le]urs} navires seront propres à l’embarquement, et de faire mettre en ordre les galleres pour pouvoir servir. … {de ceste nation … servi}teurs, il n’y en a que quatre con[tr]e nous, et que toute la noblesse crie pour avoir ung roy, et nommement veule[nt Monseigneur].

By Monday next, Lanssac tells the Queen Mother, she will be “mother of the two greatest kings in the world” unless some sudden change of minds intervenes. He advises sending someone to the King of Denmark, not to ask passage but to tell him what the Imperialists are doing, because he fears the Emperor will forestall them and that Denmark, bending to the Emperor’s will, could make trouble at the Sound, less than half a league wide and fortified on both sides. Going by land would put Anjou at the mercy of the German princes, who “show themselves sworn enemies of France, and especially of Monseigneur”, and are “extremely sorry” to find themselves shut in between two such powerful kings; the road by Italy and Dalmatia is too long and costly. The Polish lords will ask him to bring four thousand Gascons; Catherine should gather all the money she can, so that she is not then hard put to find it when the embassy comes to bring the crown, and should reckon on the ships and have the galleys made ready. Of the Polish lords only four are against them, and the whole nobility cries out for a king, and namely for Anjou. Here the cipher corrects the decipherer, who wrote “si monsieur estoit ennemy d’icy de la France”: with the stacked 8 as st and the clover sign as -nt, the signs read se monstrent estre ennemis jurez, and the enemies are the German princes, not Monsieur. Anjou was elected on 11 May. The end of the letter, f. 157r, is in clear, with a postscript on the Diet (forty thousand horse, the great tent, the Emperor’s ambassadors).

The new signs

About fifteen signs were added to the key on these pages: a pronoun sign for me and nous (“qui me donne”, “que nous avons”, “ne nous donnast”), a barred N for il and a looped barred N for est, word signs for bien and par, the clover sign for -ment/-nt, a footed Π for t, further signs for qu and x, Tomokiyo’s u sign (confirmed), and 4 = b confirmed on eleven words. On 3 October the key was compared sign by sign with Tomokiyo’s current table: his st (stacked 8), il, nous, par, q and t agree and were adopted, and an f sign proposed on 2 October was withdrawn (it is the st). The barred-N forms are now told apart on every page: the plain barred N is il throughout, the looped form on f. 124 stays p in despuis, and the “N = a” of f. 160 was a misread tall T. The hand writes mectre, ameine, neaumoins, nommé. The full list is in key.tsv.

The letters of 9 May are in clear

The catalogue and the earlier notes here expected Lanssac’s four other letters of 9 May from Płock (ff. 182–189: to Anjou, now king-elect, to Catherine, to the secretary of state Pierre Brulart, and to his father Louis de Saint-Gelais) to be in the same key with a marginal decipherment. The images show all four written wholly in clear, with addresses on ff. 183v, 185v, 187v and 189v: the election, Balagny carrying the news, and requests for favour and for the costs Lanssac had borne. The cipher letter of that day is the one to Charles IX, f. 174, in section 05.

07 What remains uncertain

  • Despuis rests on two signs read once each (ꝛ as de, the looped barred N as p, rechecked on the image on 3 October: read il, as the plain barred N is elsewhere, the word gives no French); the gloss fragment “…es dangier” is compatible with “…uis les dangiers” but the gutter takes the start of the line.
  • François: the signs give fran-c-o-r-s; the seventh sign must be the i sign ϙ, which this hand writes close to the r sign ϕ, or the word is another. Qui rests on the gloss; the cipher has ꟿ Γ, and ꟿ reads i in mais.
  • Et vostre ser is followed by four signs that read o r h e under the key; service with one misread sign is the likely word and is left in braces.
  • Two forms of the et sign (et and nt) and of the cross (h and pour) are told apart by context, not by shape, in the present transcription.
  • The election letters of section 05: f. 164’s word sign before a despendu trente mil escus is in no key, Tomokiyo’s included, and the gloss beside it is cut (no key material); a stray c between pour and venir and the two signs of l[e] in il l[e] trouble, which give li, are single signs no value fits.
