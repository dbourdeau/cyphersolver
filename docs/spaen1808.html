key not found: a plain code reaching at least 1339

Düsseldorf → The Hague · numerical code · 14–15 January 1808

Van Spaen to Van der Goes, 14 and 15 January 1808

Nationaal Archief, The Hague, 2.01.08 Ministerie van Buitenlandse Zaken 1796–1810, inv. nr. 281, DECODE R1941: a letter of two pages and a one-page annex, both in figures between clear Dutch openings and closings.

Both items were transcribed from the DECODE images and the archive inventory was checked for who sent them. Every key reachable online was tested and none fits, so nothing was decoded. DECODE notes that the annex was deciphered, but no decipherment is on the record. The code has to come from the archive.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 26 October 2026

At a glanceNot solved · text not obtained

The letter
G. C. van Spaen tot Voorstonden, “ambassador at the Court of Westphalia”, to Maarten van der Goes: a letter of 14 January 1808 (228 groups) and an annex of 15 January (75 groups), photographed twice. The inventory suggests the writers were the commissioners for the ceded districts around Zevenaar.
Where
Nationaal Archief, inv. 281 of 2.01.08; DECODE R1941, catalogued as partly decrypted (annex said to be deciphered, letter not). No decipherment is written on or attached to the record.
The cipher
A plain run of numbers from 15 to 1339 with no series marks, so not one of Croiset’s marked books. 222 distinct groups out of 303.
How it was tried
Every plausible DECODE key record was checked, Croiset’s 1803 codebook (R1035) gives word salad, and Colenbrander’s Gedenkstukken V does not print the letters. Ciphertext-only was not attempted beyond counting.
Why it resists
With most groups occurring once, the letter cannot be decoded without the codebook.
Still open
No key has been found. The record owner’s decipherment of the annex would give about 70 values; the other route is the 1806–09 code of the ceded-districts commission.

01 The documents

The letter is docketed “Exh 14 january 1808” and opens in clear, “Hoog Edel Gestrenge Heer,”. Nineteen rows of figures follow. It closes in clear, “Ik heb de eer met de volmaakste hoogachting te verblijven, … Uwer Excellentie’s ootmoedige gehoorzame Dienaar”, with a signature read as “G. C. van Spaen”. The annex is headed “Dusseldorf den 15 January 1808” and filed as “136. Exh 18 january 1808”. It has seven rows of figures and the same closing. DECODE’s images P1 and P2 are two photographs of this one annex page.

The archive inventory changes the attribution. In NA 2.01.08, inv. 281 is “Missiven van J.F.G. van Spaen en G. van Riemsdijk, commissarissen voor de overname der gecedeerde districten, de Lijmers, Huyssen, Malburgen en Zevenaar, jul 1806 – mei 1809”. G. C. van Spaen’s own letters as envoy in Berlin are inv. 244–248. The Düsseldorf letters therefore come from the Dutch commission for the territorial exchange with the Grand Duchy of Berg. Their subject is very probably that handover. A J.F.G. reading of the signature is not ruled out from the photocopy.

02 The cipher

Each group is a number of one to four digits, separated by dots. A few groups are followed by a dash, which may mark a word boundary, and a few carry a small superscript letter (729t, 793s), probably a clear ending as in other Dutch codes of the period.

Values run from 15 to 1339. The most frequent groups are 493 (six times), 297 (five), and 441, 424, 269 and 709 (four each). The sequence 602 441 373 occurs twice in the letter and once in the annex, and is probably a recurring name or title. Twenty-eight groups are shared between the two items. The transcription is targets/spaen1808/transcription.txt. In this hand 3 and 5 are hard to tell apart.

03 What was tried

  • DECODE keys. Every key record at a Dutch holder dated 1780–1815 or undated was checked, then every key record mentioning Holland, Berg, Westphalia or 1806–10. None is for this code. Croiset’s 1803 codebook (R1035), applied to the annex, gives disconnected Dutch words. Both it and the 1798 book (R1891) use marked three-digit groups below 1000, which this code does not.
  • The “solved” annex. DECODE’s note says the annex was solved, but the record carries no transcription or document file, and the neighbouring records R1936–R1948 are other letters.
  • Print. The full text of Colenbrander, Gedenkstukken der algemeene geschiedenis van Nederland V (1806–1810), was searched for Spaen, Voorstonden, Dusseldorp and Westphalen. It prints nothing from these letters.
  • Ciphertext only. Not attempted beyond counting. With 303 groups from a code of 1,300 or more values, most groups occur once, and no single solution is determined.

04 What would finish it

  • The DECODE record owner’s decipherment of the 15 January annex. It would give up to about 70 values. If the code is alphabetical (one-part), those values would also bracket much of the letter.
  • The code of the ceded-districts commission, 1806–09, among the Buitenlandse Zaken papers (NA 2.01.08) or the Van der Goes papers.
