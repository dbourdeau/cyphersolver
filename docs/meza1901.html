12 October 1901 · Mexico

Meza Salinas to Bernardo Reyes

Go to the full text ↓

Meza Salinas warns Bernardo Reyes that the Flores Magón brothers plan to distribute a pamphlet.

01 What the letter says

The letter is dated 12 October 1901 and is addressed to General Don Bernardo Reyes. The writer, Meza Salinas, warns him that the Flores Magón brothers will publish a leaflet on the 23rd of the month in which Reyes and the president will be insulted, supposedly (dizque) to expose the immorality of the administration before the Pan-American Congress. Forty thousand copies have already been ordered printed. He adds that when he can speak with Reyes, or with someone trustworthy, he will report everything that has been done and is being done in the states, and signs as a friend who holds him in great esteem.

Only part of the letter is enciphered. The date, the address and the greeting are plain, and so are a number of ordinary words in the body (Los, el 23, en que serán, Vd. y el, con objeto dizque de poner de manifiesto ante el Congreso Pan Americano, Se repartirán 40.000 que ya están mandadas). The enciphered words are the sensitive ones: the name of the Flores Magón brothers, the publishing, the insults, the president, the immorality of the administration, the printing and the reporting. The Spanish, the literal output and an English translation are under Full texts below.

Context not checked here: who Meza Salinas, Reyes and the Flores Magón brothers were, and which president is meant (the letter does not name him; the translation note says so). The sources of this project do not carry that history, and none is added.

02 The cipher and key

The cipher is a homophonic substitution: each letter of a word is written as a two-digit number, and a frequent letter has several numbers to choose from. Each enciphered word is a run of numbers joined by hyphens, one run per word (a word broken at a line end carries an = in the transcription). The letter has 142 groups in 23 runs, drawn from 46 distinct numbers. The project profile records no nomenclator (no numbers for whole words or names), so every word is spelled out letter by letter, without accents: the numbers do not encode accents or capitals.

The filed key has 46 rows, for 20 letters. The table gives them in alphabetical order of the plain letter.

Plain letterabcdefghijlmnoprstuz
Cipher numbers39 49 5936 5638 48 5834 44 5431 41 5157553230 40 509928 88 9824 84 9421 81 9125 85 9522 9219 69 7918 68 7863 7314 6460

One regularity is visible in the table: for a, c, d, e and i the three numbers end in the same digit and begin with 3, 4 and 5 (e is 31, 41, 51; a is 39, 49, 59; i is 30, 40, 50). The other letters do not follow it, and nothing further about the structure of the key is claimed here.

Worked example, line L01. After the plain Los, the first run is 57-98-95-69-68-51. The key gives 57 = f, 98 = l, 95 = o, 69 = r, 68 = s, 51 = e, so the run spells florse, which is how the filed output keeps it. The period typescript has Flores: the last two letters are in the other order. The three slips of this kind are listed under “What is deciphered and what remains open” below.

03 Where the key came from and how it was checked

Anna Beck and Larry Beck, working with ChatGPT, reconstructed the 46 values from the Spanish context around the cipher, that is, from the plain words of this same letter, which suggest what each enciphered word must be. Their account says they did not use the separate period decipherment to build the key. Brice Dudout pointed to that typescript, filed at CEHM as DLI.34.6703.2, in the archive snapshot they consulted. “Their browsing chronology is unavailable” means that the original sequence of what the contributors looked at, and when, is lost, so it cannot now be shown whether they saw the typescript before they finished the key.

What was checked: this project rechecked all 23 runs against the key, and every run reproduces the filed output with no mismatch. The typescript, a typed decipherment of the same letter, is transcribed in the repository and was compared with the filed reading. The earlier review of the submission checked the manuscript and cited the typescript for the three slips. What was not checked: the transcription of the numbers has not been recompared with the manuscript images by this project; it rests on the contributors’ descriptions of the images and the earlier review. The flourish before the signature is left open in the transcription; the typescript signs T. Meza y Salinas.

This is a fit, not an independent test. The key was inferred from this same letter, so reproducing the letter with it shows that the key is consistent with the letter, not that it was found blind. The profile records the validation as “not applicable” for that reason. The period typescript is adjacent plaintext: it exists in the archive and was available to check the words, and it is not an unseen control.

04 What is deciphered and what remains open

Two measures are kept apart. Of the groups, 142 of 142 carry a value in the key. Of the words, 24 of 24 enciphered words are sure: 21 come straight from the key, and 3 are repaired with the period typescript. The 23 runs give 24 words because run C14, hablarcon, is two words, hablar con, written without a break. No group is unvalued and no word is left open.

What is not clean, and is listed so that it is not lost:

  • florse (run C01) for Flores: the last two letters are in the other order;
  • insultads (C09) for insultados: an o is missing before the final s;
  • presidnntn (C10) for presidente: the seventh and last letters, which should be e, are both written 21, a number the key gives to n; why is not known;
  • hablarcon (C14) is two words written together;
  • the period typescript differs from the filed letter in three places: it omits de este mes after el 23, it inserts Sr. before Presidente, and it has alguno where the key gives alguien;
  • the flourish before the signature is unresolved in the transcription; the key does not touch it;
  • not checked: the history of the people and events named (section 01) and the transcription against the images (section 03).

Translation

translation.md

12 October 1901

General Don Bernardo Reyes

Dear General and friend,

The Flores Magón brothers will publish a leaflet on the 23rd of this month in which you and the president will be insulted, supposedly with the aim of exposing the administration's immorality before the Pan-American Congress. Forty thousand copies will be distributed; they have already been ordered printed.

When I can speak with you, or with someone trustworthy, I will tell you everything that has been done and is being done in the states.

Your friend, who holds you in great esteem,

Meza Salinas

`Brothers` makes the plural `Los ... Magón` explicit; `hermanos` is not itself written. `Leaflet` renders `hoja suelta`, a printed sheet or broadside. `Supposedly` preserves the writer's distancing `dizque`. `The states` must not be expanded to `the United States`. The text does not name the president; identifying the officeholder is historical context rather than a decrypted name. No individual brother is named in the letter.
