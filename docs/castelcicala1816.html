London, Paris → Naples · syllabic numeric code · April 1816 – October 1823

Castelcicala to the Marchese di Circello and to Medici, 1816–1823

Go to the full text ↓

Archivio di Stato di Napoli, Ministero degli affari esteri, busta 2337; DECODE R9553–R9589 (catalogue 166 and 167): twenty-four despatches in the ambassador’s code, about 9,400 groups, all transcribed here.

Four letters in the same box carry contemporary decipherments between the lines. Aligned with their cipher, they give a key of about a hundred certain values, extended to 162, which gives a value to 45% of the groups. The code’s structure is known. Two letters of 1823 (R9571, R9589) read from their glosses; the other twenty-three decode only into fragments, with unread groups between nearly every pair of words, so they are decoded, not read.

The documents
Naples ambassador Fabrizio Ruffo, principe di Castelcicala, to the Marchese di Circello (foreign minister) and, in 1823, to his successor Medici; 1816–1823. The catalogue held two entries (166, 167) for what is one series; “Ciscello” misreads the recipient.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Napoli, Ministero degli affari esteri, busta 2337; DECODE R9553–R9589 (login required).
The cipher
A two-part numeric code of about 2,450 groups for syllables, words and names; homophonic, with short alphabetical runs. Groups written with a fifth figure are nulls.
How it was read
Three 1823 Paris letters (R9569, R9571, R9589) in the same code were deciphered between the lines, as were ten lines of despatch 3874 (R9566). Aligning these glosses group by group gave the key; an earlier pass on 21 September had found no key.
What it says
Despatch 3874 (Paris, 1 December 1816) reports a conversation between the French interior minister and the Spanish minister Onis about the cession of the Floridas.
Still open
The key values 45% of the groups in the target letters; about a third of its values are inferences, and no letter is yet decrypted in full.

01 The documents

All the documents are in busta 2337 of the Esteri series, photographed for DECODE (records R9553–R9589, access by login). The headings give the dates and places:

  • London, 1816: 1 April (R9553, no. 2314, headed “Cifra della Segreteria di Stato”), 30 August, 6 September, 26 September, 4 October (R9556, R9557, R9579, R9558).
  • Paris, 1816: 3 June (R9555), 17, 19 and 23 October (R9559, R9560, R9587), 30 November (R9561, seven pages), 1 December (R9566, despatch 3874, with a second fragment in another hand), 3 December (R9562).
  • Undated, to Circello: R9554 (no. 2589), R9563 (2814), R9564 (3388 or 3588), R9565 (3721), R9567 (4086). The fragments R9582–R9585 date from 1817–1819.
  • Paris, 1823, to the Cavaliere de’ Medici: 4, 8 and 11(?) September, 9 October (R9569, R9571, R9570, R9589). The papal conclave that followed Pius VII’s death is the theme.
  • Another code: R9586 and R9588 (values 100–1100, faint pencil glosses), two undated letters signed by Castelcicala, are a different cipher, with their own page. R9590 (Paris, 12 March 1830) is also in another code.

The images are not in reading order. R9579 runs P2, P3, P1, P4; R9569 P2, P1; R9561 begins at P2.

02 The code

The groups run from 1 to about 2,450, with dots between them. The same frequent groups appear in 1816 and 1823 (2201, 2211, 332, 1909, 2450, 2232, 1864, 1843), so one book served for seven years. Its units are syllables, common words and names, and a word is spelled from as many as it needs:

It is homophonic: di is 1112 and 2211, pa is 1201 and 2363, ere is 1836 and 2381. It is a two-part code with local alphabetical runs: 2201 che, 2205 ci, 2211 di, 2221 fi, 2230 l, 2231 l’, 2232 la; 2082 del, 2083 dell’, 2084 della; 2045 quella, 2049 questo; 2161 is, 2162 Isola; 1919 no, 1920 non; 2445 ter, 2448 ti, 2450 to. Between runs the order is scattered. A group written with a fifth figure (21900, 18900, 16480; about a hundred cases) always falls where the decipherer wrote nothing, so these are nulls.

03 What the glossed letters say

1 December 1816 (R9566, first ten lines). Decazes, the Minister of the Interior, “who still enjoys the greatest favour with the King of France”, has met the Spanish minister Onís on his way through Paris. Speaking of the cession of the Floridas, and of the cession of Cuba to England that had been talked of, he “skilfully insinuated”… The gloss stops there.

8 September 1823 (R9571). “La Francia desidera il Cardinale [?Vo...] Castiglione per Papa, ma si biasima di avere il Duca di Lavalle troppo fatto ciò vedere, e si teme che ciò nuoccia al suddetto porporato per la sua elevazione al Pontefica[te]. Non esiste alcuno accordo tra la Francia. L'Austria per la elezione del Pontefice.” (France wants Cardinal Castiglioni as Pope. The Duc de Laval is blamed for making this too plain, which may harm the cardinal’s chances. France and Austria have no agreement on the election.) Castiglioni was elected as Pius VIII only in 1829; Leo XII was chosen on 28 September 1823.

9 October 1823 (R9589). Now that the King of Spain has his liberty and the orders given in Medici’s despatch no. 688 of 28 September, there can be no question of Castelcicala passing the note to the Conference. Postscript: the Duc d’Angoulême will not be back for two months.

4 September 1823 (R9569) has only a fragmentary gloss, and the letter is decoded, not read. It concerns the election to the papacy and whether France would use its veto; one sentence of the gloss now holds: “... ed io ho motivo di credere di no, e che [...] la suddetta elezione la Francia gli darebbe l'esclusiva”.

04 Method and what remains open

The 27 records were fetched from DECODE and transcribed from full-resolution crops by two transcription agents: 9,428 groups in the 25 letters in the main code, 1,315 distinct. The glosses were aligned group by group, and the key was extended by hand from repeated contexts. Its 162 values are graded in key.tsv: 90 taken directly from a contemporary gloss (G), 18 glossed and confirmed in a second context (X), and 54 inferred here and unconfirmed (I). With them, 45% of the groups in the target letters get a value (4,340 of 9,286 counted without the five-figure nulls, 46.7%). Scoring candidate syllables for each unknown group with an Italian 5-gram model did not separate the candidates. The known neighbours are too sparse, and the segmentation into syllables or words is unknown.

So no letter is decrypted in full. The partial decode (reading_partial.txt) is a working file, not an edition of the text. Some I values are surely wrong: “il Sr troppo l d’An ta” in R9579 must be a name. The next step is to decrypt the 1816 letters one at a time, around the Floridas–Cuba–Decazes themes the gloss supplies, using the alphabetical runs to bracket unknown groups. The code book itself, if it survives in the Esteri or the Segreteria di Stato papers of the ASNa, would settle it at once. The consular keys of Borbone 1666 on DECODE (R9532–R9541) are from 1859–60 and do not apply.
