A letter from Rome on the French ambassador’s advice, copied in cipher, 10 August 1547

Go to the full text ↓

A cipher copy of a letter from Rome of 10 August 1547 has its decipherment of the time beside it; a contributor rebuilt its key from it, and the key is partial on a held-out test.

01 The gathering

In the Archivio di Stato di Firenze, Mediceo del Principato 384 (the universal correspondence of Cosimo I), a gathering of August 1547 holds, at c. 76r, “Copia d’un Cap.lo d’una l(ette)ra di Roma de x d’Agosto 1547”: 18 lines of continuous figures, about 1,671 of them. At c. 79r, under the same heading, are about 16 lines of clear Italian, the decipherment of the time of c. 76r: a writer at Rome reporting what the French ambassador wrote to his king (taken to be Henri II). So the content of c. 76r is known; what is new is the key. At c. 78r, “Copia / D’una littera del card.le Farnese”, is the bare cipher of the lead that started this work (catalogue 2339), which is not read. The lead was found and vetted by Daniel Bourdeau; Brice Dudout (L’Europe chiffrée) ran Claude Opus 5.5 agents that transcribed, aligned and rebuilt the key under his protocol; no human palaeographer has checked the transcription. No images are reproduced here.

02 The key and its test

A homophonic substitution with a prefix code: 6, 8 and 9 stand alone (e, o, a) and every other code has two digits (for example 01 = r, 05 = n, 07 = d, 23 = t, 27 = s, 70 = i, 74 = t; 10 = che, 24 = et); a dot over either digit does not change the value. Some codes are probably nomenclature (Ambasciatore, Francia, Inghilterra; not shown to be) and are not yet in the key. The key (key_76r.tsv) was learnt on lines 1–13 only, aligned with c. 79r, and frozen. The protocol was hashed before either page was transcribed; one measurement on the held-out lines 14–18 gives a character agreement of 0.695 against at best 0.218 for 200 shuffled keys. With thresholds fixed in advance (0.85 validated, 0.60–0.85 with a margin partial), the key is partial; the shortfall comes from codes not seen at least twice in lines 1–13. The project did not repeat the test and did not see the images.

03 The reading

Compared word by word with c. 79r (so not a blind reading), c. 76r has 243 words: 131 certain, 61 probable (at most a third of the letters not given by the key) and 51 restored from c. 79r, with none unresolved. The 192 read by the key (79.0%) are under the 95% bar, so the page calls the reading partial. With c. 79r the whole text is known, but c. 79r retouches the wording in places (listed in reading_76r.md); the cipher is taken as the source. One transcriber (an AI agent), one pass, no independent re-reading.

The same gathering also holds c. 78r, the Farnese letter. Known keys were tried on it by criteria fixed in advance, all negative: the Klee key of vatican5 (an M5 score of 0.098, measured before the c. 76r work) and A. Meister, Die Geheimschrift im Dienste der päpstlichen Kurie (1906), pp. 176–177, tables 1 and 2 (0.174 and 0.183). The key of c. 76r was not applied to it, because under the protocol that needs a validated key.

04 What remains uncertain

  • 51 of the 243 words of c. 76r rest on c. 79r: probable nomenclature codes for Ambasciatore, Francia and Inghilterra, and rare homophones not seen at least twice in lines 1–13. They can be filled from the restored words; the key was deliberately not extended after the measurement.
  • c. 78r, the Farnese letter of catalogue 2339, is not read: the known keys are negative, the c. 76r key is only partial, and no decipherment is in the gathering.
  • No edition or study of the gathering was found (Comelli’s 2022 edition of the Della Casa–Farnese correspondence, vol. II, was checked in full text during the vetting of the lead; DECODE, Cryptiana and Lasry gave nothing).

c. 76r (cipher) with c. 79r (decipherment of the time), Rome 10 Aug 1547 read in part

The writer reports that the French ambassador at Rome advised his young King (taken to be Henri II) to keep what his father left him rather than seek glory in a war; but the King's ardour, encouraged by great men of France, cannot be held back, and the ambassador is urged to work for union with England.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (79% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

# c. 76r / c. 79r in English (approximate translation of the c. 79r text, Brice Dudout with Claude, 9 Nov 2026)

Copy of a chapter of a letter from Rome of 10 August 1547.

The ambassador of France, by way of a short discourse, wrote some days ago to his King that the greatest glory he could win
at this beginning of his reign was simply to look to his own guard, to keep what his father had left him and to govern
himself well; and that the desire to gain by breaking into war went against him, since he saw in it many great dangers;
and that he should consider that he was young and had time to take up better occasions than these, which God would show
him at one time or another; and so he counselled and dissuaded him otherwise. I understand that the said ambassador was
given to judge that, with the ardour his King had to throw himself into some enterprise, which some great men of France
were hoping for, it was not possible to hold him back from this desire, and that it was for him to do the work he thought
fitting to his rank and to his knowledge of the humours of Italy, and of the end towards which [the King] was moving, that
urged him to it in these times; and that those over there who reminded the said King of this and tried to draw him back were
held to be of little spirit. He further persuaded the ambassador to make every effort to stay united with England and to
win it over entirely, since he saw no easier or more open way to have [the Emperor] troubled in his own house than to be
disunited from it.

Context (not checked against an edition): Henri II had succeeded Francis I on 31 March 1547; the ambassador is not named in
the text. The last sentence is the hardest of the text and its sense is uncertain.
