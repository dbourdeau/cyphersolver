Gondi to the Commendator Galilei, 11 May 1649

Go to the full text ↓

A Tuscan secretary of state’s letter with fourteen cipher lines and a decipherment of the time between them; the key was taken from that decipherment, tested on lines held back, and reads 99 of the 101 cipher words.

The letter
Giovanni Battista Gondi, secretary of state of Grand Duke Ferdinando II, to a “Commendator Galilei”, 11 May 1649. Four pages of Italian; the cipher is the lower half of c. 1r.
Where
Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Modena, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera G, Gondi, Giovanni Battista, cc. 1-2. Not in the project’s catalogue and not on DECODE.
The cipher
Vowels are single digits, consonants a digit followed by 0 or a single sign, several signs stand for two letters, barred signs are short words, and three-figure groups beginning with 2 are names and titles. Double letters are written once.
How it was deciphered
A decipherment of the time is written over every cipher line. The key was built from lines L00-L06 only, frozen, and tested on L07-L13.
What it says
The Grand Duke has not yet decided to recall Galilei and waits for Vincenzio de’ Medici’s answers on the Viceroy’s paper; in Milan the Viceroy’s notions were very badly received (malis.).
Still open
The pair 5 8 before aver; code 273, read as Vicerè from context only; two doubtful signs and two signs without their bar.
Credit
Found, transcribed and read by WAINUT with AI language-model agents; checked by a second, independent AI agent. No human palaeographer has checked the text.

01 The letter

The letter is in the Autografoteca Campori of the Biblioteca Estense in Modena, under Gondi, Giovanni Battista, cc. 1-2 (four images). The library’s record describes it as partly ciphered and deciphered between the lines, and gives the recipient as “Commendator Galilei”. The first half of c. 1r and cc. 1v-2v are clear Italian; the lower half of c. 1r carries fourteen lines of cipher, about 560 signs (558 in the comparison of the two copies; 538 tokens in the measured transcription file), with clear words of the letter mixed in.

The sender is Giovanni Battista Gondi, secretary of state of Grand Duke Ferdinando II. The Commendator Galilei, a knight-commander of the Galilei family (apparently serving Tuscany abroad, from the letter), is not identified, and the letter does not name the Viceroy (S.E.). Web searches on 3 and 4 Nov 2026 found nothing on the recipient.

02 The cipher and its key

  • Vowels are single digits: 3 a, 4 u/v, 5 o, 6 e, 7 i.
  • Consonants are a digit followed by 0: 10 c (and b), 30 l or r, 40 n or s, 50 m, 60 t; or a single sign: an omega-shaped sign (written W here) for d or z, 9 p (and q), 1 g (and f), 2 g. Several of these stand for two letters, so the reader chooses by sense.
  • Barred signs are words: 7 non, 5 che, 4 e, W per, 6 chi (inside richiamata); W0 is con.
  • Three-figure groups beginning with 2 are words, names and titles: 217 di/de/del, 261 Signor, 262 V.S., 264 S.E., 267 S.A., 223 Vicerè, 252 quelli; 273 is read as Vicerè from context only.
  • Double letters are written once (acenni, posa, scritura), and there is no word division.

Every value, with its evidence, is in key.tsv in the target folder.

03 How it was checked

The signs were copied before any decoding, twice: once by eye on full-size crops, and once by a separate AI agent that saw only cipher-only bands, with no key, no clear text and no decipherment. The two copies differed on 39 of 558 signs; each of the 25 disputed places was settled on full-size crops, with no sign moved toward the interlinear text.

The key was then built from the pairs of cipher and interlinear decipherment on lines L00-L06 only and frozen. On the held-out lines L07-L13, the frozen key agrees with the reading on 139 of 148 sign units (93.9%); the other nine are values the training lines did not contain, and no frozen value was contradicted. This test is graded against the project’s own reading, made with the interlinear text in view, and many values are two-way, so it shows the key is consistent across the letter rather than proving it independently.

An independent AI agent copied 280 symbols of seven lines blind and differed on 2 (0.7%); one place stayed unresolved. On 24 Nov 2026 the remaining seven lines (L00-L03, L05, L06, L10) were compared again with the image, not blind; no symbol value changed. A script rebuilds the decryption from the transcription and the key and fails on any unmarked departure from the signs. A statistical test was not run: the cipher is polyphonic, and the values come from the decipherment written over every line.

04 What it says

The cipher stretches, in their place in the letter (in bold in the reading file; clear words not read for this job left out):

Sarà però bene che V.S. accenni se ciò possa derivare o dal pesar S.E. veram., che non occorra più altro, dal non veder volentieri V.S. costà. … non risolve per ancora S.A. la richiamata di V.S., e sta intanto aspettando le risposte categoriche del Sig.r Vincenzio di Medici sopra il capo della scrittura del Sig.r Vicerè, che si mandò a V.S. la sett. pas. … si manderà a V.S. la replica di S.A. alla lettera di S.E. … a Milano furono sentiti malis. et biasimati quelli concetti del Sig.r Vicerè, de quali S.A. con ragione si era risentita.

In English: Galilei is to find out whether the Viceroy thinks nothing more is needed, or simply does not like to see him there. The Grand Duke has not yet resolved on his recall and waits for the “categorical” answers of Vincenzio de’ Medici on a chapter of the Viceroy’s paper sent the week before; the Grand Duke’s reply to the Viceroy’s letter will follow, but not before those answers are seen. In Milan the Viceroy’s notions were very badly received and blamed, and the Grand Duke had taken offence at them, with reason.

In three places the signs and the decipherment of the time differ, and the reading keeps the signs: on L01 the signs give pesar with no di before S.E., where the decipherer wrote “pensiero di S.E.”; on L12 the signs give malis. (malissimo), where he wrote “malam.te”; and on L09 two signs, 5 and a B-shaped sign, stand before aver where he wrote “havere”. His clear follows the sense, not the letters, so it was made from the cipher.

05 The count

The cipher reading has 102 words. 101 carry signs; one, a before V.S. on L07, is restored with no sign at all and is not counted. Of the 101, 99 (98.0%) are deciphered from the symbols under the cipher’s own conventions: 86 verbatim, 9 with a double letter written once, 2 on a doubtful sign settled by sense, and che and et read from signs that show no bar. Not read: the pair 5 8, and code 273, read from context only. Without the two unbarred signs the count is 97 of 101 (96.0%); without the two doubtful-sign words as well, 95 of 101 (94.1%); dropping also pesar, 94 of 101 (93.1%). The independent check counted 98 of 101 for the first figure; the contributor could not reproduce that. The clear text of the letter is outside these figures.

06 What remains open

  • The pair 5 8 at the start of L09: one occurrence, no value; the independent check reads it 5 0? B. Unread either way.
  • Code 273 on L13: Vicerè from its place under the gloss “Vic.Re” only; a second letter of this correspondence would settle it.
  • Doubtful signs in replica (L07, a blot where the 6 should be) and ragione (L13), both settled by sense and marked.
  • che (L01) and et (L12), read from a 5 and a 4 with no bar visible.
  • Outside the cipher: the clear text of cc. 1r-2v was transcribed only around the cipher, roughly; the Commendator Galilei and the Viceroy are not identified.

Prior art: a search of this project’s catalogue, leads and notes, and one web search (3 Nov 2026), found no edition or modern decipherment of the letter. The decipherment of the time on the page is the prior reading of its content.

11 May 1649, c. 1r (cipher lines)

Gondi tells Galilei to find out whether the Viceroy (S.E.) thinks nothing more is needed or simply does not want Galilei there. The Grand Duke has not yet decided to recall him and waits for categorical answers from Vincenzio de' Medici on a chapter of a paper of the Viceroy sent the week before; his reply to the Viceroy's letter will follow. In Milan the Viceroy's notions were very badly received, and the Grand Duke had rightly taken offence.

Reading

reading_c1r.md

# Gondi to Commendator Galilei, 11 May 1649, c. 1r: reading of the cipher (Italian)

The cipher passages are given in their place in the letter. Text in **bold** is from the cipher; plain text is the letter's
own clear text, read only far enough to place the cipher (`[...]` = clear words not read for this job;
`[rough: ...]` = clear words read only roughly, not checked). The line-by-line,
mark-checked form of the cipher reading is `reading_marked_c1r.txt`; `scripts/check_marks.py` checks it against the signs.

Marks in the cipher reading: `(x)` letter with no sign (the cipher writes double letters once); `[?x]` doubtful sign or
value from context only; `{che}` read as *che* where the sign shows a bare 5 (o) without its bar. Codes are expanded.

## Italian

... mandata copia. Sarà però bene che V.S. **ac(c)en(n)i se ciò pos(s)a derivare o dal pesar S.E. veram.,
{che} non oc(c)or(r)a più altro, dal non veder volentieri V.S. costà**. Ma con[...] **non risolve per ancora S.A. la
richiamata di V.S., e sta intanto aspet(t)ando le risposte categoriche del Sig.r Vincenzio di Medici sopra il capo
del(l)a scrit(t)ura del Sig.r Vicerè, che si mandò a V.S. la set(t). pas.** E havuto [...] **si manderà (a) V.S. la
r[?e]plica di S.A. al(l)a let(t)era di S.E.**, non desiderando poi [rough: portarla a S.A., or portar all'A.S.] da far**la prima di [5 8 ?] aver veduto le
sud. risposte del Sig.r Vincenzio** & [...]

[rough: Se già havessi detto ... a V.S.], le dico adesso che **a Milano furono sentiti malis. [?et] biasimati quelli con(c)et(t)i
del Sig.r [?Vicerè], de quali S.A. con ra[?g]ione si era risentita**.

Notes on the text:
- *pesar*: the signs give p-e-n/s-a-l/r, read literally *pesar* ("weighing"); *pensar* would need one more n/s sign.
  The decipherer wrote "pensiero di"; no code for *di* stands before S.E. Kept as a difference, not repaired.
- *{che}*: the bar over 7 (*non*) may cover the 5 too; *che* is the decipherer's reading and fits the sense.
- *[5 8 ?] aver*: two signs (5 and a B-shaped sign) stand before *aver*; the decipherer wrote "havere". Not explained,
  not read.
- *malis.*: the cipher abbreviates *malis[simo]* ("very badly"); the decipherer wrote "malam.te" (malamente). Kept as
  in the cipher.
- *[?et]*: a bare 4 (u/v) where the clear has "et"; the barred 4 is *e* on L03.
- *farla prima di*: the cipher *la* is the object pronoun joined to the clear *far* (*farla*), and *prima di* is
  "before": "to do it before having seen". Word division in the cipher is ours.
- The two `[rough: ...]` clear phrases were read loosely; the independent check reads the second as "portar all'A.S.".
- *[?Vicerè]*: code 273 stands where the decipherer's clear line ends "Vic.Re"; 223 is Vicerè on L06. Context only.

English translation: `translation_c1r.md`.
Translation

translation_c1r.md

# Gondi to Commendator Galilei, 11 May 1649, c. 1r: English translation

Same marks as `reading_c1r.md`: **bold** = from the cipher; `[...]` = clear text not read; `[rough: ...]` = clear text
read only roughly; `[?x]` = doubtful.

... sent a copy. It will be well, however, for Your Lordship **to hint whether this may come from His Excellency's
weighing [the matter] (pesar; decipherer: "thinking") that nothing more is truly needed, or from his not liking to see Your Lordship there**. But [...] **His Highness
has not yet resolved on Your Lordship's recall, and meanwhile he is waiting for the categorical answers of Signor
Vincenzio de' Medici on the chapter of the Signor Viceroy's paper that was sent to Your Lordship last week.** And having
[...] **His Highness's reply to His Excellency's letter will be sent to Your Lordship**, not wishing then [rough: to bring it to
His Highness] to do **it before [5 8, unread] having seen the said answers of Signor Vincenzio** and [...]

[rough: If I had already told Your Lordship ...], I tell you now that **in Milan those notions of the Signor [?Viceroy] were
very badly received [?and] blamed, at which His Highness with reason had taken offence**.

(V.S. = Vostra Signoria, the recipient; S.A. = Sua Altezza, the Grand Duke of Tuscany, Ferdinando II; S.E. = Sua
Eccellenza, here apparently the Viceroy named in the same passages.)
