Johnston to Seddon, Jackson, 16 June 1863: five words in the Confederate Vigenère

→

During the siege of Vicksburg Johnston asked in five enciphered words whether Bragg should invade Kentucky; the War Department’s decipherment is filed with the telegram, and the words fit the key MANCHESTERBLUFF.

01 The telegram

The telegram is a single leaf headed “By Telegraph”, dated “Jackson 16th June 1863” and addressed to “Hon J A Seddon”. It is the telegraph office’s written copy: the cipher words are in spaced capitals, as a clerk copies groups he cannot read. Image 2 is the docket (“1705. Genl Johnston, Jackson Miss. June 16. 1863”) and image 3 a separate leaf, “Translation of Cypher”, with the note “words underscored in Cypher”.

The five cipher words are UNIEK, CXNKVNED, JJTRRK, ISLX and KFYHJEER, 31 letters. The catalogue had the fourth as ISDX; on the full-resolution page image its third letter is a script capital L, so ISLX. The second letter of KFYHJEER is a crossed “7”, the script F of this hand.

02 The 1863 decipherment

The War Department deciphered the words on receipt. Its leaf reads: “Will not this enable us to x invade Kentucky, for this Gen Bragg & should extend over East Tennessee”, with the five cipher words underlined and the note “x words underscored in Cypher”. The text was then printed twice: in Richmond in 1864, in Correspondence between the President and General Joseph E. Johnston … during the months of May, June and July, 1863, and in the Official Records, Series I, vol. 24, part 1 (1889), p. 227, where it reads “the Ninth and Twenty-third Corps” and “General Bragg’s command should extend”. The letter to Bragg of 17 June (p. 196) quotes the sentence.

03 The key

MANCHESTERBLUFF is one of the Confederate Vigenère keys known from other messages, with COMPLETEVICTORY and COMERETRIBUTION. The catalogue entry had tried all three and found nothing. The reason is how the key moves: it runs on from one cipher word to the next, but advances by the length of each plaintext word. INVADE has six letters and UNIEK only five, so a key advanced by cipher length slips one place out of step from the second word on and matches 5 of 33 positions; restarting the key at each word matches 4.

With the key advanced by plaintext length, the expected encipherment matches the written one at 26 of 33 positions:

The key was not assumed. Subtracting the 1863 words from the cipher letter by letter gives the key stream MANEH | STARBLUF | FMANEH | ESTE | RBLUFMAN, which is MANCHESTERBLUFF read straight through with the slips. Both places where the key letter is C were enciphered with E, so the clerk’s copy of the key, or his reading of it, had E for C: one systematic slip, not two.

Controls (check.py): COMPLETEVICTORY matches 1 position of 33 and COMERETRIBUTION 3; 20,000 random 15-letter keys average 1.2, best 11; the fourteen wrong starting points of the right key give 0–4. Every other decryption is gibberish.

04 What it says

General Bragg informs me that a telegram from Louisville of the 10th says that part of the 9th, 23d Corps have been sent to reinforce Grant. Will not this enable us to INVADE KENTUCKY? For this General Bragg & should EXTEND over EAST TENNESSEE. J. E. Johnston, General.

The cipher words are in capitals. Johnston, who had told Seddon the day before that he thought saving Vicksburg hopeless, passes on Bragg’s report that Burnside’s corps were going west to Grant, and proposes that Bragg use the opening to move into Kentucky with his command extended over East Tennessee. Only the operative words were enciphered; the movement of the Union corps went in clear. Vicksburg surrendered on 4 July.

05 What remains uncertain

  • UNIEK: the fourth letter is an E with a hooked top. A hasty C would fit the key (27 of 33 instead of 26); either way the word is INVADE.
  • The cross-like sign after “Bragg” is in clear, read as “&” on both the telegram and the 1863 leaf; the prints have “Bragg’s command”.
  • Which telegraph office enciphered the words, and which copy of the key it held, is not shown on the leaf.

Johnston to Seddon, Jackson, 16 Jun 1863

During the siege of Vicksburg, Johnston passes on Bragg's news that part of the Union 9th and 23d Corps has been sent to reinforce Grant (a telegram from Louisville), and asks Seddon whether this does not allow an invasion of Kentucky, for which Bragg's command should extend over East Tennessee. The words 'invade Kentucky', 'extend' and 'East Tennessee' were enciphered.
