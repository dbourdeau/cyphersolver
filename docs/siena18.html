Siena no. 18: the League articles of Maximilian I

Go to the full text ↓

The articles Maximilian I put to the Sienese orators at Pisa in 1496, every sensitive word in cipher; the key was recovered from the ciphertext alone here and again, independently, by George Lasry, whose complete reading this page gives.

The document
Three written pages of articles: the first and second demands of Maximilian I, King of the Romans, and the Sienese orators’ counter-demands, subscribed by the royal secretary Niklas Ziegler; Pisa, November 1496 (undated; dated by George Lasry).
Where
Archivio di Stato di Siena, Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2, no. 18; DECODE R4806, catalogued “1500–1599” with no sender, receiver or status.
The cipher
Homophonic substitution on 60 signs: letter signs with homophones, signs for doubled letters (rr, pp, ss, ll, cc, tt), dotted or barred signs as nulls, and a few code capitals (B., X., M.X.) left in clear.
How it was deciphered
Twice from the ciphertext alone. Here on 24 September 2026 (annealing with a Latin model, 87.1% read); independently by George Lasry with CTTS and Claude on 15–17 October 2026, from cribs in the clear Latin around the cipher words. His key agrees with ours on 35 of his 38 letter signs.
What it says
Siena is to join the Holy League at half the Pope’s quota, lend the King 5,000 ducats, ready its artillery and open war on the enemy at Ponte a Valiano; the orators ask for every place taken within five miles of their border.
Still open
Six single signs; what B., X., S., M.X. and m. stand for.
Credit
George Lasry: independent solution, the complete reading and translation, the parties and the date (report of 17 October 2026). The project: the first key (24 September 2026) and the comparison.

These articles were filed here until 14 October 2026 with the rest of the Sienese bundle of cipher letters, in which nearly every piece has its own key. The other pieces are different ciphers, each with its own page; the bundle is listed on the collection’s page.

01The articles and their key

Three written pages (P4, P3, P1 in DECODE’s numbering; P2 is blank) carry 1,469 cipher signs in point-separated words inside the running Latin, by George Lasry’s count. P4 opens “Primo loco petita per Cesaream Maiestatem ab oratoribus Senensibus”; P1 carries the subscription “Niccolaus Ziegler, Regis Secretarius” and the King’s second demands.

The project’s key (24 September 2026) came from the annealing search with a Latin model on a transcription of 30 sign names; a check on the images split three look-alike pairs and showed that dotted signs are nulls. It read 1,271 of 1,459 signs (87.1%). George Lasry’s key, recovered without ours, names 60 signs, because his transcription separates glyphs ours had merged. Matched sign by sign, 35 of his 38 letter and doubled-letter signs carry the same value in ours (3 of them as glyphs our transcription had merged with a look-alike); one differs (a sign he reads cc, we c), and two signs that occur once each are not in ours. The one passage printed on his web page (restituerentur, ita tamen quod ipsi Senenses … stare iuri coram) is our own reading of it word for word, cipher and clear words falling the same way.

02What they say

George Lasry’s reading, quoted from his report; abbreviations expanded in square brackets, the code capitals in brackets, ÷ and ✱ single unread signs. The full Latin and translation are in lasry_report.md.

Item q[uod] pro bono rei p[ublice] Sen[ensis] ÷ et [B]. illico manus apponerent ut saltem medietas illor[um] castrorum et arcium, ad que ipsi Senensib[us] ius competit, eis restituerentur, ita t[ame]n q[uod] ipsi Senenses ad requisitionem Florentinorum sint contenti de stando iuri coram Cesare, dummodo Florentini etiam de illis que ipsi occupant sint contenti stare iuri coram ÷

“That, for the good of the Sienese republic, [the League] should at once put its hand to it, so that at least half of the castles and fortresses to which the Sienese have a right are restored to them; on condition that the Sienese, at the Florentines’ request, be content to stand to law before Caesar, provided that the Florentines likewise be content to stand to law before [Caesar] over the places they occupy.” (his translation)

P4, the King’s first demands. Siena is to state its grievances against Florence and the places the Florentines hold; accept Caesar as ordinary and supreme judge; join the Most Holy League at a quota set by Caesar, half the Pope’s; and supply passage, victuals, 100 men-at-arms and 60 light horse.

P3, the Sienese counter-demands. Every town, fortress and castle within five miles of the Sienese border taken in the war goes to Siena, and the “diligentia” and the tower at the Valiano bridge are to be handed over before any peace with the enemy.

P1, the King’s second demands. Talamone and the artillery are to be put in order; Siena lends the King 5,000 ducats, which [M.X.] will repay within two months; Siena enters the League, which will protect its present regime, and opens war on the enemy around Ponte a Valiano. Subscribed by Niklas Ziegler.

Date. The articles are undated. George Lasry places them at Pisa in November 1496, during Maximilian I’s Italian expedition with the forces of the Holy League of 1495, whose treaty set the Pope’s quota at half the others’: Ziegler is attested as the King’s secretary from 1494 and was with the court on 25 November 1496 (Regesta Imperii XIV,2 n. 4508), Pecci’s Balìa deliberations of September–October 1496 record the same demands, and the Sienese attacks on the Florentine bastion at Ponte a Valiano in 1496 were made at the League’s urging (Guicciardini III.8). This page earlier gave “1510s–1520s”; his dating replaces it.

03The two solutions compared

His reading covers the words ours left open or doubtful. Graded against our transcription and key (H read from the key, M uncertain), in ten places (eleven words, ponant and pulveres sharing one place) his readings are H: in ordinem (where we saw a struck word), ponant, pulveres, que after B., cum, armature, defendat (ours defendam), quote (ours quoti), imponitur, ius competit, habeat; three are M: Telamonem (the damaged first word), pax (ours passo?) and his own query s[anctissima]?. In six places of the clear Latin his readings are the grammatical ones. The table, sign by sign and word by word, is in lasry_comparison.md.

The statistical test on the project’s own key (docs/_validate.py, Latin letter model) gave underdetermined: our transcription kept look-alike pairs under one sign name, so a one-value-per-sign key scored below the annealer. George Lasry’s key, which separates those glyphs, explains why.

04What remains

  • Six single signs unread in his text: ÷ four times, ✱ twice.
  • The code capitals, written in clear: B. (the League?), X. (the common enemy), S., m. and M.X. (a League member guaranteeing the loan, the King himself or Milan?); George Lasry leaves M.X. unidentified.

WHAT IT IS: Latin articles/petitions ("Item quod ...", "Secundo loco petit ...", "petita ab oratoribus
  Senensibus"), i.e. a negotiated text between Siena and a royal/imperial chancery, with the substantive words
  written in cipher in the running text.  Word-divided: cipher words are separated by a point (" · ") or space,
  exactly like the clear words, so each cipher group is one plaintext word.
  - P1: end of a first set of articles, signed "Niccolo Zieglog(?)(r.) Regis Secretarius ss." (subscription in the
    same hand: probably Niklas Ziegler, secretary of Maximilian I / Charles V as King, i.e. before 1520/21?; the
    reading of the surname is uncertain) -- then "Secundo loco petit [..] Ce(saree) M(aiesta)ti //" and articles.
  - P2: verso, blank (only bleed-through of P1/P3 visible).
  - P3: articles continued; heading "petita ab oratoribus Senensibus".
  - P4: heading "[..] loco petita per Ce(saream) M(aiesta)tem ab oratoribus Senensibus"; pencil folio no. "18"
    at the foot.
  No date, no address found on the four images.  Hand: one scribe for clear and cipher, brown ink.
  Contains the clear words "Ser.mo d(omino) Regi", "Ce. M.tas", "Senenses", "d(omi)ni Senenses", "B.", "X.",
  "Mx." -- B., X. and Mx. stand alone between points and look like abbreviations/code letters, not cipher words.

SIGN LEGEND (the cipher is a set of ~30 simple pen signs, most of them letter- or digit-like)
  N    square-topped n, "П" (bar with two legs)
  n    round-topped n, "∩" (smaller, round head; distinct from N where legible; merge if in doubt)
  U    u, "ᴜ" (two uprights joined at the foot)
  +    plus / cross
  W    "ш" (three uprights on a foot bar)
  o    small o; often written with a lead-in stroke from the left ("-o"); the stroke is ignored
  c    c                 D  reversed c, "ɔ"
  Y    γ / y (V-fork with a curved tail below the line)
  h    h-like sign with the right leg descending in a tail ("ɧ")
  HX   h crossed by a long slanting stroke (looks like a deletion, but recurs, so kept as a sign)
  H    capital H (often with a dot above "Ḣ")
  T    "⊣": horizontal stroke from the left meeting an upright (like -|)
  L    "⊢": upright with a stroke to the right (|-), also "r"-like
  F    "Γ" / "ſ": tall upright with a hook/bar to the right at the top
  S    "ʃ"/"ς": slanting s-curve descending below the line
  Q    q-like: bowl on the left, stem on the right going through above and below ("ɖ"/"ф")
  4    figure 4 with a descender (open 4)
  9    figure 9 / g: bowl with a long slanting descender
  3    ʒ (figure 3 with the tail below the line)
  6    figure 6          7  figure 7 (angular "ᒣ")          8  figure 8
  OO   "∞" (two joined loops), also written "ao"
  PX   upright crossed by a star/asterisk-like stroke (✱ on a stem); may be clear "pro/per" abbreviation
  -    a dash inside a word

Contents
From: Maximilian I, King of the Romans (royal chancery; subscribed by the secretary Niklas Ziegler)
To: the orators of Siena (with their counter-demands)
Place: Pisa
Date: November 1496 — undated; fixed by Maximilian’s stay at Pisa, Ziegler’s secretaryship and the Valiano 
fighting (see context)
A record of a negotiation in three parts. The King’s first demands (P4): Siena is to state its grievances against
Florence and the places the Florentines hold, accept Caesar as supreme judge, join the Most Holy League at
a quota set by Caesar (half the Pope’s), and supply passage, victuals, 100 men-at-arms and 60 light horse.
The Sienese counter-demands (P3): places taken within five miles of Siena’s borders go to Siena, and the
“diligentia” and the tower at the Valiano bridge are handed over before any peace with the enemy. The
King’s second demands (P1): Talamone and the artillery to be put in order, a loan of 5,000 ducats to the King
repayable in two months, Siena to enter the League, which will protect its present regime, and to open war
on the enemy around Ponte a Valiano; subscribed by the royal secretary Niklas Ziegler.
Historical context and dating
The articles belong to Maximilian I’s Italian expedition of autumn 1496, when the King of the Romans lay at
Pisa and before Livorno with the forces of the Holy League of 1495 (whose treaty set the Pope’s quota at half
the other members’) and summoned Siena to join the war on Florence. Niklas Ziegler is attested as his
secretary from 1494 and was with the court on the Pontremoli pass on 25 November 1496 [Regesta Imperii
XIV,2 n. 4508]; Maximilian signed “Maximilianus rex” at Pisa on 13 November 1496 while Italians called him
“la cesarea maestà” [RI XIV,2 n. 4485; Sanudo, Diarii I]; Pecci’s Balìa deliberations of September–October
1496 record the same demands (orators to the King, bombards shipped from Talamone to Livorno, men-at-
arms, no accord with Florence) [Pecci, Memorie storico-critiche]; and the Sienese attacks on the Florentine
bastion at the Ponte a Valiano in 1496 were made at the League’s urging [Guicciardini, Storia d’Italia III.8].
The DECODE date “1500–1599” is a placeholder.
Open points. The code [M.X.] — a full-quota League member guaranteeing the loan, the King himself or
Milan — is unidentified.

P4
Asked in the first place by His Imperial Majesty from the Sienese orators.
What complaints, and how many, the dominion of Siena has against the Florentines; which fortresses, castles,
lands, strongholds or places the Florentines occupy, and how many, that belong by right to the Sienese
dominion and to which the Sienese have a claim.
Whether, on these complaints and on the right pertaining over the castles, fortresses and strongholds, the
Sienese dominion could accept that [the Emperor], as ordinary and supreme judge, should hear the case and
administer justice.
Whether the Sienese wish to make an understanding and union with the Most Holy [League], namely so that [the
League] helps, protects and defends them in their needs, and so that they in turn also help the other members of
the League according to a quota to be imposed on them in proportion to their means. That quota, by the
judgement of Caesar and with regard to the other powers, would be moderated so that in all levies they would
pay, for instance, half of the quota that would be imposed on the Pope, as [S.] pays half of the quota that is
imposed on [m.] and [M.X.].
That, for the good of the Sienese republic, [the League] should at once put its hand to it, so that at least half of the
castles and fortresses to which the Sienese have a right are restored to them; on condition that the Sienese, at
the Florentines' request, be content to stand to law before Caesar, provided that the Florentines likewise be
content to stand to law before [Caesar] over the places they occupy.
That the Sienese send ... for the bombard ...
P3
... in the territory of Siena the lords of Siena be bound and obliged to give lodging, passage and victuals, and the
instruments the lords have that are needed for war, whichever is required.
That whenever the Most Holy League makes peace, a treaty or any understanding with [X], so that the lords of
Siena are safe from them, ... the lords of Siena be bound to provide at their own expense one hundred men-at-
arms and sixty light horse.
Asked by the Sienese orators.
First, it was replied to the first chapter that it is contained in the twelfth of the demands [above].
That whenever the Most Holy League makes war on [X], all towns, fortresses and castles lying within five miles
of the Sienese borders that are taken, by force or by agreement, with all their courts and appurtenances, be
understood as and be acquired for the lords of Siena, and that [the League] must hand them over to them free
and unencumbered.
That the 'diligentia' and the tower, by force or by agreement, and all [the places above], be taken and handed
over to the lords of Siena; and that whenever any convention, treaty, peace or understanding is made by [the
League] with [X], it may not be made unless the said 'diligentia' and tower have first been handed over to the
lords of Siena free and unencumbered.
P1
... Talamone itself and their other artillery be put in order, and powder and stone shot or balls be made ready,
so that they can be used in time of need.
That they muster their troops as well as they can; His Majesty also intends to send some of his own [troops] to
them as support.

That the Sienese lend the Most Serene Lord King five thousand ducats, for which [M.X.] will bind himself to repay
the same within two months.
Niccolaus Ziegler, the King's secretary.
Asked in the second place by His Imperial Majesty.
First, that the lords of Siena enter [the League], which shall be bound to protect and defend them, the city and
the present regime with all its jurisdiction, against anyone.
That the Republic and the lords of Siena, at the request of [the League] and provided that a sufficient garrison is
given them, be bound to open war against [X] wherever it seems fit to them.
That, at the request of the Most Holy League and of the Imperial Majesty, the lords of Siena be bound to make
war on [X] around the Ponte a Valiano, without any help being given them.
That whenever war is made on the Florentines ... [the text breaks off].
