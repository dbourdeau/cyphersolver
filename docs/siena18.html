Siena no. 18: the treaty articles with the Emperor

Latin articles of an alliance against Florence in which every word that mattered was enciphered; the key came from the ciphertext alone and most of the hidden words are now deciphered.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: partial (87% of the cipher)

ciphertext-only

At a glanceKey recovered from the ciphertext alone, partial · 1,271 of 1,459 signs (87.1%)

The document
Articles negotiated by Sienese orators with a King and the Emperor, subscribed “Niccolaus Ziegler, Regis Secretarius”; four pages, undated.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Siena, Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2, no. 18; DECODE R4806, catalogued “1500–1599” with no status.
The cipher
One sign per letter for the sensitive Latin words, 30 signs, words divided by points; dotted signs are nulls, and a few signs stand for doubled letters.
How it was deciphered
No filed key fits. The staged annealing search with a Latin model gave the key from the ciphertext alone; three look-alike pairs were split on the images.
What it says
Siena lends the King 5,000 ducats, readies its artillery, opens war “in partibus Pontis Valiani” and against the Florentines, and claims the castles taken near its border.
Still open
154 signs in doubtful words, 21 unread; the parties written B., X., S. and Mx.; the year.

These articles were filed on this site until 14 October 2026 with the rest of the Sienese bundle of cipher letters, in which each letter has its own key. The other letters whose keys were found are different ciphers, each with its own page: no. 25, no. 14, no. 4 (Borghesi, 1456) and nos. 6 and 24 (deciphered by George Lasry); the unread pieces are on the bundle’s page.

01The articles and their key

Four pages of Latin articles in which every sensitive word is in cipher: 212 cipher words, 1,459 signs, written with points between them in the running Latin. The first block is subscribed “Niccolaus Ziegler, Regis Secretarius”, the secretary of Maximilian I and later of Charles V. The staged search, with a Latin model, gave the key from the ciphertext alone; a sign-by-sign check on the images split three look-alike pairs (a hooked n is q, a plain one e; a plain o is g; an open double loop is i, a closed one ll) and showed that dotted signs are nulls. 1,271 of 1,459 signs (87.1%) read as Latin sense, 154 more are in doubtful words, 21 are unread and 13 the scribe struck out.

02What they say

Item quod Senenses mutuent Serenissimo domino Regi quinque milia ducatorum, pro quibus Mx. se obligabit de restituendo eosdem in termino duorum mensium. … Item quod ad petitionem Serenissimi Regis & Cesaree Maiestatis teneantur domini Senenses rumpere bellum contra X. in partibus Pontis Valiani …

Siena is to ready its artillery, powder, stones and balls, put its troops in order and lend the King 5,000 ducats repayable in two months. The imperial side asks that B. take the city under its protection, that Siena open war on X. at Ponte a Valiano (the fortress of the Montepulciano war), give passage and victuals for a war on the Florentines, and furnish at its own cost a hundred men-at-arms and sixty light horse. The Sienese ask for every castle taken within five miles of their border, and for the Emperor to judge their claims against Florence. The letters B. and X. stay in clear and are not resolved; X. is the enemy. The year is not given: Ziegler, a King and an Emperor as two parties, and Ponte a Valiano point to the 1510s or 1520s.

03How it was checked

A statistical test (docs/_validate.py, Latin letter model) compares the reading with random keys and with keys fitted to shuffled ciphertext. The reading beats both by a wide margin, but the verdict is underdetermined: the transcription keeps some look-alike pairs under one sign name, so a strict one-value-per-sign key scores below what the annealer reaches on the real text. The reading itself, word by word, is the check: it was re-read on the images in two passes.

04What remains

  • 21 doubtful words (the ω-shaped sign reads m, tt or n) and five unread words, at the limit of the DECODE resolution.
  • B., X., S. and Mx., written in clear, are unresolved; the year is not fixed.
