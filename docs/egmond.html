Arnhem → grand master of France · simple substitution in graphic signs, with nulls · year unknown

Charles of Egmond to the grand master of France

George Lasry recovered the key to this cipher in 2023 (cryptiana GL.htm §2). This page is an independent re-solution: the key was recovered again from the ciphertext alone, from a new transcription of the Gallica images, and Lasry’s key was found only afterwards. BnF français 3015 no. 8, folio 16, has sixteen lines of cipher, two raised insertions and a ciphered address, all decrypted. The text is French. It asks the grand master of France to continue supporting Charles’s affairs and to assist his ambassador, the commander of Saint John.

The date reads “dih[u]itieme de iuillet”: apparently 18 July, with one ink-heavy sign. The year and the recipient’s personal name are not supplied.

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · complete

The letter
Charles of Egmond, duke of Guelders, to the grand master of France, from Arnhem on the apparent date 18 July (no year recovered). He says that obeying the king’s commands made him enter the war, and asks for continuing support and for help for his ambassador, the commander of Saint John.
Where
BnF français 3015 no. 8, f. 16 (Gallica btv1b9060086g, views 27–28). The catalogue called it an entirely ciphered letter with a ciphered address.
The cipher
A French substitution of sixteen lines, two raised insertions and the address, with many null signs (tall looped f forms, small circles) and paired arches for r.
How it was read
George Lasry recovered the key in 2023 (cryptiana GL.htm §2). Here it was recovered again from the ciphertext alone, from a new transcription, and Lasry’s key was found only afterwards. It rests on internal consistency, not yet checked symbol by symbol against his.
What it says
The address reads “A mon bon cousin / Monsieur le grant maistre / de France”. All of the body, address and both insertions decrypt.
Still open
The year and the recipient’s personal name; a few minor glyph uncertainties (the x in lextreme, the ink-heavy u in the date).
Credit
Key first recovered by George Lasry (2023).

01 The manuscript

The BnF catalogue places item 8 at folio 16. Gallica view 27 contains the body; view 28 contains the address. The clear closing and signature identify Charles. The separate letter at folio 24, views 39–40, is dated 3 April 1537 and also addresses the grand master, but is not a clear copy of this cipher. Its year cannot be transferred to this letter.

03 Meaning in English

My cousin, having been informed that you give all possible support to my affairs, I wished to write and ask you to continue in the future, considering the extreme need in which I now find myself after obeying certain commands of the king, which caused me to enter this war. You will learn more fully from the commander of Saint John, my ambassador, whom I have instructed to communicate everything to you. Please give him such assistance as he needs and believe what he tells you on my behalf. By doing this you will oblige me increasingly to you, as our Lord knows. Having commended myself to your good grace, I pray that he keep you in his protection. From Arnhem, this eighteenth day of July [year absent].

This translates the apparent sense, not every literal anomaly. “Certain” interprets the unexpanded aulc; “eighteenth” interprets dih[u]itieme. The cipher gives titles, not the personal names of the recipient or ambassador.

04 How the key was recovered

Short French, German and Italian substitution probes on rough transcriptions gave no reliable decryption. Extending the transcript to all sixteen lines gave French fragments under a five-gram model. Image comparison then corrected compound signs and nulls: the paired looped stems labelled DD give s, and the paired arches CE give r. Tall looped f forms, small circles and several other shapes are nulls. Early drafts had confused m/n zigzags, seven/b with corner/f, and angular u with a plain cross/g.

The address and body share letter values and null behaviour. Their coherent decryptions cross-check each other, but are not independent known plaintext. A synthetic 180-letter French substitution control was recovered exactly by the probe; it checks the solver implementation only. The searches recorded in the notes found no earlier decipherment, but one existed. George Lasry had recovered the key in 2023. His reconstructed key for the Duke of Guelders’ cipher (BnF fr. 3015) is published in section 2 of S. Tomokiyo’s “George Lasry’s codebreaking”. It notes that many symbols in the ciphertext are needlessly doubled. The work on this page is therefore an independent re-solution. It has not yet been checked symbol by symbol against Lasry’s table.

The final body transcript contains 726 labelled signs, including both insertions; the address has 48. The body uses 27 label names, including variants of u and a special label for the blotted occurrence. This is a transcription inventory, not 27 proven distinct historical characters.

05 Uncertainties and limits

  • M, line 4: x in lextreme is assigned to a small sign with its bar extending left from the upright. Native-image inspection distinguishes it from the plain cross/g, but its value is supported by one occurrence and context.
  • M, line 15: the u in dih[u]itieme is ink-heavy. The decryption points to 18 July; no year is present. The catalogue’s tentative 1520s label is not established by this decipherment.
  • M, insertion 7: the small e after t overlaps another line’s descender.
  • I/M: several m/n zigzags are distinguished partly by context. All main lines were inspected, but this remains an inferred key without an independent historical control.
  • The literal forms sueis, uouellu, cosidere, auir, aulc and the repetition en ce / ce faisant survive native-resolution re-inspection. They may reflect spelling, abbreviation, enciphering slips or residual transcription error. The edition does not choose silently between those possibilities.
  • No personal names are substituted for “grand master of France” or “commander of Saint John”. A complete year and the precise campaign remain unestablished.
  • Prior solution. Lasry recovered the key in 2023 (see How the key was recovered). The next check is to compare his key with the values inferred here.

## Meaning in modern English

My cousin, having been informed that you give all possible support to my affairs,
I wished to write and ask you to continue in the future, considering the extreme
need in which I now find myself after obeying certain commands of the king,
which caused me to enter this war. You will learn more fully from the commander
of Saint John, my ambassador, whom I have instructed to communicate everything
to you. Please give him such assistance as he needs and believe what he tells
you on my behalf. By doing this you will oblige me increasingly to you, as our
Lord knows. Having commended myself to your good grace, I pray that he keep you
in his protection. From Arnhem, this eighteenth day of July [year absent].

This translates the apparent sense; it is not a replacement for the literal
reading above. “Certain” interprets the unexpanded `aulc`; 18 July interprets
`dih[u]itieme`. No recipient's personal name or year is supplied in the cipher.

## Residual uncertainties and limits

- **M, line 4:** the small cross assigned EX/x in `lextreme` is supported by
  context. Native-image review shows its bar extending left from the upright, unlike the plain plus/g; the value still rests on one occurrence and remains M.
- **M, line 15:** the u in `dih[u]itieme` is ink-heavy, with its stem visible.
  The apparent date is 18 July. No year is present in the recovered date clause.
- **M, insertion 7:** the small e after t overlaps a descender from the line above.
  The ten-sign sequence reads `entenderes` and the caret places it after `amplement`.
- **I/M, throughout:** several m/n zigzags are distinguished partly by context;
  the early provisional labels conflated them. Their occurrence audit is recorded
  in `body_audit.json`, but this does not constitute an independent decipherment.
- The raw forms `sueis`, `uouellu`, `cosidere`, `auir`, `aulc` and the repetition
  `en ce / ce faisant` have not been silently repaired. They may reflect spelling,
  enciphering slips, abbreviations, or residual glyph errors. Native-resolution re-inspection retains these shapes; it does not establish which of those explanations is responsible.
- The title identifies the recipient as grand master of France. The actual person
  and the ambassador's personal name remain unestablished. “Commander of Saint
  John” is a title in the letter, not a recovered personal name.
- The separate clear letter at folio 24, dated 3 April 1537, is contextual evidence
  only. It is not a matching clear copy and cannot supply this letter's year.

## Reproduction

Run `python targets/egmond/decode_audited.py`. It reads explicit shape labels and the
inferred key, replays both raised insertions at checked token anchors, and writes
`complete_raw_reading.txt`. The original failed probes and earlier drafts remain
available as the research trail; they are not alternate accepted plaintexts.

# Charles of Egmond, Duke of Guelders, to the Grand Maître of France, Arnhem, 18 July [year absent]: English translation

My cousin, since I have been informed that you give all the support you can to the good of my affairs, I wished to write
you the present letter to beg you to be pleased to continue in future, considering the extreme necessity in which I now
find myself for having obeyed certain commands of the King, which is the reason I have entered this war, as you will hear
more fully from the commander of Saint John, my ambassador, whom I have charged to communicate everything to you. Be
pleased to give him such assistance as he will need, and to believe him in what he tells you on my behalf; in so doing you
will oblige me more and more to you, as our Lord knows, whom, after commending myself to your good grace, I pray to keep
you in His care. From Arnhem, this eighteenth of July.

⟨Wholly your good cousin. CHARLES.⟩ ⟨To my good cousin, Monsieur the Grand Maître of France.⟩
