This letter was filed with three French reports sent to Rákóczi, which use the Bonac–Groffey key DECODE R639. It is in a different cipher; those letters have their own page: three letters to Rákóczi, 1706–1707.

01 The letter

The letter is signed in autograph “Franciscus Princeps” and dated in clear “Leopoli die 27. Junii Anno 1711”; DECODE’s 7 June is a misreading. It is addressed to “Fidelis nobis sincere dilecte”, an envoy on his way to Holland, who is in poor health and handles money and the mathematical instruments ordered from Mangold. A clear postscript deals with those instruments. Thaly’s Archivum Rákóczianum I/3 (the letter-books of 1710–1712) does not print the letter, but its no. 44, an order to Mihály Klement about the Hegyi wines, makes Klement the likely addressee (searched 23 October 2026). The six pages carry 2,531 cipher groups in 180 lines, transcribed here from the images; cipher and clear alternate within the lines.

02 What it says

Rákóczi sends a copy of the “Tractatus sic dictae Pacis Carolianae”, the Peace of Szatmár, which he calls a fraud worked out in Vienna, and reports that Archduke Charles was proclaimed king in every Hungarian county, contrary to the 1687 article of the Pozsony diet. He will not recognise a king proclaimed outside a free diet:

nos Regem extra dietam promulgatum, non deposito praevio modo juramento suo, agnoscere non posse sine [?totali] ruina libertatum nostrarum

He appeals to the Tsar and the Polish Republic to mediate. He offers to come to a free diet in person or by plenipotentiaries and to recognise a lawfully crowned king, provided the confederates recover their estates. He says the Allies looked only to a “diversionem armorum domus Austriacae” once Hungary was quieted, not to Hungarian liberties, and asks for the war to go on. Page 4 reports Pálffy’s artillery before “arcem nostram Munkatsiensem” and the restitution of Transylvanian liberty. Page 5 turns to the general confederation in Poland, letters to go “per Saxoniam”, the Muscovite advance, a warning to trust only news sent directly from Rákóczi, and a complaint about the “Indiscretum procedendi modum Magni Cancellarii Regii Raday”, the Grand Chancellor Pál Ráday. Page 6 concerns receipts left with a Jew and a member of the Economic Council who took treasury money for his own use.

03 The decipherment and the key

The clerk wrote the decipherment above every ciphered stretch. Of the 2,531 groups, 2,524 lie on a line with an interlinear; the other seven continue words written on the neighbouring line (a one-group ending, a catchword repeated at the head of the next page, and three groups whose words stand on the line above).

The key is not preserved. Each cipher line was aligned with the Latin above it by an alignment algorithm (hard expectation-maximisation), with each code taking up to four letters. The result is a syllabic cipher with homophones. Single letters have several values (190 and 430 s; 140 and 380 n; 60 and 300 e; 20 and 260 a; 130 and 370 m), open syllables have their own codes (257 re, 428 ti, 68 se, 362 de, 96 no, 457 ri, 148 si, 92 ce, 152 co, 59 tu, 378 ta), and a few words and names are coded whole. Of 224 codes, 156 occur at least twice. Decoding the letter with this key reproduces 3,180 of the clerk’s 4,547 letters (69.9%). The mismatches are rare codes, homophones seen once and the clerk’s own abbreviations. Where the clerk’s handwriting is doubtful, the cipher often settles the word, as in the sentence quoted above and in “ne fors etiam”.

The running text (23 October 2026). The reading audit of 22 October 2026 had set the letter back to decoded, not read: the clerk’s interlinear merged with the clear passages line by line stood out of reading order, with doubts or alternatives on most lines. It is now written out as one running Latin text (R483_reading.md, English R483_en.md): the interlinear stretches and the clear passages interleaved as they stand in each line, every clear word and every doubtful interlinear word re-read on the six DECODE images, and the clerk’s doubts settled group by group with the rebuilt key. 72 changes are listed in R483_corrections.tsv: ab inimicitiis for the clerk’s ab Armistitiis, inimicitias suas for amicitiis fidis, tranquillos reddat animos for his letter-by-letter Transi- l w s re d da, domino Jablonski for Fablanski; juramento suo, which the clerk skipped, is restored from the figures. No word is supplied without figures under it. Counted by words, with the open and doubtful words as holes, the running text reads at 94.9%: just under the 95% a complete reading needs.

The Bonac–Groffey table R639, which deciphers the French letters once filed with this one, gives nonsense on it.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Five words of the clear opening on page 1 (“Supponimus redditas jam […] …”): fast cursive not settled on the DECODE images; a higher-resolution scan from the archive is the way on.
  • About a dozen doubtful words where the clerk and the key disagree and the group occurs once or not at all in the rebuilt key ([?concludendam], [?remediationis], [?possidere], [?commisso], domino Jablonski […]).
  • 68 of the 224 rebuilt codes occur only once (C).
  • The recipient is not named; probably Mihály Klement (above).

which a better can hardly be supplied than through the restoration of the liberty of Transylvania, which in the
present conjuncture the Archduke ought not to make difficulties over, so that, the minds of the Hungarians being won
and bound entirely, he could apply his army according to the necessity and exigency of his affairs.

[?If] [?therefore] you yourself, because of your state of health, could not continue your journey to Holland, you are
to communicate this without delay, as the need of our interest requires, to those who are well disposed and to whom
it shall be necessary. You are to send [?the same] views to the King of Poland's Commissary General, a man named
Spiegel, who is here in Poland, and to write them out as soon as possible in [?letters] directed through Saxony, so
that we can adapt the present conjuncture, whether tending to peace (if a suitable one can be made) or to war, to our
affairs and the interest of our country. Further we announce what [?happier] news our correspondents have brought of
the progress of the Muscovite arms, from which it can firmly be hoped that that war will be finished with easy labour,
as the Tsar desires. As for your expenses, we believe it is fully clear to you what we have so far disapproved; [you
are] to adapt yourself in future too to the state of affairs and of the times, whose straits restrict us as well.
Trust least of all [?letters] and information from private persons, unless they come directly from us; for in the
present circumstances it can hardly be decided whom one should trust. Ráday ought long ago, by our order, to have
[?written] to Lord Jabłonowski […] about the indiscreet way of proceeding of the Royal Grand Chancellor, assuming that
you had continued your journey to Holland; but at the same time we received letters of the said Grand Chancellor
written from Dukla, in which he notifies that he does not want to keep the wines once [?accepted] still at vintage
time, the original receipts having been issued about them and being held by us, but to leave them in the care of a
certain Jew, to be handed over to our deputed man when he comes.

But since we believe that we have satisfied [the matter] entirely by [the decision] of our Economic Council issued on
it […], the wines having been consigned to the custody of his own man from the time the receipts were issued, carried
at our expense to the appointed place and left in his custody, if any damage has befallen the royal cellar through
the carelessness of the keepers, we do not consider ourselves answerable for it. For the wines, the money advanced to
Hegyi of the [?Council] of the Realm's Economy [is to be claimed] from him to whom he can claim [it] as owed beyond our
orders; for he, [?having committed] a breach of faith with the others, has turned the money to his own uses, and is
not at all subject to our authority. For the rest we remain benignly inclined to you with our princely favour. Lwów,
27 June, year 1711. Francis, Prince.

We are astonished that no mention is made in your letters of the mathematical instruments and other things ordered
from Mangold, for whose payment your assignation too has been dispatched; we wish to enjoin you seriously that all
those things which we ordered to be made there be carried out without delay and sent here.
