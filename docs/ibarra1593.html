Diego de Ibarra from Paris, 10 July 1593

A Spanish letter from Paris during the Estates of the League, in the syllabic cipher of the Duke of Feria and the Count of Mansfeld: Tomokiyo’s table gives its syllables, but its three-letter word codes are still unknown.

Method: read with known key · Extent: none (key obtained, text not read)

The letter
Don Diego de Ibarra, Spanish agent in Paris, 10 July 1593; three lines in clear, then twenty lines of cipher.
Where
BnF français 3984 no. 68, f. 145 (Gallica, canvas 268). Catalogue item 19, with three despatches in the Spanish office cipher.
The cipher
The syllabic cipher of the Duke of Feria and the Count of Mansfeld (1593): numbers for syllables, single signs for letters, three-letter symbols for words.
How it was deciphered
Tomokiyo’s key image, which cites this folio, was transcribed and applied to a full transcription of the leaf; a shuffled-key control confirms it, and one more value (57 = li) was fixed by a constrained search.
What it says
Fragments and a few sentences: waiting for the cavalry, “A mi no me parece que puede con esta gente”, “No estamos desesperados”, danger, “espero en Dios que ha de acudir”, the size of the army in Flanders.
Still open
Most three-letter word codes (pra, dur, gar, var, tor, vis, xur …); seven were fixed on 23 October 2026 from the contemporary decipherment of a sibling letter, f. 130–131. Two numbers the model cannot separate.

This letter was filed with three despatches of 1593 in the Spanish office cipher, a different cipher with its own page: Miranda and Sessa, 1593.

01 The letter

The BnF dépouillement of fr. 3984 describes no. 68 as a “Lettre, avec chiffre, de «D. DIEGO DE IBARRA,… De Paris, a 10 de julio 1593». En espagnol.” It is f. 145, canvas 268 of the Gallica copy. The letter opens in clear and changes to cipher mid-sentence:

This leaf is in a different cipher, the Feria–Mansfeld key, whose numbering is unrelated: ba 24, ca 25, da 10, la 59, ma 49, que 66, and de the single figure 8. The letter opens in cleartext and changes to cipher mid-sentence:

“… a la venida del mensagero que despache, porque espero que me dara V.S.a mas claridad de las cossas, porque en la primera me dise V.S.a” — then que aguarda cavallería y … y no continua … que aya recivido su …

02 The cipher and the key

Satoshi Tomokiyo’s page on the ciphers of Philip II’s reign carries, as an image, a Syllabic cipher for Duke of Feria and Count of Mansfeld (1593), cited at fr. 3984 ff. 130 and 145. This leaf bears it out: its values run far higher than in the office cipher of 1592–93 (97, 98, 800, 7243), and it carries the three-letter word codes he describes (pra, rom, mon, xen, vul, cil; he gives vas = segnor as an example).

All twenty cipher lines were transcribed on 5 October 2026: 598 groups. Decoded with his table, they give Spanish: a mi no me parece que puede, se [ha] de poner todo lo que se puede, lo que podemos, aliento, y si ella apareciera, y palabra, no estamos desesperados, peligro, espero, de acudir a lo que esta, pareceria que venia a ayudar … y la cavalleria, a lo menos no parece sino. A Spanish character model of the period scores the decode at −2.500 per character; 200 keys with the same values shuffled among the numbers score at most −3.378. A free hill-climb over all numbers collapses onto que and was rejected; a constrained search for the numbers missing from the table fixed 57 = li (peligro, aliento). 64, probably ha by context, is not separated from na by the model and stays open.

Scored line by line for sense, 224 of the 598 groups (37.5%) give Spanish. The diacritic finals and doubling marks were left out of the transcription, so the words come out without their final consonant. Phrases scattered among unread word codes do not make sentences: read as a text, the letter is decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026), and the 37.5% is withdrawn as a reading figure.

03 A crib in the same volume (23 October 2026)

The other folio Tomokiyo cites for this key was opened on 23 October 2026. F. 130 is a letter of Mansfeld to Feria, Formentieres, 7 July 1593, wholly in this cipher, and f. 131r–v holds its contemporary French decipherment, together with that of Feria’s own letter of 10 July (cipher f. 143). The crib fixes 64 = ha, 63 = he, 62 = hi, 31 = jo, 19 = fa, 81 = vi, and the word codes xen = Infanteria, pun = hombre, mon = gente, tel = exercito, fon = Flandes, dir = Magestad, Vas = señor (mon and tel flagged for review). With them about a quarter of the letter’s sentences build; the partial reading is texts/no68_reading_b28.md (three-letter codes still open in angle brackets, [?word] doubtful):

A mi no me parece que puede con esta gente la [?tregua], me parece la que <gar> en <sil> de <vis> <77> de <cle> que puede ha[?…] y que <var> es to[?…].

No estamos desesperados de [?presente] <bel> de <pus>, solo nos de <77> ta de todas estas <vel>; el peligro que en ta <vir> <xur>, espero en Dios que ha de acudir a lo que esta[?…] de su <vis> [?…] poco <ful> <xas> <cel> lo que <pra> di[?…] <xur>.

The letter stays decoded, not read: the other three-letter groups (pra, xur, gar, dur, var, tor, vis …) carry much of the sense, and the 1593 French decipherer left them blank too.

04 What stays open

  • The three-letter word codes other than those the f. 131 decipherment fixes (pra, dur, hes, gar, rom, vul, cil, var, vis, tor, xur …), which Tomokiyo does not tabulate and the decipherer of 1593 also left blank. The nearest candidate list, fr. 3995 f. 101 beside the Spanish office key, is an Italian nomenclature of another correspondence.
  • The numbers 77 and 95 and the groups 200T, 800T: no candidate stands out.
  • The diacritic finals are still to be added to the transcription from the image.

Diego de Ibarra, Paris, 10 July 1593, BnF fr. 3984 no. 68, f. 145 decoded, not read

Ibarra writes from Paris during the Estates of 1593 in the Feria-Mansfeld cipher: the messenger he is despatching, cavalry awaited, 'no estamos desesperados', peril, and help that might come with the cavalry. The three-letter word codes, which carry much of the sense, have no published table and stay unread.

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.

Duke of Feria (1593)*, which Tomokiyo published as `spanish3mansfeld.png` and cites at fr. 3984 ff. 130 and

## The limit on this letter

Lines 7-10 decode to clean syllables but not to continuous Spanish, and the reason is structural rather than
palaeographic: this cipher's **nomenclature is not published anywhere**. Its three-letter groups — *pra, dur,
hes, ʒel, gar, pun, rom, mon, xen, vul, cil, denp* — carry much of the sense, and Tomokiyo notes only one of
them in passing (*vas* = *segnor*) without tabulating the rest.

**Checked and excluded:** BnF fr. 3995 f. 101 (canvas 194), the large multi-column nomenclature on the leaves
adjacent to the Spanish office key, is **Italian, not Spanish** — *Austria, Ambasciatore, Assemblea,
Battaglia, Bisogno, Borbone, Borgo o Sobborgo, Cardinale di, Castello, Cattolici, Concilio generale,
Congregazione generale, Corte di* — so it belongs to another correspondence and does not serve this cipher.
The Feria-Mansfeld nomenclature has still to be found.

## Sense

Ibarra writes from Paris on 10 July 1593, at the height of the Estates, about **the coming of the messenger
he is despatching**, hoping for **more clarity on matters**; **cavalry being awaited**; something that **does
not continue**; and **that he may have received his** …
