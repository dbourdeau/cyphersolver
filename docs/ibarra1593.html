Paris → the Spanish correspondence of the Duke of Feria · Feria–Mansfeld syllabic cipher · 10 July 1593 · known key applied, partial

Diego de Ibarra from Paris, 10 July 1593

A Spanish letter from Paris during the Estates of the League, in the syllabic cipher of the Duke of Feria and the Count of Mansfeld: Tomokiyo’s table gives its syllables, but its three-letter word codes are still unknown.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: partial (38% of the cipher)

With Satoshi Tomokiyo (key or decipherment used) and Arya Sanketbhai Patel (identification)

On this pageThe letterThe cipher and the keyWhat stays openSources

At a glanceKnown key applied · partial · 224 of 598 groups

The letter
Don Diego de Ibarra, Spanish agent in Paris, 10 July 1593; three lines in clear, then twenty lines of cipher.
Where
BnF français 3984 no. 68, f. 145 (Gallica, canvas 268). Catalogue item 19, with three despatches in the Spanish office cipher.
The cipher
The syllabic cipher of the Duke of Feria and the Count of Mansfeld (1593): numbers for syllables, single signs for letters, three-letter symbols for words.
How it was deciphered
Tomokiyo’s key image, which cites this folio, was transcribed and applied to a full transcription of the leaf; a shuffled-key control confirms it, and one more value (57 = li) was fixed by a constrained search.
What it says
Waiting for the cavalry, what can be done, “no estamos desesperados”, danger, and help that would seem to come.
Still open
374 groups: the three-letter word codes (pra, dur, hes, gar, pun, rom, mon, xen …), which no published table or crib fixes, and five numbers the model cannot separate.

This letter was filed with three despatches of 1593 in the Spanish office cipher, a different cipher with its own page: Miranda and Sessa, 1593.

01 The letter

The BnF dépouillement of fr. 3984 describes no. 68 as a “Lettre, avec chiffre, de «D. DIEGO DE IBARRA,… De Paris, a 10 de julio 1593». En espagnol.” It is f. 145, canvas 268 of the Gallica copy. The letter opens in clear and changes to cipher mid-sentence:

This leaf is in a different cipher, the Feria–Mansfeld key, whose numbering is unrelated: ba 24, ca 25, da 10, la 59, ma 49, que 66, and de the single figure 8. The letter opens in cleartext and changes to cipher mid-sentence:

“… a la venida del mensagero que despache, porque espero que me dara V.S.a mas claridad de las cossas, porque en la primera me dise V.S.a” — then que aguarda cavallería y … y no continua … que aya recivido su …

02 The cipher and the key

Satoshi Tomokiyo’s page on the ciphers of Philip II’s reign carries, as an image, a Syllabic cipher for Duke of Feria and Count of Mansfeld (1593), cited at fr. 3984 ff. 130 and 145. This leaf bears it out: its values run far higher than in the office cipher of 1592–93 (97, 98, 800, 7243), and it carries the three-letter word codes he describes (pra, rom, mon, xen, vul, cil; he gives vas = segnor as an example).

All twenty cipher lines were transcribed on 5 October 2026: 598 groups. Decoded with his table, they give Spanish: a mi no me parece que puede, se [ha] de poner todo lo que se puede, lo que podemos, aliento, y si ella apareciera, y palabra, no estamos desesperados, peligro, espero, de acudir a lo que esta, pareceria que venia a ayudar … y la cavalleria, a lo menos no parece sino. A Spanish character model of the period scores the decode at −2.500 per character; 200 keys with the same values shuffled among the numbers score at most −3.378. A free hill-climb over all numbers collapses onto que and was rejected; a constrained search for the numbers missing from the table fixed 57 = li (peligro, aliento). 64, probably ha by context, is not separated from na by the model and stays open.

Scored line by line for sense, 224 of the 598 groups (37.5%) give Spanish. The diacritic finals and doubling marks were left out of the transcription, so the words come out without their final consonant.

03 What stays open

  • 374 of 598 groups. Most of the sense sits in the three-letter word codes (pra, dur, hes, gar, pun, rom, mon, xen, vul, cil, var, vis, tor …), which Tomokiyo does not tabulate. Cribs do not reach them: Paris and Su Magestad are in clear on the leaf, and Feria, Mansfeld, Liga fit no syllable run, so they would sit among those codes. The nearest candidate list, fr. 3995 f. 101 beside the Spanish office key, is an Italian nomenclature of another correspondence.
  • The numbers 64, 31, 77, 81, 95 and the groups 200T, 800T: no candidate stands out.
  • The diacritic finals are still to be added to the transcription from the image.
  • The other letter Tomokiyo cites for this key, fr. 3984 f. 130, has not been opened: Gallica refused the request on 14 October 2026. A second letter in the cipher could fix some word codes by repetition.
