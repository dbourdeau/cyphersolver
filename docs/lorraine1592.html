01The document, and the search for the ciphered originals

No. 97 is at folio 109 (Gallica canvas 227): “Coppie d’une lettre … de Charles de Lorraine à monsieur de Vaudemont”, Nancy, 18 June 1592. The volume is the duc de Nevers’s intercept and correspondence file for 1591–92. The task came with an instruction to check first for the ciphered originals of the letters intercepted in January 1592. They are not in the volume. No. 22, at folio 31, is a plaintext decipherment only: a fair copy of the French of three intercepted Vaudémont letters, with no cipher anywhere on the sheet, headed “Dechiffrement des lettres de Monsieur de Vaudemont à Monsieur de Lorraine [son] pere, interceptees et apportees à Monsieur de Dinteville, le XX[e] Janvier 1592”. DECODE agrees: its record for no. 22 (9444) is marked Decrypted. The other ciphered pieces of the volume (Birago nos. 31 and 36, Potier no. 79, Dinteville nos. 114 and 116) are Nevers’s own correspondents and their keys, not Lorraine’s. So no. 97 is a single copy with no sibling.

The letter mixes clear French and cipher inline on the same lines, and the postscript is clear. The clear text concerns the same business as the cipher: munitions promised and undelivered, Chaumont, the sieur de Buzonville, the attempt on Chasteauvillain.

02No key has ever been published

DECODE record 9449 is this letter, by shelfmark, sender, receiver and date, and its status field reads Non-decrypted; of the eight DECODE records for Français 3621 only this one and no. 116 have that status. No key record in DECODE is attributed to Charles III or to Vaudémont. Keys for other Lorraine ciphers are published and are not this system: Bréval to Henri II of about 1620, and Bassompierre to Charles III of 1 August 1593, both reconstructed by Satoshi Tomokiyo. Desenclos and Lasry’s 2024 paper on the Henri IV–Nevers ciphers of Français 3995 never mentions Français 3621, and Henri Lepage’s 1864 edition of Charles III’s League correspondence stops in 1591 and prints no cipher table.

A prior reading did exist. The search above looked for a published key and missed a published claim. Satoshi Tomokiyo’s list of unsolved historical ciphers marks this letter (“Duke of Lorraine (1592)”, DECODE R9449) as solved by him in 2025, and quotes its opening: “J’ay este infiniment mal satisfait du peu d’execution …”. He published no key and no further text. The entry was already there on 6 September 2026, before this attempt; it was found on 24 September, after the reading, and was not used. The reading here opens with the same words, which independently confirms the first line.

Four 1592 key sheets in the Nevers key book, Français 3995, whose DECODE profile matched this cipher, were fetched and examined. None of the four is this key.

03The key recovery of 18 September (contributed)

An earlier attempt had treated the page as a cipher of invented symbols: segment the glyphs, cluster them by shape, solve the cluster sequence. The segmentation worked (the baselines slope, and a shear of −0.031 resolves the block into 21 line bands), but the clustering was unnecessary. The glyphs are ordinary cursive minuscules, the same letters as the clear text, some with dots or a bar, with a handful of special signs, and at one and a half times native size they can be transcribed directly from the page. The contributor’s transcription recorded 1,109 tokens: 1,071 letter symbols in 44 distinct forms, and 38 nomenclator figures. Its letter frequencies match French rank for rank through fourteen places, and its index of coincidence lies between those of a monoalphabetic and a fully homophonic system.

On a control with a known key of the same size, the random-move annealer of the earlier attempt recovered only 37–56% of letters at −2.64 per character while the true key scored −1.62: the failure was in the search. A steepest-ascent search with restarts from local optima recovers known keys at 98.7–99.6%. Two traps were closed on the way (free nulls that delete every hard position, and a degenerate etenete… key). The key it found was checked against the manuscript, not only the model: the eight-form group it deciphers as chasteau occurs twice in the cipher, and Chasteauvillain stands in the clear on the same page, in the body and in the postscript.

A 4-gram search over 44 free parameters can produce French-looking text from random input, so the pipeline was also run unchanged on shuffled nulls of the same symbols and frequencies, and scored blind:

Thirty-three long words against none to three: there was French under the transcription. From controls with deliberately merged glyphs, the contributor estimated that about a quarter of the glyph transcriptions were still wrong, and proposed re-transcribing the page with the key in hand. The 18 September decryption gave, for example, chasteauilainss [145] nntaises deramenermonarme dooites esquartiers de-, with whole words correct and the joins between them wrong.

04The re-transcription of 22 September, and what it corrected

The re-transcription was done as proposed: the native IIIF image of canvas 227 (4046 × 5762), sheared −0.031 and cut into 21 line bands, each viewed in five crops at 2.3×, every word checked against the 18 September decryption. Laid out as pairs, the 18 September values already showed the system: glyph a gave i and glyph i gave a, o gave r and r gave o, q gave d and d gave q, n gave s and s gave n. Every letter of the hand stands for its partner in eleven fixed pairs, the whole 22-letter alphabet of the period:

At least part of the nomenclator is therefore syllabic, like the syllable figures of the fr. 3995 keys, and not only a list of persons and places.

The 18 September key had 44 symbols instead of eleven pairs mainly because of two transcription merges. The transcription’s c was two glyphs, the secretary e (which stands for t) and c (which stands for f). The search had to send the merged form to t, so every f in the letter was lost (intiniment, satistait). This is why the old key had no value at all for b, f, g, x and z, a gap the earlier page had already flagged as the place where mis-transcribed glyphs had gone. Its B was likewise two glyphs, the beta-like b (p) and the looped secretary h (x, hence h): du peu but ceulx and d’execution. The dotted and barred variants that the search had spread over extra homophones are doublings. Correcting those turned most of the short connecting words of the old decryption into plain French with no further guessing.

What changed in the decipherment:

  • prisonniers is pionniers: the Duke complains of pioneers and munitions promised, not of prisoners.
  • 139 and 145 are not names. The earlier page guessed, from the clear text, that they might be Chasteauvillain and Chaulmont; in context they decode as vous and me (grade C).
  • The f words come back: infiniment, satisfait, fait, affin, faire, fauldra, la Faulche.
  • The chasteauilains slip that the null test exposed is confirmed at both places (L14, L18): the scribe left out the v twice, so it is an emendation, not a fault of the key.
  • The measure: the old claim that about three quarters of the letters were right was an estimate from controls. Counted word by word against the new decipherment, the 18 September key and tokens give the same words for 449 of 1,105 tokens, 40.6%; the re-transcription gives 790, 71.5% (section 06).

In substance: the Duke has been infinitely ill satisfied with the little execution of what was promised. 06How much is deciphered, measured

The rule, stated in measure_reread.py: the unit is the cipher token (one letter sign, a dotted double counting once; one special sign; one null; one figure). Each word of the decipherment is graded H secure, C probable, M doubtful or I not deciphered, and a token counts as deciphered if its word is H or C. Every word is also decrypted mechanically with the pair key, so a decipherment that departs from the key shows up as an emendation.

07What remains uncertain

315 tokens are not deciphered. Each open piece, with the reason it is open:

  • Re-measured 5 October 2026. After a further pass on 5 October 2026, 844 of 1,102 tokens (76.6%) are deciphered (measure_reread.py); 787 (71.4%) also pass a lexicon check (measure_sense.py). These count tokens in recognised words, not sentences: read as a text, the decode has a gap in almost every line, and the letter is decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026). Settled: λ, γ, U, π and m̄ are nulls, and L18’s run reads l’effect du dict; quelq, auld…is and promes were downgraded to M because they fail the lexicon check.   • Re-read 23 October 2026. The graded re-read of 5 October was written out as running text with the clear lines (ct/no97_partial_reading.md, with English), the standalone crossed t taken as a null (eight of eight places between complete words). One sign in five is still open, and only about one sentence in seven holds: the letter stays decoded, not read. DECODE R9449 and R9444 were opened again (one image, no attachment), and the DECODE record list and the BnF keys of 1580–1600 were searched for a key in this pair system; none was found (fr. 3623 no. 23, R9453, is the Bienvenut–Nevers key of 1590). A key or duplicate may survive in the Lorraine ducal archive (Archives départementales de Meurthe-et-Moselle), not consulted.

fr. 3621 no. 97, Nancy, 18 June 1592 decoded, not read

A contemporary copy of Charles III of Lorraine's letter to his son the comte de Vaudémont, with clear French and cipher alternating on the same lines. The Duke is infinitely ill satisfied with the little execution of the promises of pioneers and munitions made to him, urges his son to press the attempt on Chasteauvillain, and writes of putting someone there to command and hold the plain and of bringing his army back to quarters. The volume is the duc de Nevers's intercept and correspondence file for 1591-92.

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.

# BnF fr. 3621 no. 97: the re-read (22 Sept 2026)

Charles III of Lorraine to the comte de Vaudémont, Nancy, 18 June 1592 (fol. 109, Gallica canvas f227,
DECODE R9449). This replaces section 5 of `no97_reading.md` as the reading of record. Transcription and
word grades: `no97_reread.txt`; measurement: `../measure_reread.py`.

## 1. What the re-read changed: the system is a reciprocal pair cipher

Going back to the page with the 18 Sept decode beside it showed that the key was not 44 free symbols
at all. Laid out as pairs, the 18 Sept values already said so: glyph *a* = i and glyph *i* = a, *o* = r and
*r* = o, *q* = d and *d* = q, *n* = s and *s* = n. Every letter of the hand stands for its partner in eleven
fixed pairs:

    a<->i   b<->p   c<->f   d<->q   e<->t   g<->u   l<->y   m<->z   n<->s   o<->r   h<->x

(22 letters, the whole alphabet of the period). Plus:

* **two dots under a letter double it**: ṇ = ss (*promesses*, *assister*, *passer*), c̤ = ff (*affin*),
  c̈ = tt (*remettre*);
* the looped æ is a **null**, used as a word divider (*peu · d'execution*, *belles · promesses*);
* a small set of **special signs** (λ, Θ, ꝗ, m̄, π, γ, capital E, t with a low bar, reversed 3, a figure-4
  form); Θ = s is secure (*chemins*, *audits*); the rest are open;
* **figures for syllables and words**, at least some syllabic: **31 = "ma"** (*com-31-nder*, *de-31-nde*,
  *me 31-nde*, *31 resolution*), **139 = "vous"** (*chemins, 139 tiendres*; *et 139 ne manquies*),
  **145 = "me"** (*affin que 141 ne 145 delaisent*).

The two transcription merges that did most of the damage in the 18 Sept decode:

* the transcription's **c** was two glyphs, the secretary **e** (→ t) and **c** (→ f). The old key had
  to send it to t, so every f in the letter was lost (*intiniment*, *satistait*); five plain letters
  (b f g x z) had no cipher value at all, as the page write-up noticed;
* its **B** was two glyphs, the beta-like **b** (→ p) and the looped secretary **h** (→ x): *du peu* but
  *ceulx* and *d'execution*.

Correcting those two, and reading the dotted and doubled forms as doublings, turns most of the 18 Sept
"connective tissue" into plain French with no further guessing.
