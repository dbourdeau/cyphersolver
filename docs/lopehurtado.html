Lope Hurtado de Mendoza at Rome, 1522 — an unpublished cipher broken, four letters read

The imperial agent in Rome reporting to Charles V on Adrian VI, in a cipher nobody had published. Two clear copies bound with the letters cribbed the key, and four of nine letters are read.

At a glanceBroken · four of nine letters read

The letter
Nine despatches of Lope Hurtado de Mendoza from Rome to Charles V, September to November 1522, in the months before the French came back into Italy.
Where
Real Academia de la Historia, Salazar 9/26, on DECODE as R9634, R9644–R9646, R9648–R9650, R9652 and R9656. Catalogued with Alonso Sánchez’s Venice letters, among which they are bound.
The cipher
Three-letter code groups written as Latin letters, with spelled runs in a substitution alphabet, inline with clear Spanish. It differs from Sánchez’s: the same group xul means despues there and lo here.
How it was read
R9644 and R9650 carry the clerk’s own full clear version. Aligning cipher against it fixed forty-seven code values, with twenty-nine more probable. Unaided, the key reads el papa in a letter with no clear version.
What it says
Adrian VI temporising between Charles V and Francis I: the French resolved to come into Italy, the Pope tan escasso que nunca hombre lo fue mas, so stingy no man was ever more so, his household unpaid.
Still open
Five letters have no clear version. There the key resolves 42% to 57% of tokens, fragments rather than a reading. Scans sharp enough to separate similar signs would complete the alphabet.
Credit
Tomokiyo’s 2025 article reconstructs the sibling ciphers of Sánchez and Juan Manuel. It does not mention Hurtado.

01They are not Sánchez's letters

These nine records sit in Salazar 9/26 among Alonso Sánchez's despatches from Venice, were catalogued with them, and look at first like more of the same: the same months, the same recipient, cipher mixed with clear inside the sentence, code groups of the same shape and the same three-letter build. The catalogue treated them as one target.

Two things separate them, and neither is a matter of impression. Sánchez's nomenclator uses a strict third-position inventory — every code group ends in b c d f g h l m n — while Hurtado's groups take finals p and z (tep, tap, groups ending -z), which Sánchez's key does not contain. And where the two keys use the same group they disagree: xul is despues in Sánchez and lo here, ho is que in Sánchez where this cipher uses ton. Same design, same chancery tradition, different key.

Nor is it Juan Manuel's, the third imperial cipher of 1522 Rome: none of the values fixed here appears in Tomokiyo's 173-code table for it. That table is itself a partial recovery, so this is consistent rather than conclusive, and it is recorded that way.

02Nobody had published the key

Satoshi Tomokiyo's Correspondence in Cipher of Imperial Ambassadors Alonso Sanchez and Juan Manuel (1522) (Cryptiana, 6 September 2025) reconstructs two of the three ciphers in this group of volumes. It does not mention Lope Hurtado, and his records — R9634, R9644–R9646, R9648–R9650, R9652, R9656 — fall outside both of the ranges it lists. A search turns up no decipherment anywhere. DECODE records seven of the nine as Non-decrypted.

03The crib was bound with the cipher

R9644 (ff. 237–243, 1 November 1522) holds the ciphered letter on ff. 237–239 and the clerk's own full clear version on ff. 241–242, headed Al Rey — De Lope Hurtado, de Roma, del primero de noviembre, written out in lettered sections with A, B, C standing in the margin of both. R9650 (ff. 269–272) has the same arrangement, its clear on f. 272. And R9648 (9 November, Non-decrypted) turns out to be R9644's duplicate, so that one clear reads two records.

04The first anchor

The clear at the foot of section A reads … porque esta tan escasso que nunca hombre lo fue mas; hasta que el arçobispo venga creo que sera mejor no apretallo mucho. On the cipher page the middle clause stands in the clear, bracketing the run exactly — six tokens for six words, with no room for slippage:

tontabxudxulɣmxon
quenuncahombrelofuemas

and tu = no from no apretallo mucho. From there the alignment walks: zun top at da por, ᵇʒ7 and teɡ at era and para, zil ɣub zed zar at con el arçobispo de Cosenza, ɣub taf at el papa, rab at v. ma.

05How the cipher is built

Three-letter code groups carry most of the text and are written as plain latin trigrams; spelled runs between them use a substitution alphabet. Three features matter for anyone reading it, and none is in the published account of the sibling ciphers:

  • Homophones at code level. ta and tu both mean no, and both occur in the same line. The alphabet has them too: the single spelled word perdida writes d with two different signs.
  • Codes carry syllables, not only words. xur+zun = na+da; ton+ℓ∞74 = que+rian; zil + a spelled ocia = conocia; nul+vuc+s = obligados. zun is da the whole word in da por, the second syllable of na·da, and the middle of que·da·rian.
  • And codes run together to make one word. lu+ho = porque, lul+ta = quales, ton+d+o = quedo.

Taken together: word boundaries in this cipher go both ways, and any tokeniser that treats one group as one word will mis-read these letters in both directions.

06The key works where it was not built

A key fitted to its own evidence will read the evidence and nothing else. This one does not stop there.

R9648, which DECODE calls Non-decrypted, gives nine tokens against nine words with nothing left over — ʋ ton ɣub taf tu ɣof ɣuc ton + spelled venga = «y que el papa no esta en que venga» — and further down la paz por los infieles y por la necessidad. Its spelled perdido decodes letter by letter from six signs won on the other record, in a word not looked for.

R9656 is the real test: a despatch of 20 November with no clear version and no duplicate anywhere. The key reads there unaided — after the clear words las cartas traxo don Correo q vino con la valante, ɣub taf stands at el papa.

Measured with a resolver that applies confirmed values only: 70% of tokens on R9648 f. 262, 62% on R9650 f. 270, 57% on R9645 f. 243, which has no crib of any kind. Those figures are unchanged by the alphabet downgrade below, because they were carried almost entirely by the code groups — which is itself the evidence that the reading rests on the safe half of the key.

07What the letters say

Four of the nine are read complete as to content: R9644 and R9650 from their clear versions, R9648 as R9644's duplicate, and R9652, which DECODE records as Partially decrypted but which carries no cipher at all — the leaf is written entirely in the clear and signed Lope Hurtado.

R9644, 1 November. Managing a Pope who will not move. Charles V had written to warn Adrian VI directly; Hurtado approves, because if the Pope will not do his duty he should at least not be able to plead that nobody told him — no se disculpe despues con dezir que v. magestad no le mando prevenir. The Pope's voluntad is good and his obras satisfy nobody; he pleads the Turks and the Emperor's need, and is tan escasso que nunca hombre lo fue mas. Hurtado presses for the papal household to be paid before the other side buys them: si no los corrompiessen dandoles. The King of France asks a safe-conduct to send an ambassador and the Pope will not have him come.

R9650, November. The intelligence that mattered: los franceses son determinados de venir en Italia, y enbian gran suma de dinero a Leon, brought by post from the archbishop of Bari, with Hieronymo Adorno's intercepted letters behind it. Adrian is urged to act sin dilatar mas, and takes it tan tibiamente que no se espera de todo bien.

R9652, November. A licenciado Bernardino, «gran bellaco», is in Rome; the Duke of Sessa wants him seized; the Pope will order it if the man is inside Rome, declines a night arrest when he is found lodging with his brother in the city (aqui no seria bien), and offers a formal suplicacion instead. It closes with las nuevas de Rodas que escrive el visorrey — the viceroy of Naples on the siege of Rhodes.

That last line joins the two halves of this archive. Sánchez's R9653 of 20 November carries the Rhodes news from the other direction, out of Candia and the Signoria. Rome and Venice were reporting the same disaster to the Emperor in the same fortnight, in two different ciphers, bound now in the same volume.

08What is open, and how good the reading is

  • Five records have no clear version — R9634, R9645, R9646, R9649, R9656 — and there the key works alone. Measured on sample leaves, it resolves 42% of tokens on R9656 and 57% on R9645; R9634, R9646 and R9649 have not been measured. What resolves is concentrated in the function words and the names, with the spelled content words as the misses. R9645 comes out as … se si no con […] con vuestra magestad, que no […] en […] esta[do] […] de vuestra magestad …; R9656 as … no […] el papa […] la […] que […] su el papa. That is fragments, not a reading. An earlier draft of this page said these five read “in substance”; measured, they do not, and the claim is corrected here. Enough survives to identify what a passage is about when the surrounding clear text helps, and not enough to read the letter.
  • The substitution alphabet is the limit. Nine signs were first taken as fixed, each cross-checked on several words — ⋃=p, 8=e, ʒ=r, x/6=d, L=i, 7=a, 4=n, m/ᶻ=o. An 8× zoom on the spelled perdido in R9648 then showed its e and d signs looking near-identical, so they had been told apart on weaker evidence than “fixed” implies: all ten alphabet sign entries are now probable only, and the resolver shows them in angle brackets. The forty-seven confirmed values are code groups. Beyond those nine signs, transcriptions of the spelled words contradict each other at DECODE's resolution. One of those contradictions turned out to be a missing homophone rather than a bad reading, so the rest may resolve the same way; unassigned signs are left unassigned rather than guessed.
  • Confidence is tracked, not assumed. The key file carries a confidence column; the resolver applies confirmed values only and shows probables in angle brackets. An audit for two codes claiming the same plaintext produced four retractions, kept in the file as commented RETRACTED lines — among them an early guess that zar meant Bari, when it means de. Nothing probable was used to derive anything else, so nothing downstream needed unwinding.
  • A correction to DECODE. R9652's status should be not enciphered, not partially decrypted.

09The one thing that would finish it

The blocker is measured, not guessed. DECODE serves these leaves at 3440×2465 for a two-page opening — about 1700 px across a folio, which is enough for the latin trigrams and not enough to tell two similar cursive signs apart. That is the cause of the alphabet limit, and no amount of further crib-walking changes it.

DECODE has been checked with a logged-in cookie and has nothing better. The full-size file filesrv returns is that 3440×2465 opening; MASTER_, MS_, ORIG_ and .tif variants all come back as the server’s not-found placeholder, and the Image Manager answers “You do not have permission to access” to a viewer session, so its zoom view is unreachable too. The ceiling is DECODE’s stored derivative.

The volume belongs to the Colección Salazar y Castro, which the Real Academia de la Historia has been digitising at its own Biblioteca Digital. If Salazar 9/26 is there, those images will be far better than DECODE’s derivative copies, and the substitution alphabet becomes ordinary work. That would in turn give a word-perfect edition of the ciphered leaves and lift the five records with no clear version out of fragments and into a reading.

It could not be done from here: bibliotecadigital.rah.es returns HTTP 403 to automated requests and puts a bot check in front of its search, in the same way DECODE’s image server needs a logged-in session. The search term is the old signature, Salazar y Castro, A-26, and the leaves wanted are ff. 237–243, 260–272 and 334–335.
