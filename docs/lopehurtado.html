Rome → Charles V · nomenclator of three-letter code groups with a substitution alphabet, inline with clear Spanish · Sept–Nov 1522

Lope Hurtado de Mendoza at Rome to Charles V, 1522

Real Academia de la Historia, Salazar 9/26 · DECODE R9634, R9644–R9646, R9648–R9650, R9652, R9656 · the imperial agent in Rome reporting to the Emperor on Adrian VI, in the months before the French came back into Italy

Nine letters catalogued with Alonso Sánchez’s Venice letters, among which they are bound, in a different nomenclator. The key was recovered from the clerk’s contemporary clear versions bound with two of them; with it, one letter from Genoa reports a plan to invade France through Provence, eighteen months before the 1524 campaign.

The letters
Nine records of Lope Hurtado de Mendoza, the imperial agent, to Charles V, September–November 1522: eight from Rome on Adrian VI, and one (R9634) from Genoa, 13 September 1522.
Where
Real Academia de la Historia, Colección Salazar y Castro 9/26 (A-26), DECODE R9634, R9644–R9646, R9648–R9650, R9652, R9656. Bound and catalogued with Alonso Sánchez’s Venice letters, but in a different key.
The cipher
Three-letter code groups for words and syllables, with a substitution alphabet for spelled words, inline with clear Spanish. Runs open with a mark (Ꮒ) often followed by null groups.
How it was deciphered
R9644 and R9650 carry the clerk’s full clear versions; aligning them with the cipher gave the code values. On 2 October 2026 the key was extended on R9634 itself (ɋ = r, a plural z after codes, ducado, mar, gente, the nulls after the opener) and applied to the four letters with no usable clear.
What it says
Adrian VI temporising between Charles V and Francis I, his capitulation with the duke of Ferrara, the fortress of Ostia; and from Genoa, Geronimo Adorno’s plan for the Emperor and Henry VIII to invade France through Provence, la tierra mas flaca que el tiene, at 70,000 ducats a month, in March.
Measured
Tokens read as sense (3 October 2026): R9634 872 of 1,191 (73.2%), R9645 368 of 489 (75.3%), R9646 97 of 121 (80%), R9649 89 of 89 (100%), R9656 177 of 225 (78.7%, re-transcribed from the image); the five together 1,603 of 2,115 (76%). Under 95%, so partial.
Still open
About 40 code groups that occur once in R9634 and nowhere else in 21 letters, and spelled words in all four partial letters whose signs cannot be told apart at DECODE’s resolution; a word-perfect text of the ciphered leaves of R9644, R9648 and R9650.

01They are not Sánchez's letters

These nine records sit in Salazar 9/26 among Alonso Sánchez's despatches from Venice, were catalogued with them, and look at first like more of the same: the same months, the same recipient, cipher mixed with clear inside the sentence, code groups of the same shape and the same three-letter build. The catalogue treated them as one target.

Two tests separate them. In Sánchez's nomenclator every code group ends in one of b c d f g h l m n; Hurtado's groups also end in p and z (tep, tap, groups ending -z), which Sánchez's key does not contain. And where the two keys use the same group they disagree: xul is despues in Sánchez and lo here, ho is que in Sánchez where this cipher uses ton. Same design, same chancery tradition, different key.

Nor is it Juan Manuel's, the third imperial cipher of 1522 Rome: none of the values fixed here appears in Tomokiyo's 173-code table for it. That table is itself a partial recovery, so this test supports a separate key without proving it, and it is recorded that way.

02Earlier work on these records

Satoshi Tomokiyo's Correspondence in Cipher of Imperial Ambassadors Alonso Sanchez and Juan Manuel (1522) (Cryptiana, 6 September 2025) reconstructs two of the three ciphers in this group of volumes. It does not mention Lope Hurtado, and his records (R9634, R9644–R9646, R9648–R9650, R9652, R9656) fall outside both of the ranges it lists. A web search found no decipherment. DECODE records seven of the nine as Non-decrypted, that is, with no decipherment attached.

03The crib was bound with the cipher

R9644 (ff. 237–243, 1 November 1522) holds the ciphered letter on ff. 237–239 and the clerk's own full clear version on ff. 241–242, headed Al Rey — De Lope Hurtado, de Roma, del primero de noviembre, written out in lettered sections with A, B, C standing in the margin of both. R9650 (ff. 269–272) has the same arrangement, its clear on f. 272. R9648 (9 November, with no decipherment on DECODE) is a duplicate of R9644, so one clear version covers two records.

04The first anchor

The clear at the foot of section A reads … porque esta tan escasso que nunca hombre lo fue mas; hasta que el arçobispo venga creo que sera mejor no apretallo mucho. On the cipher page the middle clause stands in the clear, bracketing the run: six tokens for six words, with no room for misalignment:

tontabxudxulɣmxon
quenuncahombrelofuemas

and tu = no from no apretallo mucho. From there the alignment extends: zun top at da por, ᵇʒ7 and teɡ at era and para, zil ɣub zed zar at con el arçobispo de Cosenza, ɣub taf at el papa, rab at v. ma.

05How the cipher is built

Three-letter code groups carry most of the text and are written as plain latin trigrams; spelled runs between them use a substitution alphabet. Three features matter for decryption, and none is described in the published account of the sibling ciphers:

  • Homophones at code level. ta and tu both mean no, and both occur in the same line. The alphabet has them too: the single spelled word perdida writes d with two different symbols.
  • Codes also stand for syllables. xur+zun = na+da; ton+ℓ∞74 = que+rian; zil + a spelled ocia = conocia; nul+vuc+s = obligados. zun is da the whole word in da por, the second syllable of na·da, and the middle of que·da·rian.
  • Several codes can make one word. lu+ho = porque, lul+ta = quales, ton+d+o = quedo.

A code group can therefore be part of a word, a whole word, or one of several groups forming a word. A decryption that treats each group as one word goes wrong in both directions.

06Tests on letters the key was not built from

A key fitted to its own crib can reproduce the crib and nothing else. This one was tested on other records.

R9648, which has no decipherment on DECODE, gives nine tokens for nine words with nothing left over: ʋ ton ɣub taf tu ɣof ɣuc ton + spelled venga = «y que el papa no esta en que venga»; further down, la paz por los infieles y por la necessidad. Its spelled perdido decrypts letter by letter from six symbols fixed on the other record; the word was not searched for.

R9656 is the stronger test: a despatch of 23 November with no full clear version and no duplicate (a faded clear copy on f. 335r is legible only in half of each line). Applied without a crib, the key gives el papa for ɣub taf after the clear words las cartas traxo don Correo q vino con la valante.

Measured with a resolver that applies confirmed values only: 70% of tokens on R9648 f. 262, 62% on R9650 f. 270, 57% on R9645 f. 243, which has no crib of any kind. The figures do not change when the alphabet values are downgraded to probable (below), because almost all resolved tokens are code groups, the better-established half of the key. These were coverage figures for sample lines; the whole-letter readings of 2 October 2026 (sections 08 and 09) measure tokens read as sense instead.

07What the letters say

The content of four of the nine is complete: R9644 and R9650 from their clear versions, R9648 as R9644's duplicate, and R9652, which DECODE records as Partially decrypted though it carries no cipher at all. That leaf is written entirely in clear and signed Lope Hurtado.

R9644, 1 November. Charles V had written to warn Adrian VI directly; Hurtado approves, because if the Pope will not do his duty he should at least not be able to plead that he was not warned: no se disculpe despues con dezir que v. magestad no le mando prevenir. The Pope's voluntad is good and his obras satisfy nobody; he pleads the Turks and the Emperor's need, and is tan escasso que nunca hombre lo fue mas. Hurtado presses for the papal household to be paid before the other side buys them: si no los corrompiessen dandoles. The King of France asks a safe-conduct to send an ambassador and the Pope will not have him come.

R9650, November. The main news is that los franceses son determinados de venir en Italia, y enbian gran suma de dinero a Leon, brought by post from the archbishop of Bari, with Hieronymo Adorno's intercepted letters behind it. Adrian is urged to act sin dilatar mas, and takes it tan tibiamente que no se espera de todo bien.

R9652, November. A licenciado Bernardino, «gran bellaco», is in Rome; the Duke of Sessa wants him seized; the Pope will order it if the man is inside Rome, declines a night arrest when he is found lodging with his brother in the city (aqui no seria bien), and offers a formal suplicacion instead. It closes with las nuevas de Rodas que escrive el visorrey, the viceroy of Naples on the siege of Rhodes.

Sánchez's R9653 of 20 November carries the same Rhodes news from Venice, out of Candia and the Signoria. Rome and Venice reported the siege to the Emperor in the same fortnight, in two different ciphers, now bound in the same volume.

08Genoa, 13 September 1522: the Provence plan (R9634)

R9634 (ff. 14–16) was transcribed on 2 October 2026 from DECODE’s three openings; DECODE attaches no decipherment or transcription to it. It is not a Rome letter. The clear close on f. 16r is dated de Genoba xiij de setiembre 1522 and signed by Lope Hurtado; DECODE has the month but neither the day nor the place. There is no clear version in the record and none in print (Bergenroth’s Calendar of State Papers, Spain ii calendars no Hurtado letter of September 1522), so the reading rests on the key alone.

Written four months after the imperial sack of Genoa (30 May 1522), the letter reports a plan that “sensible and honourable men” and Geronimo Adorno, the new imperial doge, put to Lope Hurtado. The Emperor and the King of England, queriendo destruir al Rey de Francia, should make war on him through Provence:

que es la tierra mas flaca que el tiene, y donde el exercito de V. Mt. puede entrar seguro, una tierra fertil … [para proveer con las galeras de Genova] … donde pueden entrar bien … los de la mar y los de la tierra

The French are muertos de miedo after the sack. Taking Provence and Lyon would let the allies tomarle lo otro que le queda y cercarle en Paris, which could not be done por Flandes ni por España. Asked what force it would need, Adorno said six hundred men-at-arms, light horse and soldiers, paid 70,000 ducats a month (lxx U yob z), if the Emperor and the King of England agreed on it; the troops already in the duchy of Milan would serve, and the Pope would be glad to see them go, por no los tener alojados en sus tierras. It must be kept secret, por que no toviesen aviso, and settled now for March: es cosa segura, de poca costa e provechosa. The clear close commends Adorno as tan buen servidor de V. Mt.

Charles V and Henry VIII did invade Provence in 1524, when Bourbon and Pescara laid siege to Marseille. This is an early form of that plan, put to the Emperor from Genoa in September 1522.

What the letter added to the key (key_1522_r9634.tsv): a new sign ɋ = r (tierra three times); z after a code as the plural s (xul z los, yob z ducados), which is not the 1524 standing-alone z = n; the codes yob ducado, xep mar, xu gente, zar∂ des-; and null groups after the run opener Ꮒ: ʃta confirmed in four letters, the others probable. The e/t confusion of the 1524 letters recurs: the closed 8 and the open-topped ϑ cannot be separated at DECODE’s resolution, so muertos, acordasen, faltasen and gastara are graded probable.

Measured from the line-by-line reading (r9634_reading.tsv): 842 of 1,191 tokens read as sense on the first pass, 846 after a retry against the values won on R9645 (71%), and 872 (73.2%) on 3 October 2026, when codes were pooled over all 21 Hurtado letters and spelled words were matched against a Spanish lexicon with every reading of their doubtful signs (vio, capitan, fue, vivo, tenia, ser, sostener, all probable). The key values 80% of the tokens as confirmed and 94% with probables; the gap is spelled words whose letters all have values but give no word, and about 45 code groups that occur once. A language check on the read text returns modern Spanish first.

09The other four letters without a usable crib

Earlier versions of this page listed R9634, R9645, R9646, R9649 and R9656 as having no clear version. That was out of date for two of them: R9649 carries its own clerk’s clear on f. 268, and R9656 a faded clear copy on f. 335r, legible only in the right half of each line. All four were re-read on 2 October 2026 with the extended key.

  • R9649, Rome, 9 November 1522 (89 of 89 tokens, 100%). Deciphered completely, and checked word for word against f. 268. Adrian VI asks Charles to tell him to keep good guard on the fortress of Ostia, which Cardinal Santa Cruz (Carvajal, as dean of the college) claims: en la fortaleza de Ostia, y que no la de a nadie, porque no querria darsela ni tener le quexoso. The licenciado Bernardino has landed from Barcelona and is hiding at his brother’s house in Rome. The groups yω ʃta after the opener have no counterpart in the clear and are nulls.
  • R9646, [Rome, November 1522] (97 of 121, 80%). Adrian VI’s capitulation with the duke of Ferrara is signed and a copy is coming. Charles’s request (la decima, probable) meets papal caution: the Pope abia miedo que oviese de […]; the archbishop advises offering money al papa para sus necesidades … que creo que por dinero ….
  • R9645, Rome, November 1522 (368 of 489, 75.3%). Re-transcribed in the R9634 notation. The glosses at the foot of f. 243v (Bernardino’s report from London: the King of England is agreed with the Scots, who have thrown out the duke of Albany; the French boast that the English and the Emperor’s troops did nothing) confirm xu = gente and a null after the opener, and settle yib = duque. Unglossed f. 243r now reads as the terms of the Ferrara capitulation: the fief money of Pope Alexander, a hundred men-at-arms for the Church, no league save with the Pope and the Emperor, no harbouring of their enemies.
  • R9656, Rome, 23 November 1522 (177 of 225, 78.7%). Re-transcribed from the images in the R9634 notation on 3 October 2026 (it was 131 of 214, 61%, in the older notation): the old ʒ was mostly ɣ̊ = r, and the f. 334v run now reads in full against the faded copy, que esta muy sospechoso el papa que de alla se le haya mandado lo que ha fecho (zub = alla). The archbishop of Bari sent the Pope no certain news of the French coming; the Pope says that if the French come it will be to their harm; he is muy sospechoso that the Emperor ordered what Don Juan Manuel has done. Six spelled words of f. 334r (ll. 6, 9, 13–16) still fail: no reading of their signs is a Spanish word.

10What is open, and how complete the decipherment is

  • Four letters are deciphered in part, measured as tokens read as sense: R9634 73.2%, R9645 75.3%, R9646 80%, R9656 78.7% (sections 08 and 09; R9649 is complete). What stays open in them: in R9634 about 40 code groups seen once (none recurs in the 21 Hurtado letters) (ʃe, xer, zud, zac, ʃum, yat, zub, ʃof, xen, zer, yul …) and yel and ɣ̊e four times each, with no crib and none in print; spelled words whose letters all have values but give no word (two of them probably place names), and the force numerals other than the 600 men-at-arms and the 70,000 ducats; in R9645 tec (ze = el from four pooled contexts, probable), ʃen and the spelled words of the unglossed lines; in R9646 two spelled words and the codes zip, zob, ʃof; in R9656 six spelled words of f. 334r and the end of f. 334v l. 1. DECODE has no larger image than the one used, and the RAH Biblioteca Digital, which may hold better scans, sits behind a bot check that only a person in a browser can pass. Each spelled-word gap has one sign at the limit of the scan (8/ϑ e/t, ∂ s/p/t, ε l/g). Graded: the codes and the alphabet signs confirmed on the clear versions are H; values taken from a single R9634 context (yob, xep, the nulls other than ʃta) are I; words marked probable in the reading files are M.
  • The substitution alphabet is the limit. Nine symbols are fixed and cross-checked: ⋃=p, 8=e, ʒ=r, x/6=d, L=i, 7=a, 4=n, m/ᶻ=o. Beyond these, transcriptions of the spelled words contradict each other at DECODE's image resolution. One contradiction proved to be a homophone missing from the key, and the others may resolve the same way; unassigned symbols are left blank.
  • Confidence is recorded for each value. The key file carries a confidence column; the resolver applies confirmed values only and shows probables in angle brackets. A check for pairs of codes with the same plaintext led to four retractions, kept in the file as commented RETRACTED lines, among them an early guess that zar meant Bari, when it means de. Nothing probable was used to derive anything else, so nothing downstream needed unwinding.
  • Corrections to DECODE. R9652's status should be not enciphered; DECODE has partially decrypted. R9634 is dated Genoa, 13 September 1522 on f. 16r; DECODE has the month only and files it with the Rome letters.

11What would complete it

The limit is image resolution. DECODE serves these leaves at 3440×2465 for a two-page opening, about 1700 px across a folio: enough for the Latin-letter trigrams, too little to separate two similar cursive symbols. That is the cause of the alphabet limit, and further crib work does not change it.

DECODE, checked with a logged-in session, has nothing better. The full-size file filesrv returns is that 3440×2465 opening; MASTER_, MS_, ORIG_ and .tif variants all come back as the server’s not-found placeholder, and the Image Manager answers “You do not have permission to access” to a viewer session, so its zoom view is unreachable too. The ceiling is DECODE’s stored derivative.

The volume belongs to the Colección Salazar y Castro, which the Real Academia de la Historia has been digitising at its own Biblioteca Digital. If Salazar 9/26 is there, those images will be far better than DECODE’s derivative copies, and recovering the substitution alphabet becomes routine. That would give an exact text of the ciphered leaves and should allow a full decipherment of the four letters now deciphered in part, and of the faded clear copy of R9656 on f. 335r.

It could not be done from here: bibliotecadigital.rah.es returns HTTP 403 to automated requests and puts a bot check in front of its search, in the same way DECODE’s image server needs a logged-in session. The search term is the old signature, Salazar y Castro, A-26, and the leaves wanted are ff. 14–16, 237–244, 252, 260–272 and 334–335.
