Huang Xing’s three-kana telegrams

Three telegrams of May 1916 between Huang Xing and the anti-Yuan leaders in Guangdong, written as romanised kana, decoded with Satoshi Tomokiyo’s formula; one decode filed in 1916 is corrected by the cipher.

The telegrams
Two telegrams of 20 May 1916 to Huang Xing in Tokyo, from the Military Affairs Council and from six of its members at Zhaoqing, and Huang Xing’s reply of 25 May. All three went through Japanese hands: the consul at Zhaoqing and the Foreign Ministry relayed them.
Where
Diplomatic Archives of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, via JACAR: B03050731500 frame 0246 (the reply, with a decode on frame 0247); B03050089100 frames 0076–0078 (the 20 May stream, with 1916 worksheets); a second copy of the stream in B03050731100, images 24–26.
The cipher
Each character’s four-digit standard telegraph code is written as three kana by a fixed arithmetic rule, and the kana are sent as romaji in ten-letter words.
How it was decoded
Tomokiyo identified the rule in September 2026. The reply was decoded here from this project’s own transcription with one correction. The 20 May telegrams were transcribed, corrected and read by Harumi-Matsuba, and checked here with an independent decoder.
Credit
Satoshi Tomokiyo (the formula); Harumi-Matsuba (issue #48, the 20 May telegrams and the reply’s addressees); saberwatchmanga (the 1916 print of the first telegram).
What it says
Welcome and thanks from the Council; the Zhaoqing leaders’ plans to march on Jiangxi and Hunan; Huang Xing asks when Zhang Shizhao sails for Japan.
Still open
Five garbled groups of the first telegram filled from its print, four of the second inferred; 86 of the 229 groups of the 20 May stream read only after correcting a misspelling. All transcriptions were made by AI and not checked by a person.

01 The telegrams

In May 1916 Huang Xing was in Tokyo and the provinces that had risen against Yuan Shikai’s monarchy had set up a Military Affairs Council (軍務院) at Zhaoqing in Guangdong. Messages between them went through the Japanese: the consul at Zhaoqing, Ōta, telegraphed them to the Foreign Ministry, which passed them on, and the Ministry filed copies.

20 May. Ōta’s telegram no. 42 of 21 May (B03050731100) forwards, at Cen Chunxuan’s request, a cipher text that his cover note says holds two telegrams: one from Cen and the provincial leaders, one “from Li Genyuan and others to Huang Xing” about the Xiehe army entering Hunan and Jiangxi. The same romaji, 126 ten-letter words ending owari, is copied a second time in the series on Sun Yat-sen’s circle (B03050089100, frames 0076–0078), where the Ministry’s analysts worked on it in pencil on frames 0079–0081 and got about 83 characters of the second telegram.

25 May. Huang Xing’s answer went the other way. Foreign Minister Ishii’s telegram to Ōta of 25 May, 4.35 p.m. (B03050731500), asks him to pass on to Lin Hu and Li Genyuan a telegram from Huang Xing, “an acknowledgement of the telegram received the other day”. The cipher is on frame 0246, and the Ministry filed a cursive decode (frame 0247) and a clerk’s Japanese rendering (frame 0248) with it.

02 The three-kana rule

The plaintext is the standard Chinese telegraph code, in which every character has a four-digit number (一 = 0001, 行 = 5887). Each number c is written as three kana. Tomokiyo found the rule and published it as a spreadsheet formula on 23 September 2026:

kanaruletable
firstS1[c div 200]the 46 kana of the syllabary column by column: a ka sa ta na ha ma ya ra wa, i ki shi chi … wi, u …, e … we, o … ro
secondS2[(c − 1) mod 10]a i u e o ka ki ku ke ko
thirdS3[((c − 1) div 10) mod 20]ho he fu hi ha no ne nu ni na to te tsu chi ta so se su shi sa

So 隱, code 7148, is RE-KU-TA, and 行, 5887, is KE-KI-NI wherever it occurs. The rule reaches code 9199, and a telegraph clerk who did not know it saw only pronounceable Japanese syllables. This project had looked at the reply in September 2026 without the rule. It found three kana to the character and that the same character always gave the same three kana, but took the code for a private code book. It also posited one superfluous kana, which was in fact a CHI misread as O-HI.

03 Huang Xing’s reply

A decoder written here from the rule alone, with the repository’s own telegraph-code table, was run on the transcription of frame 0246 made here on 15 September, before the rule was known. With one change, Tomokiyo’s reading of O HI as CHI, all 46 groups decode:

隱印崧詔鬯行諸兄鑒：正寄函間，適接哿電，敬悉一切。護國軍能速入湘贑，甚好。行嚴何日東渡？望速啓行，先電示。興。徑

“To Yin, Yin, Song, Zhao, Chang and Xing, brothers: just as I was sending a letter, your telegram of the 20th arrived, and I have noted everything. It is very good that the National Protection Army can move quickly into Hunan and Jiangxi. What day does Xingyan cross east? I hope he sets out soon; wire me first. Xing, the 25th.” 哿 and 徑 are the rhyme characters for the 20th and the 25th, and 徑 matches the date of Ishii’s telegram.

The 1916 decode, corrected. Harumi-Matsuba pointed out (issue #48) that the cipher disagrees with the text this page gave in September, taken from the cursive decode on frame 0247. The decoder here confirms it from an independent transcription. The addressees are 詔 and 鬯, not 誥 or 蕘 and 邕; the second clause is 正寄函, not 正發書; and there is no 章 before 行嚴, which is an interlinear gloss on the decode and is not in the cipher. The text printed in 近代史資料 no. 50 agrees with the cipher on all of these, but lacks the 速 of 能速入湘, which the cipher and the Japanese copy both have. The six addressees are courtesy names: Lin Hu (隱青), Li Genyuan (印泉), Cheng Zikai (崧生), Wen Qun (詔雲), Yang Yongtai (暢卿, written 鬯) and Zhang Shizhao (行嚴). Harumi-Matsuba identified them from the 1916 staff lists of 軍務院考實; the footnote in 近代史資料 has four of them and other names for the fourth and fifth.

04 The telegrams of 20 May

Harumi-Matsuba transcribed the stream with Claude from the JACAR images, compared it with four blind transcriptions by OpenAI Codex (94.6% letter agreement for the run made after the corrections), and decoded it with the same rule into 229 groups (sankana-public, 20 October 2026). An earlier public audit of the same stream, saberwatchmanga’s sun-circle-cipher-1916 (10 October), found that the first telegram had been printed in 1916 and left the second unresolved.

Telegram 1, groups 0–107, the Council’s welcome. Printed in 1916 in 護國軍紀事 no. 5 as 軍政府歡迎黃克強電, so this is a check, not a new reading. The decoded text matches the print in 102 of 107 characters. The differences are 我, only in the print, and three places where the telegram reads 慮, 提挈公 and 查 against the print’s 憂, 教言 and 察:

黃克強先生鑒：奉删電，知公抵日，歡躍逾恆。堯等碌碌庸才，勉起討賊，正慮綆短，適逢公歸。願承不遺，俯賜提挈。公首倡共和，富有方略，各國情勢，考查復殷，將來內政外交，諸賴指導。大局所繫，惟公圖之。唐繼堯　岑春煊　梁啓超　劉顯世　陸榮廷　龍濟光　呂公望　蔡鍔　李烈鈞　陳炳焜　哿

Telegram 2, groups 108–228, from six at Zhaoqing. No print has been found, and the continuous reading is new:

公抵日，喜甚。潤輔當告公近形，以定進行方略。軍務院僅擁虛名，委員、代表等會皆未組織。協和軍旬內可齊，卽由韶入贑。非俟幹卿協和兵入湘贑，吾人恐難發言。馮段調停。此間暫置代理參謀事，源專任之。虎任第四師長，收集舊部約萬，亦卽出贑。楷調粤中湘軍卽出湘。釗擬來東，面達近情。虎、源、楷、羣、泰、釗。哿

“Glad you have reached Japan. Runfu will tell you how things stand here, so that we can settle our plans. The Military Affairs Council exists only in name: its committees and the delegates’ meeting are not yet organised. The Xiehe army will be assembled within ten days and enter Jiangxi by Shaozhou. Unless Ganqing and the Xiehe troops enter Hunan and Jiangxi, we can hardly make ourselves heard. Feng and Duan are mediating. For now the acting chief of staff’s duties here are given to Genyuan. Hu becomes commander of the 4th Division, gathers about ten thousand of his old troops, and also marches into Jiangxi. Kai will move the Hunan troops in Guangdong and march into Hunan. Zhao will come east to report in person.” The signatures are Lin Hu, Li Genyuan, Cheng Zikai, Wen Qun, Yang Yongtai and Zhang Shizhao, the same six that Huang Xing’s reply addresses. The consul’s cover note confirms that Li Genyuan was among the senders. Who “Runfu” is has not been settled, and Lin Hu’s title does not match later staff lists, which make him commander of the 6th National Protection Army.

05 How much was corrected

The rule itself is exact, but the romaji of the 20 May stream is not: 86 of its 229 groups decode only after a misspelling in the romaji is corrected. A dropped or misread kana shifts every later group, so the stream as written goes wrong early. The two copies agree in 114 of 126 words, so most of the errors were already in the transmission. The corrections are listed with their reasons and certainty in the contributor’s corrections.csv. How far they can be trusted differs between the telegrams. In the first, the print was in view when they were made, so their agreement with it proves little; the fair test is the 59 groups that needed no correction, of which 54 match the print and the other five are the wording differences above. In the second there is no outside check except five spellings that the second copy writes as corrected. Its 43 corrected groups are graded M and the four inferred ones I (underlined above where the reading is inferred or of medium certainty, as the contributor marks them).

06 What was checked here

  • A decoder was written from the rule alone (threekana.py), with this repository’s telegraph-code table rather than the contributor’s converter or table.
  • The reply, from this project’s own transcription of 15 September with Tomokiyo’s one correction: 46 of 46 groups, the contributor’s text exactly.
  • The contributor’s final table: all 219 coded groups decode to the contributor’s character, 219 of 219. This checks the rule and the code table. It does not check the transcription or the 86 corrections, which were not redone.
  • Telegram 1 against the print as transcribed by saberwatchmanga: 102 of 107, as claimed (刪 and 删 counted as one character).
  • The second copy: B03050731100 was fetched, and images 24–26 are Ōta’s telegram no. 42 with the same romaji.

Nobody has checked the transcriptions letter by letter: the reply’s was made by Claude here, and the stream’s by Claude for the contributor, compared only with other AI transcriptions.

# Military Affairs Council to Huang Xing, 20 May 1916: English

Translation of `t1-0520_reading.md` (23 Oct 2026).

## Translation

Mr Huang Keqiang: we have received your telegram of the 15th and learn that you have arrived in Japan, to our
exceeding joy. We, [Tang Jiyao] and the others, men of ordinary talent, have exerted ourselves to rise and punish the
traitor, and were just fearing that our rope was too short when you returned. We hope you will not cast us aside and
will deign to lend us your hand. You first called for the republic and are rich in plans; you have studied the
situation of the powers closely; in the future, domestic and foreign affairs will all depend on your guidance. The
whole situation hangs on it; we look to you alone to plan it. Tang Jiyao, Cen Chunxuan, Liang Qichao, Liu Xianshi,
Lu Rongting, Long Jiguang, Lü Gongwang, Cai E, Li Liejun, Chen Bingkun. The 20th.

Lin Hu, Li Genyuan and four others to Huang Xing, 20 May 1916 (groups 108-228)

The six at Zhaoqing report that the Military Affairs Council holds only a name, its committees not yet organised; the Xiehe army will soon enter Jiangxi from Shaozhou; Lin Hu takes the Fourth Division; and Zhang Shizhao plans to come east to report in person. # Lin Hu, Li Genyuan and four others to Huang Xing, 20 May 1916: English

Translation of `t2-0520_reading.md` (23 Oct 2026), same holes.

## Translation

[We are] very glad that you have reached Japan. Runfu will tell you of the recent situation, so that the plan of action
can be settled. The Military Affairs Council holds only an empty name; the committee, the delegates' meeting and the
rest are not yet organised. The Xiehe Army can be assembled within ten days and will then enter Jiangxi from
Shao[zhou]. Unless we wait until Ganqing's Xiehe troops enter Hunan and [?Jiangxi], we fear we can hardly speak out.
Feng and Duan are mediating. Here the business of acting chief of staff is provisionally arranged, [Li Gen]yuan taking
it alone. [Lin] Hu is made commander of the Fourth Division, gathers his old units, about ten thousand, and will also
go out into Jiangxi at once. [Cheng Zi]kai is moving the Hunan troops in Guangdong to [?go out] into Hunan at once.
[Zhang Shi]zhao plans to come east to report the recent situation in person. Hu, Yuan, [?Kai], Qun, [?Tai], Zhao.
The 20th.
