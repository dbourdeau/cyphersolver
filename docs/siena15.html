Innsbruck → the Signori of Siena · homophonic substitution with code words · 4 January 1547 · key partly recovered from the ciphertext

Siena no. 15: Mario Bandini at the imperial court, 1547

Go to the full text ↓

An eight-page letter from Siena’s orator to Charles V, with eleven short passages in cipher; George Lasry gave values to 75 of the 240 cipher signs by crib dragging, and the rest is still open. Decoded, not read: only a few clauses come through whole.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 18 October 2026 · updated 23 October 2026

Method: key recovered from ciphertext-only · Extent: none (key obtained, text not read) · On the map

Contributed by George Lasry (solution)

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · none (key obtained, text not read)

The letter
Mario Bandini, Sienese orator at the imperial court, to the Signori of Siena, Innsbruck (“Eltspruch”), 4 January 1546 Sienese style, that is 1547.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Siena, Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2, no. 15; DECODE R4803, catalogued “1500–1599” with no status.
The cipher
About 65 signs: a homophonic alphabet of Greek-like and invented signs, probable nulls, no word dividers, and Latin-letter code words (buf, abs, som, Za) each followed by a bar with three dots.
How it was tried
George Lasry transcribed the passages in CTTS and, with Claude Opus 5.5, guessed and dragged cribs inside the clear context.
What it says
Only fragments: the cipher speaks of an occasion to be used and of discord among the factions; a minister warns that a fire would be kindled in a coded place.
Still open
165 of the 240 cipher signs and the four code words, so the passages are decoded, not read. Checked again on 23 October 2026: all eight DECODE images of R4803 (the eighth is a clear postscript) and the neighbouring records R4802 and R4804 carry no decipherment, and no printed text was found. About 30 sign classes have no value in 240 signs, too few for a rebuild from the ciphertext alone; Lasry’s own CTTS transcription is needed for the next step.
Credit
George Lasry, with CTTS and Claude Opus 5.5: transcription and partial decipherment, sent by email to Daniel Bourdeau on 17 October 2026. This page reports his result.

The two other sixteenth-century Sienese pieces of George Lasry’s report, no. 17 (Tantucci, 1528) and R4794 (the unnumbered artillery slip), are different ciphers with their own pages; their key figures were compared sign by sign with this one and every shared shape has another value.

01The letter and its key

240 cipher signs and three Latin-letter code tokens, in eight lines of the second page and six of the sixth, set as eleven short passages in the clear Italian. Bandini protests that he did not come to Germany to quarrel with Piergiovanni Salvestri, dismisses the “calumnies” that Siena is displeased at the Emperor’s victory, and reports that a minister (Granvelle?) refused the thousand he brought and asked for Sienese vases “all’antica”.

02The partial decipherment

George Lasry’s reading, unchanged (code words in brackets; ? = sign not read):

P2, lines 15–22. prudentia loro — in ?e? se di ?a?i?i ?unc d? [som] n? i? di?a senza de? s? io ?u?in? esse presenti ??d? hanno da servirsi [dell’] occasione e procurar se li potesse intervenir come ?h padri ?e? [buf] in?io?i?? di lo us? che piu ?a discordia tra loro [buf] ? Altro la ? [abs] redusse ale mani d?nds?ri anchiora V. SS.e Ill.me so’ prudenti et dunq[ue] anco da guardar [buf] [?] di ?ci?r? i?ae?u adosso se[Za] su? e?i? — De le calunnie che V. SS.e Ill.me mi significa
P6, lines 1–6. reservandosi tutto a concludere con S. M.ta Ces.a / [abs] sc?it ?uno mi disse n?cn ?i ?rh? perche si entrasse in ???ione che si accenderebbe ?? fuoco in [abs] ?t?s c?n? soggiunse: essent principes qui vellent persolvere centum milia coronatorum ut Senenses viderent hunc errorem ut haberent occasionem turbandi ??t o? pe? iculo? queste

His translation of P6: “reserving everything to be concluded with His Imperial Majesty. [abs] … he told me …, so that one should enter into … [confus]ion(?), which would kindle … fire in [abs] …; he added: there would be princes who would be willing to pay out a hundred thousand crowns, so that the Sienese might see this error, so as to have an occasion of disturbing … peril(?).”

The date: the Emperor’s recent “victory and glory” is the Schmalkaldic campaign of autumn 1546, which fixes 4 January 1546 in the Sienese year (beginning 25 March) as 1547. Siena was then a republic under imperial protection, split between its Monti; in 1547 Charles V put it under Don Diego Hurtado de Mendoza.

03What remains

  • 165 of the 240 cipher signs; about 30 signs have no value, and several transcription classes are probably variants of one sign.
  • The code words buf (three times), abs (three times), som and Za, and the persons or places behind them; “His Excellency” who spoke in Latin (Granvelle?).
  • The filed key “Cifra con m. Bernardino Buoninsegni oratore a S. M.tà” (DECODE R4764), compared with this letter in the project’s survey of 24 September 2026, gave no conclusive match.

Mario Bandini, Sienese orator at the imperial court, to the Signori of Siena, Innsbruck, 4 January 1547, after the Schmalkaldic campaign: Piergiovanni Salvestri at Innsbruck with the Florentine orator, an occasion to be used and discord in cipher, a minister (Granvelle?) warning that a fire would be kindled in a coded place, and Sienese vases 'all'antica' for Burgundy via Figueroa. A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.

CRIB CANDIDATES (clear words touching the runs)
  - L2.15 run follows "mi rimetto alla prudentia loro ———" and is followed on L2.17 by "hanno da servirsi":
    the run probably names who "hanno da servirsi" (subject plural).
  - L2.17-18 "e procurar(e) se li potesse intervenir come P R q padri": the 3 signs before "padri" are
    probably one word (e.g. "santi", "loro", "buoni"): a very short crib.
  - "abs" stands three times immediately before cipher (L2.19 "la o ST abs reducesse", L6.1 "C(esare)a abs DIV",
    L6.3 "fuoco in abs DIV") and "bus/buss/bos" twice (L2.18, L2.21 "da guardar bus DIV"): probably clear
    abbreviations or code words; worth testing as names (e.g. "abs" = a person/state).
  - L6.3 "accenderebbe STAR # RHO fuoco": three signs between "accenderebbe" and "fuoco" (an adjective or
    article-like word, e.g. "gran", "il"); STAR and # are nulls in the candidate key R4764, leaving one sign.
  - L6.4-6.5: the Emperor's words are quoted in Latin: "essent principes qui vellent persolvere centum milia

    clause such as "qui ... inciderent"?); L6.4 "LAM P+ U D" before "soggiunse" ends the Italian run of L6.3.

Contents
From: Mario Bandini, Sienese orator at the imperial court
To: the Signori of Siena ('V. SS.e Ill.me')
Place: Innsbruck ('Eltspruch')
Date: 4 January 1546 Sienese style = 1547 — dated in the clear text; the Emperor's recent 'victory and glory' 
in Germany is the Schmalkaldic campaign of autumn 1546
Bandini, elected orator to the imperial court by the Signori and confirmed by the Senate, protests that he did
not come to Germany to quarrel with Piergiovanni Salvestri, who has arrived at Innsbruck and is seen
frequenting the Florentine orator and his secretary; it would be beneath the dignity of the republic to argue
with private persons. The Signori have suspended the settlement of the affairs of the order of the Nove until
the Emperor's full resolution; Bandini has always wished every family and every order to hold its due place in
the city, the sooner the better, but leaves it to their prudence. Here the cipher begins: it speaks of an
occasion that must be used and of discord among them (the factions), of someone who brought matters


[report page 30]

back into the hands of a coded party, and of what the Signori must also guard against; the code words buf,
abs, som and Za stand for the persons or places concerned. In clear again he dismisses the 'calumnies' that
Siena is displeased at the Emperor's victory and glory, citing the city's obedience, down to sending the
relegated exiles wherever His Majesty ordered. On P6 a minister ('S. Ex.tia', apparently Granvelle), reserving
everything for the Emperor's decision, told him in words put into cipher that if Siena entered into
[confusion?] a fire would be kindled in a coded place, and added in Latin that there were princes ready to
pay a hundred thousand crowns so that the Sienese should see their error and give them an occasion to stir
up trouble. The same man refused outright the thousand (ducats?) Bandini brought him, saying it would be
talked of ten years hence and that he already knew of it from elsewhere; Bandini will try again and awaits
orders. The minister also asked for Sienese vases 'all'antica', to be sent through the ambassador Figueroa at
Genoa to Lyon and on to Burgundy, and Bandini promised a hundred fine pieces. The bearer is Mattio delle
Poste; Alessandro Pieri(?), who has been unwell, will follow shortly.
Historical context and dating
Siena was a republic under imperial protection, split between its Monti (Nove, Popolo, Riformatori,
Gentiluomini). Charles V's chief minister Nicolas Perrenot de Granvelle, a Burgundian, had imposed a
settlement among them in 1541; new disorders in 1545–46 brought the exile of faction leaders (the 'relegati'
of the clear text) and, in 1547, the Emperor's decision to put Siena under Don Diego Hurtado de Mendoza
with a Spanish garrison, the prelude to the revolt of 1552 [Hook, Siena: A City and its History, 1979;
Cantagalli, La guerra di Siena, 1962]. Bandini wrote from Innsbruck, on the Brenner road to the Emperor's
winter quarters in Swabia, just after Charles V had dispersed the Schmalkaldic League's army (November
1546) [Brandi, The Emperor Charles V, 1939]. The 'victory and glory of His Majesty' in the letter is that
campaign, which fixes the Sienese date 1546 (year beginning 25 March) as 1547.
Open points. The code words buf (three times), abs (three times), som and Za and the persons or places
behind them, 'His Excellency' who spoke in Latin (Granvelle?), the party that 'brought matters back into the
hands' of someone, and Piergiovanni Salvestri's backers are unidentified.
