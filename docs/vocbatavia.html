The VOC number cipher of Batavia, 1623–1643

Go to the full text ↓

Four letters from the Governor-General and Council at Batavia to the Company’s directors hide cargoes, ships and plans in one number cipher; the key was rebuilt from glosses of 1634 and tested on two later letters.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 27 November 2026

At a glanceKey from adjacent plaintext · partial (73 of 136 cipher words, three letters)

The letters
Four letters of the Governor-General and Council at Batavia to the Heren XVII: 25 December 1623, 20 February 1634, a postscript of 9 September 1640 and 23 January 1643. Most of each letter is clear Dutch; a few passages and words are in cipher.
Where
Nationaal Archief, The Hague, VOC 1.04.02, inv. 1079–1080, 1108 and 1111, 1134 and 1141. Not on DECODE (its only VOC record, 1564, is a key of 1674 in another system).
The cipher
System A: numbers from 1 to about 74 for letters, two to four numbers for a common letter. System B, in 1640 and 1643: letters and numbers each standing for a plain letter, for names, places, goods and amounts, with Roman numerals as code words.
How the key was found
The 1634 letter has its cipher words glossed in the margin; they gave 47 number values. With 13 more set from the 1623 context, the key (KEY_v2) was frozen and applied without change to the 1640 postscript and the 1643 letter.
What it says
Cargoes of pepper, mace and nutmeg (1623); the silver of Acapulco, Japan and cloths (1634); how the duplicate travels by an English ship (1640); trade hopes in Japan and a voyage to seek the north coast of Tartary and the gold and silver islands east of Japan (1643).
Still open
Nearly all system-B words: the B key comes only from contemporary glosses and has no held-out test. 1640 postscript 9 of 27 cipher words read, 1643 letter 11 of 55.
Credit
Found, transcribed and worked by Malcolm McBratney with AI agents (Claude), pull request #996. The key rests on the contemporary glosses. The checks were done with AI tools and no person checked each record by hand.

01 The letters

The four letters are in the VOC archive (1.04.02) at the Nationaal Archief, most of them in more than one copy. Each is from the Governor-General and Council at Batavia to the Heren XVII:

  • 25 December 1623, Governor-General De Carpentier and Council, with a resolution of 2 October 1623: inv. 1080 scans 376, 380 and 486 (the copy used), and inv. 1079 scans 129, 133 and 263, a copy with the cipher explained in the margin. Three cipher blocks, A, B and C: 54 cipher words in 276 numbers.
  • 20 February 1634, Governor-General Brouwer and Council: inv. 1108 scans 1154–1155 and inv. 1111 scan 564. Fourteen glossed passages: 35 cipher words in 217 numbers.
  • 9 September 1640, postscript, Governor-General Antonio van Diemen and Council: inv. 1134 scan 583 (no gloss, the copy used) and scan 617 (a copy with a decipherment between the lines). Coolhaas’s summary says the letter went by the English ship Caesar.
  • 23 January 1643, Van Diemen and Council, sent by the English ship Ulysses: inv. 1141 scans 897–908 (copy 2, no gloss, the copy used; cipher on 901, 902, 905 and 906) and scans 881–893 (copy 1, with a decipherment between the lines on 886 and 890).

A body of the 1640 letter (scans 570 and 605) uses system B and was not scored. A letter of 7 March 1643 (inv. 1141 near scan 876) was found only as a glossed copy and was not transcribed.

02 Two systems

System A is a homophonic substitution on numbers from 1 to about 74, set among clear Dutch words. A common letter has two to four numbers (a = 9, 10, 18; e = 28, 70, 71; n = 2, 48, 50 in KEY_v2). In the copies of 1623 to 1640 a stroke divides the words; copy 2 of 1643 writes a stroke between every number, so its word division is lost. The same table is used in all four letters.

System B appears in 1640 and 1643. Letters and numbers both stand for plain letters (r = a, d = n, m = t, b = e; in B, 2 = t, 12 = o, 16 = n, 24 = e), and a number in B does not have its A value. It is used for names, places, goods and amounts: mrkmrkcbd = Tartarien, crfrd = Japan, 2.12.16.16.24.16 = tonnen. The Roman numerals XXI and IV are code words for silver and gold. The B values were built from the contemporary glosses only (key/KEY_B_v1.tsv and key/LC_KEY_v1.tsv).

How a reader of the time told an A run from a B run is not known.

03 The decipherment

1623, block A. “Four days after the departure of the English ships [we] intend to send the yacht de Hase to the Netherlands with full advices, in case the others could not follow so soon.”

1623, block B. “In the Wapen and the Leyden are loaded twenty-eight thousand sacks of pepper, eight hundred sockels of mace, two hundred and eighty thousand pounds of nutmegs.” Block C concerns strengthening the fleet at the destined places around Manila.

1634. The glossed words: the entrance (enbocadero) of Espiritu Santo for the silver of Acapulco; Japan and the yacht Venlo; English damasks, Turkish camlets, rassets, perpetuanas and other cloths; tael.

1640 postscript. “[Het] [dubbel] [deser], neven verscheijde [copien] van [?rieuen], [gaet] in [handen] [vanden] [schipper] des schips den Caesar {…} [o?] [door] Cornelis Sybertsen pastijbacker op den nieuwen dijck tot Amsterdam {…}”: the duplicate of this letter, with copies of letters, goes into the hands of the skipper of the Caesar, to be delivered through Cornelis Sybertsen, pastry-baker on the Nieuwendijk at Amsterdam. The sixteen B words around it are not read.

1643, scan 905. “Tot ontdeckinge vande Noordt Custe {Tartarien} ende van daer … de XXI ende IV rijcke […] bij {oosten} {Japan} … de cours is van hier bij noorden Celebes nae Ternate ende van daer bij oosten [Japan], om de [noort] custe van {Tartarien} aen te soecken.” To discover the north coast of Tartary, and from there to look again for the rich silver and gold [islands] east of Japan, the flute Castricum and the yacht Breskens are planned, under the skipper Maerten Gerritsz Vries. The words in { } and the numerals are known here only from the gloss of copy 1.

1643, scans 901–902. The advices from Japan give great hope of good trade, with the prospect that the Chinese and the other nations, except the Company, will be refused that kingdom; amounts given in tonnen. The system-A words read are uyt, geven, negotie, rycx, ontseyt, ses, winst, tonnen, voor; the rest comes from the gloss.

04 Method and tests

The 1634 letter has every cipher word explained in the margin (inv. 1108). Those glosses gave 47 number values. The frozen 1634 key decoded the 1623 letter into Dutch, but its first pass, 32 of 54 cipher words found in a word list (59.26%), is under the project’s own 65% line. Thirteen values were then set from the 1623 context; the 1623 margins in inv. 1079, checked after decoding, agree with the decode word for word except for one word (conden, see below). With those, KEY_v2 has 60 numbers. It was frozen on 25 November 2026 at 20:10 UTC and not changed afterwards.

KEY_v2 was then applied to two letters not used to build it:

  • 1640 postscript (scan 583): 9 of 11 system-A words (81.82%). Key shuffles: 0 of 5,000 at seeds 20261112 and 7 reach the real score.
  • 1643 letter (copy 2): 59 of 86 number groups covered by dictionary words (68.60%), under a word-division rule frozen before the decode. Key shuffles 0 of 5,000 at both seeds; ciphertext shuffles under the real key 0 of 5,000 at both seeds (rank 1 of 5,001). The glossed copy 1, opened after the score, agrees for 10 of 12 checkable runs.

A letter key for system B built from the 1643 glosses (LC_KEY_v1) failed its test on the 1640 letter words: 5 of 13 (38.46%). On 27 November 2026 every B word (47 in all) was decoded again with the gloss-built KEY_B_v1: 32 take a value for every sign, but the 1643 ones only give back the glosses the key was built from, and no new value is supported by two or more words. No B word moved from hole to read.

The repository’s statistical test (docs/_validate.py, Dutch letter model, the 1623 letter, 276 numbers) gives a pass: the reading scores −2.914 per letter against −6.457 for random keys and −3.588 for keys refitted to shuffled text.

Blind second readings, by AI readers who saw only the images: the 1640 postscript 56 of 57 numbers agree, the 1643 letter 85 of 86, and the 1623 blocks A and B 121 of 124 and 127 of 127 (non-Anthropic models, run by Malcolm McBratney). The transcriber had KEY_v2 in view while transcribing 1640 and 1643; this is disclosed in the method files.

05 What remains open

The count is of cipher words only, sure words only (scripts/house_count.py): a word counts as deciphered when the key gives every letter and nothing is supplied or emended. Words that rest on a gloss alone are holes.

  • 1623: 53 of 54 cipher words. The one hole is kolden, read konden (26 = l taken as a slip; the margin has conden).
  • 1634: 35 of 35, but this is the key source: every word is glossed, so it is not a test.
  • 1640 postscript: 9 of 27. ?rieuen and the blotted group rest on the scan 617 gloss; all 16 B words are open (13 in the transcription and three more, k bd. p., after “Amsterdam” that the image shows).
  • 1643: 11 of 55. Every B word, the numerals XXI and IV, d’advisen (74 has no key value; copy 1 writes 70) and several partly keyed number runs are holes.
  • Over the three letters, 73 of 136 cipher words (1634 left out). The extent is partial.
  • Doubtful signs in 1643 copy 2: 61 in 901.4 (could be 01); 10 in 902.4 (copy 1 and the blind reader have 16; tonnen under B supports 16); 74 in 901.1 (copy 1 has 70); 39 and 5 in 905.3 (Leupe’s printed text, by OCR, has 89 and 6; his page image was not checked). In 1640: the blotted group (the gloss gives 57) and 12 (gloss b).
  • No person has yet compared the transcriptions with the images or applied the key by hand. The reading review is left to the maintainer.

06 Earlier work

The letters were deciphered in their own time: the 1623 copy in inv. 1079 has the explanation in the margin, and the 1634, 1640 and 1643 copies carry glosses. Coolhaas’s edition of the Generale Missiven (vol. I, 1960, p. 129) notes for 1623 that the explanation was written in the margin “in patria”, but prints no deciphered text. Cheng (1995, vol. II p. 27), cited for this letter by Andrade, was not seen: it may print the passage. Leupe (1858), in his edition of the Vries voyage, prints the scan-905 passage of 1643 and the meaning of its cipher words (Tartarien, the gold and silver islands, Japan, noort); this was found after our reading and was not used. So the content of 1623 and of the 1643 passage is not new. As far as checked, the number table itself is not in print.

# Track B result: VOC cipher of 1623 and 1634 (26 Nov 2026 AEST)

## Bottom line
- The 1634 glosses give a homophonic letter key: 47 numbers valued [measured: frozen/K1634_key.tsv].
- That frozen key decodes the 1623 letter into plain Dutch: same cipher system across 1623-1634.
- Strict first-pass score 32/54 tokens = 59.3%: BELOW the 65% bar. Shuffles 0/5,000 at both seeds.
- The 1623 content was already deciphered in 1623-24 (margins of inv. 1079) and Coolhaas (GM I, 1960) says so. NOT FIRST to read it.
- No PR package prepared: the figure of record misses the bar, and the reading exists. Nothing sent or filed.

- Homophonic letter cipher, numbers 1 to about 72, 2 to 4 numbers per common letter (a: 9, 10, 18; e: 28, 70, 71; n: 2, 48, 50;
  t: 22, 32, 56, 58; h: 8, 27, 37; f: 1, 19, 62; w: 24, 54, 60 ...). i/j share signs; u/v/w share 31 and 44. Words split by '/'.
  No code words (whole-word signs) seen in the 89 cipher words of both letters (35 + 54 = 89).

## Bottom line
- Identified: Van Diemen and Council to the Heren XVII, Batavia 23 Jan 1643 (signed 1141_0908), sent by the English ship Ulysses
  (GM II p. 194 heading: KA 1050 unfoliated after fol. 383, copy fol. 384-389) [sourced].

- Key shuffles 0/5,000 at both seeds; matched control (ciphertext shuffled) 0/5,000 at both seeds, rank 1 of 5,001. PASS, modest margin.
- The glossed copy 1 (0886, 0890), opened after the score, agrees with the decode for 10 of 12 checkable runs.
- New: the letter also uses a second, letter-for-letter cipher (Tartarien = mrkmrkcbd, Japan = crfrd ...) and Roman numerals as code words
  (XXI = silver, IV = gold). This is the system of the 1640 'LC' words.
