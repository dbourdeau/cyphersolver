SOA Plzeň, pracoviště Klášter u Nepomuka, Rodinný archiv Windischgrätzů, inv. nr. 1433, karton 202 (DECODE R5029, no decipherment on record). A German letter in clear with thirteen enciphered passages. The key Jakub Mírka reconstructed in 2023 from the brothers’ other letters, attached to the record, was applied unchanged: 155 of the 181 cipher groups (85.6%) read as sense. With the clear text transcribed in full (23 October 2026), the letter builds coherent sentences and reads at 94.7% of its words, under the 95% a complete reading needs. No earlier decipherment of this letter was found.

The letter
Ernst Friedrich von Windischgrätz to his brother Leopold Viktorin, Brussels, 18 November 1721 (DECODE lists the direction the other way round; the clear text settles the writer): eight pages in German and French, with secrets written as dotted numbers.
Where
Windischgrätz family archive, Plzeň (inv. 1433, karton 202); DECODE R5029, listed with no decipherment and with the wrong Emperor’s keys R5017 and R5018.
The cipher
Each letter has two numbers (A = 24 or 36, B = 12 or 48 ...), and numbers above 48 form a small nomenclator.
How it was read
The brothers’ own key is a spreadsheet on the record itself, by Jakub Mírka (19 June 2023). Applied here, it decrypts every letter-spelled passage to German at once; no cryptanalysis was needed.
What it says
The Emperor himself advised against the writer making an excuse; the passages concern the succession and service (niemahlen anzunehmen).

155 of 181 cipher groups read as sense (85.6%). 26 groups, 19 distinct nomenclator codes, are fixed by neither the key nor the letter.

01 The document and the key

Five DECODE images: the first page (“Brüssel den 18. 9bris 721”, “Hochgebohrner Graf”), two openings, and the last page (seen from both sides). The text is ordinary German and French in clear; the secrets are written as dotted numbers in the line.

Mírka’s key gives each letter two numbers: A = 24 or 36, B = 12 or 48, C = 23 or 35, E = 22 or 34, and so on to Z = 1 or 37; the first column runs 24 to 1 down a fixed order of the alphabet, the second is the first plus 12 or 36. Numbers above 48 are a small nomenclator, of which he identifies 54 Althann, 85 der/dem, 86 die, 135 hat, 145 ich, 152 Kayser, 197 Plan, 198 Prinz, with guesses for 99 [Engländer?], 103 [Emp…?], 151 [K…?], 167 [Micosch?] and others.

The writer, waiting for the Congress of Cambrai to open, complains that he gets no support; the Emperor himself advised against making an excuse; a patron [205] works under hand, perhaps with the Prince (Eugene); love for [103] is being lost daily, even by the Emperor, after the courier finally brought the Prince’s plan; the English [?] want to go home and “I shall succumb, the Prince triumph”. In clear, the Madrid courier has brought Pozobueno orders on the exchange of renunciations. The matter (Cambrai, Pozobueno, Beretti Landi) is Ernst Friedrich, the imperial plenipotentiary at the Congress: the clear text settles the writer, and the letter is to Leopold Viktorin (DECODE lists the reverse).

03 What remains uncertain

  • Re-measured 5 October 2026. The four sibling letters on DECODE (R5025–R5028) were fetched and searched for glosses: 78 = Compagnie (“der Ostend. Compagnie dem Kayser”), 103 = Prince Eugen, 99 = Engländer, 167 = Count Miosch. A full-resolution re-read corrected one group (gethan). measure.py counts sense: 155 of 181 groups (85.6%). The nomenclator is alphabetical, and bracketing proposes values for 13 of the 19 open codes (182 nicht, 131 haben, 172 mit …), which would give 96.1%; they are proposals, not counted.
  • Open codes (19 distinct codes, 26 groups): 101, 102, 111, 130, 131 (twice), 139, 146, 149, 151, 164, 168, 172 (twice), 181, 182 (three times), 204, 205 (three times), 206 (twice), 209, 225. Mírka’s guesses 99, 103 and 167, and the code 78, are glossed in the sibling letters (above). 205 is a person; 182 and 131 recur in the succession passage.
  • Slips (grade C): “1.39.4” has a flourish that could be taken as 24 (ZUAEHE); decrypted as ZUSEHE. 45 (H) stands where A is wanted in GETHHN, and NIEHEMAHLEN has an extra H.
  • All letter-spelled words are grade H: the key gives them without choice.
  • Reading audit, 22 October 2026. The first page and the left half of the second opening build sentences; on the later pages open codes, guesses and clear text given only in extract leave holes in most clauses. The letter counted as decoded, not read until the clear text was transcribed in full and a running German text written. Both were done on 23 October 2026 (R5029_transcription.txt, R5029_reading.md, English R5029_en.md): the clear text transcribed from the five images at full resolution, and the cipher set in from Mírka’s key and four codes glossed in the sibling letters. The reading builds coherent sentences but reads at 94.7% of its words (measured 24 October 2026), with the open codes as holes: just under the 95% a complete reading needs.

R5029, Brussels, 18 Nov 1721 read in part

Ernst Friedrich von Windischgrätz, imperial plenipotentiary for the Congress of Cambrai, writes from Brussels to his brother at court in German with thirteen enciphered passages, deciphered with Jakub Mírka's key from the DECODE record: Prince Eugene is not well disposed to him and the Emperor advised against an excuse; everyone, even the Emperor, loses love for Eugene over the Ostend Company, the Prince's plan having lain unopened for two months; the English want to go home; the Spanish courier brought Pozobueno orders on the renunciations, so the Congress should open; he refuses a succession on a point of honour and asks to learn the Emperor's disposition towards him and his expenses.

Builds coherent sentences, but its word share is at or above the 95% read bar, and it stays read in part for another reason stated on this page (95% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

Gott gebe, dass das Miosch versprechen einen guten effect haben, sonsten will der Wertheimber nicht mehr zuhalten.

Das dieselben mir nun selbst einrathen an eine [?Breunsche] Succession [?nicht] zu gedencken, ist mir umb so lieber, als ich mir ein principe d'honneur gemacht solches nebst villen anderen ursachen hauptsächlich derumb niehemahlen anzunehmen, damit [?nicht] seine, ich hat der Prinz [206] umb dessen dienste zu [?haben], welches schändlich wäre, und was beschehen, nur zu [?ihro] Kayser gethan [?haben].

Ich bitte aber Euer Excellenz seyen so gnädig mir die wahre gemüths neigung Ihrer Kayserlichen Majestät gegen meine wenigkeit zu berichten, damit ich getrost wissen möge, was zu hoffen, wie mich von meinen grossen depensen zu erhollen haben und wider die mir gemachte so ansehentliche feindschafften soutenirt sehen därffte.

Dieses wirdt mich animiren umb desto eyfriger denen schweren geschäfften des Congress zu unterziehen.

Spes sustinet, animat, et auget.

Ich verharre in aller consideration h. C.

# R5029: Brussels, 18 November 1721, English translation

Translation of `R5029_reading.md` (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026). The holes are the same: `[?word]` rests on the sense
and the alphabetical order of the code list only, `[205]` is a code with no value, `[word]` is supplied.

## Translation

Brussels, 18 November 1721.

High-born Count.

From Your Excellency's letter of 1 November, safely received, for which I most humbly give thanks, I gather among other things that Eugene is not at all [?well-disposed] towards me, because the Emperor himself advised [?him] against making an excuse.

If I am supported from there even a little, the advice is very good, but otherwise not; [205] is probably also holding [?with] the Prince under hand.

For he writes to me in that way, quite absurdly, when he was told that we had visited each other, and I [?answer] him, as the enclosed copy shows, which I send only in true, most dutiful brotherly confidence; but since [205] will surely mention it to no one, I most humbly ask you likewise to say nothing of it to anyone, not even to Althann, unless it should come to my justification.

In this way I make enemies through no fault of mine, but blessed are they who suffer for justice's sake.

I believe that with [205] it is "one hand washes the other"; of this and the rest there would be strange things to say, but I will not play the prosecutor.

On the matter of the [Ostend] Company it is almost unbelievable how not only the [151] but everyone else loses the love for Eugene, even the Emperor, [?more] every day, seeing that after the courier at last, after so long, arrived [?with] the Prince's plan in [?Vienna], Eugene has [?not] let it [?be opened] for over two months now, so that the time passes and the whole business gets stuck for these reasons, while the Emperor looks on quite calmly.

In this way I could hardly keep the people patient any longer.

The English and also the [?French] want to go home, and might then [?spread] little to the credit of our [?court] and [204] everywhere.

[102] please, once more, for love of the fatherland and the public good, to make the last attempt.

If nothing comes of it, I can still less sustain the business: thy destruction is of thyself, O Israel.

I shall succumb, the Prince triumph, but the Emperor will [168] lose the Company and the [181] [206] were, as the outcome will soon show.

My services, however, which Your Excellency could in any case lay before the highest place as your enlightened judgement sees fit, will always remain true marks of my most dutiful loyalty and zeal; if they are not recognised, virtue is still to be praised for its own sake.

The Congress should now open soon, because the [?Spanish] courier come back from Madrid has brought Pozobueno in London the order, at the exchange of the respective renunciations concerning the titles, to set up exactly such a reserving article as the Count of Windischgrätz (those are the very words) made with the Marquis Beretti Landi at The Hague at the time of the [?Spanish] accession; which has much consoled me, and I now see nothing more that could hinder the opening of the Congress.

God grant that Miosch's promise has a good effect; otherwise Wertheimer will not hold on any longer.

That you yourself now advise me [?not] to think of a [?Breuner] succession is all the more welcome to me, as I have made it a point of honour never to accept it, chiefly, among many other reasons, so that it should [?not] be said: the Prince has [206] me in order to [?have] his services, which would be shameful, and what was done, done only for [?Her] Emperor [?have].

But I beg Your Excellency to be so gracious as to tell me His Imperial Majesty's true disposition towards my humble self, so that I may know with confidence what to hope for, how I am to recover from my great expenses, and whether I might see myself supported against the very considerable enmities raised against me.

This will spur me to undertake the heavy business of the Congress all the more zealously.

Hope sustains, encourages and increases.

I remain with all consideration, h. C.
