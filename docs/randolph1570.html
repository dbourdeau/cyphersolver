Randolph to Sussex, 5 July 1570: the cipher and its decipherment on the next leaf

BL, Cotton MS Caligula C II f. 277 (DECODE R4931; catalogue item 99). DECODE dates it 1569 and gives no reading. It is Thomas Randolph's letter to the Earl of Sussex, "At Edenborowe the 5te of Julye 1570".

DECODE offered R4930 as the key record, but that record is Burghley's notes on the Norfolk letters. The decipherment is f. 278, filed as its own "decrypted" record, R4932.

Summary. Catalogue item 99 was a DECODE record of a Randolph letter to Sussex, "non-decrypted", dated 1569, with "what the cipher hides not known". The letter is dated at Edinburgh, 5 July 1570. It is clear English with five cipher runs, the longest about seventy words. Boyd's Calendar of Scottish Papers iii (1903), no. 339, abstracts Sussex's clear copy of it. Using that abstract as a crib, a language-model anneal read about 70% of the cipher words before any decipherment was found. It recovered "murtherers and traitors", "determined to have made a regent" and "whome nether by lawe, duetie or conscien they thinke them selves bounde unto". The next leaf in the volume, f. 278 (DECODE R4932), then turned out to be a contemporary decipherment: a clerk re-copied each cipher word and wrote the plaintext above it. That leaf confirms every value the anneal had found, and it closes the remaining gaps. The letter says the Scottish Queen's party "have litle confidens [in] the Q. of England, that so ofte changeth her course". The King's lords, who had "determined to have made a regent, dyd staye it" on hope of better comfort. They now fear being forced to join "murderers and traytors, bothe of kyng and regent". Randolph adds that he has spoken with Morton, that the soldiers' pay should be continued, and that Grange does not look for the Scottish Queen's "home cumming". The only doubt left is in three faint words of the clerk's first gloss line.

01 The documents

f. 277r–v is the letter in Randolph's hand, signed and dated at the foot of the verso. f. 278 is a half-leaf docketed "5 July 1570". On it the cipher words are copied out with strokes between them and the plaintext written above, and the three short runs are deciphered in clear at the foot. DECODE has the two leaves as separate records, R4931 ("Non-decrypted") and R4932 ("Decrypted"). The record it links as the key, R4930, reuses the images of f. 277 alongside Caligula C II ff. 76–77. Those two leaves are Burghley's notes on the Norfolk and Bishop of Ross letters, and no cipher key is there.

02 The cipher

This is a word-divided substitution. Its signs are Latin letters, figures and invented marks, with two or three signs for the common vowels, and some signs stand for persons or phrases. The signs are ⊟ = "the S. Q. partie in Scotland", θθ = "the S. Queene", ΠΠ = "the Q. of England", and a struck +++ for the Earl of Morton. The values the glosses confirm include: = c, c u/v, D r, q s, ɛ n, δ t, ϙ o, Ӿ o, 4 d, # i, 8 e, ʒ a, 3 f, Π g, ⍏ n, U h, Ω t, ϖ m, λ m, ≡ i, Γ h. Tomokiyo's pages do not list this key. His Randolph ciphers are the 1559–60 Sadler–Croft ones.

03 The reading

The clear text is in roman type and the deciphered text in italics. The deciphered wording is the f. 278 clerk's, checked word by word against the cipher on f. 277.

f. 277r, ll. 1–10. I have receaved your L. lre of the seconde of this present, and what maye answer some parte thereof your L. hathe I trust seen by myne wrytten from hens the same daye, wch in effecte as this. The S. Q. partie in Scotland wyl reke ther owne […] the S. queene handes […] fortefy, have litle confidens [in] the Q. of England, that so ofte changeth her course; and now of late of all, whereas they were determined to have made a regent, dyd staye it full sore agaynst their willes, upon hope of better comfort, and shall be forced to yeld [to] ennemies, and [be] conforted [with] murderers and traytors, bothe of kyng and regent, and to obbey her whome nether by lawe, duetie or conscien they thinke them selves bounde unto. This your L. shall reyde not spoken of my selfe, but this is a great parte of their greif, and all most everie mans speche, and preached in pulpet in playner wordes then I lyste to wryte.

f. 277r, ll. 16–18. …to be sorte wth your L. it thinke utterlye undon, what so ever be come of us. I have spoken with therle of Morton, who is of that mynde that before I have wrytten, and in verie playne speche lamentethe the state of his countrie…

f. 277r, l. 27. …the mony, whiche I knowe will marveylouslye discomforte him, as it dothe also some other…

f. 277r, l. 29. …that the soldiars may be continewed in pay untill the Convention be ended.

f. 277v, l. 14. I knowe he loketh not [for] the S. Q. home cumming.

04 Method

The two images of R4931 are byte-identical to two of R4930's, so the "key" record was checked first and found to hold no key. Boyd's abstract, made from Sussex's clear copy, was lined up with the cipher runs. The runs were transcribed into sign tokens and annealed against an English n-gram model (en-1640s) with word divisions kept. With no crib pinned, the anneal already produced "murtherers and traitors", "regent", "whome … nor conscience they thinke them selves … unto". Pinning the crib words fixed about 70% of the text. The rest would not converge, because several signs that look alike on the page (plain, curly and barred x; 3 against a tailed ʒ) carry different values. A sweep of the DECODE records from the same volume then found R4932, the decipherment, one leaf on. Every value recovered earlier agrees with it.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Three words of the clerk's first gloss line are faint or odd: "wyl reke ther owne", "handes", "fortefy" (C). The cipher under them is not otherwise confirmed, and the sense of that opening phrase stays doubtful in the original decipherment too.
  • The bracketed [in], [to], [be], [with], [for] are words the clerk left out of the gloss (M).
  • Everything else is read (H).
