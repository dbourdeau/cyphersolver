Prospero Santa Croce to Cardinal del Monte, 1553: five ciphered despatches, deciphered on arrival and printed in 1972

Go to the transcriptions ↓

ASV Segreteria di Stato, Nunziatura di Francia 3, ff. 117, 128, 230, 248, 263 (DECODE R6–R10; catalogue item 245). DECODE marks them "partially decrypted" and dates them 28 July 1552, the first date of the volume.

All five were deciphered in Rome on arrival, and the decifrati are bound with them. Lestocquoy printed the letters in 1972 with the cipher passages deciphered. George Lasry’s key was applied to the transcriptions as a check. The one passage Lestocquoy left out, three struck-through cipher lines on R9, was decrypted with the same key.

At a glanceExisting decipherment · complete

The letters
Five ciphered despatches (26 March – 23 December 1553) from Prospero Santa Croce, nuncio in France, to Cardinal Innocenzo del Monte in Rome; catalogued as “what the cipher hides is not known”.
Where
Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Nunziatura di Francia 3; DECODE R6–R10.
The cipher
A homophonic alphabet of one- and two-digit elements, 1 as a null, and code groups for names and common words beginning with a dotted digit.
How it was read
All five were deciphered on arrival and printed by Lestocquoy (Acta Nuntiaturae Gallicae 9, 1972). Lasry’s key, applied to the volunteer transcriptions, agrees with the print.
What it says
The cancelled cipher on f. 248r (R9, not in the book) is the postscript written in clear beneath it: the royal fleet for Corsica has met a storm, and there is no word from Strozzi.
Credit
George Lasry attached the edition scan and his reconstructed key to each record on DECODE in October 2020.
Still open
The dotted code groups were not given values, since the print supplies the words. A few digits in R9 are illegible under the strokes, and the volunteer transcriptions have gaps.

01 The documents

The five records are leaves from Santa Croce's first French nunciature. In each, a letter in clear Italian moves into figures for its sensitive passages.

02 The cipher

Lasry's reconstruction, as filed on the records, is a homophonic alphabet of one- and two-digit elements: a = 28, 38 or 8; e = 24, 34 or 4; i = 2, 22 or 32; o = 26, 36 or 6; t = 07, 76 or 86; u = 02, 04, 06 or 08; r = 03 or 56; s = 05 or 66; and single values for the other letters (b 48, c 58, d 68, f 44, g 54, h 64, l 42, m 52, n 62, p 46, z 96). The digit 1 is a null that mostly falls between words. Code groups for names and common words begin with a dotted digit. Element boundaries are not marked, so a run like 2 2 8 can be split in several ways. The decoder in the repository resolves the splits with a sixteenth-century Italian language model.

03 The printed letters, checked

For R6, R7, R8 and R10 the text is Lestocquoy's. The decrypts follow it where the volunteer transcription is clean, but on their own they have holes or garbled words in almost every clause: they are decoded, not read, and the reading is the print (reading audit, 22 October 2026). For example, Lestocquoy's p. 157 reads "ci è maneggio stretto col [duca di Ferrara], non so se sia per haver denari con promesse di mercanti et avvantaggio d’esso Duca o pur per qualche lega o per haver artigliaria con la scusa che ha usato il [duca di Firenze] a Siena". The R6 decode follows those words where it is clean. R8's "Il Re Chr.mo resta mal contento dell’impresa di Corsica … credo che si risolverà di mantenerla" agrees both with p. 218 and with the interlinear decipherment on the leaf. R7 (Ferrara as "generale supremo di Italia con vinti mila scudi", the Naples enterprise) and R10 ("Molti persuadeno S.M. di havere a far guerra in Italia") match pp. 162–163 and 244.

04 R9: the cancelled postscript

Lestocquoy prints the letter of 14 December and its clear postscript (p. 240) but not the struck-through figures above the postscript. The figures were transcribed through the cancellation strokes and decrypted with the key. Long stretches match the postscript:

  • line 1: 46 6 03 86 = port…, and 58 6 56 05 2 … 58 28 = Corsi…ca
  • line 2: 44 26 03 [07] 02 62 8 = fortuna; 58 6 52 4 62 4 = come ne; 68 6 02 4 = dove
  • line 3: 28 06 2 66 36 = auiso; 68 4 42 = del; 05 07 03 6 96 96 2 = strozzi

So the cancelled cipher is the postscript itself: l’armata regia che partì per Corsica ha havuto fortuna et non si sa come né dove sia et non credo che ci sia aviso del Strozzi. In English: the royal fleet that left for Corsica has met a storm, no one knows how or where it is, and I do not think there is any news of Strozzi. The secretary began to encipher it, gave up, struck the figures out and wrote the sentence in clear.

The cancelled lines themselves give only aligned words between illegible digits, so taken as cipher they are decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026): the sentence is known from the clear postscript, not from the figures. In this repository none of the five entries is therefore held as a reading: the reading is Lestocquoy’s printed decipherment of 1972, which is not copied here.

05 What remains uncertain

  • The code groups (dotted elements such as [57], [43], [63], [99]) were not given values. The print supplies the words, so nothing depends on them.
  • In R9, a few digits between the aligned stretches are illegible under the strokes. The alignment rests on the stretches listed above (grade C for the passage as a whole, H for the aligned words).
  • The volunteer transcriptions have gaps (underlined figures untranscribed, a few doubtful digits), so the decrypts have holes in almost every clause and are decoded, not read. The printed text is the authority, and the reading.
