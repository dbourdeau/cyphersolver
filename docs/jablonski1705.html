Berlin → Pál Ráday · numeric syllabary with homophones and code words · 1705–1707 · key rebuilt from the enciphering drafts

Jablonski to Pál Ráday, 1705–1707

Go to the full text ↓

The Berlin court preacher’s drafts to Rákóczi’s envoy carry their own Latin under the cipher numbers; the key is rebuilt here from four of them and tested blind on a fifth.

Method: key recovered based on adjacent plaintext · Extent: complete

The letters
Five letter drafts of Daniel Ernst Jablonski, Berlin, to Pál Ráday: 13 Mar, 18 Apr and 11 Sep 1705, 7 Dec 1706 and 14 Feb 1707. Red number groups are written above the Latin words they encipher.
Where
Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin – PK, Nachlass A. H. Francke 11,2/15 nos 8, 13, 33 and 11,2/16 nos 42, 44 (digitised, Public Domain Mark). Leads 2169 and 2170; not on DECODE.
The cipher
A one-part numeric syllabary in alphabetical order: single letters with homophones, two- and three-letter syllables, eleven code words for rulers and countries, and nulls. The same table serves 1705 and 1706–07.
How it was rebuilt
From 350 number-and-word pairs (1,368 groups) of four drafts, then frozen and measured once on the draft of 14 Feb 1707, its 541 groups transcribed with the Latin masked: 0.930 of characters against 0.247 for the best of 200 shuffled keys. Re-run here with the same result.
What it says
In the held-out draft Ilgen, in the King of Prussia’s name, urges the Hungarians to make peace with the Emperor before the Franco-Imperial peace; a postscript is about two casks of wine passed on to the King. The content is Jablonski’s own Latin, not new.
Still open
Two number runs that Jablonski quotes from Ráday’s letter and says come from another cipher; groups 171 and 278; whether Révay (1974) already prints this key.
Credit
Daniel Bourdeau’s Kalliope sweep found the drafts (leads 2169, 2170). Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée) transcribed the pairs and the held-out draft, rebuilt and tested the key, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents (PR #385). The plaintext is Jablonski’s.

01 The drafts

Jablonski kept copies of his outgoing letters in draft books, now in the Francke papers at the Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin. In the letters to Pál Ráday, envoy of Ferenc Rákóczi II, he wrote the Latin in clear and set the red number groups above the words to be enciphered, so each draft holds both the cipher and its plaintext. Kalliope describes them as partly overwritten with a secret script.

02 The key and its test

The table (key.tsv, CC BY 4.0) runs in alphabetical order:

  • single letters with homophones, 30–70 (30 a, 31 b, 32 c, 33 d, 34 e, 35 a … 64 t … 70 u), and round numbers 110 u, 120 t, 130 s, 140 r, 160 p, 180 n, 190 m, 210 l, 220 i, 250 f, 260 e, 270 d, 280 c, 290 b, 300 a;
  • syllables in alphabetical order, 84 ab, 85 ac, 86 ad … 97 ba, 98 be … 185 qua … 201 ta … 223 ut;
  • code words 225 imperator/Caesar, 226 Hungaria, 227 Prussia, 228 Polonia, 229 Suecia, 231 Anglia, 232 and 237 Gallia, 233 Hispania, 234 Italia, 236 Elector, 241 Rex;
  • nulls below 30 and 301–310, 390, 401.

It was built from 350 figure/plaintext pairs (1,368 groups) of the four training drafts by an iterated segmentation, then corrected by hand along the alphabetical order. Of its 213 groups, 146 are sure (seen twice or more), 38 probable (seen once) and 29 nulls; 171 and 278 are unresolved and left out.

The test was fixed in writing before the held-out draft was looked at: every number group on scans 91–92 of 11,2/16 (no 44, 14 Feb 1707), letter body and postscript. The groups were transcribed blind from renderings that showed only the red ink, 541 groups, and decoded with the frozen key; only then was the Latin under them read. Score: one minus the edit distance over the length of the reference, on letters, with j→i and v→u (the script also folds w→u and y→i, which the protocol does not name; declared by the contributor). The frozen key renders 0.930 of the characters (958 decoded letters against 952 reference letters); the best of 200 keys made by shuffling its values renders 0.247 (mean 0.198). The thresholds, 0.85 and a margin of 0.30, were met. Nine groups count as wrong: 2 illegible and 7 outside the key (215, 15, 61, 178, 113, 53, 40). The key was not changed after the measurement.

When the work was integrated (11 Nov 2026), the contributor’s measuring script was run again on the frozen key, transcription and reference: it gives the same 0.9296 against 0.2468, and the SHA-256 hashes of all seven frozen files match the recorded ones.

The key also agrees with the letter as Ráday received it on 3 Aug 1705, with his glosses (Wikimedia Commons, from Révay 1974): 191 ra, 208 vi, 203 ti, 170 o, and bello gallico, armorum, principes spelled as the table gives them. That letter is glossed and printed, so this is a check, not a measurement.

03 The held-out draft

The draft of 14 Feb 1707 starts and stops the cipher inside sentences. Read with the key, the enciphered runs of the letter body say (italics where the decode slips and the Latin under the groups is followed):

… per Cracoviam … Dantiscum. Exc. Dn. Ilgen nihil a milite Saxonico timendum esse, ut qui universus a Belgis parata pecunia conductus adversus Gallos proxima aestate est militaturus; sed metuenda esse arma Caesaris, quandoquidem Pax cum Rege Galliae prorsus vicina sit … Monet itaque et obtestatur Exc. Ilgen nomine Regis, qui pio corde vobis, ut ante Pacem Gallicam vos, cupit quam optime Pacem pangatis, ne mox mole Belli opprimamini.

“His Excellency Herr Ilgen [says] that nothing is to be feared from the Saxon soldiery, since the whole of it, hired with money provided by the Dutch, will serve against the French next summer; but the arms of the Emperor are to be feared, since peace with the King of France is quite near … His Excellency Ilgen therefore advises and implores you, in the name of the King, who with a sincere heart wishes for you that you make peace on the best terms you can before the French peace, lest you be crushed soon by the weight of the war.”

The postscript recalls the two small casks of noble wine received through Ráday’s kindness a year and a half before, which Jablonski judged too fine for a private man and offered to the King, who accepted them graciously, and asks that one or two more be sent by way of Cracow to the postmaster or Breslau to Schiller. The full decode, line by line beside the Latin of the draft, and the English translation are under Full texts.

Of about 176 words in the enciphered runs, 8 hold a hole (the 9 groups illegible or outside the key) and 16 more carry a clerk’s slip or a misread group (militaturut, paax, grandm, uile for vina, exse) or the code word 225, which gives imperator where the draft says Caesar.

04 What remains uncertain

  • The two “ex aliena Cifra” runs. On 11,2/15 no 33 (scan 72) Jablonski quotes two runs from Ráday’s incoming letter, unglossed, saying they seem to come from another cipher: 204.155.275.30.147.111.18.213.8.205.7 and 95.137.112.15.48.137.12.178.144.187.16.90.85.45 (25 groups). They do not read with this key, which bears out his remark. They may belong to Ráday’s own key; Benda (1954) mentions a clavis for the Jablonski correspondence in the Ráday archive, not seen.
  • Values. 38 values are seen only once; 171 and 278 are unresolved; 15, 40, 53, 61, 113, 178 and 215 are not in the table.
  • Prior key. Révay’s book of 1974 reproduces the received letter of 3 Aug 1705; no open text of it was found, so whether it prints this key could not be checked. If it does, the key is his and the work here is an independent rebuild and its test.
  • Heading. The two red heading lines of scan 23 (no 8) read illustris domine with the key; outside the measured portion.
  • One reader. An agent transcribed the groups from the SBB images; no human palaeographer has checked them, and the images were not seen in the integrating session.
  • Not compared. The 1704 drafts (lead 2168), whose numbers run lower and may use another system, and the 1712 letter to Rákóczi (lead 2171), a different system, are separate leads. R483’s rebuilt syllabic key (Rákóczi 1711) is a different system: of 81 groups valued in both keys, 2 agree.

# jablonski1705 - reading of the measured portion (11,2/16 no 44, scans 91-92), as sentences

Our decode (`decryption.txt`) read as Latin, normalised. Where the decode slips (a cipher-clerk error, a doubtful group or
a group outside the frozen key) the word follows the Latin that Jablonski wrote under the groups; such words are in
*italics*. The draft starts and stops the cipher inside sentences (the clear words around the groups are not part of the
portion), so the stretches below are the enciphered runs, in order.

**Scan 91 (letter body).** ... per Cracoviam ... Dantiscum. Exc. Dn. Ilgen nihil a milite *Saxonico* timendum esse, ut qui
universus a Belgis parata pecunia conductus adversus Gallos proxima aestate est *militaturus*; sed metuenda esse arma Caesaris,
quandoquidem *Pax* cum Rege Galliae prorsus *vicina sit*, quo tempore Caesar et *proprium* Exercitum *grandem* habebit et a
minoribus *dimissos* Imperii Statibus *milites* conducere poterit. *Monet* itaque et obtestatur Exc. Ilgen nomine Regis, qui pio
corde vobis, ut ante Pacem Gallicam vos, cupit quam *optime* Pacem pangatis, ne *mox* mole Belli *opprimamini*. Videtis
sane nihil *vos tot* annis profecisse, nisi quod Patriam Vestram magis quam hostiles terras devastaveritis. Si vero Caesar
Exercitu auctus fuerit, *ecquid* de vobis futurum esse putatis?

**Scan 92 (P.S.).** ... Patere ut in ... generosa illa *vina* quae Vestro beneficio ante sesquiannum habui (equidem duo minora
vasa), quod illud generosius vinum *esse* arbitrarer quam quod me hominem privatum deceret, Regi humillime *obtuli*, a quo etiam
gratiose acceptum fuit ... maiora duo ... unum vel duo vascula minora vini generosi vel Cracoviam ad *Postamagistrum* vel
*Breslaviam* ad *Schillerum* ... ita dirigere *dignetur* ut ab *istis* viris ...

Words in italics: 24 of about 176 words of the runs. Eight words of the decode hold a "?" (the 9 groups that are
illegible or outside the frozen key: Saxonico, dimissos, optime, opprimamini, tot, ecquid, Schillerum, ita);
the rest are clerk's slips or misread groups (militaturut, paax, grandm, propriun, uile, exse, notu li, imtis) or the code word 225 = Caesar/imperator.
read_share (hole rate) = 1 - 8/176 = 0.955.

# jablonski1705 - English translation of the measured portion (11,2/16 no 44, Berlin, 14 Feb 1707)

The enciphered runs of Jablonski's draft to Pál Ráday, after `reading.md`. "..." marks clear words outside the runs.

**Letter body (scan 91).** ... by way of Cracow ... Danzig. His Excellency Herr Ilgen [says] that nothing is to be feared from
the Saxon soldiery, since the whole of it, hired with money provided by the Dutch, will serve against the French next summer;
but the arms of the Emperor are to be feared, since peace with the King of France is quite near, and at that time the Emperor
will have a great army of his own and will be able to hire the soldiers dismissed by the lesser Estates of the Empire.
His Excellency Ilgen therefore advises and implores you, in the name of the King, who with a sincere heart wishes for you
that you make peace on the best terms you can before the French peace, lest you be crushed soon by the weight of the war.
You surely see that in so many years you have achieved nothing except to lay waste your own country more than the enemy's
lands. But if the Emperor's army is increased, what do you think will become of you?

**Postscript (scan 92).** ... Allow that ... those noble wines which, by your kindness, I received a year and a half ago
(two smaller casks indeed): since I judged that wine too noble for a private man like me, I most humbly offered it to the King,
by whom it was also graciously accepted ... two larger ... one or two smaller casks of noble wine, either to Cracow to the
postmaster or to Breslau to Schiller ... may he deign to send them so that by those men ...
