Regensburg → Milan · homophonic substitution of Italian · 2 September 1576 · key recovered from the ciphertext

Alfonso II del Carretto to his agents, 2 September 1576

Go to the full text ↓

The marquis of Finale writes from the imperial Diet to his agents in Milan about a man sent to the King as trustworthy, in a cipher whose key was recovered from the ciphertext alone.

The letter
Alfonso II del Carretto, marquis of Finale, to his agents Lodovico Allaria and Paolo Fenocchio in Milan, Regensburg, 2 September 1576, signed “il principe marchese del Final”. The first eleven lines are in cipher, the rest in clear.
Where
Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Modena, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera D, Del Carretto, Alfonso II, c. 10 (Lodovico Media Library). Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A substitution of Italian with homophones for the vowels and Latin, Greek-like and digit shapes for the consonants; word division kept, an isolated o as a null, no nomenclator seen.
How it was deciphered
The key was broken on lines 1–8 alone by annealing with a model of 16th-century Italian, frozen, and checked on lines 9–11 against an independent machine break of those lines: 0.961 of characters against 0.263 for the best of 200 shuffled keys.
What it says
Beccaria has said that no one could carry out His Majesty’s orders better than Count Spinola; the marquis will make the truth known, though the man was sent to His Majesty as trustworthy, and “neither he nor the one behind him will reach his aim”.
Still open
Six words of the torn margin note, and 25 probable words that depart from the key by one sign. The persons named are not identified.
Credit
Brice Dudout (Europe chiffrée) transcribed the cipher, recovered and validated the key and read the letter, working with Claude Opus 5.5 agents. The letter came from Daniel Bourdeau’s catalogue (no. 2164).

01 The letter

In the late summer of 1576 the Diet of the Empire sat at Regensburg, where Maximilian II would die in October. Alfonso II del Carretto, marquis of Finale on the Ligurian coast, was there and wrote to his two agents in Milan. The upper part of c. 10r is eleven cipher lines with a torn note in the left margin and one interlinear insertion; the rest of the letter is in clear. The neighbouring c. 9, a letter of 1574 to the same agents, is in clear and mentions letters “in ziffra”. No decipherment of the time is on the leaf, and no edition was found.

02 The key and its test

The vowels have several signs each (a = D, m; e = G, O, t, l; o = C, i and two groups written oPo and oP; i = u, n; u = 7 and a dotted rho), and the consonants single shapes (h = 4, d = ezh, f = d, r = e, s = a, t = x, n = k, m = h). The key was found by simulated annealing on lines 1–8 with a four-letter model of 16th-century Italian learnt from other texts; six seeds gave the same key. One value was corrected by sense on those lines (d = f, in fra, far, difficultà), and the key was frozen.

Lines 9–11 (54 words) were fixed as the held-out part before any calculation. They were broken a second time, alone and automatically, and that independent break was frozen before the frozen key was applied to them. The two agree on 0.961 of characters, against 0.263 for the best of 200 shuffled keys; the thresholds set in advance were 0.85 and a margin of 0.30. The reference is a machine break, not a clear text of the time, and the transcription was made in one pass.

03 The reading

182 cipher words, 174 in the block and 8 in the margin: 149 certain, 25 probable, 2 restored and 6 unresolved, all six in the torn margin. That is 95.6% without the restored words. The probable words depart from the key by one sign each; several are probably slips of the single-pass transcription, and harbbian for habbian, twice, looks like a habit of the encipherer. The Italian and an English translation are under Full texts.

04 What remains uncertain

  • Six margin words are lost or cut by the tear of the left edge of c. 10r.
  • Twenty-five words depart from the key by one sign (D/G, ezh/reversed c, e/c, rho-o/u); a blind second reading of those signs on the full-resolution images has not been done.
  • Beccaria, Signor Pietro and the man in question are not identified; Cavagna’s study of the marquis’s library (2012) and the agents’ papers in Genoa or Milan were not checked.
  • The images carry no stated licence and are not reproduced.

c. 10r: cipher block L01-L11 and torn margin M1-M5

Beccaria has said that no one could carry out His Majesty's orders better than Count Espinula (Spinola); the marquis has not failed and will not fail to make the truth known, for his agents' honour and His Majesty's service, although it is awkward that the man was approved and sent to His Majesty as trustworthy; let the agents do their duty on their side, for the man is better known to His Majesty and his ministers than is believed, and neither he nor the one behind him will reach his aim.

182 cipher words (block L01-L11: 174; margin M1-M5: 8): 149 certain, 25 probable, 2 restored, 6 unresolved.
**174/182 = 95.6% read by sense** (176/182 = 96.7% with the two restored words): **read**.

# delcarretto1576 - English translation of the cipher

Translation of reading.md (Brice Dudout, with a Claude Opus 5.5 agent, 6 Nov 2026). Probable words are not marked here

> Beccaria has said, among other things, that there is no one who could carry out His Majesty's orders better or as
> well as Count Espinula (Spinola), and he assures us that he will not fail to carry them out, as long as it is left to
> him alone; then let Signor Pietro judge the rest and the quality of the man. But be sure that we have not failed, nor
> shall we fail in any point, to make the truth known, as much for the honour of Your Lordship as for the service of His
> Majesty, and ours too, although it brings some difficulty that this man was approved in the way he was by Your
> Lordship, who sent him to His Majesty as a trustworthy person, and that he has behaved rather well than badly.
> So let Your Lordship rest easy and attend to doing your duty on that side, as we have done and shall do on this one;
> for since this man is known to His Majesty and to his ministers better perhaps than is believed, we trust that neither
> he nor the one for whom he acts will achieve their aim.

Margin (torn): "… al … … things … …".

Context (to be checked by a historian): Alfonso II del Carretto, marquis of Finale, at the Diet of Regensburg (1576),
writes to his agents in Milan, Lodovico Allaria and Paolo Fenocchio, about a man recommended to Philip II and about
Count Spinola; Finale was then contested between its marquis, its subjects, Genoa, Spain and the Empire. "Signor
Pietro" and "Beccaria" are not identified.
