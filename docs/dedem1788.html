Van Dedem at Constantinople, 1788–1799

Nationaal Archief, 3.01.26 (L.P. van de Spiegel) inv. 212, and 2.01.08 inv. 346 (DECODE R2120, R2122, R2131, R1895). These are ciphered despatches from the Dutch ambassador to the Porte to Grand Pensionary Van de Spiegel and, in 1799, to the Agent van Staat Van der Goes.

Summary. The catalogue entry grouped four DECODE records, all marked non-decrypted. One of them, R2120, was in fact read at the time: its two cipher pages were photographed again under R2121, together with the clerk’s full clear decipherment, a despatch of 22 December 1788 (DECODE dates R2120 to 11 October). It concerns an Ottoman loan of seven or eight million, secured on the revenues of nine provinces. The letters of 15 January 1789 (R2122, 258 groups) and 9 February 1793 (R2131, 50 groups) use the same code, and it is heavily homophonic: one group stands for about one word, and a common word has several groups. No key for it survives on DECODE. The undated Fagel key series is a set of alphabetical Dutch codebooks of another size. Three deciphered siblings (R2121, R1947 and R2053, about 1,300 groups in all) were transcribed and aligned with their solutions. Only about half the target groups occur in them, and the aligners found no consistent group values, so no reading is offered. The 1799 despatch (R1895) uses a different system, with marks over the digits, and was not attacked.

02 The despatch of 22 December 1788

This is the clerk’s decipherment (R2121). Van Dedem reports a conference with the Reis Effendi, the Kaymakam and the Kâhya Bey. He told them that the States would not take on the Porte’s proposed loan directly, but might allow it to be raised quietly. The Porte then set out its security:

om speciaal voor de geheele Leening en de interest tot de geheele aflossing toe te verbinden al de inkomsten van de Provincie Morea, Negropont, Salonique, Volos, Urfan en Cara Agatz, als ook die van de Landen van Damascus, Sorien en Rodosto … het geheel vast jaarlijks tribut van Jenischeher

“to pledge, specially for the whole loan and the interest until full repayment, all the revenues of the provinces of Morea, Negropont, Salonica, Volos, Urfan and Cara Agatz, and those of the lands of Damascus, Sorien and Rodosto … the whole fixed yearly tribute of Jenischeher.” The Porte also offered cotton and wool from Salonica and Smyrna at preferential prices. Van Dedem suggests that the Directors of the Levant Trade, or failing them Hope & Co., should take the loan up. Some place names were left in clear inside the cipher.

03 What was tried on R2122 and R2131

  • Keys. DECODE’s notes link these records to fourteen others in the same code: Kroll 1784–85, Van Haeften 1776, Schenck 1777, and Van Dedem 1789 and 1809. None of them is a key. The undated Fagel series “Cijffers zonder bestemming” (R2842–R2852, before 1793) is made of large alphabetical Dutch codebooks. In R2843 the words from a to ad alone run past 670, so it is not this code.
  • The code. It is not alphabetical. For example, welke falls near 2888, landen near 1277 and als near 17. It is also strongly homophonic: in R2121’s clear text de occurs 36 times, but no group occurs more than 11 times, and 541, 641, 841 and 941 are all frequent. The fixed opening 2504 2604 2704 and the frames 701 801 … 301 401 501 601 look like nulls.
  • Cribs. R2121 (Dutch, 633 groups), R1947 (8 Nov 1789, French, 498) and R2053 (1 Dec 1789, French, 196) were transcribed together with their solutions. Only 58% of R2122’s groups and 52% of R2131’s occur anywhere in them. The French and Dutch cribs share only 17% of their groups.
  • Alignment. Hard-EM and leave-one-out word aligners were run with restarts, and a variant that treats the hundreds digit as a dummy was also tried. None converged to consistent values: the same group came out as different words in different places. Without reliable values nothing in the two letters can be read.

04 What would finish it

  • The codebook of the Constantinople legation (NA 1.02.04, legatie Turkije) or Croiset’s working sheets.
  • More deciphered letters in the same code, above all Dutch ones. The Kroll letters (R1948–R1952) and those of Van Haeften (R2134) and Schenck (R2141) carry solutions and have not yet been transcribed.
  • For R1895: a key to Croiset’s marked codes of the Batavian period.
