Van Dedem at Constantinople, 1788–1793

The letters
Ciphered despatches from the Dutch ambassador Van Dedem at Constantinople to Grand Pensionary Van de Spiegel (1788–1793) and, in 1799, to the Agent van Staat Van der Goes. The 22 December 1788 despatch concerns an Ottoman loan of seven or eight million.
Where
Nationaal Archief, 3.01.26 inv. 212 and 2.01.08 inv. 346 (DECODE R2120, R2122, R2131, R1895), all listed with no decipherment. R2120 was in fact deciphered at the time: its pages were rephotographed as R2121 with the clerk’s full clear decipherment.
The cipher
A heavily homophonic code: one group stands for about one word, and a common word has several groups. R2122 (15 January 1789, 258 groups) and R2131 (9 February 1793, 50 groups) use it; the 1799 despatch (R1895) uses marks over the digits and was not attempted.
How it was tried
Three deciphered siblings (R2121, R1947, R2053, about 1,300 groups) were transcribed and aligned; only about half of the target groups occur in them and no consistent values came out. The Fagel codebooks are a different, alphabetical system.
What it says
Only the clerk’s R2121 text is read: the Porte pledges the revenues of several provinces for the loan, and Van Dedem suggests the Directors of the Levant Trade or Hope & Co. take it up.
Still open
R2122, R2131 and R1895 are unread. Needed: the legation codebook (NA 1.02.04) or Croiset’s working sheets, or more deciphered letters in the same code.

02 The despatch of 22 December 1788

This is the clerk’s decipherment (R2121). Van Dedem reports a conference with the Reis Effendi, the Kaymakam and the Kâhya Bey. He told them that the States would not take on the Porte’s proposed loan directly, but might allow it to be raised quietly. The Porte then set out its security:

om speciaal voor de geheele Leening en de interest tot de geheele aflossing toe te verbinden al de inkomsten van de Provincie Morea, Negropont, Salonique, Volos, Urfan en Cara Agatz, als ook die van de Landen van Damascus, Sorien en Rodosto … het geheel vast jaarlijks tribut van Jenischeher

“to pledge, specially for the whole loan and the interest until full repayment, all the revenues of the provinces of Morea, Negropont, Salonica, Volos, Urfan and Cara Agatz, and those of the lands of Damascus, Sorien and Rodosto … the whole fixed yearly tribute of Jenischeher.” The Porte also offered cotton and wool from Salonica and Smyrna at preferential prices. Van Dedem suggests that the Directors of the Levant Trade, or failing them Hope & Co., should take the loan up. Some place names were left in clear inside the cipher.

03 What was tried on R2122 and R2131

  • Keys. DECODE’s notes link these records to fourteen others in the same code: Kroll 1784–85, Van Haeften 1776, Schenck 1777, and Van Dedem 1789 and 1809. None of them is a key. The undated Fagel series “Cijffers zonder bestemming” (R2842–R2852, before 1793) is made of large alphabetical Dutch codebooks. In R2843 the words from a to ad alone run past 670, so it is not this code.
  • The code. In plain numerical order it is not alphabetical (the second pass below finds the order). For example, welke falls near 2888, landen near 1277 and als near 17. It is also strongly homophonic: in R2121’s clear text de occurs 36 times, but no group occurs more than 11 times, and 541, 641, 841 and 941 are all frequent. The fixed opening 2504 2604 2704 and the frames 701 801 … 301 401 501 601 look like nulls.
  • Cribs. R2121 (Dutch, 633 groups), R1947 (8 Nov 1789, French, 498) and R2053 (1 Dec 1789, French, 196) were transcribed together with their solutions. Only 58% of R2122’s groups and 52% of R2131’s occur anywhere in them. The French and Dutch cribs share only 17% of their groups.
  • Alignment. Word aligners (hard EM and leave-one-out) were run with restarts, and a variant that treats the hundreds digit as a dummy was also tried. None converged to consistent values: the same group came out as different words in different places. Without reliable values the first pass decoded nothing in the two letters.

04 The code is alphabetical in blocks

Feyseel Nur’s second pass (4 October 2026, pull request 21, with Claude and Codex) re-indexes each group within its block of 500: thousands, then whether the hundreds digit is 5 or more, then the last two digits as a row, then the hundreds digit modulo 5 as a column. In that order the crib values follow the alphabet. Frequent words fill whole rows: en is 541, 641, 741, 841 and 941; nearby are 537 egter, 837 eindelijk, 543 engagement, 643 engager, 644 entrer.

The test: on 95 re-aligned crib pairs the rank correlation within blocks is 0.799 for this layout, against 0.351 for plain numerical order, and no shuffled assignment of words reaches 0.368 in 10,000 trials. An independent re-derivation by OpenAI Codex gives the same layout.

The resulting table has 354 values, graded by how they are attested. In R2122 (255 groups) 107 have a firm value, and in R2131 (46 groups) 16 do. The decoded words fit the loan of R2121 in R2122: ENVOYÉ het verlangen en van PORTA ontrent het doen een … negociatie. In R2131, a reading about the Porte keeping neutral in the war with the French is suggested by the values but not established. The groups without a value keep only an alphabetical bracket. With holes in nearly every clause, neither letter built sentences on that table, and the reading audit of 22 October 2026 classed both decoded, not read. The codebook found the next day (section 05) settled them.

05 The codebook, found in the legation archive

On 23 October 2026 the inventory of the Constantinople legation (Nationaal Archief 1.02.20, legatie Turkije) turned up, under the chargé Kroll (1784–85), inv. 785, “Sleutel geheimschrift”. Its six scans on the Nationaal Archief site are the codebook itself, headed “Kroll. Un Chiffre”: pages of 500 groups in five columns of 100, the words alphabetical across the rows, letters and syllables at the foot of each column, titles in 3001–3500, and a note on noun and preterite forms. It is this code: Feyseel Nur’s layout above is its layout, and the firm values of the second pass recur in it. Every group of R2122 and R2131 was looked up on the scans (key/kroll785_values.tsv) and the cipher re-read on the DECODE images (in R2131, 2261 is 2262). Both letters now give Dutch sentences, with about fourteen groups that give no sense where they stand.

9 February 1793 (R2131). The whole cipher paragraph:

Ik heb de eer UwHoEdGestr: te informeeren [?als dat] de Ottomanische Porta sig op eergister volkomen aan de Envoyé van Pruissen [?verbonden] heeft, om gedurende deze oorlog met de Franschen sig in [?alle] [?deelen] neutraal te houden, zonder eenige alliantie meer [met] meergemelde Natie aan te gaande.

“I have the honour to inform Your Honour [?that] the Ottoman Porte, the day before yesterday, [?bound] itself fully to the Prussian envoy to keep itself neutral in [?all] [?respects] during this war with the French, without entering into any further alliance [with] the aforesaid nation.” The group 319 (Son Altesse) stands where alle is needed, and 591 gives a stray g. The paragraph builds coherent sentences but reads at 87.2%, under the 95% bar.

15 January 1789 (R2122). Van Dedem’s démarches after the resolution of 2 October; the Porte’s wish for a loan negotiation in the Republic, which he has let the Prussian envoy know of; and a portrait of the English ambassador:

Ik [heb] het zelfde omtrent de Engelsche Ambassadeur niet durven observeeren, wijl daar hoogenaamt geen staat op te maaken is, en hij een allergevaarlijkst sujet en vol intrigues is, en hij zo ras het met zijn particulier belang strookt, steets gewoon is van zijn eigen woorden die van een ander te maaken, en om tot zijn doel te geraaken, zig met duijzenderley leugens op te houden en zijn beste vrienden te compromitteren

“I [have] not dared to observe the same towards the English ambassador, since no reliance at all can be placed on it and he is a most dangerous subject and full of intrigues; and as soon as it suits his particular interest he is always used to making his own words those of another, and, to reach his end, to busy himself with a thousand lies and to compromise his best friends.” The letter ends on a new Swedish subsidy.

06 What remains open

  • Fourteen groups whose codebook value gives no sense where they stand: 1230, 1330 (twice), 80, 760 (twice), 3928, 1605, 1463, 2622 in R2122; 319 and 591 in R2131; and 4414 in R2122, which is no code in the book. Each occurs once or twice, too few to settle a different value or a slip.
  • An English translation of R2120’s contemporary decipherment is not yet written.

# R2121: Van Dedem van de Gelder to [the Griffier Fagel], Pera of Constantinople, 22 December 1788: English translation

Dated 22 December 1788. Missive of Mr van Dedem van de Gelder.

Right Honourable Sir! In a subsequent negotiation with the Reis Effendi, the Kaymakam, and the Kâhya Bey, lieutenant of
the Grand Vizier, of State, I gave him to understand, first, that however much I could assure him that Their High
Mightinesses continue to be most strongly attached to the interests of this Court, and show themselves inclined on every
occasion to give tokens of their true sentiments and sincere friendship, I must nevertheless forewarn him that I did not
at all believe that Their High Mightinesses could or would charge themselves with the direct execution of the proposal he
had made; that the only thing which I flattered myself could possibly take place, and at the same time serve to do this
Court a direct advantage and give a telling proof of goodwill, would be for Their High Mightinesses to facilitate the
carrying out of this negotiation, either directly or by connivance, as the circumstances of the time would best allow,
and [?] it solely by their tacit consent. Secondly, that the Porte should first fix and state at what rate the annual
interest would run, and in what manner and by what fixed and certain funds — apart from the general obligation of this
whole Ottoman Empire — it intends to make the lenders entirely safe and easy, both for the sum lent and for the promised
annual interest and its further discharge; and within or after what time and in what manner the actual repayment will
finally be made. But since the Ministry does not know what is required in the Republic on such occasions, though it is
absolutely inclined to accept all reasonable conditions, the request is that I should first hand over a plan in which
all the conditions on which a loan of 7 or 8 millions could take place are specified. But at my instance it has been
provisionally settled that, specially for the whole loan and the interest until full repayment, all the revenues of the
province of the Morea, Negroponte, Salonica, Volos, Urfan and Kara Ağaç shall be pledged, as also those of the lands of
Damascus, Syria and Rodosto, which nine lands each yield the Porte annually fifty thousand kilos of wheat, Constantinople
measure. Further, for greater security and for payment of the interest, they wish in addition to pledge the whole fixed
annual tribute of Yenişehir, which brings the Porte four hundred and fifty thousand piasters in money a year. Moreover
the Ministry will undertake every year to cede by preference to the Republic a quantity of cotton and a portion of the
produce of the lands of Yenişehir and Smyrna, as also a portion of the wool that comes from Salonica, at the current
(price) or a fixed sum; and if (the) money is transported with one or more warships or other large ships which one would
[?find] for the war, the Ottoman Porte [?...] [?the same] at a moderate sum […] buy. And since I consider this moment a
good time to obtain for the Republic in general, and for commerce and navigation in particular, privileges of great
importance which otherwise could not be hoped for, and since I would moreover already dare to answer that, if the
management of it were entrusted to me, I would engage the Ottoman Empire to set the interest so high that one could
count on at least 6 per cent clear, I consider myself obliged to commend the matter anew to Your Honour's serious
consideration, and to put it to you whether, for the reasons given, it would not be the business of the Directors of the
Levant Trade to take its execution upon themselves, so as by that means to give new lustre to commerce in that part of
the world. If so, I beg Your Honour to communicate the contents of this letter to them, since, having no cipher with
those gentlemen, I neither can nor dare write to them directly about it. But if Your Honour finds the proposal
objectionable, I put it to you whether in that case the point could not be handed with hope of success to Messrs Hope
and Co., as being perfectly suited for it; and since it would always be necessary here &c. &c.

I have the honour &c. Pera of Constantinople, 22 December 1788. (Signed) F. G. van Dedem van de Gelder.

## Notes

- 1230 (honderd) and 1330 (honderd duizend) occur where no number is meant (twice 1330); they may be the "niets
  beteekenende caracters" that the Berigt (inv. 686) allows in the middle of a letter, but nothing marks them as nulls.
- 80 = "daar zal" gives no sense in "zonder [80] minst moeite"; "minst" (1622) is written without the -e.
- 760 = "faciliteeren" twice follows an infinitive ("volvoeren 760", "agterhouden 760") where a noun or participle is
  needed (volvoering, agterhoudend). The codebook's Nota explains that a noun in -ing is made by writing the infinitive's
  character followed by a "g" character, with "760 faciliteeren" as its example; Dedem's clerk seems to have copied the
  example's number instead of a "g". Not adopted as a value: the reading keeps [760].
- 2622 = "troupes" ("door het manqueeren van de troupes instructie") is carried but awkward; 3928 = "de Middellandsche
  Zee" and 1605 = "Maison" give no clear sense where they stand; 1463 = "de Keizer van Rusland" after "te kennen ge"
  leaves the verb unfinished.
- 4414: no such code (the codebook stops below 4000); the group is legible as written.
- "de Minister van Zee" (1821 + 3213) is the Kapudan Pasha, the Ottoman admiral.
- The underlines and hooks under most groups (straight, wavy, angled) were checked against the codebook and the 1775
  Berigt: neither describes them, and the decode makes sense without them.

van die resolutie door het manqueeren van de [?troupes] instructie genoegsaam werkeloos hebben moeten blijven, daarna,
in allen deele geregeld, en het avantage hebbende bij elke gelegendheyd ten klaarsten te bespeuren [dat] het gantsch
Ministerie een bijzonder vertrouwen in mij steld, kan ik UHoogEd: Gest: (na waarheyd) versekeren [dat] mijne
insinuaties niets van goed effect geweest zijn, en ik niet twijfel of dezelve zullen bij continuatie van aanbelang
genoeg gerekent worden, om ingang te vinden.

Uyt eerst voormelden hoofde, heb ik mede dienstig geoordeeld de Envoyé van Pruissen het verlangen van de Ottomanische
Porta omtrent het doen eener Negociatie in de Republiek niet geheel te moeten laaten ignoreren, ten einde de Koning
zijn Meester, wie zijn belang zeker mede is [dat] de Porta tegenwoordig niet verleegen gelaaten, noch aan eenig
ongepaste vernedering bloot gesteld word, daar van tot deszelfs narige opening te geeven.

Ik [heb] het zelfde omtrent de Engelsche Ambassadeur niet durven observeeren, wijl daar hoogenaamt geen staat op te
maaken is, en hij een allergevaarlijkst sujet en vol intrigues is, en hij zo ras het met zijn particulier belang
strookt, steets gewoon is van zijn eigen woorden die van een ander te maaken, en om tot zijn doel te geraaken, zig met
duijzenderley leugens op te houden en zijn beste vrienden te compromitteren, durvende ik mij ter overlegging van dien
gerust beroepen en verlaaten op het getuygenis dat Zijne Majesteit de Koning van Pruissen en alle andere hoven welke
alhier een minister hebben daarvan kunnen geeven.

bedenkende [?de Middellandsche Zee] aan de volvoeren [760] der desseinen van de drie geallieerde Mogenheden zou kunnen
schadelijk zijn, heb ik daar aan in [?Maison] reeds voordeel gedaan. En zal ik, ofschoon de Minister van Zee omtrent
alles zeer agterhouden [760] handeld en nooit iets daarvan [4414] te kennen ge [?de Keizer van Rusland], egter bij
continuatie dat point aanraaden. En het geen niet weinig die zaak zou goed doen, is de reussite der bekende
Negociatie, dewijl daar na direct een som nadien kant kon gebruikt worden.

tot mijn schrijven van gisteren en heeden aan de Heer Griffier, waar mede ik onder versekering van mijn respect met
alle verschuldigde hoogagting d'eer heb te verblijven. Hoog Edele Gestrenge Heer! UHoogEd: Gestrenge onderd: en
gehoorz: dienaar. Pera van Constantinopolen den 15 Jan. 1789.

## Notes

- 1230 (honderd) and 1330 (honderd duizend) occur where no number is meant (twice 1330); they may be the "niets
  beteekenende caracters" that the Berigt (inv. 686) allows in the middle of a letter, but nothing marks them as nulls.
- 80 = "daar zal" gives no sense in "zonder [80] minst moeite"; "minst" (1622) is written without the -e.
- 760 = "faciliteeren" twice follows an infinitive ("volvoeren 760", "agterhouden 760") where a noun or participle is
  needed (volvoering, agterhoudend). The codebook's Nota explains that a noun in -ing is made by writing the infinitive's
  character followed by a "g" character, with "760 faciliteeren" as its example; Dedem's clerk seems to have copied the
  example's number instead of a "g". Not adopted as a value: the reading keeps [760].
- 2622 = "troupes" ("door het manqueeren van de troupes instructie") is carried but awkward; 3928 = "de Middellandsche
  Zee" and 1605 = "Maison" give no clear sense where they stand; 1463 = "de Keizer van Rusland" after "te kennen ge"
  leaves the verb unfinished.
- 4414: no such code (the codebook stops below 4000); the group is legible as written.
- "de Minister van Zee" (1821 + 3213) is the Kapudan Pasha, the Ottoman admiral.
- The underlines and hooks under most groups (straight, wavy, angled) were checked against the codebook and the 1775
  Berigt: neither describes them, and the decode makes sense without them.

# R2122, Van Dedem to Van de Spiegel, "Duplicaat", Pera 15 Jan 1789: English

Translation of `R2122_reading.md`, with the same holes.

## Translation

Duplicate. High Noble Honourable Sir!

Being of the view that the interest and political system of Their High Mightinesses and that of [1230] their two new
allies and [1330] the present state of affairs, through the resolution of the second of October, must more than ever
before be held to be one and the same, all my démarches, both at the Porte and elsewhere, which until the receipt of
that resolution, for want of the [?troops'] instruction, had to remain largely idle, have since then been regulated in
every respect; and having the advantage of perceiving most clearly on every occasion [that] the whole Ministry places
a particular trust in me, I can assure Your Honour (in truth) [that] my insinuations have been of no good effect, and I
do not doubt whether, if continued, they will be reckoned of enough importance to find acceptance.

For the first-mentioned reason I have also thought it useful not to let the Prussian envoy wholly ignore the Ottoman
Porte's wish concerning the making of a negotiation in the Republic, so that the King his master, whose interest it
certainly also is [that] the Porte be not now left embarrassed nor exposed to any unseemly humiliation, may be given
notice of it for its further opening.

I [have] not dared to observe the same towards the English ambassador, since no reliance at all can be placed on it and
he is a most dangerous subject and full of intrigues; and as soon as it suits his particular interest he is always
used to making his own words those of another, and, to reach his end, to busy himself with a thousand lies and to
compromise his best friends; for the proof of which I dare confidently appeal to and rely on the testimony that His
Majesty the King of Prussia and all other courts that have a minister here can give of it.

And without [80] least trouble I could wholly oppose [1330] the granting of a new subsidy to Sweden, but, considering
[?the Mediterranean] could be harmful to the execution [760] of the designs of the three allied powers, I have already
made an advantage of it in [?Maison]. And although the Minister of the Sea [the Kapudan Pasha] acts very reservedly
[760] about everything and never [4414] makes known anything of it [?the Emperor of Russia], I shall nevertheless,
continuing, recommend that point. And what would do that matter no little good is the success of the known
negotiation, since a sum could then be used directly in that direction.

Not wishing to detain Your Honour's attention further this time, I refer for the public news to my letters of
yesterday and today to the Griffier, with which, assuring you of my respect, I have the honour to remain with all due
esteem, High Noble Honourable Sir, Your Honour's humble and obedient servant. Pera of Constantinople, 15 Jan. 1789.
