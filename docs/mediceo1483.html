An undated Medici cipher letter, XLVIII 214 (about 1478–85)

Go to the full text ↓

An unsigned cipher letter in the Medici papers turns out to share its signs and nulls with a letter of 1478 whose key George Lasry recovered; with that key about a third of it reads.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 10 November 2026 · updated 12 November 2026

Method: read after matching with key from external sources · Extent: partial (35% of the cipher)

With George Lasry (key or decipherment used) and Brice Dudout (identification) · Also credited · Daniel Bourdeau (pointer to a source) · Archivio di Stato di Firenze (pointer to a source)

At a glanceExternal key matched · partial (35% of the cipher)

The letter
Sixteen lines of cipher (787 tokens) with one clear phrase, “Poi un altra volta”; no date, place, address or signature. The addressee is probably Lorenzo de’ Medici; the writer is not identified.
Where
Archivio di Stato di Firenze, Mediceo avanti il Principato, filza XLVIII, doc. 214 (old c. 222), recto. Catalogue entry 2309. Not on DECODE.
The cipher
A substitution with homophones and nulls, in the sign set of Siena no. 11 (Donato Acciaiuoli to Lorenzo de’ Medici, Rome, 1478): the same signs and the same nulls.
How it was read
George Lasry’s key of Siena no. 11 was applied unchanged; it covers 96.4% of the tokens. A test on this letter failed (0.2335 against at most 0.0719); held-out tests on two portions of another letter of the family against its clear copy gave 0.6419 against at most 0.2045 and 0.6519 against at most 0.1854: partial. Two single values derived later, D1 and TRI = che, held on the second, fresh portion (D1 adds little to the agreement: 0.6519 with D1 against 0.6390 without).
What it says
Condottieri and their contracts, Count Antonio and something he had in Venice, Count Carlo, and a sum of “uindici milia ducati” (15,000 ducats).
Still open
About 55–65% of the letter; 13 sign forms not in Lasry’s key (and PIBAR = n and three proposed nulls untested); the clear copy used for the tests is not published, so they cannot be repeated by others; the date, writer and addressee.
Credit
The key is George Lasry’s. Brice Dudout (L’Europe chiffrée) recognised the cipher, transcribed the letter, tested the key and read it in part, working with AI agents. The catalogue entry and its vetting are Daniel Bourdeau’s.

01 The letter

Doc. 214 of filza XLVIII of the Mediceo avanti il Principato is a single leaf: sixteen lines of cipher on the recto, one phrase in clear in line 3 (“Poi un altra volta”) and an illegible closing paraph; the verso is blank. No decipherment is on the leaf or filed with it. It was transcribed sign by sign from the archive’s image at 1272 px, the largest the viewer gives, using the sign names of the Siena no. 11 transcription and new names for forms not found there. The images were used for private research and are not reproduced here.

The sign set is that of Siena no. 11 (Concistoro 2308, DECODE R4800), including its nulls. Compared before any decoding, the keys at hand cover the tokens of this letter as follows: Siena no. 11 96.4%, MAP LXXII 183 65.3%, the Dieci key of 1414 49.4%, MAP CXIX 117 39.0%.

02 The key and its tests

The key is George Lasry’s for Siena no. 11, written in sign names with no value changed; where a sign has two values in his figure, the one with the larger count was taken. Signs not in his key (2.8% of the tokens, 3.6% before TRI was added) give nothing. Two derived values are used in the reading. D1 (“PLUS ODAG” read as one sign = c) was proposed after the first test from decodings such as “hon / honte” where “con / conte” is expected; TRI (a triangle) = che was one of several candidates drawn from material already seen. Both were untested on 10 November 2026 and held on 12 November on a portion not used before (third test below). The key as a whole stays partial.

  • On this letter: failed. One measurement on lines 6–16 (534 tokens), the share of letters falling in Italian words of five letters or more: 0.2335 against at most 0.0719 for 200 shuffled keys, a margin of 0.16, under the 0.30 required. The same script on Siena no. 11 itself gives only 0.396, so the measure is harsh, but the verdict stands.
  • Held out on XLVIII 294: partial. XLVIII 294 (Bernardo Rucellai, February 1485) uses the same sign family and has a clear copy of the time at c. 276. One measurement on c. 306v lines 1–9 (339 tokens): letter agreement 0.6419 against at most 0.2045 for 200 shuffled keys, a margin of 0.437; the margin passes, 0.85 is not reached. The portion has no “PLUS ODAG”, so D1 was not exercised.
  • Held again on XLVIII 294, c. 306r: partial for the key, D1 and TRI held. On 12 November 2026, c. 306r lines 1–16 (411 tokens, never transcribed, decoded or measured before) against the upper part of the clear copy c. 276r, with the keys, statistics and thresholds hashed before the clear was read, by the contributor’s record: letter agreement 0.6519 against at most 0.1854 for 200 shuffled keys (margin 0.467; Lasry’s key alone 0.6390), partial again. D1: 5 of the 6 occurrences of “PLUS ODAG” fall on a c in the clear, p = 0.005 against 200 random-position controls: held. TRI = che: 4 of 5, p = 0.005: held. PLUS alone = o did not hold (0 of 3; two of the three fall on c, an observation made after the measurement); PIBAR = n has no occurrence; the proposed nulls cannot be tested this way. Declared limits: the transcriber knew the key and the candidates, the upper c. 276r had been looked at for anchors, and the clear copy is not published, so no one else can repeat the test.

The two decodings were re-run here from the key files and the transcriptions: the XLVIII 294 test portion gives the same 313 letters, and the decoding of XLVIII 214 agrees with the published one except for a few signs between dots.

03 The reading

Read word by word on the decoding with D1 and TRI (12 November 2026; on 10 November, with D1 alone: 39 / 13 / 11 / 45): 46 certain words, 13 probable, 11 with restored letters, and 46 unresolved stretches. The certain and probable words cover about 35% of the 683 decoded letters: about 35% read. A further 10% rests on restored letters, which are holes. TRI adds six che. In the fragments below, {braces} mark probable words, [brackets] restored letters and ‹…› unresolved stretches.

‹…› {el} conte antonio che egli haueua ‹ma…› in vinegia ‹…› {col} conte ca[r]lo ‹…› si ‹…› non o[b]tenend[o] ‹…› {la libe[ra]tione} ‹…› con docta ‹…› uindici milia ducati {i[n]} diciotto ‹…›

What reads is a matter of condottieri and their contracts (“conducti”): Count Antonio and something he “had” in Venice (“conte antonio che egli haueua”, with che from TRI), Count Carlo, a sum of 15,000 ducats and the word “diciotto”; further on a “liberatione”, an “intentione” of “el signore”, “non potendo”, “utile a questo”, “in questo modo”. These are fragments, not the sense of the whole letter.

The date is a hypothesis only. If Count Carlo is Carlo Fortebraccio (died 1479) and Count Antonio is Antonio da Marsciano, the letter falls about 1478–79, in the Pazzi war; otherwise about 1482–85, in the War of Ferrara (the filza holds papers of 1482–83).

04 What remains open

  • About 55–65% of the letter: 46 unresolved stretches and 11 restored words. The text decodes with the key but long stretches do not read as words, and 13 sign forms are not in Lasry’s key.
  • PLUS alone aligned twice on c in the 12 November test, after the pre-registered value (o) failed; another letter of the family with a clear is needed (XLVIII 294 is used up, XLVIII 158 is too short). The clear copy of c. 276 is not published.
  • The key is partial for this family (0.6419 and 0.6519 against 0.85), and the transcription was made at 1272 px; a higher-resolution image from the archive would settle doubtful forms.
  • Date, writer and addressee: nothing is written on the leaf, and the names read do not settle the date.

## Date, writer, addressee (from the content only; nothing is written on the leaf)
- **Addressee**: probably Lorenzo de' Medici (the fonds; "el signore"; the polite "vedete").
- **Writer**: not identified. The letter uses the cipher of Siena no. 11 (Donato Acciaiuoli, Rome, 1478), which Bernardo Rucellai
  also uses in February 1485 (XLVIII 294): a Medici chancery cipher handed to more than one envoy. The closing paraph is illegible.
- **Date**: undated. Hints: "conte Antonio", "conte Carlo", Venice, 15,000 ducats, "condutti" (condotte). If conte Carlo is Carlo
  Fortebraccio da Montone (d. 1479) and conte Antonio is Antonio da Marsciano, the letter would fall about 1478-79 (Pazzi war);
  otherwise 1482-85 (War of Ferrara; Daniel's catalogue dates the filza 1482-83). **A hypothesis, not established.**

# Translation (English summary): undated cipher letter, ASF MAP XLVIII 214 (catalogue 2309), read in part

An undated, unaddressed cipher letter, most likely to Lorenzo de' Medici, written in the cipher of Siena no. 11 (George Lasry's
key). The text reads only in fragments: a matter of condottieri and their contracts ("conducti"), Count Antonio and Count Carlo;
something Count Antonio had (done or held) in Venice; a sum of fifteen thousand ducats and the word "eighteen" (of what is not read); a "liberation";
an intention attributed to "the lord"; "not being able to", "useful to this", "in this way". A single phrase is written in the
clear: "Poi un altra volta" ("Then another time").

This is a summary of the fragments that read, not a translation: about 35-45 % of the letter is read (12 Nov 2026), and nothing here should be
taken as the sense of the whole. Readings are marked word by word in `reading/xlviii214.md`.

## Riassunto (italiano)
Lettera cifrata senza data né indirizzo, probabilmente a Lorenzo de' Medici, nel cifrario di Siena n. 11 (chiave di George Lasry).
Si leggono solo frammenti: condotte di capitani, il conte Antonio e il conte Carlo, Venezia, quindicimila ducati, una
"liberatione", un'intenzione attribuita al "signore". Un solo passo in chiaro: "Poi un altra volta". Lettura parziale (35-45 %).

Key: K_SIENA11.tsv, K_SIENA11_D1_TRI.tsv
