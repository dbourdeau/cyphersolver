Mary Queen of Scots to the Duke of Norfolk, “the 20th”, 1570

The letter
Mary Queen of Scots, at Tutbury, to the Duke of Norfolk in the Tower, dated only “the tventi of this instant”, 1570; one of four of her private-cipher letters to him kept together.
Where
British Library, Cotton MS Caligula C II f. 74r. Unlike the other three, it has no contemporary decipherment and none in print.
The cipher
Mary’s private cipher to Norfolk, with nomenclator signs.
How it was read
Satoshi Tomokiyo rebuilt the key from the three deciphered siblings and decoded about the first half. Here the whole letter was transcribed and decrypted with his key, on new images from the British Library’s IIIF service. No key was recovered here.
What it says
Mary tells Norfolk to write to the B[ishop of Ross] of your part and thanks God for delyvring us of our [enemy] Murray. She ends yours feithful til death.
Still open
Five nomenclator signs have no value in the key. Two groups in line 1, the end of line 4 under a stain and a few single words are unread.
Credit
Much of the letter-level decoding is Tomokiyo’s; new here are the transcription, the continuous decipherment, the fill of his gaps, the names and the date.

01 The letter and its listing

Cotton MS Caligula C II is a volume of Scottish state papers from Burghley’s circle. Folios 66 to 75 hold Mary’s ciphered letters to Norfolk that were intercepted or surrendered, with deciphered copies. Tomokiyo’s cryptiana page on Mary’s ciphers (mary.htm) lists them this way:

FolioDate on the letterDeciphermentIn print
f. 66r“last of Januarie” [31 Jan 1570]transcription f. 67, decipherment f. 72Labanoff iii p. 19
f. 69“the xv of this instant” [15 Jan 1570]f. 70Labanoff iii p. 11
f. 74r“the tventi of this instant”nonenone found
f. 75[18 Apr 1570]clear text f. 73, roughly correspondingLabanoff iii p. 35

Tomokiyo lists f. 74 as having no contemporary decipherment; he does not list it among open ciphers. He found no matching letter in Labanoff’s Lettres de Marie Stuart, vol. 3, or in Froude, and took the address my oun good lord as a sign that it too is from Mary to Norfolk. He then reconstructed the key from the deciphered letters (“Mary-Norfolk Cipher (1570)”, corrected 16 July 2023). With it he printed the first two lines as a paraphrase, “I have, my own good lord, your friendly [letter??] [with??] no [g?]entle satisfaction and for * I will never [do??] anything in hand …”, and left two further pieces of work that are not on the visible page. One is an HTML comment in the page source: a continuous transcript as far as about line 11 (… in? deliveri[n]g us of our * the Queen of England …). The other is an image, mary_CottonCaligulaCII_f74.png, with his decode written in magenta over each of the 23 lines, letter by letter. Unkeyed signs are shown as asterisks, doubtful letters as queries, and words are not divided.

That overlay is the starting point, and it has to be credited. For most lines it already has the letters, including murton is gon to, Talbo and herkebus. This page adds the decipherment as continuous text. The letters are divided into words and made into sentences, and many of his asterisks and queries are filled. Line 8, which the comment leaves out when it runs and I shall be your sister scrup from the end of line 7 into line 9, is restored. The names are identified and the letter is dated.

The verso, f. 74v, has no address. It carries only a short note written upside down relative to the text, with a mark above it; the note says Norffolke (or Northfolke).

02 The images

The British Library’s new viewer publishes the volume as a IIIF manifest (bl.digirati.io/iiif/ark:/81055/vdc_100162985756.0x000001, reached from the Search Archives record for Caligula C II). The image service claims IIIF level 2 but will not return an image more than about 1,024 pixels on a side. The full leaf is 7,144 × 10,328 pixels, so fetch.py and region.py ask for 1,024-pixel tiles and paste them back together. The text block of f. 74r is the region from x 1000 to 6500 and y 4900 to 9800, at full resolution. strips.py finds the centre of each line in five vertical bands from the ink profile and cuts 23 strips that follow the slope of each line, so a line can be examined at magnification from end to end.

03 The key

The cipher is a simple substitution of one sign for each letter, with no homophones except f. Nulls are scattered through the text, even inside words. About twenty word signs and a handful of name signs complete it. This is Tomokiyo’s reconstruction. Before it was applied to f. 74, it was checked against f. 66r, whose contemporary transcription and decipherment survive (ff. 67, 72). The opening of f. 66r, My oun lord, I vret to you befor to knou your pleasur if I schould sik to mak an entrepryse, decrypts correctly with it.

F. 74 adds three uses the table does not state. ǂ standing alone is and; inside a word it is still k (kan, spok, lyk, York). The pair εʃ is me. A б with a bar through it is friend, besides the ♁ form. There are no signs for j, q, w or z. The writer uses v for w (vreit, veil, vel or vo), which is Scots spelling, not a gap in the cipher.

{blot} I trust God he [?will] help us, as he began […] already in delivering us of our {o—} [?Murray].

The Queen of England has spoken […] courteously to the Bishop of Ross of you […] showed; and if I would cause her friend to be shot with a harquebus-shot, it would not hurt me, God be judge between us.

I am assured that she [?gives] me the blame of Murray's death {ø}; but God knows if […] her mind would […], at least you know the truth of this.

God will, I trust, prosper us both, the better that we […] {Ш—} […]les.

The rumour is that Morton is gone to […]; but not for truth I write [?one] this: tonight Lord Talbot {∂} [told] me, not for truth, it comes from York.

[…] I will end, and pray you not to constrain yourself or hazard yourself to write yourself, but cause {⊗}, but write to any other in your name, [so long as] you are well; and remember your own.

I desire no more, for if I hear nothing, I fear ever that some tales make you offended at me.

I shall, God willing, never make cause, but since the last […] I am ever in doubt of the like.

I pray him to preserve you from feigned friends, and send us more comfort; an[d] [?yet] we have had in this meantime, in weal or woe, I shall ever remain yours faithful till death.

{Mary}. The twentieth of this instant.

The names. The B. of Ross is John Leslie, bishop of Ross, Mary’s ambassador in London. Your sister Scrup is Norfolk’s sister Margaret Howard, wife of Henry, Lord Scrope of Bolton, who had been Mary’s keeper at Bolton in 1568. Murray is James Stewart, Earl of Moray, Regent of Scotland, and Murton is James Douglas, 4th Earl of Morton, the leading man of the King’s party after him. Lord Talbot is the courtesy title of the Earl of Shrewsbury’s eldest son, and Shrewsbury was Mary’s keeper. The cardinal is not named; her uncle Charles, Cardinal of Lorraine, is the obvious candidate but the leaf does not say so. Veyt is Scots wyte, blame, and brut is bruit, a rumour. In for and I hear nothing, and means if. The re-reading of 23 October 2026 found that the sign before of your pert is a single φ, not the key’s sign for the Bishop of Ross, so the first mention is [?b.]; the Bishop is named by his title only in the B. of Ross of you.

05 Dating

The letter gives only the day, the tventi of this instant, without a month or place. The content fixes it closely enough:

  • After Moray’s murder. The Regent was shot with a harquebus at Linlithgow on 23 January 1570 by James Hamilton of Bothwellhaugh and died the same night. The letter trusts God for delyvring us of our {o—} [?Murray], speaks of Murray death, and has Elizabeth talking of whether I would caus schut her friend a harkebus-schut. So the 20th is not 20 January.
  • Elizabeth already knows, and blames Mary. Labanoff’s chronology puts Elizabeth’s receipt of the news, and her placing of the Bishop of Ross in the Bishop of London’s custody, on 10 February.
  • Norfolk in the Tower. He was there from October 1569 to August 1570, which fits the warnings about writing and the talk of forces and meanes. Mary’s letters of January to April 1570 printed by Labanoff are dated from Tutbury.

That leaves 20 February or 20 March 1570. One point leans towards February without settling it. Labanoff prints (iii, pp. 31–33) a decipherment of Mary to Norfolk dated the 19th of march which opens I have forborn this long time to write to you, in respect of the dangers of writing and grieves for Pembroke, who died on 17 March. A letter written the next day that says nothing of Pembroke would be odd. So would one written a day after she said she had not written for a long time. F. 74 says neither.

06 What remains uncertain

  • Thirteen unkeyed signs, each once. ⊕ (l. 2), ⊔ (l. 4), ∨ (l. 6), ⊘ and o° (l. 8), rʒ (l. 9), ø (l. 13), ⊗ (l. 17), ∂ after Lord Talbot (l. 16), three in line 1, and a family of letter signs with a long tail-dash ({a—} l. 5, {o—} l. 11, {Ш—} l. 15) that looks like a class of word signs. Our {o—} Murray would fit enemy by sense only; no value was adopted. Folios 66, 69 and 75, in the same cipher, have not yet been searched for them.
  • Open spots. The opening sentence, the experiance sentence (l. 4, under a stain), lines 10–11 and line 15 do not build. Legible runs in lines 11 (after spokn), 15 (after ve), 17 and 20 are transcribed but not resolved (texts/f74r_signs.tsv). Demen in line 7 is what the signs carry, but its sense is not secure.
  • Print. The Calendar of State Papers, Scotland, vol. iii (Boyd, 1903) was searched on 23 October 2026: it calendars the sibling letters but not f. 74.
  • Word division and sense. The signs run on without spaces and nulls stand inside words, so the division into words is an editorial choice. In lines 5–6 and 9–10 the sentences are construed as closely as the words allow, and the paraphrase is one interpretation of them.
  • The month. February or March 1570, as set out above. Nothing on the leaf decides between them.
  • The hand. Tomokiyo remarks that the hand of the Norfolk ciphers looks like Mary’s own but differs in the h and in spelling from her autograph letters, and suggests comparing it with her secretary Raulet’s. That was not attempted here.

## Notes

Key values adopted: none. No unkeyed sign could be given a value that holds at every occurrence on this page; each
occurs once (⊕ l.2, ⊔ l.4, ∨ l.6, ⊘ l.8, o° l.8, rʒ l.9, ø l.13, ⊗ l.17) or twice-to-three times in a related family
({a—} l.5, {o—} l.11, {Ш—} l.15: a letter sign with a long horizontal tail-dash). The tail-dash family looks like a
nomenclator class (letter + dash = a word); "our {o—} Murray" would fit "enemy", "your {a—} and myn friends" would
fit "servants/friends" — sense-only, not adopted. ff. 66/69/75 were not searched for these signs in this pass.

Flagged (sense-only or shape-uncertain):
- l.1 "{3̲2}" (3 over 2 with underline) and "{ꝗ} {ʃ̊}" after "friendli": unkeyed; the 22 Oct reading left them open too.
  "lentle" (4 8 a X 4 8): the first sign is clearly 4 (l), not A (g); the old "[g]entle" was an emendation. Kept as
  [?lentle] (= gentle by sense only).
- l.4 "{⊔}": a heavy u-shape with base bar after "experiance", not in the key. "[?favour]": the first sign under the stain
  is not legible; the old "[f]avour" kept as partly carried.
- l.5 "[?ues]" for "use" (ρ 8 H, stained); "a vey kan" (ω, ρ 8 7, ǂ ω a) replaces the old "a way can".
- l.7 "demen" (∠ 8 + 8 a) replaces the old supplied "be not [lost]": the signs carry "demen" (= Scots "demean"?,
  sense not secure); "[?b.]" one sign φ after "the" (the old "B[ishop of Ross]" is not carried; the Ross sign IV is not
  there).
- l.8 "the […] friendes": a blotted X̸ and 2-3 signs under the stain.
- l.10 "{ǂ double-barred}" read [?will] (4̶ expected); "begen […]": 3 signs under a blot.
- l.11 "spokn […]": after "spok" two nulls and "a 8 ρ a" (n e v n) — not resolved; "[?Murray]": a blotted L-like sign.
- l.13 "[?geves]": first sign under a crossing line; "that veyt": the sign is ᵹ (that), not X̸ (the).
- l.15 the run after "ve" (8 a Π б 2 X б ρ ω a {Ш—} H + ω ρ ∂ 4 8 H) is not read.
- l.16 "{∂}" after "Talbot": a ∂-shaped word sign (your?) that makes no sense as "your".
- l.22 first sign: long ſ with crossbar, not the key's 7 (y); "yet" rests on sense.
- ll.21-23 tokens were checked against the strips but more summarily than ll.1-17.

Look-alike letters: 3 of 1493 letters (0.2 %): l.6 the first sign of "mynded" (ħ-like, read as m-letter group),
l.10 the double-barred ǂ read as the will sign, l.22 the crossed ſ read as y. Total letters counted with
`decode` column of `f74r_signs.tsv`, word signs expanded.

07 Sources

  • British Library, Cotton MS Caligula C II, ff. 66r, 69r, 74r–v, 75r: IIIF manifest https://bl.digirati.io/iiif/ark:/81055/vdc_100162985756.0x000001, from the Search Archives record 040-001102379.
  • S. Tomokiyo, “Mary Queen of Scots’ ciphers”, Cotton MS Caligula C II section (cryptiana): the key (“Mary-Norfolk Cipher (1570)”, corrected 16 July 2023), the listing of ff. 66–75, and the overlay decode of f. 74.
  • A. Labanoff, Lettres, instructions et mémoires de Marie Stuart, vol. 3 (1844): the Norfolk letters of 15 and 31 January, 19 March and 18 April 1570, and the chronology of 1570. F. 74 is not among them.
  • Calendar of State Papers, Scotland, vol. 3 (1903), no. 520: Cecil’s “Extract out of the lettres in ciphre 1570, 1571” with the letters of 15 January, the last of January and 18 April appended. It calendars no letter of the 20th.

The notes with the key and the decipherment (NOTES.md), the IIIF manifest, and the tile-stitching and line-strip scripts (fetch.py, region.py, strips.py) are in the repository (targets/norfolk1570/). The images are not committed and can be fetched again with the scripts.
