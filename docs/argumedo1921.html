Mexico City → Félix Díaz · two-digit letter table · 5 and 20 October 1921 · deciphered in part with Narváez’s table

Argumedo to Félix Díaz, October 1921

Go to the full text ↓

Two felicista letters hide names in number pairs; Narváez published the table and ten runs, and the other 25 runs, deciphered with his table, tell of a Michoacán landowner’s armed men and a colonel’s commission.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 25 November 2026

Method: known key applied · Extent: partial (82% of cipher tokens valued, not the word count)

The letters
Cristóbal Mateo Argumedo, who signs “Don Mateo” and “Argumedo” in cipher, to General Félix Díaz, from Mexico City, 5 October 1921 (typed) and 20 October 1921 (handwritten in ink). Clear Spanish, with names and key words in number pairs.
Where
CEHM-Carso (Centro de Estudios de Historia de México, Fundación Carlos Slim), Fondo DCXXI, Archivo de Félix Díaz, DCXXI.3.244.1 and DCXXI.3.250.2, two folios each. No DECODE record found.
The cipher
One two-digit number for each letter of the 27-letter Spanish alphabet: E = 01 … Z = 23 (Ñ = 11), then A = 34, B = 35, C = 36, D = 37.
How it was deciphered
With Roberto Narváez’s published table (2015), applied unchanged. On the ten runs whose clear he printed, it gives 88 of 89 letters. The 25 runs he did not print were then deciphered and read in their sentences.
What it says
A Michoacán landowner, through his son, offers his whole hacienda and a guerrilla of 80 to 100 armed men; the son asks for a nombramiento to win over the peons of Panindícuaro. On 20 October the document is granted “con el grado de coronel”.
Still open
Seven words of the new runs carry a writer’s slip of one code, an overstrike, or a letter of uncertain sense; each is taken from the sense. Whether Narváez’s 2019 book prints these runs could not be checked.
Credit
Contributed by Brice Dudout, with AI agents, in pull request #962. Key, system and the clear of ten runs: Narváez (2015). The CEHM-Carso viewer path came from Andrew Beling’s pull request #941.

01 The two letters

Both letters are in the Archivo de Félix Díaz at CEHM-Carso. The first, DCXXI.3.244.1, is typed on two folios, dated “Octubre 5 de 1921”, addressed in cipher to “Señor Gral. Díaz”, signed in cipher “Don Mateo”, and carries a received stamp “DEC 31 ANS’D”. The second, DCXXI.3.250.2, is dated “Oct 20/21” and signed “Argumedo” in cipher. Narváez describes it as typed, but the images show a pen hand in ink. Pencil letters above some groups of its first folio, and a pencil “C. M. Argumedo” under the signature, are in a later hand of unknown date; they are not used here.

“Don Mateo” is Cristóbal Mateo Argumedo, as Narváez identifies him. The letters are in clear Spanish, with names and a few key words in two-digit pairs: 26 runs in the first letter, 9 in the second.

The images are on the CEHM-Carso public viewer (links under Sources). The CEHM site says “Todos los Derechos Reservados” and publishes no terms of use, so no image is reproduced here; the strip at the top is typed from the transcription.

02 The table

Each letter of the Spanish alphabet, Ñ included, has one two-digit number. The table starts at E: E = 01, F = 02, G = 03 and so on to N = 10, Ñ = 11, O = 12 … Z = 23. A, B, C and D come last, as 34, 35, 36 and 37. The numbers 24 to 33 do not occur in these two letters. The writer divides the runs into words with “.-”, “.” or “:” in the first letter and “=” in the second; in A06, A16 and A25 the words run together.

Narváez broke the system and printed the table (Documenta & Instrumenta 13, 2015, p. 122), with the clear of ten runs. He wrote that the table reads the rest, but cut the first letter with “[…]” and gave the four runs of the second letter’s second folio in cipher only. The table is used by no other target in this repository: the felicista letters of Duhart (a swap of five letter pairs) and Sánchez (Narváez’s key 313) are in other systems, with their own pages, duhart1921 and sanchez1922.

03 How it was deciphered

  • The four page images were fetched from the CEHM-Carso viewer, and all 35 runs were transcribed by an AI model. The transcription and the key were frozen by SHA-256 before any decoding. The hashed working files are kept by the contributor and are not in the repository.
  • Control: the ten runs whose clear Narváez prints were decoded with his table. 88 of 89 letters agree. The one miss is the writer’s: PELIGRO is enciphered 13-01-08-05-03-15-10, ending in N, and Narváez corrects it silently. No null-key test was run, since the key is a printed one applied unchanged, not built from these letters.
  • The 25 runs Narváez did not print were decoded with the same table and read in their clear sentences. Every departure from the key’s output is marked in the reading.
  • Key replay: the table applied to the two transcriptions reproduces the reading with every difference marked. Of 49 cipher words as the transcription divides them, 41 come out exactly and 8 are marked emendations; one overstruck pair has no value.
  • The integration review of the pull request, by an AI agent for the maintainers, reproduced both decryptions with its own decoder, recounted the words, compared the runs with the images (A01 to A26 and B05 to B09) and read both letters as Spanish sentences: grade B. No person has checked the transcription or the reading.

04 The reading

The runs, in bold, in their sentences. Braces mark a word the signs do not give as written: {x/y}, the signs give x and the reading is y. The full readings, translations and transcriptions are under Full texts.

5 October 1921

Argumedo first mocks the festivities that “la horda reinante” held with the people’s money, and “la turba de A{?/G}ua Prieta”. Then the business:

El Señor {H.} Heredia, que me ayudó en el caso de Ben{e/i}tez, escribió sobre nuestro negocio a un rico hacendado del Estado de Michoacán […] dicho caballero, que se llama {D/A}lfredo Gutiérrez, muy satisfecho y entusiasmado, me contestó: «Diga Ud al Sr Gral. D{e/i}az que mi padre […] pone a su disposición su hacienda entera y una guerrilla de 80 a 100 hombres que ha logrado armar y los tiene enteramente listos.» Sólo me pidió este Sr un nombramiento que le acredite personalidad para conseguir como nuestros a los peones del distrito de Panindícuaro (donde está su hacienda) y circunvecinos.

Mr H. Heredia, who helped me in the Benítez affair, wrote about our business to a rich landowner of the State of Michoacán […] that gentleman, whose name is Alfredo Gutiérrez, very satisfied and enthusiastic, answered me: “Tell General Díaz that my father […] puts at his disposal his whole hacienda and a guerrilla band of 80 to 100 men that he has managed to arm and keeps entirely ready.” This gentleman asked me only for an appointment giving him standing to win over to our side the peons of the district of Panindícuaro (where his hacienda is) and the neighbouring ones.

The letter also names, in cipher, General Vicente Benítez, whom Argumedo had put in safety with funds he raised, and his son Cástulo (both printed by Narváez); Argumedo’s delegates Mata and Montaño; Lic. Mena, through whom he tried for the appointment “ya que a don Miguel es tan difícil verlo”; and Juan T. de Arana. It ends “El momento es nuestro, Sr don Féli{z/x}”.

20 October 1921

The second letter warns of the danger, the work of “nuestro buen amigo Don P{d/a}ncho y socios” (runs printed by Narváez), and then answers the first:

Ya por fin, a dale y dale, obtuve el documento que a Don Miguel pedí para la persona de Panindícuaro de quien le hablé en mi anterior, y el cual se le concedió con el grado de coronel.

At last, by dint of insisting, I obtained the document that I asked of Don Miguel for the person from Panindícuaro of whom I spoke to you in my last, and which was granted to him with the rank of colonel.

Agua Prieta stands for the Sonoran group of Obregón and Calles, after the Plan of Agua Prieta of April 1920, and “la horda reinante” for Obregón’s government; Panindícuaro is a municipality of Michoacán. These identifications are the contributor’s notes. “Don Miguel” and Lic. Mena are not identified.

05 Counts and what is open

Words are counted over the cipher words only, and a word counts as read when the key gives it as written. Slips are counted as holes: the table is Narváez’s reconstruction, not a surviving key of the period, so the one-digit rule does not apply.

  • 5 October (DCXXI.3.244.1): 38 cipher words, 31 read as the key gives them, 7 marked (0.816): partial, under the 95% bar. Its 21 new runs: 27 words, 20 read.
  • 20 October (DCXXI.3.250.2): 12 cipher words, 10 read, 2 holes, both in Narváez’s control runs (PELIGR{N/O}, P{D/A}NCHO): 0.833. It counts as read under the short-text floor (fewer than 40 cipher words, at most 2 holes). Its 4 new runs: 5 words, 5 read.
  • Both letters: 41 of 50 cipher words (0.82), under the 95% bar, so the extent is partial. The 25 new runs: 25 of 32 words. Counted this way, AGUA PRIETA is two words and the “H.” before Heredia is a hole; the key replay above counts AGUA PRIETA as one cipher word and the H as exact, hence its 41 of 49.

The seven marked words of the new runs:

  • A{?/G}ua Prieta (A04): the second pair is a 0 with 6 and 3 typed one over the other. 03 gives Agua Prieta; 06, the figure Narváez read, gives no word.
  • Ben{e/i}tez twice (A08, A11) and D{e/i}az (A17): 01 (E) written for 05 (I). At A11 the review looked at the page at full size: the 01 is on the page, the writer’s slip.
  • {D/A}lfredo (A16): 37 (D) for 34 (A), the same slip as Narváez’s “Pdncho”, which the first letter writes “Pancho” in clear.
  • Féli{z/x} (A24): 23 (Z) for 21 (X), or the writer’s spelling.
  • {H.} before Heredia (A10): typed “04:”; it may be an initial or a repeated keystroke.

No other copy of either letter was found, so nothing can turn these words into words the key gives as written. The Amaya letter of the same fondo (DCXXI.3.242.1, Havana, 27 September 1921), the one other felicista letter noted with two-digit numbers, was looked at on 25 November 2026: its pairs run to 79, 86, 90, 96 and 97, outside this table, so it is another system.

Narváez 2019. His book Criptografía diplomática, política y militar en México (1813–1926) (SRE, 2019) could not be opened or searched: Google Books has no preview and no search inside for it. A review in Historia Mexicana says its chapters were published before, 2007 to 2015, and modified for the book. Whether its pages on Argumedo print more than the 2015 article is not known. If they print these runs, the method for them becomes “read from existing decipherment”.

   that concerns our enterprise; that gentleman, whose name is **{D/A}lfredo Gutiérrez**, very satisfied and
   enthusiastic, answered me: 'Tell Mr **Gen. D{e/i}az** that my father, who esteems and admires him, from this moment
   puts at his disposal his whole hacienda and a **guerrilla band** of 80 to 100 men that he has managed to **arm**
   and keeps entirely ready.'"
9. "This gentleman asked me only for an **appointment** (nombramiento) giving him standing to win over to our side the
   peons of the district of **Panindícuaro** (where his hacienda is) and the neighbouring ones."
10. "And for this I made use of Lic. **Mena**, since don **Miguel** is so hard to see, and as always, I got nothing!"
11. "The moment is ours, don **Féli{z/x}**."
12. "Mr **Juan T. de Arana** and other good friends who send you their greetings are ready and prompt for the work."
13. Signature: "And I, with respectful affection and as always, at your disposal. **Don Mateo**."

Argumedo to Félix Díaz, 20 Oct 1921: 9 cipher runs

The danger threatening the felicistas is laid at the door of 'Don Pancho and partners'; the document asked of Don Miguel for the man from Panindícuaro was granted with the rank of colonel.

# Reading of DCXXI.3.250.2 (Argumedo to Félix Díaz, 20 October 1921)

<!-- measure: cipher_markup=** -->

Reading by an AI model (Claude, claude-opus-5-5) for Brice Dudout, 25 Nov 2026. No person has checked it yet.
Key: `key.tsv` (Narváez 2015, p. 122, applied unchanged). Mechanical decryption: `decryption_DCXXI.3.250.2.txt`.
Narváez prints the clear of the f. 1 runs and the signature (controls) and the four f. 2 runs B05-B08 in cipher only;
B05-B08 are the new part. The letter is handwritten in ink (Narváez calls it typed).

Marks: `{x/y}` the signs give x, read y (a slip of one code by the writer). Marked words count as holes. Accents are
added in the sentences; they are not departures.

## Marked runs

```marked
B01: GRAL DIAZ
B02: PELIGR{N/O}
B03: DON P{D/A}NCHO
B04: SOCIOS
B05: DON MIGUEL
B06: PANINDICUARO
B07: GRADO
B08: CORONEL
B09: ARGUMEDO
```

B02 ends in 10 (N) where the sense wants 12 (O); Narváez prints "peligro" without comment. B03 is printed "Pdncho"
by Narváez; the letter of 5 October has "don Pancho" in clear.

## The runs in their sentences

Cipher runs in bold, clear text as written with accents added.

1. f. 1, address: "Sr **Gral. Díaz**."
2. f. 1: "Confirmo en todas sus partes mi anterior 5 del actual, y aunque de ella todavía no tengo respuesta, me
   apresuro a dirigirle ésta para llamar su atención sobre el **peligr{n/o}** que nos amenaza según verá por el
   recorte incluso, pues ya Ud comprenderá que en todo esto va la mano de nuestro buen amigo **Don P{d/a}ncho** y
   **socios**."
3. f. 2: "Ya por fin, a dale y dale, obtuve el documento que a **Don Miguel** pedí para la persona de
   **Panindícuaro** de quien le hablé en mi anterior, y el cual se le concedió con el **grado** de **coronel**."
4. f. 2, signature: "Lo saluda con respeto y cariñoso afecto su siempre leal **Argumedo**."

## Notes

- Sentence 3 answers the letter of 5 October (DCXXI.3.244.1): the *nombramiento* asked there for the son of the
  Michoacán landowner (Alfredo Gutiérrez, district of Panindícuaro), through Lic. Mena because "Don Miguel" was hard
  to see, was granted by Don Miguel with the rank of colonel.
- Counts (cipher words of this letter): 12 cipher words, 10 read as the key gives them, 2 marked (PELIGRO, PANCHO,
  both in Narváez's control runs): read share 10/12 = 0.833; under the README short-text floor (fewer than 40 cipher
  words, at most 2 holes) the letter counts as read. The new runs B05-B08: 5 words, 5 read.
