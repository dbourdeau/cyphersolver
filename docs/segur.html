Béarn · numerical cipher: homophonic alphabet and syllable table in alphabetical order · 1585–86

Henry of Navarre to Ségur

October 1585, February 1586, 1 April 1586 · Bibliothèque nationale de France, Cinq cents de Colbert 401, ff. 233, 239, 288v

Three letters to the envoy negotiating a German army, listed by Tomokiyo among his unsolved ciphers as letters of Henry III · the key was recovered from the ciphertext alone on the Gallica images, by a solver built on the alphabetical design of the key and checked on matched controls; no earlier decipherment of these three letters was found · the sibling key Tomokiyo reconstructed from f. 321 has different values and reads another leaf, f. 333, which has its own page · George Lasry’s saved decryptions of 15 October 2024 cover these three letters (and f. 143); they are unpublished.

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · complete

The letters
Three letters of 1585–86 from Henry of Navarre to Ségur, who was negotiating with the German princes. They press him to conclude with Duke John Casimir, raise the largest levy possible and march it at once; the 1583 letter at f. 143 is a letter to Ségur.
Where
BnF, Cinq cents de Colbert 401, ff. 233, 239 and 288v (f. 143 also in figures); Gallica ark:/12148/btv1b10035574w.
The cipher
461 figures over 102 distinct values: an alphabetical syllable table in blocks of five (ba be bi bo bu…), letters, doubles, nulls, and a few word-signs written in letters.
How it was read
The blocks-of-five structure was found first. An annealer with the letters’ clear French as context gave a key that came out alphabetical by itself; the same method recovers 97% of a matched control.
What it says
Clervant is to raise two thousand reiters and ten thousand écus; Navarre asks which road the army will take, the Vivarais or Dauphiné; in April 1586 he confesses “extreme anxiety” at having no news.
Credit
S. Tomokiyo’s page lists the four letters and the different key he reconstructed from the interlinear decipherment at f. 321.
Still open
A dozen word-signs written in letters (pi, na, gne, H, L…) and three nomenclature numbers are glossed from context or left open. The 1583 letter at f. 143 is in another key and is not deciphered.

01 The documents

Gallica serves the volume (ark:/12148/btv1b10035574w) over IIIF; the canvases are unlabelled, and the cipher pages sit at canvases 154 (f. 143), 257 (f. 233), 263 (f. 239), 325 (f. 288v, a slip attached to the letter of 1 April 1586) and 359 (f. 321, the key letter with interlinear decipherment). The 1585–86 letters are secretarial copies in one chancery hand: clear French with cipher passages of a few words to fourteen lines, the figures dotted apart, and a handful of tokens written in letters (pi, na, gne, Ra, H, L) or as a small square. Together: 461 figures over 102 distinct values, 25 letter-glyphs.

The 1583 letter at f. 143 turned out to be to Ségur, from a correspondent at Beaupréau, and its clear text describes the system: “vous y avez toutes choses tellement dispersées en lettres, syllabes, doubles, nulles et tout le reste qu’il est impossible de le descouvrir … Les noms sont en lettres et syllabes.” Letters, syllables, doubles, nulls, and names written with letter-signs: that is exactly what the 1585–86 key proves to be. The 1583 letter itself is in a different key, not deciphered: a different cipher, with its own page.

02 Recovering the key

  1. Two ranges. Values 1–53 carry 311 tokens, 54–123 carry 146, and only three exceed 123. Counting the upper range by residue mod 5 gives 60 · 30 · 22 · 20 · 14 for one alignment and near-flat counts for the other four: blocks of five with one dominant position, which is what an alphabetical syllabary (a e i o u, e commonest) produces. The structure was fixed before a single letter was known.
  2. A structured annealer. Each number below the table is a free homophone (22 letters or a null, nulls capped); each block of five is one consonant with the vowel given by position; letter-glyphs are skipped; and the clear French around every cipher passage is kept as fixed context for a 16th-century French 5-gram model (Catherine de Médicis’ letters, Teulet, Labanoff; 9.4 M letters). Moves reassign or swap letter symbols and block consonants. Five of twelve seeds converged on one key.
  3. Controls. Passages of Catherine de Médicis enciphered with random keys of the same shape, the same token counts and the same interleaving of clear text. With the table start guessed at 64 the method recovered one control at 90 % and one at 47 %; with the true structure (start 54, fourteen consonants) it recovers a control at 96.6 % of letter tokens and 12 of 14 blocks, missing only x and f at one token each.
  4. The blind key was already alphabetical. Without being told so, the annealer put a at 4–6, b at 7, c at 9–10, d at 11–12, e at 13–15, g at 18, h at 20–21, i at 22–24, l at 26, m at 27–28, n at 29–30, o at 31, p at 34–35, r at 38–39, s at 40–42, t at 43–44 and u at 45–47. Against the final key it had 86 % of the letter tokens and 77 % of the syllables right even with the table start wrong by two blocks. The decryption gave capituler et conclure promptement avec ceux que vous adviserez … le duc Casimir … faire la levée … la faire marcher … deux mille reistres … dix mil escus … Vivarès ou Daulphiné directly.
  5. Context fixed the rest. et conclure = 52 62 29 9 83 105, secours = 110 62 46 39 42 and le duc Casimir = 80 68 9 59 111 86 38 force 62 = co, 59 = ca, 65 = de; bien = 56 14 29 gives 56 = bi: the table begins at 54 with b c d f g l m n p q r s t v. que vous and qu’il fix 99–103 = qua que qui …; Clervant fixes 119 = va; nece□aire makes the square ss, mi2e makes 2 = ll, lev1 makes 1 = ée; 52 = et, 53 a null. Every digit the French disputed was re-examined at twice the working zoom; two were my errors (90 for 40 in les deux, 63 for 65 in de Clervant), the others stand as written and are noted below.

The rest of the volume, and f. 366 (added later on 16 September 2026)

All 440 canvases of the volume were fetched and examined on contact sheets. Cipher occurs on six leaves only: the four above, Tomokiyo’s ff. 321 and 333, and one leaf not on his list, f. 366, an autograph of Navarre to Ségur from the autumn of 1586 (on a fait avancer l’armée et les forces contre moy … d’user de toute la diligence que vous pourrez pour effectuer ce dont je vous ay chargé) with three short cipher lines at its foot. Its low figures and a letter-written sign gu put it in the ff. 233 key; the last line decrypts as escri, the first two (ri-e-c-h-a-v-f-fe-n, re-de-du-pi-n gu la-que) look like names in the king’s own spelling and are left open. There is no further secretary letter in this key in the volume, so the word-signs stay unglossed. Nine other letters of Navarre in the volume are in clear.

The two leaves Tomokiyo lists under Henry III, ff. 321 and 333, are Navarre’s too, but in the key Tomokiyo rebuilt from the f. 321 interlinear decipherment: the same chancery design with the letters shifted by eight. f. 333 (La Rochelle, 10 July 1586) was decrypted with that key and is a different cipher, with its own page. f. 366 is left here as probably this key, open; it is not counted among the letters deciphered.

05 Context

Jacques de Ségur-Pardaillan was Navarre’s agent in England, Denmark and the Empire from 1583, and Claude-Antoine de Vienne, sieur de Clervant, his colleague for the German levy; Duke John Casimir of the Palatinate was the prince through whom Huguenot armies of reiters had been raised since 1567. Navarre’s October 1585 letter follows the bull of 9 September excommunicating him and the summer campaign that cost him most of his Guyenne places; the “contract with the Churches” and Clervant’s two thousand reiters refer to the standing obligation under which the Huguenot assemblies financed the German horse, and the ten thousand écus from [180] is most naturally the English subsidy (an inference, not a decryption). The February and April 1586 letters belong to the long wait for the army that was finally assembled in 1587 under the baron of Dohna and destroyed at Auneau. The postscript’s Drak is Drake’s West Indies voyage: Santo Domingo fell on 1 January 1586, and the news reached France in February.

06 Method and files

  1. Images from Gallica IIIF (targets/segur/fetch_gallica.py); transcription at full resolution with 2× re-reads (ct_233.txt, ct_239.txt, ct_288.txt; f. 143 is now in targets/segur1583/).
  2. Mod-5 residue test and structured annealer with clear-text context (targets/segur/solve.py).
  3. Matched controls with random keys of the same shape (targets/segur/control.py, eval_control.py).
  4. Key file and renderer (key_v4.json, decode.py); edited reading with token notes (reading.md).
  5. Sweep of the volume (fetch_sweep.py, mksheets.py); f. 366 transcription (ct_366.txt); design-constrained annealer (solve_mono.py; on the ff. 233 set it recovers 287 of 290 letter tokens); word-sign likelihood ranking (gloss_check.py, gloss_check.txt). The f. 333 files are in targets/navarre1586/.

Checked: transcription, structure test, three controls, convergence over seeds, alphabetical order of the recovered key, word-level French of every passage. Not checked: Tomokiyo’s f. 321 table figure by figure against the struck-through leaf; the word-signs; the historical glosses; the second Ségur volume (500 Colbert 402, not digitised); BnF fr. 17820 is an eighteenth-century fair copy in clear. ## f. 233 — [Sainte-Foy?], October 1585

Monsieur de Segur. Je suis en peine de n'avoir point eu de vos nouvelles depuis vostre partement d'Angleterre; je vous en ay
escrit quatre fois … Seulement je vous prieray, si de ça me l'avez fait, **{X} capituler et conclure promptement avec ceux
que vous adviserez,** s'il est possible **avec[?] le duc Casimir ou par son consentement, et faire la levée la plus grande et
la faire marcher** le plus tost que faire se pourra. Car pour que quelque **prompt secours** que **est necessaire**, j'escry à
Mr de Clervant de faire **la levée de deux mille reistres** selon qu'il est **obligé {gla} la contracte[?] avec les Eglises[?]
… et[?]** en l'an **1579[?]**, pour le moings quelque partie s'il ne peult le tout, et s'il n'a le moyen de ce faire, **qu'il
s'aide des dix mil escus de {180}**, ainsy que ledit Sr de Clervant vous dira, a quoy je vous prie tenir la main et que je soye
bien tost adverty de tout ce … du **chemin qu'il tiendra en ce cas**, evitant le danger de le[?] … **chemins le Vivarès ou
Daulphiné** pourroient estre propres, en nous advertissant de bonne heure. Je vous prie mander de mes nouvelles à mon frere
Mons.r le Prince là part où il sera. Nos ennemis ont voullu triompher de la separation … [news of the war in Guyenne and
Languedoc] … Vostre affectionné maistre et parfait amy, Henry.

Token-level notes: `X` opens the passage (a word-sign: *de*?); `4 45 23 20` should be *avec* but reads *a-v-i-h*
(20 = h elsewhere: *marcher*, *chemin*, *Daulphiné*; 10 = c; the encipherer's slip, or 23 20 for 13 10);
`104 20 115` in *contra-h-te* likewise (*contracte*); `13 28 81 110 40` = *e m li se s*, probably *Eglises* with 28 for 18;
`87 52` = *mo et*, unread; `15 79` = *e la* or the year *1579* run together; `101 215` = *qui* + *25* (*l*) with a stray
stroke = *qu'il*; `180` a nomenclature entry, the source of ten thousand écus (the Queen of England? — inference).

### f. 233, October 1585

Monsieur de Ségur. I am troubled at having had no news of you since your departure from England; I have written to you four times … Only I will ask you, if you have not already done so from over there, **{X} to treat and conclude promptly with those you shall judge fit,** if it is possible **with Duke Casimir or by his consent, and to make the largest levy [you can] and have it march** as soon as may be.
