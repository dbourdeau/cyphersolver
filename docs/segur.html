Henry of Navarre to Ségur

October 1585, February 1586, 1 April 1586 · Bibliothèque nationale de France, Cinq cents de Colbert 401, ff. 233, 239, 288v

Three letters to the envoy negotiating a German army, catalogued as undeciphered letters of Henry III, read from the Gallica images with a structured annealer

Daniel Bourdeau · solved 16 September 2026

solved

Summary. S. Tomokiyo’s page lists four letters “of Henry III to Segur” in figures at ff. 143, 233, 239 and 288v of Cinq cents de Colbert 401, in a cipher different from the one he reconstructed from the interlinear decipherment at f. 321. The volume is Ségur-Pardaillan’s négociation pour le roi de Navarre with the German princes, and the three letters of 1585–86 are signed Henry at Montauban and speak of “mon frere Mons.r le Prince” and of the duke Casimir’s levy: they are Henry of Navarre’s. Their 461 figures fall into two ranges. Taken mod 5, the values from 54 up pile into one slot of every five — the signature of an alphabetical syllable table, ba be bi bo bu, ca ce ci co cu … An annealer built on that structure, with the letters’ clear French kept as context for a 5-gram model, converged on a key whose letter part came out in alphabetical order by itself (a 4–6, b 7–8, c 9–10 … u 45–47, x y z 48–51), and the same procedure recovers 97 % of a matched control. The letters press Ségur to conclude with Duke John Casimir, raise the largest levy possible and march it at once; name Clervant’s two thousand reiters and ten thousand écus; ask which road the army will take, the Vivarais or Dauphiné; and, in April 1586, confess “extreme anxiety” at having no news. Status: key and text read; a dozen word-signs written in letters (pi, na, gne, H, L …) and three nomenclature numbers are glossed from context or left open. The 1583 letter at f. 143 is in another key of the same design and is not read.

01 The documents

Gallica serves the volume (ark:/12148/btv1b10035574w) over IIIF; the canvases are unlabelled, and the cipher pages sit at canvases 154 (f. 143), 257 (f. 233), 263 (f. 239), 325 (f. 288v, a slip attached to the letter of 1 April 1586) and 359 (f. 321, the key letter with interlinear decipherment). The 1585–86 letters are secretarial copies in one chancery hand: clear French with cipher passages of a few words to fourteen lines, the figures dotted apart, and a handful of tokens written in letters (pi, na, gne, Ra, H, L) or as a small square. Together: 461 figures over 102 distinct values, 25 letter-glyphs.

The 1583 letter at f. 143 turned out to be to Ségur, from a correspondent at Beaupréau, and its clear text describes the system: “vous y avez toutes choses tellement dispersées en lettres, syllabes, doubles, nulles et tout le reste qu’il est impossible de le descouvrir … Les noms sont en lettres et syllabes.” Letters, syllables, doubles, nulls, and names written with letter-signs: that is exactly what the 1585–86 key proves to be.

02 Solving

  1. Two ranges. Values 1–53 carry 311 tokens, 54–123 carry 146, and only three exceed 123. Counting the upper range by residue mod 5 gives 60 · 30 · 22 · 20 · 14 for one alignment and near-flat counts for the other four: blocks of five with one dominant position, which is what an alphabetical syllabary (a e i o u, e commonest) produces. The structure was fixed before a single letter was known.
  2. A structured annealer. Each number below the table is a free homophone (22 letters or a null, nulls capped); each block of five is one consonant with the vowel given by position; letter-glyphs are skipped; and the clear French around every cipher passage is kept as fixed context for a 16th-century French 5-gram model (Catherine de Médicis’ letters, Teulet, Labanoff; 9.4 M letters). Moves reassign or swap letter symbols and block consonants. Five of twelve seeds converged on one key.
  3. Controls. Passages of Catherine de Médicis enciphered with random keys of the same shape, the same token counts and the same interleaving of clear text. With the table start guessed at 64 the method read one control at 90 % and one at 47 %; with the true structure (start 54, fourteen consonants) it reads a control at 96.6 % of letter tokens and 12 of 14 blocks, missing only x and f at one token each.
  4. The blind key was already alphabetical. Without being told so, the annealer put a at 4–6, b at 7, c at 9–10, d at 11–12, e at 13–15, g at 18, h at 20–21, i at 22–24, l at 26, m at 27–28, n at 29–30, o at 31, p at 34–35, r at 38–39, s at 40–42, t at 43–44 and u at 45–47. Against the final key it had 86 % of the letter tokens and 77 % of the syllables right even with the table start wrong by two blocks. The text read capituler et conclure promptement avec ceux que vous adviserez … le duc Casimir … faire la levée … la faire marcher … deux mille reistres … dix mil escus … Vivarès ou Daulphiné at once.
  5. Reading fixed the rest. et conclure = 52 62 29 9 83 105, secours = 110 62 46 39 42 and le duc Casimir = 80 68 9 59 111 86 38 force 62 = co, 59 = ca, 65 = de; bien = 56 14 29 gives 56 = bi: the table begins at 54 with b c d f g l m n p q r s t v. que vous and qu’il fix 99–103 = qua que qui …; Clervant fixes 119 = va; nece□aire makes the square ss, mi2e makes 2 = ll, lev1 makes 1 = ée; 52 = et, 53 a null. Every digit the French disputed was re-read at twice the working zoom; two were my errors (90 for 40 in les deux, 63 for 65 in de Clervant), the others stand as written and are noted below.

04 Reading

f. 233, October 1585. Clear text in roman, cipher in bold, unread signs in braces; an English rendering follows each letter, cipher passages again in bold.

Seulement je vous prieray, si de ça me l’avez fait, {X} capituler et conclure promptement avec ceux que vous adviserez, s’il est possible avec le duc Casimir ou par son consentement, et faire la levée la plus grande et la faire marcher le plustost que faire se pourra. Car pour que quelque prompt secours que est necessaire, j’escry à Mr de Clervant de faire la levée de deux mille reistres selon qu’il est obligé {gla} la contracte avec les Eglises[?] … en l’an 1579[?], pour le moings quelque partie s’il ne peult le tout, et s’il n’a le moyen de ce faire, qu’il s’aide des dix mil escus de {180}, ainsy que ledit Sr de Clervant vous dira … que je soye bien tost adverty … du chemin qu’il tiendra en ce cas, evitant le danger … chemins le Vivarès ou Daulphiné pourroient estre propres, en nous advertissant de bonne heure.
Monsieur de Ségur. I am troubled at having had no news of you since your departure from England; I have written to you four times … Only I will ask you, if you have not already done so from over there, {X} to treat and conclude promptly with those you shall judge fit, if it is possible with Duke Casimir or by his consent, and to make the largest levy [you can] and have it march as soon as may be. For since some prompt succour is necessary, I am writing to M. de Clervant to make the levy of two thousand reiters which he is bound to {gla} [under] the contract with the Churches[?] … in the year 1579[?], at least some part if he cannot [raise] the whole, and if he has not the means to do it, let him use the ten thousand écus from {180}, as the said Sieur de Clervant will tell you, to which I pray you lend a hand, and that I may soon be advised of everything … of the road he will take in that case, avoiding the danger …; the Vivarais or Dauphiné roads might be suitable, giving us early warning. I pray you send my news to my brother Monsieur the Prince wherever he may be … Your affectionate master and perfect friend, Henry.

Only I will tell you {900} to spare no means {gne} {gna} to succour [us] with all diligence, and to set every piece in motion for so necessary an effect; commit everything to it. And for the rest, make very certain account of me … [postscript, signed D.:] since {e} we have had no {news} from you. Sir, I am perpetually your servant. We hope God will bless your designs. Drake has made a very great prize. He is the first seaman, and among the most valiant, in the world. I kiss your hands most humbly as your most humble and most faithful servant. D.

{I} am in extreme anxiety at having had no [word] of your {affairs} since {e}; I do not know {where} {to you} to write, nor to whom, nor in what state our {affairs} stand. I pray you send me your news, and make, if it is possible for you, the greatest levy that was ever made. The two thousand reiters that Monsieur de Clervant is bound to furnish would be very necessary to us, and even more so in {H} {L}. I pray you confer with him about it. The rest of the volume, f. 333 and f. 366 (added later on 16 September 2026)

All 440 canvases of the volume were fetched and read on contact sheets. Cipher occurs on six leaves only: the four above, Tomokiyo’s ff. 321 and 333, and one leaf not on his list, f. 366, an autograph of Navarre to Ségur from the autumn of 1586 (on a fait avancer l’armée et les forces contre moy … d’user de toute la diligence que vous pourrez pour effectuer ce dont je vous ay chargé) with three short cipher lines at its foot. Its low figures and a letter-written sign gu put it in the ff. 233 key; the last line reads escri, the first two (ri-e-c-h-a-v-f-fe-n, re-de-du-pi-n gu la-que) look like names in the king’s own spelling and are left open. There is no further secretary letter in this key in the volume, so the word-signs stay unglossed. Nine other letters of Navarre in the volume are in clear.

f. 333 (La Rochelle, 10 July 1586, a news-sheet in the third person signed Henry) was transcribed and read with Tomokiyo’s f. 321 table: aussi tost que les reistres marcheront il montera à cheval; […] et son frere sont de bonne volonté; il fault avancer la levée et la faire marcher le plustost qu’on pourra. The reading adds 24 = f, 34 = l, 36 = m and 61 = et to his table, and shows that key to be the ff. 233 key shifted by eight in the letters (a 12–14 … u 53–55, et 60–62) with h and j rows added to the syllabary. The free annealer on f. 333 alone (58 letter tokens) did not converge, and neither did it on a matched control of that size; but an annealer that builds in the chancery design itself (homophones in alphabetical order, so the letter key is a vector of homophone counts) re-derives the f. 333 letter key blind, every seed agreeing, with 93 % of the letter tokens right against Tomokiyo’s table (90 % on a matched alphabetical control), the syllable rows being the weak part at that size; the key rests on the f. 321 interlinear decipherment and on the French it yields here. f. 321 itself has every figure struck through by the decipherer and was not re-transcribed.

05 Context

Jacques de Ségur-Pardaillan was Navarre’s agent in England, Denmark and the Empire from 1583, and Claude-Antoine de Vienne, sieur de Clervant, his colleague for the German levy; Duke John Casimir of the Palatinate was the prince through whom Huguenot armies of reiters had been raised since 1567. Navarre’s October 1585 letter follows the bull of 9 September excommunicating him and the summer campaign that cost him most of his Guyenne places; the “contract with the Churches” and Clervant’s two thousand reiters refer to the standing obligation under which the Huguenot assemblies financed the German horse, and the ten thousand écus from [180] is most naturally the English subsidy (an inference, not a reading). The February and April 1586 letters belong to the long wait for the army that was finally assembled in 1587 under the baron of Dohna and destroyed at Auneau. The postscript’s Drak is Drake’s West Indies voyage: Santo Domingo fell on 1 January 1586, and the news reached France in February.

06 Method and files

  1. Images from Gallica IIIF (segur/fetch_gallica.py); transcription at full resolution with 2× re-reads (ct_233.txt, ct_239.txt, ct_288.txt, ct_143.txt).
  2. Mod-5 residue test and structured annealer with clear-text context (segur/solve.py).
  3. Matched controls with random keys of the same shape (segur/control.py, eval_control.py).
  4. Key file and renderer (key_v4.json, decode.py); edited reading with token notes (reading.md).
  5. Sweep of the volume (fetch_sweep.py, mksheets.py); f. 333 and f. 366 transcriptions (ct_333.txt, ct_366.txt); Tomokiyo’s table extended (key_321.json); design-constrained annealer and controls (solve_mono.py, control5.txt); word-sign likelihood ranking (gloss_check.py, gloss_check.txt).

Checked: transcription, structure test, three controls, convergence over seeds, alphabetical order of the recovered key, word-level French of every passage. Not checked: Tomokiyo’s f. 321 table figure by figure against the struck-through leaf; the word-signs; the historical glosses; the second Ségur volume (500 Colbert 402, not digitised); BnF fr. 17820 is an eighteenth-century fair copy in clear. The sender attribution and the readings marked [?] should be verified before they are repeated.
