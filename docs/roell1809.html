Roell to Van Dedem, 9 February 1809 — transcribed, no key survives online

Two code despatches of 1809 to the Dutch ambassador at Constantinople, every line in figures. They were transcribed and analysed, but no key or clear copy survives online to read them.

Daniel Bourdeau · attempted 21 September 2026 · updated 22 September 2026

key not found
On this pageThe recordsWhat was triedWhat remains openSources

At a glanceAttempted · not read

The letters
Two despatches, one of seven pages dated 9 February 1809 and one of six pages undated, to the Kingdom of Holland’s ambassador at the Porte. They are neither addressed nor signed. DECODE attributes them to the foreign minister W. F. Röell, but his despatches in the same archive are in clear Dutch.
Where
Nationaal Archief, 1.02.04 legatie Turkije, inv. 804, DECODE R1469 and R1470. No interlinear text, margin gloss or decipherment.
The cipher
A homophonic numerical code: 2,585 groups, 931 of them different, running to about 3,300. No group reaches one per cent of the text, and the frequent groups are spread over the whole range, so it is not alphabetical.
How it was tried
The groups were parsed from DECODE’s transcription and counted. The code is not the legation’s 1788–93 code, nor the ministry’s other open codes of 1804 and 1808. Röell’s papers list letters from Van Dedem and from the code-maker Croiset for 1808–09, but no codebook.
Still open
Both letters. Without the key or a clear copy they cannot be read.

01 The records

  • R1469 (images 6681–6687): opens “Monsieur” and ends on p. 7 with a paraph. 1,297 groups, 622 distinct.
  • R1470 (images 6688–6693): a docket on p. 1 read by DECODE as “? 69 Fermer? no? 9?”, then code. 1,288 groups, 707 distinct, the highest 3264.

DECODE already doubts the address: Van Dedem was on his way home when the letter was sent.

02 What was tried

  1. Frequencies. The commonest groups are 1390 and 460 (12 each in R1469) and 804, 75, 357, 296 and 394 (10 each in R1470). In a French text de alone is 4–5%, so a group rate of about 1% means heavy homophony. The frequent groups run from 35 to 2920 with no clustering, which excludes a one-part code whose alphabetical ranges could be predicted.
  2. The legation’s 1788–93 code (Van Dedem 1788–99), for which French (R1947, R2053) and Dutch (R2121) decipherments exist. It is a different code. Only 30–33% of each crib’s distinct groups occur here, against 28% by chance, and just 0–5 of each crib’s 30 commonest groups are among the 200 commonest here.
  3. The ministry’s other codes of the period, Van Spaen 1808 (plain groups to 1339) and Fagel 1804 (syllabic groups with clear endings). These are different systems.
  4. Keys and print. The DECODE metadata dump has no key record for this legation or ministry. Colenbrander’s Gedenkstukken V (1806–1810) gave nothing by web search. The inventory of Röell’s papers (NA 2.21.008.78) lists letters from F. G. van Dedem and from S. F. Croiset, both 1808–09, but no cipher.

03 What remains open

  • Both letters in full: nothing is read.
  • The trailing marks on some groups are not in DECODE’s transcription and were not re-transcribed. They may be part of the code.
  • What would move it: the codebook in the legation papers (NA 1.02.04, near inv. 804), Croiset’s 1808–09 letters to Röell (NA 2.21.008.78), or a clear copy of any despatch in this code. Henk Boon, Onze man in Constantinopel (2012), was not checked.
