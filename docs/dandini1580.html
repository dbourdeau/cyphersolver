The Cardinal of Como to the nuncio Anselmo Dandini: two cipher enclosures, 1580

Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 283C, f. 52 and ff. 89–90 (DECODE R72, R73). The DECODE records call these cipher enclosures from the Cardinal of Como to Mgr Anselmo Dandini “partially decrypted”.

R72 has the clerk’s contemporary decipherment written below the figures, and R73 was decrypted by George Lasry. Lasry’s key gives nonsense on R72, which keeps the frame and nomenclator of that key but assigns the letters differently. The R72 letter values were rebuilt with a solver and checked word by word against the clerk’s decipherment.

Summary. The catalogue entry (item 244) listed two ciphertexts from the papal Secretariat to its nuncio in France in 1580, with a note that one was “deciphered in the original”. Both now have a full plaintext. R73 (ff. 89–90, about 1,080 groups) was decrypted by George Lasry in October 2020; his key and his decryption are attached to the DECODE records. R72 (f. 52, the enclosure of Como’s letter of 16 May 1580, about 520 groups) gives nonsense with that key. The groups still parse in Lasry’s frame: two-digit letter codes and three-digit nomenclator groups closed by a mark. The nomenclator is the same (266^ che, 966^ tanto, 366^ per), but the letters are reassigned. Annealing the letter values against Italian, with the nomenclator words held fixed, gave readable text at once. The block of clear writing under the figures, which looks like a postscript, is the contemporary decipherment, and the two agree word for word. Both letters are about the rumour that Henri III was keeping company with a nun, which Gregory XIII wanted stopped. A few nomenclator groups and 32 illegible groups in R73 remain open.

01 The documents and the keys

Francia 283C holds the Secretariat’s original letters and ciphers to the nuncio for 1580: “Corrispondenza fra il Cardinale di Como (Gallio) e Monsignore Dandini Nunzio a Parigi”. DECODE has two ciphertexts from it:

  • R72, f. 52: a single page of figures, filed with Como’s clear letter from the Villa, 16 May 1580 (ff. 50–53, attached to the record). The decipherment is written in clear below the figures.
  • R73, ff. 89r–90r: three pages of figures, undated, on damaged paper, with no decipherment on the leaves.

Lasry’s reconstructed key (DECODE attachment D3190, 24 October 2020) is deterministic. Letters are two digits with an even second digit, vowels have one or two homophones, and there are no nulls. Nomenclator groups are three digits followed by a special mark, for pronouns, titles and common words: 124^ S.S., 824^ N.S., 822^ V.S., 224^ S.M.tà, 266^ che, 200^ non, 800^ mente, 988^ essere. His decryption of R73 (D3191) gives the whole letter apart from illegible groups.

R72 fits the same frame. Parsed with a small dynamic program (two digits, or three digits plus the mark, which SofPe’s 2019 transcription gives as 7 or t/+), every group except 38 falls inside Lasry’s table. The nomenclator words come out right, but the letters between them are nonsense. The letter codes had been reassigned:

02 R72: Como to Dandini, 16 May 1580

This is the clerk’s decipherment at the foot of f. 52. The decryption with the rebuilt key agrees with it, except that the cipher has commercio where the clerk wrote concetto:

La voce et l’opinione della pratica del Re Chr.mo con la monaca è uscita tanto publica che se ne ragiona hormai per tutto, et da ogniuno è tenuta per vera: onde quando bene non fusse vero il fatto come vien detto a V.S. da chi presuppone di saperne l’intiero, è pure sì grande il scandalo che nasce dal concetto di S. M.tà in quel luogo, che per levarsi la infamia che gli ne viene, dovrebbe astenersi in tutto dall’andar più a quel monasterio: et V.S. doverà ricordar questo punto, et incaricarlo gagliardamente, già che si ha voluto persuaderle che il fatto non è vero. Quanto alla pratica scoperta per conto del credito et sforzo del denaro per la spesa, N. S.re persevera nella sua deliberatione persuaso a ciò da molte ragioni che non occorre scrivere.

“Talk of the Most Christian King’s affair with the nun has become so public that it is discussed everywhere and taken as true by everyone. So even if the thing were not true, as Your Lordship is told by someone who claims to know the whole of it, the scandal it causes is so great that, to rid himself of the infamy, His Majesty ought to stop going to that monastery altogether. Your Lordship is to raise this point and press it on him hard, since they have tried to persuade you that it is not true. As for the scheme uncovered over the credit and the raising of money for the expense, His Holiness holds to his decision, for many reasons that need not be written.”

03 R73: the same affair, at more length

Lasry’s decryption, with its gaps, first turns to a princely marriage negotiation that “N.S. è certificato” a gentleman has raised: the Pope would take it up “con buon fondamento”. It then comes to the nun. Rome does not want Dandini to raise it with the Queen: “non lauda M.S. che V.S. tenghi proposito alcuno con la Regina nel particolare della monaca, per non pigliare occasione di far con tal coperta qualche rumore non conforme alla mente di S.S.”. The Pope has already acted through the confessor, “l’officio che N.S. ne fece con il confessore”, and the reproof is to come from him to the King, “tanto circonspettamente che non ne nasca rumore né disordine alcuno”.

04 Method

  1. The two records were fetched from DECODE with a session cookie: page images, the clear covering letter, SofPe’s and KaJo’s transcriptions, Lasry’s key, and his decryption of R73.
  2. Lasry’s key on R72 gave nonsense. A plain two-digit tokenisation, annealed as a free homophonic cipher, collapsed or failed to converge.
  3. R72 was parsed in Lasry’s frame instead. The letter values were annealed by swaps over a fixed letter multiset, scored with Italian 4-grams from a Renaissance corpus, with the nomenclator words kept as known plaintext. Five of six restarts agreed.
  4. The clear block on f. 52 was transcribed and aligned with the output. Three codes were corrected (22 l, 62 e, 72 i), and the leading stray “1” was set aside.

05 What remains uncertain

  • R72: nomenclator groups 766^, 866^, 030^ and 448^ have no value yet. In context they fall at “come vien detto a V.S.” and “presuppone”. Letter codes 24, 00, 11 and 32 are graded M (from the anneal only). The text itself is grade C, from the contemporary decipherment confirmed by the key.
  • R73: 32 groups illegible in the transcription. Nomenclator groups 866^, 466^, 766^, 324^ and 300^ have no value in Lasry’s file. The letter is undated.
  • The monastery and the nun were not identified here.
