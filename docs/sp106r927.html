Nancy → “Monsieur” · homophonic pigpen, French · December, year unknown (17th century)

A French letter in a homophonic pigpen cipher, SP 106/10 ff. 241–243

The National Archives, Kew, SP 106/10 ff. 241–243 (DECODE R927), described as an “undeciphered letter in the masonic alphabet, say 800 codegroups”, with no key, sender, recipient or date. The September 2026 search found no earlier decipherment or published key, but George Lasry reports that the letter was decoded by Sheila Richards in Secret Writings (1973); this reading is an independent re-solution, not a first break. f. 243 was transcribed by hand and the key recovered from the ciphertext alone; f. 241, its continuation, and the image catalogued as f. 242 followed on 2 October 2026 (it is f. 243 again, with the flap down, as WAINUT showed in October), and after every unread word was read again sign by sign on 3 October, 95.6% of the letter’s symbols read as sense.

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · partial (96% of the cipher)

The letter
A French letter of the seventeenth century from a client or servant at Nancy, in December, to a “Monsieur” whose favour he asks; signed with a name read “Nicle”(?). It runs f. 243 → f. 241; the image of f. 242 is the same leaf as f. 243 with the flap folded down. A proposed identification of the persons is given below as a hypothesis.
Where
The National Archives, Kew, SP 106/10 ff. 241–243; DECODE R927, catalogued as an “undeciphered letter in the masonic alphabet”.
The cipher
A homophonic pigpen: nine cell shapes, each with one to three dots and sometimes an inner stroke, several symbols for the common letters, two of them polyphonic; a few special symbols for names. The pencilled key on the flap does not fit.
How it was deciphered
The key was recovered by annealing on a hand transcription of f. 243 and fixed with probable words. On 2 October 2026 ff. 241 and 242 were transcribed by hand and decoded with it, and four values were added. On 3 October every sign of every unread word was cut out at three to four times the size and read again; two more values were fixed and the share read rose from 84.0% to 95.6%.
What it says
Sorrow that the recipient, after the marriage of “ce gentilhomme”, believed something “au prejudice de l’entiere affection que j’ay vouée a vostre service”; a proposal to send someone to congratulate “Sa Majesté” on recovering from a long illness; the writer’s “zele … a son tres humble service”.
Credit
George Lasry reports that Sheila Richards decoded the letter in Secret Writings (1973). The book was not seen here; this reading is an independent re-solution.
Still open
Two short stretches of f. 241 under ink blots and a stain, and a few single signs, at the resolution of the only images; the 14 special symbols in name positions, which no surviving key explains; and so the sender’s identity, the recipient and the year. With these, the reading of f. 241 builds coherent sentences but reads at 90.1% of its words (measured 24 October 2026), under the 95% a complete reading needs.

01 The document and the pencilled key

There are three images: f. 241, f. 242 with a paper flap pasted over part of it, and f. 243. The archive photographed the text sideways. Turned 90° anticlockwise, it runs in horizontal lines with word spaces. The flap carries a pencil grid, the standard dotted pigpen (a–i one dot, k–s two, t–z three). Applied to the letter it gives oeaeydakdorsefekdoi…, with g/q at 16% of the text and t almost absent. None of the 48 rotations, reflections and dot orders, and none of the 9! reassignments of shapes to cells, gives any language.

02 The system

Each symbol is one of the nine cell shapes, followed by one, two or (rarely) three dots, and sometimes with an inner stroke. The shape does not fix a group of letters: e is ◻·, ◻ with an inner mark, and one or two rarer forms; s has five or six symbols; i three. Working key (shape named by its open side; the digit is the dot count):

a ⊔1   b ⊓1   c ⊏1, ⊏ with bar 1   d Γ1   e ◻1 (+ inner-mark forms)   f/q L1   g ⊔2
h ⊏-bar 2   i ◻2 (+ variants)   l ⊏2, ⊏3   m ¬1   n ⌟1   o ⊐1   p ◻3   q L2
r Γ2, Γ3   s ⊓2 (+ variants)   t ⊐2, ⊔2   u/v ⌟2   y ¬2

L1 serves for both f (affection, effets) and q (que). It is either a polyphonic symbol or two variants not separated in transcription. Two circle or “9”-shaped symbols and a few stacked composites are unresolved. They stand where names or abbreviations are expected.

03 The decipherment of f. 243, the first page of the letter

First worked as the last page; the transcription of f. 241 on 2 October 2026 showed that the letter begins here (section 04). On 3 October every sign of the words not yet deciphered was cut out at three to four times the size and read again: the errors had been the open side of corner shapes, dot counts and inner strokes, and two signs the writer cancelled with an ink blot. Two values were corrected in the key (F*1 = l, from “que les occasions” twice; D*1 = o, from pouvois). A joint re-annealing of every value over the three pages raised the model score but turned g and h against gentilhomme, longue and humble, so it was not adopted.

Translation: “I could not have received a keener sorrow than on learning that, from the marriage of this gentleman, who lately greeted [name] on behalf of [name] my master, you had believed some things to the prejudice of the entire affection I have vowed to your service. Had there been anyone in whom I could confide, you would have known through him that I cherish nothing so much as the occasions to show you its effects. I am writing to [name] about what this gentleman was going to do over there, and I beg you most humbly, sir, to continue me the honour of your goodwill, and to believe that I honour you …” The first decryption had read the middle of the page as “Je ne vous dis rien” and left most of it open.

04 The close of the letter (f. 241), and f. 242

On 2 October 2026 both remaining pages were transcribed by hand, every symbol read by eye from enlarged, deskewed strips, and decoded with the key (polyphonic symbols resolved word by word by a French language model). Two findings changed the shape of the letter:

  • The page order is f. 243, then f. 241. f. 243 ends “et croire que je vous honnor-”; f. 241 begins “-e et cheris ce qui se peut” and ends with the subscription and the dateline. What was called the last page above is the first, and f. 241 is the close.
  • f. 242 is not a second copy. It was first taken for one, line for line the same. WAINUT pointed out (October 2026), and the images confirm, that images P2 and P3 show the same leaf, with the same holes, blots and special symbol, photographed with the flap folded down and lifted. The agreement between the two transcriptions therefore checked the transcription, not the text.

f. 241, regularised after the re-reading of 3 October; [..] marks symbols not read as sense, [name] a special symbol:

[… que je vous honnor]e et cheris ce qui se peut. S’y vous trouviés a propos de disposer [67] d’envoier ledit [@] de la part de Sa Majesté vers [6] pour se resjouir de sa santé apres la longue maladie qu’il a eue, je m’asseure que les personnes que vous aimés n’en seroint marris, et de mon costé je leur ouvriray mon coeur de tout ce que je jugeray estre du [..] [67] et du [..] (WAINUT conjectures du service du [67] et du vostre), vous conjurant me faire la faveur d’asseurer Sa Majesté du zele que j’ay a son tres humble service, estant, Mo-sieur, vostre tres humble et fidel serviteur [Nicle? or Ville?]. De Nancy le [day] decembre.

“… and cherish you as much as can be. If you thought fit to arrange for [name] to send the said [name] on behalf of His Majesty to [name], to rejoice at his health after the long illness he has had, I am sure that the persons you love would not be sorry for it; and for my part I will open my heart to them on all that I judge to be of [..], begging you to do me the favour of assuring His Majesty of my zeal for his most humble service, being, sir, your most humble and faithful servant [Nicle?]. Nancy, the [..] of December.”

Three readings here come from an independent transcription and key by WAINUT (October 2026), whose 20 symbol values all agree with ours: voiage on f. 243 (we had read mari-age with one symbol unread), Mo-sieur before the subscription, and the separation of the special symbols into three: a “67” (a king, “Sa Majesté”), a “6” (the writer’s master, recovering from a long illness) and a circled symbol [@], a man who could be sent from one to the other. The signature is still open between Nicle and Ville: the two transcriptions differ in the dots of two symbols, which the images cannot settle.

Who (a hypothesis, WAINUT). If the signature is Ville, the writer may be Henri de Livron, marquis de Ville, gentleman of the chamber of Charles IV, Duke of Lorraine, and his envoy to Charles I in 1628–29; [6] would be Charles IV, [67] Charles I, the “Monsieur” perhaps Henry Rich, Earl of Holland, and the year perhaps 1629. Ville appears in the Calendar of State Papers Venetian XXI (April 1628) and Domestic 1628–29 as the Duke’s envoy, and in 1629 dealing with Holland. No document names the persons of this letter, and the identification fails if the signature is Nicle.

Measured (a word counts when its decrypt differs from the regular word by at most one letter): 843 of the letter’s 882 symbols read as sense (95.6%), f. 243 98.3% and f. 241 92.5%; leaving out the 14 special symbols in name positions, 97.1%. On 2 October, before the sign-by-sign re-reading, the figure was 84.0% (f. 243 81.7%, f. 241 86.5%). On 2 October four values were added to the key: H3 = z (zele), C*2 = q (ce qui), and two polyphones, C1 = f/q (faveur, confiance against que, qu’il) and F2 = l/h (plus against cheris, gentilhomme); C3 = l and A*2 = q are provisional, one occurrence each.

Neighbouring records. R922–R933 were opened on DECODE: R923–R925, R928 and R929 are the digit code of the Henrietta Maria and Goffe letters of 1645–46, R930 a strip of alchemical-type symbols, R931–R932 numeric, R933 a key sheet with English names. None is in this pigpen, and none carries a decipherment or clear copy of R927.

05 Method

The glyph boxes and dots were segmented automatically, then corrected by eye from enlarged crops. The solver is simulated annealing over a homophonic key, scored by French, English, Latin and nine other quadgram models, with a letter-frequency penalty. It was tested first on synthetic homophonic text, with and without 10% noise. On the automatic transcription every language failed (best −4.5/char, no words). On the hand transcription French scored clearly best (−4.39), and the solver output contained que dun, de la part de, plus. The key was then fixed from whole words and the solver run again.

06 What remains uncertain

  • Writer, recipient and year: the letter is dated at Nancy in December and signed with a name read “Nicle”(?), perhaps a contraction; the recipient is “Monsieur”; the gentleman whose journey is mentioned is not named. WAINUT’s hypothesis (above) would make the writer the marquis de Ville and the year 1629; it rests on the signature. The names sit in the special symbols. No docket or address leaf survives in the record, and without a year or a name the calendars cannot be searched. The formulae and spelling point to French of the seventeenth century.
  • The special symbols (circle/“9” forms, stacked composites): not decoded, probably names or titles.
  • f. 243 reads in full but for two signs lost to a blot (line 3) and a paper hole (line 7); there is no second copy (the f. 242 image is the same leaf). The closing formulae are secure (H), the rest C.
  • f. 241: the close and the dateline are secure (H). Counted by words, its reading reads at 90.1% (24 October 2026), under 95%. Two stretches stay unread: b_ruiledu … et du uoii in line 13 (“de tout ce que je jugeray estre du [..] [67] et du [..]”) and bienr before the subscription (now deciphered sieur after WAINUT), under ink blots and a stain, plus single signs at three line ends and the day of the date. f. 241 has no second copy, the National Archives have not digitised SP 106, and DECODE holds only these 2,300-pixel images, so this is the limit until the leaf or a better photograph is seen. They are the reason f. 241 is at 92.5% while the letter is over 95%: the project’s threshold for a complete text (95% of the cipher gives sense) admits gaps blocked from outside, and these are.
  • Writer’s spellings kept as read: alloet, fairc, hunblem, persnne, bienveullance.
