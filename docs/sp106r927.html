Unknown writer → “Monsieur” · homophonic pigpen, French · undated (17th century)

A French letter in a homophonic pigpen cipher, SP 106/10 ff. 241–243

The National Archives, Kew, SP 106/10 ff. 241–243 (DECODE R927), described as an “undeciphered letter in the masonic alphabet, say 800 codegroups”, with no key, sender, recipient or date. No earlier decipherment or published key was found. The last page was transcribed by hand and the key recovered from the ciphertext alone; that page is deciphered almost in full, the first page only in fragments.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 19 September 2026 · updated 24 September 2026

Summary. The letter is written in tic-tac-toe (pigpen) cell shapes, each followed by one to three dots. A later hand pencilled the textbook dotted-pigpen key on a flap (akt · blu · cmw / dnx · eoy · fpz / gq · hr · is). That key gives no language in any orientation or dot order. The cipher is homophonic: the symbol is the cell shape plus its dot count (sometimes with an inner stroke), and common letters have several symbols. An automatic transcription with about 25% errors defeated every solver. A full hand transcription of the last page (450 symbols) led the solver to French, and probable words (“monsieur”, “de vostre”, “croire que je vous honnore”) completed the key. The last page is deciphered almost in full. It is the end of a letter from a client to a patron, condoling on or reacting to “the marriage of this gentleman”, and it closes et vous supplie tres humblement, monsieur, me continuer l’honneur de vostre bienveuillance. The first page is deciphered only in fragments. Writer, recipient and date are unknown.

01 The document and the pencilled key

There are three images: f. 241 (the first page), and f. 243 photographed twice, once with a paper flap folded over it and once with the flap lifted. The archive photographed the text sideways. Turned 90° anticlockwise, it runs in horizontal lines with word spaces. The flap carries a pencil grid, the standard dotted pigpen (a–i one dot, k–s two, t–z three). Applied to the letter it gives oeaeydakdorsefekdoi…, with g/q at 16% of the text and t almost absent. None of the 48 rotations, reflections and dot orders, and none of the 9! reassignments of shapes to cells, gives any language.

02 The system

Each symbol is one of the nine cell shapes, followed by one, two or (rarely) three dots, and sometimes with an inner stroke. The shape does not fix a group of letters: e is ◻·, ◻ with an inner mark, and one or two rarer forms; s has five or six symbols; i three. Working key (shape named by its open side; the digit is the dot count):

a ⊔1   b ⊓1   c ⊏1, ⊏ with bar 1   d Γ1   e ◻1 (+ inner-mark forms)   f/q L1   g ⊔2
h ⊏-bar 2   i ◻2 (+ variants)   l ⊏2, ⊏3   m ¬1   n ⌟1   o ⊐1   p ◻3   q L2
r Γ2, Γ3   s ⊓2 (+ variants)   t ⊐2, ⊔2   u/v ⌟2   y ¬2

L1 serves for both f (affection, effets) and q (que). It is either a polyphonic symbol or two variants not separated in transcription. Two circle or “9”-shaped symbols and a few stacked composites are unresolved. They stand where names or abbreviations are expected.

I am writing to [&] about what this gentleman was going to do over there, and I beg you most humbly, sir, to continue me the honour of your goodwill, and to believe that I honour you …”

04 The first page (f. 241)

This page was segmented automatically, and the classifier often mistakes ⊔ for ◻ here. Only the hand-checked columns give text: “… disposer … envoier le …”, “… la santé … la longue maladie qu’il a eue, je m’asseure …”, “… que les personnes …”, “vous aimés, n’en seroint …”, “… mon costé …”, “… mon coeur …”, “… faveur …”. A full hand transcription of this page is the open work.

05 Method

The glyph boxes and dots were segmented automatically, then corrected by eye from enlarged crops. The solver is simulated annealing over a homophonic key, scored by French, English, Latin and nine other quadgram models, with a letter-frequency penalty. It was tested first on synthetic homophonic text, with and without 10% noise. On the automatic transcription every language failed (best −4.5/char, no words). On the hand transcription French scored clearly best (−4.39), and the solver output contained que dun, de la part de, plus. The key was then fixed from whole words and the solver run again.

06 What remains uncertain

  • Writer, recipient, date and the gentleman whose marriage is mentioned: not named in the deciphered parts. The formulae and spelling point to French of the seventeenth century.
  • The special symbols (circle/“9” forms, stacked composites): not decoded, probably names or titles.
  • Last page: the closing formulae are secure (H). The middle lines are deciphered with gaps (C), and a few words are conjectural (M).
  • First page: fragments only (M/I).
