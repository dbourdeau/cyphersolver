Paris → his cousin in England · numerical nomenclator in a clear letter · 19 April 1641

Robert Reade to his cousin, Paris, 19 April 1641

British Library, Harley MS 7001 ff. 148–149, DECODE R7766. The letter is in clear English with two ciphered passages, 254 groups in all. DECODE lists the writer and the recipient as unknown.

The signature and the endorsement identify the writer. The Deciphering Branch’s reconstruction of Secretary Windebank’s cipher, filed in Add MS 32256 and not linked to this letter, was found to fit. Its values decipher 90% of the significant groups; values rebuilt here bring that to 93.5%, and the rest are conjectures. Measured on the words of the reading, the letter builds coherent sentences but reads at 89.7%, under the 95% bar. No earlier decipherment of the letter was found.

At a glanceExternal key matched · partial (93% of the cipher)

The letter
Robert Reade, nephew and secretary of Sir Francis Windebank, to Windebank, Paris, 19 April 1641. Windebank was Charles I’s Secretary of State and had fled to Paris in December 1640. Partly in cipher.
Where
DECODE R7766, catalogued with writer and recipient unknown; signed “R. Reade” and endorsed “my Cousin Reade from Paris”.
The cipher
Letters at 1–100 with the odd numbers below 50 as nulls, symbol letters, and an alphabetical code up to about 735.
How it was read
BL Add MS 32256 holds a key sheet for the same cipher, “Windebank to his Son. Paris. March 1642” (DECODE R9115). Of 215 significant groups, 193 are deciphered from its values and 8 from values rebuilt here.
What it says
The clear text concerns Strafford’s trial, the King’s papers and Walter Montagu’s arrival; the cipher carries household business, including that Sir William St Ravy has done Mr Forster no good offices in Paris.
Still open
14 code groups the reconstruction leaves blank, offered as conjectures (7%).

01 The letter

The letter covers three pages (ff. 148r, 148v, 149r). The address leaf f. 149v carries the endorsement and “Given by Mr G. Holmes”. It answers the recipient’s letter of 1 April and follows Reade’s own letter of the 16th. The clear text covers:

  • a malicious report that Reade had said, to two French gentlemen in a garden, that the Lords of the Parliament are fools. He denies it at length and asks the recipient to raise it with the Lord Chief Justice;
  • the roads full of soldiers, which keep the household in its quarter of Paris for another month;
  • “My Lo: Lieutenant”, Strafford, then on trial, “fallen into the hands of noblemen and persons of honor”;
  • the King’s box of papers and a copy held by Mr Treasurer;
  • the Queen’s business;
  • Mr Montagu’s arrival, “very civilly” received;
  • Sir William St Ravy leaving for England;
  • a defence of Mr Forster, lodged with the household.

The cipher begins where the letter turns to Windebank himself: “It is to be seriously considered what is to be done”.

02 The key

DECODE R9115 is a two-page working sheet. The Deciphering Branch printed a blank grid of numbers and filled in the values as they were recovered. The first page is headed “Windebank to his Son. Paris. March 1642”, the second “Windebank’s C[ipher] continued 1640”. The cipher has four parts:

  • 1–50: single letters. Every odd number is marked + (a null). The even numbers are letters: 2 c, 4 f, 6 n, 8 h, 10 r, 12 t, 14 a, 16 g, 18 p, 20 b, 22 u, 24 o, 26 l, 28 e, 32 k, 34 m, 36 i, 40 s, 42 x, 44 d.
  • 51–100: more letters and syllables: 51 r, 53 c, 54 l, 55 s, 56 a, 57 d, 58 m, 59 t, 60 e, 62 n, 64 i, 66 p, 68 o, 69 h, 72 u, 73 and, 74 am, 75 as, 93 again, 97 an.
  • Symbol letters: m n, E o, ω m, Ǝ r, ⊔ i, + l, φ s, Δ t, * y, ⊕ a, π c, ɔ d, ∪ e, C p, and others.
  • A code in alphabetical order from 100 to about 735: 128 be, 183 can, 202 do, 276 from, 323 him, 335 his, 362 King, 457 Parliament, 533 shall, 572 to, 574 the, 578 that, 636 will, 732 England, 735 France.

Reade’s letter uses the same cipher. The most frequent code groups in it are the reconstruction’s the, to and that, and its symbol letters are the key’s. Several characters first transcribed as digits are the key’s symbol letters: φ looks like an 8, ɔ like a 2, and the looped n-sign like a D.

03 The decipherment

Plain type is deciphered from the key. Words in italics come from values rebuilt here. Words in [brackets] are conjectures for groups the key leaves blank (section 04). Nulls are dropped.

Passage A (f. 148v foot to f. 149r), after the clear “It is to be seriously considered what is to be done”:

in case a proclamation shall goe out against my [455: patron?] as against the last [673], for as to the one side, I am of opinion they have not enough against him to confiscate his fortune, and they would be [willing] to take the advantage of his refusall to return upon the a summons. So on the other side, I do not find how his person can be strong if he shall return. The only [way] i[s] [conceived] to be that [His Majesty] will please to prevent that cause, if they purpose to take it, bu[t pres]sing that [Mr Forster] [should] give in his [90]; but God knows why that will suit.

Passage B (f. 149r), after the clear “Sir William St Ravy (as I hear) parts this day for England, but I do but hear it from others, for he comes not at us”:

I am [informed] he has done [Mr Forster] no good [offices] here, and that he will not do him the best in Court; they have an eye [over] him.

The clear text goes straight on: “It may be that Mr Forster may have ill offices done him in England…”. This confirms the sense of the passage and supports the conjecture that 718 is Forster.

04 What remains uncertain

The passages have 254 groups, 215 of them after the nulls are dropped. Grades follow the README conventions: H a value on the key, C a rebuilt value with firm context, M a conjecture.

  • Re-measured 5 October 2026. Key and rebuilt values together decipher 201 of 215 cipher groups (93.5%). The margin entry for 650 on the key sheet (R9115 f. 1) was read at full resolution: it is 650. w[r]it with an h above, not way, and none of the candidates fits the context, so 650 stays grade M. Neither key page has side notes for the other twelve open values. The fourteen single-occurrence blanks need key material: the Reade letters in TNA SP 16 (physical access).
  • H, 193 groups (89.8%): deciphered from values on the key, including 203 done and 729 “ct.” (Court), which are side notes on the sheet. 562 is “strong” on the key, in the period sense of safe or secure (“how his person can be strong if he shall return”). Until 30 September 2026 this page printed a rebuilt “secure” there instead.
  • C, 8 groups (3.7%): values rebuilt from the code’s alphabetical slot and the context. They are 100 advantage, 275 find, 437 one, 524 return (twice) and 573 they/the (three times). 573 fits “they” in two places. In “upon 573 a summons” “the” fits better, and there the 14 (a) is probably a slip. The key sheet has a partly legible entry at 573, “u. th…”: the word begins th, and the rest runs under a ruled line. It fits they or the but is too damaged to confirm either.
  • M, 14 groups (6.5%): blank on the reconstruction and conjectured.
    • 650 way, 160 conceived, 319 His Majesty, 539 should.
    • 718 Forster (twice), 359 informed 444 offices, 452 over.
    • 660, “they would be [willing] to take the advantage”: willing, glad or ready fit the sense. On the key 660 is blank among the y-words (652 you, 662 you, 664 your, 666 yet), where a w-word would not stand, and no y-word gives the sense.
    • 487 (inside “bu…ing”), 90 (his answer or account), 455 (my patron?) and 673 (the last Lord Keeper? Finch fled in December 1640 as Windebank did).

Only a fuller copy of the key would settle these. No other Windebank key is on DECODE, and no Windebank letter in this cipher was found there.

05 Method

  1. The images were fetched from DECODE and the two passages transcribed. The signature and endorsement gave the writer.
  2. The key of “Mr Browne” (R8694, Add MS 72438) was tried first and failed. Its code stops at 371, and its alphabet gives nothing.
  3. A search of the DECODE record list for Windebank found R9115 in Add MS 32256. Both of its pages were transcribed at full resolution and entered as a table.
  4. The table was applied to the passages, the doubtful glyphs were checked again against the key’s symbol list, and the result was measured.

No printed edition or calendar of the letter was found. Harleian letters of this kind are outside the Calendars of State Papers Domestic.

R7766, Robert Reade to his cousin, Paris, 19 Apr 1641 (Harley MS 7001 ff. 148-149) read in part

Robert Reade, nephew and secretary of Secretary Sir Francis Windebank, who followed him into exile in Paris, writes to a cousin at court, probably Thomas Windebank. The first cipher passage weighs what to do if a proclamation goes out against Windebank: they have not enough against him to confiscate his fortune, his person would not be safe if he returned, and only His Majesty can prevent it; the second says Sir William St Ravy has done Mr Forster no good offices. Read with the Windebank cipher reconstruction DECODE R9115; 14 tokens are conjectures.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (90% here); the holes are marked in the reading.
