Lyon → the duke of Nevers · substitution cipher of graphic signs · September 1574

Henriette de Clèves and Renato Birago to the Duke of Nevers, Lyon, September 1574

→

Go to the full text ↓

BnF français 3315 ff. 15–16 and 21, two letters from Lyon in September 1574. One has a minute word-by-word decipherment between its lines and a clear ending; the other keeps Renato Birago’s cipher flap over a complete clear Italian fair copy. The text was taken from these contemporary decipherments: f. 21 from its clear copy, and f. 15 from its interlinear decipherment, checked word by word against the cipher decoded with an alphabet rebuilt here from the gloss (22 October 2026). Only four overwritten lines of f. 15v and a dozen single groups stay open; with them the f. 15 reading builds coherent sentences but reads at 94.1% of its words, under the 95% a complete reading needs. Tomokiyo had described the system but published no decipherment.

At a glanceExisting decipherment · partial (94% of the cipher)

The letter
Renato Birago to the Duke of Nevers, Lyon, 28 September 1574 (f. 21), and a letter of Henriette de Clèves, Lyon, 16 September [1574] (f. 15; the year is inferred from the archival setting).
Where
BnF français 3315, ff. 15 and 21 (Gallica btv1b90600800), with a clear ending on f. 16r.
The cipher
An alphabet of graphic signs with special signs for doubled consonants and nulls. It does not use the Nevers key at ff. 12–13; it resembles Nevers no. 2 (1584, BnF fr. 3995 f. 16) but differs in decisive assignments.
How it was read
Both leaves carry their own decipherment. On f. 21 a folded cipher sheet covers a clean, signed Italian copy of Birago’s whole letter; on f. 15 an early hand wrote French plaintext between the cipher lines. No key was rebuilt and no solver was run.
What it says
Birago says Nevers’s visit is late, Piedmont is better discussed orally, and Their Majesties wish to provide Nevers with a government. Henriette writes at midnight, her eyes hurt from weeping.
Still open
On f. 15 the interlinear decipherment is transcribed only in part and the ciphered middle lacks a reliable line-by-line transcription. One phrase on f. 21 is uncertain, and the exact 1574 alphabet is not rebuilt.

02 Birago’s letter, 28 September

Birago had expected Nevers at Lyon throughout the month for the feast of St Michael. Since he has not come, Birago hopes to see him shortly, though not as soon as he would like. The bearer’s purpose has come at a bad time: affairs of the kingdom are such that virtually anything else would be considered before it. He therefore returns with the dispatch that will make the answer clear.

Et quanto alle cose dil Piemonte, ella intenderà similmente com’elle siano passate … rimettendomi a parlargliene poi più minutamente di bocca, quando Iddio mi farà la gratia di vederla.

Birago will not commit the Piedmont business to a fuller written discussion. He adds that Their Majesties are very willing to provide Nevers with a governorship and every other thing he deserves, and that he has done and will continue to do every good office of a true servant and friend. A semi-diplomatic transcription of the whole leaf is in targets/nevers1574/f21_transcription.md; only one short expression in the first sentence remains uncertain.

03 Henriette de Clèves, 16 September [1574]

The cipher fills two dense pages and the first 19 lines of f. 16r. Above almost every run a smaller contemporary hand has supplied its plaintext. The letter opens:

Monseigneur, je ne veulx faillir a vous advertir que ce jourd’huy a esté rendu a monsieur de Savoye Pignerol et Savillan, au grant contentement du Roy et de la Royne mere, qui ont faict ce beau chef d’euvre.

The duchess then went to the Queen Mother’s council. Catherine asked why Nevers should not pass through, since he did not lose his government for that; the duchess answered that, cut down as it was, she held it for lost. At court it had been told to the King that Nevers had written of “une ligue aupres du Roy de quatre ou cinq”. Two governments “qui ne pouvoient faillir, comme Picardie et Languedoc” are in prospect, and his friends say that if he keeps his government as it now stands “vous vous feriez beaucoup de tort”. The reading (f15_reading.md, English f15_en.md) has 4 holes in 402 tokens on f. 15r and f. 16r; on f. 15v lines 2–5 are overwritten and their gloss is faded. Over the whole letter the reading builds coherent sentences but reads at 94.1% of its words (measured 24 October 2026), under the 95% a complete reading needs.

The outer address is clear: A Monsieur, Monsieur le duc de Nevers, pair de France. The cipher runs on from the foot of f. 15v into the first 19 lines of f. 16r (“Je luy ay” continues there), and then gives way to a clear ending in the duchess’s hand. The writer says it is midnight, her eyes hurt from weeping, she wishes herself in paradise rather than suffer the way they are being made to suffer, and asks God to grant her “bon frère” patience. The closing reads “De Lyon ce 16 de septembre”; the year 1574 is inferred from the archival setting. A superficially similar appeal of Henri III to the duchess printed by Lavisse belongs to an earlier occasion, before his departure for Poland, and cannot serve as a crib for this letter.

04 A related key of 1584

Tomokiyo observed that ff. 15 and 21 do not use the Nevers key copied at ff. 12–13. They closely resemble Nevers collection no. 2, issued a decade later in 1584 and preserved in BnF fr. 3995 f. 16, but differ in decisive assignments: on the 1574 leaves F is e, 9 is q, and x, y and z are left in clear. The 1584 table therefore shows the design of the system (an alphabet of graphic signs, special signs for doubled consonants, and nulls), but it cannot be applied to this text as it stands.

The 1574 alphabet was rebuilt on 22 October 2026 by aligning the f. 15 signs with the French gloss above them (f15_key.md): a=1, c=C, d=a b-shape, e=F or E, g=0, i/j=7, l=3, m=+, n=2, o=8, q and s=9, r=T, t=4, u/v=6, with a doubling sign, name signs for the King, the Queen Mother and the Queen, and XX with digits for a number. Every value is attested in several words. It is close to the 1584 no. 2 alphabet but not the same.

05 What remains

  • F. 15v ll. 2–5 and the gloss-only line at its head (about 70 words). The decipherer wrote over the cipher signs and the gloss above is faded or struck; the original or a multispectral image might recover them.
  • About a dozen single groups on f. 15v (ll. 7, 10, 12–16, 20–23) and f. 16r (ll. 1, 14), which neither the signs nor the faded gloss settle; the group XX before “une ligue” is not spelled.
  • One phrase on f. 21 (grade I). The fair copy appears to write per saluato suo; it may contain a personal name or abbreviation. The rest of the clear copy is transcribed at grade H.

Henriette de Clèves to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 16 Sept [1574], ff. 15-16 read in part

The duchess tells her husband that Pignerol and Savillan have been handed to the Duke of Savoy, to the contentment of the King and the Queen Mother, so that Nevers is left without his government; her exchange with the Queen Mother (who first approves warning him, then says he does not lose his government) and her tears; a report to the King that Nevers had written of a league of four or five near the King; promises of the next vacant government (Picardy, Languedoc); his friends say he would harm himself by keeping the reduced government. Cipher continues on f. 16r (19 lines), then a clear closing: it is midnight, she would rather be in paradise.

# Henriette de Clèves to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 16 September [1574]

## f. 15r

Monseigneur, je ne veulx faillir a vous advertir que ce jourd'huy a esté rendu a monsieur de Savoye Pignerol et Savillan, au grant contentement du Roy et de la Royne mere, qui ont faict ce beau chef d'euvre. ## Notes

* Context. Henri III, back from Poland, met Emmanuel-Philibert of Savoy at Turin in August-September 1574 and
  agreed to return Pignerol and Savigliano; the duke of Nevers was governor of the French places in Piedmont, so
  the cession left him "plus de gouvernement". The letter reports the news, the duchess's audience with Catherine
  de Médicis (la Royne mere / la Royne), a rumour at court that Nevers had written to the King about a "ligue" of
  four or five, and his friends' advice about the remaining governorship. The two governorships "qui ne pouvoient
  faillir" are Picardie and Languedoc. Birago's letter of 28 Sept (f. 21) speaks of the same "governo".
* 15r l.3: the group after "du" is not spelled out; it is glossed "Roy" (same sense as `SS` elsewhere).
* 15r l.12: "et recevoir une conte…": signs `F4 TFCF687T 62F C824F..`; the end of the line is crossed by the gloss.
* 15r l.17: "tiens": the sign group is partly struck; the gloss is "t[?]".
* 15r l.21: "veid": signs `6Fb74`; the gloss reads "veit/veid".
* 15v ll.1-5 (after "25 ans ceste couronne si fidellement"): the cipher is overwritten letter by letter by the
  decipherer and the gloss above is faded; only "ayant tant", "aviez", "la moitié", "elle a", "recompense" and
  "France" are carried. This is the main hole of the letter (about 70 words).
* 15v l.7: `T1b879` after "chose que" does not resolve ("racontoit"?); `XXm` is glossed "24".
* 15v l.9: "qu'il c'estoit faict" as written (`CF94874`), i.e. s'estoit.
* 15v l.10: the group `XX` before "une ligue" is not spelled; left unread.
* 16r l.1: `21 CF(99F` after "vous n'a…" does not resolve.
* 16r l.14: `C824F2bTF` (glossed illegibly) left unread.
* The f. 16r clear ending was re-read on the native image: "de Lion ce 16 [one word struck] septembre". The word
  after "je ne vous" (underlined with a flourish) is not read.
* Ernest Lavisse, *Histoire de France* VI.1, prints an emotional appeal of Henri III to the duchess written before
  his departure for Poland. It is not this letter and was not used as a crib.

# Henriette de Clèves to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 16 September [1574] (translation)

## f. 15r

My lord, I would not fail to let you know that today Pinerolo and Savigliano were handed over to Monsieur de Savoy, to the great satisfaction of the King and of the Queen Mother, who have done this fine masterpiece. Having learnt this, I went to the Queen's council and said to her: "Madame, my husband wrote to me in the last of his letters that on his way back here he would pass through his government; he was planning to stay a week to put things in order there, which he no longer needs to do, since he no longer has a government. Will you allow me to tell him so, so that he wastes no time [?and receives] a [?...]."

She answered that I would do very well to do so; and then she corrected herself and said to me: "Why should he not go there? He does not lose his government because of this." I answered that, left as small as it was, I [?hold] it as good as lost, because you had too much heart to keep what a mere gentleman would refuse. She added that it was true that it was left very small indeed.

When she had gone to bed she told me, because she saw me weeping, that I must not torment myself over this, and that the loss is not my husband's but entirely the King's. I told her that I was not weeping for the King's loss, but for your misfortune, that after serving

# Renato Birago to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 28 September 1574 (BnF français 3315 f. 21r): English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `f21_transcription.md`, the contemporary clear fair copy found under the
cipher flap. One doubtful phrase, `[?Salvato] suo` (written *saluato suo*: a name, or the participle "saved"). Italian.

## Translation

Most illustrious and most excellent lord,

I wrote to Your Excellency these past days what was needed for your [?Salvato], and I believed you would be here by
the end of this month to keep the feast of Saint Michael; but since you have not come so far, I hope you will come
soon, though it will not be as soon as I desire. For the rest, the occasion for which the present bearer has come
here has been ill-timed and out of season, more than could be, because the affairs of this kingdom are today in such
a state that one would sooner think of anything else in the world than of that. And so he returns with such a
despatch as Your Excellency will learn, on which I have nothing more to tell you.

And as to the affairs of Piedmont, you will likewise learn how they have passed, on which it does not seem to me fit
to enter into any other discourse with Your Excellency for now, leaving it to speak to you of it more minutely by
mouth, when God grants me the grace of seeing you. Only I will tell you that Their Majesties are of very good will to
provide Your Excellency with a government, and with every other thing, as you deserve. In which (although I know it is
not necessary) I have not failed, nor shall I fail, to do for you every good office that a true servant and friend of
yours should do.

And with this, after humbly commending myself to Your Excellency's good grace, I pray Our Lord God to grant you a long
and happy life. From Lyon, the 28th of September 1574.

Your Excellency's most affectionate servant,
Renato Birago
