conte di Olivares

Madrid → Doge and Senate of Venice · numerical syllabic cipher, Italian · 26 October 1628

Alvise Mocenigo to the Doge, Madrid 1628

Archivio di Stato di Venezia, IT ASVe 0045 010 Busta 30 f. 174; DECODE R1874. Catalogued as an undated ciphered letter from an unknown sender to an unknown recipient. It is a despatch of Alvise Mocenigo, Venetian ambassador in Spain, dated Madrid, 26 October 1628. The Venetian chancery’s complete decipherment of it is imaged in the same record; the text here is transcribed from that decipherment and checked against the cipher. It is calendared in CSP Venice 21 no. 515.

The letter
Alvise Mocenigo, Venetian ambassador at Madrid, to the Doge, 26 October 1628; the clear text names Spinola, La Rochelle and Monterrey.
Where
ASVe Busta 30 f. 174; DECODE R1874, 16 images: the despatch, a clerk’s decipherment, a second enciphered copy, and the address leaf.
The cipher
A syllabic nomenclator: groups of 5 or 6, an optional 1–4, then two digits, about two letters of plaintext per group.
How it was read
From the chancery’s own decipherment (images 8955–8959), checked against the cipher by paragraph mark 6499 and the thrice-repeated run for “conte di Olivares”.
What it says
The Calendar of State Papers, Venice, vol. 21, no. 515, covers it: Cottington’s journey dropped after Buckingham’s death, and Rubens negotiating a truce with the Dutch.
Still open
The key was not rebuilt. Ciphertext-only annealing and alignment gave nothing; it needs a group-exact transcription and more anchors.

01 The record

  • 8951–8954: the despatch, addressed “Ser:mo Principe”. Clear news paragraphs (the Nuncio Monti, the Marquis of Vigliena, Spinola, a rumour that Monterrey had died at Genoa) alternate with long cipher paragraphs marked 8 and ‡ in the margin.
  • 8955–8959: the decipherment, in another hand. It gives the whole letter in clear, clear passages included, with the ciphered paragraphs numbered 6, 7, 8 and 9.
  • 8960–8964: a second copy, enciphered with different groups (1,405 against 840 for the same text), dated “Madrid li 26 Ott:re 1628”. DECODE’s transcription gives the year as 1606.
  • 8965: address leaf, numbered 74. 8966: a deciphered page of Mocenigo’s despatch of 29 October 1628, CSP Venice 21 no. 520 (long consultations on English and Dutch trade, a licence said to have been got by a Jesuit for some 100,000 crowns).

The DECODE images are not in the public domain and may be published only with the archive’s permission, so none are reproduced here.

02 The cipher

The groups are numbers written as clusters. The looped sign that looks like b is the period’s 6, and the s-like sign is a 5, which DECODE’s transcription renders as “13” or “21”. Every group has the form 5 or 6, then an optional 1–4, then two digits. After the first digit only 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8 and 9 occur. The two copies have about 2,245 groups of 355 kinds. The group 6499 marks the edges of the numbered paragraphs. Each group stands for about two letters of plaintext: paragraph 6 (“Et già si è publicato… di ogni gran Casa di Spagna”) has 386 groups for about 760 letters, and paragraph 7 (“Continuo io… in alcun tempo”) has 245 for about 445. That makes it a syllabic cipher or nomenclator, not a simple homophonic alphabet.

Recovering the key from the ciphertext alone failed. Letter-by-letter homophonic annealing against a Renaissance Italian model gave gibberish, although the same solver decrypts a synthetic Italian homophonic cipher of the same size without error. Treating whole prefix classes as nulls did not help either. A monotone alignment of the groups against the decipherment, seeded with the Olivares runs, settled into even two-letter splits and gave no usable values. Rebuilding the key would need a fresh, group-exact transcription and more anchors.

03 The decipherment

The two ciphered paragraphs checked against the cipher, from the decipherment (8955–8956):

[6] Et già si è publicato, che seguirà anco il Re alla giornata, persistendo l’Ecc.mo nelle prime risolutioni, che il Re ò abbracci le tregue, overo le faccia assignam.ti pronti, effettivi, et sufficienti per le imprese necessarie. Il Conte di Olivares ha desiderato con buone parole, et promesse condurlo à risolutione di ritornar in Fiandra; anco con qualche violente termine lo hà procurato, ma il Marchese constantem.te è stato ne’ primi propositi, nè hà potuto non ricever molti disgusti. Continuano quelli delli conti, che le sono dimandati, et quello che più lo affligge è haver dato sua figlia in moglie à Don Diego Messia, il quale è mantenuto dal Conte di Olivares nel posto di auttorità et gratia che haveva prima, se le diminuisse la speranza del titolo di Grande, et resta la figliola con minor fortuna di quella che senza il favore del Conte di Olivares poteva conseguire con il parentado di ogni gran Casa di Spagna.

[7] Continuo io nelli affari propri con molti soldati di S. M.tà, li quali si offerseno di nuovo al servitio della Ser.ma; alcuni sono officiali di molta esperienza, et con tanto maggior affetto le corrispondo; sono la maggior parte Napolitani de’ terzi reformati… et però certa cosa, che il Re non può render tutti contenti, et molti con ragione si vegono abbandonati, et altri senza le sodisfattioni de’ proprij crediti, et desperati di poter li conseguire in alcun tempo.

That is: it is given out that the King will go on campaign. Spinola holds to his first position, that the King must either accept the truce with the Dutch or give him ready and sufficient funds for the campaign. Olivares has tried fair words and harsher means to send him back to Flanders, and has failed. Spinola is also harried over his accounts, and grieved that he married his daughter to Diego Mesía, whom Olivares keeps in favour while holding back the grandeeship. Paragraph 7 reports Mocenigo’s recruiting of officers left unpaid by Philip IV, most of them Neapolitans from disbanded tercios, for the service of the Republic. Paragraphs 8 and 9 (8957–8959) cover Savoy’s campaign against Genoa over Oneglia and Ormea, Cottington’s cancelled journey, Rubens, Catalonia and Valencia, and Savoy’s bid for the government of Milan.

Grades: the decipherment is contemporary (H). The transcription above is a first pass from the scans. Two words are doubtful (M): “prime risolutioni” and “terzi reformati”.

04 What remains open

  • The key. It is a syllabic nomenclator; “conte di Olivares” = 672 2133 6210 2190 62x9 637 2127 in both copies is the only group run tied to text so far.
  • A full transcription of the decipherment pages 8955–8959. DECODE carries only a rough one.
  • DECODE R1862 (Busta 27 f. 354, 1628, four pages, “Ser.mo Principe”) is in the same cipher; DECODE places it at Rome, so it may be another Venetian despatch rather than Mocenigo’s. It has not been examined beyond its first page.

# R1874, Alvise Mocenigo to the Doge and Senate, Madrid, 26 October 1628: the contemporary decipherment

## Reading

[5] Si dispone tutte le cose per la partenza del duca di Alcala, vice re di Napoli, e cattivo augurio di quelli che la tengono per dubiosa, scoprendosi sempre nove mutationi et variationi in tutte le cose; et quello che e stato promesso ad uno, l'altro e conferito ad un altro soggetto, di modo che se per il passato erano prima certe deliberationi eseguite, che il tutto hora non si sa qual provisione possi haver la resolutione, che tutte notte si muta. Il marchese Spinola, che e rimasto fin hora nella casa del marchese di Leganes, suo genero, ha fatto accomodare la sua propria, nella quale viveva Monte Rei, et pare sia habitata con tutte le circostantie che indicano dover lui trattenersi longamente in corte; et gia si e publicato che seguira anco il re alla giornata, persistendo l'eccellentissimo nelli primi risolutioni, che il re o abbracci le tregue overo le faccia assignamenti pronti, effettivi et sufficienti per le imprese necessarie.

[6] Il conte di Olivares ha desiderato con buone parole et promesse condurlo a risolutione di ritornar in Fiandra; anco con qualche violente termine lo ha procurato, ma il marchese constantemente e stato ne' primi propositi, ne ha potuto non ricever molti disgusti. Continuano quelli delli conti che le sono dimandati; et quello che piu lo affligge e haver dato sua figliola in moglie a don Diego Messia, il quale e mantenuto dal conte di Olivares nel posto di auttorita et gratia che haveva prima, se le diminuisse la speranza del titolo di grande, et resta la figliola con minor fortuna di quella che senza il favore del conte di Olivares poteva conseguire con il parentado di ogni gran casa di Spagna.

[7] Continuo io nelli affari propri con molti soldati di Sua Maesta, li quali si offerseno di nuovo al servitio della Serenissima Republica. Alcuni sono officiali di molta esperienza, et con tanto maggior affetto le corrispondo. Sono la maggior parte napolitani de' tercij reformati, et mi trattengo ne' generali per esser ordinariamente verbosa et legera questa natione; et pero certa cosa che il re non puo render tutti contenti, et molti con ragione si vegono abbandonati, et altri senza le sodisfattioni de' proprij crediti et disperati di poterli conseguire in alcun tempo.

[9] Anco della mossa del signor duca di Savoia contro Genovesi mi viene riferito esser con concerto et de' Spagnoli et de' medesimi Genovesi, perche vi pensi Sua Altezza le terre di Oneglia, di Ormea, con li altri luochi che restavano nelle forze della Republica. Si sono mostrati Genovesi risoluti a non voler restituire la galea, come intendo, et le artegliarie; onde per questo altro camino, senza il mezo de' Francesi, carcano Spagnoli haver aggiustato quelle differenze.

[10] Si sono provveduti dodeci cavalli d'ordine di Sua Maesta, li quali con altri regali di cose d'odore si preparano d'inviare a Turino in dono al signor duca.

[11] Al padre Caetano, teatino, al quale sempre hanno servito li cocchi del re et si sono continuamente inviati rinfrescamenti, si da una chiesa ultimamente vacata nel regno di Napoli, et sara questo fatto confidente de' Spagnoli per ogni occasione di loro servitio. Hano il nevono di Ventimiglia et l'ambasciatore ordinario conceputo gran speranze di conseguire una grossa summa di danaro, ricercata a nome di Sua Altezza a conto de' suoi crediti con il re, di dote et altro; et pure si pensava a provedere a questo effetto di seicento mila scudi. Difficilmente pero si crede che habbia a seguire esborso alcuno nella congiontura presente, per la strettezza che ha il re, et perche carcano Spagnoli sostenere sempre con nuove speranze il duca, del quale ben sanno quanto si possono fidare et come habbino a governarsi con lui. Al tempo che Sua Maesta sara in Barcelona dicono che li principi, et forse Sua Altezza medesima, si condurra a quella volta con galee per baciarle le mani.

[12] Ha il duca fatto passar ufficio dalli suoi ambasciatori di ricever nel porto Villa Franca la regina d'Ungaria in questo suo passaggio; ne da parte alcuna si tralasciano le migliori corrispondenze, per quanto si scopre sin hora.

[13] Si accrescono li disgusti de' Genovesi per le operationi sinistre, come intendo, di Monte Rei et del marchese di Castagneda. Commissioni sono arrivate alli ambasciatori qui, per le quali e partito per l'Escuriale; ne possono per altra via esser pervenute che con il corriero inviato dalli ambasciatori di Spagna.

[14] Doveva, come ho penetrato, passare a questi regni il secretario Cottington di ordine del re d'Inghilterra, et il passaggio suo a punto era per seguire in questo tempo che e stata sopra la costa di Francia l'armata, dalla quale si spiccavano a questo effetto doi o tre vascelli per metter il ministro in luoco sicuro; ma e svanito questo dissegno doppo la morte di Buchingam, et don Andimion Porter, che si trova qui, e senza alcuna commissione e sta aspettando che le sopragiongino nuovi ordini. Il Ruben continua le trattationi di tregue con Olandesi, et secondo le occasioni Spagnoli haveranno disposto, come si puo persuadere ognuno, la trattatione per risolvere quello le sara di maggior servitio.

[15] Sempre piu si vanno essacerbando li animi di quei popoli, et nelle corti che ha da tenere il re, cosi in Barcelona come in Valentia, non e lontano che possa succedere alcun inconveniente, se il conte di Olivares non rimette del vigore et severita con che tratta quei affari.

[16] Li ministri del signor duca di Savoia hano, come vengo avvisato, fatto officij perche non sia risoluta l'espeditione del duca di Feria in governatore di Milano, et gia pare che non piu si parli di lui. Dalli confidenti del duca intendo non voler Sua Eccellenza andare a quel governo senza certe promesse de assegnamenti che ricerca; se fosse vero l'avviso della morte di Monte Rei, pretenderebbe esser presidente del Consiglio d'Italia, et nella penuria de soggetti di valore puo molto avanzarsi il duca in auttorita, tutto che non sij confidente del conte di Olivares. Gratie.

# R1874, Alvise Mocenigo to the Doge and Senate, Madrid, 26 October 1628: the cipher paragraphs, English translation

[5] Everything is being arranged for the departure of the Duke of Alcalá, Viceroy of Naples, and it is a bad omen for
those who hold it doubtful, since new changes and variations keep appearing in all things; and what has been promised
to one is conferred on another subject, so that, whereas in the past certain decisions were carried out at once, now
no one knows what provision the resolution may have, since it changes every night. The Marquis Spinola, who has
stayed until now in the house of the Marquis of Leganés, his son-in-law, has had his own house made ready, in which
Monterrey used to live, and it seems to be inhabited with all the circumstances that indicate he must stay a long
time at court; and it is already made public that he will even follow the King on his journey, the Most Excellent
persisting in his first resolutions: that the King either embrace the truces or else make him prompt, effective and
sufficient assignments for the necessary enterprises.

[6] The Count of Olivares has wished, with good words and promises, to bring him to resolve to return to Flanders;
he has tried it even with some violent terms, but the Marquis has stood constantly by his first intentions, nor
could he avoid receiving many vexations. Those over the accounts that are demanded of him continue; and what afflicts
him most is to have given his daughter in marriage to Don Diego Messía, who is kept by the Count of Olivares in the
post of authority and favour that he had before, though his hope of the title of grandee has diminished, and the
daughter is left with a smaller fortune than she could have obtained, without the Count of Olivares's favour, by
alliance with any great house of Spain.

[7] I continue in my own business with many soldiers of His Majesty, who have offered themselves anew for the
service of the Most Serene Republic. Some are officers of much experience, and I respond to them with all the more
warmth. Most of them are Neapolitans of the disbanded tercios, and I keep to generalities, this nation being
ordinarily wordy and light; and yet it is a certain thing that the King cannot make everyone content, and many with
reason see themselves abandoned, and others without satisfaction of their own credits and despairing of ever
obtaining it.

[9] Also concerning the move of the Lord Duke of Savoy against the Genoese, I am told that it is in concert both with
the Spaniards and with the Genoese themselves, because His Highness is thinking of the lands of Oneglia and Ormea,
with the other places that remained in the Republic's hands. The Genoese have shown themselves resolved not to
return the galley, as I understand, and the artillery; so the Spaniards are seeking to have settled those
differences by this other road, without the mediation of the French.

[10] Twelve horses have been procured by order of His Majesty, which, with other gifts of perfumes, are being
prepared to be sent to Turin as a present to the Lord Duke.

[11] To Father Caetano, the Theatine, who has always had the King's coaches at his service and to whom refreshments
have continually been sent, a church lately vacant in the Kingdom of Naples is being given, and this will make him a
confidant of the Spaniards on every occasion of their service. The nephew of Ventimiglia and the resident ambassador
have conceived great hopes of obtaining a large sum of money, requested in His Highness's name on account of his
credits with the King for dowry and other things; and indeed it was being considered to provide six hundred thousand
scudi for this purpose. It is hardly believed, however, that any payment will follow at the present juncture,
because of the King's straits, and because the Spaniards seek always to sustain the Duke with new hopes, knowing
well how far they can trust him and how they must conduct themselves with him. When His Majesty is in Barcelona, they
say that the princes, and perhaps His Highness himself, will travel there with galleys to kiss his hands.

[12] The Duke has had his ambassadors make representations offering to receive the Queen of Hungary in the port of
Villafranca on this passage of hers; nor is the best correspondence neglected on any side, as far as can be seen so
far.

[13] The Genoese's vexations are growing because of the sinister doings, as I understand, of Monterrey and of the
Marquis of Castañeda. Commissions have arrived for the ambassadors here, on account of which he has left for the
Escorial; nor can they have come by any other way than with the courier sent by the ambassadors of Spain.

[14] Secretary Cottington, as I have found out, was to come to these kingdoms by order of the King of England, and
his passage was to take place precisely at this time, when the fleet was off the coast of France, from which two or
three vessels were to be detached for this purpose to put the minister in a safe place; but this plan has vanished
since Buckingham's death, and Don Endymion Porter, who is here, is without any commission and is waiting for new
orders to reach him. Rubens continues the negotiations for truces with the Dutch, and, as anyone may be persuaded,
the Spaniards will have arranged the negotiation according to circumstances, so as to settle what will be of
greatest service to them.

[15] The minds of those peoples are growing ever more exasperated, and in the Cortes that the King has to hold, both in
Barcelona and in Valencia, it is not unlikely that some trouble may occur, if the Count of Olivares does not abate the
vigour and severity with which he handles those affairs.

[16] The Lord Duke of Savoy's ministers have, as I am informed, made representations so that the dispatch of the Duke
of Feria as Governor of Milan should not be resolved, and it already seems that he is no longer spoken of. From the
Duke's confidants I understand that His Excellency does not wish to go to that government without certain promises
of the assignments he requests; if the report of Monterrey's death were true, he would claim to be President of the
Council of Italy, and in the dearth of men of worth the Duke may advance greatly in authority, although he is not a
confidant of the Count of Olivares. Thanks.
