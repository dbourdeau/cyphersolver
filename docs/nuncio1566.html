Cardinal Alessandrino to the nuncio in Spain, 1567

→

Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Spagna 1, ff. 165–324 · DECODE R85–R90, six ciphertexts, ten pages · catalogued as “unknown sender to unknown recipient, 1566”

Sender and year identified from the archive’s description of the volume. George Lasry’s key (2020) is attached to the records, with no plaintext posted. Applied with a language model it gave only fragments; a contemporary decipherment of one letter and three copies of another, found among the record’s images, supplied the missing code values, and all six are now deciphered word by word.

At a glanceKnown key applied · complete

The letter
Six ciphered enclosures of Cardinal Alessandrino (the cardinal nephew) to the nuncio in Spain, Giovan Battista Castagna, in 1567, 7,817 digits in all.
Where
Vatican, ASV i. 1025, Segretario di Stato, Spanish nunciature papers; DECODE R85–R90 (catalogue item 261), listed with no sender, no recipient and the volume’s opening date, 1 January 1566.
The cipher
A polyphonic cipher where each digit stands for two letters, with a nomenclator of four-digit groups, dotted pairs and 0 as a null.
How it was read
George Lasry’s 2020 key, attached to the records with no plaintext posted, is the same key byte for byte that decrypted Alessandrino’s 1568 letters. The same decoder, run unchanged, resolves all six.
What it says
The Pope wants Philip II to make his journey to Flanders; the archbishop of Cologne is to be deprived; the King of France should imprison the Constable Montmorency and his son until Condé and Coligny lay down their arms.
Still open
One recurring word, some dotted groups outside Lasry’s list and single-letter choices. The printed correspondence (Serrano 1914) could not be reached.

01 The documents and the key

DECODE lists the six as “ASV i. 1025, Segretario di Stato, doss. I”, records ASV_i1025_SdS_Spain_I-1 to I-6, with the archive’s list of the volume’s units in the comments. Their folios are 165r (R85), 236r (R86), 249r–250r (R87), 301r–v (R88), 314r–v (R89) and 324r (R90): 7,817 digits in all, transcribed for DECODE by RebAn in 2016. Each cipher is the enciphered part of a letter that opens in clear: f. 163r, before R85, is about hurrying the archbishop of Toledo, Bartolomé Carranza, to Rome; f. 299r, before R88, is about “il negotio di Milano”. No decipherment is written on those pages.

Lasry’s key “ASV – S6” (24 October 2020), on every record: 1 u|t · 2 i|m · 3 f|g · 4 a|l · 5 o|s · 6 e|n · 7 d|r · 8 b|c · 9 p|z · 0 null, with a nomenclator of four-digit groups X0Y0 (1010 Nostro Signore, 2090 che, 3010 Re, 4030 S. M.tà, 8010 Re di Francia …) and dotted pairs. The full table is on the 1568 page. The decipherment of R87 and the copies corrected and extended it (22 October 2026): 8010 is [la] Regina [di Francia], the Queen Mother, not the King; 4010 is S.M. (Philip II in R87, Charles IX in R85, R88 and R89), not Sua Santità; 1020 essere, 1040 lettera, 8030 religione, 8070 ugonotta, 8080 heretici, 8090 havere; 3 0̇ 9 is qua, and 209 is chi as a syllable.

02 “Put the Constable in a secure prison”

R88 and R89 are two copies of one cipher, and R90 continues R89; the damaged figures of R88 (rows 10–17 of each page) are supplied from R89. From the reading of R89 (f. 314r–v, texts/R89_reading.md):

… ma [la] Regina è tanto timida et crede tanto al cancelliere, a Morviglier, a Limoges et a l’Abaspina, suoi consiglieri, che non si sa risolvere a far cosa buona, et va sempre medicando il male col peggio. … Et per dirvi, [la] Regina a iuditio mio non è ugonotta, credo bene che sia una mala cristiana … In tanto vi dirò questo solo, che se S.M. facesse mettere in una prigione sicura il Conestabile et Memoransi suo figliolo, protestandoli che se in termine di tre giorni non facessero deponere le armi al Amiraglio et al principe di Condé li faria tagliare il capo, vedereste che si metteria fine a tanti tumulti assai presto …

[la] marks a word the code carries without the article; … marks a stretch left out here.

“The Queen is so timid, and trusts so much in the Chancellor, Morvilliers, Limoges and l’Aubespine, her counsellors, that she cannot bring herself to do any good, but always doctors the evil with worse … in my judgement she is no Huguenot; I truly believe she is a bad Christian … Meanwhile I will tell you only this: if His Majesty had the Constable Montmorency and his son put in a secure prison, warning them that unless within three days they made the Admiral and the Prince lay down their arms he would have their heads cut off, you would see an end to these troubles, and soon, for it is believed the whole plot was laid with the knowledge of the Constable and his son … and the Queen will find herself deceived one day, and repent too late of not having believed the good.” The passage is a chapter of a letter from France to a gentleman at the Roman court, passed on so that the nuncio can use it with Philip II. Earlier decodes had made the subject the King of France: code 8010, which the decipherment of R87 shows to be the Queen Mother. The Prince is Condé and the Admiral Coligny, in arms after the Surprise of Meaux (28 September 1567); the Constable died of his wounds at Saint-Denis on 12 November. The letter therefore belongs to October or early November 1567. R90 (f. 324r) carries the close: the King should be “instrutta delli humori di quella corte et della natura di [la] Regina principalmente”, and should let the Queen know that “poco fideli li saranno quelli che sono infideli a Dio”. Its second sentence is also written in clear on f. 302r of the R88 copy.

03 All six, in brief

  • R85, f. 165r. The Pope wants to be sure whether the King’s journey will take place, because if so he would meet him “in quello loco che li fusse più comodo”. The Queen of France and her son wish to be asked to a parley with Philip; the Pope does not know how far to trust “parole di donne”, but urges the King not to doubt that such a colloquio could bring “gran frutto alla religione catolica”. Two code groups (3050, 3020) are open.
  • R86, f. 236r. Milan: the Pope means to proceed against the authors of the attack on the archbishop’s jurisdiction, above all the senate, “con le sue armi spirituali”; the dignity of the Holy See requires proceeding at least against the Capitano di giustizia and the fiscal, “il quale ha fatto il peggio di tutti”, and some of the senate; the King should deprive his two ministers of their office. One code group (2020) is open.
  • R87, ff. 249r–250r. Read with its contemporary decipherment (f. 251r). The Pope wants the King’s journey to Flanders, for religion there, in France and in Germany, and would have him invite the Queen of France to a colloquio. Then: “N.S. vuole ancora che V.S. facia intendere a Re che l’arcivescovo di Colonia eletto già sono molti anni non à mai ottenuto la confirmatione da questa santa sede”, nor made the profession of faith “in quella forma che ordina il Concilio di Trento”; he is treating with the Duke of Cleves; the Pope means to proceed to his deprivation, and since “il capitolo di Colonia non è in tutto catolico”, the King should see that it does not choose a suspect person. Friedrich von Wied resigned in 1567.
  • R88, f. 301r–v; R89, f. 314r–v. France, above; one group of R88 (7 3 8) is unread.
  • R90, f. 324r. The close of the France letter: the Queen Mother does not have the good counsellors she thinks she can trust.

04 Method

The decoder is targets/alessandrino1568/decrypt.py, copied without change: a search that tries, at each digit, both of its letters, the null, and any nomenclator group starting there, and keeps the best-scoring partial texts under the repository’s shared Italian five-gram language model (it-cinquecento, no spaces). The six pages decode at −1.7 to −1.9 nats a letter, the level of the 1568 letters, and end in a few letters of clerk’s padding. The two copies R88 and R89, decoded independently, give the same text wherever their transcriptions are clean, which is a check on both the key and the letter choices.

The re-reading of 22 October 2026. The character decode above gives fragments, not sentences. R87’s contemporary decipherment on f. 251r (its top lines lost; it stops at “resistere”) was transcribed and aligned with the figures, and R88, R89 and R90 were compared with each other and against the clear sentence on f. 302r. These fixed the code values above, and every letter was then deciphered word by word on the figures, every word aligned with its figures (research/reading_audit/b09_align.py), with twelve transcription labels corrected on the images. Of the words, 169 of 190 (R85), 155 of 164 (R86), 437 of 448 (R87), 326 of 361 (R88), 336 of 349 (R89) and 45 of 48 (R90) are carried by their figures; the rest are bracketed. The readings, texts/R85_reading.md to R90_reading.md, have English beside them.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Three code groups are not resolved: 3050 and 3020 (in this use) in R85, and 2020 in R86. A few words are only partly carried and marked [?word] (R86 [?quel] fatto, [?havere], [?via]); in R85 one group is blotted and one run ticked through; in R88 one group (7 3 8) is unread.
  • The group earlier decoded as “colospo” is colloquio (col-lo + 509 qui + o), resolved by the R87 decipherment.
  • The last four rows of R87 rest on the figures alone, since the decipherment stops at “resistere”; where the decipherer and the figures differ (dal qual, ma perché, esso pretenso arcivescovo, o dependente), the figures are followed.
  • Not compared with Serrano’s edition of the Pius V correspondence (1914), which prints Alessandrino–Castagna letters and may give some of these in clear; it could not be reached (HathiTrust 403, not on the Internet Archive).

R85, Spagna 1 item 1, f. 165r [1567]

Rome, 8 Jan 1567: the Pope wants to know whether the King's journey will go ahead, because he would then meet him wherever suits him; the Queen Mother and the King of France wish Philip to invite them to a parley; the Pope distrusts "words of women" but urges the King to admit, even seek, the meeting, leaving it to his prudence, and offers to act as common father.

### f. 249r

His Holiness wishes the King to go to Flanders as soon as possible, trusting in God that this journey of his must set
the world to rights and be a reform of all Christendom; and therefore he thinks that it will serve to restore religion
not only in that province but also in France and in Germany. His Holiness is given to understand that the Queen [Mother]
of France has a very great desire to be invited by the King to a meeting, from which it is believed great good and
profit could be drawn for Christendom, and that the King, who as far as is known seeks nothing but the service of God
and the public good, will obtain whatever he knows how to ask. And although His Holiness does not see that he has so far
obtained anything from His Majesty that is in the service of God, nor even of reform, but that he puts everything off
to a more convenient time,

### f. 249v

His Holiness nevertheless judges that it would be well for the King to invite the Queen of France to a meeting, to
satisfy the world and for the greater service of his own affairs too, because if the ruin of France is not repaired by
this journey, His Holiness thinks little other hope can be had of it. His Holiness further wishes Your Lordship to let
the King know that the archbishop-elect of Cologne, elected many years ago now, has never obtained confirmation from this
Holy See, because His Holiness does not mean to confirm him unless he first makes the profession of faith in the form
the Council of Trent prescribes; and that it is understood that the said elect is making certain treaties or alliances
with the Duke of Cleves, who is held [by] His Holiness to be little of a Catholic, and that together they are concerting
to make of the archbishopric a mere temporal dominion. Therefore His Holiness

### f. 250r

means to proceed against him to deprivation; but because, as the devil is wont to do, he will come to stir up many of
his members to oppose this design, he wishes the King to go to Flanders as soon as possible and see, with his
confidants and vassals, to prevent such a work, because besides being very harmful to Germany it would be no less
harmful to the King's Low Countries. It is also understood that this so-called archbishop is negotiating to resign the
church; and because the chapter of Cologne is not wholly Catholic, it is feared that schemes may be made to designate it
to a suspect person, or one dependent on heretics, or weak, so that he cannot resist whoever will attack the
ecclesiastical state of that archbishopric. So it is wished that the King will perform the office that is hoped of him,
and that His Holiness be [?then] informed of everything.

# R88 — English (Cardinal Alessandrino to the nuncio Castagna, cipher of Rome, 4 November 1567)

Translated from `R88_reading.md`; holes as there. Same text as R89 + R90 (another copy); differences are of spelling

### f. 301r

As to the troubles of France, if the Queen [Mother] will act in earnest they will soon end, for one may reckon this a
fire of straw; but the Queen is so timid, and trusts so much in the Chancellor, in Morvillier, in Limoges and in
l'Aubespine, her counsellors, that she cannot resolve to do anything good, and keeps treating the evil with something
worse. And believe me, she does not sin from ignorance, for everything that has happened to her was foretold to her many
times; but there is so much ambition to rule in her that there is not as much in the whole world. And to tell you, the
Queen in my judgement is not a Huguenot, but I do believe she is a bad Christian, and that neither love nor charity nor
even justice reigns in her, letting herself be persuaded that keeping the kingdom divided is the true way to rule; and
how much she deceives herself anyone can easily see. If I had the chance to speak with you I would disclose a great
many things that I will not commit to the pen. Meanwhile I will tell you only this: that if His Majesty had the
Constable and Montmorency his son put into a safe prison, declaring

### f. 301v

to them that unless within three days they made the Admiral and the Prince of Condé lay down their arms he would have
their heads cut off, you would see an end put to so many troubles very soon; because it is believed that this plot was
laid entirely with the knowledge of the Constable and his son, and so it is feared they may make some shameful
patch-up; and it cannot be otherwise, the Constable being with His Majesty, who will lead him to do whatever he wants.
And the Queen, who believes she will rule by some means, will find herself deceived one day, and will repent too late of
not having been willing to believe the good. […] I have wished to share with you this chapter of a letter that came from
outside to a gentleman at this court, so that you may confer it with the King, so that, should he think fit to do some
office with that Majesty, he may do it on firmer ground, assuring her that the said letter is written by a person very
well informed of the humours of that court and above all of the nature of the Queen.

### f. 314r

worse. And believe me, she does not sin from ignorance, for everything that has happened to her was foretold to her many
times; but there is so much ambition to rule in her that there is not as much in the whole world. And to tell you, the
Queen in my judgement is not a Huguenot, but I do believe she is a bad Christian, and that neither love nor charity nor
even justice reigns in her, letting herself be persuaded that keeping the kingdom divided is the true way to rule; and
how much she deceives herself anyone can easily see. If I had the chance to speak with you I would disclose a great
many things that I will not commit to the

Rests on: the figures (DECODE transcription, RebAn 2016) read word by word with the key as extended by the R87

second sentence is written in clear in the copy of the same letter with R88, f. 302r: "Sarebbe forse bene che S. M.

## Reading

### f. 324r

instrutta delli humori di quella corte et della natura di [la] Regina principalmente. Saria forse bene che Re facesse
sapere a [la] Regina che ella non à quelli buoni consiglieri appresso di sé de' quali crede potersi fidare, perché poco
fideli li saranno quelli che sono infideli a Dio.

## Notes on the figures

- "della natura di [la] Regina": 7 6 4 4 | 6 4 1 1 7 4 | 7 2 | 8 0 1 0 (8010 = [la] Regina [di Francia], as in R87's decipherment).
- "sapere": 5 4 5 0 2 6 — sa + 502 (the code for "per") + e.
- "Re facesse sapere a [la] Regina": 3010 (Re, the Catholic King; f. 302r "S. M. Cat.ca") ... 4 0 8 0 1 0 (f. 302r
  "alla Regina di Francia").
- The row ends with 9 7 3 0^. 9 8 3 7 9 3, the closing run of nulls also seen at the end of R85 and R87.

# R90 — English (Cardinal Alessandrino to the nuncio Castagna, last part of the cipher of [4 November 1567])

Translated from `R90_reading.md`; it continues R89 ("written by a person very well ...").

### f. 324r

informed of the humours of that court and above all of the nature of the Queen [Mother]. It would perhaps be well for
the King to let the Queen know that she does not have those good counsellors about her whom she thinks she can trust,
because those who are unfaithful to God will be little faithful to her.
