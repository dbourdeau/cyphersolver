  • not solvedThe Indus script: Parpola’s decipherment and 2,364 registered testsposted 24 Sept 2026c. 2600–1900 BCEAsko Parpola’s Dravidian readings checked on two independent corpora, then 2,364 hypotheses registered before testing (1,224 held, 1,140 failed), with Linear B and Ur III seal legends as controls. The grammar a reading must fit is set out: an ending slot chosen by the name’s last sign, line breaks that never split a word unit, numerals fixed with what they count. Seal names are individual compositions, as Linear B personal names are. No sign value is established.
  • not solvedLinear A: how far the tablets can be read without knowing the languageposted 24 Sept 2026c. 1800–1450 BCLinear A is a script with an unknown language, not a cipher, and no bilingual text exists. Tested against a random null, the Linear B sound values carry over (six word matches against 0.49 expected), and 41.8% of the corpus gives sense without the language: numbers, fractions, commodities, KU-RO totals, KI-RO deficits and three place names. Tablet HT 34 contradicts the published values of the fraction signs H and A; the proposed Semitic and Luwian relatives fit no better than Hawaiian or Yoruba. Sixty-seven rounds of pre-registered tests describe the language’s sound shape (rare e and o, standing mainly after R and T; consonant harmony), the Minoan names that survive at Knossos, and the scribes’ bookkeeping, until new tests stopped yielding results.
