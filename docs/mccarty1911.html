El Paso → San Antonio · digraphic table, the “Clave G.A.” card · 29 March and 19 April 1911 · deciphered with a known key, read in part

González Garza to “John McCarty”, El Paso, March–April 1911

Go to the full text ↓

Two coded telegrams of the Maderista junta in Texas, one on how “Dr”, probably Madero, and “Ag” parted on the road to Chihuahua, deciphered with a key card from the same archive that was first tested on eight telegrams deciphered at the time. Two telegrams of 5 May 1911 in the same card follow: one, on seized goods and a Captain Lewis, was deciphered at the time; the other gives only fragments.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 12 November 2026 · updated 24 November 2026

Method · Clave G.A. telegrams (1432.1-4, 1561.7-13): known key applied (partial) · CMXV.17.1695.1-2 (5 May 1911): existing decipherment (complete) · CMXV.17.1695.2-2 (5 May 1911): decoded, not read: known key applied (none)

The telegrams
CMXV.15.1432.1-4, 29 March 1911, Federico González Garza (El Paso) to “John McCarty”, 437 Main Ave., San Antonio, the cover name of Adolfo González: six coded words in a clear Spanish sentence. And CMXV.16.1561.7-13, 19 April 1911, González Garza to Adolfo González: four coded words. Added from pull request #831: CMXV.17.1695.1-2 and 1695.2-2, two telegrams of 5 May 1911 to González Garza (as W. Olliphant) from Marfa and Presidio.
Where
Centro de Estudios de Historia de México (CEHM), Fundación Carlos Slim, Archivo Federico González Garza, Fondo CMXV. Part of catalogue entry 1462; not on DECODE.
The cipher
A digraphic table of two rows and fifteen columns from the archive’s key sheet (“Clave G.A. (una de las usadas en San Antonio)”, CMXV.16.1557.1): a plain letter is one of five row letters followed by its column letter, and the coded groups of a run are read together two letters at a time.
How it was read
The card, applied unchanged, was first tested on eight sibling telegrams whose clear text was written on the sheets at the time: agreement 0.866 on 769 cipher letters against at most 0.257 for 400 controls, thresholds fixed in advance. It decodes all ten coded words of the two telegrams. The telegram of 5 May from Marfa was deciphered at the time (a pencil gloss on the form, and the whole text in the typed list CMXV.18.1795.1); the card gives the same name.
What it says
On the 13th Dr and Ag separated at Galeana, Ag taking the road to Guerrero and Dr that to Chihuahua, camping on the 23rd at Encinillas; and, on 19 April, Dr wants Elías to come too, the old key stays in use while the crisis lasts because it is quicker, and funds and ammunition are to be sent at once. On 5 May: goods sent by (or to) Captain Lewis have been seized, and the invoices are to be sent with José Pineda, the coded name, to claim them back.
Still open
Two words of the first telegram (GALEANA, GUERRERO) rest on a faulty or lost pair repaired by the sense: 18 of its 20 words give sense (90%), under the 95% bar. The class “read in part” is set by this telegram alone (1432.1-4, 90%); the other telegram, 21 of 21 words, is read. “Ag” is not identified. The second telegram of 5 May (1695.2-2) gives only fragments with the card. No person compared the transcriptions with the images; an AI review compared the Marfa telegram of 5 May.

01 The telegrams

CMXV.15.1432.1-4 is the sender’s copy of a telegram on a Texas Telegraph Company form, typed, from Federico González Garza at El Paso, 29 March 1911. Six words are coded; the rest is clear Spanish: “El 13 separáronse en [coded] [coded] y [coded]; este tomó rumbo [coded] y aquel rumbo [coded], acampando 23 en [coded].” The CEHM record calls it “en clave”; no decipherment stands on the sheet and the telegram is not in the typed list of telegrams of the time, CMXV.18.1795.1, which gives the coded ones deciphered (its entries run to at least 116 on the CEHM scans, many in clear and some in English). CMXV.16.1561.7-13 is a typed copy of 19 April, without a gloss and not in 1795.1 either, with four coded words.

Two telegrams to González Garza (as W. Olliphant) of 5 May 1911 use the same card; Daniel Jalkut transcribed them from the CEHM images with Claude Code and applied the card (pull request #831). CMXV.17.1695.1-2 (CEHM record 12896), from J. González at Marfa, 8:58 pm, is clear Spanish with four coded groups, kopjg Ftskf Tlkap Lvnme. A pencil gloss over them gives “Jose Pineda”, and the typed list CMXV.18.1795.1 has the whole telegram as entry 113, which he found and which the review saw on the CEHM scan. Catalogue entry 1462 had it as partly deciphered, after the CEHM record, which already prints the name. CMXV.17.1695.2-2 (record 12897), from Presidio, 11:31 pm, has eight coded groups after garbled clear words about Luque and Mulato; its pencil gloss breaks off, and entry 114 of the typed list, right after 1695.1-2, is blank. The two sheets carry crossed pencil labels (“1695 (2-2)” on 1-2 and the reverse); the shelfmarks here follow the CEHM records. The contributor’s files, which also hold other telegrams of the entry, are in targets/duron1911/siblings.

The addressee “John McCarty” is, according to the typed list and the CEHM record, a cover name of Adolfo González. “Dr” is probably “Doctor”, the cover name of Madero in this traffic (the period glosses of sibling telegrams use “Dr” where 1795.1 types “Madero”); “Ag” is not identified. No further history is claimed here.

02 The card

The card, signed “Dr. V.G.”, is a table: the two rows have the row letters W Z C T G and M V P K I, and the fifteen columns the letters B L R A F Q E N H D X Y O S J. A plain letter is written as one of its row’s five letters followed by its column letter. The coded words of one run are taken together: in the telegram of 19 April the pairs run across the five-letter groups (PY ZY G|H VR IB), and the first attempt, group by group, failed. The card was transcribed from the CEHM image the day before the reading and not changed since; no second reader has checked it.

03 The test of the card

The card was tested once, with a pre-registered protocol, on eight sibling telegrams of the same traffic (March–April 1911) whose clear text was written in pencil over the coded words at the time. The cipher letters of the telegrams were transcribed blind (the scans were filtered so that the pencil glosses were erased), and the period clear texts afterwards; one telegram whose gloss could not be masked was dropped, and another, in a different system, was excluded by a filter that uses no value of the card. The statistic is the edit-distance agreement between the card’s decryption and the period clear text, against 400 controls (permuted tables and shuffled texts). The thresholds were fixed in advance: 0.85, a margin of 0.30 and at least 300 letters. Result: A = 0.866 on 769 cipher letters of 28 segments, against at most 0.257. We recomputed 0.8655 from the table of segments in the file; the scripts and the clear and cipher files of the test are kept outside the repository, so it was not re-run. The test passed; its order (hashes frozen before the decoding) is by the contributor’s record.

04 The reading

El 13 separáronse en {GALEANA} DR y AG; este tomó rumbo {GUERRERO} y aquel rumbo CHIH[uahua], acampando 23 en ENCINILLAS.

In English: on the 13th Dr and Ag separated at Galeana; the latter took the road to Guerrero and the former the road to Chihuahua, camping on the 23rd at Encinillas. Braces mark a word where one pair is faulty: the last pair of GALEANA is CA (m) for na, and GUERRERO has a stray EB with one pair lost; the sense fixes both. CHIH is the abbreviation of Chihuahua.

DR quiere venga también Elías. Mientras pasa crisis sigan usando por más rápida CLAVE antigua. Manden violentamente FONDOS y PARQUE, todas clases.

In English: Dr wants Elías to come too. While the crisis lasts, go on using the old key, since it is quicker. Send funds and ammunition of all kinds at once. All four coded words (KNGB = DR, PYZYG HVRIB = CLAVE, PLMJP AVNVJ ZF = FONDOS, MFZHC BZDCN PB = PARQUE) decode letter for letter. A telegram of the previous day ended with the clear words “nueva clave” (its own page); this telegram is itself in the card, which fits the contributor’s reading that “the old key” is the card.

Counted by the README rule: 1432.1-4 has 20 words, of which GALEANA and GUERRERO are restored by the sense: 18 of 20 read (90%; the contributor counts 19 of 20). 1561.7-13 has 21 words, all read. Together 39 of 41 (95.1%), but the first telegram alone is under the bar, so the target is read in part.

The telegram of 5 May from Marfa, as the typed list CMXV.18.1795.1 gives it (entry 113):

Nemi informa mercancías enviadas capitán Lewis decomisadas. Envíen facturas José Pineda para reclamarlas. Conviene llamen Lewis. Esperen carta. — J. González

In English: Nemi reports the goods sent [by] Captain Lewis seized. Send the invoices [with] José Pineda to claim them back. You should call Lewis. Await a letter. The telegram leaves out its prepositions; the two in brackets follow the CEHM record. Only José Pineda is coded. Joined and cut into pairs, the four groups give ko pj gF ts kf Tl ka pL vn me = j o s e p i n f d a with the card, one slip (pL, f for e), and the pencil gloss and the typed list give the name in full, so the two coded words are taken from the decipherment of the time. On the form, the clear words “Luis” and “Etamen” are Lewis and llamen, as the typed list has them, and the pencil word over “Luis” reads Lewis. These two words are outside the count above.

The second telegram of 5 May, from Presidio (1695.2-2), gives only aton at the start and …oucsenn?s with the card, where the broken pencil gloss also stops. It is shown in the full texts as decoded, not as a reading.

05 What remains uncertain

  • GALEANA and GUERRERO each rest on a faulty or lost pair repaired by the sense; only the original telegram form would show whether the slip is the sender’s or the typist’s.
  • “Ag” is not identified; the sources that might name him (the recipients’ papers, Narváez 2011 and 2019) were not searched, and the books are not available to the project.
  • No second reader has compared the card or the telegrams of March and April with the CEHM images; the images may not be redistributed, and the CEHM image server failed TLS verification in the integrating session of 12 November, so no image was compared there either. The review of pull request #831 reached the images over plain http and compared the Marfa telegram of 5 May and entry 113 of the typed list; that is a model’s comparison, not a person’s.
  • The Presidio telegram of 5 May (1695.2-2): the card gives only fragments of its 42 coded letters, the gloss of the time breaks off at the same places and the typed list leaves its place blank, so the decipherer of the time did not finish it either. Its image was not looked at in the review.
  • Whether the telegrams are in print is not known.

# Translation

On the 13th, Dr and Ag separated at Galeana; the latter took the road to Guerrero and the former the road to Chihuahua, camping on the 23rd at Encinillas.

González Garza.

"Dr" is probably "Doctor", the cover name for Francisco I. Madero in this correspondence (the period decipherment of a sibling telegram, CMXV.15.1458.4-6, writes "matar doctor", which the typed list CMXV.18.1795.1 glosses "(Madero)"; in CMXV.14.1392.1-2 and 1411.4-4 the period gloss "Dr" stands where 1795.1 types "Madero"). "Ag" is not identified (Abraham González, the Maderista leader in Chihuahua, would fit, but nothing on the sheet says so).

# Translation (CMXV.16.1561.7-13, 19 April 1911)

Dr wants Elías to come too. While the crisis lasts, go on using the old key, since it is quicker. Send funds and ammunition of all kinds at once.

González Garza.

"Parque" is the Mexican military word for ammunition and supplies. "The old key" is set against the new key of 18 April (CMXV.16.1551.3-4, `reading_grille.md`): the turning grille is slower to work than the Clave G.A. card.

# Translation (CMXV.17.1695.1-2, J. González, Marfa, 5 May 1911)

Nemi reports the goods sent [by] Captain Lewis seized. Send the invoices [with] José Pineda to claim them back. You should call Lewis. Await a letter.

J. González.

The Spanish is the decipherment of the time, typed in the list CMXV.18.1795.1, entry 113 (CEHM scan 21): "Nemi informa mercancías enviadas capitán Lewis decomisadas. Envíen facturas José Pineda para reclamarlas. Conviene llamen Lewis. Esperen carta." The four coded groups are the name José Pineda (the pencil gloss on the form; the Clave G.A. card gives josepinfda). The reading and the transcription are in `../duron1911/siblings/` (PR #831, Daniel Jalkut); this translation was made at the integration, 21 November 2026.

  Gobernador viene de Guerrero con 400. Franco sigue. Auxilio capitán Escudero ... Mándame de aquí auxilio ... Escaso
  [?Alanís vive]."

## CMXV.17.1695.1-2: J. González, Marfa, 5 May 1911 (Clave G.A. card). Existing decipherment

- The catalogue lists it as partly read, and the CEHM record prints "José Pineda". **The typed list of period
  decipherments CMXV.18.1795.1 has the whole telegram** (scan 21, entry 113): "Nemi informa mercancías enviadas capitán
  Lewis decomisadas. Envíen facturas José Pineda para reclamarlas. Conviene llamen Lewis. Esperen carta." So the clear
  words "Luis", "Etamen" of the form are Lewis and llamen.
- What this adds: the pencil gloss "José Pineda" covers all four coded groups (`kopjg Ftskf Tlkap Lvnme`), and the
  Clave G.A. card read in pairs gives the same (ko pj gF ts kf Tl ka pL vn me = j o s e p i n f d a; pL = f is a slip
  for e).

## CMXV.17.1695.2-2: "J. Gouzateo", Presidio, 5 May 1911 (Clave G.A. card, fragments). Not read

- `ykego ijkabji Gydyn birbh kukjg nkygf kbpap apgtf`: the card gives `aton` at the start (offset 1) and
  `...l..v..oucsenn?s` (offset 0), where the period pencil gloss also stops ("at ono ll e va oucpennos"). In 1795.1,
  entry 114, right after 1695.1-2 (entry 113), is left blank: perhaps the place kept for this telegram.

## Not read

- **CMXV.17.1648.2-3** (Gabriel, San Antonio, 27 Apr 1911): the Hernández swap and Clave G.A. cards do not read it,
  nor does a simple-substitution solver that recovers a control of the same length. The recipient wrote **"No se
  entiende"** across the form. In 1795.1, entry 103 (San Antonio, 1 May) is signed "Gabriel Madero" in full and entry 98
  (San Antonio, 30 April) "Adrían Gabriel", so the sender is perhaps Gabriel Madero; the 1795.1 page that would hold
  26-29 April (entries 94-97) is missing from the CEHM scans, so whether this telegram was typed there cannot be checked.
- **CMXV.34.3344.1** (Ávila to Villa, 15 Jun 1914, two-digit groups): the two-digit syllabary card of CMXV.16.1557.1
  scan 3 tried as is and with every additive digit offset (both orders, 200 variants): nothing reads.
- **CMXV.16.1561.10-13** is not in cipher. 1795.1 types it in clear (scan 12, entry 66): "Buhger necesita con urgencia
  otros cinco mil. B. Hernández."
