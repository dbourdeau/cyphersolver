El Paso → San Antonio · digraphic table, the “Clave G.A.” card · 29 March and 19 April 1911 · deciphered with a known key, read in part

González Garza to “John McCarty”, El Paso, March–April 1911

Go to the full text ↓

Two coded telegrams of the Maderista junta in Texas, one on how “Dr”, probably Madero, and “Ag” parted on the road to Chihuahua, deciphered with a key card from the same archive that was first tested on eight telegrams deciphered at the time.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 12 November 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: partial (95% of the cipher)

Contributed by Brice Dudout (solution) · Also credited · Contemporary key or decipherment (key or decipherment used) · Daniel Bourdeau (pointer to a source)

At a glanceKnown key applied · partial (95% of the cipher)

The telegrams
CMXV.15.1432.1-4, 29 March 1911, Federico González Garza (El Paso) to “John McCarty”, 437 Main Ave., San Antonio, the cover name of Adolfo González: six coded words in a clear Spanish sentence. And CMXV.16.1561.7-13, 19 April 1911, González Garza to Adolfo González: four coded words.
Where
Centro de Estudios de Historia de México (CEHM), Fundación Carlos Slim, Archivo Federico González Garza, Fondo CMXV. Part of catalogue entry 1462; not on DECODE.
The cipher
A digraphic table of two rows and fifteen columns from the archive’s key sheet (“Clave G.A. (una de las usadas en San Antonio)”, CMXV.16.1557.1): a plain letter is one of five row letters followed by its column letter, and the coded groups of a run are read together two letters at a time.
How it was read
The card, applied unchanged, was first tested on eight sibling telegrams whose clear text was written on the sheets at the time: agreement 0.866 on 769 cipher letters against at most 0.257 for 400 controls, thresholds fixed in advance. It decodes all ten coded words of the two telegrams.
What it says
On the 13th Dr and Ag separated at Galeana, Ag taking the road to Guerrero and Dr that to Chihuahua, camping on the 23rd at Encinillas; and, on 19 April, Dr wants Elías to come too, the old key stays in use while the crisis lasts because it is quicker, and funds and ammunition are to be sent at once.
Still open
Two words of the first telegram (GALEANA, GUERRERO) rest on a faulty or lost pair repaired by the sense: 18 of its 20 words give sense (90%), under the 95% bar. The class “read in part” is set by this telegram alone (1432.1-4, 90%); the other telegram, 21 of 21 words, is read. “Ag” is not identified; no one compared the transcriptions with the images.
Credit
Brice Dudout (with AI language-model agents): transcription of the card and of the telegrams, the test of the card and the readings. Daniel Bourdeau: catalogue entry 1462 and the pointer to the key cards. A different cipher of the same traffic, the “nueva clave” of 18–20 April, has its own page.

01 The telegrams

CMXV.15.1432.1-4 is the sender’s copy of a telegram on a Texas Telegraph Company form, typed, from Federico González Garza at El Paso, 29 March 1911. Six words are coded; the rest is clear Spanish: “El 13 separáronse en [coded] [coded] y [coded]; este tomó rumbo [coded] y aquel rumbo [coded], acampando 23 en [coded].” The CEHM record calls it “en clave”; no decipherment stands on the sheet and the telegram is not in the typed list of 66 period decipherments, CMXV.18.1795.1. CMXV.16.1561.7-13 is a typed copy of 19 April, without a gloss and not in 1795.1 either, with four coded words.

The addressee “John McCarty” is, according to the typed list and the CEHM record, a cover name of Adolfo González. “Dr” is probably “Doctor”, the cover name of Madero in this traffic (the period glosses of sibling telegrams use “Dr” where 1795.1 types “Madero”); “Ag” is not identified. No further history is claimed here.

02 The card

The card, signed “Dr. V.G.”, is a table: the two rows have the row letters W Z C T G and M V P K I, and the fifteen columns the letters B L R A F Q E N H D X Y O S J. A plain letter is written as one of its row’s five letters followed by its column letter. The coded words of one run are taken together: in the telegram of 19 April the pairs run across the five-letter groups (PY ZY G|H VR IB), and the first attempt, group by group, failed. The card was transcribed from the CEHM image the day before the reading and not changed since; no second reader has checked it.

03 The test of the card

The card was tested once, with a pre-registered protocol, on eight sibling telegrams of the same traffic (March–April 1911) whose clear text was written in pencil over the coded words at the time. The cipher letters of the telegrams were transcribed blind (the scans were filtered so that the pencil glosses were erased), and the period clear texts afterwards; one telegram whose gloss could not be masked was dropped, and another, in a different system, was excluded by a filter that uses no value of the card. The statistic is the edit-distance agreement between the card’s decryption and the period clear text, against 400 controls (permuted tables and shuffled texts). The thresholds were fixed in advance: 0.85, a margin of 0.30 and at least 300 letters. Result: A = 0.866 on 769 cipher letters of 28 segments, against at most 0.257. We recomputed 0.8655 from the table of segments in the file; the scripts and the clear and cipher files of the test are kept outside the repository, so it was not re-run. The test passed; its order (hashes frozen before the decoding) is by the contributor’s record.

04 The reading

El 13 separáronse en {GALEANA} DR y AG; este tomó rumbo {GUERRERO} y aquel rumbo CHIH[uahua], acampando 23 en ENCINILLAS.

In English: on the 13th Dr and Ag separated at Galeana; the latter took the road to Guerrero and the former the road to Chihuahua, camping on the 23rd at Encinillas. Braces mark a word where one pair is faulty: the last pair of GALEANA is CA (m) for na, and GUERRERO has a stray EB with one pair lost; the sense fixes both. CHIH is the abbreviation of Chihuahua.

DR quiere venga también Elías. Mientras pasa crisis sigan usando por más rápida CLAVE antigua. Manden violentamente FONDOS y PARQUE, todas clases.

In English: Dr wants Elías to come too. While the crisis lasts, go on using the old key, since it is quicker. Send funds and ammunition of all kinds at once. All four coded words (KNGB = DR, PYZYG HVRIB = CLAVE, PLMJP AVNVJ ZF = FONDOS, MFZHC BZDCN PB = PARQUE) decode letter for letter. A telegram of the previous day ended with the clear words “nueva clave” (its own page); this telegram is itself in the card, which fits the contributor’s reading that “the old key” is the card.

Counted by the README rule: 1432.1-4 has 20 words, of which GALEANA and GUERRERO are restored by the sense: 18 of 20 read (90%; the contributor counts 19 of 20). 1561.7-13 has 21 words, all read. Together 39 of 41 (95.1%), but the first telegram alone is under the bar, so the target is read in part.

05 What remains uncertain

  • GALEANA and GUERRERO each rest on a faulty or lost pair repaired by the sense; only the original telegram form would show whether the slip is the sender’s or the typist’s.
  • “Ag” is not identified; the sources that might name him (the recipients’ papers, Narváez 2011 and 2019) were not searched, and the books are not available to the project.
  • No second reader has compared the card or the telegrams with the CEHM images; the images may not be redistributed, and the CEHM image server failed TLS verification in the integrating session, so no image was compared there either.
  • Whether the telegrams are in print is not known.

Telegram of 29 March 1911 to John McCarty (CEHM-Carso CMXV.15.1432.1-4) read in part

González Garza at El Paso to Adolfo González at San Antonio: on the 13th Dr (probably Madero) and Ag separated at Galeana, one towards Guerrero, the other towards Chihuahua, camping on the 23rd at Encinillas.

Builds coherent sentences, but under the read bar of 95% of words read as sense (90% here); the holes are marked in the reading.

# Coded run -> pairs (row letter + column letter) -> letters, with key.tsv (the "Clave G.A." card), applied mechanically.
# Words typed next to each other form one run; "?" = a pair that is not on the card (row+row or column+column).
IHTHT YKBGH PACA	ih th ty kb gh pa ca	g a l e a n m	-> GALEANA (last pair CA = m, faulty for NA or PA = n; one wrong letter)
INTB	in tb	d r	-> DR
THKH	th kh	a g	-> AG
KHTNI BGBTB PBEBP J	kh tn ib gb tb pb e b pj	g u e r r e ? ? o	-> GUERRERO (the typed EB is not a pair; one pair lost, the sense fixes it)
KYIXG LKX	ky ix gl kx	c h i h	-> CHIH (abbreviation of Chihuahua)
IBKAI YGLIA GLGYT YKETF	ib ka iy gl ia gl gy ty ke tf	e n c i n i l l a s	-> ENCINILLAS

# Translation

On the 13th, Dr and Ag separated at Galeana; the latter took the road to Guerrero and the former the road to Chihuahua, camping on the 23rd at Encinillas.

González Garza.

"Dr" is probably "Doctor", the cover name for Francisco I. Madero in this correspondence (the period decipherment of a sibling telegram, CMXV.15.1458.4-6, writes "matar doctor", which the typed list CMXV.18.1795.1 glosses "(Madero)"; in CMXV.14.1392.1-2 and 1411.4-4 the period gloss "Dr" stands where 1795.1 types "Madero"). "Ag" is not identified (Abraham González, the Maderista leader in Chihuahua, would fit, but nothing on the sheet says so).

Translation

translation_1561-7-13.md

# Translation (CMXV.16.1561.7-13, 19 April 1911)

Dr wants Elías to come too. While the crisis lasts, go on using the old key, since it is quicker. Send funds and ammunition of all kinds at once.

González Garza.

"Parque" is the Mexican military word for ammunition and supplies. "The old key" is set against the new key of 18 April (CMXV.16.1551.3-4, `reading_grille.md`): the turning grille is slower to work than the Clave G.A. card.
