A numeric cipher sheet on the relief of Philippsburg (HStAD D 4 Nr. 76/6)

A loose sheet of two-digit numbers in the papers of Landgrave Philipp of Hesse-Butzbach, deciphered with the key strip found in the same file: German about regiments marching to relieve Philippsburg, with 95 of 124 words sure.

At a glanceDeciphered with a key from the same file · partial (95 of 124 words sure, 76.6%)

The letter
A German note of about 28 lines and two small pasted slips. The writer says he has been at Zabern (read as Saverne; unchecked) pressing the Imperial and Spanish [commanders] to relieve Philippsburg; the addressee is to encourage the officers and soldiers to hold until New Year. Neither is named.
Where
Hessisches Staatsarchiv Darmstadt, D 4 Nr. 76/6, “Varia [Landgraf Philipps v. Hessen-Butzbach]” (Arcinsys he:v5549727), view 13. Not in the catalogue or the leads here; DECODE was not checked.
The cipher
Two-digit numbers run together, one per letter, in alphabetical order from a=11 to z=34 (24 letters; v is written u, j is written i); words are separated by dots. Two further numbers, 38 and 39, stand for a second o and a second u. An overbar doubles a letter.
How it was deciphered
The key is a small strip on view 11 of the same file. It was applied to the whole sheet: 667 of 682 numbers fall in 11–34 and the result is continuous German. The numbers were transcribed twice by an AI model on different crops and settled on the image.
What it says
Regiments of the Emperor and of Spain are on the march and will be here “gegen mittwoch und donnerstag”, then go on towards Philippsburg; the money for them is said to be already “gesch…” (the verb is lost) and to be brought.
Still open
11 unresolved places: nine under a blot, a hole, a crossing-out or a cut slip, and two that are legible but unexplained, the number 398 and the group “sammigerku…”; the writer, the addressee and the date. A blind check of the digit in “entsetzung” and 18 other numbers is still unanswered, and the other leaves of the file were not searched for a clear copy.

01 The sheet and the key

The file D 4 Nr. 76/6 of the Hessian house archive at Darmstadt is catalogued as “Varia” of Landgrave Philipp of Hesse-Butzbach and dated 1641. The finding aid lists, among other things, a writing calendar for 1641, devices, alchemical and cabbalistic writings, ciphering tables for names, tables for developing a secret writing and notes on events of the war. The file is a miscellany with some cipher material: NOTES add a “Union” alphabet of 1618–1620 (not in the finding aid) and items of Ludwig V, who died in 1626.

The sheet is view 13 of the digitised microfilm (film frame 0416) and is written sideways. It has about 28 lines and two small pasted slips. The numbers are two digits, run together, with a dot between words and a double bar at a line break inside a word. A blot and a hole with a pasted slip affect lines 1, 7–8, 20, 24 and 26–28. The sheet lying above it on the same view uses a different, vowel-sign key (a e i o u h) and has nothing to do with this one.

The key is on a small strip at the lower left of view 11 (film frame 0414):

Not on the strip: 39, which occurs ten times and behaves as a second u (und, zu, uff, zum), and 38, which occurs four times and behaves as a second o (sollicitirt, alhero, noch, bishero). Both were deduced from the sheet. One number, 398, on a slip, is off the key and unresolved.

No image of the sheet is reproduced on this page. The images are digitised microfilm of the Hessisches Staatsarchiv Darmstadt, reached through the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek / Archivportal-D (see Sources); the terms for reusing a crop were not confirmed here.

In English: “Much-honoured Sir, to further the relief of Philippsburg I have myself been at Zabern [Saverne] for several days and pressed the Imperial and Spanish [commanders] without ceasing, until at last the French … several passes …; and thereupon it has been arranged that several Imperial and Spanish regiments are now on the march hither and, God willing, will certainly arrive here on Wednesday and Thursday, and will then march on this side [of the Rhine] towards Philippsburg. It is therefore of the highest importance to encourage the officers and soldiers as best one can to keep up their bravery and steadfastness … as praiseworthily as hitherto, and to be patient until New Year’s Day; the money for them has already been [provided], which is to be brought … the comital (?) … all … as also … [fragments; slips: ‘…twentieth…’, ‘my … 398 …’].”

The count is by cipher words, sure words only: 124 words in all (letter body, fragments and slips; the verb split over lines 7–8 is counted as two words, so 123 and 10 unresolved if it is joined), 95 sure, 18 probable or restored, 11 unresolved, so 95 of 124 (76.6%) and the target is only partly deciphered. The contributor’s own count with the probable words is 113 of 124 (91%), and 105 of 109 (96%) for the body of the letter, lines 1–25; it is given here only as that. In the body the writer has been at Zabern pressing the Imperial and Spanish commanders, regiments are on the march, the officers and soldiers are to hold until New Year, and the money for them is ready. The word “Zabern” is read as a place name (Saverne); that identification was not checked against other sources here.

Date and background are not settled. The file is dated 1641 by the almanac it contains, but it is a collection that also holds items from 1618–1626. Brice Dudout suggests winter 1633/34 from the content (Philippsburg blockaded, Imperial and Spanish troops at Saverne, relief promised before New Year); the military events have not been checked here. (This background is not from the sources and was not checked here.)

03 Method

The images were fetched from the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek’s IIIF server (68 views of the file); the cipher view was cut into regions requested at the full 3500 × 2544 resolution, because the whole-image request is capped at 2500 px. An AI model made two passes on different crops, the second without reopening the first file, so the passes are independent only in that sense, and the differences were settled on the image (transcription/pass_differences.md). One point stays open: in entsetzung (line 1) the arbitrated digits give an extra number (“enstsetzung”), and at high zoom the word seems to have one number fewer; the word is counted as probable either way.

The key was not recovered from the ciphertext: it was found on view 11 and applied. It was checked on the whole sheet, where 667 of 682 numbers (97.8%) fall in 11–34 and the result is continuous German; the 15 numbers off the strip are 39 (ten times), 38 (four times) and the single 398. There is no held-out test, because no key was reconstructed. The reading was made by the same AI model; no second reader has checked it against the image yet, so the reading grade is B.

No reading or edition of the sheet was found in the repository, in the project’s own folders or in a quick web search; that search was short and is not proof that none exists.

04 What remains uncertain

Eleven places are unresolved. Nine of them lie under a blot, a hole, a crossing-out or a cut slip on the microfilm, which is black and white; a better image could settle some of them. The other two, the number 398 (off the key) and “sammigerku…” (no sense found), are legible but unexplained.

  1. Line 7, end: “pas…”, a word cut by a crossing-out (25 11 28, then lost). With line 8 it is a verb in -irt; “passirt” or “forcirt” are guesses.
  2. Line 8, start: “…rt” (27 29), the end of the same word; the first numbers are blotted.
  3. Line 20, first word, under a blot.
  4. Line 24: “gesch…” (17 15 28 13 18, then lost); “geschafft” is a guess.
  5. Line 26, fourth word (hole or pasted slip).
  6. Line 26, last word: “all…” (11 21 21, then cut by the hole).
  7. Line 27, last word (hole).
  8. Line 28: “wer…” (31 15 27, then a fragment under a blot).
  9. Slip 1, second word (slip cut).
  10. Slip 2: the number 398, off the key.
  11. Slip 2: “sammigerku…” (28 11 22 22 19 17 15 27 20 30, then cut); no sense found.

Most of the probable words rest on a single doubtful reading each (“im”, “Zabern” and the restored letters are inferred): “die” is written 14 18 15 (h for i); “entsetzung” lies under the blot; “etliche” (line 3) is underlined or struck; “tag” has an 11 that looks like two strokes; “keyserischen” and “keyserische” are half faded; “sollicitirt” and “alhero” rest on 38 = o; “geliebts” is split over a line break; “mittwoch” (29 29 31) appeared as 29 34 in one pass; “Ligt also” at a line start is written over; “dapffer” (14 11 25 16 16 15 27) was legible only once, the first pass having “defension”; “gräfliche” has an unclear start; “zwantzigsten” is on a slip with a doubtful number. In line 25 the word is “gebracht” (17 15 12 27 11 13 18 29); an earlier text posted in issue #745 had “gebraucht”, a slip.

Writer, addressee and the date (1641 from the file, 1633/34 suggested, not checked) are open.
