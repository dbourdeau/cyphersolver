# Witzel's substrate words: Dravidian-looking or Munda-looking?

Dravidian-ness = mean log-probability per character under a Dravidian model minus a Munda model, relative to 500 random Sanskrit headwords (0.489). Positive = more Dravidian-like than ordinary Sanskrit words.

|---|---|---|---|
| Witzel: Dravidian loans (1.6) | 13 | -0.018 (-0.58 to +0.97) | 6 of 13 |
| Witzel: Para-Munda (1.2, 1.4, 2.3) | 38 | -0.393 (-4.75 to +0.90) | 11 of 38 |
| Witzel 1.9, Southern Indus / Meluhhan (his forms, by hand) | 7 | -0.081 (-0.81 to +0.69) | 4 of 7 |

- Meluhhan forms and their scores: sinda -0.81, ili -0.81, ellu +0.43, nankal +0.02, godi -0.14, kangu +0.69, meluhha +0.07.

Reading: the positive control fails - the Dravidian loans of Witzel are no more Dravidian-like than ordinary Sanskrit words (mean near zero), while the Para-Munda words look Munda-like only because they were chosen for their Munda-like prefixes. The sound-pattern model cannot tell the families apart in Sanskrit spelling, and the Southern Indus language of Witzel is a handful of reconstructed forms (several reconstructed from Dravidian cognates). No test is possible here; the case of Witzel rests on etymology, not on anything this script can check. His position (Northern Indus = Para-Munda, prefixing; Southern Indus = Meluhhan, neither Dravidian nor Munda) is the main alternative to the Dravidian reading and is recorded as such.
