# Wells (2006) and Mahadevan (1970) tested

## W1 Is the heading a prefix bound to what follows?

- heading texts: 439. Pairs sharing the sign after the heading that share the opener: 42.2% (35.9% if the openers were shuffled): excess +6.3 points.
- names with an ending: 1995. Pairs sharing the last sign that share the ending: 91.5% (75.9% shuffled): excess +15.6 points.
- The ending is chosen by the name; the opener hardly by what follows it. The heading behaves as a separate formula (the segmenter cuts after it 95%% of the time), not as a prefix selected by a root.

## W2 A prefix set?

- names of 2+ non-numeral signs: 1407. Five commonest initial signs cover 27% of names (240 x108, 33 partners, 235 x93, 29 partners, 806 x68, 13 partners, 220 x53, 22 partners, 803 x51, 18 partners); five commonest final signs 30% (760 x125, 42 partners, 100 x100, 22 partners, 220 x75, 17 partners, 176 x61, 19 partners, 240 x56, 8 partners).
- Name-initial position is spread over many signs, as a root position is; no small initial set combines with many roots the way a prefix set would, beyond the fish and numeral-like signs that also stand alone.

## W3 Insertions

- names that recur with and without one inserted sign: 31 insertions; inserted signs: 240 x5, 235 x4, 415 x2, 32 x2, 1 x2, 176 x1, 231 x1, 19 x1, 840 x1, 482 x1, 233 x1, 706 x1; stroke numerals 6 of 31 (19%).
- An attribute or numeral inserted before the head of a compound is ordinary in Dravidian (attribute + head); insertion does not by itself require infixes.

## M1 Openers by site in Mahadevan's 1977 corpus alone

- M77 texts opening with the heading, Mohenjo-daro and Harappa blocks: 282; openers Mohenjo-daro {'820': 71, '861': 145}; Harappa {'861': 42, '820': 24}; mutual information with site 0.001 bits, permutations as large 664 of 1000.

- In M77, 817 and 861 are one sign (both map to MSg267): Mahadevan did not separate them. In the ICIT-derived corpus, Mohenjo-daro against Harappa with all three openers: 0.002 bits, permutations as large 661 of 1000 - no site effect. The effect found in the sixth pass comes from Lothal ({'817': 13, '820': 5, '861': 4}) and from the TAG:B sealings ({'817': 10}), impressions of a few seals. Mahadevan (1970) was right; the site / object-type claim is withdrawn.
