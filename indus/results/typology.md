# Word order and female names: two grammar tests

## T1 The possessive formula

- the man sign 90, 178 tokens (ICIT-derived and M77 additions): text-final 117, inside 34, text-initial 27. Before it: 740 112, (start) 27, 90 14, 390 2, 125 2, 350 2. After it: (end) 120, 90 14, 400 13, 55 7, 615 2, 500 2.
- 112 of 178 tokens (63%) stand right after the ending 740; 27 (15%) open a text. The formula is "X-740 man", the possessor (or the name with its personal suffix) first and the head noun last. A language that puts the head noun first and the possessor after it, "man X-GEN" (Sumerian lu2 X-ak; the cuneiform "lu2 me-luh-ha", man of Meluhha), would write the man sign first.
- the texts that open with 90: 90 90 55 55 2 ? 890 892 x7; 90 90 x4; 90 400 x3; 90 x3; 90 308 2 236 x1; 90 400 740 90 400 x1. A "man + name + ending" text, the head-first order: 1 (90 500 740).

## T3 The grammar tests against the families

| family | a class suffix on the singular noun, persons against stars (ending class, noun_class.py) | possessor before head ("X-740 man") | both |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dravidian | yes: gender-number suffixes on the noun itself (-an masc., -am / -tu non-masc.), rational / non-rational in Tamil | yes: the genitive precedes its head | **yes** |
| Indo-Aryan (Sanskrit) | in part: gender shows in the stem ending, but three genders across many declension endings, and the nakshatras are mostly feminine, some masculine and neuter | yes, as a rule (rajnah purusah) | in part |
| Munda | no: animacy shows only in dual and plural forms; heavenly bodies are animate like persons (Hoffmann 1903) | yes | no |
| Burushaski | no: four classes (human masc., human fem., x, y) shown by agreement and plural suffixes, not by a suffix on the singular noun | yes | no |
| Sumerian | no: human / non-human shows in pronouns, verb agreement and the human plural -ene, not on the singular noun | no: head first, lu2 X-ak | no |
| Elamite | yes: animate -r (3rd sg.) against inanimate -me | no: head first, modifiers and possessors after it | no |

On these two tests only Dravidian fits outright; Indo-Aryan fits in part. The result is conditional: it assumes that 740 and 520 are suffixes of the name (they are fixed per name, final, and bound to it, segment.py), that 90 is a head noun (the man sign) and that the fish names are stars. Each step has its own evidence in this folder; none is proven.
