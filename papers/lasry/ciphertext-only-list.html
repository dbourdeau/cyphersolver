Historical ciphers: ciphertext-only solutions

cyphersolver project (Daniel Bourdeau, with George Lasry) · 22 ciphertext-only first breaks and 3 further Cipherbrain challenges · list of 25 September 2026 · https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/

Key recovered from ciphertext only

First breaks: the key was rebuilt from the ciphertext alone, with no plaintext and no existing key. Three items had also been solved earlier by George Lasry but never published; the LLMs solved them independently, and his unpublished solution is noted.

1. Sienese envoys and others to the government of Siena before 1429 to 1547

Keys recovered from the ciphertext alone for three letters (nos. 25, 18, 14) and from an alphabet fragment for a fourth; none of the 43 keys filed with the letters fits. No. 25 reads 96.2%, no. 18 (Latin treaty articles with the Emperor) 87.1%. Several other letters in the file remain open.

Method
ciphertext-only break · partial
Primary source
Archivio di Stato di Siena, Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2 "Lettere in cifra"
Secondary source
DECODE R4790-R4814 (no status); the archive's 1952 inventory calls the letters 'solo parzialmente decifrate'
Write-up
https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/siena1421.html

2. Matheo de Segura to the Constable of Castile Madrid, 8 Aug 1596

Two short passages in a homophonic cipher; the key was rebuilt by annealing and resegmenting the image. 119 of 125 cipher tokens read (95.2%). The passages concern predictions of the world ending or the king dying by September.

Method
ciphertext-only break · complete
Primary source
BnF, espagnol 336 no. 99, ff. 196r-198r · Gallica
Secondary source
Tomokiyo, cryptiana (spanish3.htm, unsolved.htm): the last es. 336 item listed as undeciphered · spanish3.htm · unsolved.htm
Write-up
https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/segura1596.html

3. Giovan Gioacchino da Passano (attributed), London, to Francis I 15 Mar 1530 (copy heading)

An account of Wolsey's wealth: cash, credits, revenues, jewels and plate, Winchester, St Albans and York. A dotted re-transcription made the break possible. 386 of 402 signs read coherently (96.0%).

Secondary source

4. Mémoire en chiffre to the connétable de Montmorency 12 Dec 1560

Ten pages in a cursive pseudo-script: a homophonic substitution with two-stroke units and four word codes, broken from the images alone. It reports on the Protestant rising in the Agenais a week after François II's death. 98.9% of 1,715 cipher words read.

Method
ciphertext-only break · complete
Primary source
BnF, français 3157 no. 67, ff. 152r-156v · Gallica · BnF catalogue
Secondary source
BnF catalogue (Gallica harvest); no DECODE record
Independent solution
Solved independently by the LLMs, from the ciphertext alone and without knowledge of any earlier solution. In private communications, Lasry wrote that he had also solved it in 2023 (improved in February 2026), but his solution has not been published.
Write-up
https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/memoire1560.html

11. Sun Yat-sen's intercepted telegrams Mar 1916 - Mar 1917

12. Warsaw letter to the Bishop of Olmütz (?) 24 Dec 1627

A homophonic substitution whose alphabet is in plain order, with syllables, nulls and thirteen word codes, recovered by annealing. The writer presses for a canonry of Olmütz for a son of the Queen of Poland. Read in full; the word codes are glossed from context.

13. Henry of Navarre to Ségur 1585-86

Three figure-cipher letters to the envoy raising a German army. The key, an alphabetical syllabary, was rebuilt by a structured annealer and checked on matched controls. The letters cover Duke Casimir, Clervant's reiters and the road through the Vivarais.

14. Cardinal Soglia to nuncio Viale Prelà at Innsbruck Rome, 15 June 1848

A 64-cell two-digit table with homophones and syllables, 5 as word separator, and an alphabetical one-part code. It is a counter-order: stay with the Emperor and suspend the request for passports. Ten code words read from context; 11 code groups open.

16. Johann Heinrich Kauderbach (The Hague) to Brühl / Friedrich August II 1754-56

Unseparated figures in which 5 and 8 are nulls; one key serves all ten despatches. It was found by one-to-one annealing onto the vocabulary of his 1761 key. All ten despatches read at 65-97%, covering the Dutch augmentation debate and the Treaty of Versailles.

Method
ciphertext-only break · complete
Primary source
Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, Prins Willem V inv. 201
Secondary source
DECODE R1043, R1044, R1954, R1958, R1959, R2078-R2082 ("never solved" by the Hague cabinet)
Write-up
https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/kauderbach1754.html

23. Johan Banér to Torsten Stålhandske (intercepted copy) Hof, 29 Dec 1640

A homophonic two-digit alphabet with 14 code words; none of the Swedish keys on DECODE fits. The key was rebuilt from context cribs (durch, und): the letter orders a march toward the Upper Palatinate. 89.8% of tokens read; the code words are open.

Method
key from adjacent plaintext · partial
Primary source

Secondary source
Cipherbrain challenge (Klaus Schmeh, 2 June 2019) · Cipherbrain, 2 June 2019

24. Pseudo-Arndt, "Das zweyte Silentium Dei" Glücksbrunn, 1798

Read with the key drawn in the book itself (p. 83), so no key was recovered. All 31 cipher passages (1,545 signs) were transcribed and read. Page 24 had already been read by Lasry and Kopal (Cipherbrain 2022); five short passages are read here for the first time and earlier readings corrected.

Secondary source
Cipherbrain (Klaus Schmeh, 4 March 2022); DECODE R2873 · Cipherbrain, 4 March 2022

25. Thomas Urquhart's Cyphral Octastich 1652

A book cipher on the book itself, verified independently: 238 of 284 hits against shuffled controls, reading a royalist prayer for Charles II. The same rule and plaintext had been published by Vals AI in Aug 2026, so this is a verification, not a first break. The distich claim was not reproduced.

Method
known key applied · partial
Primary source
Last leaf of Ekskybalauron / The Jewel (London, 1652); Jack and Lyall edition (1983), p. 212
Secondary source
Schmeh's Top 50 no. 28 (Cipherbrain, 2015); HCPortal transcription · Top 50 list · HCPortal
