# R8356 — BL Harley MS 260 ff. 112v–113r (Walsingham letter-book, June 1571)

Status: attempted, open (catalogue entry "Elizabeth R. to unknown recipient, Jun 1571"). The cipher is one
nine-sign letter run and two boxed codes in a clear letter of Walsingham to Burghley. No key survives, and the
run is too short to break. The letters themselves are clear and printed in Digges 1655.

## What the record is

DECODE R8356 ("Elizabeth R. to unknown recipient", Jun 1571, 2 pp., "Short parts encrypted only") is two images
of Walsingham's Paris **letter-book** (running head "June 1571"), the same volume as R8357, R8358, R8361 and R8363.

| image | folio | content |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | 112v | end of Leicester → Walsingham ("Yr assured friend R. Leicester"); **Walsingham → Burghley**, 25 June 1571 (begins) |
| P2 | 113r | Walsingham → Burghley ends ("the xxv of June 1571 … Frauncis Walsingham"); **Elizabeth R. → Walsingham** ("To the right trustie and welbeloved Frauncis Walsingham Esq. o[u]r Amba. resident w[i]th o[u]r good brother the French Kinge"), begins, clear |

DECODE's "Elizabeth R." comes from the heading on f. 113r. The Queen's letter (on the French ambassador and
M. de L'Archant, captain of Anjou's guard, and the marshal Montmorency's coming) is **clear**. The cipher is all in
Walsingham's letter to Burghley on f. 112v.

## The cipher (f. 112v)

- l. 1: "The [12] 6 1 7 4 a H 6 H H shewed me his M[aste]r[']s letters dated the ixth of this moneth …"
  (`[12]` is a boxed code; the nine signs follow; `a` is a small a, the three `H` are the same sign).
- l. 12: "This as I can gather was that should come from [E4] who discovereth it to his M[aste]r".

Digges, *The Compleat Ambassador* (1655) p. 106 (text `burghley1572/lit/digges1655.txt` l. 9544ff.) prints
the letter with the run as figures ("He':1627 40q0n 6> nu shewed me his Mistresses Letters dated the seventh …")
and [E4] as "54". So the run was never glossed in print. The letter is not in CSP Foreign 1569–71 as a
decipherment.

## Analysis

- The run's pattern is A B C D E F A F F (positions 1=7, 6=8=9). No English word or name fits that pattern as
  one-to-one substitution. It is therefore homophonic, mixed with code signs, or several short codes.
- The sibling R8361 run (`7 7 6 + w 5 ‡ 6 3 ∩ 6`, Nov 1572) shares signs (6, 7) but has no word overlap
  that could serve as a crib. Nineteen signs in two runs are not enough for a solver.
- [12] and [E4] each occur once. In context [12] + run = the man who showed Walsingham "his master's
  letters" (a foreign ambassador's servant or an agent), and [E4] is the source "who discovereth it to his master".
  There is not enough to fix either.

## Remaining gaps
- nine-sign run on f. 112v l. 1 - blocker: too-short; nine signs, pattern fits no word, no key
- codes [12] and [E4] - blocker: no-key-material; each occurs once, no Walsingham 1571 key on DECODE, in Tomokiyo or in print
