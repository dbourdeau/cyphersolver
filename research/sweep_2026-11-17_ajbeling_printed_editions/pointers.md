# Pointers for existing entries and for later sweeps (not leads)

Found on the way during the sweep in this folder, 16-17 Nov 2026, by AI agents; no person has checked them. None is a
new lead; each may save someone a search.

1. **Mandrot, Dépêches des ambassadeurs milanais en France sous Louis XI et François Sforza, vol. 4 (1465-66), is in
   full view on HathiTrust**: `mdp.39015019090169` (also `njp.32101074209048`),
   https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015019090169. Catalogue 1321 (Giovanni Bianco to the Duke of Milan,
   Lyon, 6 June 1466) says this volume could not be opened ("Mandrot is on archive.org only for T.1-T.3"). An in-volume
   search for "en clair" gives hits on pp. 68, 78, 264, 280, 296, 305 and others, and the editor writes "Il existe aux
   archives de Milan une traduction contemporaine en clair des passages chiffrés": the volume prints the contemporary
   decipherments of the Sforza chancery for the despatches it edits. Whether it prints Bianco's letter of 6 June 1466
   was not checked.

2. **Catalogue 829 and 830 (Kerlérec's intercepted letters, Huntington, Loudoun papers) have French ministry copies
   with the clerk's decipherment between the lines.** Pusch's article on Kerlérec's ciphers in Louisiana History (2008), Table 1 ("Dispatches used in the author's
   analysis", p. 476), lists
   Kerlérec's despatch no. 127 (12 Dec 1756, third copy) at ANOM Colonies C13A vol. 39 ff. 190-191 and no. 135 (28 Jan
   1757, second copy) at C13A vol. 40 ff. 27-28. On the ANOM images, C13A 39 image 0378 is headed "No. 127 /
   Troisième ... 12 Xbre 1756 / M. de Kerlerec" and its three pages of number code are glossed throughout; no. 135 is
   glossed the same way. So 830 (12 Dec 1756) and 829 (28 Jan 1757) look already read in these copies. Caution: that
   the Huntington letter of 12 Dec 1756 is no. 127 rests on the date alone (Kerlérec could have written two coded
   letters that day); 28 Jan 1757 matches no. 135 in date and in Pusch. The London covering letter (CO 5/48, Loudoun to
   Pitt, 17 June 1757, in the Library of Congress transcripts on the Internet Archive) marks both "sent to Mr Willes".
   We hold a transcription of no. 135 with the clerk's gloss and can send it if wanted.
