## What was seen

- **Casanatense Ms. 1309, c. 3r.** We paid for a colour reproduction (3752 x 5000 px), received 17 Nov 2026. Credit: image © 2026 Giovanni De Angelis / SIAE, Biblioteca Casanatense. The image is for private study and is not included here or in any derived form.
  - The leaf is in clear Italian, 17 lines plus a catchword.
  - Its top is the end of a letter: "dieci righe di cifra, et à V.S. Ill.ma faccio humilissima r[iverenz]a".
  - Then comes a paragraph beginning "1000 Mi hà avvisato, che havendo l'Ambasciatore Zeno quà scritto, che si mandassero quà quello, che havevano contro i Gesuiti per rispondere al Papa ...".
  - The leaf ends "... che cerchi divertirlo del far officio".
- **Penn Ms. Codex 553** (University of Pennsylvania, OPenn, CC BY 4.0) has the same passage at f. 5v (last six lines) to f. 6r, line 11.
  - The sentence is complete there: "Saran con questa dieci righe di Cifra".
  - The cover number is written "1000 Mille".
  - Penn f. 5r has a second four-figure cover number after "in Roma".

The Manus phrase "dieci righe di cifra" therefore quotes the letter. The nuncio enclosed ten lines of cipher, and the register does not copy them as cipher. The clear paragraph that follows in both copies is probably their content. The Manus phrase "nomi propri cifrati" refers to cover numbers such as 1000, not to ciphertext.

## For a later worker

The original dispatch (Vatican, Segreteria di Stato, Venezia, 1621; not checked) may survive with its ten cipher lines. If it does, it would pair with this clear text. Neither register holds the nomenclator that the numbers come from.
