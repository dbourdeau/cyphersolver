# 8-3-12: where the decode and the 19th-century decipherment differ

Every place where the mechanical decode of transcription 2 (`../lead892_test/decode_T2.txt`, aligned in
`../lead892_test/key_gap_occurrences.json`) does not give the letter of the 19th-century decipherment
(`../lead892_test/reference_19c_decipherment.txt`). Places are line.sign in the original (images 3-4). Checked on the
line images at 2x on 23 Nov 2026 by an AI agent (Claude Opus 5.5). "Both" means transcriptions 1 and 2 agree on the
sign.

## A. The cipher says something else: the cipher is right, the 19th-century text is not a literal reading

| Place | Cipher (decode, both transcriptions) | 19th-century decipherment |
|---|---|---|
| A10-A12 | *intelliges mentem **dilectionis** et maiestatis **eius** caesareae* | *mentem **dilectissimi fratris** et maiestatis **suae** caesareae* |
| A15 | *una cum traductione **italica*** | *traductione **italiana*** |
| B07-B08 | *rebus et negotiis **istis** nostris magnopere* | *rebus et negotiis nostris magnopere* |

On the image the signs of A11 read d-i-l-e-c-t-i-o-n-i-s e-t m-a-i-e-s-t-a-t-i-s, of A12 e-i-u-s, of A15 l-i-c-a and of
B08 t-i-i-s i-s-t-i-s with no damage. An independent AI reviewer confirmed all three on the image. Austro-Turcica 1541-1552, No. 165
(p. 437; seen 24 Nov 2026 by an AI agent of ours in the library's OCR text of the Bavarian State Library copy
bsb00150599), prints *mentem dilectionis et Maiestatis eius Caesareae* and *traductiones Italica* as the cipher has
them, and *negotiis nostris* without *istis*.

## B. Spelling in the cipher; the decipherer normalised it

| Place | Cipher | 19th-century |
|---|---|---|
| A05.10 and B03.8 | *Drogutum* (o, twice) | *Dragutum* |
| A04.13 | *violacionibus* (c) | *violationibus* |
| A04.26 | *indutiarum* (t) | *induciarum* |
| A16.17 | *Teudes* (u-sign where n belongs) | *Tendes* (the Count of Tende) |
| A09-A10 | *litaris* (one t, a for e) | *litteris* |
| A20.25 | *Gonaaga* (a-sign where z belongs) | *Gonzaga* |
| B07, B03 | *itaqe*, *quemadmodm* (u left out) | *itaque*, *quemadmodum* |

The meaning is the decipherer's; the letters are the cipher clerk's slips or habits.

## C. Our key or sign sheet is short; the decipherment is right

- Square-u sign = g (12 places), looped sign = q (4 + 2), turned-y sign = h (3), pi with feet = g (3): sign-sheet
  links, see `../README.md`.
- 7 = ll (4), 8 = mm (3), 8 = x (1), alpha = x (2): double-letter numerals and x, already in the key.

## D. Transcription or reading doubts; the decipherment is right

A03.18 *ad* (8 read as qo), A05.22 *alios* (oP read as P), A05.27 *piratas*, A13.3 *tibi* (the b-sign read as breve),
B04.11 *sibi* (the i-sign read as T), B06.19 *speramus* (qo/8), B11.19 *que*, B18.23 *et* (+ or q+). In each the
image fits the decipherment's letter at least as well as the transcribed code.

So: no place where the 19th-century decipherer misread a sign to give a different meaning, except that he rewrote
three phrases (section A) into the wording he expected.
