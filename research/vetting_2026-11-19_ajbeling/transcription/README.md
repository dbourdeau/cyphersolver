# Reading the September sign tables

The source is The National Archives, SP 15/31 f. 63r, Charles Paget to Cardinal Allen, Brussels, 1/11 September 1589. Open scan 65, the leaf on the right. U01–U08 denote the physical rows of the first long cipher block; L01–L09 are the second. The separate short opening is excluded from the tables. [Manuscript](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/MC4304880878/SPOL) · [Public archive catalogue](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C3411827)

The two readers independently recorded sign classes from common neutral specimens. Two different signs can look similar. A source label is a description for comparison, not a plaintext letter. Each pass gives the row, horizontal coordinate in pixels on the research crop and a sign label. A question mark anywhere denotes uncertainty. N-prefixed labels are local reader descriptions. The consensus requires the same label (05 and its duplicate exemplar 15 are equivalent) and unique reciprocal-nearest coordinates within 14 pixels. Every unmatched position remains in the file. Counts are 686 observations in pass A and 666 in B, 707 merged positions, 445 agreed positions representing 42 distinct shape labels. Do not quote this as a percentage of the letter read.

To locate a body sign on the PDF, render page 1 of the two-scan extract with Poppler at a 4,400-pixel long edge. The body crop begins at x=2,790, y=480. Table x-coordinates run horizontally from that crop’s left edge. To locate each body row, add 2,790 to its x. The original y center is 483 + row baseline − 0.036 × x. The upper row baselines in order are 90, 148, 205, 260, 311, 365, 418, 472, and the lower ones are 590, 648, 697, 755, 808, 862, 916, 974, 1,026. Row crops cover roughly 36 pixels above and below those centers. The atlas source rows O, U and L refer to the separate short opening, the upper block’s first fully graphic row, and the lower block’s last fully graphic row. Those three exemplar images have their own x origins; the source-strip coordinates and verbal descriptions are in shape_legend.tsv. No source images are distributed.

A simple homophonic alphabet allowing several signs per plaintext letter was attempted. It failed on all ten synthetic calibration texts and was therefore never fitted to the letter. The manuscript’s own word boundaries and the letter’s cipher plaintext remain undetermined.

Run `python3 -I -B verify.py` in this folder to rebuild the position table from both independent readings and verify it cell by cell.

A second calibration retained context across holes, trained on fifteen rows, and tested on two reserved rows. One of ten synthetic keys reached 70% accuracy (eight successes required). The median was 26 of 59 held letters. The two models used the same corpus families and compatible assumptions. Two windows overlapped in the second calibration; the result is insufficient for fitting or quoting real plaintext.

[Exemplar positions](exemplar_centers.tsv) places every atlas specimen directly on the full scan 65 rendering, 4,400 × 3,143 pixels. The table also gives PDF point coordinates. The origin in render pixels is top left. For opening strip O, page x = 2,824.6 + 44x/45 and page y = 356.5 + 44y/45. On U and L, add 2,803 to strip x and respectively 514 or 1,333 to strip y. Three separately spaced landmarks on each strip gave correlations of 0.984–1.000. Allow one to two rendered pixels. The tabulated points follow the atlas baseline and are approximate centers for locating individual marks.
