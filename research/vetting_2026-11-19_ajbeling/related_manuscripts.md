# Which cipher signs recur in the other manuscripts?

The September letter from Charles Paget to Cardinal Allen occupies SP 15/31 f. 63r. The facing f. 64r carries a separate report for Barnes with an English decipherment above the signs. Green attributes that decipherment to Thomas Phelippes. Two readers independently located the forms 45 at *of*, 44 with a right-hand curl at *to*, 25 at *my*, and a hooked p-like form at *the*. The curl’s boundary remains uncertain. The original item gives no named sender for the second letter. [September manuscript, scan 66](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/MC4304880878/SPOL) · [Green, pp. 279–280, nos. 43–44](https://archive.org/details/cu31924091775373/page/n292)

In the same volume, SP 15/31 f. 33r has contemporary interlinear English in instructions attributed to Charles Paget in May 1589. Green abridges a passage about ships and manpower with “&c.”. After asking about ships currently at sea, the manuscript adds a separate question about the ships, soldiers and mariners that could be raised. Both readers could make out that distinction. A run resembling 8–0–4–8 follows clear *able* twice. The first has *to make* written above it. A longer repeated sequence begins where two glosses ask “what number”. These are phrase correspondences within the May letter. Individual numeral values and precise word divisions remain uncertain. [May manuscript](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/MC4304880841/SPOL) · [Green, pp. 270–271, no. 27](https://archive.org/details/cu31924091775373/page/n283)

Two readers compared specimens of all six forms or sequences against all seventeen physical rows in the two long September blocks, with the source labels concealed. Neither found an exact occurrence. A possible 4 followed by a 5-like sign on upper row eight has an uncertain boundary. It cannot support the word *of*. Four facing-folio specimens were also compared with the separate opening and with the numbers in the clear-text portion without a secure match. No September word is established by either document.

The three old Paget sheets in SP 106/2 were tested against fourteen reserved rows. Two readers matched neutral target specimens independently to anonymous key tiles. Repeated placements counted only when both accepted the same tile with high confidence. Reading holes and row ends break adjacency. The preregistered pass mark required 30% coverage, eight distinct shapes, 24 adjacent pairs across four rows, and a score higher than every one of 1,000 shuffled keys. Each experiment shuffled letter values separately from word values. [Key book, scans 70, 72 and 73](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/MC4323480003/SPOL)

The Chartley volume, SP 53/22 f. 47r–v, gives a fuller witness for the same Paget–Mary cipher seen in old 97a. Both tables assign the same pair of forms to H and a second pair to I, a crossed circle to *to*, and an upright joining two loops to *but*. The Chartley sheet adds word signs, nulls, a doubling mark, rulers and countries. Only one of 31 additional word or name forms attracted independent high-confidence agreement with a target specimen. The hash-like form for *that* occurs at 14 of the 579 reserved positions. There are zero adjacent candidate word pairs. That single recurring sign establishes zero plaintext words; all 1,000 shuffles tie on the empty pair score. [Chartley manuscript, scans 49–50](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/MC4322680002/SPOL)

A reverse search began with the five numbers that Green prints in Paget’s surrounding prose. Both readers found 32 on the facing f. 64r in rows 7, 16 and 27. Two instances have a separate curved mark. The readers could not agree on an aligned word or name, and they found no secure gloss for 14, 30, 48 or 28. The facing decipherment consequently identifies none of the five. Green’s printed 48 also remains uncertain on Paget’s manuscript.

The strict visual matching procedure also missed on the first cipher line of Paget’s 4 January 1585 letter to Mary, whose decipher survives. It assigned eight of 109 positions with no adjacent pair.
