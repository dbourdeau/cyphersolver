# Two short private ciphers in letters to Niccolò Machiavelli

Marcello Simonetta’s note to the national edition of Machiavelli’s *Lettere* identifies two surviving passages which the editors could not decipher. Both are in the Filza Bargagli, now Biblioteca nazionale centrale di Firenze, Nuovi Acquisti 1004. Their senders and cipher systems differ. The same essay describes the common chancery cipher used in the Buonaccorsi letters and the three versions of it in Carte Machiavelli VI/82. [*Lettere* III, pp. 1866–1868](https://machiavelli.sputnikdns.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettere_Tomo-3.pdf#page=574)

- **Totto Machiavelli to his brother Niccolò, Rome, 29 March 1505.** Just under a line of cipher follows a request for a prompt answer in §8. Totto had been asking about the income of the provostship at Santa Maria a Cigoli. The editors leave a gap and cite the shortage of comparable material. The original is N.A. 1004, f. 30r. A separate 1503 draft mentions sending Totto a key. Its verso preserves a possible contemporary attempt to decipher a reply. It supplies neither a table nor evidence that the brothers still used the same key in 1505. The short passage requires a key or another independently matched document. [No. 99, I pp. 352–354](https://machiavelli.sputnikdns.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettere_Tomo-1.pdf#page=484) · [No. 70, I p. 259](https://machiavelli.sputnikdns.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettere_Tomo-1.pdf#page=391) · [Earlier manuscript, verso](https://teca.bncf.firenze.sbn.it/ImageViewer/servlet/ImageViewer?idr=BNCF0003854515&azione=showImg)
- **Piero Soderini to Niccolò Machiavelli, Florence, 22 February 1509.** Antonio della Valle wrote this letter for the head of the Florentine government during the siege of Pisa. About two lines of cipher interrupt §4 after a discussion of pay and the length of infantry service. The letter resumes in clear with instructions about supplies reaching the city. The editors say the substitution differs from the one Soderini usually employed. The original is N.A. 1004, f. 49r. The printed edition lists a facsimile as plate 18. A photograph of that plate or the original would allow a diplomatic transcription. [No. 177, II pp. 695–697](https://machiavelli.sputnikdns.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettere_Tomo-2.pdf#page=135) · [Plate index, III p. 2010](https://machiavelli.sputnikdns.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettere_Tomo-3.pdf#page=718)

Two other surviving cipher letters had already been read for this edition. Francesco Vettori concealed sensitive passages in his letters of 4–5 and 7 August 1526 by arranging letters vertically. The edition gives their readings. In his first letter Vettori also reports difficulty reading a private cipher used by Machiavelli on 31 July. That incoming letter is lost. [No. 331, III pp. 1515–1517](https://machiavelli.sputnikdns.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettere_Tomo-3.pdf#page=223) · [No. 333, III pp. 1526–1528](https://machiavelli.sputnikdns.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lettere_Tomo-3.pdf#page=234)

The headnote to no. 331 also mentions Vettori’s 24 August 1526 letter, no. 335, among the uses of a simpler vertical cipher. No. 335’s own edition marks no cipher passage. We inspected every face of Giuseppe Molini’s 1832 copies of the 7 and 24 August letters, BNCF Carte Machiavelli V/113–114. On f. 3r he retained separated cipher letters at the known passage of 7 August. His copy of the 24 August letter runs in ordinary prose through the signature. Whether the cross-reference describes anything visible in the London original still requires inspection of British Library, Add MS 10281, ff. 186r–187v.
