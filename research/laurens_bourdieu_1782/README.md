# Five numerical designations in Henry Laurens’s letter to James Bourdieu, 10 August 1782

Henry Laurens was trying to get home from France. On 3 August he had been too ill to write and his son had written for him. A week later he told Bourdieu he hoped to take his family to America. Before crossing the Atlantic, he wanted protection against another arrest. If a British ship took him, he asked his London friend James Bourdieu, would he be dragged back to the Tower? A British general had already been delivered in London in exchange for his release. Laurens wanted a letter he could show if he was stopped again. He also had something to report about the peace negotiations in Paris, and for that part of the letter he used numbers. [Laurens to Bourdieu, 10 August 1782, first page](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/iiif/2/amerrevsc:2203/full/1800,/0/default.jpg) · [Complete annotated letter](full_text/joint.md)

### Why Bourdieu wanted a word with Franklin

Bourdieu had offered to go to the French court before Richard Oswald was approached. Now he worried that Benjamin Franklin, the American diplomat in France, and the French foreign minister, the comte de Vergennes, might think he had meddled. He asked Laurens to explain his conduct. In a postscript to Franklin on 7 August, Laurens offered to send a fuller account if Franklin had heard anything to Bourdieu’s detriment. Laurens described Bourdieu as a determined supporter of American independence and a faithful friend to his own country. In Laurens’s account Lord Shelburne thought Bourdieu too closely aligned with French interests and had treated him as a bystander. Bourdieu wanted the men conducting the talks to understand why he had offered his services. The letter gives us Laurens’s account of Shelburne’s opinion. [Laurens’s postscript to Franklin](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/iiif/2/amerrevsc:2497/full/1800,/0/default.jpg) · [Editorial identification of Lord S., note 23](https://books.google.com/books?id=x2CevkQ720gC&pg=PA552)

Three days later, writing to Bourdieu himself, Laurens repeated the substance of that defense. This time he put numbers where he had written names and political terms. Set the manuscripts side by side and the same sentence can be heard in two forms. ## Franklin at 19 in two passages

Laurens’s separate letter to **Richard Oswald**, also dated 7 August at Nantes, names Dr Franklin and describes him as “abundantly polite & kind”. On 10 August Laurens says “the 19 / has been abundtly polite & I may say friendly”. Both passages concern the manner of a correspondent. Two readers independently read the number and abbreviation on the Bourdieu page and the name and phrase on the Oswald page. They differed on the ending of the following noun in Oswald. [Oswald manuscript, leaf 2495](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/iiif/2/amerrevsc:2495/full/1800,/0/default.jpg) · [Bourdieu manuscript, leaf 2204](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/iiif/2/amerrevsc:2204/full/1800,/0/default.jpg) · [Reader A](oswald_reading_a.txt) · [Reader B](oswald_reading_b.txt)

The second instance of 19 comes a few lines later. On 10 August Laurens writes “neither 56 nor 19 have heard”. His 7 August postscript to Franklin reads “neither Count Vergennes nor yourself have heard or said any thing to his detriment”. The second person addresses Franklin. The parallel table records both positions in the pair and the Oswald passage separately. The Oswald comparison identifies the same person at the first 19 through a separate phrase in a letter to a different recipient. Chesnutt and Taylor had already suggested Franklin for 19 in June. These August witnesses support its use twice in this letter. The historical assignments may vary across other letters. [Franklin postscript](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/iiif/2/amerrevsc:2497/full/1800,/0/default.jpg) · [June edition note](https://books.google.com/books?id=x2CevkQ720gC&pg=PA527)

The four positions in the marked quotation plus the two occurrences of 19 give **six of 13 code positions with an identifiable referent**. One of those six, 23, is established only to the level of French interests. The seven other positions remain outside this count. No plaintext share is assigned to the surrounding unciphered English.

## The other numbers

The order of the two names in the Franklin postscript suggests Vergennes for 56. For 19 the Oswald letter supplies a second comparison involving Franklin. We lack a second such link for 56, so its position stays open. The transformed passage puts *his detriment* against *the dishonour or prejudice of 67*, which implicates Bourdieu but leaves the expression at 67 open. The addressee and dates alone do not settle 21. The May 16 Ostend catalogue packet includes letters to Oswald, Bridgen and Franklin, and two of them received letters again on August 7. [May correspondence, USC](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/digital/collection/amerrevsc/id/2604/)

## What Laurens told Bourdieu on 28 August

The 28 August letter opens, “My last Letter to you is dated the 10.th Inst.” (read once more from image 2460 by one AI reader; not in reader_a.txt or reader_b.txt). The favor of the 16th, which Laurens says arrived the day before, is Bourdieu’s incoming letter. Two AI readers independently read the complete page without names or numbers suggested to them. They both found *45*, *19* and *68*, in that order. Reader A transcribed “I have lately heard from 19. Seemingly a studied silence respecting the / concerns of 68.” Reader B read the same words, with capitals in “Studied Silence”. The slash marks the manuscript line break. Both read the address as Bourdieu, Esquire, London. A read the given name as *Jones* and B read it as *James*. The library catalogues this first letter as Henry Laurens to James Bourdieu. Both readers saw a complimentary close without a visible signature. [Image 2460, exact reading copy](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/iiif/2/amerrevsc:2460/full/1400,/0/default.jpg) · [Library record, Laurensh576-1](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/digital/api/singleitem/collection/amerrevsc/id/2460) · [Image SHA-256](august_28/INPUT.sha256) · [Reader A](august_28/reader_a.txt) · [Reader B](august_28/reader_b.txt)

On 7 August Laurens had raised James Bourdieu’s concern that Franklin and Vergennes might think he had meddled in the peace negotiations. Franklin acknowledged that letter on 19 August. His reply discusses Laurens’s health, Bath and the hazards of a winter crossing. He says nothing about Bourdieu. The endorsement dates receipt to 24 August, four days before Laurens reported hearing from 19. On 3 September Laurens thanked Franklin for the letter of the 19th. The omission and delivery date corroborate the proposed pairing, **19 = Franklin** and **68 = Bourdieu**. The 28 August passage gives no name or date for the communication from 19. The reviewer selected the passage retrospectively. The August 10 count remains six of thirteen. [7 August postscript](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-37-02-0468) · [Franklin’s reply and endorsement, vol. 38, pp. 18–19](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-38-02-0016) · [3 September acknowledgement](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-38-02-0051) · [28 August reading plan](august_28/PLAN.md)

Laurens advises Bourdieu to read *No. 45* after discussing prospects for peace. Only 45 carries the prefix *No.* It could be a numbered paper. The letter gives no title and does not say whether it was printed. The June reference to the “45 affair” does not establish that both numbers designate the same thing. ## Prior work and the Shelburne copy

The 7 and 10 August letters are held in the Henry Laurens Papers, accession 13612, South Caroliniana Library, University of South Carolina. The library marks the objects public domain. The status of the 10 August witness as draft, retained copy or dispatched letter remains unresolved. [10 August catalogue record](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/digital/collection/amerrevsc/id/2206/) · [7 August catalogue record](https://digital.tcl.sc.edu/digital/collection/amerrevsc/id/2500/)

The Historical Manuscripts Commission described the August 10 item as partly in cipher in its *Fifth Report* (1876), p. 256, Shelburne papers, vol. 35, no. 55. The present Clements Library inventory describes this volume as copies and translations of intercepted letters. The South Carolina witness carries the same correspondents and date. The Michigan witness still requires direct collation. Repository catalogue entry 772 concerns French diplomatic intercepts in that volume and does not identify this Laurens–Bourdieu item.
