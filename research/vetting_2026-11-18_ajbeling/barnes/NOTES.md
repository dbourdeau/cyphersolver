# Barnes to Paget, 14 March 1593

Green, Calendar of State Papers, Domestic, Elizabeth 1591–1594 (1867), p. 328, item 75, places this draft under 14 March 1593. The public repository at 31eca71 (unchanged at 7d9b91fb8) lists 1594 in the main date fields of lead 957, but its October vetting already gave the correct year and identified the March component of lead 903 as the same item. The original is The National Archives, SP 12/244 f.140. The photograph on Gale scan 149 includes the body and sideways margin of f.140r. Its largely blank endorsed verso is on the left of scan 150. The densely written facing leaf on the right is a different document. [Original](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/MC4304381077/SPOL) · [Green, p.328, scan 344](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/MC4304300001/SPOL)

The earlier project screen had seen part of the recto. We obtained both complete viewer images and asked two readers independently to describe the shapes and their pixel positions. Both saw cipher embedded in the recto's ordinary writing. Examining the margin after a quarter turn revealed two more sequences to the second reader. One occurs in the passage followed by “hath bene”, a flank for the first asterisked gap in Green near his account of projected travel by sea. The next margin passage continues after the discussion of Scotland. Neither flanking statement identifies the letters or words inside the gap. Several dark marks have lighter writing above them. The two readers had no key or calendar reading for their transcriptions and did not use the interlinear words to select shapes. Public prior-work records cover the earlier Cryptiana, Lasry, DECODE and edition checks. No further literature search or priority claim was made for this transcription. **Agreement.** Reader A marked 133 positions or joined groups. Reader B marked 111, including unresolved spans. The counting units differ. We used pixel coordinates on the same original, entry order and a conservative shape vocabulary. Only exact unqualified digits and the distinct square with a central dot were eligible. Centers had to lie within 15 pixels for the body or 20 for the rotated margin. A pair counted only when removing it reduced the maximum number of ordered matches in that run, so equally plausible alignments stay open. This yielded **43 shared shape positions**, including the isolated square with a central dot, whose function is unknown. The six accepted run counts are 8, 12, 10, 8, 4 and 1. Every other observation is open in both readers’ respective position tables. Different label vocabularies can conceal real further agreement. A shape resembling a written digit does not on its own establish a numeric codegroup.

This is a transcription comparison. We read zero new cipher words. No key was tested, no control run was warranted, and no key value is proposed. A subsequent decipherment test would need an independently attested mapping, fixed boundaries between codegroups, and a separate holdout passage. All working scans stay in the project. The held source patch includes only the dates, shelfmarks and links.
