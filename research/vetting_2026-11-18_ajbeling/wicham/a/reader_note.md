# Wicham, blind folio and date reading A

Scan 162 right (f. 151r) and scan 163 left (f. 151v) form the opening and continuation of one letter. “2 of May” and “the 28” in its first sentence describe two received letters. At the end of the author’s own text I read “this first of June / wᵗʰ vs.” There is no written year at this close. A short group beginning with a 2-shaped mark occurs before the date, as does another in the opening after the abbreviated title Mr. These are candidate cipher groups. The abbreviation itself is ordinary secretary usage. “I”, the abbreviations for pronouns, “15” with a currency superscript, and the many underlines and corrections do not establish cipher usage. Most writing on these two faces is plain English. Postscript writing concerning a letter, money and news is also legible as prose. “Harry Wicham” is a written subscription. A small annotation above the correction near the money passage appears to specify a period of months.

Scan 163 right (f. 152r) is effectively blank. Scan 164 left (f. 152v) has the recipient and occupation at mid-page, approximately “A Monsʳ / Mounsieur Fransis Direck / marchant en Anvers.” Thus the images supply an Antwerp merchant named in an address. A separate, much larger notation higher on the fold reads “1602 April 10 / Enclosures”. The adjacent opening in scan 162 also shows large April 1602 date writing on the preceding leaf. The physical address side precedes the September letter in the bound volume. The facing position does not by itself prove to whom the September letter was sent, and the large later annotation cannot independently date the writing of either letter.

Scan 164 right (f. 153r) is a newly opened letter with its own beginning, closing and signature. Its closing runs “other newes / hear is not, but yᵗ I expect to heare from yᵘ. adieu 21 of September”, then “yoʳˢ euer as his owne”, then “Ha: Wicham.” The first date numeral looks like 2; the smaller second looks like 1. No year follows. I read the distinct note in the upper left margin approximately as “ob. [4?] Feb. 1595”, with its first words and day uncertain. There is no clear basis to use this year as the letter’s year of writing. This marginal ink, its placement, the actual September closing, and the separate 1602 endorsement should remain distinguishable in a catalogue entry. I found no salutation naming the recipient in the September body.

The September cipher is directly visible in the middle of the letter. Numeric groups read 137 after “so longe wᵗʰ”, 138 after a second “wᵗʰ”, and 144 after “may undo”. The first 1 in each is unusually short. In the same passage a multiglyph group follows “he deales wᵗʰ one”, and another lies between “the vieldest” and “that euer”. These groups contain loops, bent strokes and crossed/angular shapes. Smaller writing above several groups looks like interlinear glossing. A bracket in the left margin and short, lighter marginal words approximately resembling “owen” and “Paget” annotate this passage. I have recorded these as visible annotation only. Bracket, gloss, cross-outs and signature initials are distinct from the inline cipher. No inference about a historic person, a place concealed by a group, the authorship of the glosses, a key or the plaintext of any symbol is required by these images.
