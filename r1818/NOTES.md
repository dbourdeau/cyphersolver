## What it is

- Both sides are filled edge to edge with about 40 lines each of very small writing: roughly 7,000 to 8,000 signs.
  There are crossings-out, words written between the lines, underlined runs, and on p. 1 a column written vertically
  in the left margin. The top left of p. 1 has an underlined heading, and the first line of p. 2 begins with an
  underlined "Irin 75 ...".
- The script mixes three kinds of signs. First, Latin letter shapes in a cursive hand (a, i, l, m, n, r, t, v,
  b, f, S, F, N). Second, invented signs: a 3/z-shaped sign (very frequent), large E-forms, a circled a, a circle
  with a face, hearts, a large X/chi, a long-s/beta, and crossed letters. Third, numerals (75, 17, 58, 88, 1, 2, 3).
  Commas are used as word dividers.
- The letter-shaped runs do not read as Swedish, Latin or German as written ("Sisbdat", "Siraʒes", "Siven", "fnamp",
  "billa", "arto"), so it is not plain text in an old hand. It is a cipher.
- Repeated units in the six-line sample (`transcription_sample.txt`): "F S , ʒCt" twice, "y S ," / "v S ," three
  times, "bʒa"/"b8a" four times, and "Nr" before a word four times. Word-level repeats like these suggest code signs
  for frequent words sitting alongside a letter substitution. The record name "Sami" and the Västerbotten holder
  leave Swedish and a Sami language both possible for the plaintext.

## Why it stops here

1. **Legibility (the blocker).** At about 125 px/cm the strokes of this hand are 2-3 px wide, and the signs overlap
   or are crossed out. In the six sample lines, roughly half the signs cannot be told apart with confidence (the
   ʒ-like sign in particular stands for several shapes that the scan merges). A ciphertext-only attack on a mixed
   letter-and-code system with an unknown plaintext language needs a sign-accurate transcription, and this scan
   cannot give one. Upscaling adds no information.
2. **No outside material.** There is no key, no crib, no sibling record, no reference on DECODE, and nothing found
   in print or on the web (searches on the archive, Megyesi/DECRYPT and "Sami" cipher: nothing).
