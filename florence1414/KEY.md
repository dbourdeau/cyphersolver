# Cifra Decemviri di Balìa 1414: Gabbrielli's partial key as printed by Meister

Source: Aloys Meister, *Die Anfänge der modernen diplomatischen Geheimschrift* (Paderborn 1902), p. 49,
"Florenz, Beispiele: 1. Cifra Decemviri di Balia 1414. (Lettere della Signoria risponsive; filza Nr. 1.)",
footnote 2: "Florenz, Staatsarchiv Chiavi delle cifre II. Pars 3 Nr. 1." (ULB Münster PDF index 61; crop
`meister_p49_key1414.png`, public domain.) Meister gives no sender, date within 1414 or letter number.

Column placement read from the 300-dpi crop (sign x-position under the letter row).

Word signs: **per = 8**, **et = f**, **che = dum**.

Totals: 35 letter signs + 3 word signs = 38, which is Somogyi's count for "Fi1" (*Verbum* 2016, table: 38 = 35 + 3,
type "4C"). m, h, k and z blank; b, d, f, p, q, x have one sign each. No nulls and no nomenclator of names are
listed, unlike Gabbrielli's 1424 Ricasoli table (96 signs). Gabbrielli built both tables from words he could read
in the letters (glosses), so, as in `florence1429/`, the table is a seed and not the full key.

Two readings of the table to keep in mind when the letters are in hand:
- × stands under both e (row 2) and u (row 2). Either one sign did duty for two letters, or the print conflates
  two similar crosses (the e one is a saltire, the u one a slightly larger ×).
- "a" = l and "D" = l, "S" = f, "q" = c, "f" = et, "8" = per: several signs are Latin letters used for other values,
  so a quick glance at a letter can mistake cipher for clear text.
