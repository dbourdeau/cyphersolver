# Iberia scan, 23 Sept 2026: pre-1449 undeciphered ciphertexts (and hard unsolved Iberian ciphers)

Scope: Crown of Aragon (ACA), Castile (AGS, RAH), Navarre (AGN), Portugal (ANTT), ARV, ARM, ACB (Benedict XIII).
Already known and excluded: ACA Reserva 12 (Salas 1931), reg. 3225 f.59r 1435, regs 3226/3224/2693/3113,
1429 Castile-war key, 1437 Montcada-Amat key, Sástago 1495-96, RAH 9/7-9/8, BNE MSS/20211, DECODE.

## Candidates

### Lead: Galende Díaz 2013 ("La criptografía medieval: el Libro del Tesoro", UCM PDF) p. 45
- States that for the 13th-15th c. there are almost no Castilian cipher traces but Aragon keeps "criptogramas del
  obispo de Barcelona, Francisco Climent, de los reyes Alfonso V y Juan II o de notarios barceloneses".
  Francesc Climent Sapera was bishop of Barcelona 1410-15, archbishop of Zaragoza 1415-30. Chasing below.
- The Libro del Tesoro itself (BNE Res. 20, pseudo-Alfonso X alchemical octaves) is a dead end: key found by
  Fernández-Monzón & Cominges 1966 (Punta Europa 110-111); cipher octaves match clear copies (Alicante, Salamanca).

### Lead: Francesc Climent Sapera ↔ Benedict XIII, ciphered correspondence c. 1398-1410s
- Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana (art. "Francesc Climent Sapera"): as canon of Barcelona and Benedict XIII's confidant he
  "mantingué una llarga correspondència xifrada durant les gestions diplomàtiques per a obtenir l'obediència dels reis
  de França i de Castella" (i.e. c. 1395-1403 Avignon siege era). Galende Díaz 2013 p. 45 also names "criptogramas
  del obispo de Barcelona, Francisco Climent". No shelfmark found online yet; most likely Arxiu Capitular de
  Barcelona (Climent founded chapels there; 2012 monograph Climent Parcet & París Muñoz, "La catedral de Barcelona i
  el patriarca Francesc Climent, àlies Sapera, 1349-1430: un enigma en el cisma d'Occident") or ACA Cartes reials
  Martí I. ACB published catalogue ("Efemèrides"/Catàleg PDF) has no xifra entry. Status (read or not): UNKNOWN.
  -> Worth a direct enquiry to ACB / the 2012 book. If it exists it is c. 1400, i.e. a record target.

### Lead (not Iberian archive): Alfonso V autograph cipher letter to the Duke of Milan
- Zurita, Anales, lib. XV cap. 5 (cited Pedro/Claver book, Zaragoza 2025, n. 25): Alfonso wrote a ciphered letter
  in his own hand to the duke of Milan (Filippo Maria Visconti, so 1440s). Would sit in ASMi (Visconti archive
  largely lost 1447) - pass to the Italy sweep. Status unknown.

### Find: Pisan cipher key, Castel di Castro (Cagliari) 27 Aug 1325 — ACA, Cancillería, Cartas Reales Diplomáticas Jaime II, no. 10044
- Printed by Finke, *Acta Aragonensia* II (1908) no. 606, pp. 934-935 (archive.org actaaragonensiaq02jame, djvu text
  line ~20360): a Pisan captain in Castel di Castro writes to Azzo and Giovanni Malaspina, rectors of Sassari,
  "presentibus intercludimus permutacionem Alphabeti, per quod vos nobis et nos vobis, quando gravis materia
  superesset, rescribamus". Contemporary paper copy (ACA CRD 10044), i.e. intercepted by the Aragonese during the
  conquest of Sardinia. Key = reversed/shifted alphabet (a->q, b->r ... ) plus ~11 name signs (king of Aragon,
  infante, judge of Arborea, governor, commune of Pisa, Sassari, Castello, Villa di Chiesa, the Sards).
- This is a KEY, not a ciphertext; it is read by construction. Value: (1) one of the oldest dated cipher keys in
  any Iberian archive (1325); (2) any ciphertext in this system among ACA CRD Jaime II 1323-26 Sardinian
  intercepts (Pisan/Malaspina/Doria/Arborea letters) would be instantly readable - worth a sweep of CRD Jaime II
  nos. ~9000-11000 on PARES for letters containing cipher. Prior-art status of such letters: unknown.
- Check DECODE for "1325" before claiming (DECODE had 616 records 1300-1496, mostly keys; this one may be there).

### Find: Navarre chancery key, c. 1394-1406 — AGN (Archivo Real y General de Navarra), Papeles sueltos leg. 178, carp. 3, fol. 87r
- Serrano Larráyoz, "Una clave criptográfica de la cancillería de Carlos III el Noble de Navarra", *Príncipe de
  Viana* 59/213 (1998) 171-182 (PDF: https://www.culturanavarra.es/uploads/files/PV213-pagina0171.pdf, needs a
  browser UA). Key copied into the chancery formulary (Idoate 1956, AHDE 33; Canellas 1988), on leaves torn out in
  1924 and later recovered. Content dates it 1394-1406 (Benedict XIII, Cherbourg, cardinal of Pamplona Martín de
  Zalba †1403, king of Sicily separate from Aragon). Three simple substitution alphabets (one struck), two labelled
  "l'alferiz" (the alférez Carlos de Beaumont) and "l'arcidiane" (an archdeacon); nulls; ~30-sign nomenclator in
  French/Navarrese (kings of France, England, Castile, Portugal, Aragon, Sicily, Scotland; dukes of Berry, Burgundy,
  Orléans, Bourbon, Brittany; Pierre count of Mortain; Benedict XIII; the war, the peace treaty, the truces,
  Cherbourg; Florentine merchants Gautier, Sauvage, Guillerm Garin as probable couriers).
- This is a KEY (read). Serrano calls it the oldest Iberian key (older than Montcada 1437 and the 1429 key).
- LEAD: letters of Charles III's envoys c. 1394-1406 (Hundred Years' War embassies: France, England, Cherbourg)
  written in this key, by the alférez or the archdeacon, may survive in AGN Comptos, Cajones (catalogued by
  J. R. Castro, *Catálogo del AGN, Sección de Comptos*, vols. XX-XXVI for 1394-1406). Serrano (1998) cites no
  ciphertext. If one exists it is readable with the key: a c. 1400 reading. Status: no ciphertext known to me.
- Serrano also gave a poster "Escritura y poder. Claves criptográficas utilizadas en la cancillería del reino de
  Navarra durante los siglos XIV al XVI" (IMC Lleida 2014): implies more (even 14th-c.) Navarrese keys. Not online.

### Lead (for maria1435 follow-up, not new): the 1429 key's home volume
- Recall from maria1435/NOTES.md: the 1429 key is in Valencia, Col·legi del Corpus Christi, Fons Maians
  (B.A.H.M.) ms. 677 "Llibre d'estols e armades" f. 435v, "cum baiulo generali Valencie" (Joan Mercader, batlle
  general 1418-40), and ff. 160-161 hold an "aliud abecedarium" copied from a lost "Segon d'estols e armades".
  NEW angle: the batlle general's side of that correspondence is ARV, Bailia (Lletres i privilegis / Lletres
  reials to the batlle, 1429-30). Letters received in that key, if any survive, would sit there - readable with
  the printed key, dated 1429-30. Not online; ARV catalogue does not flag cipher. Ask ARV.

### *** CANDIDATE 1: Benedict XIII cipher instruction + autograph letter, Arxiu Capitular de Barcelona, capsa "Documentos referentes a la familia Luna I" (1394-1423, prob. c. 1398-1410)
- Source: Aloys Meister, *Die Geheimschrift im Dienste der päpstlichen Kurie* (Paderborn 1906), pp. 22-23
  (archive.org diegeheimschrift00meis, djvu text line ~1585): Benedict XIII kept Clement VII's cipher practice;
  "Beispiele davon finden sich in Barcelona im Archivo de la Iglesia Catedral in Kapsel: Documentos referentes a
  la familia Luna I, und zwar ein Handschreiben Benedikts XIII. mit einigen chiffrierten Zeilen und zweitens eine
  ungefähr drei Seiten in Folio füllende Instruktion, von der ein großer Teil in Geheimschrift geschrieben ist."
  Footnote: second-hand, "kind communication from Father Ehrle, who assures me they are mixed with signs" -
  i.e. Meister never saw them and printed no key or transcription.
- Fits the Gran Enciclopèdia's "llarga correspondència xifrada" between Benedict and Francesc Climent Sapera
  (canon of Barcelona, later bishop), whose papers went to the cathedral. Date range 1394-1423; if Climent's
  missions for the obedience of France and Castile, c. 1395-1403.
- Length: ~3 folio pages "largely in cipher" = plausibly 1,500-4,000 cipher signs - well above ciphertext-only
  threshold. Plus a letter with some cipher lines (same or related key). Language: Latin, Catalan or Aragonese.
  Cipher type (per Meister's description of Clement VII/Avignon ciphers): simple substitution with letters,
  figures and digits mixed, dotted/stroked variants, nulls; per Ehrle "mixed with signs". Breakable ciphertext-only
  if it is anything like the Clement VII alphabets Meister prints (Schlüsselsammlung Nr. I) - and those printed
  Avignon keys should be tried first.
- Images online: NO (ACB is not digitised for this; the capsa name is Meister's 1906 label and may be recatalogued).
- Prior-art status: no reading found in any source checked (Meister, Galende 2013, GEC, Sanahuja 2020,
  González Sánchez ELEM, Vito 2025). Needs a check of: Ehrle's own Benedict XIII studies (Archiv für Literatur- und
  Kirchengeschichte 5-7, "Aus den Acten des Afterconcils von Perpignan" and "Neue Materialien zur Geschichte Peters
  von Luna"), Puig y Puig *Pedro de Luna* (1920), Climent Parcet & París Muñoz 2012 monograph, and Sebastián
  Puig y Puig's appendices. If Ehrle printed a transcription it will be there.
- WHY THIS IS THE TOP LEAD: c. 1400 (possibly 1395-1403), long, in one system, in a Spanish archive that grants
  photographs. If unread it would be the oldest ciphertext-only read anywhere by a wide margin.

### Related keys for Candidate 1
- Meister 1906 prints Gabriel de Lavinde's Avignon cipher alphabets (Clement VII, 1379; Vat. Collect. 393
  ff. 166-181) as Schlüsselsammlung Nr. I; he states Benedict XIII's ciphers followed the same principles. Those
  alphabets are the first thing to test on the ACB instruction. Meister also prints the Pisan-pope key
  Domenico (curia) <-> Marco Canetoli, Bologna, after 1412 (Bologna ASB, Demaniale S. Francesco 237/4369).

## Dead ends (checked 23 Sept 2026, do not repeat)
- Libro del Tesoro (BNE Res. 20, pseudo-Alfonso X alchemical octaves): key found 1966 (Fernández-Monzón & Cominges);
  cipher octaves have clear counterparts. Read.
- Urgell rebellion 1413-15 (CoDoIn ACA III no. 30): Margarida de Montferrat's "cipher" is jargon code words
  (lion = prince of England, cock = Benedict XIII...), printed in clear. Not a ciphertext.
- Juan Claver (Zaragoza 2025 monograph, zaguan.unizar.es BOOK-2025-316): 1490s-1500s, read by Parisi.
- Castile before 1450: Galende 2013 says "apenas hay vestigios" of cipher in Castile 13th-15th c.; criptohistoria.es
  history, Alcocer 1934 (BVMC, Cloudflare-blocked) start with the Catholic Monarchs. Nothing found.
- Valencia 1356-66 (Sanahuja Ferrer, Edad Media 21, 2020): no encryption found in Valencian spy correspondence.
- González Sánchez, ELEM (UCM) on 15th-c. Iberian espionage: only one ciphered case = Salas letter (read).
- AGN Navarra "Archivo Abierto" (navarra.archivoabierto.es): searches cifra/cifrada/cifras/clave return only
  16th-18th c. items (Rena 1521 etc.). Medieval Comptos not described at that level online.
- PARES full-text (find?texto=): cifra, xifra, cifrada, ciffra, zifra, criptografica, anagramas, signos
  convencionales, escritura cifrada, carta cifrada -> only the 1437 Montcada key (show/3205682) is pre-1500.
  PARES descriptions of ACA material do not flag cipher; only image sweeps would find it.
- Finke, Acta Aragonensia I-III (James II 1291-1327): Finke says secrecy was kept "durch allerlei Geheimschriften"
  but prints no ciphertext; the only cipher item is the 1325 Pisan key (above). Coded-message arrangements 1301
  (clear phrases) only.
- Zurita, Anales (1579/1610 prints, OCR grep "cifra"): only the 1441 Alfonso V -> Duke of Milan autograph cipher
  letter (lib. XV). Nothing else pre-1450.
- ACB published series index (arxiu.catedralbcn.org INDEX-Series-web.pdf) and catalogue: no "xifra" entry; Cisma
  papers are in "Documentació en paper del temps del Cisma" and Baucells' 1985 Cisma inventory (not online).
- Portugal: web searches for 15th-c. Portuguese ciphered letters (D. Duarte, regent D. Pedro, Afonso V) found
  nothing; Livro dos Conselhos has no known cipher.
- Google Books API quota exhausted in this session (no snippet search of Galende 1995).

### ACA reg. 3222 (Secretorum locumtenentiae reginae Mariae 1, July 1420 - Dec 1421) - SWEPT, NO CIPHER
- All 308 PARES images (show/13153125) downloaded and viewed as contact sheets (images 22-307 with text; 5-21
  and 300-307 blank/binding). Queen María's letters from Zaragoza, Calatayud, Tortosa, Barcelona to Alfonso V
  (then in Sardinia/Corsica/Naples), Juan II of Castile, the infantes, Álvaro de Luna, Valencian officials, Rome:
  all in clear. No cipher runs, no "in cifra" notes seen at contact-sheet resolution. (Caveat: contact-sheet
  scale; a single short cipher word inside a line could be missed, a paragraph could not.)
- Not swept (time): 2691 Secretorum 1 (king, 1419-26), 3218-3220 CSS locumtenentiae 1-3 (1422-26), Itinerum
  Sigilli Secreti 2781-2786 (1420-25), 2671, 2571, Sardiniae 1-2. Based on maria1435 (king's chancery registered
  cipher letters in clear, marked "in ciffra") the king's registers are more likely to yield "fuit expedita in
  cifra" notes (evidence of cipher use + plaintexts) than ciphertext. Queen's 3218-3220 remain worth a sweep.

### ACA CRD Jaime II Sardinian intercepts 1323-26: any ciphertext? - NOT FOUND ONLINE
- PARES full-text searches (Malaspina/Sasser/alfabeto/Pisa/abecedario) return nothing medieval; CRD Jaime II
  item descriptions do not flag cipher. The key itself (CRD 10044) is studied by Luisa D'Arienzo, "Un cifrario
  segreto pisano nella Sardegna del Trecento", Archivio Storico Sardo 44 (2005) 443-478 (not online here). The
  Voynich-Ninja/NAM citations describe it as a word-substitution (nomenclator) example; no mention of surviving
  ciphertext. Whether D'Arienzo found letters in this alphabet is UNKNOWN - her article is the check. Even if they
  exist they are readable with the printed key (Caesar-like reversed alphabet), so not a ciphertext-only target.

### Portugal (ANTT digitarq API, full scan of "cifra" 1,603 hits and "cifras" 267 hits)
- Nothing before 1532 that is a ciphered document: earliest cipher-related items are CC/1/49/20 (1532, Duarte da
