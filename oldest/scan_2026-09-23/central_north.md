# Oldest-cipher scan, 23 Sept 2026: Central, Northern and Eastern Europe, the Empire, Byzantium/Ottoman

Goal: undeciphered ciphertexts before 1449 (ideally before 1425), images online, breakable ciphertext-only;
secondary, hard unsolved ciphers of any date from this region not on DECODE / cryptiana / Schmeh / Dunin.
Findings appended as they are checked.

## Hungary: Zsigmondkori oklevéltár I-XIV (1387-1427), full-text sweep

- Downloaded all 14 volumes of the Sigismund-era calendar from REAL-EOD (real-eod.mtak.hu 16888 ... 17312) and
  grepped for titkosírás / rejtjel / cifra / zifra / chiffre / Geheimschrift. One hit only:
  **ZsO XIV no. 269**, Brassó 12 Mar 1427, Lodovico Sabini (Filippo Maria Visconti's envoy to Sigismund) to the
  duke, "Titkosírással írt, olasz nyelvű jelentés" (report written in cipher, Italian). ASMi, Carteggio
  extra dominium; printed Osio, *Documenti diplomatici tratti dagli archivi milanesi* II no. 286, and in the
  Carteggio sforzesco calendar II 25/180. The calendar summarises the content, so it was read (Osio prints it).
  **Dead end as a target, but a lead:** Milanese envoys at Sigismund's court 1420s-30s wrote in cipher; Osio I-III
  may print further items "in cifra" without text. That is ASMi material (Italy sweep).
- No ciphered Hungarian royal or baronial letter surfaces in the calendar for 1387-1427. Hungaricana full-text
  search itself returns 403 to scripts.

## Teutonic Order (GStA PK, XX. HA, Ordensbriefarchiv)

- Literature: Hans Koeppen, "Die Anfänge der Verwendung von Chiffren im diplomatischen Schriftwechsel des
  Deutschen Ordens", *Festschrift Kurt Forstreuter* (Würzburg 1958), pp. 173-191 (not online; the key text for
  this whole question). Sławomir Jóźwiak, *Wywiad i kontrwywiad w państwie zakonu krzyżackiego w Prusach*
  (Malbork 2004) dates the Order's first ciphered letter to **1384** and describes the systems: word substitution
  (cover words), pseudonyms and letter abbreviations, and a line-marking device (letters s / k / l at the head of a
  line = skip / read backwards / read normally). These are jargon codes and transpositions, not letter ciphers;
  Koeppen's edition of the procurators' reports (*Die Berichte der Generalprokuratoren*, vols. 1-4, 1960-76,
  covering 1384-1436) would have printed the ciphered passages resolved, since the Order's keys sit in the OBA.
- Concrete item found: **OBA (JH I) 2987**, Marienburg 26 June 1419, Grand Master Michael Küchmeister to the
  procurator (Peter von Wormditt / incoming Johann Tiergart), draft "z. T. in Chiffren" (Joachim-Hubatsch regest,
  via the Hamburg Preußisches Urkundenbuch regesta, http://www.spaetmittelalter.uni-hamburg.de/Urkundenbuch/pub/orden1419.html).
  A sender's draft; almost certainly in Koeppen's vol. 2/3 with the cipher resolved. Grepping the Hamburg regesta
  1380-1460 for Chiffre/Geheimschrift/Ziffer gives only this one item (the regesta there are a selection).
- **Status: probably read (Koeppen). Not a target unless Koeppen 1958 lists passages he could not resolve.**
  Access problems: Joachim-Hubatsch *Regesta* vol. I is on archive.org (regestahistorico001-2joac) but
  lending-restricted (text 401); DDB, which carries OBA items with images, is behind a bot wall. Next step is a
  library copy of Koeppen 1958 and of *Berichte* vol. 3-4 (search "Chiffre" in the introductions).

## Empire: Deutsche Reichstagsakten, Ältere Reihe vols. 1-11 (1376-1437) + vol. 22 (1471), full-text sweep

Downloaded the archive.org OCR of every DRTA volume for 1376-1437 (29 scans) and grepped for Chiffre/chiffriert/
Geheimschrift/cifra/zifra. Every cipher mention is Italian or a key, none a German cipher letter:
- **1402, DRTA 5 nos. 138 and 248**: Francesco Novello da Carrara sends a *cifra* (key) to Bishop Georg of Trent
  (27 Apr 1402) and, at King Ruprecht's request, to Ruprecht (5 Aug 1402, "unam cifram, qua ... scriberentur
  secreta literarum"); copies in Venice, Marciana lat. XIV 93 ff. 31, 90 (Carrara letter-book; printed
  Valentinelli, *Archiv f. Kunde österr. Gesch.-Qu.* 26, 367). The keys themselves are not enclosed in the copy.
  **Lead only**: a ciphered letter between Ruprecht and Carrara in 1402-05 would be a pre-1425 target; none is
  known to me. Look in Marciana lat. XIV 93 for letters marked "per cifram" and in the Ruprecht register (GLA
  Karlsruhe 67/801-807; Chmel/Oberndorff *Regesten der Pfalzgrafen* vol. 2).
- 1428: Milanese instruction for envoy Pezzi to Sigismund, with "una zifra" (key) to be copied in Milan (ASMi,
  printed Osio II 393-396). Key only.
- 1433, DRTA 11: letters of the Sienese envoys with Sigismund (Siena, Concistoro, lettere 1433) "z. T." or "zum
  großen Teil chiffriert"; DRTA quotes them in clear, so they were read. Italy sweep.
- 1471, DRTA 22 (Friedrich III, Regensburg): Nuremberg envoys' reports (Jobst Haller) with a code-word jargon
  (sperber = keiser, raub = stadtsteuer); key found in StA Nürnberg A-Laden-Akten S I L 73 nr. 17; a few code words
  left unresolved by the editor (e.g. "kolmeis"). Word code, read, late.
- Osio, *Documenti diplomatici* II (1428, Perugia letter to a Milanese courtier) omits "brevi passi in cifra di cui
  non si è rinvenuta la chiave", and vol. III (1440s) has "parola cifrata ... mancandoci la chiave": short
  unread Milanese passages. Italy sweep, noted for the Italy agent.

## Hanse, Livonia, Lithuania, Poland, Basel: printed-edition sweep (dead end)

archive.org OCR of 84 volumes grepped for Chiffre/Geheimschrift/cifra/zifra/"litteris occultis": all
*Hanserecesse* series I-III scans on archive.org, *Hansisches Urkundenbuch*, *Liv-, Est- und Kurländisches
Urkundenbuch* (Bunge vols. on archive.org), *Codex epistolaris Vitoldi* (1882), *Codex epistolaris saeculi XV*
(Lewicki 1876), *Concilium Basiliense* (Haller vols. 1-7). **No ciphered letter at all before 1450.** The only hit
is an Italian note in CB V about numerals. Hanseatic and Baltic correspondence of this period is in clear. The
Veckinchusen letters (Stieda 1921, 546 letters, c. 1400-1426) also grepped: no cipher, only merchants' marks.

## More printed-edition sweeps (dead ends)

- Priebatsch, *Politische Correspondenz des Kurfürsten Albrecht Achilles* vols. 2-3; Wolkan, *Briefwechsel des Eneas
  Silvius Piccolomini* (Fontes rer. Austr. II 61-62, 67-68); *Magyar diplomacziai emlékek* (Mátyás era,
  Nagy/Nyáry 1875-78); *Codex diplomaticus Saxoniae regiae*; *Monumenta medii aevi ... Poloniae*: grepped, no
  German, Hungarian, Polish or Czech ciphered letter before 1450. Hits are Italian ("per zifram" in an envoy's
  report of 1447 in Wolkan II; "scribantur in cifra" in a Venetian instruction of 1459 in MDE I).
- Friedrich III's notebook, ÖNB Cod. 2674 (1437-56): the "AEIOU" sign games; a 2022 MGH conference paper
  (A. Walder) only asked whether it holds cryptography. Printed by Chmel; new MGH/Harrassowitz edition. No cipher
  text of any length is described. Not a target.
- Rudolf IV of Austria's "Geheimschrift" (1358-65, charters, tomb, St Stephen's): alphabet known, read.
- Kyeser, *Bellifortis* (1405, Göttingen 4° Cod. Ms. philos. 63/64a, Vienna 5278, Chantilly): reversed words
  (sirepip = piperis), a short "chiffrierte Botschaft" in three 7-chapter copies, and a letter-shift gloss on
  Wolfenbüttel f. 68r, all resolved by Cermann, *Manuscripta* Suppl. 8 (2013). Chantilly f. 153v "cryptic text"
  is a charm (Quarg: Old Albanian), not a cipher.
- Fontana, *Bellicorum instrumentorum liber* (BSB Cod. icon. 242, Padua c. 1420): Latin in a sign alphabet, read
  by Wilhelm Meyer, transliterated in Cod. icon. 242a. Italy anyway.
- German glosses and texts in Geheimschrift, 8th-15th c.: bfk / dot-vowel / cgl substitutions, trivially read.
  The DFG/Vienna project (Stephan Müller, *Handbuch der deutschen Glossen und Texte des Mittelalters in
  Geheimschrift*, c. 100 texts + 150 gloss witnesses, due De Gruyter 2027) is the census to ask for any
  item it could not resolve. Müller's slide deck (old Paderborn URL) is offline.

## Scandinavia: SDHK sweep (dead end)

Riksarkivet's full SDHK dataset (all c. 44,000 Swedish medieval charters, CSV
https://filer.riksarkivet.se/registerdata/SDHK/csv/sdhk_2411.csv) grepped for chiffer/lönnskrift/cifra/Geheimschrift:
no ciphered letter. The only "dechiffrera" hits mean reading a hard hand (1804 transcription). Riksarkivet's
"Chifferklaver" series starts in the 1500s. The one Kalmar-Union cryptic item is Conrad Gruter's machine book for
Erik VII (BAV Vat. lat. 5961, 1424), and that is a gold-initial acrostic, already read.

## Secondary goal: Hungary, Láng 2015 list of ciphered texts (MTA doctoral thesis)

Benedek Láng, *Titkosírás a kora újkori Magyarországon* (MTA doktori mű, 2015,
https://real-d.mtak.hu/922/7/dc_758_13_doktori_mu.pdf), pp. 233 ff. is a table of every known early modern
Hungarian ciphertext with a status column. 56 rows are marked "megfejtetlen" (undeciphered). The ones worth a look:
- **C.Ung.03, David Ungnad (imperial ambassador), Constantinople 12 Mar 1576 → Maximilian II**, HHStA Türkei I
  Karton 32, Konv. 3, ff. 199-203: **fully enciphered**, German?, undeciphered. Láng's OUP blog (2017) says
  Ungnad used simple alphabetic substitution, so it could be broken ciphertext-only. Five folios.
- **C.Sin.03, Sinzendorff → Rudolf II, Constantinople 3-7 Sept 1578**, HHStA Türkei I Karton 37, Konv. 2, ff. 1-8:
  fully enciphered, German, undeciphered. Eight folios.
- **C.Breu.01, Breuner, 12 May 1583**, HHStA Türkei I Karton 48, Konv. 3, ff. 137-144, 147-151: fully enciphered,
  German, undeciphered. DECODE has an HHStA Chiffrenschlüssel record marked author "Breuner?" (R1590), so this
  may be a key-lookup read rather than a hard break.
- C.Wes.03/05/06, Wesselényi conspiracy, HHStA Ung. Akt. Spec. Fasc. 327 Konv. D "Chiffres 1664-1668": 22 keys
  and about 5 cipher letters in numbers only, about 6 A4 pages, undeciphered. The project's wesselenyi1664
  (E 199, R674) is a different item; these keys may read these letters.
- Mihály Teleki, 1678: about 14 letters to Naláczi, Rhédey, Haller and others in "numbers, probably
  homophonic", printed undeciphered in *Teleki Mihály levelezése* vol. 8 (Gergely 1905-26). That is one system
  across many letters, a good ciphertext-only case. The project's CATALOGUE.md no. 293 (DECODE R4819-R4834, Teleki's
  letter book) is related; the printed ciphertexts are probably what is needed.
- Others: Brodarics → Nádasdy 1532 (graphic signs, Latin, partial; MNL OL E 185 ff. 52-53); Báthory András →
  Nádasdy 1553-54 (3 letters) and Kerecsényi → Nádasdy 1555-56 (4 letters), Hungarian signs printed in MTT IV/6-7;
  Archduchess Maria Christierna 1597 (German numbers, MTT II/11); Dániel 1649; Mednyánszky 1651/1655; Bay 1706
  (the project already read Bay R478).
- Before starting any of these: check DECODE (Láng's team loaded HHStA keys there) and Láng's *Real Life
  Cryptology* (2018), in case one was read after 2015.

## Bohemia and Moravia (dead end before 1450)

- Palacký, *Urkundliche Beiträge zur Geschichte des Hussitenkrieges 1419-1436* (2 vols., two scans each) and
  *Geschichte von Böhmen* vols. 3-5, *Codex diplomaticus et epistolaris Moraviae* (15 vols. on archive.org):
  grepped, no ciphered letter.
- Oldřich II of Rožmberk (1418-62), *Listář a listinář* (Rynešová/Pelikán, 4 vols. 1929-54): full text on AHISTO
  (nlp.fi.muni.cz/projekty/ahisto). Searched vol. III (1445-48) for cifra/zifra/cyfra/šifra/"tajným písmem", and the
  AHISTO regest search for cifra/zifra/cifris/Geheimschrift/cziffra: no hits. Rožmberk ciphers in Třeboň are
  16th-century (Vilém, Petr Vok), per Mírka, *Crypto-World* 3-4/2013. Hus's Constance letters use cover names,
  not cipher.
- Early modern Czech lead (Mírka 2013): Hanka 1847 printed about 60 ciphered Rudolf/Matthias/Althann letters of
  1608-12 (Archiv Národního muzea, Sbírka D, kart. 9) with keys; all read (the last one by Maloch c. 1857). Not a
  target.

## Byzantium, Ottomans, Mamluks (dead end)

No extant ciphered diplomatic text before 1450 surfaced. Al-Qalqashandi (Mamluk chancery, *Subh al-a'sha*, 1412)
and Ibn al-Durayhim describe cipher systems, but the Mamluk letters that survive (Cairo → Aragon at ACA, BnF
arabe 4440 exchanges) are in clear. Byzantine cryptography survives as short scribal colophon ciphers (simple
substitutions, read, per Gardthausen). Ottoman chancery ciphers start well after 1450. For this region the
Constantinople items that matter are the Habsburg envoys' letters of 1576-83 (see the Láng list above).

## Summary of this sweep

Across the Empire, the Teutonic Order, Hungary, Bohemia, Poland-Lithuania, the Hanse and Livonia, and Scandinavia,
no **non-Italian** ciphertext before 1450 turns up that is undeciphered and substantial. Everything ciphered
before 1450 that reached this region is (a) Italian envoys' reports (Milan, Siena; read or ASMi material), (b) keys
(Carrara 1402), or (c) Teutonic Order jargon/transposition, edited by Koeppen. The best pre-1450 lead is
the Teutonic Order: find Koeppen 1958 and *Berichte* III-IV and check for passages he left unresolved.
