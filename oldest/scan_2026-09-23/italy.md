# Italy scan, 23 Sept 2026: pre-1449 undeciphered ciphertexts (Italian states c. 1350-1450)

Scope: Lucca, Siena, Florence, Venice, Milan, Mantua, Genoa, Ferrara, Bologna, Naples, Papacy, Malatesta; and the
key literature (Meister 1902/1906, Pasini 1872, Gabbrielli). Already known and excluded: Florence Dieci Responsive 2
no. 171 (1424, ours), Mantua 1395 Armanini, Mantua 1428-30 Gonzaga incl. Pandolfo Malatesta 1428, ASMn b.1081 1438,
Venice Steno 1411, Tranchedini 1449, Montefeltro 1451/59, Senatore Naples 1451-59, Sforza/Maino 1446, Ameilh 1363-64.

## PRIORITY: Meister 1902, Florence "Beispiele 1" (1414) and the other early Gabbrielli material

Source read directly: Meister, *Die Anfänge der modernen diplomatischen Geheimschrift* (Paderborn 1902), ULB Münster
PDF https://sammlungen.ulb.uni-muenster.de/download/pdf/3075984.pdf (83 pp., image only, no text layer; printed
p. 43 = PDF index 55, p. 49 = index 61, p. 50 = index 62, p. 51 = index 63).

- **p. 49, "Florenz, Beispiele: 1. Cifra Decemviri di Balia 1414. (Lettere della Signoria risponsive; filza Nr. 1.)"**
  Footnote 2: "Florenz, Staatsarchiv Chiavi delle cifre II. Pars 3 Nr. 1." Meister names **no sender** and gives no
  folio or letter number. The table is a partial homophonic alphabet (a has four signs, c/e/g/i/l/n/o/r/s/t/u two or
  three; several letters, e.g. h, k, m, blank) plus three word signs: per = 8, et = f, che = dum. It is a
  Gabbrielli reconstruction, so the same caveat as the 1424 key applies: built from glossed words, not a found key.
- **Where the letters are.** Gabbrielli's key volume II (compiled 1864) is in three parts: (1) keys from the
  carteggio of the Otto di Pratica 1482-1530; (2) Signoria outgoing (missive) 1481-1530; (3) **Signoria incoming
  (responsive) 1414-1530, 45 keys, 14 of them 15th-century** (Meister p. 45). So the 1414 ciphered letters are in the
  ASF series now called *Signori, Carteggi, Responsive originali*, filza 1 (Meister's "Lettere della Signoria
  risponsive filza 1"), and are letters to the Dieci di Balìa of 1414 (the Dieci of the Ladislaus war, which ended
  with the peace of June 1414 and Ladislaus's death in August). Sender still to be identified; Gabbrielli's vol. II
  has an index of senders ("Dieser wie jeder folgende Band hat einen Index der Absender", p. 45), which is the fastest
  route (ASF reading room, or an Ilardi reel if one was filmed; only reel 58 = vol. I is on Yale Dataverse).
- **Read or printed?** Gabbrielli read enough words to build the table (so, like no. 171, "read in part in 1863", as
  glosses). No continuous reading is known to me in print: Meister prints only the key; Guasti, *Commissioni di
  Rinaldo degli Albizzi* vol. 1 (1867) covers 1399-1423 embassies but prints letters in clear; a targeted check of
  Guasti vol. 1 for 1414 cipher passages is still to do (it is on archive.org). Status: **open lead, pre-1425, key
  partially in print, images not online** (ASF Signori Responsive are not on DECODE as far as the repo records show).
- **Only two early Florentine keys.** Meister p. 46: the oldest surviving Florentine original key is 1481; Gabbrielli
  could reconstruct keys only for 1414 and 1424 from the ciphered despatches. Gabbrielli's vol. I (Dieci 1424-1530,
  197 keys, 62 of the 15th c.) and vol. II (45 keys) do not list unread pre-1425 letters in Meister's account;
  Meister gives no list of ciphered letters Gabbrielli failed to read. The 1424 vol. I no. 1 footnote (p. 50) names
  the four Ricasoli filze (Dieci filze 1-4) and nothing earlier. So: **no other pre-1425 ASF cipher letters are named
  in Meister**; any others would have to come from Gabbrielli's own indexes (vol. I index is partly on reel 58,
  files `58-index*.pdf` per florence1429/NOTES.md; vol. II index not online).
- Also on p. 44: ASF Carte Strozziane (Filza Strozziana Nr. 23) fascicle "Cifere di Lorenzo de Medici con diverse e
  lettere a Cosimo Medici": 13 original keys and 6 letters, Cosimo/Lorenzo era (post-1434). Not pre-1425.

## Siena: a dated 1421 cipher letter (Meister pp. 50-51)

- Meister saw one busta "Lettere in cifra e loro chiavi Nr. 3" at the Siena Staatsarchiv, two fascicles:
  (1) *Cifrari*, 42 original keys, 18 of the 15th c., oldest dated **1433** (Antonio Petruccio; printed p. 51 as
  "Siena, Beispiele 1": a monoalphabetic-ish table plus Niccolò Fortebraccio, Duca di Milano, Serenissimus);
  (2) *Lettere in cifra* of the 15th-16th c., 29 pieces, **"datiert Nr. 1 1421; Nr. 4 1456; Nr. 11 1478; Nr. 17 1528"**.
- So ASSi holds a ciphered letter dated **1421** (Lettere in cifra fasc. 2 no. 1). Meister says Sienese practice was
  to cipher single sentences or words, not whole letters, so it may be short. Sender, length and whether it was read:
  unknown; not printed by Meister. Today the busta is probably ASSi **Concistoro 2308** ("Cifrari I", see below) or
  its neighbour.
- **Images:** Yale Dataverse, Ilardi reel 1503, doi:10.60600/YU/QDOHXM, "Cipher letters. Siena, Archivio di Stato,
  Lettere Cifrate" (13 single-page PDFs, open via API, file ids 7906-7918). Viewed 23 Sept: frame 0 = slate
  "Concistoro"; frame 1 = cover "Concistoro 2308, Cifrari I"; frames 2-9 = nomenclator key sheets of the 1450s-70s
  (one headed "... ambasciatori a Milano 1454", one "Cifra mandata a Lodovico di Salimbene potestà di Casole", one
  "Cifra ... 1475"). These are Meister's fascicle 1 keys, not the letters. Frames 10-12 not yet checked for the 1421
  letter. Useful in any case as a set of Sienese mid-15th-c. keys (sibling keys for Sienese cipher passages).
- Next step: ASSi Concistoro 2308/2309 finding aid; ask for fasc. 2 no. 1 (1421).
- Update: Ilardi reel 1503 frames 10-12 viewed: also key sheets (Meister's nos. 14-16, "cum Leonardo", "con li
  signori di Siena", Antonio de Petrucciis). **The reel holds only keys; the 1421 letter is not on it.**

## Lucca: Paolo Guinigi's key book (ASLu, Governo di Paolo Guinigi n. 5)

- Marco Vito, "La crittografia diplomatica e militare nell'Italia del Quattrocento", *NAM* 6 (2025) fasc. 21,
  pp. 251-280 (PDF https://www.nam-sism.org/Articoli/Articoli%202025/NAM%20N.%2021.%207.%20VITO%20La%20crittografia%20diplomatica%20e%20militare%20nell'Italia%20del%20Quattrocento.pdf):
  ASLu **Governo di Paolo Guinigi n. 5** is a codex of Guinigi's cipher keys, dated keys from **1412** to 1439;
  c. 22r "alfabetum grecum cum Antonio de Giglis"; c. 2r nomenclator drawings; a key "con Ladislao" (Ladislaus of
  Naples, so before Aug 1414) with one extra homophone for a e i o, nulls and a 52-drawing nomenclator (Fig. 4).
  Vito prints keys only; he mentions no ciphered letters. Keys = key material for any Guinigi cipher letters
  1400-30 (letters to/from Guinigi are in ASLu *Governo di Paolo Guinigi* and calendared in the *Carteggio di
  Paolo Guinigi 1400-1430*, Fumi & Lazzareschi, Regesti del R. Archivio di Stato in Lucca III, 1925).
- Also cited by Vito: Mantua 1401 Simeone da Crema key (ASMn E.I.2a no. 32 f. 6r; Kahn p. 107) - key only;
  Meister 1906 pp. 5-10 Viterbo/Orvieto nomenclator 1326-27 - key only; Lavinde 1379 (Romanini 2020 IMU 61).

## Meister 1902, full survey of the other sections (read from the ULB PDF, index = printed page + 12)

Meister p. 15 gives the earliest cipher he found per state: Venice 1411 (real cipher), Mantua 1395, **Modena 1395,
Lucca 1404, Florence 1414, Siena 1433** (keys; letter 1421), **Pisa 1442**, Milan 1447, Genoa 1481.

- **Lucca (pp. 54-57).** ASLu *Governo di Paolo Guinigi* n. 5 is Guinigi's own out-journal: copies of outgoing
  letters, private correspondence, notes on couriers, and 74 keys (first 11 undated; no. 12, f. 13, dated 25 July
  1412 "cum domino Jacobo de Vivianis"; last dated 1439). **Ff. 34-37: copies of letters with ciphered passages
  dated 1404 to Jacobo de Fartinellis; f. 38 one of 1406.** "Die eingetragenen Briefe haben alle nur einige Teile in
  Chiffrenschrift." Meister prints keys from ff. 1, 2, 7, 12 (cyfra cum domino episcopo; alia cyfra cum ... episcopo
  with a homophonic alphabet a-z + nulls; cyfra cum domino Ladislao; cyfra cum Marino Caravello and Zaccaria
  Trevisano of Venice) - pp. 55-57. He does **not** decipher the 1404/1406 passages. So: **ciphertext (1404, 1406)
  and 74 keys in the same codex, no reading printed by Meister.** Length unknown ("einige Teile"); Guinigi's early
  keys are often pure nomenclators (14 of the first 26), so the passages may be partly code words. Prior-art check
  below.
- **Modena/Este (pp. 33-35).** ASMo Cancelleria ducale, Archivio proprio. The oldest ciphered passages: the letter
  register *Nicolai III epistolae et decreta 1393-1400*, **f. 159v, copy of a breve of the Marquis to Astorgio
  (Astorgius de Manfredis, lord of Faenza), 30 June 1395, "in welchem drei Stellen chiffriert sind"**, and a key of
  the same time (Mappe I fasc. 1 no. 1? Meister's "Schlüssel 1"). Three short passages; probably too short for
  ciphertext-only, but a key of the same date exists. Oldest Este key in Mappe II (ambassadors): 1435 (Ugutio de
  Abbatia, Milan, 23 June 1435, printed p. 35); Meister notes "schon 1430 ein dreistelliges Chiffrenalphabet".
- **Mantua (pp. 38-39).** ASMn E.I.2a *Libri di cifre* fasc. 1: "Quaternetus zifrarum" 1406 (Martinus Ghisulfis),
  55 keys 1401-1416 (Malatesta, Bondioli to Venice 1404, Pio 1404, Strozzi 1404, Milan, Carpi, Prato 1412, Torelli
  1415, Capra 1415, Penzauro at Constance). E.XXV.3 Rubrica Roma: 1426 (sic; = 1428) Forlì letter of Riccardo de
  Mutiliana "ganz in Chiffren", 22 Apr 1428 Pandolfo Malatesta, 9 Aug 1428 unsigned all-cipher, two 1430 Cattanei
  letters. **All five are DECODE R1854, already done here** (r1854/: four read, P1 open). Meister's "1426 Nov. 6"
  vs R1854's "6 Nov 1428": date discrepancy worth a check against the image docket. E.V.3: Armanini 1395 (known).
- **Venice (pp. 16-24).** Vowel-dot/cross systems 1226-27 (Liber plegiorum; read by Meister), Greek/Hebrew letters
  1290-91, nomenclators 1350 and 1358 (Pasini 1873 pp. 292-293). "The oldest dispatch ciphered in the proper sense"
  in the Venetian archive: **28 June 1441**, lithographed as tav. I of Pasini's 1873 essay (*Il R. Archivio
  generale di Venezia*, pp. 291 ff.). Status of its reading: see below.
- **Milan (p. 25).** Oldest key 1447 (Aragon ambassador); nothing earlier in Pot. Est. Cifre e chiavi.
- **Pisa (pp. 57-58).** 1442 key (Gozzoli-Lamberti, Opera della Spina filza 1895, printed from Lupi 1875); the
  cipher letters are in Cod. C. 29/30, late 15th c. (1496), mostly with interlinear solutions. Not pre-1449 letters.
- **Genoa (pp. 58-60).** Starts 1481. Nothing earlier.

### Lucca prior-art check: RULED OUT (read in print 1925)
- Fumi & Lazzareschi, *Carteggio di Paolo Guinigi 1400-1430* (Regesti del R. Archivio di Stato in Lucca III, 1925),
  PDF (516 pp., image only): https://archiviodistatoinlucca.cultura.gov.it/fileadmin/risorse/allegati/biblioteca/fumi_lazzareschi.pdf
  p. 7 no. 15, 26 Feb 1404, "(Volg.) - * Jacobo de Faitinellis [Roma]", c. 32: the letter is printed at length with
  the ciphered words and passages set in spaced italics, already deciphered (*Papa*, *re Ladislao*, *Fiorentini*,
  whole clauses such as *Il duca di Orliens ... in Francia*). Meister's "Fartinellis" = Faitinellis. So the 1404-06
  cipher passages in Gov. Paolo Guinigi n. 5 were read with Guinigi's own keys and printed; Pelling (Cipher
  Mysteries, 10 June 2020, "Paolo Guinigi and ciphers") quotes the editors: italics = passages in cipher or signs.
  Incoming ciphered letters calendared there (e.g. Dino ser Paci, Rome 1406; Guido da Pietrasanta, Nicolao da
  Moncicoli, Nicolao Arnolfini 1413/1418) are treated the same way. No images online. Residual possibility: a
  passage the editors marked as unread; not checked page by page (no text layer). Low priority.

### Venice "28 June 1441": RULED OUT, a Meister misprint for 1411 (= Steno, known)
- Pasini's essay in *Il R. Archivio generale di Venezia* (1873), archive.org `ilrarchiviogene00venegoog`, djvu text
  l. 19104 ff.: the oldest Venetian cipher document is Doge Michele Steno's ducal to Fantino Michiel and Bartolomeo
  Nani (ambassadors to the Pope and Ladislaus), "data del 28 giugno 1411", 36 lines of which 29 in cipher, Secreti
  del Senato vol. IV c. 184v, lithographed as Tavola I with the decipherment from the register. Meister's "1441"
  is a misprint. Already known/read.
- Side lead from the same footnote: a Senate *parte* of 13 May 141? (OCR "14IS"; Secreti vol. V p. 231, c. 22v)
  ordering a letter to the Venetian ambassadors in Poland/Hungary to be written "per ziffram", "probably in the
  same cipher" as Steno's. Whether a ciphertext survives (register copies are normally in clear): unknown. Low.
- 1350 and 1358 Venetian items (Pasini pp. 292-293) are word-substitution lists (nomenclators) in clear, not
  ciphertexts.

### Florence 1414: Guasti check (no sender found; Guasti prints ciphers already read)

## Meister 1906 (*Die Geheimschrift im Dienste der päpstlichen Kurie*), archive.org `diegeheimschrift00meis` djvu text

- 14th-15th-c. curial items (ASV Arm. C fasc. 78 nos. 1-13; Petrus de Gratia/Ameilh letter-book Arm. LIII t. 9,
  1363-64) are vowel systems and nomenclators; Meister deciphered them (he gives the "Auflösung" of each sample, and
  says of Ameilh's letters that where no gloss existed "gelang mir die Entzifferung"). Not candidates.
  Arm. C fasc. 78 nr. 2: a nomenclator "and a ciphered letter, dated III° martii", addressed to the Pope, 15th c.
  (vowel-reversal + consonant-reversal system, pp. 18-19); Meister prints the system and a sample line with its
  solution. The rest of that letter's text is not printed; trivially readable with the printed system. Short.
- **Lead (crossover with the Iberia sweep): Benedict XIII, Barcelona.** Meister p. 22-23 (from Ehrle): "Archivo de
  la Iglesia Catedral [Barcelona], Kapsel: *Documentos referentes a la familia Luna I*": (a) an autograph letter
  of Benedict XIII with some ciphered lines, and (b) **an instruction filling about three folio pages, a large part
  in cipher**, "mixed with signs" (Lavinde-type system, i.e. homophonic letters + signs + nulls, not a vowel
  system). Date c. 1394-1417. Meister did not read or print them. This is the concrete shelfmark behind the Iberia
  file's "Climent / Benedict XIII ciphered correspondence" lead. Length (3 folio pages, largely cipher) is enough
  for ciphertext-only work, and Lavinde's key book (ASV Collect. 393 ff. 166-181, printed by Meister as key
  collection no. I) gives the sign repertoire. **Top candidate if not read since** (check Ehrle, Archiv für
  Literatur- und Kirchengeschichte; Puig i Puig 1920 *Pedro de Luna*; Climent Parcet & París Muñoz 2012).
- **Bologna, Pisan-obedience key, after 1412.** ASBo, Demaniale, S. Francesco (PP. Min. Conv.), mazzo 237/4369:
  Marco Canetoli's account register containing his key with "domino Dominico familiari in curia apostolica"
  (alphabet + nomenclator: Bononia, Papa, Gozadini, Guidotti, Isolani, Pepoli, Bentivogli, Zambeccari, Marchio
  Ferrarie ...; nulls). Meister: Canetoli "maintained a ciphered correspondence" with Domenico after the 1412
  Rome council; only the key was found. Letters: unknown, none cited. Key only -> low.
- Lavinde 1379 key book: key only (Romanini 2020 prints more). Clement VII ciphertexts: none cited.
- ACB check: the ACB series index (https://arxiu.catedralbcn.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/INDEX-Series-web.pdf)
  lists Schism codices (Petrus de Luna treatises, Acta concilii Perpinianensis, "Littera ad papam Benedictum",
  a private copy of a Benedict XIII document of 1395) but no entry "xifra/cifra"; the "Documentos referentes a la
  familia Luna I" capsule is not identifiable in it. Needs a direct enquiry to ACB.

## Siena: ASSi Concistoro 2308 "Cifrari e lettere cifrate", sec. XIV-XVI (confirmed in the printed inventory)
- Cecchini (ed.), *Archivio del Concistoro del Comune di Siena. Inventario* (PAS X, Roma 1952), PDF
  https://dgagaeta.cultura.gov.it/public/uploads/documents/PAS/5b27904b5c1ff.pdf, p. 366, series XII "Cifrari":
  "Sono gli avanzi di una serie dispersa, a cui sono unite anche **varie lettere in cifra di provenienze diverse,
  solo parzialmente decifrate**. N. 2308. Sec. XIV-XVI. Cifrari e lettere cifrate. Filza cart. sciolta."
- This is Meister's busta (keys fasc. 1 + 29 cipher letters fasc. 2, oldest dated **1421**). The archive's own
  inventory says the letters are only partly deciphered. The Ilardi reel 1503 (Yale Dataverse) filmed only the
  first 13 frames (cover + key sheets); the letters were not scanned, but the reel on film at Yale presumably
  continues (Bowern's note on reel 58 says she scanned only the first 45 of ~150 frames; same pattern likely here).
- Candidate: **ASSi Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2 no. 1, cipher letter dated 1421** (sender/recipient unknown), plus
  undated 15th-c. letters in the same fascicle, with ~18 15th-c. Sienese keys (1433 onward) in fasc. 1 to test.
  Breakability: Sienese letters often cipher only phrases (Meister), so length is the question; the keys in the
  same filza make even short pieces readable if a key matches. Prior art: none found (Meister prints only keys).
  Images: not online; ask ASSi for Concistoro 2308, or ask Yale (Bowern) to scan more of reel 1503.

## Florence 1425: Averardo de' Medici & Piero Guicciardini, Faenza, 27 Oct 1425, to the Dieci - cipher unread in 1425 copy
- Guasti, *Commissioni di Rinaldo degli Albizzi* vol. 2 (archive.org `bub_gb_3ZkvD-9ug8IC`, djvu l. 27950-28000,
  printed p. 457-458, Commissione XLIII): the Dieci forwarded to Rinaldo's embassy in Rome the letter "de'
  nostri commissari da Faenza, de' dì 27" (Averardo di Francesco de' Medici and Piero di Luigi Guicciardini,
  footnote 2), asking for it back. Rinaldo's register copies the clear opening (Perin Turco, messer Sbardellato)
  and then breaks off: "**Quanto più pensiamo etc. quia multa sunt ciferata, nobis ignota. Sed infra, in fine:
  Faventie, 27 octobris 1425**". The embassy did not have that key; Guasti prints no decipherment.
- The original went back to the Dieci: ASF, Dieci di Balìa, Responsive (1425 filza; Gabbrielli numbering filze
  4-7 cover 1424-25). **Gabbrielli's key volume has no key for 1425-29**: reel 58 frames 58-4 (no. 3 "Johannes",
  1424, filza 7), 58-5 (no. 4 "Zaninus et Conradinus", 1424), then 58-6..58-9 (nos. 5-8) are all 1430, Dieci
  filza 8 (Fibindacci 1430; "Johannes Ludovicus comes"; a "Zenobia/Clisca comitissa"; commissioners). So the
  Averardo cipher of 1425 was apparently never reconstructed by Gabbrielli, and the one contemporary copy says the
  cipher was "unknown".
- Status: **pre-1449 (Oct 1425), apparently unread, "multa" = a substantial cipher portion; Averardo was
  commissioner in Romagna 1424-26, so sibling cipher letters to the Dieci should exist in the same filze** - a

## Ranking (23 Sept 2026; earliness x breakability x image access)
1. **Benedict XIII cipher instruction, ACB Barcelona, "Documentos referentes a la familia Luna I"** (c. 1394-1417;
   ~3 folio pages largely in cipher + autograph letter with cipher lines; Lavinde-type homophonic; no reading
   found). Earliest and longest; images need ACB. Shared with the Iberia sweep.
2. **Averardo de' Medici & Piero Guicciardini, Faenza 27 Oct 1425, ASF Dieci Responsive** ("multa ... ciferata,
   nobis ignota"; no Gabbrielli key for 1425-29); siblings likely (Averardo's Romagna commission 1424-26).
3. **ASSi Concistoro 2308**: cipher letters XV-XVI c., "solo parzialmente decifrate" per the 1952 inventory,
   oldest dated 1421; 18 15th-c. Sienese keys in the same filza (13 frames on Yale Dataverse reel 1503). Also the
   secondary goal (unsolved Italian ciphers not on DECODE/Tomokiyo).
4. **Florence 1414, ASF Signori Responsive filza 1** (Meister's "Cifra Decemviri di Balia 1414"; sender unknown;
   partial key printed; letters read in part by Gabbrielli only).
5. **Alessandro Sforza to Francesco Sforza, 1 Apr 1447** (Osio III no. CCCCX; "alcune linee in cifra" unexplained)
   and **Perugia 15 Jan 1428 to Galeazzo Bernardo da Crema** (Osio II no. CCXXXIII; short passages, key not found).
   Short; ASMi.
