# Oldest-cipher scan, 23 Sept 2026: France, Burgundy, Avignon, England, Scotland, Low Countries; medieval MS cryptograms

Goal: undeciphered ciphertexts before 1449 (ideally before 1425), images online, breakable ciphertext-only;
secondary, hard unsolved ciphers of any date not on DECODE / cryptiana / Schmeh / Dunin.
Findings appended as they are checked.

## Alchemical cipher survey (Lang, Zotov & Piorko, "Sources of Alchemical Cryptography", HistoCrypt 2025, dspace.ut.ee)
Over 100 alchemical cipher instances from library visits. The pre-1450 items in it are keys/tables or a line or two,
not ciphertext worth a solve:
- Glasgow MS Hunter 110 (late 14th c., English hand): number/letter tables on one page. Key-like, no text.
- Wolfenbüttel HAB Cod. Guelf. 80.4 Aug. 8° (15th c.): one line of ciphertext. Too short.
- Gotha Chart. B 1156 (c. 1455): six pages "with some coded lines" and a cipher-alphabet list. Short; post-1449.
- Manchester Rylands Latin MS 65 (15th c., Italian alchemy): cipher table (possibly polyalphabetic) + nomenclature
  page; no ciphertext reported. Rylands German MS 1 (15th c., Bavarian): cipher alphabet only.
- Sloane MS 1118 (late 15th c.): a page of cipher alphabets, no text. Harley MS 2407: a 0/1 string, one line.
- Their footnote: Timmermann 2015 (*Verse and Transmutation*, Cambridge alchemica) lists more Cambridge ciphers, not
  surveyed here.
Dead end for the pre-1449 goal. Secondary: Wellcome MS 309 (Gerlach 1572, ~170 pp. German in a 51-sign homophonic
cipher) is the longest, but Julius Kohn's manuscript key is bound in it, so it is read in effect.
Wellcome MS 259 (1649): "further names follow in an unresolved cipher", a few words only.

## Dead ends, quick
- French and English diplomatic cipher before 1450: the literature is consistent that French diplomats began
  enciphering only c. 1500 (monoalphabetic, homophonic from the 1520s) and English c. 1505 (Henry VII); no
  pre-1450 French/English/Burgundian ciphered despatch surfaced in searches. Pre-1450 ciphers touching France are
  Italian/Aragonese/papal ones (other scans).
- Catherine of Aragon 1509 "undeciphered" cipher passage: already solved (catalogue 326, MTC3 2018).
- TNA SP 78/129 f.180r, Perwich 1670 Morland transposition: already a target here (`perwich/`).
- BL Add MS 10035 "Subtelty of Witches" (1657 label, 410 pp. entirely in cipher): solved, HistoCrypt 2022
  ("A Reformation Era Cipher Mystery"): substitution over Latin verbs from Calepino, post-1543.
- Raab Collection computus cipher (Flanders/Luxembourg c. 1400-10): cipher alphabet written out beside it,
  decoded; private sale. Dead end.
- Alphabetum Kaldeorum (LMU 4° Cod. ms. 810, 1428; Rudolf IV): key and read inscription; Central scan's area.

## Meister 1906 (*Die Geheimschrift im Dienste der päpstlichen Kurie*, archive.org diegeheimschrift00meis), early chapter
Read for 14th-15th c. papal ciphertexts. What it holds:
- Viterbo nomenclator instruction 1326/27 (ASV Arm. C fasc. 78 / D fasc. 2): a key, printed. Not a target.
- **Petrus de Gratia, archbishop of Naples, letter-book 1363-64** (ASV Arm. LIII t. 9, pp. 1-125): drafts and copies of
  letters to Urban V and cardinals, partly in vowel-substitution ciphers with nulls (signs or b/f/k/p/x for vowels,
  l/m/n/r/s sometimes replaced). Some passages glossed; Meister says he deciphered the rest and prints plates I-V.
  Read (and trivial systems). Dead end, but the oldest papal cipher correspondence; if the project ever wants a
  "1363" item it is a transcription job, not a break.
- Lavinde 1379 key book (ASV Collect. 393 ff. 166-181): keys only. Meister names no surviving Clement VII
  ciphertext.
- **LEAD, Avignon obedience: Benedict XIII (Pedro de Luna, 1394-1423) ciphertexts in Barcelona.** Meister p. 22-23,
  citing Ehrle: Barcelona, Archivo de la Iglesia Catedral (Arxiu Capitular, ACB), capsule "Documentos referentes a la
  familia Luna I": (1) an autograph letter of Benedict XIII with some ciphered lines; (2) an instruction of about
  three folio pages, "a large part in cipher", in a Lavinde-type system (letters mixed with signs, per Ehrle).
  Meister never saw them and prints no decipherment. Probably the same material as the Iberia scan's Climent Sapera
  lead ("llarga correspondència xifrada", Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana; Galende 2013 "criptogramas del obispo de
  Barcelona, Francisco Climent"). Three folio pages largely in cipher = perhaps 1,500-3,000 signs, Latin or
  Aragonese/Catalan, monoalphabetic with nulls/homophones in the Lavinde style: breakable ciphertext-only.
  Date c. 1395-1415. Status: no publication of a decipherment found; ACB web search gives nothing; ACB codex index
  (INDEX-Series PDF) lists Luna treatises (nos. 128-146) but not this capsule; images not online. Needs an enquiry
  to ACB (arxiu.catedralbcn.org) for the "familia Luna" capsule and a photo order. Best pre-1425 lead of this scan.
- Pisan obedience: Marco Canetoli ↔ curial Domenico key, after 1412 (ASBo, Demaniale S. Francesco 237/4369): key
  only; Canetoli's ciphered letters not located (Italy scan).
- Meister footnote: "Kalendarium" of the King of Navarre (Charles II), seized with Jacques de Rue (1378): a 122-word
  nomenclator, no alphabet, c. 1375 (Moranvillé, *Chronographia regum Francorum* II 348). Checked below.

- Checked: Charles II of Navarre's "kalendarium" (seized with Jaquet de Rue, March 1378), printed in full in
  *Chronographia regum Francorum* II (Moranvillé 1893) pp. 349-350 and Cochon's *Chronique normande* p. 153: a
  code-name list (Rex Francie = Nummularius, Imperator = Agrippa, Rex Anglie = Laceratus, Rex Navarre = Callidus,
  Parlamentum = Zodiacus...). Words for words, no alphabet: any letter in it is already readable from the list.
  Dead end as a cipher target (a curiosity for a paper footnote: a 1378 French-seized nomenclator in print).

## Bodleian: "Medieval Manuscripts in Oxford Libraries" TEI (github.com/bodleian/medieval-mss, 11,122 records) grepped
for cipher/cypher/cryptogra/secret writing: 35 hits. All are ownership cryptograms (vowel-for-number, bfk,
musical cipher of a friar's name in MS Lat. th. d. 1, 1430-36), keys (Bodl. 572, St John's 17), or recipes with
1-5 vowel digits (Ashmole 399), all read in the catalogue itself. Only unread items: MS Barocci 27 (Greek, Cyprus?
c. 1310-25), "unintelligible cryptography on fols. 58r and 85r by another later owner", and Barocci 122 (1280)
f. 22v "some cryptography": Byzantine owner notes, very likely a few words each; images via Digital Bodleian if
digitised. Not a record target. (The web catalogue sits behind an Anubis bot wall; the GitHub TEI is the way in.)

## Cambridge and BL printed catalogues (archive.org full text) grepped for cipher/cypher/cryptogram
James, Trinity College (4 vols), Corpus Christi (2), Gonville & Caius (2); CUL *Catalogue* 1856-67 (5 vols);
Harleian catalogue 1808 (3 vols); Ayscough 1782 (Sloane). 80 hits, none a pre-1450 ciphertext of any length:
- Trinity O.1.? no. 1089: 16th-c. recipe book, "a good deal of cypher resembling music", key on the last leaf of the
  companion notebook: keyed, late.
- Trinity no. 1351 (Dee's MS 34, s. xv ex.): "On f. 68 more cypher-writing with numerals and Greek letters", cipher
  alphabets in a *De modis scribendi* text. Short, late 15th c.
- Trinity O.2.40-41 area (no. ~1109), Womyndham of Kirkby-super-Wreak 1482-92: "names in cypher" in an English
  astrological tract. Name signs only.
- Trinity no. 582 (s. xv in.): owner inscription in cipher "Quod johannes Cok"; the rest are bfk owner cryptograms
  (e.g. "bstcbpkbt ... ktfpr"), CCCC 326 (Old English), CCCC 512 (simple ciphers among medical verses), Caius 457
  (name in cipher). All read or trivially readable, a few words each.
- Harley: all Tudor-Stuart diplomatic ciphers (Walsingham, Leicester, Cobham), no medieval item.
Conclusion: the English medieval manuscript layer holds owner/scribe cryptograms, not texts. Dead end for the goal.

## Wellcome Collection API (works, manuscripts, dated to 1500 or earlier, query cipher/cypher/cryptogram)
- **Wellcome MS.105** (wellcomecollection.org/works/y3dqr9mf), German medical compendium copied before 1435
  (c. 1430-34): item 16, ff. 101v-102r, "medical receipts, in Latin and German, partly in cypher". Two columns of
  recipes, so the cipher part is likely vowel-substitution words or short runs (the usual recipe-book practice).
  Date qualifies (<1435) but the text is probably tens of signs and almost certainly a trivial system. Worth one look
  at the images if Wellcome has digitised it (not checked: item has no public image URL in the API).
- Wellcome "Der Güldene ..." (1478): one title phrase in cypher. Trivial.
Everything else in the Wellcome alchemical cipher set is 16th-18th c. (see Lang et al. above).

## Gallica SRU (dc.type manuscrit; cryptographique / cryptogramme / écriture secrète / alphabet secret / chiffré), pre-1500
~170 records; after dropping "chiffré" = foliated and liturgical "secrète", the cryptographic items are all
one-line pen trials or owner notes: BnF lat. 12971 (s. xiii ex.-xiv, Saint-Germain; notes on John XXII) f. 1r top
margin, one line in a vowel-shift cipher with "virgo maria" glossed above (image checked, canvas f2); Echternach
Gospels lat. 9389 f. 223v (15th-c. vowel-digit note of Thomas d'Este, read in the notice); lat. 5 (Limoges) pen
trials; Isidore (Corbie) Ps. 70 in cryptography (Bischoff 1954 p. 15, read); fr. 571 bfk colophon. No text-length
ciphertext. Dead end.

## Other sweeps, dead ends
- e-codices full-text ("Geheimschrift", "cryptogram", "Kryptogramm", "chiffre"): Old High German bfk glosses,
  Aethicus-alphabet texts (e.g. Julian of Toledo *De partibus orationis* written wholly in the Aethicus Ister
  alphabet: a known alphabet, read), dot-vowel words, owner notes (Otto of Passau in an Ockham MS, f. 121r). Nothing
  unread of length.
- HistoCrypt 2018-2025 titles and Pelling's "Fifteenth century cryptography revisited" thread: no unsolved
  pre-1450 text cipher outside Voynich; the 1395-1411 keys they cite (Mantua, Lucca, Sercambi's Milanese ciphers
  c. 1397, Modena 1435) are Italian and go to the Italy scan.
- Opicinus de Canistris (Avignon, Vat. lat. 6435 / Pal. lat. 1993): rambling Latin/dialect, no cipher.
- Grévin & Véronèse, "Les caractères magiques au Moyen Âge" (BEC 162, 2004): magical characters, not ciphertext.
- Jacques Cœur, René d'Anjou, Philip the Good, Council of Basel/Constance envoys (French, English, Burgundian):
  no ciphered item surfaced; Teutonic Order procurators belong to the Central scan.
- The Benedict XIII capsule is not in the repo's DECODE harvest (catalogue_harvest/decode, no Barcelona
  cathedral or Avignon record).

## Ranking (this scope)
1. **ACB Barcelona, "Documentos referentes a la familia Luna I": Benedict XIII autograph letter with ciphered lines
   + c. 3-folio-page instruction largely in cipher, c. 1395-1415** (Meister 1906 pp. 22-23 via Ehrle). The only
   pre-1425 text-length ciphertext found in this scope with no printed decipherment. Lavinde-type monoalphabetic
   with nulls → ciphertext-only breakable at that length (Latin or Aragonese/Catalan). Blocker: not digitised;
   needs an ACB enquiry and photographs. Merge with the Iberia scan's Climent Sapera lead.
2. Wellcome MS.105 (German, before 1435), ff. 101v-102r recipes "partly in cypher": early and in London, probably
   short and trivial; one look at the images decides.
3. Petrus de Gratia letter-book 1363-64 (ASV Arm. LIII t. 9): already deciphered by Meister (plates I-V); a
   transcription/verification job, not a break, if the project wants the oldest papal cipher letters on record.
4. Bodleian Barocci 27 (Greek, c. 1310-25), "unintelligible cryptography" ff. 58r, 85r: Byzantine owner notes,
   probably a line each.
Nothing in France, England, Scotland, Burgundy or the Low Countries before 1450 turned up: their diplomatic
enciphering starts c. 1500, and the manuscript layer is owner cryptograms.
