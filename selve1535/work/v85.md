## Block 3 (short run, y ~ 2375, struck through; interlinear gloss above it)

v85 l19  clear-before: C[omm]e(?) au S[ieu]r
  signs: monsieurde??????.
  read:  monsieur de ?????? (name code or unkeyed signs)
  clear-after: fugeriu(?) ... les (clear text continues)
  gloss above, in a different, lighter hand: "mons^r de Rang[e]z(?)" — the name reads Rang.. / Lang..; the brief
        suggests "Rangon" (Guido Rangone, captain in French service in Italy, 1536).
  note: the six name signs are / X ʒ X / X then S (null). Pattern ABCBAB. It does not fit "Rangon"
        (r-a-n-g-o-n) or "Langey" under the table; X here cannot be m or u in any French name. Probably a
        separate name code. The gloss confirms "monsieur de" and gives the name.

## Continuous reading

[... et ne luy rester]oit pas beaucop a gaigner pour avoir le passaige franc par terre d'Italie en Espaigne,
comodité pour luy si grande qu'il ne la sçauroit cher achapter; et vous leveroit la navigation de la mer
Mediterrenée, lequel luy [??] asseureroit Gennes, Naples et Sicille.

Et quand les predictes raisons que l'invit[oi]ent a ladicte entreprise de Marseille, luy semble qu'estant vostre
armee de mer dans le port de ladicte ville, qu'elle ne luy peult eschapper, aiant armee de mer plus puissante qui
sera suffisante pour la garder de sortir. Et estant(?) ledict(?) port tellement exposé a la baterie de ceulx qui
seront assis du costé de la montaigne de la garde, qu'il n'y aura vaisseau qui s'y peusse saulver dedans.

[lower on the page, clear: ... au Sieur] monsieur de [name code; glossed "Rang.."] [clear continues]

## English gist

(Continuing from view 84: if the Emperor takes Marseille he becomes master of Provence) and there would not be
much left for him to gain to have a free land passage from Italy to Spain, a convenience so great to him that he
could not buy it too dearly; and it would take from you the navigation of the Mediterranean, which would secure
him Genoa, Naples and Sicily.

And beyond the reasons that draw him to the Marseille enterprise, he thinks that with your fleet in the port of
that town it cannot escape him, since he has a stronger fleet that will be enough to keep it from coming out.
And the port being so exposed to the battery of those who will be posted on the side of the hill of La Garde
[Notre-Dame-de-la-Garde], no ship could be safe in it.

Lower down, one name in cipher, "monsieur de ...", with a contemporary interlinear gloss "mons^r de Rang..".

## Notes on the key

- `∂` (d-like with a curl) = a: beaucop a, avoir (l1), sçauroit (l4), armee (l10), aiant (l12), vaisseau (l17),
  garde (l16). Agrees with the v84 finding.
- Tall `ℓ` serves c (beaucop, franc, cher, achapter, ceulx, coste, dicte) and d (de, l12 "de mer"); the hook `⌐`
  (C with long top) mostly d (d'Italie, dicte, dans, de, du, garder). Decide by the French.
- `£` (h) is drawn like a ‡ with a curled top; easy to take for e (l4 "cher", "achapter", l12 "eschapper").
- `X` plain = m (tellement, montaigne) and u (qui, qu'estant); the looped `Ⴟ` = u/v (vaisseau, vous, leveroit,
  luy, vostre). `Ƶ` (ʒ with a crossbar) = x (exposé, ceulx); its one other occurrence (l2) makes no sense as x.
- `ʒ` with tail = y (luy l6, l9; s'y l17).
- Short f `ʃ` is drawn as an S lying on the line (franc, suffisante); the null S is smaller and upright.
- `ω` = e (passaige, entreprise, armee, asseureroit, semble).
- Blots / corrections: φ (l6, l8), blotted 7 (l17). They look like the cipherer's corrections, not new letters.
- Two strike-throughs: l18 (a horizontal line through a cipher line that is still part of the sentence) and the
  l19 run (struck, with the gloss written above). Neither is the long diagonal cancellation.
- The l19 gloss is the only interlinear decipherment on the page; it agrees with "monsieur de" and supplies the
  name the cipher gives as a code.
