# R8362: Burghley to Walsingham, 24 January 1572/3 (BL Harley MS 260 f. 395; DECODE R8362; catalogue entry "W. Baurleighs?")

Status: no write-up. Closed 22 Sept 2026. The letter is clear, and Digges printed it in 1655. The only "cipher" on
the page is two boxed 9s, which mark cover-names. There is no letter cipher or code to read.

## What the record is

DECODE R8362 (one image, "W. Baurleighs? to unknown recipient, 27 Jan 1572") is f. 395r of **Walsingham's embassy
letter-book** (Harley MS 260, a fair copy; running head "Januarie 1572"). The letter is signed "Your assured frind
William Baurleighe", that is, **William Cecil, Lord Burghley**. It is addressed to **Sir Francis Walsingham**,
ambassador in France. It is dated "from Westm the 24th of Januarie 1572", which is **24 Jan 1573** in modern
reckoning. DECODE's "27" is a misreading of "24th", and its "Baurleighs" is the copyist's spelling of Burghley.
The following folio begins "To my verie ...".

Printed in Dudley Digges, *The Compleat Ambassador* (1655), under "To the Right Honourable Francis Walsingham"
(`burghley1572/lit/digges1655.txt` l. 24938-24965; the OCR is garbled around a page break). Digges prints
"Glisco Davison longeth to hear from Lyons [s]". CSP Foreign has no decipherment, and none is needed.

## The "cipher" (`transcription.txt`)

- "Glascoe Davison longeth to heare from Lyons **[9]**"
- "Steward tould you that one should come from Roome **[9]**"

These are the same boxed-9 markers that R8361 has after "Florence" and "Rome", and R8364 has after "Glasgow".
The inventory in `r8361/digges_codes.txt` shows that a box *after* a word marks that word as a cover-name (a
person or party written as a place name), and the digit inside the box varies. The letter is in plain words, so
all of it reads. Who "Lyons" and "Roome" stand for is a question of historical identification, not a cipher.
Glasgow is plausibly the Archbishop of Glasgow, Mary Queen of Scots' ambassador in Paris. The Rome/Florence party
comes from the same 1572-73 ship-plot correspondence as R8361/R8364.

Cipher tokens (measured): 2 markers, both explained. Every word of the letter reads.
