# Cardinal Matthäus Schiner to Henry VIII, Trent, 14 June 1516 (BL Cotton Vitellius B XIX f. 131) — catalogue 290

Status: found already read (Büchi and Schiess, 1925). Written up as `docs/schiner1516.html`.

**Result, 23 Sept 2026.** The cipher that *Letters and Papers* II no. 2044 (Brewer, 1864) calls "partly in cipher,
not deciphered" was deciphered and printed in 1925. Albert Büchi, *Korrespondenzen und Akten zur Geschichte des
Kardinals Matth. Schiner*, vol. II (Basel 1925, Quellen zur Schweizer Geschichte NF III/6), prints it in full as
**no. 541, pp. 67–72**. His note (p. 72) reads: *"Autograph. Am Rande stark beschädigt, teilweise chiffriert, Chiffer
dort nicht aufgelöst und hier in Cursiv wiedergegeben."* So the original carries no decipherment, and the italics
are Büchi's own decipherment. His preface (pp. VIII–IX) says he and Traugott Schiess, the volume's editor, rebuilt
the keys ("Schlüssel dazu fanden sich nirgends, sondern mussten erst rekonstruiert werden"). They broke every
cipher in the volume except no. 582. They never printed a key.

The catalogue entry said that no reading had been found (sweep of 22 Sept 2026). It had checked L&P and Tomokiyo,
not Büchi. Nothing is deciphered here. What this work adds: the 1925 reading was found; its 14 cipher stretches
were located and counted, with English renderings; L&P was shown to print the words of six of the stretches; the
abstract on the next leaf was shown to summarise the ciphered content in 1516; and the folio numbers were
reconciled with the BL's current catalogue.

## 1. The document

- **Shelfmark.** BL Cotton MS Vitellius B XIX, **f. 131 in the old foliation**, which L&P and Büchi cite. It is
  **f. 129** in the BL's current catalogue (record 040-001102999, item 57: "The Card. of Sion, to Henry VIII; about
  the war in Italy, negociations, &c. (Orig. Lat.) Trent, June 14, 1516"). The abstract is L&P's f. 133, now
  f. 131 (item 58: "Abstract of a letter of the Card. of Sion; about the discord between him and count Galeazzo").
  The volume is **not digitised** ("Digitised Content: No" on the BL record; checked 23 Sept 2026). It is one of
  the Cotton volumes burnt in 1731, and the margins are lost. Extracts are in `src/bl_catalogue_vitellius_b_xix.txt`.
- **Writer, date.** Matthäus Schiner, cardinal of Sion (Sitten), in his own hand, at Trent, 14 June 1516, three
  days after reaching Verona. He was leading Maximilian's army in Italy on English money.
  Addressed *Christianissimo ac sacratissimo d. d. regi Anglie ac Francie, domino metuendissimo*.
  L&P: "Hol., Lat., pp. 4, partly in cipher, not deciphered … Sense obscure."
- **Language.** Latin, cipher included.

## 2. What the cipher says

Büchi's italics give 14 cipher stretches (`cipher_passages.txt`, taken from `buchi1925_no541.txt`). They are
words and short phrases set into clear Latin, from a single word to a 28-word sentence. The count is 88 words.
Four of them lie in the burnt margin and are Büchi's conjectures: *[quod omnia]* in 11 and *[regi Catholico]* in 12.
Two more words are partly restored: *fa]cerem* in 11 and *expende]re* in 4. English renderings below are made here.

The cipher holds the secret of the letter. Maximilian had offered Henry VIII, through Schiner, the duchy of Milan
and the succession to the Empire (7, 8, 11). Schiner told one of Henry's agents under oath. The agent was Pace, as
the abstract makes clear. Soon afterwards Francesco Sforza, Duke of Bari, repeated it back to him, and Galeazzo
Visconti and his circle knew of it too (9, 10). The cipher also gives Schiner's charge that Galeazzo had first
taken Milan for himself and then offered it to Ferdinand of Aragon, and that it was said to have been offered to
Henry (12). The rest of the letter is in clear: Schiner's defence against his English critics, the tumult of the
Swiss at Bergamo, the loss of Brescia, money, and the peace Wolsey asked about.

## 3. L&P, the abstract, and "not deciphered"

- L&P 2044 prints a Latin extract with gaps. The gaps fall on stretches 1–6 and around 12, but L&P gives the words
  of stretches 7–12: the duchy of Milan, the crown of the Empire, Bari, and Galeazzo's double dealing. Its
  footnote 4 identifies a cipher monogram for *Vestrae Majestati*, "not … met with except in these two places",
  and the asterisks mark it inside stretches 11 and 12. Its marginal summary is exactly the ciphered content:
  "Maximilian's offer of the Empire and duchy of Milan to Henry is known to Galeazzo and others." So
  Brewer's office read at least part of the cipher; "not deciphered" was true only of stretches 1–6, 13 and 14.
  Without the leaf there is no telling whether the six stretches L&P prints were lighter in cipher (a monogram
  and a few signs), or whether Brewer worked them out. Text in `src/lp2_2044-2045_bho.txt`.
- L&P 2045 (Büchi 542, old f. 133) is a Latin **abstract made in England in 1516**. Its heading, "Ex literis Rmi D.
  Cardinalis Sedunensis … die xiiij Junii ex Tridento", is supplied from a marginal note recorded before the fire.
  It opens "Primo queritur, quod dux Barri omnia rescierit … et existimat per R. Paceum fuisse indicatum", and
  argues that the "Romanum imperium" and the "ducatum Mediolani" are neither impossible nor unbecoming. That
  is the ciphered content of 2044. The English had the letter read in 1516, from a deciphered copy or by knowing
  the key, and the abstract is an independent check on Büchi's reading of stretches 7–12.

## 4. How it was found (23 Sept 2026)

1. `catalogue_harvest/decode/list.json` (10,106 DECODE records): no Cotton Vitellius record; the only Cotton
   records of 1516 are Spinelly's in Galba B V (see `spinelly1516/`).
2. BL searcharchives: Cotton MS Vitellius B XIX, "Digitised Content: No". The manifest the search turned up
   belongs to Galba B VI, the other hit.
3. BHO L&P II, June 1516 11–20: no. 2044 (the letter) and no. 2045 (the abstract), with Brewer's footnotes.
4. Tomokiyo's cryptiana "unsolved" list: his Schiner item is a *letter to* Schiner, Saragossa, 5 Feb 1520, a
   simple numerical cipher solved by Nicollier, Jacquemet and Evéquoz (HES-SO Valais). It is a different system and
   a different correspondence.
5. Büchi's edition. HathiTrust is behind a Cloudflare bot check, which was not bypassed. Both volumes are on
   e-Helvetica, the Swiss National Library's platform (nbdig-59316; vol. 2 = `nbdig-59316_2.pdf`, 708 pp., 295 MB,
   full text layer). The text layer has no italics, so the cipher stretches were marked from the page images of
   pp. 67–72 (PDF pages 94–99). The scans stayed in the session scratchpad; the lead crop on the site page is
   taken from p. 67.
6. Büchi's preface lists the ciphered numbers of vol. II: 530, 541, 543, 582, 585, 586, 596, 599, 601, 637, 653,
   700, 701, 726, 730, 758, 771, 795, 798, 799, 802, 804, 808, 809, 847. Several are Schiner–England letters in
   Vitellius B XIX and at the Record Office (e.g. 585, Agnou 5 Dec 1516, Vit. B XIX f. 342, with an interlinear
   decipherment). None is in the open catalogue; checked by grepping `catalogue.json` for 1511–1527 entries naming
   Sion, Schiner, Pace, Wingfield, Visconti, Colonna, Lang, Vitellius or Galba.

## 5. The cipher system

Unknown here. Büchi prints no key, no sign table and no facsimile. He names five kinds of cipher used by Schiner,
Lang, Marc Antonio Colonna, Tizzone, Pecchio, Holzhalb, Reichenbach, Carpi and Manuel, "vor allem im Verkehr mit
England". L&P adds only the monogram for *Vestrae Majestati*. In other letters of the volume Büchi glosses single
signs, e.g. no. 585 "Chiffer, bedeutet Ch." (Chièvres) and "Chiffer, bedeutet E." (Eboracensis). That suggests a
nomenclator of name signs alongside a letter cipher, but it is not established for this letter.
