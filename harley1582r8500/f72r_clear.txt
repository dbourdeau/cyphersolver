# BL Harley 1582 f.72r (sib/f71_3.jpg): contemporary clear decipherment of sections a/b/c of f.71r-v. [?] = uncertain.
a The Frenche Amb. hath not written him self but hath
made his wyfe to wryte to the Dutchesse of Nemours
and sent her lre to another frend of hers to deliver to the
sayd Dutchesse, that the Queene of Scotts is in very ill
state, that yf she be not helped and that quickly there is
no remedye but death wth her ether publick or pryvat,
that ys presently the K: do not beleve the matter ether in wry-
ting or sending earnestly to her Emb[assador] about ytt to take the matter
very heavily and earnestly or else to make some League wth
her Ma[tes] enemyes that the Queene of Scotts was quite undon
that yf the K: would enter into that course of League that she
doth assure that there were wayes ynowe to broyle the [wardes?/randes?] in
England, that there is no delayes to be used for Mr Secretary
Walsyngham had sayed openly at his table not two dayes afore he
wrote that there weare those thinge proved and abroode against the
Scottishe Queene that there was no prince in Christendom but would
be ashamed to speake for her and that she was undonne. This letter
is thought that the Frenche Ambassador made his wyfe to wryte for
feare least ytt should [?] [?] interrupted[?] wth the Frenche greatly & there
here that ytt mought be cleared and ytt turned to a womans doinge.
b This is the grounde wherupon Brulard was sent to me and I
thinke the K: will know of ytt.
c There is uppon friday last dispatched by Villeroy from hence the
Frenche Amb. secretary for the least he geveth him self that
name wch Villeroy was very fearfull that I should know for
feare least by knowledge of ytt I should have made him be
sought uppon the sea, and in deede I knowe not of ytt tyll he
was gonne. He carryeth a dispatch to the Ambr about this matter &
by the same bond[?] hand that the Ambrs wyfe sent unto the Dutchesse
of Nemours her lre, the Dutchesse of Nemours hath also
written unto her agayn and sent by the same bearer wherein she
wryteth that I have had commaundement from the K: to wryte to
her Ma. about the Scot. Queene, that she feareth ytt will do
no great good, and by the K.[?] she is to weake a man to enter into
a League, that all the best remedye is to see yf she could by any
meanes find a way to make away Nau and thother that belon-
geth to the Scot. Queene that there mought be nothing confessed
against the Scot. Queene, yf that did not helpe there is no way
but God to helpe.
