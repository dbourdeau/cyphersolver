# David Ungnad, Constantinople, 12 March 1576, to Maximilian II: a despatch wholly in cipher

Status: in progress (blocked: the ciphertext is not online; HHStA photographs of Türkei I Kt. 32-3 ff. 199-203 needed)

Session of 23 September 2026. Catalogue entry 342. Goal set by Daniel: read it to at least 95%.

## What the target is

Láng 2015 (*Titkosírás a kora újkori Magyarországon*, repertory of ciphertexts p. 252) lists it as **C.Ung.03**:
David Ungnad → Maximilian II, Constantinople, 12 March 1576, ÖStA HHStA Türkei I Karton 32 Konv. 3
(1576 I–IV) ff. 199-203, language "német?" (German?), "megfejtetlen" (no decipherment), "teljes" (wholly in
cipher). The same table lists Ungnad's other ciphered despatches as deciphered ("feloldva"): with Carolus Rym,
Latin, 1573-74 (C.Ryu.01-08, Kt. 29-30), and alone, German, 1-16 January 1577 (C.Ung.04, Kt. 34 Konv. 3
ff. 3-6, 16-17, 7-11) and 3-18 August 1577 (C.Ung.05, Kt. 35 Konv. 3 ff. 129-139).

Context: early in April 1576 a bundle of Ungnad's letters was intercepted and handed to Grand Vizier Sokollu
Mehmed Pasha, who had renegades (Adam Neuser among them) try to decipher them (Láng, OUP blog, Aug 2017). A
despatch of 12 March could belong to that period. Ungnad's chaplain Stephan Gerlach kept a day-by-day diary of the
embassy (*Tage-Buch*, 1674), which would give cribs for March 1576.

## The key is on DECODE (found this session)

**DECODE R392** = OSZK Quart. Lat. 2254 f. 23r-v, "Ungnad (David)", a copy made by Antal Gévay in the 1830s-40s
from a Vienna original (Láng T.Gev.20). Two images, fetched with the project cookie and viewed. Transcribed in
`KEY.md`: a monoalphabetic sign alphabet (q and x blank), 11 signs for ch, st, sch, ff, ss, ll, ä, ö, nn, rr, tt,
about 50 nomenclator entries (Latin cover words such as *primus* = Orator, *consilium* = Bassa, *mores* = Türcken,
*spes* = Türk. Kaiser; numbers such as 62 = Konstantinopel, 47 = Ofen; a few signs), and numbers written in
letters (RIM = 250). DECODE has the record with no correspondents, no language and status N/A.

No homophones, no nulls. If the 1576 letter is in this key, reading it is a table lookup. If it is not, it is still
five folios of German monoalphabetic cipher (Láng 2017: Ungnad used "simple alphabetic substitution"), which is
long enough for a ciphertext-only break.

## Escalation

- Siblings: Ungnad's deciphered German despatches of 1577 (Kt. 34-3, Kt. 35-3) would test the key against a
  contemporary decipherment. Not digitised either (checked in the Archivinformationssystem). Blocked the same way.
- Siblings, other fonds: Khevenhüller/Riegersburg 4-6 (Ungnad → Archduke Charles, 1574, partly ciphered, German) would give clear context plus cipher in the same hand and years; no scans, private deposit. Blocked.
- Clear pages: the letter is wholly in cipher (Láng: "teljes"); a covering clear letter or register copy may be
  among ff. 1-304 of 32-3, but the Konvolut has no images.
- Known keys: found (R392, KEY.md). Nothing else on DECODE, HCPortal or in Láng's two books. Added 24 Sept 2026
  (sinzendorff1578/): DECODE R1384 is Hans Breuner's key for his 1575 embassy to the Porte ("Ciffra pro Dno Joanne
  Preyner, Prima Maij 1575", German, transcribed in `sinzendorff1578/key_r1384.txt`), a second candidate for the
  March 1576 despatch; R1380 is an Italian Ottoman-affairs key of c. 1571-74.
- Print: no edition prints the cipher text; Gerlach's *Tage-Buch* gives context only.
- Key rebuild / retry: not possible without the ciphertext.
