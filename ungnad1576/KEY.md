# David Ungnad's cipher key (Gévay copy, OSZK Quart. Lat. 2254, f. 23r-v = DECODE R392)

Transcribed 23 Sept 2026 from the two DECODE images of R392 (`IMG_R392_I3463_P1.png`, `IMG_R392_I3464_P2.png`,
2304 × 3072, images not in the repository). The sheet is a 19th-century copy by Antal Gévay (1796-1845, archivist
in Vienna) headed "Ungnad (David)", leaf 23 of his collection of Habsburg keys. Cipher signs are in pink ink, the
values in black. Láng (2015, repertory T.Gev.20; 2018 p. 60 n. 21) dates it "1570–1580?" and classes it as a German
monoalphabetic cipher with graphic signs, digraph signs and a nomenclator of about 90 entries.

The signs are graphic, so they are described here in words. Unicode look-alikes are given where they help.

## Numbers written in letters (f. 23r right column, f. 23v top right)

The cipher writes numbers as letter groups. Gévay lists them as observed values, not as a system:

KK = 30, cross = 19, e = 15, B = 2, RII = 200, O = 70, RIM = 250, d = 14, bb = 22, aa = 21, RIK = 210, F = 6,
K = 10, b = 12, M = 50, F = 6 (the 6 written over another figure), G = 7, f = 16; Kj = 31, kk = 28, ij = 29.

The pattern is Roman-like with R for hundreds (RII = 200, RIK = 210, RIM = 250) and single letters for units and
tens (B 2, F 6, G 7, K 10, b 12, d 14, e 15, f 16, M 50, O 70). It is not complete enough to write every number.

## How far this key reaches

- It is German (ä, ö, sch, ch, the German cover-word meanings), which fits the 12 March 1576 despatch (Láng:
  "német?") and Ungnad's deciphered German despatches of January and August 1577 (Láng C.Ung.04, C.Ung.05, Türkei
  I Kt. 34 and 35).
- It has no homophones and no nulls, so a transcription of ff. 199-203 can be tested against it at once: if the
  sign inventory matches (about 25 letter signs + 11 digraph signs + nomenclator), the read is a lookup.
- If the signs do not match, the letter is still a German monoalphabetic cipher by Láng's description and ~5
  folios long, which is ample for a ciphertext-only break.
