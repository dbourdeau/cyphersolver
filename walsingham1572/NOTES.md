# Walsingham to Burghley, Paris, 20 January 1572/3 (BL Harley MS 260 ff. 398-399, DECODE R8363)

Catalogue item 123. Session 2026-09-22.

Status: attempted, closed unread. The letter is clear English apart from one six-sign place name and one placeholder
mark; the place name is not read.

## Result

**Attempted and closed from the evidence.** Of the letter's ~560 words, one six-sign group is cipher. There is no
key for it and too little text to break it.

- **What the record is.** Harley MS 260 is a letter-book: a later clerk's fair copies of Walsingham's Paris embassy
  correspondence, 1570-73. R8363 is three images (ff. 398r, 398v-399r, 399v). They hold:
  - the end of an earlier letter, "At Paris the 21th of Januarie 1572", signed Frauncis Walsingham. It is clear
    text. It is calendared in CSP For. x between nos. 726 and 727 as a copy dated 21 Jan 1573 ("Copy. Endd.: A copy of Mr. Secretary's letter": Cardinal of Lorraine, the
    dispensation for Monsieur, Marshal Tavannes);
  - the **ciphered letter**: "To the right hono[ra]ble and my verie good Lord the Lord Treasurer" (Burghley), dated
    "At Paris the xxth of Januarie 1572", i.e. **20 January 1573** in the new-style year. DECODE's date (21 Jan) and
    its "..." recipient both come from the tail of the preceding letter.
- **The cipher in it** (f. 398r, last two lines):
  "The matter of most importance conteyned in the same was touchinge a Gentleman of **[9]** departed hence to
  **2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4** w[i]th intention to imbarke there yf the barke were not departed."
  - `[9]` is a bracketed mark the copyist uses in place of cipher he did not copy. It stands for a name in R8362
    ("longeth to heare from Lyons [9]", "from Roome [9] the shipp …") and in R8364 ("contayning [9]"). In Digges
    it is printed "[7]" or "[s]". It carries no signs, so there is nothing to read.
  - `2 1 ⌘ ε 9 4` has six signs: the place the gentleman went to so that he could take ship. Digges (1655) printed it
    in cipher type as "2 ) ⌘ 6·) 4".
- **Prior art.** Digges, *The Compleat Ambassador* (1655), p. 313, prints the whole letter from this letter-book with
  the cipher left as cipher. CSP Foreign x (1572-74) no. 725 is a signed original to Burghley of the
  same day. It opens with the same matter (the letters and books sent by the Vidame's servant, who left on the 2nd) but
  goes on to the book (a defence of the Queen of Scots) and Dr Dale. It says nothing of the gentleman, Steward or the
  port. Either the calendar left out the secret part, or Walsingham sent a second letter that day. The SP 70 original
  was not seen (State Papers Online). No decipherment is printed.

## Why it cannot be read here

- **No key.** No Walsingham–Burghley key for 1572-73 is on DECODE (no key record from Harley 260 or for
  Walsingham before 1577). Tomokiyo's elizabeth.htm has none. The Cecil–Norris table of 1567-68 (`norreys1567/`)
  was changed in Oct 1568. Applied to the group anyway, it gives "r a ? ? i p", which is nonsense.
- **Same-key material in the volume.** Of the DECODE records from the same weeks, one more digit-sign group turns up, in Burghley's
  answer (R8364, f. 419r, Feb 1572/3): "a Shipp sent to **2 0 ⌘ H 6 1** tt ymagined that Ball there had a meaninge to
  provoke Glasco …". It is the same system: it starts `2 ? ⌘`. It is probably the same port or a neighbouring one. That
  gives 12 signs of 9 kinds. The 1571-72 records (R8356-R8361) were fetched but not searched sign by sign.
- **Too short.** With a homophonic digit-and-sign alphabet, 12 signs fit any six-letter port with a common first
  letter. DIEPPE, CALAIS, NANTES, BREST(E) and ROUEN(S) can all be made consistent. The context (a Maisonfleur/Alençon
  or Scottish go-between leaving Paris to embark) fits Dieppe best, but that is a guess (grade I), not a reading.
