# Henriette de Clèves to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 16 September [1574]

BnF français 3315, f. 15r-v and the clear ending on f. 16r. The two pages of f. 15 are enciphered, but an early reader wrote a word-by-word French decipherment between the cipher lines. The gloss is cramped, crossed by the larger cipher signs, and locally faded. The adjacent clear ending supplies the day and month; the year is inferred from the archival context and the neighbouring Birago letter.

Securely readable anchors include:

> Monseigneur, je ne veulx faillir à vous donner …

The address on the folded outer leaf is:

> A Monsieur, Monsieur le duc de Nevers, pair de France.

The cipher continues to the foot of f. 15v and is followed immediately by a clear French closing on f. 16r. A preliminary reading of that ending is:

> Je ne vous escris davantage car il est minuit et les yeulx me font si grand mal de pleurer que je ne vous sçaurois ... Je n'ay autre chose à vous dire pour ... seulement sinon que voyant de la façon que l'on nous met je voudrois estre en paradis, et surtout Dieu, mon bon frère, qu'il vous donne patience. De Lyon ce 16 de septembre.

The transcription above is provisional and preserves ellipses where the pen is hard to distinguish. A passage quoted by Ernest Lavisse from Henri III's earlier appeal to the duchess, before his departure for Poland, is **not this 1574 letter**. Its similar emotional language is not evidence for deciphering f. 15 and is excluded from the reading.

## Limit of the present reading

The opening, address, and clear ending are recoverable, but a continuous line-for-line transcription of both cipher pages would require a specialist palaeographic pass on the minute interlinear hand or reconstruction of the exact cipher alphabet. The similar 1584 alphabet in BnF fr. 3995 f. 16 is not an exact key: on these leaves `F` is *e* and `9` is *q*, among other differences. Applying it mechanically creates false text.
