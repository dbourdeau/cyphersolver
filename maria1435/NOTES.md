# Queen María of Castile to Alfonso V, Valencia 5 August 1435 (ACA, Cancillería, reg. 3225, f. 59r)

Oldest-cipher candidate A1 (see `../oldest/CANDIDATES.md`). Session 2026-09-18.

- Register copy in the queen's chancery hand. Three items on the page, all dated Valencia 5 Aug 1435:
  1. to Marimon Capellades(?), the queen's procurator at the Roman Curia (clear);

## Clear text of item 3 (working reading)

> Apres humil recomendacio, a [p…] instancia dels **[run 1]** los daquest Regne, veents la gran necessitat, he
> treballat que **[run 2]** es continuada daci a Tots Sants. E **[run 3]** / stat exseguit e complit tot quant per
> vostra senyoria es stat manat / segons per mossen Matheu Pujada(s) p… lanç… daço e dels altres fets ha avisada
> vostra excellencia, la qual me mane lo que li sia plasent, e la qual nostre Senyor exalte longament. Scripta en
> Valencia a v dagost 1435. — Senyor, vostra humil muller la Reyna Darago e de Sicilia qui humilment se recomana en
> vostra gracia e merce. La Reyna.

The slash "/" is the scribe's punctuation (also "manat / segons").

## Cipher runs (transcription, sign by sign)

Signs: digits-like `2 3 4 6 8 9`, letter-like `a d n v M t(cross) E ȝ`, and figures `ff` (double f with bar),
`⌐` (square hook with a bar), `b)` (b with a bracket), `⊙` (circle with dot), `ヨ` (E turned). `8̣` = the 8 with a
bottom-left tail that follows every `6`; `8` plain; some 8s carry a top-right stroke (to be separated).

| run | before | signs | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | instancia dels | `ff 6 8̣ 4 8 ⌐ . b) . 8 6 t 8` | los daquest Regne |
| 2 | treballat que | `ȝ a ff 8 9 d 3 8 v` (line end) `8 6 8̣ 2 . ⊙ .` | es continuada daci a Tots Sants |
| 3 | E | `n 8 a 8 3 2 8 d ff 8 6 8̣ ヨ 8` (line end) `M ȝ` | / stat exseguit e complit |

Dots flank `⌐`, `b)` and `⊙`: probably word signs (nomenclator) or nulls.

## Context and cribs

- 5 Aug 1435 is the day of Ponza; the news had not reached Valencia. The queen was lieutenant-general.
- The five-year truce of Majano (16 July 1430) with Castile ran out on 16 July 1435; the queens María and Blanca
  negotiated its extension (Wikipedia "Treguas de Majano"; the eight-month extension led to the Concordia of
  Toledo, 22 Sept 1436). So run 2 = "la treua [ab/de Castella]" is the leading crib for "… es continuada daci a
  Tots Sants".
- "a … instancia dels [run 1] los daquest Regne": the Castilians and those of this kingdom? the estates?
- "E [run 3] es stat exseguit …": the `M ȝ` before "/ stat" may be "es".

### Comparison with the letter (2026-09-18, evening)

- The **alphabet does not carry over as a whole**: only `ff` ≈ f (⩩), the 8-shapes ≈ the a/u homophones
  (ꝺ, Ɣ), `2` ≈ q, `z` ≈ y, `M` ≈ a, `E` ≈ s are look-alikes, and reading with them gives nothing; the annealer
  with these or with plain-8 = e still returns gibberish (`solve.py`, NOMEN extended to tri and 3).
- The **nomenclator does seem to carry over**, and the letter's dotted/odd signs read as a political list:
  `tri` (triangle on a base) = *regne d'Aragó*; `sq` (barred hook, dotted) = *Catalunya*; `bp` (looped b,
  dotted) = *rei de Castella*; `6` (curl with a top stroke, always followed by `8L`) = *Navarra* or *naus*;
  `3` = null; `t` (cross on a curved foot) = *retornada* or *infant don Pedro*; `O` (dotted circle) = the first
  null or *infanta de Navarra*. Run 1 then reads "a … instancia dels [ff] [Navarra] [8L] [Aragó] [8] [Catalunya]
  [rei de Castella] [8R] [Navarra?] [t] [8R] los daquest Regne", i.e. the parties to the truce, with `8`/`8R`
  as "e" (and), which fits "d'Aragó e Catalunya". This is a hypothesis with four or five sign matches, not a
  reading: the alphabet signs between the word signs (ff, 8L, 9, d, v, a, n, E, E2, M, z) remain unassigned.
- What would settle it: the queen's own key (the 1429 key was issued to the batle general; the queen's chancery
  would hold its counterpart, perhaps in ARV or in the Maians volume itself, ff. 160-161 "aliud abecedarium
  in libro intitulato Segon d'estols e armades, cartes CLX-CLXI"), or the other 1435 folios of reg. 3225.

## Direct attack on the 42 signs (solve.py)

Language model: Catalan quadgrams from ten Gutenberg texts (`lm/catalan.txt`, 3.1 M chars, modern Catalan; the
Wikisource Tirant pages came back as empty transclusion wrappers and then 429). Model: one sign = one letter,
dotted signs = word boundaries, annealing over the three runs with their clear context.

- Unconstrained best ≈ −401 (n-gram-plausible gibberish: "intre ence", "ntientresentr", as 42 signs must give).
- Every crib placement is heavily penalised: "la treua" at run-2 start −63; "treua" at any offset −35 to −90;
  "tregua" −61 or worse; "castella" anywhere in run 3 −50 to −128; "M z = es" −30. Most other offsets conflict
  because a repeated sign (8R, 8) would have to stand for two letters.
- (Superseded 2026-09-22 by the matched control below: true cribs on synthetic monoalphabetic text are penalised
  just as much, so the penalties are not evidence about the cipher.) Earlier conclusion: under a plain monoalphabetic model the expected words do not fit, so the cipher almost certainly
  uses homophones, nulls or syllabic signs (the three 8-shapes and the 6-8L pairing point that way), and 42 signs
  cannot decide it. A key or a sibling text is required; the text alone is not readable to a defensible standard.

## Matched control (2026-09-22)

`control.py` → `control_out.txt` (60 trials per design, 1,616 s). Synthetic ciphertexts copy f. 59r's layout
exactly: three runs of 11/14/17 signs, the three dotted word signs in the same places, 39 letter tokens, and a
random key drawn so that the ciphertext has exactly 18 distinct alphabet signs + 3 word signs = 21, as the target.
Plaintext: held-out Catalan (last 20% of `lang/` corpus `ca-gutenberg`, which is what `lm/catalan.txt` was); LM:
`solve.load_lm()` on the other 80%. Attack: `solve.anneal()` unchanged (20 seeds × 200,000 iterations), plus the
crib test (5 seeds with the first five letters of run 2 pinned, true or wrong). Two key designs: *mono* (one sign
per letter, what solve.py models) and *homo* (1429-key style, three signs per vowel).

| | mono | homo |
|---|---|---|
| recovered (≥ 80% of letters right) | **0/60** | **0/60** |
| ≥ 50% right | 0/60 | 1/60 |
| accuracy median / max | 0.08 / 0.44 | 0.13 / 0.56 |
| solver optimum scores above the true key | 60/60 | 60/60 |
| penalty for pinning the **true** 5-letter crib, median (q10–q90) | 42.5 (19–70) | 45.5 (27–76) |
| true crib penalty ≥ 35 | 39/60 | 40/60 |
| penalty for a **wrong** 5-letter crib, median | 56 | 64 |
| true crib outscores wrong crib | 41/60 | 37/60 |
| ≥ 80% right even with the true crib pinned | 0/60 | 3/60 |

What it means:

- At 42 signs the method recovers nothing, even when the cipher is exactly the monoalphabetic design it
  searches: in every trial the annealer finds a key that the Catalan model prefers to the true one (true keys score
  −452 to −689, optima −344 to −502; the target's −401 is inside that band). So the attack's failure on f. 59r
  says nothing about the cipher; the text is below what this method can break. **Blocker: too-short.**
- **The crib argument above is withdrawn.** The earlier conclusion ("every crib heavily penalised, −35 to −90, so
  the cipher is not monoalphabetic") does not hold: a *correct* crib on a genuinely monoalphabetic control is
  penalised by the same amount (median 42–46, two thirds of trials ≥ 35), and a true crib beats a wrong one only
  about 65% of the time. The penalties on "la treua", "treua", "castella" are neither evidence against those
  cribs nor evidence for homophones. Homophones/nulls remain plausible on other grounds (three 8-shapes, the
  6–8L pairing, the 1429 key's three signs per vowel), not on the solver's output.
