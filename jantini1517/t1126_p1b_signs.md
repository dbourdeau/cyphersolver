## Conventions and shape doubts
- **y/7 and y/m**: a y written raised just after a 7 or m, with its tail running into or beside the base's downstroke. This is one unit, the y is the upper sign. The earlier transcription wrote these as "-/7 -/y". A y that stands alone on the line only occurs inside a bracket, as [y].
- **Placing upper signs**: an upper sign sits over the RIGHT part of its base, which puts it slightly to the right of the base. Ambiguous cases were settled this way, e.g. L18 "s/m 2/m d/7 s/a m/7" and L15 "&/n 2/m zf/7".
- **7 vs r**: r is a 7 that starts with a rising left stroke, so it looks like a v or r (12 cases, mostly in L01-L02). It may be only a variant of 7. Merge the two if the statistics show no difference.
- **2 vs z**: 2 has a round curled head (a "?" shape), z has an angular flat top and foot. The line-final pattern "y/7 z/7 f/x" repeats in L01, L06 and L18 (L18 ends "m/7 f/x?"). The two may be one sign.
- **4 vs 9**: 4 is the angular form with a bar across the stem, 9 has a round bowl. Some 9s are drawn with a joining stroke that looks like a bar.
- **c?**: a reversed c above a 7 (L15, L16). It may be a separate sign.
- **S/7 (L13)**: a long S-curve crossing a 7, different from zf. The earlier transcription read it as 8?/7.
- **3?/7 (L12)**: a 3- or ʒ-shaped flourish above a 7.
- **Base 1**: a short vertical stroke on the line (L03 "u/1?", L07 "s/1?"). It may be a small 7.
- **Base ^**: L17 has "s/^" (a caret on the line). L17 ends with "-/[^]?", a bracket followed by a ">"-like mark.
- **x as a base**: L15 "1/x", and the line-final "f/x" units.
- **Struck and blotted signs**: L08 has {-/[y]? &/7 -/[d] L/[#]?} crossed out by a line. L12 has {#/# #/#}, a scribble, with x/#? just before it. A # upper means the sign is blotted (L06, L17). L15 has #/#, a blot.
- **L04 "./m?"**: an m with a dot above and a horizontal stroke through it, possibly struck.
- **L11 "s/7?"**: a 7 written over, with "≡" strokes and an s above.
